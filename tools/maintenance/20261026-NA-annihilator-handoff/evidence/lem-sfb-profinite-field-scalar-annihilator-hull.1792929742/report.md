## Mathematical investigation

For a finite extension E with uniformizer pi, the subsets U_n = {p : |a(p)| <= |pi|^n}, n >= 1, are clopen. Write e_n for their characteristic functions and M_n=e_n R_E, with transitions the inclusions M_{n+1} -> M_n. Each M_n is a retract of R_E and is also locally free of rank 1 on U_n and rank 0 on its clopen complement. Thus each M_n[0] lies in every admissible class J. For every test map b:Q -> P, an element of the ordinary limit of the M_n(Q) is a continuous function on Q vanishing off b^{-1}(intersection U_n)=b^{-1}(a^{-1}(0)). Because E is a field this is exactly the condition (a composed with b)x=0. This proves the natural equality of sheaves lim M_n=K_a, without using global sections to identify an arbitrary sheaf.

The tempting next step, replacing this limit by the ordinary derived inverse limit, fails in the precise example already signalled by HRS. Let P=beta(N), take a(m)=pi^m for m>=1, and let a vanish on the boundary Z=beta(N) minus N. Then U_n is the clopen extension of {m>=n}. All continuous functions P->E are bounded, since P is compact. The global ordinary limit of M_n is zero, whereas K_a is a nonzero sheaf: a nonempty profinite test space mapping into Z has nonzero kernel sections. In particular K_a cannot be the scalar extension of its global kernel.

There is an explicit nonzero obstruction to surjectivity of 1-s on the product of these global M_n. Set y_n=pi^{-n} e_n. If x_n-x_{n+1}=y_n with x_n in M_n(P), evaluate at an integer m>=n. Since x_{m+1}(m)=0, telescoping forces

  x_n(m) = sum_{k=n}^m pi^{-k}.

The last summand has strictly largest nonarchimedean norm, so |x_n(m)|=|pi|^{-m}. This is unbounded as m increases and contradicts continuity of x_n on compact P. Thus y is not in the image of 1-s on global sections. On this extremally disconnected base global sections are exact (HRS Lemma 4.1 and its proof), so the corresponding sheaf cokernel is nonzero. Even granting exact products, the two-term Roos complex has both H^0=K_a and nonzero H^1. Product and triangle closure place that complex in J but do not themselves place its H^0 in J: no truncation closure is assumed. This is a failure of this attempted proof, not a counterexample to the item.

An equivalent local-cohomology attempt with [R_E -> R_E[1/a]] leaves the cokernel R_E[1/a]/image(R_E) to be generated separately. Moving that obligation to a new node without an argument would repeat the content of the item and violate D1/D2. An integral finite-level Roos argument also cannot be transferred by simply commuting scalar inversion with products; that interchange is precisely not among the hypotheses.
