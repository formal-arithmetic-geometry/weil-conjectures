1. The Noetherian finite-type theorem has Noetherian base R, because a DVR has principal ideals. 2. Localization-flatness and the explicitly defined flat blowup comparison identify the local blowup over Spec O_{T,x}. The local-surface theorem actually states regularity, the Cartier exceptional divisor and E=P^1_l. The complement and properness statements give precisely the two other assertions used; the point ideal is finite type since T is Noetherian. Properness includes finite type in its definition. No excellence assumption from the stronger unrelated surface-geometry nodes is imported.

3. The parameter theorem makes both orders of a,b regular sequences. The associated-graded theorem gives an actual polynomial isomorphism, not only an equality of dimensions. Krull intersection applies to the finite A-module A and the maximal ideal, so pi has finite order. The regular-sequence chart presentation supplies the relation at=b and the quotient l[t]. 4. The strict-transform restriction and complement isomorphism identify the actual generic local rings, so both the function field and valuation of pi agree. No claim that every birational curve map preserves local rings is used. This is elementary from the cited isomorphism.

5. In a DVR the nilpotent is explicitly the class of its uniformizer modulo its m-th power. At a subsequent centre the UFD node applies because a regular local ring is a regular ring by the localization theorem. The prime equation h divides pi with the exponent read in the generic DVR. Thus pi is in the m-th maximal-ideal power. This supplies the lower bound on the next exceptional order, not an unjustified assertion of equality with one component's multiplicity.

6. The two field lemmas are used with finite l/k, already proved in step 1; they supply an inseparable element and a nonzero tensor nilpotent. Injection into the polynomial and rational-function extensions preserves that nilpotent, because scalar extension of vector spaces is exact (extend a basis). 7. The strict-transform theorem really identifies the transform with the blowup on D. The reduced point ideal on P^1_l is invertible by its two explicit PID charts; the invertible-ideal blowup theorem then proves that the map is an isomorphism. Consequently the constant field l embeds into every later centre residue field. A finite extension containing an inseparable element is inseparable. Together with steps 2 and 6 this proves the iteration without assuming any residue field is perfect.

8. The etale fibre theorem is used to identify the localized fibre with a field and hence m_A B=m_B. Etale flatness then gives the associated-graded scalar comparison.
