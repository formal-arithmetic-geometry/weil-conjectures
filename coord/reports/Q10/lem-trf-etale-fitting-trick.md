### Former Step 1: assigned to lem-trf-split-tensor-quotient-decomposition

Set $B=C_h$ and $T=C\otimes_A B$. The hypotheses of [lem-trf-etale-tensor-splitting] are exactly those of the item, so choose $D$ and $\theta:T\cong B\times D$ with first projection $c\otimes b\mapsto\bar c b$. For the fixed $A$-basis $e_1,\ldots,e_d$ of $C$, the tensors $v_i=e_i\otimes1$ are a $B$-basis of $T$: expand $c=\sum a_i e_i$ and send $c\otimes b$ to $(a_i b)_i$, inverse to $(b_i)_i\mapsto\sum e_i\otimes b_i$. These maps are well-defined by the tensor universal property [def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product]. Projecting this finite generating set through $\operatorname{pr}_2\theta$ shows that $D$, and every quotient of $D$, are finite $B$-modules.

Cited in the old argument: lem-trf-etale-tensor-splitting, def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product

### Former Step 2: assigned to lem-trf-split-tensor-quotient-decomposition

Let $J\subset T$ be the ideal generated by the elements $k\otimes1$ for $k\in K$. Tensor right exactness [lem-weil-pbc-tensor-right-exact], applied to $K\to C\to C/K\to0$, identifies $(C/K)\otimes_A B$ with $T/J$: its kernel is the $B$-span of $k\otimes b=(k\otimes1)(1\otimes b)$, which equals $J$, since multiplication by $c\otimes b$ replaces $k$ by $ck\in K$. Put $\kappa(k)=\operatorname{pr}_2\theta(k\otimes1)$ and let $H\subset D$ be the ideal generated by these elements. Then $\theta(J)=KB\times H$: multiplying any ideal element by $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ separates its coordinates, and the projected generators generate exactly $KB$ and $H$. Thus the map sending the class of $(b,z)$ to $(b\bmod KB,z\bmod H)$ induces an isomorphism
$$(C/K)\otimes_A B\cong (B/KB)\oplus(D/H).$$
Both sides and both summands are finite $B$-modules.

Cited in the old argument: lem-weil-pbc-tensor-right-exact

### Former Step 3: assigned to parent assembly Step 2

By [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change], the base-changed surjection $\pi\otimes_A B$ has zeroth kernel-minor ideal $I_0(\pi)B$. Compose it with the isomorphism of Step 2; its kernel is unchanged, so it presents the displayed direct sum with the same ideal. The direct-sum formula [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-direct-sum] gives
$$I_0(\pi)B=I_0(B/KB)I_0(D/H).$$
For $B/KB$, take the one-generator surjection $B\to B/KB$ with kernel $KB$: its $1\times1$ kernel minors are precisely the elements of $KB$, so $I_0(B/KB)=KB$ by the same kernel-minor statement. Define $L=I_0(D/H)\subset B$. Then $I_0(\pi)C_h=(KC_h)L$, as required. This includes the zero module, using the zero-rank/unit-ideal convention.

Cited in the old argument: lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change, lem-trf-fitting-ideal-direct-sum

### Former Step 4: assigned to lem-trf-split-tensor-quotient-finite-fitting

Suppose $K=(k_1,\ldots,k_r)$ in $C$. Then $k_j e_i$ generate $K$ as an $A$-module, so $A^{rd}\to C\cong A^d\to C/K\to0$ is a finite presentation [def-sc-finitely-presented-module]. For $D/H$, the $B$-module $J$ of Step 2 is generated by the $rd$ elements $(k_j\otimes1)v_i$: expand the multiplier in the basis $v_i$. Put $\varepsilon=\theta^{-1}(1,0)$. The surjection $T\to D/H$ obtained from $\operatorname{pr}_2\theta$ has kernel $B\varepsilon+J$, because under $\theta$ this is $(B\times0)+(KB\times H)=B\times H$. Thus its kernel is generated by $\varepsilon$ and those $rd$ elements. Since $T\cong B^d$, this is a finite presentation of $D/H$. We have exhibited finite relation lists for both modules, without any Noetherian hypothesis.

Cited in the old argument: def-sc-finitely-presented-module

### Former Step 5: assigned to lem-trf-split-tensor-quotient-finite-fitting

For either finite presentation $R^n\twoheadrightarrow P$ in Step 4, let $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ generate its kernel. If $n=0$, its zeroth ideal is $R$, generated by $1$. If $n>0$, write arbitrary kernel rows as $z_i=\sum_j a_{ij}x_j$. Expanding the Leibniz determinant formula [def-weil-pbc-matrix-determinant] gives
$$\det(z_1,\ldots,z_n)=\sum_{j_1,\ldots,j_n}\left(\prod_i a_{i j_i}\right)\det(x_{j_1},\ldots,x_{j_n}).$$
Thus the zeroth kernel-minor ideal is generated by the finitely many determinants on tuples from the finite relation list, by [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change]. Apply this with $(R,P)=(A,C/K)$ and $(B,D/H)$; independence identifies the first ideal with $I_0(\pi)$, and the second is the chosen $L$. Both are finitely generated.

Cited in the old argument: def-weil-pbc-matrix-determinant, lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change
