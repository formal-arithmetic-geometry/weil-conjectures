### Old step 1

Put $R=A_{\mathfrak p}$, $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak pR$, and $H=\widehat R$. Localization preserves Noetherianity, and completion preserves Noetherianity, so both $R$ and $H$ are Noetherian. The G-ring hypothesis and the completion-regularity theorem make $R\to H$ regular. Popescu therefore presents $H$ as a colimit of a small filtered diagram of smooth $R$-algebras $B_j$, with $R$-algebra coprojections.

Cited statements: `lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian`, `lem-weil-pbc-adic-completion-noetherian`, `lem-weil-pbc-g-ring-completion-regular`, `lem-weil-pbc-popescu-smoothing`.

### Old step 2

Regard each $f_j$ as a polynomial over $R$ and set $P=R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/(f_1,\ldots,f_m)$. Evaluation at the given tuple defines an $R$-algebra map $v:P\to H$, since every relation evaluates to zero. The finite-presentation factorization theorem supplies a stage $B$ and maps $P\xrightarrow{u}B\xrightarrow{w}H$ with $wu=v$. Thus, for $z_i=u(x_i)$, one has $f_j(z)=0$ in $B$ and $w(z_i)=a_i$. The affine morphism $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ is smooth by the conclusion of the Popescu presentation in step 1.

Cited statements: `def-weil-pbc-mv-polynomial-ring`, `lem-weil-pbc-finite-presentation-algebra-factors-filtered-colimit`.

### Old step 3

Fix $N\ge1$. Every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated, in particular $\mathfrak m$. Reduction of the completion gives an $R$-algebra map $\pi_N:H\to R/\mathfrak m^N$ whose kernel is $\mathfrak m^NH$. Apply smooth-section lifting to $\sigma=\pi_Nw:B\to R/\mathfrak m^N$, with ideal exactly $\mathfrak m^N$. Polynomial evaluation commutes with $t$, so $f_j(c)=0$, and $q_N(c_i)=\alpha\pi_N(a_i)$.

Cited statements: `def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring`, `lem-weil-pbc-adic-completion-mod-power`, `lem-weil-pbc-smooth-section-etale-lift`.

### Old step 4

Reducing $\alpha$ further modulo $\mathfrak m$ gives $E/\mathfrak mE\cong R/\mathfrak m=k$. Consequently $\mathfrak q=\mathfrak mE$ is a maximal ideal, lies over $\mathfrak m$, and its residue field is $k$. Put $T=E_{\mathfrak q}$ and $\mathfrak n=\mathfrak qT=\mathfrak mT$.
