### Argument allocated to lem-trf-closed-subscheme-field-stalk-test

This is the last part of original step 1 and the field-stalk/finite-type/unramified argument of original step 2, freed from any nodal or dimension assumptions. For any y in Y, use `lem-sc-lft-over-field-jacobson-space` on the closed subset closure({i(y)}) of X to choose a closed point x of X in that closure. Because i(Y) is closed and contains i(y), x lies in i(Y); identify its unique preimage as y0. Thus y specializes to y0 in Y. On an affine neighbourhood of y0, y corresponds to a prime below the maximal ideal of y0. These primes correspond to primes of O_{Y,y0}. The hypothesis says that this local ring is its residue field, which has only its zero prime, so y=y0 and i(y)=x is closed in X. This proves every point of Y is among the ambient closed points at which the stalk and finite separability hypotheses were imposed. Hence all its stalks are residue fields finite separable over k. On an affine chart Spec A of X, A is a finite type k-algebra and the closed immersion gives the corresponding chart of Y as Spec(A/I). A/I is again finitely generated over k, proving Y locally finite type. Apply `lem-trf-unramified-over-field-local-criterion` to Y; its closedness, field-stalk and finite separability hypotheses now hold at all points. It gives unramifiedness. No quasi-compactness or global finiteness of the set of points has been used.

The two work plans have comparable scope: one checks three geometric hypotheses and produces the smooth affine lci charts; the other proves closedness by the specialization/local-prime argument, establishes finite type on quotient charts, and applies the all-point unramified criterion.
