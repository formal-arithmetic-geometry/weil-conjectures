Edge cases checked before writing:

- Identity case T = W0, and the identity finite extension: the generic tensor is
  the fraction field, its sole factor closure is W0, and the quotient by a
  uniformizer is the residue field, hence reduced.
- A ramified quadratic complete trait T/W0 with π0 = t^2: taking W' = W0 is
  generally not good since T/π0 has the nonzero nilpotent class of t. Taking
  W' = T separates the generic factors in characteristic zero and removes the
  relative ramification. Goodness is therefore a substantive hypothesis in (3).
- Reducibility after splitting is permitted: every maximal field factor is
  quantified, not only one selected embedding. The tensor ring can be reduced
  without being a domain. No irreducibility of base change is assumed.
- Noncomplete excellent T is admitted, following Remark 3.1.3. No separability
  or finite generation of κ/k0 is assumed, beyond algebraicity of its perfect core.
- The n = 0 term of the perfect-core intersection is included. p = 0 and p = 1
  are excluded exactly by primality, and the prime 2 is included.
- Uniformizer quantification is not vacuous for DVRs (`exists_irreducible`). Zero
  and unit elements are not uniformizers. A unit multiple gives the same ideal.
- A zero extension ring is excluded by `IsDomain`, exactly as for a trait. A
  finite product is allowed to have any number of factors; the actual generic
  tensor is nonzero, so an empty presentation cannot witness it.
- W0 and T have independent universes u and v. The existential finite extension
  can be realized in Type u because it is a finite module over the infinite DVR
  W0. The universally quantified extensions have arbitrary independent universes
  x and y. Factor fields can be quotient models in Type (max u v w); there is no
  independent small testing universe that could make a universal condition vacuous.
