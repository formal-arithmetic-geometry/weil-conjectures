## Review by Q10
I checked the split against B–E with the source statements and every cited node open. The two new files contain only self-contained statements, empty proof lists and empty proof dependencies. Their objects, the finite-type ideal, the twist and its gluing, quasi-compactness, relative ampleness, the two invertible modules, and nonnegative twisting powers are introduced in their statements and linked to definitions. The source places include their exact Stacks labels and clauses. Each is a general intermediate tool, not one of the surface conclusions or an elementary consequence of a cited definition. Their source-tag neighbours are distinguished below.

1. The ideal-sheaf definition actually states that the kernel of a closed immersion is quasi-coherent and identifies its affine sections. The Noetherian definition says every ideal is finitely generated, and the finite-type-module definition converts these affine generators into finite type. The maximal ideal of a dimension-two regular local ring cannot vanish. This is the elementary preparation; no finiteness theorem is hidden in it.
2. `lem-per-integral-blowup-projective-geometry` says exactly integrality and projective-bundle projectivity for a nonzero finite-type ideal. Step 1 verifies these hypotheses. `lem-trf-projective-bundle-to-h-projective` assumes the base's ample module, present here. `lemma-nagata-blowup-proper` supplies properness, whose definition includes finite type; the finite-type-over-Noetherian node supplies Noetherianity. For excellence I use the actual affine blowup rings, finitely generated by ideal-generator ratios, and the statement of `lem-exc-finite-type-localization-excellent`. This avoids using the quasi-excellent-only scheme node as if it supplied universal catenarity.
3. The first new node supplies an invertible twist, quasi-compactness and ampleness on all affine inverse images. These are exactly the hypotheses of the second new node, along with the ample module on the base. Choosing one sufficiently large exponent gives an ample module on the blowup. I did not use the affine-pullback ampleness theorem for a non-affine blowup.
4. The affine-open stalk theorem identifies the base local ring with localization. The localization-flat theorem provides flatness, and the existing flat-blowup comparison is an isomorphism. I wrote the additional local-ring comparison as the equality of two iterated localizations at a prime avoiding all base denominators. Thus the comparison preserves both the fibre scheme and the local rings of points on it; no arbitrary-base-change claim about blowups is made.
5. The regular-local definition permits choosing two generators. The regular-parameters theorem states regularity for either permutation. The regular-sequence chart theorem states the quotient presentation, and the chart map and chart isomorphism specify the actual affine blowup opens. The chart-ring definition embeds it in localization, where the chart generator is a unit, proving it is a nonzerodivisor in the chart. This is a calculation and not a separate unproved regular-blowup assertion.
6. Reducing the two displayed chart presentations by the exceptional ideals gives exactly `k[t]` and `k[u]`. Their overlap inverts `t` with `u=t^{-1}`. The projective-space standard-chart statement gives precisely those coordinates and ratios. The polynomial-over-field regularity statement asserts that every prime localization is regular local, so it does prove regularity of this exceptional scheme.
7. The regularity-lifting statement applies to a Noetherian local ring and a regular sequence in its maximal ideal. Here the chart generator is a nonzerodivisor by step 5 and lies in the maximal ideal because the point is on the exceptional fibre. Its quotient is exactly the exceptional stalk from step 6. Noetherian localization is cited explicitly. The complement-isomorphism statement supplies regularity at all other points. The regular-scheme definition then applies to all stalks.
8. The one-equation node states the equality of local dimensions for a nonzerodivisor in the maximal ideal. The polynomial-dimension theorem makes `dim k[t]=1`; the chain `(0)<(t)` survives at `(t)`, attaining this dimension. The comparison in step 4 identifies that stalk upstairs. All remaining stalks are bounded by two, either through the quotient formula or the complement isomorphism. The definition of scheme dimension is the supremum of stalk dimensions, exactly what this step uses.
9. The strict-transform definition places it as a closed subscheme of the total fibre product; the restriction theorem says it agrees with ordinary pullback outside the centre. I wrote the prime-contraction calculation giving the support of a pulled-back closed subscheme. The extra hypothesis `p in W` is used to include all of E. The union is then two set inclusions and is elementary relative to those cited statements; no multiplicity identification is asserted.
10. The existing closed-ambient strict-transform theorem explicitly identifies the strict transform with the blowup in the pulled-back centre. It applies to this closed immersion without an additional regularity condition on Y. The empty strict transform when Y is the centre is checked using the degree-zero Rees algebra and the Proj definition; the disjoint case has unit ideal.

I tested the geometry on the affine plane at its origin: the charts are `k[x,t]` and `k[y,u]`, with exceptional coordinates t and u inverse on the overlap; the stalk at t=0 has dimension two. The calculation also works for a mixed-characteristic two-dimensional regular local ring, since it uses regular parameters and no ground-field structure on X. For the new tautological-relative-ampleness theorem I checked the unit ideal (identity blowup, the twist is O, ample over every affine base open) and zero ideal (empty blowup, assertions vacuous). For the twisting theorem I checked the empty source, the identity morphism, and the necessity of both the relative-ampleness and base-ampleness hypotheses. In particular pullback alone would fail on a positive-dimensional exceptional fibre. For the support formula W=p gives E and empty strict transform, W=X gives all of X', and thickened W changes multiplicities but not the set identity. For the last clause Y=p is deliberately allowed, and Y disjoint from p is unchanged.
