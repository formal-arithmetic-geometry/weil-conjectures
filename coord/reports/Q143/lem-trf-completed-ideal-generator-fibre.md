Step 1 uses lem-weil-pbc-completion-exact-finite-modules. Its statement supplies the injective completed inclusion and explicitly says that the induced maps are linear for the coordinatewise completed-ring action. The hypotheses hold: the base ring is Noetherian; I is a finite module by assumption; R and R/I are cyclic; and the inclusion/quotient sequence is exact. The value on a constant compatible sequence is obtained from the coordinate formula actually given in that statement. This avoids assuming, incorrectly, that injectivity holds at each quotient I/m^n I -> R/m^n.

Step 2 uses lem-weil-pbc-completion-finite-module-tensor. Its statement gives the surjectivity of the canonical tensor map for the finite module I and gives the pure-tensor formula. Every tensor is a finite sum of pure tensors by the definition of the tensor product; applying the completed inclusion therefore gives exactly a finite S-linear combination of c(a), a in I. Conversely that same finite combination is the image of the corresponding sum in the completed module. This proves the image assertion inside the item, rather than moving it into a new node. Corestriction to the image is an elementary construction of a linear isomorphism from an injective linear map.

Step 3 is elementary, with both inclusions written as finite sums. Surjectivity and R-linearity of alpha identify its image on m I-completion with the R-submodule sum c(b)z. Multiplying z by arbitrary S-coefficients stays in the ideal J, so that sum is exactly (mS)J. This checks the potentially delicate distinction between the R-submodule generated by m and the ideal extended to S; no flatness or unstated scalar-extension theorem is used here.

Step 4 cites def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring to obtain finite generation of m, which is the hypothesis of lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete. That node explicitly provides surjectivity and kernel m I-completion for first-coordinate projection on the completion of the module I; it does not require I=m. The inverse quotient map is checked on a representative a via its constant compatible sequence. Step 3 permits alpha and its inverse to descend to the corresponding quotients, so their quotient isomorphism is justified explicitly. Finally lem-weil-pbc-adic-completion-mod-ideal gives the canonical residue identification and the composite with R as the quotient map. Thus R-linearity becomes k-linearity under exactly the identification in the statement, and the composite has the required formula [a] -> [c(a)]. No mere equality of generator dimensions is substituted for this formula.
