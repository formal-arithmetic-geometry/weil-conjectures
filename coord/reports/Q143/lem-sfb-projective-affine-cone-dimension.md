## Review by Q143

I checked the mathematical content against the opened sources and the actual statements of all 21 cited existing nodes. Each entry in citations.json copies the cited node's whole statement verbatim; the following explains the specific use rather than relying on those mechanical quotations.

**Step 1.** `lem-weil-pbc-projective-space-standard-charts` says the standard opens cover, gives the polynomial affine coordinate rings, identifies the nonvanishing locus of T_i, and gives T_j = u_(j/i) T_i with T_i freely generating the twist on its chart. Those are exactly the facts used to identify the overlap as the principal nonvanishing locus. `lem-weil-pbc-morphism-into-affine-scheme` gives both the bijective ring-map description of affine-target morphisms and the preimage formula for D(g), not merely existence. `lem-weil-pbc-scheme-morphism-gluing` gives existence and uniqueness after the coordinate overlap equations are checked. The equations are written explicitly; no unproved separatedness or morphism-factorization assertion is used.

**Step 2.** `lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra` identifies an affine fibre product with the tensor spectrum and identifies its projections. `def-weil-affine-spectrum` gives contravariant functoriality of ring homomorphisms. The Laurent ring identification is not imported silently: the maps and their inverse images of every generator are written in the step, as are the tensor-ring maps. These are algebra homomorphisms because t and x_i are units in the corresponding rings. This is a routine generators-and-relations computation, not a new geometric clause relabelled as a node.

**Step 3.** `def-weil-affine-spectrum` gives the morphism defined by the polynomial substitution. `lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-closed-subsets-primes` explicitly says closure of a prime is V(prime); it therefore applies to (0) in L[t], which is prime because L is a field. The evaluation map B_i -> kappa(z) -> kappa(z)(t) has exactly the prime of z as kernel, and the prime (t) contracts to the origin. This proves the origin-in-closure assertion without an assumption that z is k-rational. The construction works over finite fields as well.

**Step 4.** I checked that the only new reasoning is elementary topology applied to Steps 1 and 3: Y is closed in the punctured open, its closure acquires no other point than the removed origin, and it does acquire that origin. Consequently the cone is exactly a closure. The empty case instead uses the explicitly displayed closed zero locus and is not forced through the nonempty construction. This is the only step marked elementary; every non-elementary step has cited nodes.

**Step 5.** `lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme` gives the actual reduced closed scheme and the affine radical-ideal description. `lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change` supplies the closed immersion after pullback; I do not infer a scheme-theoretic factorization merely from set containment. `lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra` computes the pullback ring. Its quotient primes show explicitly that the underlying subset is the inverse image of Z. `def-weil-finite-type-morphism` is used only for the definition: quotients of the displayed polynomial rings and their t,t^(-1) presentations have finitely many generators, and the covers have finitely many charts. No theorem about general finite-type permanence is hidden in that step.

**Step 6.** `lem-zl-spectrum-irreducible-nilradical-prime` says exactly that a spectrum is irreducible if and only if its nilradical is prime, including nonemptiness; the leading-coefficient argument supplies the domain hypothesis for k[t]. `lem-sc-p1-polynomial-dimension-one` gives ring dimension 1, and `lem-weil-pbc-spec-topological-krull-dimension-ring` converts that to the spectrum's dimension. `lem-sc-dimension-locally-algebraic` applies to the nonempty open D(t) of the irreducible finite-type scheme Spec k[t], so G has dimension 1. `lem-sfb-scheme-product-dimension` requires nonempty finite-type schemes over a field; Step 5 verifies finite type, and empty charts are excluded explicitly. `lem-pc-scheme-dimension-local-rings` bridges that node's local-ring definition of scheme dimension with the topological dimension used by the item; `lem-weil-pbc-homeomorphism-krull-dimension` justifies passing through the chart isomorphism. I specifically corrected the potential mismatch between those two dimension conventions by citing the bridge, rather than silently identifying them.

**Step 7.** `lem-weil-pbc-open-subspace-dimension-monotone` supplies each chart-to-space bound. `lem-weil-pbc-dimension-bound-open-cover` supplies the reverse bound from a common dimension bound on the cover; it does not assert nonempty-open dimension equality, which is not true for a reducible Z. `lem-weil-pbc-scheme-sober` states uniqueness of generic points for irreducible closed subsets and supplies the required quasi-sober and T0 hypotheses, with the T0 implication explained in the step. There are finitely many standard opens, some nonempty, so the maximum is attained and the shift by 1 can pass through it. I checked that product nonemptiness is justified by the t=1 residue-field point, not by an assumption of k-rational points.

**Step 8.** Again `lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme` realizes the closed cone as a quotient of the affine polynomial ring; the finite-type definition verifies the exact hypothesis required by `lem-weil-pbc-dense-open-dimension-comparison`. That latter node explicitly has NO irreducibility hypothesis; this matters for arbitrary Z, and is why I use it rather than the irreducible-open lemma. I also read its sorry-free project source implementation `AlgebraicGeometry.topologicalKrullDim_le_of_dense` in DenseCompactification.lean, lines 66--101, to verify that it concerns arbitrary quasi-compact locally finite type schemes over a field. `lem-weil-pbc-open-subspace-dimension-monotone` gives the other inequality. Step 4 already proved density, so it is not being assumed here.

**Step 9.** `lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme` ensures R_i is reduced; `lem-zl-spectrum-irreducible-nilradical-prime` then turns the irreducibility of the nonempty open Z_i into primality of zero. The finite Laurent leading-term calculation proves R_i[t,t^(-1)] is a domain, so the same node gives irreducibility of each Y_i. `def-weil-irreducible-components` supplies precisely the nonempty irreducibility notion. The open-intersection, density and closure arguments are written out; they are not justified just by saying “local irreducibility implies irreducibility,” which would be false without the overlap argument. The t=1 lift ensures the chart overlaps are nonempty. The last closure is exactly the closure proved in Step 4.
