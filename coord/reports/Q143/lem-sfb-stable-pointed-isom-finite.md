### Returned step 1

Work with the labelled sections first, keeping a permutation $\sigma\in\mathfrak S_n$ fixed. A finite-type scheme over the spectrum of a field is Noetherian by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian] (a field is Noetherian since its only ideals are zero and the whole field). Apply [lem-sfb-projective-curve-isom-graphs] to $W,W'$: its hypotheses are projectivity, flatness, finite presentation and relative dimension at most one, all included in the stable-family assumptions. Obtain the separated finite-presentation scheme $J=\operatorname{Isom}_S(W,W')$, with its universal isomorphism.

Cited statements: lem-sfb-projective-curve-isom-graphs, lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian. Source: literature/stacks-project/moduli-curves.tex, Tag 0DSQ (lemma-curves-diagonal-separated-fp), used in de Jong section 3.8; graph representability input.

### Returned step 2

Inside $J$ impose $u(s_i)=s'_{\sigma(i)}$ for every $i$. By the closed-immersion base-change clause of [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence], each equation is the pullback of the closed diagonal of $W'/S$ along the two corresponding maps $J\to W'$; this is the definition of separatedness [def-weil-proper-morphism]. Their intersection is a closed subscheme $J_\sigma$ representing the prescribed marking equalities. Affine-locally its rings are quotients of those of $J$ [def-weil-closed-immersion], hence are finite-type $S$-algebras. Its diagonal is the restriction of the closed diagonal of $J$, so it is separated. These assertions need no smoothness of $J$ or flatness of $J_\sigma$.

Cited statements: def-weil-proper-morphism, def-weil-closed-immersion, lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence. Source: literature/stacks-project/moduli.tex, Tags 0DPN (lemma-Mor-s-lfp), 0DPP (lemma-Isom-in-Mor); elementary marking equalizers.

### Returned step 3

At a point $u\in(J_\sigma)_{\bar s}(\Omega)$, a relative tangent vector is an isomorphism over $\Omega[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ reducing to $u$. Composition with the constant extension of $u^{-1}$ identifies these with automorphisms of $W_{\bar s}$ reducing to its identity and fixing each marked section. Apply [lem-sfb-marked-nodal-infinitesimal-fields]. On each normalized component, stability says $2g-2+r>0$: the cases $g=0,1$ are exactly the two special-point hypotheses, and $g\ge2$ is automatic. Thus every such infinitesimal automorphism is the identity. The geometric tangent criterion [lem-sfb-geometric-tangent-unramified] shows $J_\sigma\to S$ is unramified. This calculation applies in every characteristic and includes self-nodes, counted by their two branches.

Cited statements: lem-sfb-marked-nodal-infinitesimal-fields, lem-sfb-geometric-tangent-unramified. Source: literature/stacks-project/curves.tex, Tags 0E67 (lemma-smooth-vector-fields), 0E68 (lemma-nodal-vector-fields); geometric tangent test from varieties.tex Tag 0B2G.

### Returned step 4

Take any DVR $R$, fraction field $K$, a map $\operatorname{Spec}R\to S$, and a $K$-point $u_K$ of $J_\sigma$. By [lem-trf-semistable-marked-base-change] the pulled-back families are flat finitely presented nodal families with smooth etale marking. The original disjoint sections pull back to disjoint sections. Projectivity pulls back: base-change the given closed immersion into projective space, using [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence] for closed immersions and the fibre-product definition [def-nagata-projective-space-base-change] for projective space. Put $D=\sum s_i$, $D'=\sum s'_i$. On the original families, [lem-sfb-nodal-log-canonical-reconstruction] makes $\omega(D)$ and $\omega'(D')$ relatively ample by stability. Their pullbacks $L,L'$ remain relatively ample by [lem-trf-scheme-relative-ample-base-change] and are the log dualizing modules of the pulled-back families by the base-change clause of [lem-sfb-nodal-log-canonical-reconstruction]. Its reconstruction clause therefore identifies the two families over $R$ with the Proj of their respective log section algebras. By [lem-sfb-semistable-log-pluricanonical-lattices], $u_K^*$ restricts to an isomorphism of these graded $R$-algebras, compatibly with multiplication. Its contravariant Proj map gives $u_R:W_R\xrightarrow{\sim}W'_R$ restricting to the prescribed $u_K$. No smoothness of the generic fibre and no identity condition on $u_K$ is used.

Cited statements: lem-trf-semistable-marked-base-change, lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence, def-nagata-projective-space-base-change, lem-sfb-nodal-log-canonical-reconstruction, lem-trf-scheme-relative-ample-base-change, lem-sfb-semistable-log-pluricanonical-lattices. Source: literature/stacks-project/moduli-curves.tex, Tag 0E97 (lemma-unique-stable-model), common-model strategy; agent logarithmic comparison and reconstruction for marked families, extending the smooth/unmarked case.

### Returned step 5

The isomorphism $u_R$ respects the prescribed markings: each pair of sections $u_Rs_i$ and $s'_{\sigma(i)}$ agrees over $K$. Their equalizer in $\operatorname{Spec}R$ is closed since $W'_R/R$ is separated [def-weil-proper-morphism]; its ideal restricts to zero in $K$, so is zero since $R\hookrightarrow K$. Hence they agree over $R$. Similarly two extensions to $J_\sigma$, which is separated by step 2, have a closed equalizer whose ideal vanishes in $K$; thus they are equal. We have proved existence and uniqueness for every DVR diagram, including diagrams over nongeneric points of $S$. Since $S$ is locally Noetherian and $J_\sigma\to S$ is of finite type, [lem-sfb-noetherian-dvr-properness] gives properness. By [lem-weil-pbc-unramified-quasi-finite-at-points] the morphism is locally quasi-finite, so [pqf-lem-proper-lqf-finite] makes it finite.

Cited statements: def-weil-proper-morphism, lem-sfb-noetherian-dvr-properness, lem-weil-pbc-unramified-quasi-finite-at-points, pqf-lem-proper-lqf-finite. Source: literature/stacks-project/limits.tex, Tag 0208 (lemma-Noetherian-dvr-valuative-proper); More on Morphisms Tag 02LS (lemma-characterize-finite), applied after the actual logarithmic extension argument.

### Returned step 6

Let $I=\coprod_{\sigma\in\mathfrak S_n}J_\sigma$. An isomorphism carrying the marked union onto the marked union induces an automorphism of $\coprod_{i=1}^nT$. For every $i,j$, the inverse image of the $j$th summand under the restriction to the $i$th is open and closed in $T$. Intersecting these opens for the finitely many $i$ partitions $T$ into open-and-closed pieces indexed by $\sigma$, and on each piece the isomorphism belongs to $J_\sigma$. Conversely these pieces and their labelled isomorphisms glue to exactly one unordered marked isomorphism. Thus $I$ represents the required functor even on disconnected or nonreduced $T$; preserving the marked union means preserving its closed subscheme structure. There is one permutation when $n=0$. The finite disjoint union is finite by repeated application of [lem-weil-pbc-finite-coproduct]. It is unramified because its diagonal is the disjoint union of the open diagonals of its summands, and local finite type is checked on these open summands [def-weil-unramified-morphism].

Cited statements: lem-weil-pbc-finite-coproduct, def-weil-unramified-morphism. Source: literature/stacks-project/morphisms.tex, Tag 0CYI (lemma-finite-union-finite); de Jong section 3.8, passage to the nonordered set of sections, written out.

### Returned step 7

Now assume $S$ normal integral, with generic point $\eta$ and function field $K$. A prescribed unordered generic isomorphism is a point $a:\operatorname{Spec}K\to I$ over $\eta$, with residue field exactly $K$. Give the closure of its image in $I$ the reduced induced structure $Z$ supplied by [lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme]. Its support is the closure of one point, hence irreducible, and it is nonempty and reduced, so $Z$ is integral. Its generic point is the chosen point, with residue field $K$. The closed immersion $Z\to I$ is finite by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-finite]; composing with finite $I\to S$ stays finite by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-composition]. Thus $Z\to S$ is finite birational between integral schemes. Normality of $S$ verifies precisely the hypothesis of [lem-trf-finite-birational-normal-iso], giving $Z\simeq S$. Its inverse followed by $Z\to I$ is the required global section.

Cited statements: lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme, lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-finite, lem-weil-pbc-finite-composition, lem-trf-finite-birational-normal-iso. Source: literature/stacks-project/morphisms.tex, Tag 0AB1 (lemma-finite-birational-over-normal); the normal-base argument suppressed in de Jong section 3.8, printed pp.608--609.

### Returned step 8

For uniqueness of the global section, cover $S$ by nonempty affine opens $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$. Since $I\to S$ is finite, $I_U=\operatorname{Spec}B$ for a finite $A$-algebra $B$. Two sections extending $a$ give maps $B\rightrightarrows A$ agreeing after $A\to K$. This last map is injective by [lem-sc-integral-function-field-affine-fraction], so the maps, and therefore the sections, agree on $U$. They agree on all of $S$. Under the representing bijection this is uniqueness of the marked-curve isomorphism; no identity restriction was imposed on its generic value.

Cited statements: lem-sc-integral-function-field-affine-fraction. Source: literature/stacks-project/morphisms.tex, Tag 0AB1, affine normal-domain argument; elementary uniqueness by injection into the function field.
