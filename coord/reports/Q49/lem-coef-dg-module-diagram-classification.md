### Returned step 2

Define $\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal D$ to have object pairs and morphism complexes $\mathcal A(a,a')\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\mathcal D(d,d')$, with tensor differential and composition using the graded symmetry. Currying identifies a strict $\mathcal A$-diagram of covariant $\mathcal D$-modules with a covariant module over $\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal D$, as follows. Put $H(a,d)=F(a)(d)$, and, for homogeneous $\alpha:a\to a'$ and $\delta:d\to d'$, put $H(\alpha\otimes\delta)=F(\alpha)_{d'}\circ F(a)(\delta)$. Graded naturality gives $F(a')(\delta)F(\alpha)_d=(-1)^{|\alpha||\delta|}F(\alpha)_{d'}F(a)(\delta)$, so compositions obey the tensor-category sign $(-1)^{|\delta'||\alpha|}$. Conversely restrict $H$ to $\alpha\otimes1$ and $1\otimes\delta$ to recover the two actions. A degree-$r$ transformation is a family of maps at $(a,d)$ satisfying the two graded commutation equations; these combine to the module equation with sign $(-1)^{r(|\alpha|+|\delta|)}$. The componentwise differential is $d\eta=d_G\eta-(-1)^r\eta d_F$ on both sides. Both categories have the same values at pairs of objects, the same complexes of graded module maps, and the same objectwise quasi-isomorphisms and surjections. Thus their projective model structures agree, by the lifting definition of cofibrations. A projectively cofibrant module $P$ is homotopically projective: for any objectwise acyclic $N$, apply the enriched model axiom of [lem-coef-enriched-projective-diagram-model] to $0\to P$ and the trivial fibration $N\to0$. It says that $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(P,N)\to0$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Apply [lem-coef-dg-module-projective-resolution] to $(\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal D)^{\mathrm{op}}$ (a homogeneous left action $bm$ is read as the right action $mb=(-1)^{|b||m|}bm$ over the opposite; this turns the signed left-module map equation into the right-module equation in that node). Between these cofibrant modules, morphisms in the localized diagram category are therefore $H^0$ of the actual module-map complexes. In particular an isomorphism between two cofibrant diagrams has representatives in both directions whose composites differ from identities by boundaries; it is represented by a chain homotopy equivalence of module diagrams, hence by an objectwise quasi-isomorphism.

### Returned step 6

Form a dg category $\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}$ whose objects are trivial cofibrations $c:P\to Q$ in $\mathcal M$ with $P,Q$ cofibrant and in $\mathcal S$. Between $c:P\to Q$ and $c':P'\to Q'$ take the strict-square complex
$$\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(P,P')\times_{\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(P,Q')}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(Q,Q'),$$
with differential and composition componentwise. It is a dg category because the square equation is preserved by both operations. Let $e_0,e_1$ be its source and target dg functors to $\operatorname{Int}(\mathcal S)$, and let $s$ send $P$ to $1_P$. For the trivial cofibration $c$ and the fibrant object $Q'$, the enriched model axiom in [lem-coef-enriched-projective-diagram-model] makes $\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(Q,Q')\to\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(P,Q')$ a degreewise surjective quasi-isomorphism. Its pullback is exactly the map of Hom complexes induced by $e_0$. This pullback is again a surjective quasi-isomorphism: its kernel is the same acyclic kernel $K$. To check acyclicity of the kernel of a surjective quasi-isomorphism $t$, a kernel cycle $z$ is a boundary $dx$ in the source by injectivity on cohomology. The element $t(x)$ is a cycle; surjectivity on cohomology gives a source cycle $y$ with $t(x)-t(y)=dv$. Lift $v$ to $w$ using degreewise surjectivity; then $x-y-dw$ is a kernel primitive of $z$. For any degree-$n$ cycle in the target, choose a degree-$n$ lift; its differential is a cycle in $K^{n+1}$, hence a boundary, so subtracting an element of $K^n$ gives a cycle lift. If a cycle maps to a boundary, lift a primitive and subtract its differential; the remaining cycle is in $K$ and is a boundary there. This proves surjectivity and injectivity on cohomology in every degree. Thus $e_0$ is quasi-fully faithful; it is essentially surjective since $e_0s=1$. By [lem-coef-dgcat-model-structure] it is a quasi-equivalence and becomes invertible in $\mathsf{Hqe}$. Since $e_0s=e_1s=1$, the section $s$ is its inverse and $e_0=e_1$ in $\mathsf{Hqe}$. Every objectwise trivial-cofibration transformation $U\to V$ from step 5 defines a strict dg functor $\mathcal A\to\mathcal E_{\mathcal S}$: naturality is exactly the strict-square equation in each degree. Composing with $e_0,e_1$ shows $[U]=[V]$ in $\mathsf{Hqe}$. Applying this to the spans of step 5 proves $[F]=[G]$, hence injectivity. This constructs the needed comparison for diagrams and never infers it merely from objectwise isomorphisms.
