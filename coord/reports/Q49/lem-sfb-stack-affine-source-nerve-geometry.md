## Review by Q49

I read the item against 076Z's four successive reductions. Each represents a genuinely smaller class of source, and the proof below does not assume that an affine atlas is a covering when the statement allows an arbitrary map. The imported statement is true without a finite-type or Noetherian assumption. The proof uses only quasi-separatedness of h for the nerve geometry; its stronger qcqs assumption is harmless and retained. This does not assert that all qcqs algebraic stacks have affine diagonal.

Step 1 cites the change-of-base-to-integers theorem, whose statement explicitly preserves products and relative predicates without a hypothesis on S. It is needed because S itself may be nonseparated. The affine spectra fibre-product statement gives Q = V x_T V affine, and affine-scheme-qcqs supplies both compactness and absolute quasi-separatedness for V,T,Q. The affine-source-separated statement applies to Q -> V and V -> T with no hypothesis on their targets. I checked all those source hypotheses; V and T are assumed affine and the tensor formula has a genuine affine base T. The diagonal surjectivity calculation in the affine-scheme source also covers zero rings.

Step 2 uses the actual objects-and-arrows formula in the fibred-groupoid definition to identify the square with the base change of Delta_h; it is the relative diagonal over T, not an absolute diagonal over an arbitrary S. The qcqs morphism definition explicitly says every scheme test of this diagonal is a qcqs algebraic space. That is exactly r:R -> Q. Quasi-compactness of R is obtained by the identity quasi-compact-space test in that same definition, and not by an uncited composition theorem for stacks. The first clause of space-qcqs-source-products explicitly says that a quasi-separated map to a quasi-separated space has quasi-separated source, so it gives precisely the remaining absolute property of R. The test Q is a scheme and is affine by Step 1.

Step 3 cites the algebraic-space fibre-product existence statement for closure, the source-products statement for its qcqs clause, and the groupoid formula for the identification with V_p. The pointwise identification records each consecutive isomorphism and its inverse reconstruction using composites; it works in families and with automorphisms, not just on isomorphism classes. Its base is W_0=V, and every inductive product is over the already qcqs V. Thus the proof never invokes the unavailable unrestricted stack fibre-product clause of 075S. The off-unit fibre-product result is a generic tool; the item's split_report declares this tool use with its reason.

Step 4 uses the exact statement of algebraic-stack-qcqs-permanence: qcqs source, quasi-separated target, arbitrary map. It does not use its proof. The source is the qcqs algebraic space established in Steps 2--3 and the target is affine T. I checked the conventions: for a sheaf its diagonal is a monomorphism and its next diagonal is the identity, so the extra quasi-separatedness required of a stack diagonal is automatic; the conventional inclusion of algebraic spaces into algebraic stacks uses the same etale atlas. These comparisons assert no false equality between an arbitrary stack diagonal and a scheme diagonal. All relative qcqs conclusions concern W_p -> T.

Step 5 uses exactly the representable-diagonal clause of the algebraic-space definition; scheme tests of that diagonal are schemes. The scheme V x_S V exists even though S is not separated or affine.
