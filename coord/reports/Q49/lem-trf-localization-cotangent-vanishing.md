### Previous step 1

Put $B=S^{-1}R$. For every $R$-module $M$, the maps $B\otimes_R M\to S^{-1}M$, $(r/s)\otimes m\mapsto rm/s$, and $m/s\mapsto(1/s)\otimes m$ are inverse: tensor balancing proves the first composite is the identity, and the fraction equality in [lem-weil-pbc-localization-module-exact] proves well-definedness of the second. Indeed, $u(s\prime m-sm\prime)=0$ implies equality after multiplying the proposed images by the invertible element $uss\prime$. In particular, $S^{-1}B\cong B$ by $b/s\mapsto(s/1)^{-1}b$, with inverse $b\mapsto b/1$. These formulas need only that images of elements of $S$ are units; they also hold when $0\in S$, in which case $B=0$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-localization-module-exact.

### Previous step 2

Choose the standard augmented polynomial resolution $P_\bullet\to B$ over $R$ supplied by [lem-trf-cotangent-resolution-coherence]. Set $Q_n=P_n\otimes_R B=S^{-1}P_n$, with augmentation $p\otimes b\mapsto\epsilon(p)b$. If $P_n=R[E_n]$, coefficientwise localization identifies $Q_n$ with $B[E_n]$: a polynomial has finitely many coefficients, so one common denominator gives the inverse to this identification. The augmentation is surjective since $1\otimes b\mapsto b$. The augmented alternating-face complex $s(P_\bullet)\to B[0]$ is exact in its augmented form. Applying [lem-weil-pbc-localization-module-exact] to its exact pairs in every degree preserves that exactness; Step 1 identifies the localized complex with $s(Q_\bullet)\to B[0]$, including the augmentation. Thus $Q_\bullet\to B$ is a polynomial resolution of $B$ over $B$ in precisely the sense of the comparison theorem. No finite number of variables or denominators is needed.

Uses: lem-trf-cotangent-resolution-coherence, lem-weil-pbc-localization-module-exact.

### Previous step 3

For each $n$, the universal derivation of [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials] gives mutually inverse $B$-linear maps $\Omega^1_{P_n/R}\otimes_{P_n}B\rightleftarrows\Omega^1_{Q_n/B}\otimes_{Q_n}B$. The forward map sends $\mathrm dp\otimes c$ to $\mathrm d(p\otimes1)\otimes c$. For its inverse, the rule $p\otimes b\mapsto\mathrm dp\otimes b$ is a $B$-derivation into the left module, where $Q_n$ acts via $p\otimes b\mapsto\epsilon(p)b$: it is balanced over $R$, kills $1\otimes b$, and satisfies Leibniz. It therefore induces the inverse, sending $\mathrm d(p\otimes b)\otimes c$ to $\mathrm dp\otimes bc$. The composites fix $\mathrm dp\otimes c$ and $\mathrm d(p\otimes b)\otimes c$, respectively, since $\mathrm d(p\otimes b)=b\,\mathrm d(p\otimes1)$ for differentials over $B$. For each face or degeneracy $f:P_n\to P_m$, both routes send $\mathrm dp\otimes c$ to $\mathrm d(f(p)\otimes1)\otimes c$. Hence these maps commute with the alternating differentials and give an isomorphism of the two differential complexes.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials.

### Previous step 4

Compute $L_{B/B}$ using the constant augmented simplicial $B$-algebra $B\to B$. Each term is polynomial over $B$ on the empty set and surjects onto $B$. Its alternating-face differential from simplicial degree $n>0$ is the identity for even $n$ and zero for odd $n$, so the augmentation induces $H^0=B$ and all other cohomology groups vanish. It is a polynomial resolution as defined in [lem-trf-cotangent-resolution-coherence]. Every $B$-derivation from $B$ is zero, since it kills every element of the base. The universal property [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials] therefore identifies $\Omega^1_{B/B}$ with the zero module. The differential complex of this constant resolution is termwise zero, and the comparison theorem gives $L_{B/B}\simeq0$.

Uses: lem-trf-cotangent-resolution-coherence, def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials.

### Previous step 5

The left differential complex in Step 3 represents $L_{B/R}$ by its standard construction. By Step 2 and the resolution-comparison isomorphism of [lem-trf-cotangent-resolution-coherence], the right differential complex represents $L_{B/B}$. The chain isomorphism of Step 3 and Step 4 yield $L_{B/R}\simeq L_{B/B}\simeq0$. In the derived category of [def-weil-abstract-derived-category], cohomology of an object represented by a complex is its kernel modulo image. The displayed isomorphism therefore gives $H^i(L_{B/R})=0$ for every integer $i$. Conversely, a complex with all cohomology zero has a quasi-isomorphism to the zero complex, which becomes invertible in that localization. This also proves the stated equivalence of the two formulations.

Uses: lem-trf-cotangent-resolution-coherence, def-weil-abstract-derived-category.
