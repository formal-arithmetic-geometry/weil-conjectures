## Review by Q49

I checked B--E of delivery-errors.md against the node statements and these sources. There is no new statement-only node to audit, no Mathlib claim fabricated from a search, and no clause moved to a new node. All nine steps are below 2000 characters. I read the statement of every cited node and copied its full statement verbatim into citations.json so that each use can be checked independently of its proof.

Step 1 takes the equivalence with an affine neighbourhood and finite residue extension from `lem-sfb-finite-type-field-point-characterization`, applied with the canonical residue field and hence extension degree one. It does not conclude that u is closed in all of U. The identity and section equations give the residue isomorphisms, including equality of the two residue maps.

Step 2 takes the actual quotient isomorphism from `lem-sbc-scheme-fibre-stalk-quotient`, not just the existence of an unrelated residue-field isomorphism. `lem-weil-pbc-lft-base-change` makes the fibre locally finite type. `lem-kun-dimension-formula` requires finite type, so I apply it on a finite-type affine neighbourhood, not on an arbitrary non-quasi-compact fibre. The rationality established in step 1 kills precisely its transcendence-degree term.

Step 3 uses the groupoid definition for the operations after restriction, the representable-sheaf definition for passage to algebraic spaces, and the base-change node for local finite type. Composition is expanded by finite algebra-generator lists. The statements of the two field-groupoid nodes say respectively dim R = dim stabilizer and local topological dimension = global dimension. They do not say that the arrow scheme is the stabilizer. I verify the stabilizer of the restricted groupoid separately on test schemes; this is G_u. The identity charts identify their dimension conventions with the scheme dimensions here.

Step 4 uses `lem-kun-dimension-formula` only at the rational identity of H on a finite-type affine chart. `lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra` supplies the tensor-product presentation, which for the two residue quotients is D/(s(m)+t(m)); localizing yields the displayed local ring C/J. Neither separatedness of U over S nor Noetherianity of A or D is assumed or used.

Step 5 uses the finite-associated-primes statement on the finite self-module C, and the associated-prime depth bound with depth C = d2. If J were in an associated prime, its quotient would have dimension simultaneously at least d2 and at most d1; the latter comparison follows by lifting prime chains along a surjection. Prime avoidance is used in A on the inverse images of these primes, not on elements that might fail to lift from C. The zerodivisor-union statement then gives exactly the regularity of the image of t(f). This avoids adding nodes for the equidimensionality and minimal-prime facts from the source's slightly longer argument. The empty associated-prime case is explicitly excluded using the same zerodivisor statement and C nonzero.

Step 6 is the only elementary step: the one-equation algebra presentation and the residue quotient identify the immersion, local finite presentation and residue field directly. The existing finite-presentation composition statement gives the composite with the open immersion. There is no assertion that g is globally a closed immersion.

Step 7 uses the affine fibre-product statement for the particular chosen equation t(f). I checked that the existing fibre-regular slice statement has no Noetherian base hypothesis, assumes local finite presentation and flatness only at the point, and supplies a section with the prescribed germ, not an arbitrary unrelated regular equation. Its flat cut therefore has exactly stalk T/t(f)T, which is enough for flatness of our h at x. The global neighbourhood it constructs need not be the original affine W; the proof claims only pointwise flatness for h.

Step 8 again uses the canonical fibre-stalk quotient, followed by the existing CM nonzerodivisor quotient statement. That statement gives depth at least d2-1, not equality; I explicitly apply the separate depth-support bound to the nonzero quotient self-module to get the reverse inequality. Its support is all Spec because localization of a nonzero ring at a prime is nonzero. Thus all clauses of CM-at-x as defined in this item's statement are verified.

Step 9 associates the actual fibre products from the definition and uses the degree-one residue-field map from step 6; there is no assertion about a nontrivial extension preserving local ring dimensions. It applies the finite-type field dimension formula at the resulting rational point, giving the requested topological dimension. All non-elementary uses are cited statements, not arguments hidden in their proofs.

Small cases checked: a positive-dimensional group scheme over a field has d1=d2 and is excluded by the strict inequality; for the pair groupoid of A^n over k at a rational closed point, d1=0, d2=n and a coordinate hyperplane gives the claimed n-1; n=1 gives a zero-dimensional fibre with depth zero. Nonreduced CM fibres are allowed because regularity is obtained by associated-prime avoidance rather than reducedness. Non-Noetherian bases are allowed by the cited fibre-regular criterion.
