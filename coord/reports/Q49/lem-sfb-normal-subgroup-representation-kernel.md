Step 4 uses precisely clause (3) of 01ZM to descend the multiplication and identity equations after a common finite enlargement. These are equations of morphisms with finite-presentation sources G x G and Spec L; the target is GL_n. Preservation of inverse follows from preservation of multiplication and identity. Equality of closed kernels is reflected by the scalar extension L -> K: an ideal containment can be checked in the vector-space quotient, where v -> v tensor 1 is injective by a basis. I wrote that elementary argument rather than invoking an unquoted fpqc descent theorem. Thus the cited morphism-descent theorem is not falsely claimed to descend kernels on its own.

Step 5 is the explicit restriction-of-scalars matrix construction, using the GL definition's natural invertible-matrix points. Coefficients in L tensor k[G] are expanded in a finite k-basis of L; each is replaced by its multiplication matrix. The original inverse matrix supplies an inverse over k[G], so the matrix really defines a scheme morphism to GL_(nd). Naturality and the group law follow directly from the construction and those of alpha_L. This is a few-line coordinate argument, not an uncited Weil-restriction representability theorem.

Step 6 is elementary from step 4 and the same matrix construction: identity as an A-linear operator is equivalent to identity as an (L tensor A)-linear operator by evaluation on the standard generators. For membership descent, the explicit k-linear retraction t(1)=1 proves injectivity of A -> L tensor A for every A, including nonreduced A and the zero algebra. Evaluating each defining equation of the closed subgroup therefore gives the exact kernel equality; naturality is inherited from the displayed formulas.
