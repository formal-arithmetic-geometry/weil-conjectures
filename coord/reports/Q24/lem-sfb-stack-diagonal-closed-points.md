Hypothesis and conclusion audit:

- A field k is `(k : Type u) [Field k]`, using Mathlib's `Field` rather than a
  private field structure. The base scheme is `Spec (CommRingCat.of k)`.
- The category over schemes over k is
  `F : BasedCategory.{v, w} (Over (Spec (CommRingCat.of k)))`.
  `hF : IsAlgebraicModuliStack F` gives exactly etale stack descent,
  algebraic-space diagonal and a smooth surjective scheme atlas. Neither smoothness
  over k nor the Deligne--Mumford property is assumed.
- Finite type over k is `hft`: there exists a smooth surjective scheme atlas
  `a : h_U → F` with `LocallyOfFiniteType U.hom ∧ QuasiCompact U.hom`.
  These are the recorded scheme finite-type notions, applied to the structure map
  of the atlas. Over a field this is the usual finite-type algebraic-stack condition.
  In particular `Smooth U.hom` is not a hypothesis.
- The quasi-finite separated diagonal is `hΔ : HasSchemeStackDiagonalWith ... F`.
  Each pair of scheme charts has its actual two-fibre product represented by R,
  and the map R to their product satisfies `LocallyOfFiniteType f`,
  `QuasiCompact f`, finite geometric fibres
  `∀ (Ω : Type u) [Field Ω] [IsAlgClosed Ω] (y : Spec (.of Ω) ⟶ V),
  Finite ↥(pullback f y)`, and closed relative diagonal
  `IsClosedImmersion (pullback.diagonal f)`.
  This is the same interface used by the existing consumer
  `CategoryTheory.adequate_quasifinite_diagonal_finite`. Scheme representability
  adds no mathematical condition: a separated locally quasi-finite algebraic
  space over a scheme is a scheme (Stacks Tag 03XX; the local TeX statement was read).
- The point type is the existing `ModuliPoints F`: the quotient of actual field
  objects by the equivalence relation generated by arrows, hence by isomorphisms
  after common field extension. No set of atlas points replaces it.
- The topology is the global `moduliPointTopologicalSpace` instance from
  `StackPointTopologyFlatLfpOpen`: the supremum of coinduced topologies of
  residue-field point maps for all smooth surjective scheme atlases. In an
  algebraic stack it is the quotient topology from any smooth atlas. Thus
  `IsClosed ({x} : Set (ModuliPoints F))` means exactly that x is closed.
- The product is `moduliTwoFiberProduct (moduliBaseStructure F)
  (moduliBaseStructure F)`, the actual 2-fibre product over the base scheme
  represented by the identity category of schemes over it. It is not the
  Cartesian product of the underlying point sets.
- Its diagonal is the existing `moduliRelativeDiagonal (moduliBaseStructure F)`,
  whose object map is `(x,x,id)` and whose arrow map is `(a,a)`.
  `moduliPointMap` is the functor's actual induced map on field-object classes.
- The conclusion quantifies over every `x : ModuliPoints F` and says that
  its closed singleton implies the closed singleton of this canonical diagonal
  image in the product point space. No closed-map assertion is substituted.
- There is no assumption or supplied datum of an adequate moduli space.

Edge cases checked before writing:

- The empty stack has no point x, so the conclusion is vacuous. The atlas condition
  does not require an inhabited chart, and finite geometric fibres may be empty.
- k need not be algebraically closed, perfect, or separably closed. Only geometric
  fibre test fields Ω are algebraically closed. There is no characteristic condition.
- Purely inseparable K/k is allowed in the proof: K tensor K may be nonreduced,
  but the multiplication map onto K still has maximal kernel and closed singleton image.
- Finite fibres mean finitely many underlying points, not reduced fibres or a bound
  on their cardinalities. No etale, smooth, finite inertia or reducedness condition
  is added to F.
- Object and morphism universes v and w are explicitly bound independently; scheme
  test fields have universe u, the same as the scheme base. There are no auto-implicit
  variables, universe equalities, file-local notation, private definitions or local instances.
