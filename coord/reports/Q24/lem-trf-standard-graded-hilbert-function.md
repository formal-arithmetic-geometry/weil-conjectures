## Review by Q24

I checked the eight steps against the actual statements of all three cited nodes and against Stacks, rather than relying on titles or their proofs. In step 1, def-pc-standard-graded-algebra supplies a finite list of degree-one generators and a direct-sum grading. Taking all homogeneous components of module generators still generates M: their span contains the original generators. Projection to degree n produces the finite monomial spanning family. This is the argument of 00K0, written out, and not a new lemma.

In step 2, lem-wf-affine-noetherian-components explicitly says a finitely generated algebra over a field is Noetherian; no topology clause is used. Its field and finite-type hypotheses hold from step 1. The statement of lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-submodule-finite explicitly gives finite generation of every submodule of a finite module over a Noetherian ring. This supplies exactly the later torsion-submodule finiteness, rather than merely the definition of Noetherianity. The induction is simultaneous for all standard graded algebras with a list of at most t degree-one generators and all their finite nonnegative graded modules. The zero-generator base case is eventually zero.

Step 3 uses only the standard graded definition: the homogeneous ideal (x) has zero degree-zero part and quotient generated by the other t-1 generators. Thus the outer induction is legitimately applicable and the field k survives the quotient. In step 4 I checked both the inner induction on the killing exponent and the graded exact sequence: xL is generated by the x-multiples of generators, and homogeneous representatives lift each degree of the quotient. A kernel basis together with lifts of a quotient basis proves the dimension formula directly, so no uncited dimension-additivity theorem is hidden. This proves the nilpotent case in the item, rather than relocating it as a new node.

In step 5 the definition T consists of all x-power torsion elements, not a noncanonical chosen submodule. It is closed under addition and scalar multiplication; homogeneity follows by separating the degrees after multiplying by x^r. The finite-submodule node supplies generators of T, and the maximum of their killing exponents annihilates all of T. This makes the invocation of step 4 justified. In step 6, xm in T implies m in T, proving injectivity on the quotient without assuming x regular on the original module. The maps have degrees 1,0 in the sequence N_n → N_(n+1) → C_(n+1). The finite quotient C is killed by x, so it is covered by the outer induction. The degreewise exactness and dimension identity use the previously written homogeneous lifting and basis argument.

Step 7 is the explicit argument in 00JZ over Q: triangularity gives the binomial polynomial basis; Pascal's identity integrates a backward difference; an eventually zero difference is eventually constant by induction. This argument needs no integer-valued-polynomial assertion and no characteristic restriction on k, because the dimensions and polynomials live in Q. Step 8 uses the degreewise torsion exact sequence and adds the two eventual polynomials. It sets one threshold for both, retains the zero polynomial, and closes the stated induction.

I tested small cases: S=k and a finite graded module supported in finitely many degrees (eventual zero); M=0; S=k[x] with M=S (constant eventual dimension 1); S=k[x]/(x^r), M=S (nilpotent case, eventual zero); S=k[x,y], M=S (dimension n+1); and M=S/(x) over that ring (dimension 1). These check the nilpotent and injective branches, shifts n versus n+1, and that positivity or nonzero-polynomial hypotheses must not be added.
