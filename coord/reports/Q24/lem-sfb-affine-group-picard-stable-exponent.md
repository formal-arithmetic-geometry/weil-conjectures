Step 1 cites lem-sfb-picard-trivial-multiplicative-cover. I opened its full statement: it supplies the smooth connected affine P, the multiplicative isogeny, Pic(P)=0, and explicitly describes a finite diagonalizable kernel as D(M) with its Hopf algebra. Our G has exactly its hypotheses, with F=k. No splitting, quotient construction, or smoothness of the kernel is assumed independently.

Step 2 cites that same statement for the Hopf algebra description, and is elementary. For every m in the finite M, the q+1 multiples give a positive r_m killing m; their finite product is positive and kills all m. On the basis e^m, the n-power morphism is e^m mapped to e^(nm)=1, so it equals the neutral-point morphism. Composing any character with this morphism gives its n-th power, hence every character is n-torsion. This proves the needed assertion directly; it does not cite an unprovided diagonalizable duality or character-finiteness theorem. I retained the scheme-theoretic calculation to handle mu_p in characteristic p, where an argument using only k-valued points would fail.

Step 3 cites lem-sfb-character-picard-isogeny-sequence. I opened its statement: its connecting map is a group homomorphism, its last terms are X*(A) to Pic(G) to Pic(P) to zero, and its definition of multiplicative isogeny agrees with the cover node. Thus Pic(P)=0 gives surjectivity of delta and n delta(chi)=delta(n chi)=0. Both source and target are smooth connected affine finite-type groups over the same algebraically closed k; the kernel need not be smooth. This is exactly the part of the cited statement used, not an argument borrowed from its proof.

Step 4 cites only the new field-invariance statement. Its hypotheses are those of the item, and K is arbitrary. Surjectivity lifts [N] to a Pic(G) class, and the asserted group-homomorphism property gives n[N]=p_K^*(na)=0. Equality with the zero class is precisely the claimed tensor-power trivialization. There is no need to choose K before n, or to compare different choices of isomorphisms. The final sentence follows from the order of quantifiers and the Picard group law.

Small cases checked: G=1 allows P=1 and M=0, where the unique r_0 can be 1 and the construction gives n=1. The additive group and split tori have trivial Picard groups and are compatible with the conclusion. The new invariance statement allows K=k, transcendental extensions and imperfect K in positive characteristic. A nontrivial algebraic purely inseparable extension of an algebraically closed k does not exist, but inseparable transcendental phenomena are not excluded.
