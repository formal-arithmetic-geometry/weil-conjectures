I checked the pruning input by the following independent argument, which its later worker can formalize. Process the given finite tower while maintaining an isomorphism of open neighbourhoods of the singular loci of the current original and retained surfaces. Initially take the identity. If the next center is singular, it lies in these opens; blow up the corresponding point on the retained surface and restrict the comparison to the blowups of these opens. Outside them the schemes remain regular and unchanged. Normality follows from the common normal blowup open and the unchanged normal complement. If the center is regular, omit that stage in the retained tower and shrink both comparison opens by removing the center and its inverse image. Blowing up a regular two-dimensional point is regular, so the resulting opens still contain all singular points. If a later original center lies on a discarded regular branch, it is regular and is likewise omitted. A corresponding retained singular center is closed: the original point maps to a closed point s of S by properness and has residue field finite over k(s); the point with that same residue field in the finite-type retained fibre is closed. Local dimension and images in S are preserved by the open isomorphism. This induction produces the asserted final open isomorphism and at most N retained centers. It does not construct a morphism from the retained endpoint to the original endpoint, and none is required.

Small cases: when R is regular the empty tower is allowed, and when it is already Gorenstein no new blowup is forced. A unit center gives an identity principalizing blowup; zero-generator charts are empty. The canonical module cannot have rank zero since the base is a nonzero domain. The rank-one argument would fail for a torsion target, so torsion-freeness is explicitly supplied. At a regular point blowup, the canonical map need not be surjective along the exceptional divisor: this is why Steps 7–8 first prove that all ancestors of a singular endpoint are singular. For pruning, an entirely regular tower prunes to the empty tower (the singular-neighbourhood opens may be empty); in mixed towers, blowups over deleted regular branches cannot create a singularity. These tests justify the exact hypotheses, including length zero.
