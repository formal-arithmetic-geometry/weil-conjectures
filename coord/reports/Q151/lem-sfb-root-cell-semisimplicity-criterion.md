For step 1, the argument is elementary from the hypotheses: conjugation produces x_alpha(z) as an actual commutator, and u=1,v=s-1 in the Gauss relation kills the coroot image in any commutative quotient. An integer right inverse exists because the target cocharacter lattice is free, so integral generation of the coroots, rather than just rational spanning, kills the full torus. This checks the precise hypothesis needed before Chevalley is used.

For step 2, I opened `lem-sfb-barsotti-chevalley-decomposition`: it gives a smooth connected affine kernel, an abelian variety quotient and its scheme-theoretic kernel identification for a smooth connected separated finite-type group, without assuming the original group affine. Milne Theorem 8.27 and Note 8.30, pp.154-155, are the local source. `sbc-lem-smooth-geometrically-reduced` states exactly that smooth fibres are geometrically reduced, so over Spec(k) it makes H reduced. The dense-equalizer ideal argument is written out; there is no unjustified passage from pointwise equality on a dense set to scheme equality on a nonreduced source.

For step 3, I opened `lem-sfb-algebraic-group-homomorphism-factorization` and checked its kernel, closed-image and fppf quotient clauses against Milne Theorem 5.39, p.108. An immersion is a monomorphism, so its kernel is trivial and the quotient by that kernel is the source. Thus every subgroup used afterwards really is closed. `lem-sc-product-tangent-space-direct-sum` states the tangent isomorphism for arbitrary k-schemes at k-rational points, exactly the identities in the chart; its source is Stacks Tag 0BEB, varieties-lemma-tangent-space-product. The differential-of-multiplication and full-character calculations are explicit in the step. No differential of a character is substituted for a torus weight.

For step 4, I read the conclusions of `lem-sfb-torus-centralizer-smooth-connected`, `lem-sfb-lie-normalizer-centralizer-invariants`, and `lem-sfb-smooth-subgroup-tangent-generation`. They supply, respectively, a smooth connected centralizer, its full-group invariant tangent subspace, and generation by smooth subgroup tangent spaces. Their local source statements are Milne Corollary 13.10 (p.257), Theorem 17.38 (p.363), Proposition 10.34 (pp.197-198), and Corollary 10.17 (p.191). For step 5, I read Milne Proposition 16.64 and its uniqueness paragraph (pp.348-349) beside the full statement of `lem-sfb-torus-positive-ray-subgroup`.
