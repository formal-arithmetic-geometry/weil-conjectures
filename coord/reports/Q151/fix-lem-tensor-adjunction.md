### lem-sf-tensor-hom-signed-transposes

Returned step 8 (1623 characters):

The shift compatibility is checked on the same Hom bijection. Under the canonical shift identifications, for any integer $r$,
$$\operatorname{Hom}(L,A_{M,N[r]})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(L[-r],A_{M,N}),\qquad
\operatorname{Hom}(L,B_{M,N[r]})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(L[-r],B_{M,N}).$$
The five arrows for $(L,N[r])$ identify with those for $(L[-r],N)$: a tensor factor shifted by $r$ shifts the tensor by $r$, and each right adjoint's shift comparison is the transpose of the corresponding comparison for its left adjoint. The tensor–Hom comparisons have the signs of the shifted Hom complex; shifting its first variable gives $H(M[r],Q)=H(M,Q)[-r]$, while shifting its second gives $H(M,Q[r])=H(M,Q)[r]$. Both transposition routes therefore use the same signed evaluation map. In the projection definition, the proper projection map is the transpose of $(\varepsilon_p\otimes1)\circ\mu_p$, and the compact-support map is its composite with $Rp_*(\gamma)$, where $\gamma$ is the extension-by-zero tensor comparison followed by pullback composition. Shifting either tensor factor shifts each of these composites by the total-tensor convention, using $d_{C[r]}=(-1)^rd_C$; the evaluation comparison uses that same convention. The shifted composite is therefore the specified shifted projection transformation. Thus $h$ for the shifted arguments is the shifted $h$. By uniqueness in step 6 this proves the shift square for the exchange, in the second variable and, with the negative shift, in the first. Completion commutes with these identifications since its Koszul-limit construction is exact and the completion unit is natural.
