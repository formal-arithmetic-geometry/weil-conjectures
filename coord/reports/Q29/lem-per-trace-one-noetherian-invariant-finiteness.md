Step 1 cites def-weil-finitely-generated-module only for finite generation. The ring multiplication, unit and associativity are checked on basis terms, with the associativity identity displayed, and extended by distributivity. RIGHT R-finiteness follows from r[g]=[g]g^{-1}(r), not the left coefficient expression; using left coefficients to justify a right-module claim would be a sidedness error. Commutativity of R explicitly converts this right R-module into an ordinary left R-module, with scalar r acting by right multiplication, for the later commutative module theorem. This is a direct construction and coefficient calculation, not an uncited skew-group structural theorem.

Step 2 has no citations: it is the elementary finite-sum reindexing [h]e=e, the calculation e^2=e, and the injectivity test pi(re)=r using trace(a)=1. It proves Se=Re explicitly. No nonzero-divisor property of a or any coefficient is used. In particular pi is only an additive map; no false claim that coefficient-sum is a ring homomorphism or a right S-map occurs.

Step 3 has no citations: it is a direct ring/module identification. An element ese lies in Se=Re. Multiplication by [h] fixes it, so the proved injection forces its coefficient r to be invariant. Conversely invariant b commutes with the basis [g] and hence with e, which verifies be=ebe and multiplicativity. This checks both directions of C=Ae and the identity e of C. The module identification is verified by (re)(be)=rb e. No general Morita theorem or corner-finiteness theorem is being used.

Step 4 cites lem-trf-noetherian-submodule-finitely-presented, whose statement actually says that the submodule is finite, and nothing is taken from its proof. The ambient S is finite over the COMMUTATIVE R by Step 1; I=SeS is closed under right R multiplication since it is a right ideal. These are precisely all of the cited theorem's hypotheses. After obtaining finite R-generators y_j, the rest is two inclusions: expand their defining finite sums n s, collect the finitely many n in Se, and observe that their right S-span both lies in I and contains all y_j and all their R-combinations. This is a finite-support extraction, not a hidden Noetherian-corner lemma. Only this step is non-elementary, exactly because it invokes Stacks 00FP through the existing Mathlib node.

Step 5 cites def-weil-finitely-generated-module for the final interpretation. It verifies Se=Ie, inserts e next to every n_i because n_i=n_i e, and then uses the already proved C=Ae. This yields r=sum r_i b_i by injectivity, and supplies an actual finite generating list for R over A. There is no identification simply described as transport: the equality and coefficients are displayed. Its elementary status is relative to the established calculations of Steps 2--4.

Step 6 cites def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring for finite generation of JR and for the conclusion about A; its statement literally defines Noetherian as every ideal finitely generated. The sum rho(r)=sum g(ar) is invariant by reindexing and is A-linear; trace(a)=1 gives rho(b)=b. Finite generators of JR need not themselves belong to J, so each is expanded in J first and the finite union is collected. The displayed application of rho then proves that these actual j_i in J generate J over A. This is the definition-level ideal argument, with no unmentioned Noetherian descent theorem.
