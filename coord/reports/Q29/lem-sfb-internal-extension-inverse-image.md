## Retained returned proof (verbatim argument record)

1. Fix an $R$-module $M$ with action $\gamma_M$. By [sk-def-sfb-change-of-scalars](2), the underlying object $P_M$ of $\operatorname{Ext}_{\varphi}M$ has a coequalizer map $q_M:S\otimes M\to P_M$ for $a=(\mu_S\circ(1\otimes\varphi))\otimes1$ and $b=(1\otimes\gamma_M)\circ\alpha$, from $(S\otimes R)\otimes M$ to $S\otimes M$. Its $S$-action is induced by multiplication on the first factor. Use [lem-sfb-finite-upper-star-braided] to obtain commutative $F(R),F(S)$. Transport the multiplication, $\varphi$, and the module action by [def-sfb-modobj-transport]. Thus, writing $\mu^F$ for the tensorator, $\mu_{FS}=F(\mu_S)\circ\mu^F_{S,S}$, $\varphi'=F\varphi$, and $\gamma_{FM}=F(\gamma_M)\circ\mu^F_{R,M}$. Let $q'_M:FS\otimes FM\to Q_M$ be the balancing coequalizer for these data, with arrows $a',b'$ and its induced $FS$-action. This is precisely $\operatorname{Ext}_{F\varphi}(F_RM)$, with the actual rings $FR,FS$.

2. The map $F(q_M)$ is a coequalizer of $F(a),F(b)$. Here is the universal-property argument, rather than an additional preservation assertion. Put $G=g_*$ and use [def-sfb-finite-upper-lower-star-adjunction]. For every abelian sheaf $T$ on $Z$, its natural adjunction bijections identify
\[
\Hom(FP_M,T)\cong\Hom(P_M,GT)\cong\{h:S\otimes M\to GT\mid h a=h b\}\cong\{k:F(S\otimes M)\to T\mid kF(a)=kF(b)\}.
\]
The middle bijection is the coequalizer property supplied by [sk-def-sfb-change-of-scalars]. Naturality of the adjunction says that the resulting bijection is precomposition with $F(q_M)$, as required. This only uses a finite parallel-pair diagram.

3. Set $t_0=\mu^F_{S,M}$ and $t_1=\mu^F_{S\otimes R,M}\circ(\mu^F_{S,R}\otimes1)$. These are isomorphisms by [def-sfb-finite-upper-star-monoidal]. Naturality and the associativity identity in [def-sfb-strong-monoidal-functor], with the transported actions of [def-sfb-modobj-transport], give
\[
F(a)t_1=t_0a',\qquad F(b)t_1=t_0b'.
\]
Indeed the first equality replaces $\mu_{FS}(1\otimes F\varphi)$ by $F(\mu_S(1\otimes\varphi))\mu^F_{S,R}$ and then uses naturality in the first tensor factor; the second uses the associativity identity to move $t_1$ past $F\alpha$ and replaces $\gamma_{FM}$ by $F\gamma_M\,\mu^F_{R,M}$. Thus $\widehat q_M=F(q_M)t_0$ and $q'_M$ are coequalizers of the same pair. Their universal properties give unique maps $c_M:Q_M\to FP_M$ and $d_M:FP_M\to Q_M$ with $c_Mq'_M=\widehat q_M$ and $d_M\widehat q_M=q'_M$. Uniqueness gives $d_Mc_M=1$ and $c_Md_M=1$.

4. These underlying isomorphisms are $FS$-linear. Write $\delta$ for the action on $FP_M$ from [def-sfb-modobj-transport] and $\delta'$ for the action on $Q_M$ from [sk-def-sfb-change-of-scalars]. Both quotient maps respect multiplication on the first $FS$ factor: for $q'_M$ this is its defining induced action, and for $\widehat q_M$ it follows by applying $F$ to the defining action equation for $q_M$ and using the tensorator's naturality and associativity identity [def-sfb-strong-monoidal-functor]. Consequently, after precomposition with $1_{FS}\otimes q'_M$, both $c_M\delta'$ and $\delta(1_{FS}\otimes c_M)$ equal $\widehat q_M(\mu_{FS}\otimes1)$, with the same associator. By [sk-def-sfb-sheaf-tensor](2), $FS\otimes-$ preserves this coequalizer, so $1\otimes q'_M$ is a coequalizer map. Such a map is epic: two maps equal after it are the unique factorization of the same map. Cancel it to obtain $c_M\delta'=\delta(1\otimes c_M)$. Multiplying this equation by $d_M$ and $1\otimes d_M$ shows $d_M$ is linear as well.

5. For an $R$-linear map $u:M\to N$, the two functors send it to the maps induced by $1_S\otimes u$ and $1_{FS}\otimes F(u)$ on the respective coequalizers, by [sk-def-sfb-change-of-scalars] and [def-sfb-modobj-transport]. Precomposing the desired equality $F(\operatorname{Ext}_{\varphi}u)c_M=c_N\operatorname{Ext}_{F\varphi}(F_Ru)$ with $q'_M$ gives, on both sides, $F(q_N)\mu^F_{S,N}(1\otimes Fu)$: this uses $\operatorname{Ext}_{\varphi}(u)q_M=q_N(1\otimes u)$ and tensorator naturality [def-sfb-strong-monoidal-functor]. Cancel the coequalizer map $q'_M$. Hence the $c_M$ form a natural isomorphism $\operatorname{Ext}_{F\varphi}\circ F_R\cong F_S\circ\operatorname{Ext}_{\varphi}$. Its inverse is the displayed isomorphism in the statement, including the formula on objects.
