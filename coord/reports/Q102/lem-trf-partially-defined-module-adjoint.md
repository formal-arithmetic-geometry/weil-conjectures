## Retained arguments for the two new work packages

These are proof material for their future workers, not proof steps or proof dependencies of the new statement-only node files. Every original argument remains available here.

### Old step 1, assigned to lem-trf-partial-adjoint-generator-presentation

For each $P\in\mathcal P$ choose the stipulated object $L_P$ and natural bijection $\theta_{P,A}$. Apply Yoneda evaluation [lem-galois-yoneda-hom-bijection] to $\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}$: the covariant functor $A\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal B}(P,R(A))$ is a presheaf on $\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}$, and $A\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(L_P,A)$ is representable there. Put $i_P=\theta_{P,L_P}(\mathrm{id}_{L_P})$. The resulting evaluation formula is $\theta_{P,A}(a)=R(a)\circ i_P$. Additivity [def-weil-pbc-additive-functor] gives $R(0)=0$, so $\theta_{P,A}(0)=0$.

### Old step 2, assigned to lem-trf-partial-adjoint-generator-presentation

Fix $B\in\mathcal B$. Choose an epimorphism $q:P_1\twoheadrightarrow B$ with $P_1\in\mathcal P$. A kernel $k:K\to P_1$ exists by [def-sp-abelian]. Apply the generating hypothesis again, now to $K$, to obtain an epimorphism $r:P_2\twoheadrightarrow K$ with $P_2\in\mathcal P$, and put $d=k\circ r$. Thus $q\circ d=0$. This uses no lifting property of $P_1$ or $P_2$.

### Old step 3, assigned to lem-trf-partial-adjoint-generator-presentation

The morphism $q$ is a cokernel of $d$. Indeed, [def-sp-abelian] says that the epimorphism $q$ is a cokernel of some map $t:T\to P_1$. Since $q\circ t=0$, the kernel property gives $t=k\circ s$ for a unique $s:T\to K$. For any $h:P_1\to Y$, the equality $h\circ d=h\circ k\circ r=0$ implies $h\circ k=0$ by cancellation of the epimorphism $r$, hence $h\circ t=0$; the cokernel property of $q$ gives a unique $b:B\to Y$ with $b\circ q=h$. Conversely such an $h$ kills $d$. This proves exactly the required cokernel universal property.

### Old step 4, assigned to lem-trf-partial-adjoint-generator-presentation

Define $D:L_{P_2}\to L_{P_1}$ as the unique map with $\theta_{P_2,L_{P_1}}(D)=i_{P_1}\circ d$. For every $a:L_{P_1}\to A$, naturality of the stipulated bijections gives $\theta_{P_2,A}(a\circ D)=R(a)\circ i_{P_1}\circ d=\theta_{P_1,A}(a)\circ d$. Since step 1 proves that zero corresponds to zero and each $\theta$ is injective, $a\circ D=0$ if and only if $\theta_{P_1,A}(a)\circ d=0$. This is a direct calculation from the given naturality, not an assumption that the generating objects are projective.

### Old step 5, assigned to lem-trf-cokernel-corepresenting-bijection

Take a cokernel $c:L_{P_1}\to C_B$ of $D$, which exists by [def-sp-abelian]. By its defining universal property, $v\mapsto v\circ c$ bijects $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(C_B,A)$ with the maps $a:L_{P_1}\to A$ killing $D$. Step 4 identifies these with the maps $h:P_1\to R(A)$ killing $d$. Step 3 bijects the latter with $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal B}(B,R(A))$, by $b\mapsto b\circ q$. Thus there is a bijection $\psi_{B,A}:\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(C_B,A)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal B}(B,R(A))$ characterized by $\psi_{B,A}(v)\circ q=\theta_{P_1,A}(v\circ c)$. Each inverse is the unique factorization just described; no exactness theorem for $R$ is used.

### Old step 6, assigned to lem-trf-cokernel-corepresenting-bijection

These bijections are natural in $A$. If $u:A\to A\prime$ and $v:C_B\to A$, the defining equation and the original naturality give $\psi_{B,A\prime}(u\circ v)\circ q=\theta_{P_1,A\prime}(u\circ v\circ c)=R(u)\circ\theta_{P_1,A}(v\circ c)=R(u)\circ\psi_{B,A}(v)\circ q$. Cancelling the epimorphism $q$ yields $\psi_{B,A\prime}(u\circ v)=R(u)\circ\psi_{B,A}(v)$. Hence for every $B$ the functor $A\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal B}(B,R(A))$ is corepresented by the constructed cokernel $C_B$.
