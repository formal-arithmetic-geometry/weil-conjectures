The displayed pure-tensor computation identifies its extension with ad_H(1 tensor x) ad_H(1 tensor y); linearity then makes B_H zero on all pairs. This is an explicit evaluation, with no unproved comparison of Killing forms under base change. def-wf-lie-algebra gives the adjoint representation and bracket-span definitions; lifting each factor of a bracket and its linear coefficient proves [A,A]=rho([H,H]). The trace-criterion statement gives exactly solvability under the trace-zero hypothesis established here. It is applied only over E. Step 3 uses def-wf-lie-algebra for the derived sequence, and writes the abelian-kernel argument on H: rho(D^r H) lies in D^r A by induction, so D^n H lies in ker rho=Z(H) and its next derived term is zero. The new scalar-extension leaf then supplies its exact third clause, solvability of H iff solvability of K. Only then does def-wf-lie-radical-centre place the solvable ideal K of the original l in its zero radical. This step is now marked non-elementary because descent is cited. Step 4 uses exactly the identity leaf's invariance and symmetry, nondegeneracy already proved in step 3, and the centre membership formula of def-wf-lie-radical-centre. Its two inclusions are written: orthogonality to [x,y] forces [y,z]=0 by nondegeneracy, and centrality forces orthogonality to each bracket and hence its span. The resulting equality is derived, not assumed from a definition node.

Step 5 uses def-wf-lie-algebra to see that an abelian ideal is solvable with first derived term zero, and def-wf-lie-radical-centre to put the centre in the radical. Jacobi gives ideal closure of the centre. It then invokes the orthogonal leaf only after symmetry, nondegeneracy and finite dimension have all been established. Orthogonality to the zero subspace is the whole space by bilinearity. This supplies the final conclusion without an uncited dimension formula.
