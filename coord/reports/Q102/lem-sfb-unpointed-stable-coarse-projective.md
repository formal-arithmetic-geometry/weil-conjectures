## Argument retained for the chart-density task

The existing framed smoothing node carries the former curve-specific lci/singular-finiteness/smoothing/stable-openness work; it is reused, not copied. Once a geometric point v in an etale chart V is given, its represented stable curve receives a frame and hence that smoothing. For the new general lemma, form the algebraic-space 2-fibre product W=Spec Omega[[t]] times_F V. Its etale projection to the trait has the closed point specified by v and the given special-fibre isomorphism. Choose an etale scheme chart of W and an Omega-point above that closed point; such a point exists because the residue field is algebraically closed and an etale fibre is a disjoint union of spectra of finite separable extensions. The composite scheme-to-trait map is etale. The power-series ring is complete local with separably closed residue field and hence strictly henselian. The etale neighbourhood section argument supplies a section through the selected closed point (choose the corresponding local component, so the specified lift is retained). Composing back to W and then to V gives the required trait map and its 2-isomorphism. Its generic point belongs to V times_F U, so v lies in its closure. Every topological point of V has a geometric representative, and the underlying map of the trait is continuous; therefore the closure contains every point of V. This proves density without assuming a topology on a stack is already developed. The proof task includes the passage from the etale algebraic space to a scheme chart and the compatibility of the special 2-isomorphism; it is not just Dense.preimage.

## Argument retained for the coarse-descent task

For an object presented by (E,u) over T, choose a Zariski trivializing cover T_i and local sections s_i:T_i to E. Given an invariant f:J to B, define b_i=f u s_i. On an overlap, s_i and s_j differ by a unique morphism to G, so invariance makes b_i=b_j. Since B is a sheaf, these maps glue uniquely to T to B. Refining the cover or changing sections leaves the glued map unchanged. A pullback object uses the pulled-back sections, so the maps commute with base change; an equivariant torsor isomorphism also leaves them unchanged. They therefore define a functor from the presented fibred groupoid to B. Conversely restriction of a functor to trivial torsors gives a morphism J to B. The isomorphism between the trivial torsors associated to a point and its G-translate makes it invariant. Applying local trivializations again shows these two operations are inverse. Discrete targets have only identity natural isomorphisms, so the correspondence has the precise uniqueness needed for maps in the current coarse definition. Use f=q to construct pi, and compose the correspondence with the assumed categorical bijection for q to prove universality. Over an algebraically closed Omega the Zariski cover of Spec Omega has a global nonempty member, so every torsor in the stated presentation is trivial. A choice of section identifies a presented object with a point of J(Omega); changing that choice changes it by G(Omega), and equivariant isomorphisms give exactly that orbit relation. Thus geometric isomorphism classes are the orbit set, and the assumed q-bijection yields the desired bijection to Q(Omega). The equivalence e transports this construction to F because maps into discrete groupoids are invariant under the equivalence's natural isomorphisms. This is the entire old descent argument, isolated from construction of the quotient.

### Old step 1

Let $\mathfrak C_k$ and $\mathfrak N_k$ be the algebraic stacks of all curve families and of nodal curve families in the cited nodal-stack theorem. The stable-open-locus statement identifies the given groupoid $\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,0}$ with its fixed-genus stable open substack. This comparison includes the scheme and projective-bundle condition in the question, so it does not silently enlarge the groupoid to all algebraic-space families. It lies in $\mathfrak N_k$. Since the latter is smooth over $k$, restriction of its smooth atlas to this open is a smooth atlas with smooth composite to $\operatorname{Spec}k$. Thus the given moduli groupoid is an algebraic stack smooth over $k$.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-nodal-curve-stack-smooth, lem-sfb-stable-and-smooth-family-open-loci.

### Old step 2

For a geometric point represented by $C/\Omega$, apply the existing marked-nodal infinitesimal-fields theorem with no marked points. On every normalized component, the stability condition gives $2g_a-2+\#E_a>0$: at least three node branches for genus zero, at least one for genus one, and positivity automatically for genus at least two. Its conclusion says that every automorphism over the dual numbers reducing to the identity is the identity. The curve-stack DM-locus criterion therefore puts every geometric point of our stable open in $\mathfrak C_k^{\mathrm{DM}}$. The last clause of that criterion makes the entire open a Deligne--Mumford stack. This argument does not identify a finite automorphism group with a trivial one and does not require its order to be prime to the characteristic.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-marked-nodal-infinitesimal-fields, lem-sfb-curve-stack-infinitesimal-dm-locus, def-sfb-stable-pointed-curve.

### Old step 3

The smooth-family-locus theorem gives the open substack $\mathcal M_{g,0}$. To prove density in this particular field fibre, take any geometric stable curve $C/\Omega$. The nodal-lci criterion gives that $C$ is a local complete intersection. Its nonsmooth points are finite: the nodal-family singular-locus theorem, applied to the flat finitely presented $C\to\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$ (equidimensional of dimension one, with only ordinary double points), identifies the nonsmooth locus with the underlying set of $\operatorname{Sing}(C/\Omega)$ and makes it unramified over the field $\Omega$, hence discrete; being closed in the proper, hence quasi-compact, $C$, it is a finite set of closed points. The isolated-lci smoothing theorem now supplies a flat finitely presented projective family $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}\Omega[[t]]$ with special fibre $C$ and smooth generic fibre. Pull back the stable genus-$g$ open locus for this family. It contains the closed point, hence is the whole spectrum of this local discrete valuation ring, since an open containing its closed point contains every generization. Thus the family defines a trait in $\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,0}$ whose generic point lies in $\mathcal M_{g,0}$ and specializes to $[C]$. Every point of the stable stack has a geometric representative, so every point lies in the closure of the smooth open.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-stable-and-smooth-family-open-loci, lem-trf-nodal-curve-lci-criterion, lem-trf-nodal-family-fitting-singular, lem-sfb-isolated-lci-curve-smoothing.

### Old step 4

Put $r=19(g-1)$, so $r\geq19$, and $P(m)=20(g-1)m+1-g$. The ten-canonical parameter theorem gives the projective Hilbert scheme $Z$ and its locally closed stable locus $J$, with the $G=\operatorname{PGL}_{r,k}$-action. For an arbitrary family the projective-frame torsor gives a presentation by a $G$-equivariant map to $J$, and the theorem identifies its descent arrows with precisely the isomorphisms of unmarked curves. Consequently its stated equivalence $[J/G]\simeq\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,0}$ preserves both objects and automorphisms over every $k$-scheme. The projective-frame formulation allows line bundles pulled back from the base, so it imposes no spurious global trivialization of a dualizing power.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-ten-canonical-hilbert-parameter.

### Old step 5

Choose $m$ sufficiently large in the ten-canonical Gieseker theorem and the positive power $A$ of its Hilbert-Pluecker line carrying the $G$-linearization. The two conclusions needed for the quotient are exactly $J\subset Z^s(A)$ and the closed immersion $J\hookrightarrow Z^{ss}(A)$. Thus this is a closed invariant locus consisting entirely of stable points. Projectivity of $Z$ by itself would not give projectivity of the quotient of a locally closed locus; the semistable closedness is essential here. The cited stability theorem applies over our given field, including positive characteristic, and the passage to a positive line power avoids imposing invertibility of $r$.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-gieseker-ten-canonical-hilbert-stability.

### Old step 6

Apply the projective PGL stable-quotient theorem to $(Z,A,J,G)$. It gives a projective finite-type $k$-scheme $Q_g$ and a geometric categorical quotient $q:J\to Q_g$, universal for invariant maps to every algebraic space. The finite-projective-space convention is included in that theorem, via a sufficiently divisible Veronese of the invariant section algebra. This proves projectivity in precisely [def-kun-projective], not merely properness or an algebraic-space quotient.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-projective-pgl-stable-quotient, def-kun-projective.

### Old step 7

Apply the current coarse-moduli definition with $S=\operatorname{Spec}k$ and $r=0$. Using the equivalence in step 4, the invariant map $q$ defines a natural map from the moduli groupoid to $Q_g$. For an algebraic space $B$, a map of groupoids $[J/G]\to B$ restricts to a $G$-invariant map $J\to B$, and conversely such a map descends on each torsor presentation because $B$ is a sheaf. The categorical property of $q$ identifies these maps with maps $Q_g\to B$, giving the required initial property. Over an algebraically closed field every projective-frame torsor has a point (the vector space of pluricanonical sections has a basis), and two frames yield the same curve exactly when they differ by $G(\Omega)$. The quotient theorem's bijection $J(\Omega)/G(\Omega)\simeq Q_g(\Omega)$ therefore supplies precisely the coarse geometric-isomorphism-class bijection. The descended map is a functor over $\mathit{Sch}/k$ to the discrete groupoid $h_{Q_g}$, and its two properties are exactly the algebraic-space initial property and geometric-isomorphism-class bijection in the current definition. Combined with steps 1--3, all the assertions hold for every field and without markings.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-ten-canonical-hilbert-parameter, lem-sfb-projective-pgl-stable-quotient, def-sfb-stable-pointed-moduli-groupoids.
