### lem-trf-rational-ray-elimination-forward

Set $a_i=f_i(v)$ and partition $I$ as $I_0=\{i:a_i=0\}$, $I_+=\{i:a_i>0\}$, $I_-=\{i:a_i<0\}$. Define a new family $\mathcal F$ consisting of $f_h$ for $h\in I_0$, $f_i$ for $i\in I_+$, and $$g_{ij}=a_i f_j-a_j f_i\qquad(i\in I_+,\ j\in I_-).$$ It is finite, since the old index set and its indicated product are finite, and every member is rational linear. Steps 4 and 5 verify that its nonnegative locus is exactly $C_{k+1}$; thus this defines the induction step without assuming a separation or cone-duality theorem.

Suppose $x\in C_{k+1}$ and choose $t\ge0$ as in Step 2. For $h\in I_0$, $f_h(x)=f_h(x)-ta_h\ge0$. For $i\in I_+$, $f_i(x)=(f_i(x)-ta_i)+ta_i\ge0$. For $i\in I_+$ and $j\in I_-$, cancellation gives $$g_{ij}(x)=a_i(f_j(x)-ta_j)+(-a_j)(f_i(x)-ta_i)\ge0,$$ since both coefficients are positive. Hence every form in $\mathcal F$ is nonnegative at $x$.

This argument needs no finite-dimensional hypothesis: finite I alone ensures that both subsets and their product are finite. Retained forms are linear and each mixed form is a rational scalar linear combination of linear forms. The forward implication is exactly the cancellation calculation copied above.

### lem-trf-rational-ray-elimination-witness

Conversely, suppose all forms in $\mathcal F$ are nonnegative at $x$. The finite nonempty set $B=\{0\}\cup\{f_j(x)/a_j:j\in I_-\}\subset\mathbf Q$ has a largest member $T$, obtained by successively taking the larger of two entries. Thus $T\ge0$. For each $i\in I_+$, the inequality $f_i(x)\ge0$ gives $0\le f_i(x)/a_i$, and for each $j\in I_-$ the inequality $g_{ij}(x)\ge0$ gives $$\frac{f_j(x)}{a_j}\le\frac{f_i(x)}{a_i}.$$ Here division reverses the inequality precisely because $a_i a_j<0$: the displayed difference is $g_{ij}(x)/(a_i a_j)\le0$. Every entry of $B$ is consequently at most $f_i(x)/a_i$, so $T\le f_i(x)/a_i$. It follows that $f_i(x)-Ta_i\ge0$ for $i\in I_+$. For $j\in I_-$, $T\ge f_j(x)/a_j$, and multiplication by $a_j<0$ gives $f_j(x)-Ta_j\ge0$. For $h\in I_0$ the same inequality is just $f_h(x)\ge0$. Thus $x-Tv\in C_k$ and $T\ge0$, so Step 2 gives $x\in C_{k+1}$.

For its standalone scalar statement replace f_i(x) by b_i and f_i(v) by a_i throughout. Start with B={0} and adjoin its finitely many rational entries one at a time, replacing the current candidate by the larger entry; this constructs the largest rational member without completeness. The comparison with each positive upper bound is exactly the displayed quotient identity; the three sign cases give all b_i-Ta_i >= 0. If I_- is empty B={0} and T=0; if I_+ is empty the upper-bound assertions are vacuous. If I itself is empty, B={0}, T=0 and all residual assertions are vacuous.
