## Review by Q102

I read Stacks resolve.tex: the definition of normalized blowup (0BBS), the Nagata normalized-blowup lemma (0BFT), the local modification equivalence (0BFV–0BFX), its normalized-sequence version (0BFZ), proper regularity comparison with completion (0BG6), normalized-blowup completion descent (0BGA), normalized refinement (0BGM), complete normal surface resolution (0BGN), and the actual (4) to (3) argument of Lipman's theorem 0BGP. I also read ILO XVII 3.4.1, first paragraph, printed p.270. The use is exactly existence of a regular birational model and its preservation of the punctured regular open; no cohomology or purity theorem is used here.

For the six new statements, I checked their sources against their hypotheses. Complete-local resolution is precisely 0BGN, with the meaning of resolution written out; completeness and normality of a local domain are both required. Refinement is 0BGM, not a claim that an arbitrary normal surface already has a resolution. Properness makes closed centers lie over the closed point of the local base. The completion-descent statement is 0BGA together with the properly applicable endpoint clause 0BG6(3); I retained properness instead of silently extending that equivalence to arbitrary morphisms. The globalization statement is 0BFV–0BFZ for a finite set of closed points, with the complement quasi-compact because the base is Noetherian, and finitely many irreducible components for the same reason. Inserting identities accounts for blowups over the other selected points. The local-ring identification over a selected closed point is localization: all elements outside its maximal ideal already map to units in the local ring of a point above it. I noted that the source's x_i in 0BFZ is a notational slip for the already specified s_i.

For the local-data node, J-2 in 07QT gives the open regular locus; normal Noetherian local rings satisfy R1 by 031S, so a singular point has local dimension at least two. Since the surface has dimension two, it has dimension exactly two and is closed. Its singular closed subset has dimension zero, hence finitely many irreducible components, each a singleton. Excellence localizes by 07QU; 0C23 gives normal completion and 07NV preserves dimension. This is not the much weaker assertion about one punctured hypersurface in the existing node. For tower geometry I read 0BFT, 035Q, 035S, 02ND, 02NS, 0B3I, 0C4P and 02JX: blowups of integral schemes stay integral, normalizations are finite by excellence/Nagata, finite type preserves excellence, proper birational maps preserve dimension, and the finite and blowup maps are projective in the bundle sense. Over a normal open outside all centers, each blowup and normalization is the identity. This generic tower assertion makes no claim that any tower reaches a regular scheme.

Step 1 takes exactly integral normal excellent dimension-two normalization and the normal-open restriction from tower geometry, the J-2 definition from def-exc-excellent-ring and regular-implies-normal from rem-pc-regular-normal. Step 2 takes the finite singular set and the precise complete normal dimension-two local rings from local-data. Step 3 uses 0BGN existence followed by 0BGM refinement; both are applied to the completed local rings verified in Step 2. Step 4 uses the supplied proper sequence and normal completion in the completion-descent node, including its regular-endpoint clause. Step 5 takes the global tower, its open isomorphism, its specified local base changes and local-ring comparison from globalization; the two cases for a point exhaust V, so regularity follows from its definition. Step 6 takes integrality, projective-bundle composite and function-field isomorphism from tower geometry and applies the existing 087S statement to the original ample base Y, without assuming an ample sheaf on an intermediate scheme. Step 7 is only restriction and composition of the previously established isomorphisms and is marked elementary. All cited node statements were opened and are quoted verbatim in citations.json.

I tested the empty singular set and zero-length towers: a regular surface can use its identity resolution, and the global gluing assertion permits an empty family. A normal surface with one isolated singularity gives precisely one completed-local problem. A nonnormal excellent surface may have a curve of singularities; the argument does not wrongly declare that singular set finite, since finiteness is applied only after normalization. Normalization is the identity over every regular open by integral closure of integrally closed local domains, so the constructed map cannot disturb that locus. The dimension and normal-completion hypotheses exclude pathological nonexcellent normal local surfaces with nonnormal completions. No algebraically closed field or characteristic assumption is added, and the same complete-local results cover mixed characteristic.
