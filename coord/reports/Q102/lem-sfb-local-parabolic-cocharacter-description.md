## Review by Q102

I read Conrad Proposition 5.2.3 and its proof on printed pp.152–153 in the author's online PDF, together with Theorem 4.1.7, Example 4.1.9 and Proposition 4.1.10 on printed pp.112–115. This split follows the prescribed dynamic route: choose a closed-fibre cocharacter, lift it in the specified split torus, compare Lie modules, prove P_H(lambda)=H, and factor into P_G(lambda). I also read Milne's local PDF (iAG2017), Corollary 10.17 and the preceding discussion on p.191, Theorem 17.9(a) on p.354, Theorem 21.91 and Corollary 21.92 on pp.453–455, and Theorem 25.1 and (166) on p.544. No SGA source was used. No new node needs an additional source.

I opened the statement of each of the twelve proof dependencies. For step 1, the homogeneous-quotient node actually supplies a separated finite-type quotient and a surjective projection for every closed subgroup, so it applies both to the Borel and to H_F. The Borel-quotient node gives properness for the maximal smooth connected solvable subgroup already assumed in the item; the nested-quotient node gives the surjective map between the two coset schemes. The image-universally-closed node then supplies properness of G_F/H_F, since this target is separated finite type. I checked its source at Stacks Tag 03GN, morphisms.tex, lemma-image-proper-is-proper, including the surjectivity hypothesis. Thus the field theorem's properness hypothesis is established rather than smuggled in.

For step 2, the split-parabolic-root-sign-cone statement gives BOTH nonemptiness and equality of subgroup functors for every cocharacter in the cone. Here the field is algebraically closed, T_F is split and geometrically maximal by hypothesis, and H_F is smooth connected, closed, and has proper quotient by step 1. The relative diagonalizable duality statement supplies the monomial description of the resulting cocharacter over F. The lift is then the explicitly written Laurent-monomial Hopf homomorphism over A, not an existence appeal. This uses no henselian or reducedness assumption.

For step 3, the relative-tangent-weight-base-change statement applies to any smooth affine finite-type group with a split-torus action over a nonzero Noetherian ring; it needs neither reductivity nor connectedness. It applies to both G and H, because H is closed affine of finite type and smooth by assumption. Its conclusion includes finite projectivity, the finite full-character decomposition, and arbitrary ring base change. Naturality of the inclusion is the explicit dual-number/coaction coefficient comparison. The relative dynamic-subgroup node supplies the Lie sum with weights GREATER THAN OR EQUAL TO ZERO, including zero weights; positive weights alone would be wrong for a non-Borel parabolic. Restricting the m-character along lambda gives the integer dot product d(m). Base change to F and equality H_F=P(lambda_F) establish precisely the fibre maps used in step 4. Descending a linear isomorphism or a zero vector space along k to F is the elementary basis argument written in the proof.

For step 4, I checked the actual Nakayama statement, not a definition of a local ring: it includes the image-of-a-submodule criterion. Finite generation is provided by the finite-projective weight modules in step 3, and A is local. For a negative d(m), zero residue implies h_m=0. For a nonnegative d(m), the image in g_m surjects modulo the maximal ideal and therefore is all of g_m. The tangent inclusion is injective already on dual-number points, so this is equality inside g, not a claim that tensoring an arbitrary injection preserves injectivity. The direct sum is finite. These are two direct applications of a cited theorem, marked elementary and kept as a step; they do not merit another node.

For step 5, the relative dynamic-subgroup statement supplies smoothness, closedness, finite presentation, the Lie formula, connected geometric fibres and arbitrary base change for P_H(lambda). H itself has connected geometric fibres by the item's parabolic hypothesis. The tangent-generation statement is only over an algebraically closed field, so I use it separately on each geometric fibre with one smooth connected closed subgroup, and K equal to that subgroup. Its hypotheses and conclusion then give exactly the fibre isomorphism needed. The relative fibre criterion explicitly includes an isomorphism clause and a smooth-scheme case; I checked its Stacks source 039E, more-morphisms.tex, lemma-morphism-between-flat, and its reviewed statement. Smooth source supplies flatness; the source and target are locally finitely presented over A; the closed immersion is finitely presented because the target affine coordinate ring is finite type over the Noetherian A and its ideal is finitely generated. The finite-type-algebra-Noetherian node says precisely the algebra fact used here. This prevents a fibre-only equality argument over a possibly nonreduced base.

For step 6, factorization into P_G(lambda) is proved on every test algebra by composing the orbit extension in H_C with H_C into G_C. It is not taken from a functoriality assertion absent from the cited node. I wrote the quotient-ring argument showing that the resulting morphism is a closed immersion. Equality of its Lie modules follows from step 4 and the dynamic Lie formula and survives arbitrary base change by the prior explicit tangent comparison. Again the fibrewise application of tangent-generation is legitimate because the dynamic theorem makes P_G(lambda) geometrically connected (G is). The final fibre criterion is the same one with finite presentation already checked. The resulting isomorphism is over A as a closed subgroup of G; its functor equality therefore holds for all algebras, not just fields.

I checked the edge cases H=G (one may take the zero cocharacter, P(0)=G), G=T and empty root systems (the argument does not demand a strictly positive root), r=0 (the integer tuple is empty and the same zero-cocharacter construction applies), positive residue characteristic including characters divisible by that characteristic (full torus characters, not their differentials, are used), nonreduced local A, and arbitrary nonreduced test C. As a concrete check, in GL_2 with lambda(t)=diag(t,1), conjugation multiplies the lower-left entry c by t^{-1}; extension to C[t] requires c=0 even if c is a nonzero nilpotent in C. The upper triangular functor, rather than only its reduced points, is recovered. The zero test ring presents no issue: the equality is a functor equality on the empty affine scheme.
