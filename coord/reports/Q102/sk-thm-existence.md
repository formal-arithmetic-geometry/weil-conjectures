## Intended arguments for the new tasks (not proofs stored in the new nodes)

For lem-sfb-base-coefficient-condensed-identification, the structural embedding onto its image is an algebra isomorphism from Q_l to the bottom intermediate field. The extended norm makes it an isometry. Restriction to the norm-at-most-one subrings is bijective: the inverse image of an integral element has the same norm, hence lies in Z_l. Both inverses are continuous because the maps are isometries. For each profinite S, postcompose a continuous function by these maps and their continuous inverses. Pointwise addition, multiplication and one show that the component maps are ring homomorphisms. The inverse identities hold pointwise; precomposition by any continuous map of profinite sets commutes with postcomposition. Thus these maps define inverse morphisms of condensed rings. The integral/rational square commutes because the integral map is literally the restriction of the rational one. This includes a topological identification and a natural sheaf-level construction, not merely the algebraic botEquiv specialization.

For lem-sfb-shriek-coefficient-isomorphism, scalar restriction by e inverse and by e are inverse exact module equivalences; they do not change underlying abelian sheaves or differential maps. They induce derived equivalences and preserve all small coproducts. The finite scalar matrices on each constructible stratum are transported by the ring isomorphism, giving precisely the new coefficient constant local models; derived tensor and inverse scalar transport identify Tor testing intervals. Equivalently use the existing lem-sfb-condensed-coefficient-iso-constructible in both directions. Minimality of the two localizing closures then restricts the equivalences to them. The module pushforward comparison is induced by the identity on underlying pushforward sheaves and the naturality of the pulled-back coefficient isomorphism. Exact equivalences preserve K-injectives: apply the inverse exact equivalence to an acyclic test complex and use the Hom equivalence. Thus the pushforward comparison derives on unbounded K-injective resolutions. The open extension comparison is the unique left-adjoint comparison to the commuting open inverse-image equivalences; it is invertible because the coefficient maps are equivalences. Derive it termwise and compose it with the proper pushforward comparison to obtain the displayed H comparison. Its inverse and the equivalences identify the images of both localizing closures. Finally conjugate one given adjunction by these equivalences. It has the same left functor as the other adjunction under a; uniqueness of right adjoints yields b. Its normalization is exactly the counit equation in the statement. Inverting e gives the reverse comparison. Repleteness of the two Dctf classes and their identification by U imply both directions of the final preservation assertion. This is the compatibility of actual operations, not a bare invocation of an abstract equivalence theorem.
