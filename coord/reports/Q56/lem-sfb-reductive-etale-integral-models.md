## Source checks and edge cases

The definition follows Conrad Definitions 5.1.1 and 6.1.1; I checked that the source requires both constant coroots and free root lines. Root data retain all central directions, so this does not silently restrict to semisimple groups. The empty root set has empty base and empty pinning. A rank-zero realization is the trivial group; a positive-rank empty-root datum gives a split torus. The source's maximal torus, roots and coroots are interpreted on geometric fibres, with named monomial characters and cocharacters over the ring.

The splitting node follows Lemma 5.1.3 and Proposition 5.1.6, followed by choosing a base and generators as in Definition 6.1.1. The report records the proof route for its future splitter: first take an etale-local maximal torus, split that torus, then make roots and coroots constant Zariski locally and trivialize the finitely many root lines; take affine neighborhoods and discard the empty ones. These are not smuggled as proof steps into the statement-only node. The result allows rank and type to vary with the covering index. It asserts no integral model.

The classification node is Theorem 6.1.17, with essential surjectivity supplied by 6.1.16(2). The based datum is an ordinary fixed finite lattice datum; isomorphisms induce specified constant lattice maps, so uniqueness is not overclaimed for all locally varying maps on a disconnected base. This distinction is explicit in both definition and theorem. Taking A=Z is allowed; taking A=B_i is allowed without a field or Noetherian hypothesis. Empty roots and rank zero remain allowed.

The base-change node was checked against the smooth relative tangent construction and the root-space discussion of Sections 3.1,4.1,5.1 and the definition of pinning. It includes preservation of the underlying reductive group and torus as well as root-line and generator identifications. Nonflat A-to-C maps are allowed: smooth tangent modules are locally free and the torus grading is a direct-sum decomposition, so the comparison is not being deduced from exactness of an arbitrary tensor functor. This argument is for the future split, not an extra claim hidden in the item's assembly. Ring maps are unital and both bases nonzero, avoiding the nonempty-base hypothesis failure in the classification theorem.

The Borel node follows the positive-root subgroup and big-cell construction in Theorem 5.1.13 and the precise positive-system/Borel assertion in Remark 6.1.2, with uniqueness from Corollary 5.2.7(2). I checked the warning in Remark 6.1.2: a constant positive system produces a Borel, but not every Borel on a disconnected base comes from a constant positive system. The new statement makes only the former assertion. It gives B=G=T in the torus case, and includes no properness assertion about G itself.
