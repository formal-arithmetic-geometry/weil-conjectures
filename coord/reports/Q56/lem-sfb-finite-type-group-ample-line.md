## Review by Q56

I checked the item against 0BF7 rather than replacing it by the same quasi-projectivity assertion as a new node. Its separatedness assumption suffices for every use; the source actually also proves separatedness for algebraic groups. All characteristics, disconnected groups and nonreduced groups are retained.

Step 1 uses exactly the statement of lem-sfb-locally-algebraic-group-identity-component: C is a finite-type subgroup and H=C_red is smooth integral separated. The new family node applies to H with precisely these hypotheses. Its avoidance assertion at the identity gives a parameter v0, so there is no unjustified choice of a rational point of a possibly empty parameter space. Pullback invertibility is clause (1) of lem-weil-pbc-invertible-pullback-tensor.

For that family node I read the middle paragraphs of 0BF7. The construction chooses a divisor D with affine complement, restricts an etale chart to a finite etale W→V over an affine-space open, and forms the image of the translated divisor on H×V. Local factoriality makes that support Cartier. Its fibre support is the finite union of D j(w_i)^(-1), hence its complement is the intersection of affine opens in a separated scheme. For fixed h, the bad locus of w is proper closed; deleting its finite image allows a rational v with every w_i good. Taking M=O(E) and its fibre canonical sections gives exactly the node. It does not assert the fibre classes are equal. These geometric facts remain together as a statement-only intermediate node for the next split, not as uncited assertions used by the item's steps.

Step 2 uses lem-trf-smooth-geometrically-normal on the smooth H (no curve restriction), then 0BEH with H integral separated finite type, normal, and V open in affine space. The same M and rational parameters appear on both sides; no algebraic-equivalence assertion is substituted for rational equivalence. Its isomorphisms preserve vanishing because they are fibrewise linear isomorphisms. The closed-point residue-field node makes every closed point rational, and the Jacobson node says closed points are dense in every closed subset. Their statements justify the contradiction in the complement. Finite type implies quasi-compactness by the cited definition, completing the exact 01PS criterion. These tool uses are declared in split_report where necessary.

Step 3 distinguishes characteristics. The finite-type-over-Noetherian theorem gives C Noetherian over the field; the new reduction-existence lemma requires pO_C=0 and Noetherianity, both present. It is important that this is 0BD4(3): the existing fixed-module ampleness test does not lift a module. In characteristic zero Cartier proves C reduced, so the reduction definition identifies C with H. The smoothness part is retained in its new attachment statement, though this step only needs reducedness. No assumption of reducedness was smuggled into the item.

Step 4 reads the whole neutral-component-translates statement, including finiteness and the open-and-closed scheme structures. Gluing on a disjoint cover has no overlap cocycle to prove. Extending a section by zero, with the same exponent on all components, proves that its nonvanishing open is exactly the chosen affine open in one component. This is an elementary definition argument, not a new finite-disjoint-union ampleness lemma.

Step 5 uses an algebraic closure supplied by its definition node and the separated finite-type base-change statement, which explicitly permits arbitrary base change. Group laws pull back as equalities. The new 0BDC statement descends existence; it never claims that the chosen line upstairs has descent data. Generic tool uses do not replace a unit's proof route. Every cited node was opened; citations.json contains the actual statement text, one entry for every step/node pair.

Small cases: Spec(k), the additive group, nonaffine abelian varieties, and finite disconnected constant groups satisfy the item. In characteristic p, alpha_p and mu_p show why smoothness of G must not be assumed; their reductions fall under the reduction-existence node. For that node the reduced case is tautological and Spec(k[epsilon]/epsilon^2) passes; no characteristic-zero version is asserted. For 0BDC the identity extension and X empty pass, and no finiteness of the extension is used. For 0BEH, p=q and n=0 give the identity isomorphism; an empty U admits no p,q. For the family, H=Spec(k) allows d=0, V=Spec(k), a nowhere-vanishing section, and empty Cartier support. Cartier is tested against nonaffine groups and arbitrary nonclosed characteristic-zero fields, and explicitly excludes positive-characteristic infinitesimal groups.
