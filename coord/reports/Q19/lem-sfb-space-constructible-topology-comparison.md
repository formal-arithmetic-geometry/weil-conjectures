The new module is
`Sheaf.AlgebraicGeometry.SpaceConstructibleTopologyComparison`.
It states five theorems, all in `AlgebraicGeometry.AlgebraicSpace`, with `sorry` proofs:

1. `scheme_pointConstructibleTopology_eq` identifies the topology induced on a
   quasi-separated scheme by its usual point-class map with its local affine-chart
   constructible topology.
2. `isQuotientMap_schemePointMap_constructible` says that every surjective representable
   étale scheme presentation of a quasi-separated algebraic space is a quotient map for
   the constructible topologies. Since `IsQuotientMap` includes equality with the
   coinduced topology, this is independence from every choice of such a presentation.
3. `continuous_mapPoint_constructible` states continuity of every algebraic-space
   morphism between quasi-separated spaces for these constructible topologies.
4. `pointConstructibleTopology_le` states fineness over the atlas Zariski topology.
5. `isClosed_iff_constructible_closed_specialization` is the closedness equivalence.

Hypotheses and clauses, in the order of the node:

- The base scheme is `{S : Scheme.{u}}`. `universe u` is explicit, and
  `set_option autoImplicit false` forbids additional inferred binders. All schemes,
  field representatives and sites use the same universe as the recorded algebraic-space
  interfaces. Their point quotient is already `Type (u + 1)`; no resizing or new universe
  equality is imposed. The size-convention node is a metatheoretic convention.
- Spaces over S are `X Y : AlgebraicSpace S`; morphisms are `f : X ⟶ Y` in that full
  subcategory. Their presheaves and natural transformations are `X.obj.obj` and
  `f.hom.hom`. Sheaf fibre products retain the existing fibre-products module.
- Point spaces and their atlas Zariski topology are `Point X.obj.obj` and
  `pointTopologicalSpace X.obj.obj`. Scheme chart maps are the recorded
  `schemePointMap`, not newly selected maps.
- The node's quasi-compactness conventions use the existing notions: point-space
  quasi-compactness is `CompactSpace (Point F)`, and affine-test quasi-compactness is
  `MapIsQuasiCompact` from `ProperCurveModuli`. No extra quasi-compactness condition
  occurs in a theorem. They only explain the quasi-separatedness convention.
- Quasi-separated spaces are assumed with `hX : IsAbsolutelyQuasiSeparated X`, and
  `hY` in the morphism theorem. This is the existing absolute affine-test criterion
  of def-sfb-space-absolute-quasi-separated (Stacks 0AHR), equivalent to a
  quasi-compact absolute diagonal. In the scheme comparison the corresponding
  hypothesis is `hT : QuasiSeparatedSpace T.left`.
- The scheme construction is the existing `Scheme.localConstructibleTopology`:
  the supremum of the coinduced constructible topologies on all affine open charts.
  Those affine chart topologies are Mathlib's `constructibleTopology`, generated by
  compact opens and their complements. The scheme comparison uses the identity
  Yoneda transformation and `TopologicalSpace.induced` to compare topologies on the
  same scheme point type. It does not replace the point identification by an arbitrary
  map or postulate a new scheme topology.
- The algebraic-space construction is `pointConstructibleTopology X.obj.obj`, the
  existing final topology for every representable étale affine chart, using that same
  Mathlib affine constructible topology. No second definition is introduced.
- An arbitrary scheme presentation is `U : Over S`, with
  `p : yoneda.obj U ⟶ X.obj.obj`, `he : IsEtale p`, `hs : IsSurjective p`.
  `IsEtale` already includes representability. There is no affine, quasi-compact or
  quasi-separated hypothesis on U. `@IsQuotientMap` uses exactly
  `U.left.localConstructibleTopology` and `pointConstructibleTopology X.obj.obj`.
  Thus each such presentation gives the same canonical topology; equality between
  the quotient topologies of two presentations follows immediately.
- Every morphism is the unrestricted `f : X ⟶ Y` in the continuity statement.
  `@Continuous` displays the two constructible topologies explicitly and uses
  `mapPoint f.hom.hom`. It assumes neither quasi-compactness nor étaleness of f.
- Fineness is `pointConstructibleTopology X.obj.obj ≤ pointTopologicalSpace X.obj.obj`.
  Lean orders topologies by reverse inclusion of open sets, so this means every
  Zariski open is constructibly open, in the required direction.
- For every `F : Set (Point X.obj.obj)`, `IsClosed F` uses the existing atlas
  Zariski instance. The other side of the equivalence is explicitly
  `IsClosed[pointConstructibleTopology X.obj.obj] F` together with
  `∀ x ∈ F, ∀ z, z ∈ closure ({x} : Set (Point X.obj.obj)) → z ∈ F`.
  The closure is in the Zariski instance; x is the generalizing point and z its
  specialization. Both directions of the equivalence are present.
