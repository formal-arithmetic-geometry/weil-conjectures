### Original step 2

For $N\in D^b_{ctf}(s,\Lambda)$ set $L_N=R\mathcal Hom_X(K,Ra^!N)$, $A_N=Ra_*L_N$ and $B_N=R\mathcal Hom_s(C,N)$. The ordinary and exceptional adjunctions [fix-prop-adjunctions], the restricted tensor–Hom adjunction [det-thm-sf-ctf-stability-adjunctions](2), and the projection isomorphism [fix-prop-projection-formula] give, in the indicated direction,
$$\begin{aligned}
\Phi^N_T:\operatorname{Hom}_s(T,A_N)&\cong\operatorname{Hom}_X(La^*T,L_N)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Hom}_X(K\otimes La^*T,Ra^!N)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Hom}_s(Ra_!(K\otimes La^*T),N)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Hom}_s(C\otimes T,N)\\
&\cong\operatorname{Hom}_s(T,B_N).
\end{aligned}$$
The first sends $t$ to $\varepsilon_{L_N}La^*t$, the second evaluates against $K$, the third sends $h$ to $\mu_NRa_!h$, the fourth precomposes with $P_T$, and the last curries. These are the inverse, in order, of uncurry, precomposition with $P_T^{-1}$, exceptional transpose, curry on $X$, and ordinary transpose in each of the two source constructions [lem-sf-tensor-hom, fix-lem-tensor-adjunction]. This verifies their normalization directly, rather than treating their assertion of a canonical isomorphism as an additional map formula. All ingredients are the same specified adjunctions and projection transformation [def-exchange-projection].

Put $p_N=\mu_NRa_!(\operatorname{ev}_{K,Ra^!N}(1_K\otimes\varepsilon_{L_N}))P_{A_N}$ and $\alpha_N=\operatorname{curry}(p_N)$. Naturality of $P$ says
$$P_{A_N}(1_C\otimes t)=Ra_!(1_K\otimes La^*t)P_T.$$
Consequently $\Phi^N_T(t)=\operatorname{curry}(p_N(1_C\otimes t))=\alpha_Nt$ by [lem-pod-closed-uncurry-laws]. To see the represented inverse without any faithfulness claim about cohomology, take $v=(\Phi^N_{B_N})^{-1}(1_{B_N})$. Then $\alpha_Nv=1$; injectivity of $\Phi^N_{A_N}$ gives $v\alpha_N=1$, because both sides map to $\alpha_N$. Thus both source constructions give this same actual isomorphism $\alpha_N$. At $N=\Lambda_s$ it is exactly the displayed $\alpha_s$ of the statement. Naturality of every bijection in $N$ also gives, for $b:N\to N'$, $\alpha_{N'}A(b)=B(b)\alpha_N$, where $A(b)=Ra_*R\mathcal Hom_X(K,Ra^!b)$ and $B(b)=R\mathcal Hom_s(C,b)$.

### Original step 3

Write $w=\omega_{s,\Lambda}$ and take the identity-adjunction isomorphism $u:w\to\Lambda_s$ of [lem-pod-point-dualizing-complex]. We verify the structure-morphism mate entering [fix-cor-verdier-exchange]. Since $a_s=\mathrm{id}_s$, its mate $m:Ra^!w\to\omega$ equals $Ra^!u$: under the exceptional adjunction of [fix-prop-adjunctions](2), the transpose of $Ra^!u$ is $u\mu_{a,w}$. The composition law [thm-sf-comp-composition] is the identity when one factor is an identity, under the canonical identity identifications; its completed and transported forms in clause (3) of [fix-prop-adjunctions] are formed from these finite-level comparisons, so the comparison $R(\mathrm{id}_s\circ a)_!\cong R\mathrm{id}_{s!}Ra_!$ is the identity under the identity normalization of $R\mathrm{id}_{s!}$ used in [lem-pod-point-dualizing-complex]. Under that normalization the transpose is precisely the pasted counit $\mu_{\mathrm{id},\Lambda}R\mathrm{id}_!(\mu_{a,w})$ of clause (3), since the identity-adjunction isomorphism is the counit under that normalization. The mate $m$ has this same transpose by the composite-adjunction construction, and the adjunction bijection is injective. This proves $m=Ra^!u$ with the specified composition comparison, including the completed identity normalization; no independent choice of an isomorphism of right adjoints has been made.

The source construction of the named exchange is the inverse tensor–Hom arrow at $N=w$ followed by $Ra_*R\mathcal Hom_X(K,m)$. We have just computed both constituents: it is $A(u)\alpha_w^{-1}$. Step 2's naturality equation with $b=u$ reads $\alpha_sA(u)=\rho\alpha_w$. Multiplying by the two inverses gives
$$e=A(u)\alpha_w^{-1}=\alpha_s^{-1}\rho.$$
This is a calculation of the exchange's construction from the stated adjunctions and the already named tensor–Hom comparison, not an appeal to an unstated normalization theorem about $e$. The identity morphism of $s$ satisfies the exceptional hypothesis using the identity adjunction of the point lemma, so the exchange applies to $a$, $a_X=a$, $a_Y=\mathrm{id}_s$.

### Original step 4

We now compute the point comparison on complexes, to fix its normalization. At finite and integral coefficients put $(R,B)=(A_{E,n},(A_{E,n})_s)$ or $(\mathcal O_E,\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,s})$. Define $L_RM=B\otimes_{\underline R}\underline M$. The sheaf tensor definition [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product] gives an explicit adjunction $L_R\dashv\Gamma(s,-)$: $h:L_RM\to V$ is sent to $m\mapsto h_s(1\otimes m)$, with inverse $t\mapsto(b\otimes m\mapsto b\,t(m)|_U)$ on every object $U$. These formulas respect balancing and restriction and are mutually inverse. Exact sheafification [lem-weil-pbc-site-r-module-presheaf-sheafification-exact] makes the constant module functor exact; integrally, flatness in [lem-weil-pbc-completed-constant-sheaf-reduction] makes $L_R$ exact as well. Sections are exact by [lem-weil-point-proetale-sections-exact]. The continuous-functions formula [lem-weil-pbc-continuous-coefficients-immersion-restriction] at $s$ gives $\Gamma(s,B)=R$ integrally. Its reduction formula and exact sections give the same identity at finite coefficients: apply sections to $0\to B\xrightarrow{\varpi^n}B\to(A_{E,n})_s\to0$. Multiplication is injective since the integral $B$ is flat over the domain $\mathcal O_E$.

Here $\mathcal O_E$ is a PID by [lem-weil-l-adic-extension-integers-dvr], so it and its quotient rings are Noetherian. For a finite module $M$, [lem-sc-noetherian-finite-module-finite-presentation] gives a finite presentation. Exactness and $\Gamma(s,B)=R$ show that the actual adjunction unit $M\to\Gamma(s,L_RM)$ is an isomorphism, by applying the two functors to that presentation. It follows on cohomology that the unit is a quasi-isomorphism for every complex with bounded finite cohomology. In particular this applies to $c=FC$ by [def-weil-finite-coefficient-point]. At finite coefficients $L_Rc$ has bounded constant finite cohomology; [lem-weil-proetale-constant-sheaf-stalk] identifies its cohomology stalks with those finite modules, so it belongs to the constructible category by [def-weil-finite-torsion-constructibility]. Integrally, [lem-weil-coefficient-frobenius] makes $c$ perfect, so a bounded finite-projective model $P$ gives $L_RP$ constructible by [lem-weil-pbc-integral-stratified-perfect-characterization] on the single stratum $s$ (Noetherian of dimension zero with $\ell$ invertible). The triangle identity makes sections of the actual counit $L_Rc\to C$ inverse to the unit at $c$. Therefore [lem-weil-point-fibre-equivalence], applied to these two constructible objects, makes this counit an isomorphism. We may consequently use $L_Rc$ as a representative of $C$ through this specified counit, without identifying arbitrary pro-etale sheaves with modules.

### Original step 5

At finite coefficients choose the representative of $c$ to be K-flat with flat terms, using [lem-pc-kflat-resolution] on the one-object ringed site with ring $R$. The exact functor $L_R$ is the module pullback for the ringed-topos morphism whose inverse image is the constant-sheaf functor and whose ring map is $\underline R\to B$. Hence [lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topos-kflat-pullback] makes $L_Rc$ K-flat too. Exactness of $L_R$ and the counit isomorphism of Step 4 identify this replacement with the same object $C$. Thus the ordinary tensor evaluation on these representatives computes the derived evaluation; an arbitrary nonflat complex has not been used to compute a derived tensor. Choose a K-injective resolution $B\to I$ as in [def-pur-derived-internal-hom]. Its sections are K-injective over $R$: a map from an acyclic module complex $Q$ to $\Gamma(s,I)$ adjoints by Step 4 to a map $L_RQ\to I$; exactness of $L_R$ makes $L_RQ$ acyclic, and [def-pur-kinjective-complex] supplies a null homotopy. The same adjunction carries that homotopy back. Exact sections make $R\to\Gamma(s,I)$ a quasi-isomorphism. Thus $\Gamma(s,I)$ computes derived coefficient Hom. The definitions of sheaf Hom and its Hom complex [def-pur-sheaf-hom, def-pur-hom-complex] give an isomorphism of complexes
$$\Gamma\bigl(s,\mathcal Hom_B^\bullet(L_Rc,I)\bigr)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Hom}_R^\bullet(c,\Gamma(s,I)),\qquad h\longmapsto(m\mapsto h(1\otimes m)).$$
In degree $j$, use this formula on each component $c^p\to I^{p+j}$; both differentials are $d_Ih-(-1)^jhd_c$. It is therefore an isomorphism on the complexes themselves and hence in the derived category. With the actual counit model of Step 4, this is the coefficient-Hom identification used to construct the canonical point isomorphism of [lem-pod-point-dualizing-complex], after applying $R\mathcal Hom_s(C,u)$. We verify its evaluation, rather than presuming a normalization from the word canonical: a homogeneous $h$ of degree $j$ evaluated on $x\in c^p$ is $(-1)^{pj}h(1\otimes x)$ when the tensor factors are ordered as $c\otimes\operatorname{Hom}$; the sheaf and module evaluation give this same sign, from the same factor interchange. At finite coefficients this is exactly the balancing map through the tensor comparison [lem-sf-point-fibre-derived-tensor]. Integrally use the bounded finite-projective $P$ of Step 4. Sheaf Hom from $B\otimes_RP^i$ is exact, since $P^i$ is a retract of a finite free module. Filtering by the finitely many degrees of $P$ therefore shows that $\mathcal Hom_B^\bullet(B\otimes_RP,B)\to\mathcal Hom_B^\bullet(B\otimes_RP,I)$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Its source is the bounded model $B\otimes_R\operatorname{Hom}_R^\bullet(P,R)$. Both this model and its ordinary tensor with $B\otimes_RP$ are constructible and derived complete by the stratified-perfect characterization already used in Step 4. The canonical completeness map, as specified for the identity extension in [lem-weil-finite-free-completed-scalar-comparison], identifies ordinary tensor on these models with completed tensor. On the strict cone models of [def-weil-derived-pullback-tensor-hom], the completion map has the quotient maps as its product component and zero as its shifted-product component. Evaluation commutes with every quotient map, by the formula $h(1\otimes x)$ and its sign; it therefore commutes with the induced product maps and their cone differentials. Exact sections carries these same product components to the coefficient ones. Hence the completed tensor comparison carries this evaluation to the same module evaluation too. Thus the resulting map $FH\to c^\vee$ is the coefficient transpose of transported evaluation, namely the specified $\kappa$. Replacing $B$ by $w$ and applying $u$ in the second Hom variable composes this map with $F\rho$. This proves
$$j=\kappa F\rho$$
at finite and integral coefficients as equality of derived morphisms, not merely on cohomology. Since $j$ and $F\rho$ are invertible, so is this specified $\kappa$.

### Original step 6

At rational coefficients represent $C_E$ by an integral constructible object as specified in [def-weil-derived-pullback-tensor-hom, def-weil-finite-coefficient-point]. Its evaluated integral complex is perfect by [lem-weil-coefficient-frobenius]. On a bounded finite-projective representative $P$, the coefficient scalar-Hom comparison [lem-pc-base-change-rhom-perfect] is the chain isomorphism
$$\operatorname{Hom}_R^\bullet(P,R)\otimes_RE\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_E^\bullet(P\otimes_RE,E),\qquad h\otimes a\longmapsto(x\otimes b\mapsto h(x)ab).$$
For free terms this is evaluation on a basis; for projective terms restrict the same formula along a splitting idempotent. It commutes with $d h=d_Rh-(-1)^{|h|}hd_P$ and with the evaluation sign of Step 5. Thus rationalizing the preceding chain comparison proves $j=\kappa F\rho$ at $E$, including when its integral representative has torsion. Naturality for a localized arrow $v\otimes a$ follows by multiplying the integral naturality equation by $a$ and adding such equations.

For $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$ perform this calculation over a common finite field. The specified scalar comparisons are [lem-pur-integral-operations-scalar-extension] and [lem-weil-point-sections-scalar-extension]. Writing $\theta$ for the sections-tensor map and $\chi$ for the completion comparison, the latter is explicitly $\varphi=(\Gamma(s,\chi)\circ\theta)^{-1}=\theta^{-1}\circ\Gamma(s,\chi)^{-1}$; [lem-weil-finite-free-completed-scalar-comparison] identifies that completion with ordinary completed-ring extension on the bounded finite-projective models $B\otimes_RP$ and their Hom duals. To check the specified internal-Hom comparison itself, expand the extension-of-scalars adjunction used in its construction: the sectionwise map $h\otimes a\mapsto(m\mapsto h(m)\otimes a)$ adjoints to $h\otimes a\mapsto(m\otimes b\mapsto h(m)\otimes ab)$. This follows directly by evaluating the adjunct on $1\otimes m$ and then extending linearly, exactly as in Step 4. On the finite-projective models this is the free-term formula just written and its projective retract. Thus the source comparison is the transpose of extended evaluation and has the stated inverse; it has not been replaced by an independently chosen natural isomorphism. In a tower of fields both scalar routes send $((h\otimes a)\otimes b)(x\otimes t)$ to $h(x)abt$, including the identical evaluation sign. This checks the actual comparison maps and their differentials, not just their source and target objects. The identity and tower compatibilities in the two cited comparison statements make these equal arrows define the same morphism class at any larger common field. Hence the equation $j=\kappa F\rho$ holds with the stipulated common-stage comparisons too.
