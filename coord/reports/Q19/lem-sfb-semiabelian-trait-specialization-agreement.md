For the item I read the online Faltings source cited above and the local Stacks sources algebra.tex (04GU and 04GX), more-algebra.tex (06DI and 07QM), etale-cohomology.tex (03QO), and groupoids.tex (0BF5 and 0BFH). For the new normal strict-local trait node I read 04GU's actual local map with prescribed residue closure, 06DI's normal-domain equivalence, and 07QM's faithful flatness. The additional dominance assertion follows as follows. Strict henselization is an etale algebra colimit; after tensoring with Frac(A), each finite-presentation etale algebra is finite over that field, so every element of H is algebraic over Frac(A). Both strict henselizations are normal domains, and A→H and R→B are injective by faithful flatness. If a nonzero z in H were killed by H→B, its algebraic minimal polynomial over Frac(A), cleared of denominators, would give a relation with nonzero constant coefficient in A. Evaluating in B would kill that coefficient, contradicting A→R→B injective. Thus H→B is injective. This generic algebraicity/dominance fact is not in the existing functoriality statement, which is why this is a real unsplit lemma. The assertion includes A a field, R a field, A=R, and arbitrary transcendental residue extensions. Separably algebraic elements over k remain separable over l, so k^s embeds in l^s inside the chosen Omega even for inseparable residue extensions.

For the new density node I read the local Milne torsion/isogeny discussion and the statements of the existing abelian prime-to torsion density, smooth-diagonal torsion density and semiabelian invertible-multiplication nodes. The missing fact is genuinely the extension from torus and abelian varieties to their fppf extension, and then schematic density. A proof route for its next worker is to take the reduced Zariski closure of all prime-to-characteristic torsion, a closed subgroup over the perfect field F. It contains the torus by torus density. Every prime-to n torsion point of the abelian quotient lifts to an n-torsion point of G: lift to G(F), and subtract an n-th root of its n-multiple in the split torus. Thus the closure maps onto the abelian quotient by abelian torsion density and closedness of images of algebraic-group homomorphisms. Since it also contains the torus it is all of G. Finally G is reduced (smooth), so a closed subscheme containing these dense geometric points has zero ideal. The subgroup-closure and image assertions are real algebraic-group lemmas to split below this unsplit node, not silently used as elementary steps of the item. Tests: G=Spec F, G=G_m^r, abelian quotient with r=0, a nonsplit semiabelian extension, characteristic zero, and characteristic p with p-torsion excluded. Nilpotent thickening counterexamples do not apply because G is smooth; the statement explicitly concludes scheme equality and not merely equality of point sets.

Step 3 uses only the smooth clause (2) of pc-proper-base-change; properness is never assumed. The existing smooth-group-invertible-multiplication theorem applies to commutative smooth group objects and says precisely that kernels are etale and commute with arbitrary base change. It is applicable to schemes as group algebraic spaces; the kernel is a scheme fibre product. Since n is nonzero at the closed point, n is a unit in each local ring, exactly the theorem's hypothesis. The homomorphisms v_i carry a_i to the target kernel because [n]v_i(a_i)=v_i([n]a_i)=0. For generic agreement, Frac(H) embeds in Frac(B_i) by step 1; the latter also contains L_i, and the generic u restrictions therefore match the same u_F. The closed-immersion base-change lemma states that pullbacks of closed immersions are closed with their actual image ideal; it supplies all closed equalizers used here. Equality descends over F→F_i because an ideal of a field cannot vanish in an injective field extension, and extends from F to H because H is a domain. These are explicit ideal arguments, not an unproved separatedness/density comparison theorem. Evaluation gives agreement on every n-torsion geometric point.
