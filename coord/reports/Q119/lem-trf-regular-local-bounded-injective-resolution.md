### Old step 1; assigned to item assembly

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-finite-gl-dim] to the given regular local ring $A$. It supplies a finite nonnegative bound $d=\dim A$ such that every $A$-module has a projective resolution of length at most $d$. We shall construct the required injective complex supported in $[0,d]$.

### Old step 2; assigned to item assembly

For each $A$-module $M$, apply [lem-weil-pbc-projective-dimension-ext-characterization] with $X=M$ and $n=d$, using the resolution bound from step 1. It gives $\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_A(M,A)=0$ for every $i\ge d+1$, with Ext as in [def-pc-ext]. In particular this vanishing holds for $M=A/T$ for every ideal $T\subseteq A$. No finite-generation restriction on $M$ is used.

### Old step 3; assigned to item assembly

The category of $A$-modules is abelian and has enough injectives by [lem-weil-pbc-ring-modules-enough-injectives]. Part (1) of [lem-weil-pbc-left-exact-injective-resolution-degree-zero] therefore gives an exact augmented injective resolution $0\to A\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}I^0\xrightarrow{\partial^0}I^1\to\cdots$, with $I^j=0$ for $j<0$. Put $J=\ker(\partial^d:I^d\to I^{d+1})$ and let $\iota:J\hookrightarrow I^d$ denote its inclusion. For $d=0$, exactness identifies $\varepsilon:A\xrightarrow{\sim}J$.

### Old step 4; assigned to lem-trf-injective-resolution-final-kernel

Fix an ideal $T\subseteq A$ and an $A$-linear map $u:T\to J$. Since $I^d$ is injective in the sense of [def-sp-injective-resolutions], extend $\iota u$ to $f:A\to I^d$. The map $\partial^d f$ vanishes on $T$, so it factors as $h\pi$, where $\pi:A\to A/T$ is the quotient map and $h:A/T\to I^{d+1}$. Since $\partial^{d+1}\partial^d=0$ and $\pi$ is surjective, $\partial^{d+1}h=0$. By [lem-weil-pbc-ext-via-injective-resolution] and step 2, $H^{d+1}(\operatorname{Hom}_A(A/T,I^\bullet))=0$. Its explicit cocycle/boundary description gives $g:A/T\to I^d$ with $h=\partial^d g$. Consequently $\partial^d(f-g\pi)=0$, and $f-g\pi$ factors through $J$, giving $v:A\to J$. On $T$ the correction $g\pi$ is zero, so $\iota(v|_T)=\iota u$, and injectivity of $\iota$ gives $v|_T=u$.

### Old step 5; assigned to lem-trf-injective-resolution-final-kernel

Step 4 gives the ideal-extension condition for $J$. Apply [lem-sf-baer-criterion] to the commutative ring $A$ and the $A$-module $J$: its hypotheses hold by step 3, and its implication from ideal extensions to injectivity proves that $J$ is injective.

### Old step 6; assigned to lem-trf-injective-final-kernel-truncation

Define $L^j=I^j$ for $0\le j<d$, $L^d=J$, and $L^j=0$ otherwise. Keep the differentials of $I$ below degree $d-1$ and corestrict $\partial^{d-1}$ to $J$ at the last differential; this is possible because $\partial^d\partial^{d-1}=0$. For $d=0$, simply take $L=J[0]$. The augmentation $A[0]\to L$ is $\varepsilon$ when $d>0$ and its factorization through $J$ when $d=0$. For $d>0$, exactness of the original augmented resolution gives $H^0(L)=A$, zero cohomology for $0<j<d$, and $H^d(L)=J/\operatorname{im}\partial^{d-1}=0$; for $d=0$, the augmentation $A\to J$ is an isomorphism by step 3. All other degrees are zero. Thus this augmentation is a quasi-isomorphism. Every term of $L$ is injective: the $I^j$ by construction, $J$ by step 5, and $0$ because every map into it extends uniquely [def-sp-injective-resolutions]. Quasi-isomorphisms become isomorphisms in [def-weil-abstract-derived-category], so $A[0]\cong L^\bullet$ in $D(A)$; take $a=0$ and $b=d$.
