## Review by Q82

I checked delivery-errors B--E against the actual files and sources, including the distinction between geometric inertia and a stabilizer of the underlying topological point. The field-valued point is essential: its inertia acts trivially on the residue field and on the scalar field of its line fibre. I verified that every cited statement supplies what the step uses, and put literal fragments in citations.json.

1. Step 1 uses the closed-immersion ideal definition for quasi-coherence and affine ideal sections; Noetherian rings make those ideals finitely generated. The geometric-point and residue-field definitions identify the fixed field-valued map and its induced embedding of the residue field. Conjugating commutators preserves K, so the fixed ideal is H-stable. The local diagonal equations give the difference generators of J; they do not require an invariant affine neighbourhood. Finitely many germs can always be represented after a common shrinking. This is an explicit definition-level calculation, not an invocation of fixed-locus regularity.
2. Step 2 uses the principalization node for the invertible extended ideal, and the universal-property node for the unique lift of each translated blowup map. Each translate has the same extended ideal because H preserves the centre. The product, identity and inverse laws follow by comparing their maps over the same base morphism and using that uniqueness. The line sheaf is the actual extended ideal, so its action is canonical and its section multiplication is equivariant.
3. Step 3 is elementary averaging, written in full. H fixes the residue field; tameness and K being a subgroup make n=|K| invertible. The classes of differences span J/mJ, and the finite sums cancel under the permutation v -> vu. Therefore the average is zero. For a K-equivariant quotient with trivial K-action, applying its map to the same average gives its original value. The calculation commutes with extending the residue field by its displayed scalar formula. No Maschke, normal-space identification, or invariants-exactness theorem is being silently used.
4. Step 4 uses the extended-ideal definition and the invertible-ideal definition. Locally the extended ideal is generated by the images of the centre ideal; reducing these generators in a local rank-one trivialization proves the fibre map is surjective. This does not assert that the inclusion of that ideal into O_Y stays injective on its exceptional fibre. The line is a fibre of the module, and is nonzero even though its map into the residue field can be zero. If all H fixes the geometric point, its action on that module fibre is linear; a choice of nonzero vector gives a character. Its commutators are 1. Step 3 then contradicts the surjection. This is precisely the source's character contradiction in dual form.
5. Step 5 applies the same final-object property to W using its assumed invertible extended ideal. Both composites in the equivariance comparison lie over h p and that extended ideal is again invertible. There is no appeal to normalization or a statement for normalized blowups: ordinary blowups suffice.
6. Step 6 writes the stabilizer inclusion on geometric maps explicitly. A geometric stabilizer at w maps into the already proper stabilizer at r(w); hence it is proper. This is an elementary subgroup inclusion, not another instance of the item assumed as a lemma.
