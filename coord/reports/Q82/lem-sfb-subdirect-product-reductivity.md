## Review by Q82

I checked the actual Milne 23.20 proof against the statement and each use, rather than assuming that the source's word “surjective” means smooth or separable. For step 1, sbc-lem-smooth-geometrically-reduced explicitly states geometrically reduced fibres for a smooth morphism. Applying it to the structure morphism over the field F makes the fibre the entire group. def-pc-reduced-scheme explicitly gives reduced rings of sections on every open, so the affine coordinate ring is reduced. This is the only off-unit non-Mathlib citation; it is declared as a generic tool use supporting the existing route.

For step 2, affine-homomorphism-flat-image explicitly supplies both a faithfully flat homomorphism and a closed immersion for arbitrary finite-type affine group schemes over a field, with no smooth-kernel assumption. def-weil-closed-immersion explicitly gives the affine quotient description. Topological surjectivity forces its defining ideal into every prime, and zero-locus-eq-iff-radical-eq explicitly identifies the prime intersection with the radical. Reducedness from step 1 kills that ideal. Thus the projection itself has the faithful-flatness hypothesis required later; no unproved openness, separability or geometric-point lifting has slipped in.

For step 3, I read the full statement of unipotent-normal-quotient-image: it requires a faithfully flat ambient map, a smooth connected normal unipotent N, and an independently supplied faithfully-flat/closed-image factorization of its restriction. The first comes from step 2, the second is the arbitrary subgroup being tested for reductivity, and the third is another application of affine-homomorphism-flat-image. F is algebraically closed and all these groups are affine of finite type, exactly as the statements require. Its conclusion explicitly gives smoothness, connectedness, unipotence and normality of the closed image. I take no assertion from either node's proof.

For step 4, each reductive factor therefore forces that entire closed image group scheme to be trivial, using the inline definition in the item. def-sc-group-scheme-subgroup states injectivity on every algebra's points. Applying it to both subgroup inclusions makes their composite injective; since its two coordinates are identities, every element of N(R) is the identity. This works on nonreduced R, so it is not a deduction of scheme-theoretic equality merely from F-points. For step 5, def-weil-affine-spectrum gives the exact correspondence between affine morphisms and coordinate-ring maps. Testing at R=F[N] equates its universal point with its identity point and gives id=unit composed with counit. The reverse composition is already id_F. Hence the coordinate algebra is F, so N is the trivial group scheme. All subgroup tests are exhausted and H already has the assumed smoothness and connectedness.
