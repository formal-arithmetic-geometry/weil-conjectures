Step 4 uses exactly the new vector/wound-factor statement. F is smooth connected commutative and killed by p by step 3; it is unipotent by restricting the faithful geometric-unipotent representation of U. The source theorem gives the canonical factor C and the equivalence C=1 iff F is wound. Thus d>0. Step 5 uses the new cckp node's largest-subgroup characterization and compatibility with separable extension, the new vector-factor node's smallest-containing-subgroup characterization over k_s, and the existing centre-base-change node's equality Z(U)_K=Z(U_K). In characteristic zero C_K is the identity component because it is connected and open-and-closed; automorphisms preserve the identity. In positive characteristic an automorphism preserves F_K, then permutes pointed affine-line morphisms into F_K, and so preserves their smallest containing closed subgroup C_K. Applying this to each action automorphism given by E(K) proves equality of closed subgroup schemes, not just a statement about individual C(K)-points.

Step 6 cites field-extension-tensor-injective, whose statement is the exact injectivity k[C] -> k[C] tensor K used to descend the vanishing coefficients, plus the definitions of G_m and closed subgroup schemes. It is elementary: for each equation f of C, the restricted action pulls it back to a finite Laurent polynomial with coefficients in k[C]. Evaluation at every e in E(K) gives the zero function on C_K by step 5. A basis of the coefficient span and the polynomial root bound over the infinite separably closed K kill every coefficient. This proves factorization of the morphism on coordinate rings, hence invariance on all test algebras. The same calculation for inverse e, or the inherited action identities, gives automorphisms on C. It avoids incorrectly replacing scheme invariance by finite-field rational-point invariance.
