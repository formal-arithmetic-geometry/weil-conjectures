### Original Step 1: assigned to lem-trf-module-pushout-quotient-presentation

Put $D=Q'\oplus Q$ [def-weil-pbc-direct-sum-modules], $\delta:P\to D$, $\delta(x)=(b(x),-a(x))$, and $E=D/\delta(P)$ with quotient $\pi:D\to E$ [def-weil-pbc-quotient-module]. Define $j(y)=\pi(y,0)$ and $k(z)=\pi(0,z)$. Then $jb=ka$. For maps $u:Q'\to T$, $v:Q\to T$ satisfying $ub=va$, the linear map $(y,z)\mapsto u(y)+v(z)$ kills $\delta(P)$, so descends uniquely to $E\to T$ with restrictions $u,v$; uniqueness holds because every class is $j(y)+k(z)$. Thus $E$ has the pushout universal property, the colimit of the finite span [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit]. The unique comparison $E\to Q''$ preserving both legs is an isomorphism: the reverse comparison exists by the same property, and both composites fix the two legs, hence are identities. Under this isomorphism $j$ is the given $b'$. Also $P\xrightarrow{\delta}D\xrightarrow{\pi}E\to0$ is exact since $\pi$ is surjective with kernel $\delta(P)$.

### Original Step 2: assigned to lem-trf-quotient-pair-tensor-kernel

Fix an arbitrary $R$-module $L$ and write $h_L=h\otimes_R\mathrm{id}_L$ for every linear map $h$. The tensor universal property [def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product] gives inverse linear maps $F:D\otimes_RL\to(Q'\otimes_RL)\oplus(Q\otimes_RL)$ and $G$ by $F((y,z)\otimes l)=(y\otimes l,z\otimes l)$ and $G(s,t)=(\iota_1)_L(s)+(\iota_2)_L(t)$, where $\iota_1(y)=(y,0)$ and $\iota_2(z)=(0,z)$. The composites are identities on pure tensors by $(y,z)=(y,0)+(0,z)$, and therefore everywhere by generation by pure tensors. In these coordinates $F\delta_L(w)=(b_L(w),-a_L(w))$ and $\pi_LG(s,0)=j_L(s)$; these identities follow on pure tensors and extend linearly.

### Original Step 3: assigned to lem-trf-quotient-pair-tensor-kernel

Apply tensor right exactness [lem-weil-pbc-tensor-right-exact] to the exact sequence $P\xrightarrow{\delta}D\xrightarrow{\pi}E\to0$ of Step 1 and the arbitrary module $L$. It gives $\ker\pi_L=\operatorname{im}\delta_L$. Its hypotheses are exactly the surjectivity and kernel equality already checked; it requires neither $L$ flat nor $\delta$ injective.

### Original Step 4: assigned to lem-trf-quotient-pair-tensor-kernel

We compute $\ker j_L=b_L(\ker a_L)$. If $j_L(s)=0$, Step 2 gives $\pi_LG(s,0)=0$, and Step 3 supplies $w\in P\otimes_RL$ with $G(s,0)=\delta_L(w)$. Applying $F$ yields $(s,0)=(b_L(w),-a_L(w))$, so $a_L(w)=0$ and $s=b_L(w)$. Conversely, if $a_L(w)=0$, Step 2 gives $G(b_L(w),0)=\delta_L(w)$, and applying $\pi_L$ gives $j_Lb_L(w)=0$ because $\pi\delta=0$. This proves both inclusions directly, without identifying $\ker(a_L)$ with $(\ker a)\otimes_RL$.

### Original Step 5: assigned to the parent assembly, Step 3
