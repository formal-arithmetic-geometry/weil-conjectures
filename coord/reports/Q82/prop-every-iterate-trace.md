## Original seven arguments (verbatim baseline)

### Returned proof step 1

Fix $n\geq1$. By [lem-weil-point-counts-finite](1), $P=Y_0(\mathbf F_{q^n})$ is finite. By [prop-cohomological-finiteness], the $E$-spaces $H_c^i(Y,K)$ are finite-dimensional and vanish outside a finite interval, and their geometric Frobenius is an automorphism. By [def-weil-local-frobenius-trace], the stalk cohomology spaces are the bounded finite-dimensional cohomology of $C_{\bar x}=\operatorname{Fib}_s(L\bar x^*\operatorname{For}K)$, and the local alternating trace is precisely the displayed sum over $j$. Thus every trace exists; the global sum is finite, and the local sums, over finitely many points each with bounded stalk cohomology, are finite.

### Returned proof step 2

Use [lem-zl-general-condensed-weil-realization] to realize the finite-stage E-Weil datum K as a model-Frobenius Weil object in the bounded constructible condensed Qbar_l coefficient category. The node supplies the compact-support and fixed-stalk comparison isomorphisms, intertwines the model globalOperator and stalkFrobenius with the scalar extensions of F^n and F_x^(n), and identifies the model-Frobenius fixed points with Y_0(F_{q^n}). By step 1 and the final clause of that comparison, the fixed-point set is finite and the condensed global and stalk objects have right duals. Therefore all hypotheses of [nt-assume-trace-formula] hold, and it gives equality of the categorical global trace with the finite sum of categorical stalk traces in D^b_c(s)[[Qbar_l]]. We use the assumption only for this categorical identity; it does not itself identify categorical traces with alternating cohomology traces.

### Returned proof step 3

Transport the categorical equality of step 2 through the comparison isomorphisms of [lem-zl-general-condensed-weil-realization]. Clause 3 of [lem-zl-condensed-point-supertrace] says that the categorical trace of each realized global or stalk operator is the image under the injective coefficient map $E\to\operatorname{End}(\Qlb_s)$ of the corresponding alternating E-valued cohomological trace; its finite-dimensional hypotheses are exactly those established in step 1. The comparison node intertwines powers of the actual operators, so the categorical equality is the image under the same coefficient map of the two finite E-valued sums displayed in the statement. Injectivity gives the asserted E-valued identity after identifying End(Qbar_l_s) with Qbar_l. No representative-independence or trace-formula theorem is used in this transport.

### Returned proof step 4

Steps 2 and 3 give equality of the images of the two finite $E$-valued sums under the injective coefficient map $\epsilon_E$ of [lem-zl-condensed-point-supertrace] (cited in step 3). Its kernel is zero, so their difference is zero already in $E$. This proves the first identity. Steps 2 and 3 hold for every selection of geometric points $\bar x$ over the $x\in P$, since the stalk comparison of [lem-zl-general-condensed-weil-realization] is stated for every geometric point over $x$. This proves independence of the local alternating traces from the choices: compare two selections differing only at a fixed $x\in P$; both total sums equal the same global trace, and cancelling the other finitely many summands gives equality of the traces at $x$. Any two choices at $x$ can be compared this way. If $P$ is empty there are no local choices and the formula says that the global alternating trace is zero.

### Returned proof step 5

For the canonical constant Weil sheaf $E_Y$, the local complex is $E[0]$. The construction in [def-weil-constant-weil-structure] sends every constant section $a$ to that same section; the restrictions and insertions defining [def-weil-frobenius-point-transport] also retain $a$. In the composite of [def-weil-local-frobenius-trace], each point transport followed by the constant Weil comparison therefore acts as $a\mapsto a$; repeating this $n$ times still gives the identity. These computations hold for the constant reductions, their completed integral unit, and its rationalization defining $E_Y$. Thus the only nonzero stalk cohomology is $E$ in degree zero and $F_x^{(n)}=1$. Its matrix is $(1)$, whose trace is $1$ by [def-weil-endomorphism-trace]. Substituting in step 4 makes the left side $\sum_{x\in P}1=\#P=N_n(Y_0)$ by [def-weil-point-counts-zeta], giving the second identity.

### Returned proof step 6

If $Y_0$ is proper, [prop-cohomological-finiteness] gives a Frobenius-equivariant canonical isomorphism $u_i:H_c^i(Y,K)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^i(Y,K)$. From $u_iF_c=Fu_i$, induction on $n$ gives $u_iF_c^n=F^nu_i$. These are conjugate finite-dimensional operators, so their traces agree by the basis-independent matrix trace of [def-weil-endomorphism-trace]. Replacing each summand in step 4 (and in its constant-sheaf specialization) gives the asserted ordinary-cohomology version.

### Returned proof step 7

Finally take $Y_0=X_0$, $E=\Ql$ and the canonical constant sheaf. Step 6 applies because $X_0$ is proper. Under exactly the smooth, proper, geometrically connected, pure-dimension-$d$ hypotheses in the statement, [prop-cohomological-finiteness] gives $H^i(X,\Ql)=0$ for $i\notin[0,2d]$. The trace on the zero space is zero (the empty sum of diagonal coefficients in [def-weil-endomorphism-trace]); discard these terms. [def-weil-smooth-proper-cohomological-family] defines $V_{i,\ell}=H^i(X,\Ql)$ and $F_{i,\ell}=F_X|_{V_{i,\ell}}$, so the remaining sum is exactly $\sum_{i=0}^{2d}(-1)^i\operatorname{Tr}(F_{i,\ell}^n\mid V_{i,\ell})$. This proves the last formula, including $d=0$.
