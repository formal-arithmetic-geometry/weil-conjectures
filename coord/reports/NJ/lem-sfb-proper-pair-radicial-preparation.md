For C4 I opened and read every cited node's statement, not only its title. citations.json quotes the full exact statement for each step/node occurrence, so that the hypotheses and the clause used remain visible. In particular, the normalization existence statement supplies finite/surjective/birational, but does not itself state normality: Step 8 proves normality from the integral-closure and localization nodes. Likewise the reduced-geometric-generic-stalk criterion is a theorem, not a definition, and the Chow theorem really supplies both an immersion and an isomorphism over a nonempty open. The following are the substantive checks of the steps.

1. The Nagata theorem applies to the separated finite-type map to the qcqs point Spec k, and its cited compactification definition gives density. Proper includes finite type. The Noetherian and finite-components statements apply over a field. The closure C of Z restricts to Z in X. Every component's generic point lies in the dense open X, and none lies in Z; this proves that C is nowhere dense rather than merely closed. These closure/generic-point arguments are written in the step.
2. The 04KT node applies to the entire finite-type compactification with finitely many components; therefore it gives one finite purely inseparable e, not an unrelated field for each component. The reduced induced structure node supplies a genuine closed reduced subscheme. Base-change properness and properness of closed subschemes give properness over e, hence finite type and Noetherian. The characteristic-zero clause follows from the explicit purely-inseparable definition.
3. Finite base change, finite closed immersion and finite composition give finiteness of t. The pure-inseparable homeomorphism result applies before reduction, which does not change points. The universally-injective field-map and base-change statements, followed by the radicial equivalence, give the residue-field condition. Residue fields of a finite fibre algebra are finite-dimensional. Thus generic correspondence includes finiteness and purely inseparability, not only a topological bijection.
4. Reduced induced components are integral and nonempty, with their closed immersions giving properness. Finiteness of components and nonemptiness have already been established. On Noetherian affines their reduced closed markings have finitely generated ideals; hence the blowup hypotheses are met. No component lies inside its marking, and the complement is dense and quasi-compact.
5. The blowup properness, complement isomorphism, principalization and admissible-density statements say exactly what is used. The ideals are finite-type quasi-coherent. The base is qcqs and its chosen open is dense quasi-compact; the ideal restricts to the unit ideal there, so the blowup is admissible. Principalization supplies a non-zero-divisor, not just an invertible module.
6. The affine chart-cover statement and the chart-ring definition identify charts as subrings of localizations of domains. Nonzero-denominator charts are domains and zero-denominator charts empty. The previously established dense irreducible open then makes the whole blowup irreducible and reduced, hence integral and birational; the cited proper-composition statement gives properness over e.
7. The integral Chow theorem applies because the blowup is integral, separated and finite type over e. Its nonempty isomorphism open gives birationality. Its proper surjection combined with properness of the base makes Y_i proper over e. The proper-to-separated theorem makes the supplied immersion into projective space proper, and the proper-immersion statement makes it closed. Thus it really gives the absolute projective embedding used next, without assuming that the original compactification is projective.
8. The normalization theorem requires integral finite type over a field, verified for Y_i. It gives finite/surjective/birational. The definition gives integral-closure affine charts; the normal integral-closure theorem is applied with the identity finite extension of the fraction field, and the localization theorem gives normal local rings. Finite-over-projective is applied over Spec e, which is affine Noetherian, exactly its global-embedding special case. Projective implies proper and proper includes finite type.
9. This is the explicit elementary dominant-integral Cartier pullback argument of 02OO(2). The generic-field injections from the affine-fraction statement make every pulled-back local generator nonzero in a domain, hence a non-zero-divisor. Local generators still generate the image ideal and their vanishing sets are the inverse images. No flat pullback theorem is assumed. The Cartier ideal definition is sufficient for the conclusion once these generators have been checked.
10. I checked the potentially delicate component issue: a reduced component of a geometrically reduced scheme is not simply declared geometrically reduced. Instead take an open affine around its generic point avoiding all other finitely many components. This is geometrically reduced as an open of T, and its fraction field remains reduced after any extension because tensoring commutes with localization and reducedness survives localization. That field is unchanged by all three birational maps. The cited generic-stalk criterion then applies to the already reduced N_i. It requires no unproved normal-implies-geometrically-reduced inference.
11. The coproduct statement supplies the scheme, maps and open-and-closed summands. Normality, geometric reducedness and the Cartier ideal are local on those summands, with explicit generators from Step 9. Each component map is surjective: the blowup's proper image is closed and contains its dense complement, while Chow and normalization are already surjective. Birationality gives exactly one unchanged generic field per T_i. Step 3 supplies the finite purely inseparable generic extension to the corresponding component of the compactification.
12. The finite disjoint union is embedded by placing each existing closed projective embedding in its own coordinate block. Different blocks have empty intersection, since all coordinates would vanish there; locally a finite scheme-theoretic union is therefore just one block. The finite union of the closed images is closed, and these local descriptions identify it with the coproduct. The projective definition and projective-implies-proper result then give proper finite-type B/e. This is an explicit embedding argument, not an invocation of an uncited general projective-composition statement.
13. The finite-projective-bundle theorem applies to Spec e over Spec k. The ample definition is verified on Spec k by the single section 1 of the structure sheaf. Thus the bundle-to-H-projective conversion applies. The H-projective-composition theorem has two H-projective inputs, Step 12 and this finite field map, and gives the required projective embedding over k. I did not confuse the two conventions over a general base.
14. Separatedness of the proper compactification makes its diagonal closed. Both the graph and the product of the absolute embedding with the identity are base changes of closed immersions. Affine ring surjections compose, so their composite is closed. Base-change projective space and the relative projective-space definition identify this with the projective bundle of the finite free module. Thus r is projective in precisely the statement's convention; surjectivity, fields and the Cartier divisor were proved earlier.
