### Returned step 1

First suppose $A\ne0$. Every ideal of $A$ is a finite set, hence generated by its elements, so $A$ is Noetherian. Every finite $A$-module $M$ is finitely presented: choose a surjection $A^r\to M$ from a finite list of generators; its kernel is a subset of the finite set $A^r$ and is therefore finitely generated. The element $\ell^n$ is invertible in $k$ because $p\ne\ell$. Both $U$ and $X$ are qcqs by the separated finite-type-over-a-field criterion. The qcqs-morphism permanence lemma then makes $j$ qcqs. These are the hypotheses needed for the constructible comparison and bounded-below direct-image comparison below.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-over-affine, lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence.

### Returned step 2

Use the finite-discrete module-object/ordinary-coefficient equivalence on $U$ and $X$, denoting its derived forward functors by $E_T$. It identifies $p_T^*\underline A$ with the constant ring sheaf, preserves each Tor interval against all degree-zero modules, and identifies finite constant local models and bounded constructibility. Thus $H=E_U(K)$ is an ordinary bounded constructible pro-étale $A$-complex with Tor interval $[a,b]$. The constructible comparison gives a bounded constructible étale $A$-complex $L$ and an isomorphism $H\simeq\nu_U^*L$. Apply the Noetherian Tor-interval comparison to $L$: its inverse-image interval is $[a,b]$, so the étale interval of $L$ is also $[a,b]$. In particular $L$ belongs to both $D^+$ and $D^-$.

Cited nodes: lem-sf-module-object-ordinary-coefficient-equivalence, sk-thm-pe-constructible-comparison, det-pe-finite-tor-comparison-noetherian.

### Returned step 3

Choose $d_j=\operatorname{cd}(j)$. The open-finiteness amplitude lemma says that this is finite and that $R^qj_{\mathrm{et},*}F=0$ for every $A$-module sheaf $F$ and every $q>d_j$. The definition of cohomological dimension makes $d_j$ a nonnegative integer. This choice uses only the fixed morphism, so it is independent of $K,a,b$. In particular this is a relative direct-image bound; no bound on $\operatorname{cd}(k)$ is used.

Cited nodes: det-geo-open-finiteness-ii-amplitude, def-kun-cd.

### Returned step 4

Put $Q=Rj_{\mathrm{et},*}L$ in the ordinary unbounded module derived category. For every finite $A$-module $M$, step 1 gives finite presentation. The finite-constant projection theorem applies: $A$ is Noetherian, $L\in D^-$, and step 3 bounds all higher module direct images. It gives the canonical isomorphism
$$Q\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}M_X\simeq Rj_{\mathrm{et},*}(L\otimes_A^{\mathbf L}M_U).$$
Now apply the arbitrary-field open Tor-bound theorem with $V=U$, $T=X$, $e=d_j$, and this $L\in D^+$. Its input Tor interval, uniform higher-direct-image bound and finite-module projection hypotheses have all been verified. It concludes that $Q$ has Tor interval $[a,b+d_j]$ against every étale module sheaf, not only finite or constant test sheaves.

Cited nodes: lem-sf-projection-finite-constant, lem-sf-open-tor-bound-arbitrary-field.

### Returned step 5

The finite-coefficient open-image finiteness theorem applies to $L$: the schemes are of finite type over the field $k$, $j$ is open, $A$ is finite, and its annihilator $\ell^n$ is invertible in $k$. Consequently $Q=Rj_{\mathrm{et},*}L$ is bounded constructible. This supplies the constructibility needed to apply the Noetherian Tor-interval comparison on $X$.

Cited nodes: cor-fin-deligne-open-finite-coefficients.
