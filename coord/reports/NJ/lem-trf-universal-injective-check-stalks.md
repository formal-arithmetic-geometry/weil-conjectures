### Former step 1, retained verbatim

Fix an R-module N and a prime q of A with p=q∩R, and put S=A∖q. By [def-weil-pbc-module-localization] every element of S acts bijectively on M_q, and every element of R∖p maps into S; hence every element of R∖p acts bijectively on M_q, so M_q is an R_p-module, and likewise M'_q. (a) The localization-tensor comparison [lem-weil-pbc-localization-tensor-over-base], applied to the A-module M, the multiplicative subset S and the R-module N, gives the A-isomorphism (M⊗_R N)_q ≅ M_q⊗_R N, (m⊗n)/s ↦ (m/s)⊗n, and the same for M'. On generators (u(m)⊗n)/s ↦ (u(m)/s)⊗n = u_q(m/s)⊗n, so under these isomorphisms the localization (u⊗_R id_N)_q is u_q⊗_R id_N. (b) Apply [lem-weil-pbc-localization-tensor-product] to R, the multiplicative subset R∖p, the localization R→R_p, the R-modules M_q and N, the identity M_q→M_q (a localization of M_q at R∖p, since R∖p acts bijectively on M_q, conditions (b),(c) being trivial for the identity) and the localization map N→N_p, n↦n/1. It says that λ:M_q⊗_R N→M_q⊗_{R_p}N_p, x⊗n↦x⊗(n/1), is a localization of M_q⊗_R N at R∖p. Since each s∈R∖p acts bijectively on M_q⊗_R N (s acts as (s·)⊗id_N, with inverse (s^{-1}·)⊗id_N), λ is injective by condition (c) and surjective by condition (b) (s^{-1}λ(z)=λ(s^{-1}z)); so λ is an isomorphism, and likewise for M'_q. On pure tensors λ'∘(u_q⊗_R id_N)=(u_q⊗_{R_p}id_{N_p})∘λ. Combining (a) and (b): under these isomorphisms (u⊗_R id_N)_q is u_q⊗_{R_p}id_{N_p}. If N is already an R_p-module, every element of R∖p acts bijectively on N, so the localization map N→N_p is bijective by [def-weil-pbc-module-localization] (n/1=0 iff tn=0 for some t∈R∖p, and n/s=(s^{-1}n)/1); hence N_p=N.

### Former step 2, retained verbatim

Assume every u_q is universally injective over R_p. For an arbitrary R-module N let K be the kernel of u⊗_R id_N, an A-submodule of M⊗_R N since u⊗_R id_N is A-linear. The sequence 0→K→M⊗_R N→M'⊗_R N of A-modules is exact at K and at M⊗_R N; by [lem-weil-pbc-localization-module-exact], applied at both places (the sequences 0→K→M⊗_R N and K→M⊗_R N→M'⊗_R N), its localization at A∖m is exact for every maximal ideal m of A. Step 1 with q=m and p=m∩R identifies the localized last map with u_m⊗_{R_p}id_{N_p}, which is injective by the hypothesis applied to the R_p-module N_p. So K_m injects into the kernel of an injective map, and K_m=0 for every maximal m. The zero map K→0 is therefore bijective after every maximal localization, so [lem-weil-pbc-linear-map-bijective-at-maximal-localizations] gives that K→0 is bijective, i.e. K=0. Thus u⊗_R id_N is injective for every N, so u is universally injective over R.

### Former step 3, retained verbatim

Conversely assume u is universally injective over R. Fix q∈Spec(A), put p=q∩R, and let L be any R_p-module, regarded as an R-module by restriction of scalars. The A-linear map u⊗_R id_L is injective, i.e. 0→M⊗_R L→M'⊗_R L is exact. Its localization at A∖q is exact by [lem-weil-pbc-localization-module-exact], so (u⊗_R id_L)_q is injective. Step 1 with N=L, together with L_p=L (Step 1, last sentence), identifies this localized map with u_q⊗_{R_p}id_L. Thus u_q⊗_{R_p}id_L is injective for every R_p-module L, which is universal injectivity of u_q over R_p.
