I checked the apparent constant-sheaf discrepancy: a continuous function on the quotient space of components with values in a discrete finite module is precisely a locally constant function on the underlying scheme, and clopen fibers give local constant sections. Thus it gives the constant quotient sheaf. No flatness of A_T is inferred just from a definition.

Step 2 cites prop-weil-pbc-koszul-reconstruction and the coefficient datum. I read its full statement: it explicitly says both that a complete K maps isomorphically to the quotient-tower derived limit and that the completion maps correspond. Those are the two conclusions used here. The tower is now exactly the K-flat quotient tower of the datum, rather than an arbitrary inverse sequence. Completeness is a hypothesis, not a conclusion manufactured from normalization.

Step 3 cites those same statements for eta and K_a. Under extension/restriction adjunction, p_a eta is the reduction unit. Consequently epsilon_a composed with eta tensor 1 is the identity on K_a. This proves epsilon_a is the inverse of eta tensor 1, which is invertible since eta is invertible. It establishes the specified map rather than claiming that an isomorphism of Roos triangles makes evaluation invertible. All proof steps, including this one, are marked non-elementary. The previous Step 3's inference from normalization to evaluation being invertible has been removed entirely.

Step 4 cites the revised product lemma and the Roos proposition. The product lemma is in exactly D(Mod(T_proet,A_T)) and accepts the bounded representative supplied in Step 1. The Roos statement supplies both product occurrences and the shift from the transition maps. Projecting to factor n checks compatibility with 1-s: both paths use the (n+1)-st projection followed by the same transition tensored with the identity. This proves the asserted product compatibility without claiming the reduced tower is constant or that a Roos triangle alone proves finite reduction.

Constant sheaf exactness takes a finite-projective resolution to a resolution; extension along the flat sheaf algebra preserves it, and split finite-free presentations remain split.
