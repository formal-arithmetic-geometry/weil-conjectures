### Original step 1 → parent step 1

By [lem-pod-separated-finite-type-base-change], $U$ and $X$ are separated finite-type $k$-schemes. All the geometric hypotheses of the coefficient comparisons hold. The schemes $U$ and $X$ are Noetherian by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian]: apply it over the field $k$, whose only ideals are $0$ and $k$. For every affine open $V\subset X$, its intersection with $U$ is an open subscheme of the Noetherian scheme $X$, so is quasi-compact by [lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-open-subscheme-noetherian]. Thus $j$ is quasi-compact. Choose a closed immersion $i:Z\hookrightarrow X$ with image $X\setminus U$ by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-subset-closed-subscheme]; the same choice may be made over $k_0$ and base changed. The finite-type, separated and prime-to-characteristic hypotheses are those of the statement; no properness of $j$ is required.

### Original step 2 → lem-zl-open-compact-support-standard-bounds

Fix a finite coefficient field $E$, write $R=\mathcal O_E$, and let $K\in D^b_c(U,R)$. With the compactification $(j,\operatorname{id}_X)$, [def-weil-integral-rational-qbar-compact-support] defines $Rj_!K$ as the total derived limit of the extensions by zero of its reduction tower. The comparison $d_j(K)$ in [lem-sf-integral-open-reduction-comparison] identifies exactly this total limit with localized ringed-site extension by zero for the completed coefficient sheaves. It respects the strict quotient transitions and the open adjunction unit and counit. By [lem-pur-site-restriction-module-adjunction-exact] this latter functor is exact on module sheaves. In particular, for its termwise action, kernels, images and their quotients give $\mathcal H^r(j_!K)=j_!\mathcal H^r(K)$ in every degree. The total limit lies in $D^b_c(X,R)$ by [lem-sf-integral-compact-support-constructible], applied to the same compactification. Thus it is the genuine constructible integral functor, and preserves both standard cohomological bounds.

### Original step 3 → lem-zl-open-compact-support-standard-bounds

The classes $D^{\le0}$ and $D^{\ge0}$ in the rationalized category are the isomorphism closures of the corresponding integral classes, and at $\Qlb$ they are the isomorphism closures of the finite-field-stage classes [def-weil-constructible-standard-tstructure]. The functor at $E$ is the rationalization of the functor of step 2, and at $\Qlb$ it is $(E,K)\mapsto(E,Rj_!K)$ [def-weil-integral-rational-qbar-compact-support]. Hence each of the two classes is preserved: choose a representative in the requisite class, apply step 2, then apply the functor to the representing isomorphism. Compatibility with coefficient extension and the descent of the functor and comparison maps are supplied by clause (1) of [prop-functor-package]. Their intersection, the constructible heart [def-weil-constructible-heart], is therefore preserved. This establishes that the output is a sheaf without treating $\Qlb$ as a discrete non-torsion coefficient ring on the small etale site.

### Original step 5 → lem-zl-open-weil-iterate-comparison

Let $g_U,g_X$ be geometric Frobenius. Their squares with $j$, and the squares of their powers, are Cartesian by [lem-trf-frobenius-open-square]. The canonical base-change isomorphism $b:g_X^*j_!\simeq j_!g_U^*$ of [prop-functor-package], with its coefficient compatibilities and equivariance, defines the output Weil structure by $\theta'=j_!\theta\circ b$. A Weil morphism $a$ satisfies $a\theta=\theta_1 g_U^*a$ [def-weil-weil-sheaf-complex]; applying $j_!$ and naturality of $b$ gives the same equation for $j_!a$. Thus this is a functor on Weil sheaves. Restrict $b$ to $U$: it is the identity comparison under step 4, as it is at finite levels. Consequently $j^*\theta'=\theta$ under $j^*j_!\simeq\operatorname{id}$. For a closed $x\in U_0$ of degree $d$, the pasted comparison for $g_X^d$ restricts in the same way, so $j^*(\theta'^{[d]})=\theta^{[d]}$. In the formula for $F_x$ of [def-weil-local-frobenius-trace], the geometric point, its return $g_X^d\bar x=\bar xg_s^d$, and the point transport are the same after factoring through $U$; naturality identifies the final map induced by $\theta'^{[d]}$ with that induced by $\theta^{[d]}$. Thus the stalk identification conjugates the two local operators. At a closed point outside $U_0$ the stalk is zero.

### Original step 7 → parent step 6

For a closed point $x\in U_0$, its residue field and hence $q_x$ do not change under the open immersion. Step 5 gives identical local eigenvalues. Thus each inequality $|\iota(\alpha)|\le q_x^{w/2}$, or equality $|\iota(\alpha)|=q_x^{w/2}$, is unchanged. For $x\notin U_0$ the zero vector space has no eigenvalues, so both universally quantified conditions are vacuous. These are precisely the punctual upper bound specified in the statement and the purity condition of [def-weil-punctual-weights]. This includes the zero sheaf, empty open immersion and identity open immersion.
