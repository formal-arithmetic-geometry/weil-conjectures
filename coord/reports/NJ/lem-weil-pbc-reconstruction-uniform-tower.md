## Mathematical justification for the new tools
For immersion limit preservation I read proetale.tex lemma-closed-immersion, Tag 09AA(1), and definition-extension-zero, Tag 09AE. Closed inverse image preserves limits there; weakly-etale inverse image is restriction to a localized site, hence preserves limits. A locally closed immersion is their composite. Abelian and module limits are computed on the underlying sheaves, so transporting their operations preserves the comparison. Exactness is the ordinary inverse-image exactness, with coefficient ring the inverse image ring. No claim about arbitrary scheme morphisms is made.

For stratum detection I read proetale.tex lemma-ses-associated-to-open, Tag 09AH, including the quasi-compactness hypothesis. On a Noetherian scheme there are finitely many irreducible components. In each component the locally closed stratum containing its generic point contains a nonempty open portion. Remove the other components' intersections and the closed complements of these portions to obtain a dense open W that is a disjoint union of open portions lying in strata. Their restrictions detect F|W by the sheaf axiom. The complement is a proper closed Noetherian subscheme. Noetherian induction detects F there. Since W->X is quasi-compact on a Noetherian scheme, 09AH then gives F=0. This proves detection for arbitrary abelian sheaves and hence underlying module sheaves; exact inverse image identifies cohomology of a restricted complex with restricted cohomology. Apply the sheaf statement to all cohomology sheaves, or to the cohomology of a morphism's cone. This is the agent induction underlying the new statement, not a claim that 09AH literally states a stratification theorem. It works for an empty scheme, an empty stratum, one stratum and arbitrary pro-etale covering families.

For torsion-limit completeness I read proetale.tex Section 099P, the paragraph preceding proposition-enough-weakly-contractibles, and all of that proposition (099Q), especially (1), (4), (5). A sheaf killed by I^n is derived complete as a constant-A complex. Clause (4) applies to the countable inverse sequence without a surjectivity assumption. Locality follows from the slice-site definition of completeness. A bounded complex of complete terms is built from finitely many shifts of complete sheaves by stupid truncation triangles; the local RHom vanishing defining completeness is closed under those triangles. Alternatively clause (1) and (5) give the same result. This theorem remains in the constant coefficient category. The existing constant-completion-equivalence node, whose statement I read fully, supplies the transfer to completed coefficients and the agreement with the Koszul unit; no silent identification of ambient categories is used. Zero modules, I=0, I=A, and r=0 satisfy the stated conclusion.

For sequential evaluation fullness I read injectives.tex theorem-K-injective-embedding-grothendieck, Tag 079P, and derived.tex lemma-K-injective, Tag 070I. Here is the missing argument, rather than an unsupported attribution to those tags. Represent X by a strict tower complex and resolve Y by a K-injective tower complex J with injective tower terms, using the Grothendieck property of small module diagrams. Evaluation of J is K-injective: evaluation has an exact left adjoint L_n, with (L_nB)_m=B for m<=n and zero otherwise, so a map from an acyclic complex into J_n is a map from an acyclic L_n-complex into J and is null-homotopic. This agrees with the existing lem-weil-tower-evaluation-kinjective.

Transitions of each injective tower term are split epimorphisms. To see this for an arbitrary abelian category of module sheaves, embed a tower T into a product of right-adjoint evaluation towers R_n I_n, where T_n embeds into an injective I_n and (R_n I_n)_m=I_n for m>=n and zero otherwise. The component at level m contains the monomorphism to I_m, hence this is a monomorphism. Each R_n I_n is injective because evaluation is exact. At level m the product is the finite product of I_n for n<=m, and adjacent transitions are projections, with explicit insert-zero sections. If T itself is injective its embedding splits as a tower map. Composing the product's graded sections with this splitting supplies a graded section of T_{m+1}->T_m. This proves the property needed of J in every cochain degree; no chain-map section is claimed.

For each f_n choose a chain representative a_n:X_n->J_n, possible by 070I. Start with a_1. Suppose a_n has been chosen. The difference t^J_n a_{n+1}-a_n t^X_n is zero in the derived category by the assumed compatibility, hence is null-homotopic in J_n by 070I: it equals dH+Hd for a graded map H:X_{n+1}^q->J_n^{q-1}. Lift H degreewise along the split epimorphism J_{n+1}^{q-1}->J_n^{q-1} to a graded map Htilde. Replace a_{n+1} by a_{n+1}-(dHtilde+Htilded). Its homotopy class is unchanged and the adjacent square now commutes strictly. Induction over positive integers yields a strict tower chain map X->J, whose localization is the required lift. If all f_n are isomorphisms, its cone has zero cohomology at every evaluation; cohomology is levelwise, so its cone is zero in D(Tow), making the lift invertible. This proves EXISTENCE, not uniqueness or a natural section of the evaluation map. Zero towers and constant towers pass; finite number of stages uses the same induction. The Mathlib fullness hits concern singleFunctor or precomposition with localization, not this evaluation functor, and are not matching leaves.
