The same statement constructs the union open; lem-a1-space-point-open-calculus explicitly reflects inclusion of point images to inclusion of subfunctors, giving X_i contained in J. I checked this is an assertion of the cited statements, not merely a construction in their proofs. No quasi-separatedness is required for either statement.

Step 2 reads def-a1-space-open-subspace literally: testing on a scheme represents the inverse image of X_i by an open subscheme. The identification of the two fibre products is an identity of pairs of sections because both maps factor through the subfunctor J. It needs no gluing or separation lemma. The arbitrary-base-change clause of lem-a1-space-open-factor-scheme-tests says that the point image of the pullback is the inverse image of the open's point set. Thus the equality of the union with |J| becomes an actual open covering on every test scheme. The projection of the pullback supplies the local lift, so the criterion's covering hypothesis is verified for every section, not just for a chosen atlas. The factor lemma permits arbitrary test maps: no etaleness or flatness of the map T to J is assumed.

Step 3 cites def-a1-space-open-subspace for J being an algebraic space and def-trf-algebraic-space for the fppf sheaf condition. lem-pc-zariski-coverings-are-fppf states precisely that Zariski open coverings are fppf coverings, so compatible sections glue uniquely without any additional descent input. I then checked each of the four clauses of lem-pc-schemes-criterion: Zariski sheaf, a set of representable subfunctors, representable open inclusions, and a covering family. Its conclusion is representability over the same base S, so it gives an S-scheme, not merely a scheme after a field extension. The current latest blueprint criterion makes the ordinary small-value convention explicit; here J is a subfunctor of the fixed algebraic-space sheaf and the indexing set was obtained from the point set, within the project's universe convention. No collection of all scheme presentations is used as an index.
