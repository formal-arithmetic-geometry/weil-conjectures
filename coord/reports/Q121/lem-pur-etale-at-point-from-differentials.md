### Original step 1 — lem-pur-coordinate-differentials-affine-chart

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-smooth-locally-standard-smooth] to the structural morphism $U\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ at $u$. The affine open in $\operatorname{Spec}k$ contains its unique point, so it is all of $\operatorname{Spec}k$. Obtain an affine neighbourhood $W=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq U$ of $u$. Unpack [def-weil-pbc-standard-smooth-algebra]: choose $n\ge r\ge0$ and an identification $A=k[y_1,\ldots,y_n]/(F_1,\ldots,F_r)$ such that the image of $\det(\partial F_a/\partial y_b)_{1\le a,b\le r}$ is a unit of $A$. Write $a_b\in A$ for the image of $y_b$, and let $\mathfrak q$ be the prime of $u$ in $A$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-smooth-locally-standard-smooth, def-weil-pbc-standard-smooth-algebra, def-weil-affine-spectrum.

### Original step 2 — lem-pur-coordinate-differentials-affine-chart

By [lem-weil-pbc-standard-smooth-kahler-basis], $\Omega_{A/k}$ has basis $da_{r+1},\ldots,da_n$. The affine description in [def-sc-relative-differential-sheaf] and the germ identification in [lem-weil-pbc-spec-tilde-stalk-localization] identify the differential fibre at $u$ with $\Omega_{A/k}\otimes_A K$, where $K=\kappa(u)$: on pure tensors, $((\omega/s)\otimes\lambda)\mapsto\omega\otimes\overline s^{-1}\lambda$, with inverse $\omega\otimes\lambda\mapsto(\omega/1)\otimes\lambda$. Here $s\notin\mathfrak q$, so its residue is invertible. Tensoring the basis and its coordinate inverse gives the basis $da_{r+1},\ldots,da_n$ of this fibre [def-weil-module-base-change]. The assumed basis $dh_1,\ldots,dh_d$ therefore has $d=n-r$ elements.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-standard-smooth-kahler-basis, def-sc-relative-differential-sheaf, lem-weil-pbc-spec-tilde-stalk-localization, def-weil-module-base-change.

### Original step 3 — lem-pur-coordinate-differentials-affine-chart

Choose polynomial lifts $H_j\in k[y_1,\ldots,y_n]$ of $h_j|_W\in A$. Put $R=k[T_1,\ldots,T_d]$, acting on $A$ by $T_j\mapsto h_j|_W$. This is precisely the map on affine coordinate rings of $h|_W$ [def-sbc-affine-space, def-weil-affine-spectrum]. There is an isomorphism of $R$-algebras $$A\cong R[y_1,\ldots,y_n]/(F_1,\ldots,F_r,H_1-T_1,\ldots,H_d-T_d).$$ Indeed, evaluation $T_j\mapsto H_j$ identifies $k[T,y]/(H_j-T_j)_j$ with $k[y]$, with inverse induced by inclusion of $k[y]$; quotienting by the $F_a$ gives the displayed isomorphism. There are $r+d=n$ equations.

Uses: def-sbc-affine-space, def-weil-affine-spectrum.

### Original step 4 — lem-pur-coordinate-graph-localized-jacobian

Let $J$ be the $n\times n$ Jacobian, with rows indexed by the equations $F_a,H_j-T_j$ and columns by $y_b$, and partition its rows and columns after the first $r$ entries. Denote its upper-left, upper-right, lower-left and lower-right blocks by $B,C,D,E$, respectively; $B$ has size $r\times r$. Reduce coefficients to $K$. The matrix $\overline B$ is invertible by step 1. The derivation rules [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials, def-weil-pbc-polynomial-partial-derivative] give $dP(a)=\sum_b(\partial P/\partial y_b)(a)da_b$: this follows for each monomial by repeated Leibniz and then for polynomials by additivity. Thus $dF_a(a)=0$ gives $\overline B\,da_{\le r}+\overline C\,da_{>r}=0$. Substitution into $dH_j(a)$ shows that the coefficient matrix of the assumed basis $dh_j$ in the basis $da_{>r}$ of step 2 is $\overline E-\overline D\overline B^{-1}\overline C$, which is invertible. Subtracting $\overline D\overline B^{-1}$ times the first block rows of $\overline J$ from the last block rows gives a block triangular matrix with invertible diagonal blocks. Consequently $\overline\Delta=\det\overline J\ne0$, where $\Delta=\det J\in k[y_1,\ldots,y_n]$. In particular its image $\delta\in A$ is not in $\mathfrak q$. These formulas use empty matrices with determinant $1$ when $r=0$ or $d=0$.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials, def-weil-pbc-polynomial-partial-derivative.

### Original step 5 — lem-pur-coordinate-graph-localized-jacobian

Localize at $\delta$ [def-weil-ring-localization-away]. The presentation of step 3 gives $$A_\delta\cong R[y_1,\ldots,y_n,t]/(F_1,\ldots,F_r,H_1-T_1,\ldots,H_d-T_d,\Delta t-1):$$ the map sends $t$ to $\delta^{-1}$, and the reverse map is the unique extension of the map from $A$ since the relation makes $\delta$ invertible. The Jacobian in the $n+1$ variables $(y_1,\ldots,y_n,t)$ has upper-left block $J$, upper-right zero column, lower-left row $t\,\nabla\Delta$, and lower-right entry $\Delta$, so its determinant is $\Delta\det J=\Delta^2$, whose image $\delta^2$ is a unit in $A_\delta$. This is a square Jacobian presentation [def-exc-jacobian-presentation] of the same $R$-algebra structure, not a new choice of coordinate functions.

Uses: def-weil-ring-localization-away, def-exc-jacobian-presentation.

### Original step 6 — item assembly

Apply the square-presentation implication of [lem-exc-etale-jacobian-presentation] to the $R$-algebra $A_\delta$ of step 5: $R\to A_\delta$ is etale of finite presentation. By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-ring-map-iff-etale-affine], the induced map $\operatorname{Spec}A_\delta\to\operatorname{Spec}R=\mathbf A^d_k$ is etale. By [lem-weil-pbc-principal-localization-open-immersion], $\operatorname{Spec}A_\delta$ identifies with the open subscheme $D_W(\delta)$ of $W$, which contains $u$ since $\delta\notin\mathfrak q$. Take $U'=D_W(\delta)\subseteq U$. Its map to affine space is $h|_{U'}$: the ring map still sends each $T_j$ to the restriction of the original $h_j$, by steps 3 and 5, and affine morphisms are determined by their ring maps [def-weil-affine-spectrum]. This proves the required open-neighbourhood assertion.

Uses: lem-exc-etale-jacobian-presentation, lem-weil-pbc-etale-ring-map-iff-etale-affine, lem-weil-pbc-principal-localization-open-immersion, def-weil-affine-spectrum.
