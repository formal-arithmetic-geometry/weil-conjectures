## Original returned proof, retained for argument accounting

### Returned step 1 (714 characters)

Write $L=g^{-1}$. The continuous-cocontinuous adjunction theorem identifies its underlying set sheaf with precomposition: $(LF)(V)=F(u(V))$. Applying this identity to addition, zero and negation identifies the same formula on abelian sheaves, on morphisms and on restriction maps. Thus there is a natural additive isomorphism $e_F:\Gamma(U,LF)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma(u(U),F)$, given by the identity on these sections. The constant-left-module inverse-image theorem with $\Lambda=\mathbf Z$ says that $L$ is exact and its termwise application induces inverse image on $D^+$. An abelian sheaf has a unique $\mathbf Z$-action, by repeated addition, so this specialization changes neither the sheaf nor the morphisms.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-continuous-cocontinuous-topos-adjunction, lem-pe-constant-left-module-inverse-image

### Returned step 2 (695 characters)

Both abelian sheaf categories have enough injectives by the site-sheaf theorem; the sites are small in the stated universe. Choose an integer $a$ below the cohomology of $M$. The bounded-below injective-representative theorem gives a complex $I^\bullet$ of injectives with $I^n=0$ for $n<a$ and $QI^\bullet\cong M$. By step 1, $LI^\bullet$ is bounded below with the same lower bound and represents $LM$. The right-derived injective-computation theorem, applied to the additive evaluation functors on both sites, supplies the ordinary derived sections and their natural comparison units. In particular its injective-complex clause gives $Q\Gamma(u(U),I^\bullet)\xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma(u(U),M)$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-site-sheaves-enough-injectives, lem-weil-pbc-dplus-injective-representative, lem-weil-pbc-right-derived-plus-injective

### Returned step 3 (769 characters)

For each $n$, apply the pulled-back-injective acyclicity theorem to $I^n$: its hypotheses are precisely continuity and cocontinuity of $u$ and injectivity of $I^n$, so $H^p(U,LI^n)=0$ for all $p>0$. Evaluation $\Gamma(U,-)$ is additive and left exact by the site-cohomology definition. Hence the acyclic-complex unit theorem (Leray) applied to the bounded-below complex $LI^\bullet$ gives a canonical isomorphism $Q\Gamma(U,LI^\bullet)\xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma(U,LM)$. The maps $e_{I^n}$ of step 1 form an isomorphism of complexes, since naturality applied to $d_I^n$ gives $e_{I^{n+1}}\Gamma(U,Ld_I^n)=\Gamma(u(U),d_I^n)e_{I^n}$. Combining its localization with the two computation isomorphisms gives the asserted isomorphism from $R\Gamma(U,LM)$ to $R\Gamma(u(U),M)$.

Uses: lem-trf-site-pullback-injective-evaluation-acyclic, def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology, src-right-derived-functor-plus-acyclic-unit

### Returned step 4 (960 characters)

To specify the arrow, let $r:LI^\bullet\to J^\bullet$ be a bounded-below injective replacement. The usual derived pullback of sections is represented by $\Gamma(u(U),I^\bullet)\xrightarrow{e_{I^\bullet}^{-1}}\Gamma(U,LI^\bullet)\xrightarrow{\Gamma(U,r)}\Gamma(U,J^\bullet)$. The second arrow is exactly the acyclic-complex comparison used in step 3, by naturality of the derived comparison unit with respect to $r$ and its injective-complex normalization. Thus the isomorphism of step 3 is the inverse of this canonical pullback, in the direction written in the statement. The same naturality and the naturality of $e$ give naturality in $M$: comparison units commute with coefficient maps, and their inverses do so as well. The uniqueness clause for right derived functors identifies these constructions after any change of injective representatives, so the conclusion concerns the ordinary derived-section functors, with no choice of a new cohomology theory.
