### Retained old Step 1

Apply clause (1) of [lem-weil-proetale-weakly-etale-localization] to $u:V\to B$: composition gives the equivalence $V_{\mathrm{proét}}\simeq B_{\mathrm{proét}}/V$, with identical covering families. Its assertion about morphisms between objects also makes every given $h:W\to V$ weakly étale, so the same result applies to $h$ and to $w=u\circ h$. Clause (1) of [lem-sfb-proetale-object-inverse-image-restriction], applied to abelian sheaves and ring sheaves, identifies inverse image along each of these maps with restriction. The auxiliary constant ring in that lemma can be chosen to be $\mathbf Z$; we use only clause (1), which applies to arbitrary abelian and ring sheaves, not its constant-coefficient module assertions. This is the same abelian inverse image as [def-sfb-finite-upper-star]: both it and [def-weil-proetale-inverse-image] are sheafified left Kan extension along the opposite of the same base-change functor. Thus write $u^{-1}F=F|_V$ with $(F|_V)(T/V)=F(T/B)$, using the natural restriction isomorphism, not an assertion of literal equality between chosen skeletons.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-proetale-weakly-etale-localization, lem-sfb-proetale-object-inverse-image-restriction, def-sfb-finite-upper-star, def-weil-proetale-inverse-image.

### Retained old Step 2

Put $F=\Lambda_B$. Under Step 1, [sk-def-sfb-coefficient-morphisms] supplies a ring-sheaf isomorphism $\beta_u:F|_V\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda_V$, namely $\alpha_u$ after the inverse-image restriction identification. Evaluating at $V/V$ gives $\theta_V=(\beta_u)_{V/V}:F(V)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda_V(V)$. This is the required ring isomorphism: on a site with terminal object, a compatible family of sections is determined by its section at that object, and every such section yields the family of its restrictions. Hence $\Lambda_V(V)=\Gamma(V,\Lambda_V)$. Restriction on the localized site has the original ring operations and restriction maps by [def-pur-localized-ringed-site]. The same construction defines $\theta_W$.

Cited nodes: sk-def-sfb-coefficient-morphisms, def-pur-localized-ringed-site.

### Retained old Step 3

We will compute using the coefficient mates $\eta$ of [def-sfb-eta]. In the restriction model, the adjunction unit at an object $T/B$ sends $a\in F(T)$ to its restriction along $\operatorname{pr}_T:T\times_BV\to T$. Indeed, in the colimit formula of [def-weil-proetale-inverse-image] the unit uses the index $(T,\mathrm{id}_{T\times_BV})$; the initial graph index of [lem-sfb-proetale-object-inverse-image-restriction] maps to this index by $\operatorname{pr}_T$, so the induced colimit identification sends it to $F(\operatorname{pr}_T)(a)$. Consequently $\eta_{u,T}(a)=(\beta_u)_{T\times_BV}\bigl(F(\operatorname{pr}_T)(a)\bigr)$. With $P=V\times_BV$ viewed over $V$ by its second projection and $\delta:V\to P$ the diagonal, naturality of $\beta_u$ gives
$$\Lambda_V(\delta)\eta_{u,V}(s)=\theta_V(s).$$
For $h:W\to V$, $V\times_VW=W$. Thus for a global section $r$ of $\Lambda_V$ the same unit computation gives $\eta_{h,V}(r)=\alpha_h(r|_W)=h^\sharp(r)$. The final equality also follows directly from the section-as-map description: pullback of $\mathbf Z\to\Lambda_V$, $1\mapsto r$, sends $1$ to $r|_W$ under the monoidal-unit identification; composing with $\alpha_h$ gives precisely the stated section map.

Cited nodes: def-sfb-eta, def-weil-proetale-inverse-image, lem-sfb-proetale-object-inverse-image-restriction.

### Retained old Step 4

Let $w=u\circ h$ and $\gamma:W\to V\times_BW$ have projections $h$ and $\mathrm{id}_W$. Applying the unit formula of Step 3 with $w$ instead of $u$ and $T=V$, followed by restriction along $\gamma$, gives
$$\Lambda_W(\gamma)\eta_{w,V}(s)=(\beta_w)_W\bigl(F(\operatorname{pr}_V\circ\gamma)(s)\bigr)=\theta_W(F(h)(s)).$$
Here naturality of the ring-sheaf morphism $\beta_w$ from [sk-def-sfb-coefficient-morphisms] is applied to the $W$-morphism $\gamma$, and $\operatorname{pr}_V\gamma=h$. Thus this is an equality for the actual section $s$, without requiring it to be constant or restricting the condensed ring.

Cited nodes: sk-def-sfb-coefficient-morphisms.

### Retained old Step 5

Apply [lem-sfb-eta-comp] to $f=h$, $g=u$. Its canonical coefficient maps are the ones here: [sk-def-sfb-coefficient-morphisms] defines $\alpha$ using the inverse of the pullback composition comparison and defines $\eta$ as its mate, exactly as specified in that lemma. It gives $\eta_w=c_{h,u}\circ u_*\eta_h\circ\eta_u$. Evaluate at $V/B$. By [def-sfb-finite-lower-star-comp], $c_{h,u}$ identifies $P\times_VW$ with $V\times_BW$, by $(v_1,v_2,z)\mapsto(v_1,z)$, where $v_2=h(z)$. Under this identification the base change of $\delta:V\to P$ along $h$ is precisely $\gamma$. Naturality of the sheaf map $\eta_h:\Lambda_V\to h_*\Lambda_W$ along $\delta$ therefore reads
$$\Lambda_W(\gamma)\eta_{w,V}(s)=\eta_{h,V}\bigl(\Lambda_V(\delta)\eta_{u,V}(s)\bigr).$$
Steps 3 and 4 identify the two sides respectively with $\theta_W(F(h)(s))$ and $h^\sharp(\theta_V(s))$. This proves the asserted naturality for every $B$-morphism $h$. No monomorphism assumption is used: both the diagonal $\delta$ and the graph $\gamma$ are retained.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-eta-comp, sk-def-sfb-coefficient-morphisms, def-sfb-finite-lower-star-comp.

### Retained old Step 6

Finally fix $r=\theta_V(s)$. Set $R=h^{-1}_{\mathrm{Ab}}\Lambda_V$ and $S=\Lambda_W$. The module pullback of [def-sfb-module-upper-star] is $S\otimes_R h^{-1}_{\mathrm{Ab}}(-)$ with scalar map $\alpha_h:R\to S$. In the restriction model of Step 1, inverse image of the regular module and of its multiplication map are $R$ and $m_t$, where $t=r|_W$; this follows objectwise by restricting $x\mapsto rx$. The tensor description of [def-sfb-modobj-extension] has the canonical regular-module isomorphism
$$q:S\otimes_R R\xrightarrow{\sim}S,\qquad b\otimes a\mapsto b\alpha_h(a),\qquad q^{-1}(b)=b\otimes1.$$
The formula is balanced since $b\alpha_h(c)\alpha_h(a)=b\alpha_h(ca)$; its two composites with the proposed inverse are identities using the balancing relation $b\otimes a=b\alpha_h(a)\otimes1$. These formulas define morphisms of sheaves: the sheaf tensor is the sheafification of the presheaf tensor [sk-def-sfb-sheaf-tensor], and the relative tensor imposes the balancing coequalizer. Thus the calculations on pure tensors extend by its universal property; no assertion that arbitrary global tensor sections are pure tensors is needed.

Cited nodes: def-sfb-module-upper-star, def-sfb-modobj-extension, sk-def-sfb-sheaf-tensor.

### Retained old Step 7

On the same generators the pulled-back endomorphism is $b\otimes a\mapsto b\otimes ta$ by the morphism formula in [def-sfb-modobj-extension]. Its image under $q$ is
$$b\alpha_h(ta)=\alpha_h(t)b\alpha_h(a).$$
Hence conjugation by $q$ makes it multiplication by the global section $\alpha_h(t)=h^\sharp(r)$; equality follows on the sheafified tensor and its balancing coequalizer from equality of these bilinear maps. Step 5 identifies this section with $\theta_W(\Lambda_B(h)(s))$. This is exactly the asserted module-pullback clause under the canonical identification $h^*\Lambda_V\cong\Lambda_W$.

Cited nodes: def-sfb-modobj-extension.
