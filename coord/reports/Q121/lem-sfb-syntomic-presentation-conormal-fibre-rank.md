## Review by Q121

I checked the source against every cited node's statement, not against its proof. For step 1, `lem-weil-pbc-flat-local-ring-maps` supplies exactly the needed flat local map, with no Noetherian assumption used. `def-weil-pbc-complete-intersection-over-field` supplies principal complete-intersection neighbourhoods of every fibre prime. In `lem-weil-pbc-syntomic-at-prime-relative-global-complete-intersection`, the finite-presentation neighbourhood is the whole algebra (g=1), the prime is q, the base prime is its contraction, and the fibre prime is the kernel of the map to its residue field. Thus all three hypotheses are present. This is precisely the chart reduction in 07BT, not an application of a smoothness theorem.

For step 2, I read `lem-weil-pbc-conormal-module-principal-localization`: it applies to any commutative base and two surjective polynomial presentations of S and S_g. The second kernel is H and the original kernel is J, so the ranks occur on the correct sides of its stable isomorphism. The chosen second presentation is a relative global complete intersection, so `lem-weil-pbc-relative-global-complete-intersection-conormal-free` makes H/H² free on c equation classes. Inclusion/projection of the first summand then exhibit N_g as a summand of a finite free module. This does not incorrectly cancel a free summand or claim global freeness of N.

For step 3, `lem-weil-pbc-finite-projective-prime-local-free` is applied to a module already finite projective in step 2, over S_g and at the prime induced by q. Writing its element as a/g^r and replacing it by the neighbourhood D(ga) is justified by the two localization universal properties, written in the step. For step 4, `lem-weil-pbc-spec-basic-opens-unit-ideal-quasi-compact` states the needed covering/unit-ideal equivalence for arbitrary families; finite support of an expression for 1 gives the finite family. `lem-sc-affine-standard-cover-finite-projective` assumes exactly this finite standard cover of finite free localizations and concludes finite projectivity. These generic off-unit algebraic inputs are declared under split_report.tool_uses where required.

For step 5, `lem-weil-pbc-flat-quotient-tensor-injective` explicitly assumes flatness of the quotient module. Its quotient here is S over R, so syntomic flatness suffices, even though kappa(p) is not flat over R. `lem-weil-pbc-polynomial-scalar-extension` has arbitrary coefficient map and arbitrary variable set; it specializes directly to the n-variable fibre presentation. These statements give an injective tensor of J and identify its image with the actual kernel, not just a surjection onto it.

For step 6, `lem-weil-pbc-tensor-right-exact` requires no flatness. Apply it to J² -> J -> J/J² -> 0. Under step 5 the image of its first map is exactly bar(J)², by expanding products of reduced ideal elements. I checked both inclusions, including absorption of polynomial coefficients into J. The final tensor reassociation is written as two explicit mutually inverse balanced maps, so the resulting isomorphism is the claimed canonical coefficient-reduction map, not merely an unspecified abstract isomorphism.

For step 7, `def-weil-pbc-complete-intersection-over-field` gives the complete-intersection neighbourhood at the chosen prime of F. `lem-weil-pbc-lci-conormal-free-rank-codimension` states the rank n-d with d the topological dimension at that point, for this same arbitrary polynomial presentation. Its hypotheses require neither a perfect field nor smoothness; both would be unjustified additions here.

I checked zero rings and empty fibres: projectivity is the zero-module splitting when S=0, tensor comparisons remain valid when a fibre is zero, and its primewise rank clause is vacuous. For J=0 the conormal vanishes and a polynomial fibre has local topological dimension n even at its generic point; using dim of the local ring would give the wrong answer there. For k[x]/(x²), the singular zero-dimensional complete-intersection fibre has conormal rank 1, so no reducedness/smoothness has slipped in. Zero-variable presentations and n-d=0 are allowed. No Noetherian hypothesis or flatness of R -> k is introduced.
