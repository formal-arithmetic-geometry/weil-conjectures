## Original arguments assigned to the new nodes

The following text preserves the old proof verbatim. These are handover notes in the report, not proof fields on the statement-only nodes. The chart node takes steps 1-3 and the first properness sentence of step 4. The prime node takes the remainder of step 4 and step 5. The parent keeps steps 6-8 verbatim. Existing cited inputs named here are intended reuse by the future workers; the new node files deliberately do not pre-split those workers' work.

Original step 1: By [def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring], choose a finite generating list $x_1,\ldots,x_r$ for $\mathfrak m_B$. Delete zero entries. The list is still generating and is nonempty because $\mathfrak m_B\ne0$; thus $r\ge1$ and every $x_i$ is nonzero.

Original step 2: Apply [lemma-nagata-local-domain-valuation-dominates] to the local subring $B\subset K$, obtaining a valuation ring $A\subseteq K$ with $B\subseteq A$ and $\mathfrak m_A\cap B=\mathfrak m_B$. By [def-trf-valuation-ring], for any nonzero $b,c\in K$ either $b/c\in A$ or $c/b\in A$. Select a generator $a$ from the finite list such that $x_i/a\in A$ for every $i$: start with $a=x_1$; when adjoining $x_j$, retain $a$ if $x_j/a\in A$, and otherwise replace it by $x_j$. In the latter case $a/x_j\in A$, so each previous quotient becomes $(x_i/a)(a/x_j)\in A$. This proves the selection by finite induction. Since $a$ is one of the nonzero generators, $0\ne a\in\mathfrak m_B\subseteq\mathfrak m_A$.

Original step 3: Inside $K$ form $S=B[x_1/a,\ldots,x_r/a]$, the set of polynomial expressions in these fractions with coefficients in $B$. Then $B\subseteq S\subseteq A\subseteq K$; in particular $S$ is a nonzero integral domain. Evaluation gives a surjection $B[T_1,\ldots,T_r]\to S$, so [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian] makes $S$ Noetherian. Moreover $\mathfrak m_B S=aS$: each generator satisfies $x_i=a(x_i/a)$, giving one inclusion, and $a\in\mathfrak m_B$ gives the other.

Original step 4: The ideal $aS$ is proper: an equation $1=as$ with $s\in S\subseteq A$ would make $a$ a unit in $A$, contrary to $a\in\mathfrak m_A$. By [lem-weil-pbc-minimal-prime-over-proper-ideal], choose a prime $\mathfrak q$ of $S$ minimal over $aS$. As $\mathfrak m_B S=aS\subseteq\mathfrak q$, its contraction contains $\mathfrak m_B$. The contraction is proper (it cannot contain $1$), and maximality of $\mathfrak m_B$ gives $\mathfrak q\cap B=\mathfrak m_B$.

Original step 5: The general inequality [lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-height-quotient-bound] applied to the Noetherian ring $S$, the single element $a$, and $\mathfrak q\supseteq(a)$ gives $\operatorname{ht}_S(\mathfrak q)\le\operatorname{ht}_{S/(a)}(\mathfrak q/(a))+1$. Here $\operatorname{ht}_{S/(a)}(\mathfrak q/(a))=0$: if a prime $\bar{\mathfrak p}\subseteq\mathfrak q/(a)$ is pulled back along $S\to S/(a)$, its preimage is a prime containing $(a)$ and contained in $\mathfrak q$, hence equals $\mathfrak q$ by minimality. Surjectivity of the quotient map then gives $\bar{\mathfrak p}=\mathfrak q/(a)$. Thus every prime chain ending below $\mathfrak q/(a)$ has length zero. Consequently $\operatorname{ht}_S(\mathfrak q)\le1$. Since $S$ is a domain and $0\ne a\in\mathfrak q$, the strict prime chain $(0)\subsetneq\mathfrak q$ gives the reverse inequality, so $\operatorname{ht}_S(\mathfrak q)=1$.

Original step 6: Define the required ring as the actual subring $R=\{s/t\in K:s\in S,\ t\in S\setminus\mathfrak q\}$. The complement of the prime $\mathfrak q$ is multiplicative and excludes zero, so field addition and multiplication show this is a subring containing $S$. The fraction construction of [def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime] identifies it with $S_{\mathfrak q}$: equality of fractions in $K$ is $s t'=s' t$, exactly the localization equality because $S$ is a domain. Its maximal ideal is $\mathfrak n=\{s/t:s\in\mathfrak q,\ t\notin\mathfrak q\}$. Numerator membership in $\mathfrak q$ is independent of presentation by cross multiplication and primality. This set is a proper ideal, while a fraction outside it has inverse $t/s\in R$; hence it is the unique maximal ideal. The inclusion in $K$ makes $R$ a nonzero integral domain.

Original step 7: Apply [lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian] to $S\to R$: all $t\in S\setminus\mathfrak q$ have inverses $1/t$, every element is $s/t$, and injectivity gives its kernel condition (take multiplier $1$). Thus $R$ is Noetherian. By [lem-weil-pbc-localization-dimension-chains-below-prime], its Krull dimension equals $\operatorname{ht}_S(\mathfrak q)=1$.

Original step 8: For $b\in B$, the fraction $b/1$ belongs to $\mathfrak n$ exactly when $b\in\mathfrak q$, and this is equivalent to $b\in\mathfrak m_B$. Thus $\mathfrak m_R\cap B=\mathfrak m_B$. Finally the embedding of $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ into $K$ sends $r/s$ to the same field quotient. It is injective because equality is cross multiplication in the domain $R$. It is surjective because every $k\in K$ has the form $b/c$ with $b,c\in B\subseteq R$ and $c\ne0$. This proves the asserted equality of fraction fields with the prescribed embedding.
