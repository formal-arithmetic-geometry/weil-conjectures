## Preserved arguments and assignment to new nodes

The following are the complete seven original proof steps, not shortened summaries. ### Original Step 1: lem-trf-sharp-section-basic-chart-data

Fix $\varphi:P\to\Gamma(T,\mathcal A)$. For every open $V\subset T$, $\mathcal A(V)$ is sharp: an invertible section has invertible, hence zero, germs at every point, so it is zero by sheaf separation. In any commutative monoid a sum is invertible exactly when each summand is invertible (an inverse to $b+c$ gives inverses to $b$ and $c$). Consequently $\mathfrak m_x=\{p\in P:\varphi(p)_x\ne0\}$ is a prime ideal: it excludes $0$, is stable under addition by $P$, and its complement is closed under addition. Define $h(x)=\mathfrak m_x$.

Source: VIII 3.1.8, proof of (i), p.99: construction of the prime ideal from the stalk homomorphism; sharp case (ii).

### Original Step 2: lem-trf-sharp-section-basic-chart-data

One has $h^{-1}D(a)=\{x:\varphi(a)_x=0\}=:V_a$. Equality of a germ to zero means equality to zero on some neighborhood, so $V_a$ is open and $\varphi(a)|_{V_a}=0$ by separation. Since the $D(a)$ form a basis, $h$ is continuous. On $V_a$ define $P_a\to\mathcal A(V_a)$ by $p-na\mapsto\varphi(p)|_{V_a}$. If $p-na=p'-n'a$, integrality gives $p+n'a=p'+na$ in $P$, and applying $\varphi$ gives equal images. Thus this formula is well defined and additive; it is the unique extension killing the invertible element $a$.

Source: VIII 3.1.8, p.99: continuity and extension to localizations, expanding the straightforward verification.

### Original Step 3: lem-trf-sharp-section-basic-chart-data (unit quotient and compatibility); lem-trf-affine-fan-basic-map-local-lift (gluing)

The homomorphism in Step 2 kills $P_a^\times$, since its target is sharp by Step 1. Hence it factors through $P_a/P_a^\times=\overline{\mathcal M}_P(D(a))$ by [lem-trf-affine-fan-localization-sections]. These maps commute on $D(a)\cap D(b)=D(a+b)$: both send the class of each $p\in P$ to $\varphi(p)$ and all localized denominators to zero. For an inclusion $D(b)\subseteq D(a)$, use $D(a+b)=D(b)$ in this intersection calculation; the localization descriptions identify the restriction maps, so it proves restriction compatibility as well. For any open $U$ and a section on $U$, restrict to the basic opens contained in $U$, apply these maps, and glue on their inverse images in $T$. Agreement on intersections and uniqueness of sheaf gluing give a restriction-compatible sheaf map over $h$, equivalently the inverse-image sheaf map in the statement; this is the direct-section description of morphisms in [def-trf-fine-fan-blowup].

Source: VIII 3.1.8, p.99: sheaf-map construction; VIII 3.1.3 and Remark 3.1.4(iii), pp.98--99, for the sharp affine sections.

### Original Step 4: lem-trf-affine-fan-basic-map-local-lift

At $x\in T$, put $F_x=P\setminus\mathfrak m_x$. By [lem-trf-affine-fan-localization-sections] the source stalk is $(P-F_x)/(P-F_x)^\times$. The constructed map sends $\operatorname{cl}(p-f)$ to $\varphi(p)_x$, since $f\in F_x$ has zero image. This image is zero exactly when $p\in F_x$. In that case $p-f$ is a unit and its class is zero. Conversely a unit has zero image in the sharp target. Hence the stalk map has inverse image of the units equal to the units, so it is local. This proves the construction gives a morphism $(T,\mathcal A)\to S(P)$.

Source: VIII 3.1.8, proof of (i)--(ii), p.99: local stalk factorization and descent to sharpening.

### Original Step 5: lem-trf-affine-fan-canonical-section-rigidity

For any morphism $g:(T,\mathcal A)\to S(P)$, compose the canonical map $P\to\Gamma(S(P),\overline{\mathcal M}_P)$ with pullback of sections to obtain $\Phi(g):P\to\Gamma(T,\mathcal A)$. For $x\in T$, locality and sharpness say $\Phi(g)(p)_x=0$ if and only if the canonical germ of $p$ at $g(x)$ is zero. By [lem-trf-affine-fan-localization-sections] this holds exactly when $p\notin g(x)$. Thus the point map reconstructed from $\Phi(g)$ is exactly $g$.

Source: VIII 3.1.8(ii), statement and uniqueness of the construction, p.99.

### Original Step 6: lem-trf-affine-fan-canonical-section-rigidity (recovery); parent Step 1 (a=0 inverse identity)

The sheaf map is also determined by these pulled-back canonical sections. On $D(a)$ every section has the form $\operatorname{cl}(p-na)$ by [lem-trf-affine-fan-localization-sections], and $\operatorname{cl}(p-na)=\operatorname{cl}(p)$ because $a$ is a unit in $P_a$ and therefore zero after sharpening. Its pullback must be $\Phi(g)(p)|_{g^{-1}D(a)}$. These maps determine the maps on arbitrary opens by the sheaf gluing used in Step 3. Consequently reconstructing from $\Phi(g)$ gives exactly $g$, including its sheaf map. Conversely, for the morphism constructed from $\varphi$, Step 2 with $a=0$ gives the pullback $p\mapsto\varphi(p)$ on the whole space. The two constructions are mutual inverses.

Source: VIII 3.1.8(ii), p.99: bijectivity, with the uniqueness checks expanded on the basic opens.

### Original Step 7: parent Steps 3--4 (naturality and functoriality)

If $k:(T',\mathcal A')\to(T,\mathcal A)$ is a morphism, pullback of canonical sections gives $\Phi(g\circ k)=\Gamma(k)\circ\Phi(g)$, by composition of the sheaf maps. For a homomorphism $u:P\to Q$ of fine sharp monoids, the inverse construction applied to $P\xrightarrow{u}Q=\Gamma(S(Q),\overline{\mathcal M}_Q)$ defines $S(u):S(Q)\to S(P)$; here the equality follows by [lem-trf-affine-fan-localization-sections] with $a=0$ and sharpness of $Q$. Pullback sends the canonical section $p$ to $u(p)$, so $\Phi(S(u)\circ g)=\Phi(g)\circ u$. The same calculation for identities and composites, followed by uniqueness in Step 6, proves functoriality of $S$. These two formulas prove naturality in both variables.

Source: VIII 3.1.8, p.99: global-sections correspondence; naturality follows from the explicit construction and composition of pullbacks.
