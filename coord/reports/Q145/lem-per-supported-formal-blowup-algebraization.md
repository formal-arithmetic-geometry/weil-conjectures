The inverse-image calculation explicitly proves extension, contraction and equality of center quotient rings. The power is positive and finitely generated, as required by that node, since the affine ring is Noetherian. This calculation is elementary relative to the cited quotient theorem.

2. Step 2 uses the principal-localization criterion and resulting closed immersion in `lem-weil-pbc-affine-ideals-closed-subscheme`. It verifies that criterion before applying it. The definition `pqf-def-coherent` is used for coherence only after writing the finite-kernel argument over each Noetherian affine ring. No definition node is used as if it supplied an algebraization theorem. Affine intersections need not be affine; principal affine refinements suffice. The local exponents admit a maximum on a finite cover. The same quotient rings give the actual scheme identity of the centers.

3. Step 3 uses `thm-nagata-global-blowup-proper`, whose finite-type-ideal hypothesis follows from `pqf-def-coherent`; `def-weil-proper-morphism` then supplies finite type. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian` supplies precisely the Noetherianity needed for the next induction. 4. Step 4 uses `lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-completion-flat` on the Noetherian affine source. `def-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change-map` specifies the objects and extended ideal in the comparison, and `thm-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change` asserts that this comparison is an isomorphism. The inverse limit used is exactly `def-weil-pbc-adic-completion`.

5. Step 5 has a length-zero base case and a precisely specified extension of the partial ordinary tower. It only assembles Steps 1--4. It recovers every ideal at its original index in the reverse direction; neither uniqueness nor preservation of the ideals is inferred merely from isomorphism of blowup maps.

6. Step 6 uses the actual affine cover from `prop-nagata-affine-blowup-chart-cover`, the rings from `def-nagata-blowup-chart-ring`, and the invertible extended ideal from `thm-nagata-blowup-principalizes-center`. The intrinsic characterization of an `a`-chart is checked on a `b`-chart by invertibility of `a/b`. The equation `a*(j/a)=j` gives rigidity because multiplication by `a` is injective in the chart subring of `A_a`. For completed charts this injectivity survives the flat completion theorem; continuity and separatedness then give equality on the entire completion. This verifies the phrase “unique isomorphism” even for nonreduced bases. It does not assume birational density of a regular open in the nonreduced case.

7. Step 7 transfers center regularity and nonemptiness by the actual scheme isomorphism already constructed. Its assertion on singular support uses the statement's stipulated identification of the base formal singular locus with the completion of the reduced ordinary singular locus. Only underlying support is needed for “lying over.” The cited `thm-nagata-global-blowup-complement` supplies exactly the complement isomorphism, and restricting it to the inverse image of `X minus Z` gives the induction there. 8. Step 8 uses the quotient theorem to lift a prime *containing* `K`, so the local completion map is genuinely defined over the chosen point. Flatness is from `lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-completion-flat`; Noetherianity of the target is from `lem-weil-pbc-adic-completion-noetherian`; the target local ring is regular by the hypothesis on the last formal level. This is a general off-unit commutative-algebra tool, declared in `split_report.tool_uses`; it does not replace the unit route.

9. Step 9 verifies nowhere density before invoking `lem-per-noetherian-normalized-blowup-finality`. That statement explicitly contains the ordinary blowup's reducedness and its bijection of component generic points under a nowhere-dense-center hypothesis; I use only those clauses, not its normalization construction or universal property. `lem-per-reduced-noetherian-blowup-geometry` independently supplies the reduced and Noetherian ordinary level. At the start the generic local rings of reduced `X` are fields, so no generic point lies in its singular locus. The induction therefore verifies, rather than assumes, the required density hypothesis at every stage.
