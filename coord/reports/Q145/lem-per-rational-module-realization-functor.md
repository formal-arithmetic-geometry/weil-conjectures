Step 1 takes the inverse-limit identification, sheaf localization, and coefficient-ring restrictions from the actual statement of continuous-coefficients-immersion-restriction. It assumes noetherian X, which the item has; a locally closed stratum has the required induced coefficient sheaves. The quotient projection normalization is the inverse-limit isomorphism's specified one. It avoids the false claim that localization on every non-quasi-compact object is computed with one bounded denominator.

Step 2 uses scalar-localization-sheaf-exact precisely for actual module-sheaf scalar extension R to R[1/s]. The chosen site and universe convention supplies its smallness hypothesis. This theorem imposes no enough-points hypothesis, so none is smuggled into the application. Exact-functor-derived-factorization says both that termwise scalar extension descends and that the descended functor is triangulated. Derived-change-of-rings says that the specified derived tensor is computed on K-flat resolutions. The proof compares a resolution with the original complex using exactness, rather than assuming all complexes have flat terms. Both functors are the localization of the same termwise functor, giving the natural identification.

Step 3 uses the exact-functor-complex-cohomology statement, which supplies the natural cycle/boundary isomorphism, not just preservation of quasi-isomorphisms. I checked that its hypotheses are exactly the additivity and finite exactness established in Step 2. Naturality for a roof follows by applying the same commuting square and inverting its quasi-isomorphism leg. This proves the displayed derived-cohomology formula for all integer degrees and immediately preserves any vanishing interval.

Step 4 uses condition (iii) and boundedness from integral-stratified-perfect-characterization. That statement explicitly includes the completed category with its witnesses and permits finitely presented N with torsion. Local-derived-restriction-comparisons supplies exactness and cohomology compatibility for the actual inverse-image ring and for slice restriction; Step 1 identifies those rings with the stratum coefficient rings. The model is asserted on a cover of the stratum, never globally on the stratum. The subsequent tensor calculation is local, which is sufficient for constructibility.

Step 5 is elementary: both balanced maps are written, and multiplying scalars checks their balancing and mutual inverse identities. Sheaf tensor product is defined as sheafification of the presheaf tensor, which permits the maps to descend. The same local generators and relations show compatibility with restriction. This step is not a new associativity lemma and does not cite a source tag in place of an unproved claim.

Step 6 uses vector-space-free exactly for existence of a basis over the field E. Finiteness is proved from the finite generating list: the finite union of their finite basis supports must contain every basis element. Thus no unstated finite-dimensionality theorem is needed. Tensoring a finite basis gives E_S^d. This establishes constructibility degree by degree; Step 3 gives boundedness. I checked N=0, N=A/varpi^n, and N=A^r plus torsion: ranks are respectively 0, 0 and r. The empty scheme and zero complex give no exception. Flatness is a property of scalar localization, never of N.

Step 7 first uses exact-linear-derived-functor for A-linearity; it is not used to infer E-linearity without an argument. The rational-category definition gives same objects and localized Hom modules. The annihilation relation for equal fractions is explicitly sent to zero and divided by an invertible power of varpi. This also checks composition and addition. Integers-localization-uniformizer identifies every E-scalar with an A-fraction, so the final displayed scalar calculation proves E-linearity including denominators. Torsion morphisms map to zero, as they must.

Step 8 uses precisely the closure clause of the integral characterization, linearity of derived shifts (applied to both the A-linear integral module category and the E-linear rational module category), and the statement of linear-scalar-localization-triangulated saying that localized distinguished triangles are isomorphic to integral ones. Naturality of the shift comparison extends to fractions by multiplication with their inverse scalar. Applying F to an integral distinguished triangle then proves exactness of J. This does not require full faithfulness, essential surjectivity, or any lattice-existence result.
