For the finite-test input, the set F corresponds to the finite Gamma_0 test in that paragraph, using 4.2.2 and 4.2.3. Persistence of already extracted valuations is precisely 4.2.1(i), applied to a normal intermediate model. I included the unique lift and the closed-or-generic alternative in the statement, since the induction actually needs them. Properness gives the unique lift of the specified centre morphism, even if C is not separated over T. If the old centre is closed and the new centre is closed in its T-fibre, then its residue extension over the old centre is finite (both fibre-closed points have algebraic residue fields over the base point), so it is closed in the proper fibre over that old centre and hence in the new curve. A nonclosed point of a one-dimensional fibre is generic. This explains the alternative without assuming that every point of T is closed.

For the single-extraction input I read the actual two stages on pp. 28--29, rather than treating it as cofinality again. At a node uv=a, residual transcendence degree one and divisibility of the base value group give a parameter pi with |u|=|pi|; the blow-up of (u,pi) has the two nodal charts with parameters pi and a/pi, and the valuation lies on the smooth unit-ratio open. At a smooth centre, the residue-field argument produces a in the valuation ring and pi with residue of (t-a)/pi transcendental; the blow-up of (t-a,pi) then extracts the fibre generic point. Temkin also supplies the étale pullback and extension from the centre neighbourhood, so the lemma's output is global, not merely a local chart. These are not elementary facts; they are the content of the new statement-only extraction input, for its next worker to split. The output forgets the stronger blow-up assertion, but retains all properties the finite induction uses.
