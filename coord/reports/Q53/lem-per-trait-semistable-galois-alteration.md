I read the item against ILO XVI 3.7, printed p.254: normalization of constants, normalized projective closure, the four bullets of the G-equivariant square, and the divisorial log structure on the generic-fibre complement. For the compactification input I read Stacks morphisms.tex labels lemma-H-quasi-projective-open-H-projective (01WA), lemma-nagata-normalization (035S), and lemma-normalization-normal (035Q). These support closure and normalization; the constants assertion comes from ILO XVI, not those tags. For the log conclusion I read ILO VI 1.2, 1.4 and 1.8, printed pp.69--70.

Step 1 uses the compactification node's actual statement, including X as an unchanged dense open, equality of function fields, and geometric integrality over S_0. This preserves the field over which the later Galois assertion is needed. The base is complete, strictly henselian and mixed characteristic as required. I tested the input on X=S and on X the generic point, which is a finite-type open of S, and on a finite extension of the constants; the change of base is inside k(X), not an arbitrary field extension that could destroy the desired generic Galois conclusion.

Step 2 now cites properness of the projective generic fibre before applying lem-pc-dim-zero-finite-over-field. I opened that node: it assumes properness, not just finite type. The characteristic-zero finite field extension is trivial by geometric integrality. I opened the existing proper-valuative extension node: it provides the section over the DVR with the specified generic value. The separated diagonal from def-weil-proper-morphism makes the two endomorphisms equal on reduced integral C once they agree generically. This proves C=S_0 and handles both possible nonempty opens, S_0 and its generic point, with the trivial group and the r=n=1 chart. The generic fibre is projective by the existing projective-base-change statement.

Step 3 applies the marked nodal alteration statement only after normality, projectivity, flatness and geometric integrality have been obtained. It provides no regularity or log conclusion, only a tower with its group and generic torsor. In mixed characteristic the generic characteristic is zero, so the radicial factor in Vidal's generic-cover formulation is trivial. This is why the group can be used for the generic etale Galois cover. The trait may carry a nontrivial group action. Step 4 uses exactly the modification node's equivariant projective birational map and strictly semistable regular output; the splitting and marked hypotheses agree verbatim between these two nodes. The test tower of length one with local equation uv=pi^m requires resolution rather than claiming the initial total space regular; the smooth case permits the identity modification. The restriction d>=1 is confined to this branch, and Step 2 handles d=0.

Step 5 uses the statements of lem-trf-h-projective-composition and lem-pc-projective-base-change for qh and its restriction over X. I checked their convention against def-kun-projective and def-pc-projective-morphism: all use finite projective-space embeddings here. The closed-image property comes from properness, with projective-implies-proper cited. The torsor is retained by choosing a G-stable dense isomorphism open for h, then removing the closed image of its complement. Over the generic point the integral finite torsor is a single field point, so the removed image misses it. Restricting an integral scheme to a nonempty open preserves integrality and connectedness; no assertion that arbitrary connected open restrictions stay connected is used.

Step 6 proves finite composition explicitly with generators b_i c_j; no nonexisting theorem is cited. I opened lem-fin-finite-over-projective: its global embedding conclusion requires an affine base, satisfied by the trait. Projective composition then embeds Z over S, and its open X' is quasi-projective. The existing finite-type-over-Noetherian statement applies because both traits are Noetherian. Its consequence that every open of Z is quasi-compact lets the affine finite-generator definition give finite type for X'. This includes the zero-dimensional branch with Z=S_0 and X' its open X.

Step 7 writes the actual chart N -> N^r, its zero kernel and free cokernel Z^(r-1), and its etale comparison map. I checked def-per-fs-log-smooth-etale: it demands a smooth comparison, now supplied by the existing etale-smooth-zero-dimensional-fibres node. The base log trait satisfies the stratum dimension equality directly at its closed and generic points. The local associated chart structures therefore identify with the canonical divisorial structure and glue. Every group automorphism preserves the generic fibre, so preserves that subsheaf. The calculation also works at r=1 and when the special fibre is absent from an open.
