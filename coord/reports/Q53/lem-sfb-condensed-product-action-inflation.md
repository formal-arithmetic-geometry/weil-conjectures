### Old step 4: allocated to lem-sfb-sheaf-action-grothendieck-inflation

For a projection $\pi:P\to Q$ in the statement, define inflation by $p\cdot n=\pi(p)\cdot n$ on every section after every base change, and leave each linear map unchanged. Since $\pi$ preserves multiplication and identity this satisfies the action laws and respects equivariant maps. Addition is unchanged, so inflation is additive. By the kernel/cokernel formula of step 2, the inflated kernel and cokernel are precisely the kernel and cokernel of the inflated map: on the underlying sectionwise kernel or sheafified quotient the action is induced by the same formula $p\mapsto\pi(p)$. Thus inflation preserves kernels and cokernels and is exact.

### Old step 6: allocated to lem-sfb-sheaf-product-fixed-adjunction

An equivariant map $f:\operatorname{Inf}_\pi L\to M$ takes values in $M^N$: for $T\to S$, $n\in N(T)$ and $x\in L(S)$, one has $n\cdot f(x)|_T=f(n\cdot x|_T)=f(x|_T)$, since $N$ acts trivially on the inflated source. Thus the same section maps, with codomain restricted, define $\bar f:L\to M^N$. They are $Q$-equivariant: in the product case use the lift $(q,1)$; for $Q=1$ this condition is vacuous. Conversely compose a $Q$-equivariant map $L\to M^N$ with the inclusion. For $P=G\times H$, writing $(g,h)=(g,1)(1,h)$ proves equivariance since $h$ fixes its image; the projections to $1$ use the same fixed-image argument. The two constructions are inverse because the inclusions are injective on sections. Precomposition or postcomposition commutes with codomain restriction and inclusion, giving naturality in both variables. These are the asserted additive adjunctions with exactly the stipulated bijections.

### Old step 7: allocated to lem-sfb-sheaf-product-fixed-adjunction

Both inflations from $\mathcal A_1(R)$ to $\mathcal A_{G\times H}(R)$ leave the module and its maps unchanged and give every $(g,h)$ trivial action, so the direct and composite functors agree. For $m\in M(S)$, being fixed by all pairs after all $T\to S$ implies being fixed by $(g,1)$ and $(1,h)$. Conversely these two fixedness conditions give $(g,h)m=(g,1)(1,h)m=m$ on every such $T$.
