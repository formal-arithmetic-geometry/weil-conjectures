For each of the 15 cited nodes I opened and read its actual statement, not just its title or source. I checked the following uses against those statements, and copied those statements verbatim into citations.json:

1. `lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence` (2) supplies absolute qcqs for an E(T) object; (1) applies to a map between such objects because the source is qcqs and the target quasi-separated. `lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation` (3) supplies etaleness of that map, and `lem-weil-pbc-separated-cancellation` (b) supplies its separatedness from the separated composite to T. Finite presentation then means precisely lfp, qc and qs. The identity case and terminal universal property are immediate from the definitions; there is no assumption that T is absolutely separated.
2. `lem-weil-pbc-separated-base-change` and permanence (3) apply to B -> C and the base-change morphism A -> C, with no extra restrictions on that morphism. `lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence` explicitly has finite-presentation composition in (d) and separated composition in (e). `lem-weil-pbc-etale-base-change-finite-limits` (2) gives etaleness of the product over C, not merely of the product over T. These facts put the product in E(T); fullness gives its universal property. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-limits-from-terminal-pullbacks` gives both finite-limit existence and preservation. `def-weil-size-convention` explicitly authorizes the chosen small skeletons, and no construction depends on a representative being literally equal to an arbitrary scheme.
3. Local affineness is the defining property of a scheme. The composition/cancellation lemma explicitly says open immersions and their etale composites are etale. `lem-weil-pbc-affine-source-morphism-separated` permits arbitrary targets. The affine-source clause of qcqs permanence requires T quasi-separated, which is available even when U is not qcqs. `lem-weil-pbc-etale-morphism-open-map` supplies open images; compactness of A gives a finite selection from this open cover. This is the explicit elementary compactness argument, not an application of the existing affine-target refinement theorem to a possibly non-affine A. The resulting maps are over T and hence are arrows of the full subcategory.
4. `lem-weil-pbc-etale-covering-composition` and `lem-weil-pbc-etale-covering-base-change` state exactly joint surjectivity and etaleness of the new families; step 2 supplies their objects. The finite-index assertions are elementary set-theoretic facts. The statement of `lem-weil-pbc-pretopology-sheaf-condition` explicitly defines the generated topology as the sieves containing one pretopology family, which is the clause used here, as well as its sheaf criterion.
5. `def-weil-pbc-etale-site` explicitly describes arbitrary jointly surjective etale families as covers. The pretopology node's containing-a-family characterization applies to that full-site pretopology too. I checked both directions of the induced-sieve criterion: in the reverse direction, each selected affine arrow factors through an R-arrow, and fullness makes the factorization an E(T)-factorization. Thus the sieve closure really implies membership in R. No appeal to the proof of another node or to sheafification is needed.

Its statement requires full faithfulness, an image-generated covering sieve for every object, the induced sieve criterion, and the indicated comma-category limits.
