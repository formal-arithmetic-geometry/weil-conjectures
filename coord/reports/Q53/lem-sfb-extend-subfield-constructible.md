## Complete proof in words for the new node

lem-sfb-internal-scalar-extension-finite-presentation. Source: the local-presentation tensoring sentence in the item's blueprint proof, expanded using the current internal finite-presentation definition. Proof origin: agent. Length: 1185 characters.

1. Put $G=\operatorname{Ext}_\varphi$. By [sk-def-sfb-change-of-scalars], $G$ preserves finite colimits. The sheaf tensor satisfies its hypotheses by [sk-def-sfb-sheaf-tensor]. The regular-module comparison [lem-weil-pbc-regular-extension-comparison] gives $G(R)\cong S$. For each finite $r$, the coproduct universal property gives $e_r:G(R^r)\cong S^r$: use the images of the $r$ coproduct injections followed by the regular-module comparison. The inverse is induced by the inverse regular-module maps into $G(R^r)$; both composites are identities on every injection. This includes the empty coproduct $r=0$.

2. Choose $M\cong\operatorname{coker}(f)$ with $f:R^m\to R^n$, as in [def-sfb-finitely-presented-module]. Finite-colimit preservation from [sk-def-sfb-change-of-scalars] sends its cokernel diagram to a cokernel diagram. Conjugate $G(f)$ by $e_m,e_n$ from step 1 to obtain $f'=e_nG(f)e_m^{-1}:S^m\to S^n$. The resulting map $S^n\to G(M)$ annihilates $f'$, and any map annihilating $f'$ factors uniquely through it, by the preserved cokernel universal property. Thus $G(M)\cong\operatorname{coker}(f')$, the required finite presentation. The argument also covers zero ranks and $M=0$.

The exact cited statements used here are: sk-def-sfb-change-of-scalars, part (2), which explicitly says extension preserves every finite colimit; sk-def-sfb-sheaf-tensor, part (2), which gives the tensor-colimit hypotheses; lem-weil-pbc-regular-extension-comparison, sentence "Extension of scalars sends the regular R-module canonically to the regular S-module."; and def-sfb-finitely-presented-module, the displayed finite-biproduct/cokernel definition. No proof of the larger derived preservation theorem is used. The regular-module comparison is a generic tool from another unit, used for exactly its stated first assertion.
