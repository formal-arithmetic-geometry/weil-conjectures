## Review by Q53

I read Milne Definition 17.6 and the next sentence, printed pp.353–354, and the complete proof of Theorem 17.33 and Corollary 17.34 on p.362. I also read the new nodes' source passages: Lemma 17.32 and its full incidence proof pp.361–362, Proposition 17.28 and Corollary 17.29 p.360, and Theorem 16.47/Corollary 16.48 p.341. The centralizer product and semisimple-membership assertions are precisely what Milne uses in the first paragraph of 17.33(a). The general incidence criterion is 17.32; it is independent of being a Borel and is not the conclusion of this item moved elsewhere. Both new nodes have empty proofs and proof dependencies and introduce all their objects and terminology. Neither is an elementary two-line consequence of existing acyclic inputs.

Step 1 cites the last clause of lem-sfb-solvable-normal-subgroup-products, which explicitly gives strict dimension growth and bounded integral dimensions for smooth connected subgroup inclusions. It is not using that lemma's product assertion for nonnormal subgroups. Choosing the largest possible dimension then proves existence and extension to a Borel directly. The trivial subgroup supplies the nonempty family.

Step 2 uses exactly the existence of a maximal torus inside every Borel from lem-sfb-borel-maximal-torus-conjugacy, and the new neutral-centralizer structure with the same algebraically closed field and smooth connected G. Step 3 uses the exact normalizer-transitivity conclusion of lem-sfb-torus-normalizer-borel-transitivity with the two Borels containing that T. The proof writes why normalizing T preserves its centralizer functor and neutral component, hence proves C lies in the prescribed B, instead of citing the later full-centralizer theorem. No reductivity is assumed by any of these nodes.

Step 4 uses the scheme-theoretic centralizer equality and semisimple point of lem-sfb-torus-regular-semisimple-element. The homogeneous quotient node explicitly supplies rational representatives, so every fixed rational coset can be written xC. The new structure lemma says both that all G-semisimple points in C lie in T and that every torus of C lies in T. The conjugation calculation puts xTx^{-1} into the centralizer of t on all algebras. The identity-component node says that component is open and closed, so connectedness of the torus makes its image lie there. Maximality of the conjugate torus then gives equality with T. Thus every fixed coset belongs to the finite set of normalizing cosets in the new structure node. This is an actual injection, not an assertion that an arbitrary torus has finitely many Borels. All text of the fixed-coset argument remains in the item; it has no hidden comparison lemma and is under 2000 characters.

Step 5 applies only the density clause of the incidence node, with H=C and the element t. The smooth connected hypothesis is supplied in step 2 and the finite-fixed-set hypothesis was proved in step 4. The inclusion of saturation sets is written by replacing c by its image in B.

Step 6 uses the homogeneous quotient node for construction and rational lifting; its quotient is the fppf sheaf quotient, so it is the same quotient covered by lem-sfb-borel-quotient-proper-geometry. The latter's statement explicitly supplies integrality of G and properness when H is a Borel. The new incidence node then supplies the closed set with exactly the rational saturation. The remaining argument is given in full: irreducibility forces the two nonempty opens to meet, and the statements of lem-sc-lft-over-field-jacobson-space and lem-weil-pbc-closed-point-residue-field-base give a rational point there, contradicting containment. These generic scheme tools are declared in split_report.tool_uses when off-unit. No equality between a union of rational conjugates and all scheme points is silently assumed.

I tested the small cases: G trivial; G a torus (C=G, U=1, quotient a point); G connected unipotent (T=1, C=G); GL2 (C a diagonal torus, two normalizer cosets); and the upper triangular group (the same neutral-centralizer method without reductivity). In the incidence statement H=1 has only the identity saturation and its identity element does not have finitely many fixed cosets unless G is trivial; H=G has a point quotient and both conclusions hold. This excludes the tempting false unconditional density statement. Nonreduced stabilizers are not identified with their rational points in any construction. Positive characteristic is allowed throughout and the neutral centralizer in the source is smooth. The source is Milne, not an SGA trace route.
