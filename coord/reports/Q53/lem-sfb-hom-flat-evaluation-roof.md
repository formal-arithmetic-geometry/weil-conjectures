### Old step 1

Exactness of $E$ and the natural cohomology comparison give $H^r(Eu)=E(H^r(u))$, so $E$ preserves quasi-isomorphisms. For any complex $M$, choose a flat-term K-flat resolution $T\to M$. The defining K-flat computation of $E^{\mathbf L}QM$ and the quasi-isomorphism $ET\to EM$ identify it canonically with $Q(EM)$. These identifications commute with maps, since both are induced by localization of the same exact functor. Set $e=E\eta_C:EC\to EJ$. It is a quasi-isomorphism. Naturality gives $I_R(e)\eta_{EC}=\eta_{EJ}e$, and both augmentations are quasi-isomorphisms; hence $I_R(e)$ is a quasi-isomorphism between K-injectives. Apply Hom-invariance with first-variable map $1_P$ and second-variable map $I_R(e)$. It gives the required quasi-isomorphism $v_S=\mathcal Hom_R(P,I_R(e))$.
