## Review by O3

I checked the files against delivery-errors B–E with their statements and the published pages open. Elkik I Lemma 1 explicitly uses the intersection of parameter torsion with a sufficiently deep principal power and improves the error to order 2(n-h). Its opening Newton argument on printed p.556 is the iteration used here. Gabber–Ramero (5.4.4) on printed p.148 identifies ideal membership with a scalar factorization of the conormal map. Their Lemma 5.4.8 is formulated with a non-zero-divisor in the surrounding setup, so I used its interpretation only and proved the torsion-compatible adaptation separately. The statement of the new node is self-contained: J², evaluation, torsion separation, and partial derivatives are defined inline or by exact definition nodes. Its statement contains no proof, and its proof dependencies are empty.

Step 1 cites exactly the definition of Noetherianity, which says every ideal is finitely generated. The parameter-torsion elements form an ideal by taking maxima of exponents. Finite generators yield a common annihilating exponent c. If t^c y is torsion then y is torsion, hence t^c y=0. Thus the required intersection vanishes without Artin–Rees, an extra theorem, or regularity of t. The constants are chosen before n and a, with n>2h and n-h>c.

Step 2 uses the actual statement of `def-per-gabber-ramero-obstruction-ideal`: H is an intersection of the presentation ideals; membership in H_P supplies a B-linear scalar factorization of the explicit partial-derivative map, and the B-action on J/J² is by representatives. This is sufficient to lift the values on the finite free basis to g_i in J and obtain the displayed identities for every f in J. The polynomial G is chosen from the finite expression witnessing ideal membership at the particular point; no universal choice uniform in a is claimed or needed. Ideality follows by adding and scaling witnesses. Finite presentation follows from the given finite variables and finite generators of J, so this use of the definition has all its hypotheses.

Step 3 uses precisely both conclusions of the new finite-correction node. Its hypotheses are the torsion separation from Step 1, the identities from Step 2, G(u)=t^h, and the stated q inequalities. To verify independently the new statement, choose g_i(a)=t^q v_i and set z_i=t^(q-h)v_i, a_i^+=a_i-z_i. Let E=f(a)-sum(partial_i f)(a)z_i. Evaluation of J² lies in t^(2q)R, so t^h E=t^(2q)w. Therefore E-t^(2q-h)w is killed by t^h. Both terms lie in t^(q-h)R, hence in t^cR, and the torsion-separation hypothesis makes their difference zero. It follows E belongs to t^(2q-h)R, which is contained in t^(2q-2h)R. In the monomial expansion of f(a-z), its constant and linear terms are f(a)-sum(partial_i f)(a)z_i; every remaining term contains at least two z factors and lies in t^(2q-2h)R. This proves the finite improvement. No division or cancellation by a zero divisor occurs. This argument is stored here for the next worker, not as proof steps in the new statement-only node.

Step 4 uses the full unit assertion in `lem-weil-pbc-adic-complete-jacobson-radical`, namely 1+x is a unit for x in the completion ideal. It applies because A is complete for tA and q-2h>=1. The polynomial congruence makes G(u^+)=t^h times such a unit, so the evaluated obstruction ideal still contains t^h. The recursion is initialized by the input a; its invariant is stated and q_k=2h+2^k(n-2h) checks all later hypotheses. The only off-unit non-definition citation is this generic unit lemma, declared in split_report.tool_uses.

Step 5 uses exactly the compatible-sequence and bijectivity assertions of `def-weil-pbc-adic-completion`. Coordinate residues eventually stabilize; they are compatible, hence have a unique preimage b. Evaluation occurs in the quotient ring, so polynomial continuity requires no separate theorem. For each fixed f and each m the m-th residue is zero, and injectivity gives f(b)=0. The same stabilization at order n-h gives the required proximity. No closedness theorem for arbitrary ideals or unchecked exchange of limits is used.
