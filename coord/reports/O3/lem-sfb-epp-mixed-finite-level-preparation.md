For step 1 I opened lem-per-complete-dvr-finite-normalization: it gives completeness, locality and the uniqueness assertion identifying the given C with normalization, not merely existence of some DVR. The coefficient-arithmetic statement gives both unchanged residue field and e=[E:K]. Thus adjoining zeta is totally ramified; characteristic zero gives separability and the algebraically closed residue hypothesis is available. The totally-ramified-base-change statement supplies the field L0 and the actual normalization B0 as B tensor A0. It does not assume L/K finite. The algebraic-closure node supplies roots, and the elementary argument using p distinct roots finds a primitive root for prime p, including p=2.

For steps 2 and 5 I checked the new prime-Galois-scalar-algebra statement supplies BOTH the whole algebra's reduction and the exact split/field dichotomy, including Galoisness and degree p. Its general source subargument is the split-or-degree-p assertion in 09F4. A proof to pursue when splitting that node is to write a separable primitive polynomial; normality makes its automorphism group act transitively on its factors after scalar extension. A prime-order group then gives either a single degree-p factor or p linear factors. Each field factor is a separable splitting field, hence Galois. This is a genuine field lemma independent of the Epp preparation, retained as a statement only. In the split case normalization uniqueness gives B0 (or B1) on EVERY branch. The existing weak-solution-descent node explicitly identifies normalization under successive coefficient base changes branchwise. Thus these steps prove the weak solution for the original A to C, not just one chosen branch.

For step 3 I read the complete finite-Galois-fixed-field statement and take only its |Aut|=[M:L] clause. The prime-order Mathlib leaf makes the group cyclic; the cyclic Kummer leaf then applies with the primitive root already in B0. A zero Kummer generator would generate no degree-p extension, so its pth power has a finite integer valuation. These hypotheses are not silently inferred from a definition node.

For step 4 I read the existing root-algebra node carefully: it asserts the DVR and uniformizer for ALL n>0, and does not require p to be invertible in A0. Rank p and the fraction field degree follow by explicit monic division and inverting pi=theta^p. Normalization uniqueness then identifies this algebra with the integral closure. Its derivative shows separability in characteristic zero. The coefficient-arithmetic node, applied to the original A and the final field, gives total ramification for the composite; there is no assumption that a composite of arbitrary extensions is automatically totally ramified. The base-change node gives the normalized B1 and its uniformizer. The valuation identity v_B1(b)=p v_B0(b) is the uniformizer identity pi=theta^p, so negative valuations work as well as positive ones.

For step 6 the 09EX node says precisely that the actual root ring is the normalization and has ramification index one even when the residue extension is purely inseparable. This is weaker than etaleness and is exactly the weak-solution clause needed. In the other case a residue pth root is nonzero since u is a unit, and every lift is a unit. The written rescaling y/v produces the specific congruence y^p=1+d without changing the generated field.

Step 7 writes out the substitution z=(y-1)/w and its inverse y=1+wz. It proves the generator and w^p P(z) in the maximal ideal by calculation, with no new node restating finite level. In step 8 I read the full statements of lem-sfb-epp-kummer-pole-calculus and lem-sfb-epp-kummer-branch-classification after the live duplicate check exposed them. The pole-calculus statement supplies the actual monic degree-p polynomial, its coefficients and the positive integer v(w), under exactly the mixed-characteristic DVR and primitive-root hypotheses available. The minimum exists in a nonempty subset of nonnegative integers, and v(w^p xi)>0 gives the strict bound N<p e1. At level zero, xi is integral. Because z generates the degree-p extension, its degree-p monic annihilating polynomial P-xi is its minimal polynomial (division by that polynomial proves the quotient identification). Thus the algebra D in the branch-classification statement is exactly M1, not merely a larger algebra containing a root. The explicit integral-parameter clause of that statement gives ramification index one on every normalized factor. The required finite-level inequality holds since w is in the maximal ideal and xi is integral. This proves the stated ideal identity for the actual C1. No negative-valuation classification is taken from this citation.

For edge cases I checked p=2, the trivial cyclotomic extension K0=K, split tensor algebras, an initially p-divisible valuation, negative p-divisible valuations, a unit with nonpower residue, and level zero. For the reused 09EX statement a nonpower residue is necessarily nonzero, so its root is a unit and the same uniformizer does generate the maximal ideal.
