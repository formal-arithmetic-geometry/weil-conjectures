### Original step 1

Put $\mathcal B=f^{-1}\mathcal O_V$ on $U$ and $C^\bullet=\operatorname{Res}_{\mathcal B}\widetilde{M^\bullet}$. Choose a K-flat resolution $P^\bullet\to C^\bullet$ by [lem-pc-kflat-resolution]. For $x=\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $s=f(x)=\mathfrak p$, [lem-pc-inverse-image-structure-stalk-iso] identifies $\mathcal B_x$ with $\mathcal O_{V,s}=R_{\mathfrak p}$ and its structural map with the actual scheme stalk map. The affine associated-sheaf stalk formula, applied in each degree and to each differential, identifies $(\widetilde{M^\bullet})_x$ with $M^\bullet_{\mathfrak q}$. The scalar-restriction comparison [lem-pc-relative-inverse-image-stalk-restriction] therefore identifies $C^\bullet_x$, with scalars transported to $R_{\mathfrak p}$, with $M^\bullet_{\mathfrak q}$. Exactness of the stalk functor takes the chosen resolution to a quasi-isomorphism on stalks. Thus $Q(P_x^\bullet)\cong Q(M^\bullet_{\mathfrak q})$ in $D(R_{\mathfrak p})$.

Cited nodes: lem-pc-kflat-resolution, lem-pc-inverse-image-structure-stalk-iso, lem-weil-pbc-spec-tilde-stalk-localization, lem-pc-relative-inverse-image-stalk-restriction, lem-weil-pbc-module-stalk-exact.

### Original step 2

For any $\mathcal B$-module sheaf $\mathcal F$, apply [lem-pc-stalk-kflat-tensor-homology] to the ringed space $(U,\mathcal B)$, $P^\bullet$, and $R_{\mathfrak p}\cong\mathcal B_x$. Together with Step 1 it gives, in every degree, $$H^i(E\otimes_{\mathcal B}^{\mathbf L}\mathcal F)_x\cong H^i(M^\bullet_{\mathfrak q}\otimes_{R_{\mathfrak p}}^{\mathbf L}\mathcal F_x),$$ as abelian groups, with the stated scalar transport on $\mathcal F_x$. Consequently, if every $M^\bullet_{\mathfrak q}$ has amplitude $[a,b]$ over $R_{\mathfrak p}$, all these stalks vanish for $i\notin[a,b]$. Exactness is detected on stalks by [lem-weil-pbc-module-stalk-exact], applied to $0\to H^i\to0$, so the cohomology sheaf itself vanishes. Conversely, assume (1). For a fixed $x$ and arbitrary $R_{\mathfrak p}$-module $L$, transport $L$ to a $\mathcal B_x$-module and take $\mathcal F=i_{x,*}L$ on the ringed space $(U,\mathcal B)$ [def-pc-ringed-module-skyscraper]. The stalk recovery statement gives $\mathcal F_x\cong L$. The displayed comparison and (1) now give vanishing against every $L$. We have proved that (1) is equivalent to amplitude $[a,b]$ for all the localized complexes over their contracted base-prime rings.

Cited nodes: lem-pc-stalk-kflat-tensor-homology, lem-weil-pbc-module-stalk-exact, def-pc-ringed-module-skyscraper, lem-pc-skyscraper-complex-stalk-recovery.

### Original step 3

Apply [lem-trf-relative-tor-amplitude-localization] with the given map $R\to A$, the given complex $M^\bullet$, and the fixed integers $a\le b$. Its condition (2) is exactly the localized amplitude condition at the end of Step 2, with $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap R$. Its condition (1) is precisely condition (2) of the item. Combining the two equivalences proves (1)$\Longleftrightarrow$(2). This does not replace relative amplitude over $R$ by absolute amplitude over $A$.

Cited nodes: lem-trf-relative-tor-amplitude-localization.

### Original step 4

Condition (1) plainly implies its restriction to sheaves $f^{-1}\mathcal G$. For the reverse implication fix $x=\mathfrak q$, $s=\mathfrak p=f(x)$ and an arbitrary $R_{\mathfrak p}$-module $L$. Set $\mathcal G=i_{s,*}L$ on $(V,\mathcal O_V)$; [lem-pc-skyscraper-complex-stalk-recovery] in degree zero gives $\mathcal G_s\cong L$. The inverse-image stalk formula for abelian sheaves gives $(f^{-1}\mathcal G)_x\cong\mathcal G_s$. It is $\mathcal B_x\cong\mathcal O_{V,s}$-linear: both scalar actions are obtained by taking germs of multiplication of representatives on a common neighbourhood, exactly as in [def-pc-topological-inverse-image-module]. Testing with $\mathcal F=f^{-1}\mathcal G$ in the comparison of Step 2 therefore shows $H^i(M^\bullet_{\mathfrak q}\otimes_{R_{\mathfrak p}}^{\mathbf L}L)=0$ outside $[a,b]$. Step 2 implies (1). This verifies the extra inverse-image-test assertion, even when $f$ is not surjective and $\mathcal G$ is not quasi-coherent.

Cited nodes: def-pc-ringed-module-skyscraper, lem-pc-skyscraper-complex-stalk-recovery, lem-weil-pbc-topological-inverse-image-stalk, def-pc-topological-inverse-image-module.

### Original step 5

Suppose $E$ locally has finite Tor dimension over $\mathcal B$. Choose open neighbourhoods $W_x$ and intervals $[a_x,b_x]$ on which its tensor cohomology vanishes against every $\mathcal B|_{W_x}$-module. The stalk comparison and skyscraper argument of Step 2 apply on the ringed space $(W_x,\mathcal B|_{W_x})$: choose its K-flat resolution using [lem-pc-kflat-resolution] and apply [lem-pc-stalk-kflat-tensor-homology]; each stalk-module test is realized by [lem-pc-skyscraper-complex-stalk-recovery]. Hence for every $y\in W_x$ the stalk complex $E_y$ has amplitude $[a_x,b_x]$ over $\mathcal B_y$. Restricting a sheaf to an open leaves its stalk at a point of that open unchanged: the neighbourhoods contained in the open are cofinal among all neighbourhoods, with identical fraction-free germ and scalar formulas. Since $U$ is affine, [lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs] gives a finite subcover $W_1,\ldots,W_r$. If $U$ is nonempty, take $a=\min_j a_j$ and $b=\max_j b_j$. Every point is in one of these opens, so its stalk complex has amplitude $[a,b]$. Step 2 gives global (1), and Step 3 gives a single finite $R$-Tor interval for $M^\bullet$. If $U$ is empty, every sheaf on $U$ is zero, so (1) holds for $[0,0]$, and Step 3 still applies.

Cited nodes: lem-pc-kflat-resolution, lem-pc-stalk-kflat-tensor-homology, lem-pc-skyscraper-complex-stalk-recovery, lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs.

### Original step 6

The single open neighbourhood $U$ at every point then witnesses local finite Tor dimension. For $M^\bullet=0$ every interval works. For the identity map $R=A$, the formulas reduce to the usual affine absolute criterion. No step has used flatness or finite presentation of $R\to A$, a bound on $M^\bullet$, or a Noetherian hypothesis.

Cited nodes: elementary.
