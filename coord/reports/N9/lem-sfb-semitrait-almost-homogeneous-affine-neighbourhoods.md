## Findings and scope

The statement agrees with Anantharaman 4.0.1 and 4.2.2 in the already imported flat separated finite-presentation scheme case. I found no statement dispute and made no statement change. In particular homogeneity means fppf-local transitivity of pairs; it does not require a rational point, connectedness of G, a nonempty fibre at each closed point, or commutativity. The finite invariant cover is preserved.

The main dangers in expanding the reference were assuming smoothness originally, treating a finite fraction-field extension as a finite normalization of the base without excellence, and using only one lift of a finite set in descent. The new cover node gives a smooth model with an integral finite-fibre projection; it does not assert that projection finite over X, and it does not assert that R' is a finite R-module. The transfer lemma requires the entire inverse image. Neither of these assertions is obtained merely from the existing fpqc object-effectivity lemma, whose hypotheses are different.

## Review by N9

I read Anantharaman's Definition 4.0.1, Proposition 4.2.2 and its proof on printed pp.53–54,57, the entire statement and proof of 4.2.3 on pp.58–59, the quoted Raynaud proposition on p.57, and the statement of 4.1.3 on p.55. I also read Appendix II Corollaries 2 and 3 on p.73 to verify the finite normalization used in the smooth-cover reduction. The new cover statement combines precisely that reduction, taking the neutral component of the normalized smooth group. The last paragraph of 4.2.3 identifies its orbits over algebraic closures with the connected components of the normalized space; this is the geometric orbit condition actually used. The source does not require excellence or commutativity. Its normalization of the base need not be finite as a module; that stronger assertion was deliberately not inserted.

For step 1 I opened the full statements of the new potential-smooth-cover node and lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian. The first supplies T, H, Y, q, normality and the geometric orbit decomposition, rather than just promising a normalization without its properties. The second asserts Noetherianity of a finite-type scheme over a Noetherian scheme, with no smoothness restriction. R is Noetherian and affine, and finite presentation of X includes finite type, so it applies. This checks the target-local-Noetherian hypothesis used in step 5.

For step 2 I read lem-sfb-dedekind-smooth-fibre-dense-affine. It really supplies an affine open dense in every geometric fibre of a smooth separated finite-type scheme over an arbitrary Noetherian integrally closed dimension-one domain. It is not a group-only or connected-only statement. The ring and scheme from step 1 meet exactly those hypotheses. I use only that clause, not the additional Cartier boundary; there is no uncited claim that arbitrary intersections of affine opens are affine.

For step 3 I checked each of its three cited statements, quoted separately in citations.json. The saturation input assumes an affine normal Noetherian domain, a smooth separated finite-type acting group with geometrically connected fibres, and a smooth separated finite-type scheme, and concludes openness and an ample invertible module on H.U. The other two inputs give the orbit decomposition and geometric density. The equality H.U=Y is written out: every nonempty open orbit meets the dense open, and transitivity sends a point of that intersection to a geometric point of the orbit after an algebraically closed extension. Images on point sets do not change under extension of the chosen geometric point. Every underlying point has such a geometric representative. This is the elementary use of the orbit condition, not an unstated general homogeneous-space quasi-projectivity theorem. Openness is a stated conclusion of the saturation node, and equality of open subschemes follows from their identical open sets.

For step 4 I opened lem-sc-ample-finite-set-affine, whose statement allows an arbitrary scheme with an ample invertible module, not just a curve. Its source is Stacks Tag 01ZY, properties.tex label lemma-ample-finite-set-in-affine. Thus it applies to Y and the finite set consisting of every point over E. Finiteness of that set is the elementary finite-union-of-finite-sets argument from the explicitly finite fibres of q. No closedness or rationality of E is used.

For step 5 the transfer node states the exact equivalence needed and makes no flatness assumption. I checked its source 4.1.3: the target must be locally Noetherian, q must be integral and surjective with set-theoretically finite fibres, and the affine upstairs must contain the whole inverse image. Step 1 and step 4 discharge those hypotheses. This avoids incorrectly applying the stronger fpqc object-effectivity statement to a normalization which need not be flat over X. The conclusion is the original property (FA), with no change in X, G, or the topology in the hypothesis.

Small cases: X empty gives Y empty and the empty affine open. The empty finite set is allowed by both finite-set lemmas. If G is trivial and X=S, take the identity smooth cover and saturation; all claims hold. A finite disjoint union of copies of S tests several orbits and disconnected X, and its finite affine coproduct contains a prescribed finite set. A generic-only homogeneous piece, such as G_m over K viewed over a DVR, has empty closed fibre and remains covered by the same argument; no step selects a point in an empty fibre. The non-smooth characteristic-p group alpha_p acting on itself tests why passing to a smooth reduced normalized cover is necessary; it is not replaced by an assumption of smoothness on the item. For the saturation input, U empty gives W empty, and the translation action of G_m on P^1 tests that the input is about the actual saturation (an affine or punctured open), not an assertion that every arbitrary H-scheme is ample. For transfer, the identity and a nilpotent closed thickening test respectively the tautological and nonflat cases; integrality/surjectivity alone must not be replaced by an open immersion.
