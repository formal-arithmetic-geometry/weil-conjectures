### Old Step 1

If $y\in C_\Omega$ is a point where $C_\Omega\to\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$ is not smooth, let $x$ be its image in $C$. If $C\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ were smooth on an open neighbourhood $U$ of $x$, its base change $U\times_{\operatorname{Spec}k}\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$, which is the open inverse image of $U$ in $C_\Omega$ and contains $y$, would be smooth by clause (2) of [lem-pc-proper-base-change]. Hence $x$ is nonsmooth, and by hypothesis there is a ring isomorphism $\varphi:\widehat A\cong k[[u,v]]/(uv)$, where $A=\mathcal O_{C,x}$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ and $\widehat A$ is its $\mathfrak m$-adic completion. The isomorphism is only a ring isomorphism, so the residue field is identified as follows. The ring $k[[u,v]]/(uv)$ is local with residue field $k$ (a power series with nonzero constant term is a unit). The map $\widehat A\to A/\mathfrak m$, $(x_n)\mapsto x_1$, of [def-weil-pbc-adic-completion] is surjective, since its composite with $A\to\widehat A$ is the quotient map. Its kernel is therefore a maximal ideal, and since $\widehat A\cong k[[u,v]]/(uv)$ is local, it is the maximal ideal of $\widehat A$. Thus $\kappa(x)=A/\mathfrak m$ is isomorphic as a field to $k$, so $\kappa(x)$ is algebraically closed. Choose an affine open neighbourhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}S$ of $x$ with $S$ a finitely generated $k$-algebra [def-weil-finite-type-morphism], and let $\mathfrak p\subset S$ be the prime of $x$. Then $\kappa(x)=\operatorname{Frac}(S/\mathfrak p)$ is a finitely generated field extension of $k$. Suppose it had transcendence degree $d\ge1$. Choose a transcendence basis $t_1,\dots,t_d$ among the generators, so that $e=[\kappa(x):k(t_1,\dots,t_d)]$ is finite. Take a prime $\ell>e$ different from the characteristic. The polynomial $X^\ell-t_1$ is irreducible over $k(t_1,\dots,t_d)$ by Eisenstein's criterion at the prime $t_1$ of $k[t_1,\dots,t_d]$ and Gauss's lemma. It has a root in the algebraically closed field $\kappa(x)$, so $\ell\le e$, a contradiction. Hence $\operatorname{trdeg}_k\kappa(x)=0$. By [lem-kun-closed-point-field](a), applied to the finite-type $k$-scheme $U$, the point $x$ is closed in $U$ (so $\mathfrak p$ is a maximal ideal of $S$) and $\kappa(x)$ is finite over $k$. Since $k$ is algebraically closed, the structure map $k\to\kappa(x)=S/\mathfrak p$ is an isomorphism.

Uses: def-weil-finite-type-morphism, def-sbc-smooth-morphisms, def-weil-pbc-adic-completion, def-weil-pbc-multivariate-power-series, lem-pc-proper-base-change, lem-kun-closed-point-field

### Old Step 2

With $U=\operatorname{Spec}S$ and the maximal ideal $\mathfrak p$ of Step 1, $S/\mathfrak p=k$. By the affine fibre-product identification, the open inverse image of $U$ in $C_\Omega$ is $U_\Omega=\operatorname{Spec}(S\otimes_k\Omega)$. The fibre of $U_\Omega\to U$ over $x$ is $\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(x)\otimes_k\Omega)=\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$, a single point, namely the prime $\mathfrak q=\ker(S\otimes_k\Omega\to\Omega)$, $s\otimes\lambda\mapsto\bar s\lambda$. Therefore $y$ is this point. Put $A=S_{\mathfrak p}$ and $B=(S\otimes_k\Omega)_{\mathfrak q}$, so that $A=\mathcal O_{C,x}$ and $B=\mathcal O_{C_\Omega,y}$.

### Old Step 3

Apply [lem-trf-separable-closed-point-formal-presentation] to $S$, $\mathfrak p$ and $A$. Its hypothesis holds because $F=S/\mathfrak p=k$, which is trivially finite separable over $k$. It supplies a presentation: a $k$-algebra section $\sigma:k\to\widehat A$, necessarily the structure map, and a continuous surjection of $k$-algebras $T=k[[t_1,\ldots,t_n]]\twoheadrightarrow\widehat A$ with $n\ge2$ and kernel $I=(f_1,\ldots,f_s)\subseteq(t_1,\ldots,t_n)$. Thus $\widehat A\cong T/I$ as $k$-algebras. Combined with $\varphi$ of Step 1, $T/I$ is isomorphic as a ring to $k[[u,v]]/(uv)=k[[u,v]]/(au^2+buv+cv^2)$ with $a=c=0$, $b=1$ and $b^2-4ac=1\ne0$. Clause (c)$\Rightarrow$(b) of [lem-trf-formal-jacobian-quadratic-criterion] (with $F=k$) gives a complete local $k$-algebra isomorphism $T/I\cong k[[u,v]]/(q)$, where $q=a'u^2+b'uv+c'v^2$ with $b'^2-4a'c'\ne0$. Since $k$ is algebraically closed, $q=\ell_1\ell_2$ with $\ell_1=\alpha u+\beta v$ and $\ell_2=\gamma u+\delta v$. If $a'\ne0$, factor using the roots of $a'z^2+b'z+c'$; if $a'=0$, take $q=v(b'u+c'v)$. Then $b'^2-4a'c'=(\alpha\delta+\beta\gamma)^2-4\alpha\beta\gamma\delta=(\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma)^2$ in every characteristic, so $\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma\ne0$. The $k$-linear substitution $u\mapsto\ell_1$, $v\mapsto\ell_2$ is therefore a continuous $k$-algebra automorphism of $k[[u,v]]$ carrying $(uv)$ onto $(q)$. Composing gives a $k$-algebra isomorphism $k[[u,v]]/(uv)\cong\widehat A$; it is continuous because it carries the maximal ideal onto the maximal ideal. Hence $k[[u,v]]\twoheadrightarrow\widehat A$, with $\sigma$ the structure map, is a presentation with $n=2$ and single relation $f_1=uv\in(u,v)$. The base-change clause of the same node, for the extension field $K=\Omega$ with the given inclusion $k=F\hookrightarrow\Omega$ [def-pc-field-extension], holds for every presentation. It gives $\widehat B\cong\Omega[[u,v]]/(uv)$ as complete local $\Omega$-algebras, in particular as rings. This is the required conclusion at $y$.
