## Review by N9

I checked BLR 4.3 Theorem 6 against every hypothesis of the item, then read the proof of the final assertion in 5.1 Theorem 5. A smooth generic group and a nonempty special fibre give both nonempty fibres required there. The two universal translations and associativity are exactly BLR 5.1 Definition 1. Neither connectedness, commutativity, completeness nor excellence is needed. The no-shrinking paragraph is essential: existence on a smaller open by itself does not imply the item.

For step 1 I read the new strictification statement against BLR 5.2 Definition 1 and Proposition 2, including the proof's removal of closures of the bad projection-fibre loci. I checked the strengthened choice containing the whole generic fibre: start with the translation isomorphisms on an open containing the generic product (the given generic translations are everywhere isomorphisms); the bad loci are then contained in the closed fibre, and their closures do not remove any generic-fibre point. The new statement gives a smooth separated finite-type open, so the strict realization theorem applies without an implicit finiteness claim. The realization node is BLR 5.2 Theorem 3 followed by the explicit descent in 6.5 Corollary 2; it is stated for a strict law, not just for an associative rational map. Its uniqueness clause is precisely the comparison used next.

In step 2 I checked that G itself is a realization of the strict law on U_E=G, with identity embedding. The field case of the realization node gives a group isomorphism a:M_E to G satisfying a i_E=id. This implies that i_E is its inverse, not just a dense open embedding into an unknown generic group. This eliminates an otherwise missing identification.

For step 3 I opened lem-sfb-relative-weil-codimension-extension and used exactly its extension assertion for a smooth scheme over an open in a one-dimensional Noetherian integrally closed domain. Here the open is all of Spec R. I checked those ring hypotheses from the DVR definition and the elementary monic-equation valuation argument written in the step. The new immersion criterion explicitly supplies the missing codimension-one-domain assertion; it is not taken from a proof of a different node. On a smooth trait scheme the total space is regular, and a point of the special fibre of height at most one is generic in that fibre, since the uniformizer is a nonzerodivisor. A fibrewise dense open therefore contains it, while all generic-fibre points are already in U. No properness of M or Y is assumed or used.

For step 4 I checked the new immersion criterion against BLR 5.1/5's last paragraph, generalized to a morphism of smooth models: on each relative-dimension piece the determinant of f*Omega_Z to Omega_X is invertible on U, whose complement has codimension at least two. Its nonunit locus on a regular scheme would have a codimension-one component. Thus the determinant is everywhere invertible, f is étale, and the separated birational étale map to the normal target is an open immersion by Zariski Main Theorem. This criterion is independent of group laws and is a genuine smaller geometric lemma. The empty special fibre and zero-dimensional relative pieces cause no problem; the determinant in rank zero is one. With U=X the criterion reduces to its given open immersion. I also tested the necessity of smoothness and density: blowups with exceptional divisors fail the smooth-model or fibrewise dense immersion-locus hypotheses, and dilatations like x mapping to pi*x fail the special-fibre dense-immersion hypothesis. Disconnected smooth fibres and nonperfect residue fields were not discarded.
