## Prospective proofs of the statement-only inputs

For the parameter scheme, follow Conrad Theorem 3.2.6. Fppf-locally obtain a maximal torus, identify its conjugacy functor with G/N_G(T), construct the quotient using the smooth normalizer and the quasi-affine construction, and descend that representing scheme. The pointwise self-centralizer condition needed by the theorem holds in reductive geometric fibres. The quotient description gives smoothness; existence of maximal tori on geometric fibres gives surjectivity. Corollary 3.2.7 then applies smooth local sections; that last application is already in the item's step 1, not hidden in the new statement.

For torus trivialization, use Conrad B.3.4: finite-presentation multiplicative-type groups are etale locally diagonalizable. A torus is such a group by its fppf-local presentation. Its geometric character groups are free of finite rank, so the local diagonalizable presentations have finite free character lattices. The proof passes through strictly henselian limits, lifts the special-fibre diagonalization to the completion, and descends the homomorphism by its finite torsion restrictions and fpqc descent. This is a substantive theorem about changing the local topology, not an elementary refinement of an fppf cover.

For relative root lines/coroots, use the smooth tangent bundle at the identity and the grading under D(M), as in the Section 4 introduction. Zero weights identify with Lie(T) and nonzero geometric root weights have dimension one. Weight formation commutes with arbitrary base change. If alpha persists with rank one, the rank-one centralizer of its saturated character-kernel torus is reductive; apply Theorem 4.2.6 there. The unique coroot supplied by its opposite-root Gauss relation agrees with the classical geometric coroots and commutes with base change. The already existing matrix-sheaf node identifies this morphism with a locally constant cocharacter. The proof of this new node will need to expose these structural ingredients, rather than assuming Noetherian hypotheses or inferring a group homomorphism from its Lie differential.
