I checked that an inclusion-minimal finite generating set exists by deleting redundant members of a given finite set. Any atom must occur in it; if a generator decomposes, an occurrence of itself on the right contradicts cancellation and sharpness, and no occurrence contradicts minimality. The torsion argument applies saturation to both g and -g, not just one of them. These are direct arguments from the cited definition; no monoid structure theorem is hidden here.

Step 2 uses the statement of lem-weil-pbc-nakayama-local-generators, which explicitly equates generation of a finite module and generation of its residue quotient. The finite module is I, already generated by the finitely many chart monomials, so no missing Noetherian-module lemma is needed. For each i the union of p_j+P with j different from i is a monoid ideal; the case of no such j is precisely the allowed empty ideal. Its extension is the ideal generated by the other monomials. An atom cannot belong to that union unless it equals one of the other listed atoms. A nonzero linear relation in the residue vector space removes one basis candidate and Nakayama contradicts contraction. Thus the proof establishes independence rather than just minimality by assertion.

Step 3 is finite-dimensional linear algebra, and writes out why n=r gives injectivity of the monoid map. Conversely a monoid isomorphism preserves atoms, whose count in the standard free monoid is r. It uses the proved atom generation, not a new node that simply repeats the freeness clause.
