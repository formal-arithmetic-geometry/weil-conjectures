## Review by Q60

I read the complete proof of Temkin Proposition 4.3.3, printed pp. 29--30, and its hypotheses at the beginning of §4. The parameter node was checked against Theorem 2.1.8, Lemmas 2.1.1, 2.1.2 and 2.1.6, and the precise smooth-centre application in Proposition 4.3.3. The finite-height assumption is necessary for the valuation-ring map to be etale rather than just essentially etale: R has finite height; the transcendence-degree-one Abhyankar bound gives finite height for the extending valuation and its rational subfield. Inverting t when necessary gives t∈W without changing K(t). The theorem is also valid for trivial valuations and imperfect separably closed K: it retains the smooth-centre condition instead of replacing it by normality or mere separability of a residue extension.

For the Riemann--Zariski node I read §3.2, Proposition 3.2.1, Lemma 3.2.2, Corollaries 3.2.4--3.2.5 and 3.2.7 and their proofs. The valuation ring is the union of model stalks, each stalk is the union of affine-neighbourhood rings, and models admit common refinements. These are the exact filtered objects required by algebra descent. I did not use a colimit indexed by an unspecified large category: the new statement explicitly supplies a small indexing category. Nonseparated Z is allowed; centres are morphisms as well as valuation rings, so different centres of the same valuation are not silently identified.

I opened every existing statement used in the twelve steps. Specifically:

- Step 1 uses horizontal-curve-models with empty marking for reducedness and finite component count, lfp-affine-opens-finite-presentation for A's finite presentation, and closed-irreducible-dimension-trdeg for its generic component's dimension/transcendence degree. The explicit product over other minimal primes rules out vertical components using flatness; no normality of X is assumed. Empty X is handled at the end.
- Step 2 checks the exact R=W∩K and KW centre hypotheses of the new parameter theorem. Localizing by V or by R gives the same ring since both give all of K. The original generic curve is smooth, so it provides the required smooth centre, even when its connected components are not being identified.
- Step 3 uses the new Riemann--Zariski ring-colimit clause, horizontal-curve-models for each reduced proper rational-curve model, and the affine finite-presentation node. These statements give the whole generic fibre identification and not just a dense generic open. All model rings lie in the common field F.
- Step 4 uses exactly the etale-algebra-filtered-colimit-descent statement (Stacks 00U2(9)), etale-scheme-base-change (02GO), and etale-algebra-flat (00U2(6)). I read these tags in the local Stacks source. The equality W⊗O F=L is justified by a field argument, and flatness proves B_j embeds into that field. This corrects the potential hidden assumption that an arbitrary descended etale model is automatically integral.
- Step 5 uses finite-presentation-algebra-factors-filtered-colimit (00QO/07C3) with base V, original finitely presented A, and the filtered system of base changes of B_j. Its statement gives the actual A→B_j factorization needed for the X-map.
- Step 6 applies the *existing* semistable-cofinality theorem to P_j→P, where P is the affine line, not to the possibly nonsemistable X. That node provides the eta-isomorphisms. Schematic density and the integral generic open imply that Z is integral. The new Riemann--Zariski node provides the valuation lift. The affine finite-presentation node applies after choosing E in the preimage of D_j.
- Step 7 uses the actual base-change/flatness statements and flat-ring-map-comp (00HC), the flat affine criterion, affine lfp→fp, and finite-presentation composition (00F4). I checked the two maps into W agree on A_j; their tensor map is the lift, not just a topological centre. The argument B→L is repeated after this base change, where it is needed.
- Step 8 uses dimension-trdeg and valuation-domain-fibre-dimensions (local Stacks 00QK, including equidimensionality) after localizing V at each prime. The localized algebra is still a domain containing V_p, finite type over it, and the generic ring is unchanged. Empty fibres cause no problem. Etale-smooth-zero-dimensional-fibres and smooth-relative-dimension-permanence supply the actual smoothness of Y_eta, with relative dimensions 0+1.
- Step 9 uses composition of etale morphisms (02GN) for the charts. A finite set of V-generators also generates over A, and the X-map factors through a qc affine open. Thus the quasi-modification is separated and finite type, not asserted merely because it is birational.

I checked finite type, separatedness and qcqs of the affine target. A nonzero prime of a one-dimensional domain is maximal; this verifies the closed fibres, and the generic fibre is one point.

The schematic image is integral because its affine rings embed in L; it is finite because it is closed in the finite M. The open Y_eta retains its structure under this image. Therefore the finite map to normal H is an isomorphism and the original generic map really is open.
- Step 12 uses precisely compactness and open quasi-model embeddings of the new Riemann--Zariski theorem. The map to the component is scheme-theoretic by the kernel calculation. Finite selection is elementary after those statements; all valuations and all centres are covered.
