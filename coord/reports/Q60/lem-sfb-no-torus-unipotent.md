I checked the actual PDF text of Milne Proposition 16.60, printed p.346 (PDF p.358), rather than reconstructing the theorem from its title. Its proof chooses a faithful representation, takes a closed flag orbit, passes from its possibly nonreduced stabilizer to the reduced identity subgroup, removes its torus complement and applies Proposition 14.26 to the quotient. I also read Theorem 4.9 and Corollary 4.10 on faithful closed matrix realizations, Theorem 16.33(a)--(d), Proposition 7.15 and its displayed Cartesian square, Corollaries 14.6 and 14.12, Proposition 14.26, and Appendix A.75--A.76. I do not use any SGA source. The new node has no proof or proof dependencies and contains no proof sketch.

For step 1 I opened the three cited statements: flag-realization gives the nonzero faithful representation by applying its first clause to H=1 and gives projectivity of the full flag scheme independently of the chosen flag; orbit geometry gives the strict dimension drop in any boundary, so a minimum-dimensional orbit has empty boundary; homogeneous-orbit geometry identifies that orbit with G/J scheme-theoretically. The action on flags is obtained by applying the linear representation to each subspace. The closed reduced orbit is proper by the flag node itself. This avoids assuming that G is solvable or that J is smooth.

For step 2 I checked the final clauses of flag-realization and nested-quotient geometry. They state exactly that K=(J^0)_red is smooth connected solvable and that G/K to G/J is finite, including nonreduced J. The affine/proper/finite equivalence supplies properness of that finite map, and proper composition supplies properness of Q. I verified the map direction against the square in Milne 7.15; following its misprinted sentence would invalidate this step.

For step 3 I read all five cited statements. The perfect solvable-group theorem supplies K_u with torus quotient. Algebraic closedness implies perfectness, and the terminating commutator series is also a normal series with commutative successive quotients, as required by the normal-series formulation. The Borel/maximal-torus statement applies to K with its own whole solvable group as Borel, so it actually supplies a maximal torus instead of silently assuming one exists. The torus-complement theorem states that restriction to this torus is an isomorphism onto K/K_u; absence of tori in G makes this torus trivial because both subgroup inclusions are closed. Finally the normal-quotient statement gives the scheme-theoretic kernel, proving K=K_u as schemes. No inference from equality of rational point sets is made.

For step 4 I checked the full geometric-unipotence embedding statement. Its input is faithful injectivity on every algebra's points and unipotence at algebraic-closure points, not just injectivity on k-points. This is the faithful representation in the definition of K_u. Its conclusion is a closed unitriangular embedding, which meets exactly the explicit hypothesis of the new immersion theorem. The latter supplies the immersion of Q; a closed immersion into an open subscheme is monic directly on test-scheme maps.

For step 5 the quotient node states that q is faithfully flat. Nested-quotient geometry, applied to 1 contained in K, supplies finite presentation of q. Smooth source descent requires flatness, surjectivity and local finite presentation of q; these are now all present, and its composite G to Spec(k) is the given smooth structure morphism. The smooth-geometric-reducedness node applies over the base field to Q. Surjectivity and connectedness give connectedness by pulling back a hypothetical clopen partition; q(e) ensures nonemptiness. This explicitly repairs the potential gap between a one-point quotient topologically and Spec(k) as a scheme.

For step 6 I checked proper-to-separated with base Spec(k), then proper-monomorphism-closed with j. The affine space is separated (its diagonal is cut out by the coordinate differences), so the first applies. Proper monic j is a closed immersion by the second. The closed-immersion permanence statement explicitly gives affineness, and the finite/proper equivalence then makes Q finite over k. I read Stacks morphisms.tex, lemma-finite-proper, Tag 01WN, and etale.tex, lemma-characterize-closed-immersion, Tag 04XV, for these general scheme facts.

For step 7 I opened the finite-morphism definition, scheme connectedness/idempotents theorem and finite reduced algebra theorem. They supply exactly finite-dimensionality, absence of nontrivial idempotents, and the isomorphism k to B. Their other hypotheses are reducedness, 1 not equal to 0 and algebraic closedness, all obtained or assumed explicitly. No normality or characteristic-zero restriction was added.

For step 8 I checked the torsor identity in homogeneous-orbit geometry. Pulling it back at the identity on its first factor identifies K with the fibre of q at q(e). Since the preceding step identifies the whole quotient with Spec(k), this fibre is G. Thus the equality of groups is scheme-theoretic. The geometric-unipotence embedding theorem then supplies a closed immersion into a unitriangular group. The final nilpotence assertion is the written-out index-chain calculation for the mth power of a strictly upper triangular m by m matrix, valid in every characteristic and also for m=1.
