## Preserved arguments and the work assigned to each new node

The following is explanatory material for reviewers and future workers, not proof steps embedded in the unsplit nodes.

For the finite-total lemma, the intended argument is the original finite calculation. There are finitely many nonzero terms; take the fibre-product common refinement of their constructible stratifications. Restriction to a refined stratum gives locally constant finite modules. Tag 094H applied to degree-zero flat terms identifies their derived tensor cohomology in degree zero with the ordinary tensor, and gives finite local constancy there. Tensoring with a tensor product of two flat terms is the composite of two exact tensor functors; finite sums of such functors are exact. Hence each total term is flat and constructible. The diagonal support calculation gives [a+c,b+d]. Kernels, images and cokernels defining its cohomology stay constructible by Tag 03RZ; images can be expressed as kernels of cokernels. Tag 06YQ gives K-flatness of the bounded flat complex, and also of each input. The K-flat total-tensor computation identifies its derived image with the tensor of the input images. Tensoring it with a module in degree zero still has terms only in the same interval, proving the asserted test against every module. This node is a cochain-model result, not finite-coefficient closure of arbitrary derived objects: the parent must still descend the objects, choose flat models and compare sites.

For the continuous-generator lemma, the intended task is the finite-stratification and truncation argument including the triangulated-target prerequisite, for the explicit all-continuous coefficient. A finite constant scalar model is finite free because its scalar ring is the field Omega. Thus a constructible sheaf is lisse finite free on strata. One must establish kernels, cokernels and extension closure for this coefficient convention, by the local matrix/continuous-field argument and a common finite stratification, rather than silently applying the existing finite-subextension-valued heart theorem. The cohomology long exact sequence then makes bounded constructible objects a full replete triangulated subcategory. The exact open/closed sequence for a sheaf gives a distinguished triangle; induction on a finite filtration of the stratification expresses each constructible heart object by locally closed lisse extensions. Standard truncation triangles express a bounded complex by its finitely many nonzero cohomology sheaves, with the required shifts. Conversely each stratum extension is constructible (same model on its stratum, zero on the complement), and the established triangulated closure contains every finite cone construction. This equality, not merely one direction of generation, is necessary for the parent's double induction. Bhatt–Scholze 6.8.6–6.8.9 and the blueprint rational tensor proof provide the pattern; the all-continuous compatibility adaptation is explicitly agent-stated and not falsely attributed to a finite-subextension descent theorem.

For the intersection-comparison lemma, the intended task is the original locally closed projection calculation separated from any global tensor-closure assertion. Factor each immersion into open and closed factors. The general condensed support projection formulas apply to those factors; normalized extension composition makes the result independent of factorization. Base change for open extension and closed direct image gives a^*b_!M = r_!s^*M on the actual fibre product W. The two projection formulas identify (a_!L) tensor-derived (b_!M) with (ar)_!(r^*L tensor-derived s^*M). On a common pro-etale cover the last two modules are finite free of ranks m,n; their ordinary tensor is free of rank mn and the first factor is flat, so the tensor has cohomology only in degree zero. The extension has this local model on W and zero elsewhere. Prove the normalized comparison using the actual adjunction maps, not an unsupported “same on support” assertion. This mixed base-change/comparison argument is several functorial identifications, not a new node for the elementary identity R^m tensor R^n = R^(mn). It works for any commutative condensed coefficient and has no arbitrary-complex tensor-closure conclusion.
