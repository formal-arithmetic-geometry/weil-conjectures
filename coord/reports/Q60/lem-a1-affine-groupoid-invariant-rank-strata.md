## Review by Q60

I checked the item against Stacks 03BI with its groupoid hypotheses intact: the functorial groupoid structure supplies identity, inversion and associative composition; finiteness/local freeness is assumed for source and target; affineness of both schemes is assumed here, although the Stacks decomposition works more generally. The finite indexing set excludes empty strata, and the empty scheme explicitly gives the empty indexing set. I did not replace groupoids by equivalence relations or introduce connectedness, reducedness, Noetherianity, or a fixed degree.

Step 1 uses `lem-weil-pbc-finite-locally-free-degree-locally-constant`. Its statement explicitly supplies the degree function, the clopen decomposition and the restricted finite locally free maps, so the step does not infer these assertions from a definition alone. Step 2 uses the fibre-algebra definition in `def-pc-finite-locally-free`: the identity section gives a retraction onto the residue field and therefore a one-dimensional subspace of the fibre algebra. This proves positive degree even when the fibre is nonreduced; it does not substitute the number of geometric points for algebra dimension.

Step 3 cites only the universal property actually stated by `def-pc-fibre-product`. I checked the domains: in P one has s(a)=t(b), and in Q one has s(d)=s(b), so d composed with b inverse is defined and has source t(b). Both morphisms preserve b. Associativity and the two inverse laws give the displayed inverse identities. This proves the comparison at the level of morphisms of schemes, without assuming that a bijection on points is an isomorphism and without an uncited Yoneda theorem.

Step 4 uses clause (2) of `lem-weil-pbc-finite-flat-rank-iso`, which actually states rank preservation under arbitrary base change. The two base maps are respectively t and s, and the comparison in step 3 is over R, so the two ranks are evaluated at the same z. Their fibre algebra isomorphisms preserve dimensions. Equality of the inverse-image open subsets gives equality of the open subschemes, which is the invariance needed later. This is not a factorization through an arbitrary closed stratum based only on its underlying set.

Step 5 takes quasi-compactness from the statement of `lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs` and spells out the finite-subcover/disjointness argument. It does not assume a bound on ranks before obtaining it. Step 6 uses exactly the clopen-idempotent assertion of `lem-weil-pbc-spec-clopen-idempotent` and the affineness assertion in clause (0) of `lem-weil-pbc-affine-ideals-closed-subscheme`. Both are applied once over A and once over B; continuity makes the inverse-image subset of Spec B clopen. The ideal-sheaf construction in clause (1) is not used.

Step 7 uses `def-weil-open-immersion` for restriction through an open subscheme and `def-pc-finite-locally-free` for the conditions transported by the restricted inverse. The formulas for s composed with identity, inverse and composition prove that each structure map lands in the required open. The inverse interchanges source and target and squares to the identity, so its restriction really is an isomorphism over U_r between t_r and s_r. The same restricted maps satisfy the groupoid identities; none is newly chosen. This also proves the degree assertion for t_r, which would not follow merely from restricting s.

Small-case checks: for the zero ring U and R are empty and the index set is empty. For the identity groupoid on any nonempty affine scheme there is only the rank-one stratum. For the groupoid which is a disjoint union of a one-object constant group of order two and one of order three, the two nonempty strata have ranks two and three; connectedness is therefore not being silently assumed. Nonreduced finite group schemes still have positive algebra dimension, and all rank comparisons remain valid after arbitrary residue-field extensions. The translation calculation uses no faithfulness of R to U times U, so isotropy is allowed.
