`AdicCompletion.of ... (D F C (algebraMap B C b)) = 0`. Thus the canonical composite

The second conjunct gives a C-linear equivalence
`e : KaehlerDifferential B₁ C ≃ₗ[C] AdicCompletion m (KaehlerDifferential F C)`.
Its accompanying equation, for **every** `c : C`, says
`e (D B₁ C c) = AdicCompletion.of m (KaehlerDifferential F C) (D F C c)`.
This is the universal-property characterization of the induced comparison map.

### Edge cases checked before and after writing

- `r = 0`: expansion has the whole coefficient field as its range. C is then finite
  over F, its ordinary differential module is finite and complete, and the comparison
  is its completion equivalence. The field-derivation vanishes on F as required.
- Imperfect F: expansion fixes all coefficients, whereas Frobenius would change them.
  Inseparable finite residue extensions are included; there is no separability premise.
- Identity finite map `C = F[[T₁,...,Tᵣ]]`: relative differentials over B₁ are free on
  the parameter differentials, agreeing with the completed differential module.
- Noninjective maps, for example parameter evaluation into F or an Artinian quotient,
  are included. No injectivity hypothesis was added; relative relations and completion
  are still expressed with the actual induced B₁-algebra structure.
- `n = 0` in continuity is automatic because `m^0 = ⊤`; larger n give the adic criterion.
  The completion's zero-th quotient is zero and excludes no compatible family.
- Characteristic zero and p = 0 are intentionally excluded by the node's p > 0.
  The zero ring is already excluded by Mathlib's local-ring convention, as in the
  recorded local-algebra prerequisites.
