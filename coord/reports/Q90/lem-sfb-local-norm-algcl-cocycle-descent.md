### Returned step 1

Choose an $E$-basis of $W$, of size $d$. Thus $V_\Omega=\Omega^d$ and $V_F=F^d$ with the specified product topologies, and the action matrices have entries in $E$. Define the homogeneous cochain $f(g_0,\ldots,g_n)=g_0 z(g_0^{-1}g_1,g_1^{-1}g_2,\ldots,g_{n-1}^{-1}g_n)$. It is continuous and diagonally equivariant. In the alternating deletion differential, the term deleting $g_0$ is $g_1z(g_1^{-1}g_2,\ldots)$; factoring out $g_0$ makes it the first action term of $dz$. Deleting an interior $g_i$ multiplies the two adjacent ratios, while deleting $g_{n+1}$ gives the last term. Consequently $\delta f=g_0(dz)(g_0^{-1}g_1,\ldots,g_n^{-1}g_{n+1})=0$. This calculation applies in every degree, including the degree-zero identity $\delta(g\mapsto gv)(g_0,g_1)=g_0((g_0^{-1}g_1)v-v)$.

### Returned step 2

Apply [lem-sfb-algcl-countable-closed-finite-cover] to get an increasing sequence $F_j\supseteq E$. Each $V_{F_j}=F_j^d$ is closed in $\Omega^d$, since it is the intersection of the inverse images of the closed set $F_j$ under the finitely many coordinate projections. These subspaces cover $\Omega^d$: for each coordinate choose one $F_j$, then take the largest of the finitely many indices; if $d=0$, any index works. Put $A_j=f^{-1}(V_{F_j})$. These closed sets cover the nonempty compact Hausdorff space $G^{n+1}$. Apply [lem-sfb-baire-closed-cover-interior] with $Y=X=G^{n+1}$; thus some $A_j$ has nonempty interior; write $F=F_j$.

### Returned step 3

Choose a point $(a_0,\ldots,a_n)$ of that interior and a product of open neighbourhoods $U_0\times\cdots\times U_n$ contained in it. By the open-subgroup neighbourhood basis defining a profinite group, choose an open subgroup $H$ contained in $\bigcap_i U_i a_i^{-1}$. Then $H a_i\subseteq U_i$ for every $i$, so $f(Ha_0\times\cdots\times Ha_n)\subseteq V_F$. Here left cosets $Ha_i$, rather than $a_iH$, are used, to make each coordinate an $H$-equivariant torsor for left multiplication.

### Returned step 4

Use [lem-sfb-finite-subfield-continuous-linear-retraction] to choose a continuous $E$-linear retraction $p_F:\Omega\to F$. Coordinatewise it gives $p:\Omega^d\to F^d$. Because every action matrix has entries in $E$, $p(gv)=g p(v)$ for all $g\in G$; this follows by applying $E$-linearity to each finite matrix sum. Regard $p$ also as an endomorphism of $V_\Omega$ and put $q=1-p$. Then $r=qf$ is a continuous homogeneous equivariant cocycle, since $q$ commutes with the action and the finite sums in $\delta$. It vanishes on $Ha_0\times\cdots\times Ha_n$, since $q$ vanishes on $V_F$. Apply [lem-sfb-homogeneous-cocycle-rectangle-detection] with $K=E$, $M=V_\Omega$ and this open $H$ to obtain a continuous $H$-equivariant $t:H^n\to V_\Omega$ with $r|_{H^{n+1}}=\delta t$.

### Returned step 5

Define $u=(pz)|_{H^n}$ and $b(h_1,\ldots,h_{n-1})=t(1,h_1,h_1h_2,\ldots,h_1\cdots h_{n-1})$. For $n=1$ this is the single vector $b=t(1)$. The function $u$ is continuous as an $F^d$-valued function, and $du=p(dz)|_{H^{n+1}}=0$. Evaluating $r=\delta t$ at $(1,h_1,h_1h_2,\ldots,h_1\cdots h_n)$ and using $H$-equivariance in its first deletion term gives $(z-u)(h_1,\ldots,h_n)=(db)(h_1,\ldots,h_n)$, by the same explicit deletion-to-ratio calculation of step 1. All these are finite evaluations and sums of continuous functions. Thus $z|_{H^n}=u+db$ with exactly the required coefficient inclusion. Rank zero, the trivial group, non-normal $H$, and degree one are included.
