The hypotheses A commutative, finite length r, regularity and equality with the generated ideal are all present. Step 2 has no hidden citation: each b_j is in the ideal generated by the a_i, so it is a finite linear combination. Column j of C gives those coefficients, hence alpha composed with t equals beta, and theta composed with C modulo I is the map giving the b classes. Surjectivity follows by reducing a linear expression for each x in I. This argument uses the basis already obtained in step 1 and does not claim that generation alone gives independence.

Step 3 uses `lem-weil-pbc-finite-module-surjective-endomorphism-bijective`. Its statement allows every commutative coefficient ring and every finite module, so B=A/I and B^r satisfy it even when B=0 or r=0. A linear isomorphism gives an inverse matrix N; taking determinants of N Cbar=1 gives an explicit inverse for det(Cbar). This is the reason equality of tuple lengths matters, and it avoids any local or Noetherian assumption. The determinant identities are elementary finite matrix algebra, not new nodes.

Step 4 uses the new node's explicit quasi-isomorphism assertion with exactly alpha t=beta and det(t) a unit modulo the common ideal. It uses the definition in `def-weil-abstract-derived-category` that a quasi-isomorphism induces isomorphisms on all cohomology objects, and the exact vanishing definition in `def-weil-pbc-koszul-regular-sequence`. Thus vanishing passes to b. The comparison lemma states the specified morphism is an isomorphism in D(Mod_A), not just a family of per-degree comparisons. The item applies its quasi-isomorphism assertion directly by definition; it introduces no separate per-degree comparison theorem. Interchanging a and b proves the claimed equivalence. Every step/node quote in citations.json is literally a substring of that node's statement.

For the new node I checked why the source argument works without regularity: each common-ideal generator acts null-homotopically on either Koszul complex, so I kills its homology. If u delta=1 modulo I, multiplication by delta on either homology module is invertible with inverse multiplication by u. After localization at delta the matrix t is invertible by its adjugate; exterior powers of t and its inverse are inverse chain maps, by 0624. Exact localization identifies the induced homology map with the localization of H(T). Each homology module already has delta invertible, so its natural map to that localization is an isomorphism (inverse m/delta^n maps to u^n m). Consequently T is a quasi-isomorphism and becomes the specified isomorphism in the derived category. The new node records this substantive comparison, not a restatement that changes regular generators to regular generators.

I tested r=0: I=0, determinant of the empty matrix is 1 and both complexes are A in degree zero. I tested A=0: all terms and homology vanish, and the unique quotient element is a unit. I tested I=A, including a=b=(1,0) over Z with t=diag(1,2): delta is not a unit of A but its class in A/I is a unit, and both Koszul complexes are contractible; the comparison must be a quasi-isomorphism rather than a chain isomorphism. This confirms the new statement's precise strength. For a=b=(0,0) over a nonzero field and t=1 the new lemma still applies although neither sequence is Koszul-regular; hence it is not a clause of the item. Finally, for A=k[x,y], a=b=(x,y) and columns (1-y,x), (0,1), the comparison has delta=1-y, a unit modulo I but not in A, illustrating the proper-ideal determinant-localization case. The equality alpha t=beta is checked by (1-y)x+xy=x.
