### Returned step 1

Fix the finite coefficient ring $A=\Lambda=A_{E,n}$ and the smooth separated $f:X\to Y$ of pure relative dimension $d$ in the statement. Write $M_K=Lf^*K$, $T=\Lambda_X(d)[2d]$, and $\omega=Rf^!\Lambda_Y$. By [def-pur-smooth-purity-map], the map is $\gamma_K=\theta_K(1_{M_K}\otimes\Pi_f^{-1})\tau_K$, with the specified canonical orientation $\Pi_f:\omega\xrightarrow{\sim}T$. We first check naturality of $\theta$. By [def-pur-co-smoothness], its adjoint is the map $a_K:Rf_!(M_K\otimes^L\omega)\to K$ obtained successively from symmetry, $\operatorname{PF}_{f,\omega,K}^{-1}$, $\varepsilon_{f,\Lambda_Y}\otimes1_K$, and the left unitor. For any $u:K\to K'$ in $D^b_{ctf}(Y,\Lambda)$ put $m=Lf^*u$. Naturality of the projection isomorphism in [fix-prop-projection-formula], and of the tensor symmetry and unitors, gives
$$u\,a_K=a_{K'}\,Rf_!(m\otimes1_\omega).$$
Here the counit at $\Lambda_Y$ is a fixed morphism, so the counit part of this equality is simply $(\varepsilon\otimes1_{K'})(1\otimes u)=(1\otimes u)(\varepsilon\otimes1_K)$. Taking adjoints through the natural Hom bijection of [thm-sf-ambient-adjoint-class] gives
$$Rf^!u\,\theta_K=\theta_{K'}(m\otimes1_\omega).$$
The tensor formula of [thm-pur-cohomological-smoothness] ensures that these specified maps are isomorphisms on precisely the smooth finite-coefficient domain being used; no adjunction on arbitrary unbounded pro-etale objects is needed.

Uses: def-pur-smooth-purity-map, def-pur-co-smoothness, fix-prop-projection-formula, thm-sf-ambient-adjoint-class, thm-pur-cohomological-smoothness.

### Returned step 2

The other two squares are formal. Let $L=\Lambda_X(d)$, so that $K(d)$ means tensor with this fixed sheaf by [def-coefficient-twists-detailed]. The map $\tau_K$ in [def-pur-smooth-purity-map] is the canonical identification $(M_K\otimes^L L)[2d]\to M_K\otimes^L L[2d]$. On a K-flat tensor model it identifies each summand indexed by $(p,q)$ with the same summand after the shift. The total differential of [def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves] is $d_P\otimes1+(-1)^p1\otimes d_L$; since $L$ is in degree zero and $2d$ is even, both shifted differentials agree. This identity commutes with every map in the first factor. It therefore descends to the canonical derived shift identification, and is natural for derived morphisms (equivalently, apply the identity to the arrows in a roof, then invert its quasi-isomorphism). Thus
$$\tau_{K'}(m\otimes1_L)[2d]=(m\otimes1_T)\tau_K.$$
By the bifunctor law [lem-weil-derived-tensor-functorial], the two composites that apply $m$ in the first factor and $\Pi_f^{-1}$ in the second are equal:
$$(m\otimes1_\omega)(1\otimes\Pi_f^{-1})=(1\otimes\Pi_f^{-1})(m\otimes1_T).$$
Both are exactly $m\otimes\Pi_f^{-1}$. The orientation is fixed with $f$, and does not vary with $K$.

Uses: def-coefficient-twists-detailed, def-pur-smooth-purity-map, def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves, lem-weil-derived-tensor-functorial.

Changing a K-flat representative of $M_K$ does not change the tensor factors as derived objects: [lem-weil-derived-tensor-resolution-independent] supplies their canonical comparison, with identity and cocycle laws, without assuming a direct chain map between arbitrary K-flat resolutions. ### Returned step 6

Finally the adjoint formula $a_K$ of step 1 fixes the last factor uniquely: an adjunction Hom bijection is injective, so two candidates with that same adjoint equal $\theta_K$.
