## Source and argument

I read ILO Exposé X Proposition 1.2 in full through its second assertion, including equations (1)--(5), printed pp.136--139, and the localization of Lambda in X 1.3, pp.139--140. Its ordinary smooth part constructs a projective integral representation, lifts its generators to functions, and applies the Jacobian criterion. The original proof first moves to a closed point of a fibre and permits a finite radicial base change to make that point rational. Those operations are not among this item's hypotheses or allowed pointed etale replacements. I do not use them. In particular, formula (4), identifying the differential fibre with a relative maximal-ideal quotient, is used in ILO only after the rational-point reduction. It would be false at a general point: at the generic point of A^1 over a field, the maximal ideal of the local field is zero but its relative differentials contain dt. The item itself is true and has not been weakened or marked disputed.

Instead, work at the given point and use the split integral group algebra. Its matrix units preserve local functions and commute with the relative derivation. The images of differentiated functions span each E_11 block of the differential fibre. Select a basis from those images; the other matrix units generate the full standard representation blocks. The resulting representation is explicitly a finite direct sum of standard Lambda-modules, and its differential map is a basis. This directly constructs the functions required by the item's conclusion, without assuming the point is closed, rational or separable over the base.

For the new algebra node, the intended supporting argument is as follows (the node itself contains only its statement). The primes dividing the order of G divide its exponent, so the group order is invertible in Lambda. The cyclotomic fraction field is a splitting field for G. The ring Lambda is either that field or a DVR, since Z[zeta_N] is the cyclotomic integer ring and localization is at a prime away from N. In the DVR case choose a G-stable lattice in each irreducible fraction-field representation (the sum of the finitely many G-translates of a lattice); it is finite free. The resulting algebra map from Lambda[G] into the product of endomorphism rings of these lattices is an isomorphism over the fraction field. Both orders have perfect regular trace pairings over Lambda: on the group basis the pairing is |G| times the permutation pairing g,h -> indicator(gh=1), while on the i-th matrix factor it is d_i times the matrix trace pairing. Each d_i divides |G|, so these coefficients are units. The trace-pairing identity for the inclusion then makes its determinant a unit in the DVR: its square times the target trace discriminant equals the source unit discriminant. Hence the orders coincide. This gives exactly the matrix units used in Step 2. Splitting fields, stable lattices and degree divisibility belong to the unsplit algebra node, not to an uncited step of the item. The source's projective-lifting assertion alone is not incorrectly quoted as this full matrix decomposition.

I also read Stacks `morphisms.tex`, Tag 01V7 (`lemma-smooth-locally-standard-smooth`) and Tag 02GU (`lemma-etale-at-point`, especially condition (8)), and the statements of the existing standard-smooth differential-basis and square-Jacobian nodes. The criterion for *chosen* coordinates is written out in Steps 5--6. On a standard smooth chart with n variables and c equations, append the d=n-c equations H_j-T_j. Their combined square Jacobian is invertible at the point because the d chosen differentials are a basis modulo the c relation rows. Localize at its determinant J, adjoining w with relation wJ-1; the enlarged square determinant is J squared, up to sign. Thus the existing square-presentation criterion applies exactly to this coordinate map. This calculation is a few lines from the cited results and is not made a new node.
