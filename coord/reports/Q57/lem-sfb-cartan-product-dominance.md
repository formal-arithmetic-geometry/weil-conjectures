## Supporting argument recorded for later workers

For the generic finite-word lemma, choose a nonempty word in the H_i whose image closure has largest possible dimension among all finite words. Smooth geometric connectedness makes the factors geometrically irreducible and hence the word closure geometrically irreducible. Appending or prepending any H_i contains the original image by inserting the identity. Maximal dimension and irreducibility therefore give the same closure. Over an algebraic closure this closure is stable under left and right multiplication by every H_i-point and contains the identity, so it contains every word in these points and their inverses. Density of the chosen word then shows closure under multiplication and inversion. The reduced closure is consequently a subgroup. It is defined over k: use the kernel of the affine coordinate map for the chosen word; tensoring this kernel with a field extension computes the same kernel. The reduced geometric closure and subgroup equations therefore descend. This closed k-subgroup contains every H_i and hence equals G by the generation hypothesis. A word of positive length can be chosen because I is nonempty. This dimension-stabilization argument, implicit in Milne's Corollary 17.92, is precisely the work assigned to the statement-only finite-word node; it is not silently classified as elementary inside the item.
