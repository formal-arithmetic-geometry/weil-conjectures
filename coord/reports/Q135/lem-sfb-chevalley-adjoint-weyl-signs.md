## Problems found and changes

The original node had a statement and searches but no proof. Its source already warned that Theorem 23.71 states bracket magnitudes, not the Weyl identity. I retained that distinction. A reflection of root lines alone gives an arbitrary nonzero rational scalar, so it cannot establish the sign. The proof now computes divided-power integrality and its inverse, which forces that scalar to be a unit of Z.

I use the local subgroup L_alpha generated by h_alpha and all root vectors, rather than quietly assuming all coroots are integral in a chosen Cartan lattice. This avoids adding a coroot-integrality assertion. It need not span the full Lie algebra: its intersection with every root line is nevertheless exactly the integer span of the chosen vector. This is all the argument needs.

The imported blueprint ancestor is null (the read-only ancestor tool confirms this); the inherited supporting chain and the precise Section 23h place are retained, and the new node has this item's chain prefixed to via. I read the current latest blueprint's six-functor unit and direct-image proof, particularly proof (3), which the inherited chain serves; I do not alter that route or the unit statement. ## Review by Q135

I read Milne, Algebraic Groups, Section 23h, printed pp.508--511, including the definition of the Chevalley basis, Theorem 23.71's magnitude p+1, and the integral construction leading to Theorem 23.72. I read the complete statements of the existing split semisimple root decomposition and locally nilpotent sl2 reflection nodes, and the normalization node that fixes the item's conventions. For the new leaf I read Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/RootSystem/Chain.lean:35--46,110--164,186--205, including the section-level independence hypothesis. The new statement is written for a Mathlib RootPairing over Q to avoid concealing a root-system encoding comparison in its leaf status. All families, pairings, endpoints, finiteness and independence are introduced explicitly.

Step 1 uses exactly the root-decomposition node's evaluation root pairing, reducedness and nilpotence. The item's split Cartan and semisimplicity satisfy that node's hypotheses. The remaining rank-one brackets are assumptions of the item, not consequences improperly attributed to a definition.

Step 2 uses the full statement of the integrable sl2 reflection node: its Cartan formula, signs on e and f, and isomorphism of the beta and reflected beta weight spaces. Nilpotence was obtained in step 1; alpha(h_alpha)=2 and the two weight-bracket relations are explicitly in force. The unique coefficient is rational and nonzero because both root spaces are rational lines and the map is an isomorphism. The cited theorem does not assert the coefficient is a sign, and the proof does not pretend otherwise.

Step 3 uses exactly the new interval leaf, applied to the root pairing supplied in step 1. Reducedness makes beta not equal to plus or minus alpha equivalent here to linear independence. In the interval, the backward distance at beta+j alpha is p+j, so multiplying the assumed bracket magnitudes gives (p+1)...(p+m)/m! = binomial(p+m,m). The next bracket is zero beyond the endpoint. This is a direct calculation of successive applications, not a new unproved integrality lemma. I also apply it to -alpha for F. It is short and has its sole non-elementary input cited.

Step 4 checks the two exceptional roots and h_alpha explicitly. I corrected the potentially dangerous sign in the second divided powers: E squared on e_-alpha divided by 2 is -e_alpha; F squared on e_alpha divided by 2 is -e_-alpha. Both signs follow from the weight brackets. The finite binomial cancellation proves exp(D) exp(-D)=1, so the inverse lattice preservation is established, not assumed. Step 5 compares components in the given direct sum, obtains integer coefficients for both a map and its inverse on the two root lines, and uses the elementary integer equation c times c_inverse=1. The sign is therefore unique after fixing the basis.

I tested the vacuous empty root system, the rank-one alpha and -alpha cases, the case of disconnected components with p=q=0, and root strings of lengths zero through three (including the longest G2 string). The rational-matrix sandbox check computes all four exponentials on standard string modules of dimensions 1 through 4, checks their integrality and inverse composition, and verifies signed reversal with no numerical rounding. It also checks the displayed factorial/binomial identity for p=0..3 and m=0..4. These are edge-case checks, not substitutes for the written proof. No compiler or Lake tool was run.
