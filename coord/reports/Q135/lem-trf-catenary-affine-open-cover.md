## Original proof retained verbatim

### Former step 1

For any affine open subscheme $T\subseteq S$, put $R_T=\mathcal O_S(T)$. By [def-pc-scheme] choose an affine presentation $T\simeq\operatorname{Spec}R$. Taking global sections gives $R_T\simeq R$, since the spectrum construction [def-weil-affine-spectrum] has global sections $R$. Applying the functorial spectrum construction to this ring isomorphism gives a presentation $T\simeq\operatorname{Spec}R_T$. Its underlying homeomorphism sends irreducible closed subsets to irreducible closed subsets, with inverse doing the same, and preserves inclusion: these assertions follow directly by transporting closed sets and their decompositions through the homeomorphism.

Uses: def-pc-scheme, def-weil-affine-spectrum.

### Former step 2

For a commutative ring $R$, [lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-closed-subsets-primes] says that $\mathfrak r\mapsto V_R(\mathfrak r)$ is a bijection from primes to irreducible closed subsets, reversing inclusion. Thus, for primes $\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q$, it identifies the interval of primes between them, in reverse order, with the entire interval of irreducible closed subsets between $V_R(\mathfrak q)$ and $V_R(\mathfrak p)$. Reversing the order of a finite strict chain preserves its length, and a member can be inserted on one side exactly when its corresponding member can be inserted on the other. Consequently finite length bounds and equality of lengths of saturated chains transfer in both directions. Here a saturated chain has no member strictly insertable between adjacent terms, as in [def-exc-excellent-ring].

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-closed-subsets-primes, def-exc-excellent-ring.

### Former step 3

Fix an affine open $V\subseteq S$, put $B=\mathcal O_S(V)$, and fix primes $\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q$ of $B$. In the presentation of Step 1 set $Z=V_B(\mathfrak q)$ and $Z^{\prime}=V_B(\mathfrak p)$, viewed as irreducible closed subsets of $V$ by [lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-closed-subsets-primes]. Take the point $x\in Z$ corresponding to $\mathfrak q$, and choose $i$ with $x\in U_i$. Set $W=V\cap U_i$, an open subspace of both $V$ and $U_i$; no affineness of $W$ is needed. Every irreducible closed subset between $Z$ and $Z^{\prime}$ meets $W$, because it contains $x$. The bijection of [lem-weil-pbc-open-subspace-irreducible-closed-correspondence], applied to $W\subseteq V$, therefore restricts to an inclusion-preserving bijection from this entire interval onto the interval between $Z\cap W$ and $Z^{\prime}\cap W$ in $W$. Surjectivity on these intervals follows by applying its inclusion-preserving inverse, closure in $V$, to the two endpoint inequalities.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-closed-subsets-primes, lem-weil-pbc-open-subspace-irreducible-closed-correspondence, def-pc-scheme.

### Former step 4

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-open-subspace-irreducible-closed-correspondence] again, now to $W\subseteq U_i$. Let $C=\overline{Z\cap W}^{\,U_i}$ and $C^{\prime}=\overline{Z^{\prime}\cap W}^{\,U_i}$. These are irreducible closed subsets of $U_i$ with $C\subseteq C^{\prime}$, and their intersections with $W$ are exactly $Z\cap W$ and $Z^{\prime}\cap W$. Every irreducible closed subset between $C$ and $C^{\prime}$ meets $W$, since it contains $C$ and $C\cap W$ contains $x$. Thus intersection and closure give inverse inclusion-preserving bijections between the interval $[C,C^{\prime}]$ in $U_i$ and $[Z\cap W,Z^{\prime}\cap W]$ in $W$. Composing with Step 3 identifies the whole interval $[Z,Z^{\prime}]$ in $V$ with $[C,C^{\prime}]$ in $U_i$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-open-subspace-irreducible-closed-correspondence.

### Former step 5

By hypothesis $A_i=\mathcal O_S(U_i)$ is catenary in the chain sense of [def-exc-excellent-ring]. Apply Steps 1 and 2 to $U_i$ and $A_i$: the interval $[C,C^{\prime}]$ has a finite bound on strict-chain lengths, and all its saturated chains have the same length. The inclusion-preserving bijections of Steps 3 and 4 send a strict chain to a strict chain of the same length. They also preserve saturation: an intermediate member could be inserted into the image chain if and only if its inverse image could be inserted into the original chain. Hence these two chain conditions hold in $[Z,Z^{\prime}]$, and then Step 2 transfers them to the prime interval $[\mathfrak p,\mathfrak q]$ of $B$. Since the prime pair was arbitrary, $B$ is catenary. A finite length bound also ensures a saturated chain exists: among the nonempty finite set of attainable chain lengths choose the largest, whose chain admits no insertion. If $V$ is empty, $B$ is the zero ring by its affine presentation and has no primes, so the same conclusion is vacuous. This also handles an empty indexing set, which can cover only the empty scheme.

Uses: def-exc-excellent-ring, lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-closed-subsets-primes, lem-weil-pbc-open-subspace-irreducible-closed-correspondence, def-weil-affine-spectrum.
