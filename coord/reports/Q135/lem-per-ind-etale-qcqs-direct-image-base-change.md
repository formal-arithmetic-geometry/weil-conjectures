I checked the item against the unrestricted statements in 03Q9 and 0EZU, rather than against the torsion-restricted blueprint formula. The affine locally ind-etale hypothesis on u gives the chart hypothesis of the existing strict-local invariance node for each geometric point. The proof never invokes smooth base change or a finite-coefficient theorem and never adds invertibility, constructibility or Noetherian assumptions. The source's filtered-colimit step, together with its etale case, explains why an ind-etale map has the same strict localizations, rather than merely the same geometric fibres. All new substantive proof steps have fewer than 2000 characters.

Step 1 uses the full statement of lem-weil-pbc-exchange-forget-coefficients. I checked its three clauses and final equivalence: it identifies the actual module-linear adjunction mate, not just the two objects, and reflects invertibility. This handles every commutative coefficient ring A; no assumption that a module-injective sheaf is abelian-injective is made.

Step 2 uses the statement of lem-trf-ind-etale-strict-local-invariance, not its proof: it explicitly gives the canonical functorial isomorphism over S for locally ind-etale maps and geometric points taken over the same algebraically closed field. I checked that these are precisely the hypotheses supplied by u. The cited def-pc-fibre-product states both existence/universality and functoriality. The inverse to w is explicitly induced by h inverse; uniqueness of a map with its two projections proves both identity composites. This small geometric assembly does not assume that images contained in a subscheme imply factorization.

Step 3 takes composition of cohomology pullbacks from clause (4) of lem-weil-pbc-site-cohomology-pullback; its statement also supplies canonical coefficient comparison maps and naturality. Applied to w and its inverse it makes the cohomology restriction invertible. The statement of lem-weil-pbc-exchange-strict-local-cohomology-stalk-criterion says exactly that the resulting strict-henselization cohomology pullback tests the geometric stalk of the degree-q derived exchange. It is for a sheaf, which is exactly how it is applied here, in every q >= 0.
