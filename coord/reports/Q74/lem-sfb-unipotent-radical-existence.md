## Review by Q74

I read the item against Milne 6.46 and 14.8. The base is algebraically closed, the ambient group is affine of finite type, smooth and connected, and the subgroup must be closed, normal, smooth, connected and unipotent. Every one of these properties is preserved in the split. Uniqueness is as a closed subgroup scheme, not merely equality of F-points. The matrix criterion is restricted to smooth groups, in accordance with 14.12 and its immediately following counterexample mu_p. There are no new nodes needing separate source verification.

Step 1 uses the six definition statements quoted in citations.json. I checked smoothness against the lifting definition: for Spec F over itself every required relative map is the unique structure map, and the algebra F is finitely presented over F. A one-point spectrum is nonempty and connected; it is finite type with zero generators. The identity section's coordinate map is surjective because it fixes scalars, so the closed-immersion definition really applies. Its image is normal over every algebra, and the GL_1 representation is the closed identity matrix with nilpotence exponent 1. This supplies a candidate, including in characteristic p. These are direct definition calculations, not an invoked existence theorem.

Step 2 cites the statement of `lem-sfb-smooth-unipotent-solvable`, which expressly concludes solvability for every smooth unipotent affine finite-type group; it is not just a definition of solvability. I checked that the unipotence and smoothness hypotheses match the item. The statement of `lem-sfb-solvable-normal-subgroup-products` explicitly includes smooth connected groups' persistence under field extension and the nonnegative-integer dimension bound, so it provides geometric connectedness of H and the finite set of possible candidate dimensions. The maximum of a nonempty finite set is an elementary integer choice. I also wrote out why an algebraically closed field is perfect, rather than silently assuming the perfectness hypothesis of step 3.

Step 3 uses the two existing statements exactly. Solvability of U and V has already been proved in step 2; smoothness, connectedness, normality and closedness are part of being a candidate. The product node therefore applies with ambient group H smooth and geometrically connected, and gives P affine of finite type, smooth, connected, closed, normal, solvable, and containing both factors. The perfect-solvable theorem applies to P, not to the potentially nonsolvable H. Its statement supplies a smooth connected normal unipotent P_u and expressly says it contains every smooth unipotent subgroup, so it contains U and V as subgroup schemes. This is not an appeal to an argument hidden inside that node's proof. The corresponding assertions are Milne 16.33(a)--(c), which I read.

Step 4 cites the product node's actual local-factorization assertion and the perfect-solvable node's represented fppf quotient. For any B-point x of P it writes the factorization after a faithfully flat finitely presented B-algebra C, obtains q(x)_C=1, and descends equality using the sheaf axiom. The kernel condition is expressly present in `def-sfb-solvable-group-structure`; I opened it and quoted that exact sentence. It therefore proves P(B)=P_u(B) for every B, naturally, and hence equality of the represented subgroup functors. No assertion about density of rational points, reduced images, or smoothness of an intersection is used. This is a short elementary calculation from the three cited statements, so it stays as a proof step rather than becoming a node. It establishes unipotence of P without using existence of the radical of H.

Step 5 uses the strict dimension clause of the product node for U contained in P, both smooth and connected, and the maximum chosen in step 2. This yields equality of closed subgroup schemes before deducing V contained in U. The closed-immersion definition identifies these subgroups with ideals in the fixed coordinate ring of H, so mutual inclusions prove uniqueness with the required scheme structure. The final trivial-group clause follows from step 1 and containment, with no exclusion of dimension zero.
