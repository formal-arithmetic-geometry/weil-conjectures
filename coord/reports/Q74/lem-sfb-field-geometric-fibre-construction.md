## Review by Q74

I checked the complete statement against the field examples of Bhatt--Scholze, and read the statements of every cited DAG node, not merely their proofs or titles. citations.json gives a verbatim statement for every use. No new node needs a source or a separate small-case test. I checked delivery-errors B--E for self-contained objects, genuine proof steps, exact citation scope, hypotheses, cycles, elementary designations, and actual search records. The proof has ten steps and no step longer than 2000 characters. None hides a comparison lemma or a separately hypothesized construction.

Step 1 uses sk-lem-pe-weakly-etale-stability specifically for Zariski locality on the source; it states that locality. lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs gives both quasi-compactness and quasi-separatedness of each affine open, without assumptions on the ambient scheme. def-weil-affine-spectrum supplies the ring/spectrum convention. lem-weil-pbc-weak-etale-field states finite etaleness of every finitely generated subalgebra over a field, and lem-weil-pbc-etale-algebra-over-field-product states the product decomposition, including the zero algebra. The hypotheses here really have a field as base, with no finite-type assumption on the entire algebra.

Step 2 proves the point-closed condition rather than reading it into the field lemma's residue-field clause. An algebraic domain over a field is a field by the nonzero constant term in a minimal polynomial. A specialization is tested in an affine neighbourhood of its target, which also contains its source. Thus the exact all-points-closed hypothesis of lem-sfb-qcqs-zero-dimensional-affine is satisfied. Its remaining hypotheses are precisely the item's quasi-compactness and quasi-separatedness. I did not assert affineness for a non-quasi-separated zero-dimensional scheme.

Step 3 applies the same weak-field node to the coordinate algebra and to a general ind-etale algebra after using the explicit ind-etale-implies-weakly-etale clause of lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability. The affine qcqs node applies to every spectrum, even the empty one. Finite generating sets have a combined finite generating set, so directedness and union are elementary and written out. This is not an appeal to injectivity of arbitrary maps in an ind-etale presentation: the chosen index consists of actual subalgebras. The classification node gives the stated meaning of finite etale, and all indices are sets in the item's size convention.

Step 4 uses the Galois clause of lem-weil-pbc-separable-closure-exists-galois with the intermediate field equal to k, then the exact topological-group limit statement of lem-exc-galois-krull-finite-limit. The nonempty directed finite-Galois-field index is part of that statement. The compatible-family topology is the one in lem-dl-a1-finite-group-limit-profinite. Its hypotheses are finite discrete groups and a directed set, with no transition-surjectivity requirement. This establishes profiniteness for the Krull topology actually specified in the item.

Step 5 uses only the finite-embedding-set clause of lem-exc-etale-field-galois-classification, translated by the explicit contravariant homomorphism correspondence of def-weil-affine-spectrum. The finite product is from the etale-product node. Existence of embeddings is separately supported by lem-weil-pbc-separable-extension-embeds-separably-closed; its codomain may be separably closed, so perfection or algebraic closedness is unnecessary. The idempotent calculation explains selection of a single factor. The zero-product case is separated before asserting nonemptiness.

Step 6 is an actual extension argument for each finite-stage point, not an assertion that any map of finite fibres is surjective. A nonzero selected idempotent stays nonzero under a subalgebra inclusion, so some target field factor survives. This creates an embedding of the selected source factor L into a field factor M. Both are finite separable over k; separability over L follows by minimal-polynomial divisibility. With Omega made an L-algebra by the prescribed embedding t, the existing embedding node supplies precisely an L-linear embedding M into Omega. The coordinate projection then extends t on both the selected factor and its complementary idempotent. The product classification and the embedding node state exactly these inputs; no extension of automorphisms of a nonnormal finite field is assumed.

Step 7 constructs a homomorphism from compatible restrictions by the union formula, checks independence and both ring operations, and proves the homeomorphism by the common initial topology. Only then is lem-galois-inverse-limit-surjective applied. Its finite, nonempty, surjective-transition and directed-index hypotheses were all checked in steps 3, 5 and 6. Thus it supplies the required projections from the whole fibre, not only maps between stages.

Step 8 invokes Tychonoff through lem-weil-pbc-tychonoff-product-quasi-compact. Everything after that is the explicit few-line closed-subset and clopen-coordinate argument: append the complement to an open cover, separate distinct families by a differing coordinate, and separate a two-point connected subset by that clopen cylinder. This is marked elementary relative to the cited product compactness statement. It works for an empty factor and the empty compatible-family space.

Step 9 writes the group-action identities and checks joint continuity on cylinders. At (g0,f0), fixing f|Bi and restricting g to g0 times the stabilizer of f0(Bi) makes the required coordinate literally constant. This is an open set for the Krull topology in the statement; separate continuity is not substituted for joint continuity. The image f0(Bi) is a finite field even when Bi is a product. No new general action-continuity theorem is being used.

Step 10 uses the actual finite-dimensional image of each finite subalgebra under alpha as an index on the other side. This proves precomposition continuity without an unstated descent theorem. Associativity gives equivariance and the reversed composition law exactly. For cofinal presentations the restriction inverse is explicitly defined from a larger stage, and each of its coordinates is a finite-stage restriction. The comparison fixes every evaluation, so it is canonical and compatible with G. I checked the zero algebra, B=k, a finite non-Galois separable field extension, a finite product with several field factors, a separably closed imperfect base in positive characteristic, and an arbitrary directed family rather than merely a countable tower. In particular there is no map 0 to a nonzero algebra whose nonexistent fibre point would be used.
