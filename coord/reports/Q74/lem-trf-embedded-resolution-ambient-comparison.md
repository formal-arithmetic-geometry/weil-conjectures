For a smooth identity map, extending the ideal does nothing and every nonempty label is retained. The identity is smooth, and composites are smooth by [lem-trf-smooth-composition]. For composable smooth maps $R\xrightarrow{w}Q\xrightarrow{v}P$, the center ideal at any retained label is obtained by two successive extensions of scalars; this is the same ideal as extension along $vw$, since the generators of either ideal are the images of the same original local sections. A center empty on $Q$ remains empty on $R$. A center nonempty on $Q$ but empty on $R$ is removed at the second restriction, just as in direct restriction. The comparisons of Step 3 all arise from the same blowup of these identical ideals, so the stage identifications coincide. This proves the identity and composition assertions, including their labels.

Uses: def-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change-map, lemma-nagata-strict-transform-ambient-closed, lem-trf-smooth-composition.

### Old Step 7

Finally, an isomorphism of initial reduced schemes identifies their invariant histories by clause (3) of [lem-trf-hilbert-samuel-resolution-invariant-calculus]. Repeating the induction of Step 5 therefore compares the intrinsic towers along this isomorphism. For three embeddings, all three pairwise comparisons at the next stage are the maps induced by the identity on the same intrinsic scheme and its same center ideal; both composites are that identity-induced map on its blowup. Starting at $X$, induction proves transitivity at all stages. The same observation shows that these intrinsic comparisons agree with restriction or smooth ambient pullback, since clause (3) commutes with those operations and the extended ideals are equal. The comparisons are canonical at the level of the labeled blowups of ideals, even if an individual blowup morphism is an identity.

Uses: lem-trf-hilbert-samuel-resolution-invariant-calculus, lemma-nagata-strict-transform-ambient-closed.
