I checked the eight steps against the actual dependency statements, not the dependencies' proofs. The argument deliberately distinguishes an underived kernel identity, its derived comparison, and its effect on the chosen orientation. Concentration is never inferred from mere etaleness or from a declaration with a sorry.

1. The inverse-image restriction node explicitly says that an abelian sheaf restricted to an etale test object has exactly the original sections, naturally. The modules-exactness node supplies the constant-ring and module-action comparisons, so the same identification is A-linear. The support definition gives the actual kernel on each test object. Since `W ×_T Z = W ×_P C`, the two maps to the complement are literally identical. I wrote out this calculation and naturality, so this step is elementary relative to its cited statements; no new kernel-base-change node is appropriate.

2. The injective-restriction node is explicitly for sheaves of modules over a constant ring and arbitrary etale morphisms; no finite type, separation or Noetherian condition is required. Exact module inverse image takes the resolution to a resolution, and preservation of injectives makes it a computing resolution. Thus the termwise kernel equality really represents the two claimed derived objects. The pullback-stalk node gives the stated identification at `e t`, and exactness of the abelian stalk gives the same assertion for the induced A-module action on kernels and quotients. I checked that the concentration hypothesis is supplied at every point of C, hence at this particular point, without assuming it on all of P or on the base B.

3. The coordinate-support definition specifies the germ map as a factorization through the actual kernel, and explicitly says the derived comparison commutes with etale restriction. The supported-cup lemma states naturality of Kummer classes, localization boundaries and ordered cup products for arbitrary morphisms of schemes; it therefore applies to p even when B is not finite type and to e. The finite-twist lemma gives tensor/evaluation pullback compatibility for every integer twist. The step records the formula on every coefficient v; it is not an unspecified claim that an orientation is transported. No purity is needed to establish that formula.

4. The input hypotheses identify both degree-q cohomology modules using precisely these classes. Substituting the formula from Step 3 gives `H^q(gamma) u_K = u_D`; multiplying by the inverse identifies its matrix with the identity. This is elementary and is valid without choosing a generator or trivializing the twist line.

5. I read the full statement of `lem-pur-good-truncation-exact-normalization`. It gives naturality for *derived morphisms*, the explicit good-truncation roof and the bijection on Hom sets, not merely an existential concentration isomorphism. Apply it after shifting by q = 2b, for which both objects have cohomology only in degree zero. Its abelian-category hypotheses apply to ordinary A-modules. Shift back with the same convention on both sides. The displayed identity yields both the inverse and its exact normalization. This replaces the tempting but insufficient use of the constructible concentrated-complex lemma.

6. The coordinate-support statement explicitly supplies composition of supported germ restrictions. Restricting an affine base open induces an open immersion of P and T, so the same literal test-object kernel calculation applies. Pullback naturality of the coordinate class and naturality/faithfulness of good truncation give commuting derived squares and their inverses. No claim that general direct image commutes with arbitrary base change occurs here.

7. The all-integer finite-twist lemma explicitly includes evaluation and negative dual powers.
