First, rho is simplicial. For any monotone alpha:[m] to [n], the zero block of epsilon alpha is the inverse image of the zero block of epsilon, and the same holds for the one block. Restricting sigma along alpha and then to a block is exactly the same restriction as first forming the corresponding join simplex and then applying alpha. Composition and identities of restrictions give the simplicial identities, including when a block becomes empty. The coordinatewise formula therefore preserves the pullback equation and defines its simplicial operators. This uses the two cited definitions and a literal restriction calculation; no separate theorem node is hidden here (C1, D3).

Second, the endpoint formula satisfies the pullback equation because rho_C(0,sigma) is the pure first-copy sigma and rho_C(1,sigma) the pure second-copy sigma. The induced map satisfies it because f'a=hf and g'b=hg on each block, while h commutes with restriction: (h star h)rho_C(epsilon,sigma)=rho_{C'}(epsilon,h sigma). The same coordinate calculation shows these maps commute with every operator. Their composites and identities are coordinatewise composites and identities; no universal choice or unknown data is used.

Third, with both structural maps identities, the pullback equation forces z=rho_C(epsilon,sigma). Consequently the displayed projection and inverse are mutual inverses in every degree and commute with the operators already checked. For the fiber cone, u r is constantly c, so (u star id_C)(r star c)(w) is rho_C(epsilon,c_n). Its image really belongs to A star_C C, including the pure apex and pure A_c cases. Restricting w restricts its two blocks and the degenerate simplex c_n, proving that j_c is a simplicial map. Its boundary is i_A r and its apex is i_C c. These few-line computations explain why these clauses do not require new elementary nodes (D1 and D2).

For the hypothesis and small-case audit (E), when C=Delta^0, rho is the usual identification Delta^1 with Delta^0 star Delta^0 and the relative join recovers A star B. If C is empty then maps into it force both factors empty and the relative join is empty. For a discrete nonempty C, the mixed simplices occur only between simplices lying over the same vertex, as the pullback equation requires. For a general C, mixed simplices can lie over a nonconstant sigma: no accidental fiberwise-disjoint-union definition was substituted. In dimension zero every parameter is an endpoint; in dimension one a mixed edge uses f(a)=d_1 sigma and g(b)=d_0 sigma, confirming the orientation. A nondegenerate sigma with repeated vertices causes no ambiguity because restriction is by ordered indices, not just by the list of vertex values.
