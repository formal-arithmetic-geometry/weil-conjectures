Every prime of $T$ contracts at each stage to a prime avoiding the inverted element, by [lem-weil-pbc-localization-prime-residue-field-iso], and the original map $\alpha$ is quasi-finite at each such prime. Therefore $D_h\to T$ is finite type and quasi-finite at every prime. Composition with $C\to D_h$ has the same properties by [lem-trf-quasi-finite-composition], the statement of Stacks 00PO. In particular $T$ inverts the image of $f$; it is the iterated localization customarily denoted $A_{gfh}$. More precisely write $\alpha_f(h)=a/(gf)^N$ in $A_{gf}$ with $a\in A$. Since $h\notin\mathfrak q_D$ and $\alpha_f^{-1}(\mathfrak q_B B_f)=\mathfrak q_D$, one has $a\notin\mathfrak q_A$. Therefore the contraction of $\mathfrak q_T$ to $C$ is precisely $P=\psi^{-1}(\mathfrak q_D)$. Compute this ideal, rather than asserting it is unchanged. For every $F\in R_f[x]$, the fibre-prime correspondence gives
$$F\in P\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \bar\psi(\bar F)\in Q\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \bar F\in(x_{r+1},\ldots,x_n)\subset k[x].$$
The last condition says that all coefficients of $F(x_1,\ldots,x_r,0,\ldots,0)$ map to zero in $k$. The kernel of $R_f\to k$ is $\mathfrak pR_f$, so it is equivalent to $F\in(\mathfrak pR_f,x_{r+1},\ldots,x_n)$. Thus
$$P=(\mathfrak pR_f,x_{r+1},\ldots,x_n).$$
This proves the required prime contraction using the normalization statement, the lifted-coordinate square, and [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-ring-primes-residue-fields].

### Returned step 7

The map $C\to T$ induces an $S_0$-morphism $q:Z_0\to\mathbf A^n_{S_0}$. It is locally quasi-finite by [pqf-def-quasifinite], since the same pair of affine charts works at every prime. The finite-type ring map gives a locally finite-type affine morphism. Its quasi-compactness can be checked on basic opens of $\operatorname{Spec}C$: the inverse image of $D(c)$ is $\operatorname{Spec}T_c$, which is quasi-compact by [lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs]; an affine open in the target is quasi-compact and therefore is a finite union of such basic opens. The affine fibre formula [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra] and the pointwise ring condition show that every singleton in each fibre is open, hence the finite fibres are discrete.
