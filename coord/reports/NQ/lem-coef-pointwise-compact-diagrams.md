## Auxiliary argument retained for its future worker

For every simplicial degree $n$, the defining formula gives $\operatorname{Fun}(J,\mathcal C)_n=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{sSet}}(J\times\Delta^n,\mathcal C)$. Since $J$ is the degreewise disjoint union of the $J_a$, the source is the degreewise disjoint union of the $J_a\times\Delta^n$, and a map out is exactly a family of maps from these summands. This identification commutes with faces and degeneracies, so restriction is an isomorphism of simplicial sets $\operatorname{Fun}(J,\mathcal C)\cong\prod_a\operatorname{Fun}(J_a,\mathcal C)$.

Apply the same degreewise calculation to the right-pinched mapping-space definition: an $n$-simplex of the mapping space is a map from the pinched simplex into $\operatorname{Fun}(J,\mathcal C)$ with prescribed endpoint faces. Under the product isomorphism from Step 1, such a map is precisely a family of corresponding maps for the $J_a$, and the endpoint conditions are imposed componentwise. Thus the displayed restriction map is an isomorphism in every simplicial degree, hence a natural equivalence of spaces. Empty products give the terminal space, and the construction is visibly compatible with simplicial operators, postcomposition, and restriction of summands.

Small cases: for an empty family both sides are terminal simplicial sets; for a singleton restriction is the identity; for two copies of a point this is the coordinatewise right mapping space of a product. No compactness, accessibility, or colimits in the target are required. The coproduct comparison is natural because it restricts a single simplicial map to each summand; endpoint conditions and postcomposition are componentwise equalities. In the item’s skeletal step the boundary inclusion is a monomorphism, every boundary has lower dimension, and the number of new cells is less than kappa. In its tower step the nerve is countable and therefore kappa-small only in the uncountable case, with the finite-dimensional argument used at kappa equal to aleph_0.
