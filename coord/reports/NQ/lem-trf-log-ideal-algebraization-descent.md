I checked B--E against the item and the open sources, with each cited node's statement open. Every step is below 2000 characters. The chart-generator calculation in step 1 is the only step marked elementary: local monoid-ideal membership and alpha(em)=alpha(e)alpha(m) give both ideal inclusions explicitly.

Step 1 uses def-trf-fs-log-blowup only for the chart pushout description and the definition of coherent log ideal as generated etale-locally by a nonempty finite chart set. I have not used that first definition's separate locally Noetherian or saturated-log-blowup hypotheses as extra hypotheses on X. Sheaf generation means local membership: a local section of the ideal is locally a generator times a monoid section. I explicitly avoid identifying a sheaf image with the image on global sections.

Step 2 uses pqf-def-coherent for finite free quasi-coherence, lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-module-cokernel-image for quasi-coherence of the image and its affine section formula, and lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction for the natural counit reconstructing that image including its inclusion. The finite free module map is an O_U-linear map between quasi-coherent Zariski modules, and U is affine, exactly as these statements require. The exactness assertion of lem-sfb-scheme-etale-module-exact-finiteness-comparison identifies the associated image with the actual small-etale sheaf image; it does not merely assert an abstract module equivalence. Finite direct sums are preserved by an additive exact functor. Thus the displayed ideal is the tilde of the ring ideal generated by alpha(E), not an unproved identification of monoid and ring ideals.

Step 3 uses the site-local presentation definition def-trf-space-quasi-coherent-module and the essential-surjectivity/restriction-inverse assertions of lem-trf-scheme-zariski-etale-quasi-coherent-equivalence. The chartwise associated modules from step 2 are in its quasi-coherent essential image. The locality is built into the definition; on a test object one pulls back the covering charts and their presentations. Restriction to Zariski opens gives the actual subsheaf of O_X and proves equality/uniqueness of ideal subobjects. The finite-type equivalence in lem-sfb-scheme-etale-module-exact-finiteness-comparison applies because J has the finite local generating maps constructed in step 1. This theorem explicitly has no Noetherian hypothesis. I deliberately conclude finite type only, not coherence over an arbitrary ring.

Step 4 uses precisely the etale-restriction comparison, natural in module maps and composition, stated in lem-trf-etale-extension-free-presentations-hom, and full faithfulness in lem-trf-scheme-zariski-etale-quasi-coherent-equivalence. It does not cite the cohomological comparison as if it were module descent. Double and triple overlaps may be nonaffine; the comparison nodes work on arbitrary schemes, so no unjustified affine-intersection assumption occurs. Once the two ideals have the same etale injection, full faithfulness identifies them with their inclusions. Cancellation against that injection proves the diagonal and triple-overlap identities. The already constructed K is the effectivity witness, so there is no hidden additional existence theorem for descent data.

Step 5 uses the same chart pushout of def-trf-fs-log-blowup, together with strictness as specified in the item, to describe local representatives in the pulled-back associated log structure. The ideal on Y is generated by the images of the old generators; adjoining units multiplies their images by units and does not change the generated ring ideal. The etale restriction comparison of lem-trf-etale-extension-free-presentations-hom is applicable because f is etale. The inverse equivalence on Y identifies f* K with the newly constructed image ideal and inclusion. Uniqueness of maps respecting the injection also proves compatibility of the descent maps. No assertion that arbitrary nonflat pullback preserves a submodule inclusion is used.
