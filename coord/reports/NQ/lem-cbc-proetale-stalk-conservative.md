## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported clause (2) claimed that an isomorphism in the derived category is equivalent to a quasi-isomorphism of ordinary section complexes on every affine ind-etale basis object. Ordinary evaluation is left exact and need not be exact; the basis theorem concerns sheaves, not exactness of evaluation.

Explicit counterexample: take T = Spec F_q and Lambda = F_2. The continuous representation of its procyclic Galois group on M = F_2 e_1 + F_2 e_2, with Frobenius e_1 -> e_1 and e_2 -> e_2 + e_1, gives a short exact sequence 0 -> F_2 e_1 -> M -> F_2 -> 0 of etale module sheaves. Pull it to the pro-etale site. Exact comparison inverse image leaves this a bounded acyclic three-term complex. But its sections on the identity object T (an affine ind-etale basis object) are Galois invariants: M^G = F_2 e_1, whose map to the quotient's invariants F_2 is zero. The section complex is not acyclic. Stacks 0GLZ, `lemma-describe-pullback-from-etale`, identifies these pro-etale sections on the identity object with etale sections. Thus the morphism from zero to the acyclic three-term complex is a derived isomorphism failing the imported section-complex test.

The faithful replacement is: test the maps of sections of each COHOMOLOGY SHEAF on the basis. This follows from the very same basis route, preserves detection for unbounded complexes, and assumes no evaluation acyclicity. Isomorphism of complexes has also been explicitly interpreted in the derived category, as the imported cone argument intended. Literally interpreted as a degreewise isomorphism, the old wording was false even for a contractible nonzero complex over a point.

The warning's phrase “None of this extends to arbitrary pro-etale sheaves” was also inaccurate: cover and basis detection do extend. The corrected warning concerns geometric-point detection alone and explicitly records zero geometric stalks of the nonzero quotient sheaf.
