lem-pur-adic-basis-limit (Lemma 6.8.2(1), pp. 58-59: inverse limits of constant quotient sheaves. The basis and cofinal-filtration refinement below is an agent argument, not a numbered assertion of the source.):

1. The basis and its coordinate inverse identify $B/t^nB$ with $(A/t^nA)^d$: membership in $t^nB$ is exactly divisibility of every coordinate by $t^n$. Their inverse and additive equations survive constant sheafification, which preserves finite sums. All these maps commute with the quotient transitions. Uses: def-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf, lem-weil-pbc-sheafification-exact.

2. The equality $(t^n)B=(v^{en})$ identifies the latter quotients. A compatible family indexed by $en$ extends to index $m$ by reduction from any $en\geq m$; a larger common index proves independence. Thus limits over all $m$ and over $en$ are inverse by these explicit maps. Taking the inverse limits of both basis maps gives inverse maps $R^d\leftrightarrows S$, since finite products and limits are sectionwise. The maps $A/t^mA\to B/v^mB$ exist because $e\geq1$, commute with transitions and define the asserted ring map. The basis maps are linear for it on every projection, hence on the limit; projection uniqueness proves uniqueness of the ring map. Uses: lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-colimits-forgetful.


lem-pur-finite-free-quotient-completion (Lemma 3.4.12, pp. 22-23; finite direct-sum calculation of the quotient-tower units, written here as a general ringed-site argument.):

1. Tensor the basis map and its inverse with each term of $P$ to identify $Q$ with $P^d$. They commute with multiplication by $a^n$, so their cokernels identify $Q\otimes_S S/a^nS$ with $(P\otimes_R R/a^nR)^d$, including all quotient maps and transitions. In coordinates the map is $p\otimes\sum_i r_i b_i\mapsto(r_i p)_i$ and its inverse is $(p_i)_i\mapsto\sum_i p_i\otimes b_i$. Countable products commute with a fixed finite sum by rearranging coordinates; the two cone differentials and their zero-component units are the same finite block sums. Limits of module sheaves are sectionwise. Uses: lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-colimits-forgetful.

2. K-flat change of rings makes $Q$ K-flat with flat terms, so the regular unit criterion applies over both rings. If $P$ is locally complete, that criterion makes $\eta_{R,a,P}$ invertible. Its finite sum is invertible. Restriction of scalars preserves kernels and cokernels and is faithful: these claims follow from the same underlying abelian sheaf and the faithful exact forgetful functors. It thus preserves cohomology and detects acyclicity, hence detects whether the cone of a map is zero. Consequently $\eta_{S,a,Q}$ is invertible, and the criterion makes $Q$ locally complete over $S$. Uses: lem-pur-kflat-change-of-rings, lem-pur-regular-quotient-completion-unit, def-pc-sheaf-restrict-scalars, lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-colimits-forgetful.


lem-pur-quotient-scalar-comparison-descent (Definition 3.4.10 and Proposition 3.5.1, pp. 22,24: completion via derived reduction towers. Explicit scalar augmentation and resolving-class descent are expanded here; not a verbatim assertion of the source.):

1. Each tensor projection is balanced and $S$-linear. Compatibility of $S\to B_n$ with transitions says their product is killed strictly by $1-s$, so $(0,(p\otimes\bar s)_n)$ is a chain map to the shifted cone. The two component equations determine it degreewise. For a chain map $f:P\to P'$ both routes have components $f(p)\otimes\bar s$ and zero, proving naturality. Tensor, products and the cone formula also carry a chain homotopy to a chain homotopy. Uses: def-weil-abstract-derived-category, def-pc-sheaf-restrict-scalars.

2. The flat-term K-flat resolving class exists. Derived change of rings sends quasi-isomorphisms between its members to quasi-isomorphisms after tensoring with $S$ or any $B_n$. Exact restriction of scalars preserves them. Exact countable products and the cone triangle therefore show the same for $G$. The resolving-class left-derived theorem gives $LF,LG$, with invertible counits on this class. Apply the universal property of $LG$ to the composite $LF\circ Q\to F\xrightarrow{\beta}G$. It gives a unique $\chi:LF\to LG$, whose representative formula follows by the counit isomorphisms. This proves full derived naturality and representative independence, without choosing a triangle filler. When $S=R$, multiplication sends $p\otimes r$ to $rp$ and the displayed components become precisely the quotient components, proving the identity normalization. Uses: lem-pc-kflat-resolution, lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings, lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-colimits-forgetful, lem-weil-derived-limit-existence, lem-pur-left-derived-from-kflat-class.


lem-pur-regular-quotient-completion-unit (Tag 0A0E, lemma-derived-completion-koszul, one regular generator; canonical-unit refinement using the strict Roos comparison and Bhatt--Scholze Lemma 3.4.12.):

1. Apply the regular Koszul reconstruction theorem to $a,P$: its strict Koszul-to-quotient tower map is a levelwise quasi-isomorphism, compatible with the augmentations. The normalized strict Roos comparison represents the total derived quotient limit by $C_a(P)$ and carries its strict augmentation to exactly $(0,(q_n)_n)$. The Koszul reflector therefore identifies $C_a(P)$ and this specified unit with the local derived-completion reflector and its unit. In particular its value is locally complete and its unit is invertible exactly on locally complete objects. Uses: lem-pur-derived-complete-koszul-reconstruction, lem-weil-strict-roos-comparison, lem-weil-pbc-ringed-koszul-completion-reflector.

2. If the specified unit is invertible, transport the cone triangle along it to obtain the displayed Roos triangle; products in the derived category are termwise products by exactness. Conversely, such a triangle makes $P$ a derived limit of the same quotient tower as $C_a(P)$. Uniqueness up to isomorphism of derived-limit objects gives $P\cong C_a(P)$. Local completeness, being vanishing of specified derived Hom objects, is invariant under this isomorphism. The first step now inverts the specified unit. We never infer equality of two triangle fillers. Uses: lem-weil-derived-limit-existence, def-weil-derived-limit.


lem-pur-unit-power-completeness (Tag 099B, lemma-derived-completion: augmented localization model for a principal ideal; comparison for a unit times a positive power is the explicit argument below.):

1. On every object, inverting $b$ inverts $a$ with inverse $u^{-1}/b^e$, and inverting $a$ inverts $b$ with inverse $u b^{e-1}/a$. These define inverse localization maps fixing $\mathcal O$ and commute with restrictions, hence give an isomorphism after sheafification. Together with identity in degree zero they identify the two augmented localization complexes, including their maps to $\mathcal O[0]$. Uses: def-weil-pbc-sheafification.

2. Apply the extended-Cech completion theorem to each of the two principal ideals. It says that derived Hom from each augmented complex is its local-completion reflection with the indicated unit. Precomposition with the augmented isomorphism of step 1 identifies these functors and units. An object in either complete subcategory has invertible reflection unit there; the same is then true for the other unit. Since a reflector has complete values, invertibility of its unit implies the object itself is complete. Applying this in both directions proves the equivalence. Uses: lem-weil-pbc-extended-cech-completion-model, def-pur-derived-internal-hom.
