## Review by NQ

I read the item against Milne 7.29--7.30 and 17.3, with the actual source open. Milne's Grassmannian parametrizes locally direct-summand families, not arbitrary submodules over coefficient rings; the proof retains that restriction. Milne's fixed-point theorem requires a smooth connected solvable acting group and a nonempty complete scheme. Here the acting group is the split torus, and the submodule locus is closed in the projective Grassmannian. Algebraic closure makes the resulting fixed point k-rational. I kept the scheme-theoretic formulation at every coefficient algebra, including rings with nilpotents. There are no new-node statements needing a separate source comparison.

Step 1 is elementary: M itself supplies a positive dimension, a finite nonempty subset of {1,...,n} has a minimum, and a proper nonzero vector subspace has strictly smaller dimension. It neither asserts nor needs that M is a semisimple B-module.

Step 2 cites lem-sfb-free-quotient-grassmann-projective. I opened its statement: it represents locally free rank-r quotients, has a universal quotient, commutes with arbitrary base change, includes quotient ranks zero and N, and is H-projective. Our quotient rank is n-d, rather than d. On the locus of an invertible quotient minor, the matrix normalizes to [I A], whose kernel is the free graph (-Av,v). This proves directly that the kernel is a rank-d subbundle and the split exact sequence survives arbitrary base change. There is no unsupported appeal to a subspace Grassmannian with a different convention.

Step 3 cites lem-sc-affine-free-map-zero-locus and lem-sc-local-closed-locus-gluing, whose full statements I opened. The first requires an affine scheme and finite free source and target; I therefore restrict to affine opens trivializing both the universal subbundle and quotient. The second requires the condition to restrict to opens and to hold if it holds on an open cover; vanishing of a sheaf morphism has exactly these properties. Each matrix map is a map between finite free sheaves, and its zero subscheme is locally of finite presentation. The resulting X represents vanishing after arbitrary pullback, not merely a set-theoretic subset. Taking a basis of the finite-dimensional algebra B makes its stability condition a finite tuple of these equations. These are existing generic tools, not new nodes restating this item's conclusion.

Step 4 cites lem-pc-projective-proper, lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence, and def-weil-proper-morphism. I checked their statements give, respectively, projective implies proper; closed immersion is proper and proper maps compose; and proper includes finite type. No finite-type hypothesis is silently obtained from projectivity by citing only its definition. The chosen W supplies an actual k-point, hence nonemptiness. The proof does not assert that X is smooth, reduced, or connected, none of which Borel's theorem here requires.

Step 5 reuses the representing Grassmannian statement and the gluing node's universal factorization criterion. The new subbundle is gL, and its quotient is identified by g^{-1}; this checks constant rank over arbitrary algebras. The explicit formula b(g l)=g((g^{-1} b g)l), together with normalization for every algebra, verifies invariance of the defining bundle-map equations, including nilpotent coefficients. Applying it on the universal family over T times X supplies the factorization through X as a morphism of schemes. Functoriality in scalars and uniqueness of a closed-immersion factorization give the action identities. This step does not replace a scheme-theoretic statement by rational-point density.

Step 6 cites lem-sfb-multiplicative-type-largest-subtorus, def-sc-gm-ga, and lem-sfb-solvable-proper-fixed-point. I opened all three statements. The first explicitly says every torus is smooth and connected; the second supplies the Laurent coordinate ring, which gives an affine finite-type presentation for its finite product; and commutativity makes the first commutator subgroup trivial. The last explicitly gives a k-rational point fixed after every algebra extension, for a nonempty proper finite-type scheme that need not be reduced or connected. These are precisely the hypotheses proved in steps 4--5, rather than a reductive-group version of Borel's theorem. There is no assumption that the algebra B itself is commutative.

Step 7 is elementary assembly: x classifies a positive-dimensional B-stable subspace of minimal dimension, so it is simple, and its fixed-point equality is exactly the asserted equality of subbundles over every R. The statement's all-algebras clause is preserved rather than inferred only for k-points.

For small cases I checked n=1; d=n with B=End(M), where the quotient rank is zero and X=Spec k; B=k times the identity, where d=1 and X is a projective space; and r=0, where the whole torus is trivial. I also checked a nonsemisimple example B=k[N] on k^2 with N(e2)=e1 and N(e1)=0, normalized by diag(t,1). Its simple submodule k e1 works in every characteristic. On the standard line chart L=R(e1+a e2), B-stability gives a^2=0, so the closed submodule scheme can really be nonreduced. The action changes a to a/t and preserves this nilpotent equation, illustrating why the universal algebra check matters. Upper triangular B similarly gives a fixed simple line.
