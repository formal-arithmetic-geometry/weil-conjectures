## Finite-evaluation rectangle construction

This supplies a proof plan for the new generic child so that it can be judged independently. Fix n, H and the left H-torsors X_i=Ha_i (left multiplication action), for i=0,...,n. Let J_r be the free integral module on tuples of vertices of distinct colours i_0<...<i_r, with each vertex in X_(i_j), differential alternating deletion, and augmentation to Z. It is the augmented chain complex of the join of these n+1 sets. The diagonal H-action is free on every nonempty tuple.

The augmented complex is exact below degree n, with a finite-formula contraction in those degrees after forgetting H. To see this without a hidden topological exactness assumption, take the tensor product of the n+1 two-term augmentation complexes Z[X_i] -> Z. Choose basepoint a_i in each X_i, split each augmentation, and write each complex as its kernel in degree one plus the contractible basepoint-to-Z summand. Distributing the finite tensor product gives one summand, the tensor of all kernels, concentrated in degree n+1, and contractible summands for all the others. Their contractions consist only of inserting fixed basepoints and subtracting their augmentations. The tensor total shifted by one is the augmented J. Thus the contraction identities needed through degree n-1 involve finite sums of tuple substitutions, with integral coefficients and no division.

Construct an H-equivariant chain map U from the homogeneous bar chains of H to J through degree n. On a basis tuple first normalize by its first vertex h_0: for (1,h_0^-1 h_1,...), fill its already-defined boundary using that contraction, then translate the result by h_0. The inductive boundary is a cycle; exactness below n just established supplies its fill. Each operation is insertion, multiplication, inversion or finite summation, so the tuple formulas are continuous and finite in every fixed degree. The augmentation starts with [h] mapped to [ha_0]. Embed J into the homogeneous bar chains of G by forgetting the colours and retaining their ordered vertices. Both IU and the usual inclusion of bar(H) into bar(G) lift the identity augmentation. Construct their H-equivariant chain homotopy by the same normalized-first-vertex recursion, using the bar contraction (prepend the identity) to fill each difference cycle. This works also in degree n, since bar(G) is augmented-acyclic in every degree.

Dualizing against a homogeneous degree-n cocycle f gives res(f)=delta(t f)+P f. Here P f evaluates f only on top tuples X_0 x ... x X_n, and t f is a finite integral combination of evaluations. In particular P f vanishes if f vanishes on that rectangle. The residual P f is a cocycle, since it equals res(f)-delta(t f); it need not extend as a full cochain map, and the new statement does not claim that it does. Every scalar norm bound is at most one by ultrametricity.

Finally use finite-index transfer and its finite prism homotopy k with cor res - m id = d k + k d. On cocycles this gives

    c = d((cor(t c)-k c)/m) + cor(P c)/m.

These define T and R. The transfer and k formulas use a finite coset transversal; all their substitutions are continuous on clopen cosets. They use integral coefficients, followed by division by m. Since |m|_ell=1, both bounds are at most one. Passing between homogeneous and inhomogeneous coordinates is evaluation at (1,g_1,g_1g_2,...) or the adjacent-ratio substitution, so it preserves the finite-evaluation and norm properties. This construction depends on G,H,n and the rectangle, not on the coefficient vector space; it preserves coefficient subspaces and parameters. It can be formalized using the existing finite-index-transfer and homogeneous-coordinate nodes when its own split is taken.
