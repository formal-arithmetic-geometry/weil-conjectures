If C is normal, every element of Q(R)=Q(C) integral over R is integral over C and lies in C; all elements of C are already integral over R. Step 1 uses the proper ordinary blowup statement together with the definition of proper to obtain finite type, then finite-type stability of quasi-excellence and its Nagata consequence. I checked that Nagata and Japanese definitions give finite integral closure for the fraction field of each minimal-prime quotient. The affine componentwise normalization statement gives the finite product of those closures, and the finite-type algebra Noetherian theorem gives Noetherian affine charts. The coherence criterion really states finite-type quasi-coherent iff coherent on a locally Noetherian scheme. This use is restricted to the quasi-excellent ordinary tower.

Step 2 uses the full new finality statement twice, not a definition of normalized blowup. The component-dominating normalization-lift node supplies the map to S's normalization; its component hypothesis follows from the explicitly cited generic-point properties. Both bases in these finality applications are locally Noetherian by Step 1. Both schemes are normal and principalize the same ideal; uniqueness makes the two maps inverse. The first stage is treated separately because Y_0 is X rather than its normalization.

Step 3 uses the extended-ideal definition for the zero-scheme inverse image, the normalization node's actual surjectivity and generic-field clauses for nonemptiness and nowhere density, regular-implies-normal, and the normalization-isomorphism statement for a normal locally finite-component scheme. Those hypotheses hold for the Noetherian endpoint. Length zero is the defining construction, with no invented stage.

Step 4 checks the Noetherian source and target hypothesis of Popescu for the affine regular map A to R. The smooth base-change theorem applies to every normal A-algebra B, including a non-Noetherian normalization. The smooth-over-normal and filtered-colimit-normal statements have no Noetherian restriction. The tensor pushout universal property, not a categorical-existence assertion with an unchecked index size, identifies the filtered colimit: the Popescu index is the small filtered diagram in that statement. The same smooth diagram remains available after arbitrary base change. This is the expanded proof of the normality input in ILO 2.2.9.

Step 5 uses the affine total-fraction normalization statement, whose hypothesis is reducedness and locally finitely many components, satisfied for the Noetherian schemes in this step. The new flat-integral lemma supplies the integral-closure identification without any hidden finiteness assumption. The formula on pure tensors commutes with restrictions, giving actual gluing maps, rather than merely local abstract isomorphisms.

Step 6 uses the chartwise flat-blowup comparison, whose statement explicitly identifies the extended centers and restrictions. Flat base change makes each projected tensor algebra flat. For reducedness, the cited flat-tensor-exact theorem preserves D's injection into its integral closure; after tensoring, Step 4 makes the target normal, so the source is reduced. For finite components, the cited going-down theorem forces each minimal prime to contract to a minimal prime of the original Noetherian stage. The original tower's generic fields equal the initial generic fields by the new finality lemma and the normalization generic-properties statement. Over each such field, an affine pullback fibre is a localization of an affine Noetherian ring of T, so has finitely many components. There are only finitely many original generic points on each affine. This verifies the locally finite-component hypothesis of the affine normalization statement even when the pullback is not Noetherian. The tensor/fraction argument then applies at every ordinary blowup followed by normalization, completing the induction. I did not silently assume that regular base change between Noetherian schemes keeps arbitrary intermediate fibre products Noetherian.

Step 7 uses the explicit chart maps, the new total-fraction map, and the tensor universal property. Equality is checked on generators, then on overlap restrictions. These equations prove identity and composition compatibility and the normalization-of-an-ordinary-tower comparison by induction. No separate comparison theorem is being asserted without proof.

Step 8 uses the Cartier-center blowup-isomorphism statement for the unit ideal. Pulling back an inverse proves that an isomorphism stage stays an isomorphism; composition compatibility from Step 7 proves deletion compatibility. A unit stage on a nonnormal scheme is a normalization and is retained unless it is itself an isomorphism. Finally the locally Noetherian affine cover has a finite subcover by the quasi-compactness definition. These are elementary consequences of the cited statements, not new nodes.

Edge cases checked: the empty affine (zero ring), reducible reduced bases (finite products of component fields), length zero, a regular endpoint, the unit ideal on a nonnormal base, open immersions with empty pulled-back centers, and a nonfinite normalization on a nonexcellent Noetherian base. The ordinary paragraph's nonempty-center assertion uses surjective normalization only; it does not incorrectly require a regular pullback to have nonempty centers.
