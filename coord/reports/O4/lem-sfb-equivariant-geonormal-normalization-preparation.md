## Review by O4

I read varieties.tex lemma-finite-extension-geometrically-normal (0BXT), lemma-normalization-and-change-of-fields (0C3N), lemma-normalize-projective (0GK4), and lemma-normalization-locally-algebraic (0BXR), as well as the geometric-normality criterion. I read algebra.tex lemma-perfection (046W), and morphisms.tex lemma-normalization-normal (035Q), including its normal-source factorization. These sources support the particular preparation and lift used here. I opened each of the ten cited DAG nodes and checked its statement, rather than taking extra assertions from its proof.

Step 1 uses precisely the existence statement of lem-weil-pbc-normalization-geonormal-insep and the perfect purely inseparable overfield supplied by lem-weil-pbc-perfect-purely-inseparable-overfield. Both allow arbitrary fields, with no faithfulness or perfection hypothesis. The finite extension is embedded into that overfield by unique roots; the proof writes out the common-exponent check, so no unquoted uniqueness theorem is required.

Step 2 is an elementary argument on unique roots and finitely many algebraic generators. The formula defining each extended automorphism is independent of the exponent; common powers check the ring operations, multiplication law, and inverse. Taking the field generated by the finite orbit makes it stable, finite, and purely inseparable. An automorphism fixing k fixes each unique root, proving the subgroup assertion even for a nonfaithful action. There is no appeal to extending arbitrary automorphisms to an algebraic closure, and no uncited compositum theorem is needed beyond the finite-generator degree argument.

Step 3 uses the actual homeomorphism statement of lem-pc-homeo-pure-insep and the identity of topological spaces in def-weil-pbc-scheme-reduction to prove irreducibility of T. It uses lem-pc-projective-base-change for S_l. The projective normalization node explicitly includes normalization of the reduction, finite surjectivity, properness/projectivity, and the normal integral component decomposition, so it applies even if S_l itself is nonreduced. The base-change normalization node takes integral finite-type C=S, a tower of finite purely inseparable fields e/k and l/e, and geometric normality of N_e. All of these hypotheses were established before applying it. Its statement explicitly gives both the isomorphism with N_e base changed to l and geometric normality after that enlargement: universal homeomorphism alone would not prove the required isomorphism.

Step 4 uses the fibre-product and reduction definitions only to construct the base-change action and preserve the nilradical. The existing dominant-lift theorem really states existence AND uniqueness for a dominant map from a normal integral source. Its target is T, which is integral by step 3, and its source is B, normal integral by that same step. Apply it to h_j composed with the normalization, then use uniqueness for the identity, products, and inverses. This proves the group laws rather than merely asserting functoriality. The action is allowed to move the ground field: the proof works with scheme automorphisms, and includes the inverse in Spec of the field action to handle contravariance.

Step 5 uses def-sc-function-field, whose final paragraph explicitly covers arbitrary integral schemes rather than only curves. Surjectivity followed by the homeomorphism gives dominance of q. Thus pullback is a homomorphism between generic stalk fields, and a unital map of fields is injective. Restricting the already proved equivariance identities to generic stalks proves the two field inclusions are equivariant. The inverse-pullback convention is stated explicitly, removing the potential left-versus-right action ambiguity.

I checked small cases: trivial group, a group acting trivially on k but nontrivially on S, nonfaithful actions, characteristic zero, dimension zero (S the spectrum of a finite possibly inseparable field extension), and S_l nonreduced. In particular T, not S_l, is asserted integral; geometric integrality is not claimed, since separable constants can split after extension. No normality of S is assumed. In characteristic zero the finite purely inseparable extension in step 1 is equality, so l=k without an extra perfectness hypothesis. Every step is below 2000 characters. No new statement-only nodes were needed, so the elementary/duplicate/item-clause/Mathlib-leaf pitfalls were avoided by using the existing, strictly more general tools and writing the root/group arguments in the item's proof.
