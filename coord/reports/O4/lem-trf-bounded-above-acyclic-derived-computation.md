## Original returned proof, preserved verbatim

### Original Step 1

Fix $b$ with $X^i=0$ for $i>b$. Construct $a:P\to X$ by downward recursion, with $P^i=0$ above $b$. The induction invariant is that $Z^{n+1}(P)\to Z^{n+1}(X)$ is epic, where $Z^i=\ker d^i$; above $b$ this is the identity of zero. Form the compatible-pair object $T^n=X^n\times_{Z^{n+1}(X)} Z^{n+1}(P)$, using the corestriction of $d_X^n$ and the cycle map induced by $a$. Choose a projective epimorphism $P^n\twoheadrightarrow T^n$ by enough projectives. Its two components define $a^n$ and $d_P^n$, the latter followed by $Z^{n+1}(P)\hookrightarrow P^{n+1}$. Thus $d_P^{n+1}d_P^n=0$ and $a^{n+1}d_P^n=d_X^na^n$. To check $a^n$ is epic, refine an arbitrary generalized element $x$ of $X^n$ to lift $d_Xx$ through the epic cycle map, then refine again through $P^n\twoheadrightarrow T^n$. The refinement criterion (a) proves the assertion. If $x$ is a cycle, lift the pair $(x,0)$ instead; its lifts are cycles, so the same criterion proves $Z^n(P)\to Z^n(X)$ epic and closes the induction. This constructs all degrees, indexed by $b-n\in\mathbf N$, without a limit operation.

Uses: lem-sp-abelian-refinements.

### Original Step 2

The epic cycle maps from Step 1 imply that $H^n(a)$ is epic. For injectivity, take a generalized cycle $p:T\to P^n$ whose image class in $H^n(X)$ is zero. By the cokernel refinement criterion (c), after an epimorphic refinement there is $x:T'\to X^{n-1}$ with $a^np=d_X^{n-1}x$. Then $(x,p)$ is a generalized element of $T^{n-1}$, since $p$ is a cycle. Lift it after a further epimorphic refinement through $P^{n-1}\twoheadrightarrow T^{n-1}$. Its lift $r$ satisfies $d_P^{n-1}r=p$. Hence the class of $p$ is zero. Generalized elements of $H^n(P)$ themselves lift after refinement through $Z^n(P)\twoheadrightarrow H^n(P)$, so this proves the kernel of $H^n(a)$ is zero by criteria (a)--(c). Therefore $a$ is a quasi-isomorphism. All terms are projective and the specified upper bound is retained, proving the first clause.

Uses: lem-sp-abelian-refinements, def-weil-abstract-derived-category.

### Original Step 3

Let $P$ be any bounded above complex of projectives. It is K-projective by the cited bounded-above criterion: its Hom complex into every acyclic $N$ is acyclic. Degree-zero cocycles of that complex are the chain maps $P\to N$ and degree-zero coboundaries are the maps $d_Nh+hd_P$, so every chain map $P\to N$ is null-homotopic, which is the K-projectivity of the cited Hom bijection. For every quasi-isomorphism $s:Y\to P$ in $K(\mathcal A)$, the cited Hom bijection lifts $Q(s)^{-1}$ uniquely to $t_s:P\to Y$ in $K(\mathcal A)$. Its injectivity gives $s t_s=1_P$. It also gives $u t_s=t_{s'}$ whenever $s'u=s$, because the two maps have the same image under $Q$. Thus $1_P$ is an initial object of the denominator category $S/P$, with unique arrows $t_s:1_P\to s$. Put $F=Q\circ V$ on the homotopy category. The maps $F(t_s):F(P)\to F(Y)$ form a cone on the denominator diagram. In the definition of essentially constant pro-diagram, choose stage $j_0=1_P$, retraction $r=1_{F(P)}$, and common stage $k=1_P$ for every $s$. The factorization condition is then the identity $F(t_s)=F(t_s)$. This diagram therefore has essential value $F(P)$, and its canonical map to its stage $1_P$ is an isomorphism. By the assumed meaning of $LV$ in Situation 05S8 and Definition 05S9 this map is $\varepsilon_P$. This proves projective-complex computation directly, including projective objects placed in degree zero and the zero object.

Uses: lem-sc-bounded-above-projective-kprojective, lem-coef-kprojective-derived-hom-bijection, def-pc-essentially-constant-pro-diagram, def-pur-saturated-triangulated-localization.
