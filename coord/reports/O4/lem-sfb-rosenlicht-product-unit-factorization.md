## Argument retained for the new statement-only lemma

Let m:V tensor_F W -> A be multiplication, and set E=m^{-1}(F.1), K=ker m. If either subspace is zero, or 1 is not in the image of m, there are no inverse pairs and the assertion is immediate. Otherwise E/K is one-dimensional. The Segre embedding identifies P(V) times P(W) with a closed subvariety of P(V tensor W). Intersect it with the projective linear subspace P(E), obtaining a projective scheme Z. It has no point in P(K): any such closed point would be F-rational because F is algebraically closed, and would give nonzero a in V and b in W with ab=0 in the domain A. A nonempty finite-type scheme over F has a closed F-point, so the intersection is empty, not merely empty on rational points. Thus Z lies in P(E) minus the hyperplane P(K), an affine space; it is closed there, hence affine. It is both proper and affine over F and therefore finite (01WN); equivalently one can use 0EKE for affineness of the complement restricted to Z. A finite F-scheme has finitely many F-points. These F-points are precisely pairs ([a],[b]) with ab in F^times, and projection onto [a] gives the unit classes in the statement: rescale b by (ab)^{-1} to obtain the inverse of a, which still lies in W. Conversely any a and inverse b produce such a point. Therefore the unit classes are finite. No finite generation or normality of A was used; only the finite-dimensional spaces V and W enter the finite-type incidence scheme. This is a genuine auxiliary lemma about arbitrary domain algebras and two subspaces, not the product-variety theorem or one of its clauses.
