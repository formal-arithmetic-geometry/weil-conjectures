### Original step 1

The constant-ring identifications are valid for arbitrary $B$. By sheafification, maps from $\underline B_T$ to a ring sheaf $F$ are ring maps $B\to F(T)$: a map from the constant presheaf is determined by its section on the final object. The ring-sheaf adjunction therefore gives
$$\operatorname{Hom}(u^{-1}\underline B_V,F)=\operatorname{Hom}(\underline B_V,u_*F)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ring}}(B,F(U))=\operatorname{Hom}(\underline B_U,F).$$
Here $(u_*F)(V)=F(U)$ because $U\times_VV=U$. Thus the canonical constant-section map is invertible, by the representing-object uniqueness argument: the two universal maps have identity composites since they represent the identity on these Hom sets. The identifications respect composition and ring maps because they send every constant section to itself. This checks the coefficients of the operations in [def-cbc-strict-constant-tower-operations], without imposing finiteness on $B$.
