## Review by Q65

Step 1 uses `lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-noetherian` with precisely the given finite B-module C and Noetherian B. `lem-sc-affine-noetherian-underlying-space` supplies the topological Noetherian condition for `lem-trf-normal-topologically-noetherian-components`, whose statement gives finitely many clopen normal integral components, including the empty case. The displayed product is the sheaf gluing equation on this finite disjoint cover. Multiplication by g is used on each characteristic idempotent to check that no nonzero component has g_j=0. In particular the subsequent open is genuinely dense, rather than merely an arbitrary principal open.

Step 2 uses `lem-weil-pbc-etale-scheme-base-change` for the component restriction and `lem-sbc-finite-etale-open-integral-closure` for its actual finiteness statement. I checked that each X_j is integral, normal and Noetherian, and V=X_j is affine; P_j is finite etale over U_j=D(g_j). Its ring of sections over V is exactly F_j, not a function field or an algebra with extra field hypotheses. The product of the component integral closures is E: monic relations project, and conversely their degrees can be padded to agree so the coefficients assemble. Finite component generators extended by zero then generate E. This finite-product assembly is elementary and was kept in the step; it was not made into a redundant node.

Step 3 uses `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-noetherian-finite-presentation` only after checking C Noetherian and observing that module generators also generate the algebra. `def-sbc-locally-finite-presentation` supplies exactly the affine meaning used when translating this to the morphism. No finite-presentation claim over B is required for the limit lemma; its hypothesis concerns f_0 over Y_0=Spec C.

Step 4 uses `lem-weil-pbc-finite-iff-integral-lft` in the finite-implies-integral direction, so every element of F satisfies a monic relation over C_g. It uses `def-weil-ring-localization-away` for its fractions. I wrote the denominator exponent as N(n-i-1) and checked it is nonnegative for every i<n. The map E_g to F is injective because g is a unit in F, not because C maps injectively to F (which need not hold). Surjectivity is the displayed monic equation for g^N x. This identifies the canonical map as a C_g-algebra map, including rank-zero covers.

Step 5 uses exactly the extension conclusion of `lem-sbc-normal-dense-open-integral-section`: X is locally Noetherian normal, D(g) is dense by step 1, and the affine U is X. Its section rings are C and C_g. The separate injection C to C_g follows from the assumed injectivity of multiplication by g and the fraction definition. The converse integrality direction is T-c. Thus it proves precisely the equality of integral closure with the image, not a normality assertion for an unrelated ring.

Step 6 uses the actual strict-henselization map B_v to A in `lem-sc-strict-henselization-faithfully-flat`. The base-change map in `lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-base-change` is over B_v with R'=C tensor_B B_v; I wrote its tensor reassociation explicitly. `lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-surjective-spectrum` gives surjectivity and `lem-weil-pbc-flat-ring-map-iff-flat-affine` gives geometric flatness. An affine surjective flat morphism is a singleton fpqc cover under `def-weil-pbc-fpqc-covering`: over any affine open its inverse image is affine and covers it. `lem-galois-fpqc-descend-etale` therefore applies to the assumed pulled-back morphism, giving etaleness over B_v. No descent of arbitrary auxiliary models or faithfulness of B to A is assumed.

Step 7 constructs a small directed index set of finite subsets of B minus v. I verified the colimit ring is B_v using fractions and their finite equality witnesses, and used the exact affine conclusion of `lem-weil-pbc-limit-affine-transition` to identify its limit spectrum. The cited `lem-weil-pbc-descend-etale` really allows morphisms between qcqs schemes over the stages; it is not restricted to structure morphisms. I checked S_0, X_0 and Y_0 are affine, every transition is affine, f_0 is locally of finite presentation by step 3, and f at the limit is etale by step 6. The resulting finite product h avoids v by primality, even at the initial empty subset. This is an open in Spec B, not just in Spec C or an etale neighbourhood.

I wrote the tensor generators proving finiteness, and the map on fractions identifying punctured tensor products. The structural equations are respected on e tensor d and on g^{-1}, so the restricted cover is the base change of the given F, through exactly step 4's canonical map. This step is elementary; the source's finite-base-change assertion is actually proved here on generators.
