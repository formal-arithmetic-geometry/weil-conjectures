Thus relative flatness of $H$ on an open $V$ is equivalent to that of $\mathcal G$ on $i^{-1}V$, using [def-pc-scheme-module-flat-over]. Given an open $U\subseteq Z'$ containing $z'$, the closed embedding in [def-weil-closed-immersion] gives an open $V\subseteq X'$ with $i^{-1}V=U$ and $x'\in V$ (write $U=Z'\cap V$ in the subspace topology). This proves (4) iff (2), and also identifies the two marked stalks over $S'$.

### Original Step 4 — assigned to lem-trf-finite-pushforward-flatness-singleton

For use in the affine comparison, specialize [lem-pc-affine-cover-module-tensor-flat-criterion] with $T=A$, $q=1_A$, $Y=\operatorname{Spec}A$, and, for an affine $W$, the one-member affine cover $U_0=W$. The identity base-change pullback is the original module by the pullback universal property [def-pc-module-pullback]; multiplication identifies $A\otimes_A A$ with $A$ and $A\otimes_A N$ with $N$ (inverse $n\mapsto1\otimes n$). Consequently, for a quasi-coherent module $M$ on an affine scheme $W$ over $S'=\operatorname{Spec}A$, flatness over $S'$ is equivalent to $A$-flatness of $\Gamma(W,M)$. This is a direct specialization of the cited affine-cover criterion, with no finiteness or Noetherian assumption.

### Original Step 5 — assigned to lem-trf-finite-pushforward-flatness-singleton

Since $\pi$ is finite, it is affine by [def-weil-finite-integral-morphism], so inverse images of affine opens are affine by [pqf-def-affine-morphism]. Also $\pi_*\mathcal G$ is quasi-coherent by clause (1) of [lem-weil-pbc-affine-pushforward-base-change]. Suppose (2) holds on $U\ni z'$. By [lem-trf-finite-singleton-fibre-localization] choose $V\ni y'$ with $\pi^{-1}V\subseteq U$, then shrink $V$ to an affine neighbourhood. Thus $\mathcal G$ is flat over $S'$ on the affine $\pi^{-1}V$. By Step 4 its sections are flat over $A=\Gamma(S',\mathcal O_{S'})$. By the definition of pushforward, these are precisely $\Gamma(V,\pi_*\mathcal G)$ with the same $A$-action. Step 4 gives (3) on $V$. Conversely, given (3), choose an affine $V\ni y'$ inside that flat neighbourhood. The same section equality and Step 4 imply that $\mathcal G$ is flat on $\pi^{-1}V\ni z'$, proving (2).

For the marked stalks, [lem-trf-relative-flatness-source-cover] applied to $g$ at $x'$ gives $\mathcal F_x$ flat over $\mathcal O_{S,s}$ iff $H_{x'}$ is flat over that same ring; flatness of $g$ follows from [def-weil-etale-morphism].
