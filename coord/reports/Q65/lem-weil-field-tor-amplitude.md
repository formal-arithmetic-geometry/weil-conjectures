## Source error

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the displayed ordinary tensor expression in the imported statement is ill-typed as written. An object of the raw scalar localization `D^b_c(Y,O_E)[1/varpi_E]`, or a finite-stage pair in the common-stage category, is not an object of `D(Mod(E_Y))` merely by being called a field-stage complex. `def-weil-derived-tensor` is explicitly the ordinary tensor over a discrete constant ring sheaf. `def-weil-derived-complete-integral-detailed`, in contrast, uses the completed coefficient sheaf, and `def-weil-finite-tor-amplitude` defines the field-stage subcategory by equality with the rationalized/common-stage category.

The failing type check is the attempted use of an integral representative as an ordinary field-module complex. For example the complete torsion object `(O_E/varpi_E)_Y` has nonzero integral underlying sheaf but becomes zero in the rationalized category, since its identity is killed by the inverted scalar. It cannot be carried unchanged into the displayed ordinary tensor expression. Also completion is not the discrete constant coefficient sheaf: on a pro-etale profinite test object with parameter space `Z_l`, continuous integral functions include the coordinate function, which is not locally constant.

The repair retains both cases and all test sheaves, without a new hypothesis on Y, K or M. It names the realization `K_F=J_F(K)` in the ordinary tensor formula, explicitly constructs this realization in the new comparison lemma, and separately retains the actual categorical field-stage finite-Tor assertion. This does not identify tensor over the constant field sheaf with tensor over a continuous or condensed field ring object. The functor target need not have constructible cohomology as a discrete-coefficient sheaf. Proving intrinsic continuous-coefficient Tor bounds is not substituted for the original constant-coefficient formula.

The blueprint's argument also treated boundedness in the ordinary category as if it followed from the raw category definition alone. The new comparison lemma isolates precisely this missing passage, including the finite-extension compatibility required for the algebraic-closure case. This is a comparison and boundedness fact, not a restatement of finite Tor amplitude. The parent still proves the tensor cohomology and uniform-in-M vanishing.
