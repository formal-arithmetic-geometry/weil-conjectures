- Step 1 uses the definition of a finite morphism to obtain an affine finite module B over A, and writes out why B is a finitely generated k-algebra. Algebraic closedness makes each finite separable extension trivial; this gives geometric irreducibility for the entire affine X and T. - Step 4 checks all the closed-point hypotheses in the finite-type-domain localization-dimension theorem. X's pure dimension and integrality give dim A=d via the spectrum/ring dimension equality. A closed point of T is rational, so its image is rational and closed in X; the etale local-dimension equality applies to these affine rings. - Step 5 uses the fibre criterion for generation, not a definition to assert that criterion. Pulling back local coefficient ideals gives upstairs base support exactly pi^{-1}S. - Step 6 uses the chain formula to rule out the upstairs finite set being all of T, and the Jacobson-density statement to choose a closed point in its nonempty open complement. It does not cite a density theorem restricted to the wrong closed subset.
