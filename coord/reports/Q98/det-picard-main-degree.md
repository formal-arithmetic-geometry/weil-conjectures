Step 1 uses exactly the integrality clause of lem-sc-smooth-connected-curve-local-rings, the projectivity conclusion of lem-sc-proper-dim-one-projective, and the field-extension conclusion of prop-pc-xk-integral. Properness and dimension one check projectivity; algebraic closedness gives the separably closed hypothesis; the supplied P checks the section hypothesis. The result explicitly permits all residue fields, rather than assuming they are algebraically closed.

Step 2 uses the O_C instance of Riemann–Roch, the cohomology scalar-extension isomorphisms of lem-sc-flat-base-change-coherent, and the definition of degree as the Euler-characteristic difference. Its use of lem-pc-degree-base-change is restricted to clause (1), which really does cover every field extension. The vector-space basis argument preserves dimensions. This justifies subtracting the same 1-g at nonclosed points, without applying the algebraically closed Riemann–Roch statement directly to their residue fields.

Step 3 uses the two actual clauses of lem-pc-proper-base-change and the flatness/local-finite-presentation conclusion of lem-pc-smooth-flat. An invertible module is locally free of rank one, so its relative flatness follows from the projection's flatness on stalks. Properness provides the quasi-compact and quasi-separated conditions needed with local finite presentation. Thus all hypotheses of sk-thm-picard-coherence(a) hold over the whole Picard scheme, without any Noetherian assumption on it. The alternating-sum equality is written out using cycles and boundaries of a bounded finite-dimensional complex; it hides no extra theorem. Step 4 is elementary: clopen fibres, open restrictions, and gluing on disjoint opens prove the scheme coproduct and the open-target factorization. In particular it does not use the false claim that set-theoretic factorization through an arbitrary closed subscheme suffices.

Step 5 uses the stipulated universal rigidified module and precisely field-extension degree invariance from lem-pc-degree-base-change. It checks the actual extension kappa(h(s)) to kappa(s), so the represented subfunctor is the fibre-degree subfunctor on every T. A pullback line bundle from T restricts to a one-dimensional vector space; choosing its basis proves directly that the fibre degree is unchanged. Step 6 uses the statement of cor-pc-sigma-pic-iso and the kernel definition def-pc-pic-sigma-functor. Their smooth projective hypothesis was obtained in step 1. Pic(k)=0 is proved by choosing a basis, and the scalar adjustment of a rigidification explains why k-points mean isomorphism classes of line bundles, rather than line bundles with extra trivialization choices.

Step 7 uses the full displayed formula in lem-trf-curve-euler-tensor-generic-rank with generic rank exactly one. Step 1 establishes its integral, proper, dimension-one hypotheses on each fibre; an invertible module is coherent there. Subtracting chi(O) derives tensor additivity, and the dual assertion follows by tensoring with the inverse. This replaces the incorrect imported additivity citation. rem-pc-group-structure states that multiplication and inversion are induced by the Picard functor's operations, which is precisely what is restricted to the represented degree-zero subfunctor.
