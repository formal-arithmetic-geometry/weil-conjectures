I read Stacks Project `divisors.tex`, Tag 0BD8,
`lemma-closure-effective-cartier-divisor`, including its entire proof. The source
assumes only a locally Noetherian scheme, an open, an effective Cartier divisor
on that open, and factorial local rings at the missing points. Its conclusion
is existence of an effective Cartier divisor whose intersection with the open
is the original divisor. The accepted DAG statement says exactly this.

Hypotheses and conclusion, clause by clause:

- `{X : Scheme.{u}} [IsLocallyNoetherian X]` renders the arbitrary locally
  Noetherian scheme. `u` is unrestricted. There is no affine, quasi-compact,
  integral, reduced, separated, or density assumption on X.
- `(V : X.Opens)` renders an arbitrary open subscheme. `V.toScheme` is its
  canonical scheme, and `V.ι` is its inclusion into X.
- `(D : V.toScheme.IdealSheafData) (hD : D.IsInvertible)` is the divisor
  presented by its quasi-coherent defining ideal. I read the recorded
  `def-pc-divisor-line-bundle` declarations in
  `WeilConjectures.AlgebraicGeometry.EffectiveCartier`, particularly
  `IdealSheafData.IsInvertible.isInvertible_dual_toModules`, and the defining
  `Scheme.IdealSheafData.IsInvertible` in
  `WeilConjectures.Mathlib.AlgebraicGeometry.IdealSheaf.Invertible`.
  The latter means locally generated by one nonzerodivisor; it is not merely
  local principality. I also read `IsEffectiveCartierCenter` and
  `isEffectiveCartierCenter_iff_isInvertible_ker` in the recorded EffectiveCartier
  module: ideals with this predicate are precisely effective Cartier closed
  subschemes. Thus using the ideal changes the presentation, not the statement.
- `hfactorial : ∀ x : X, x ∉ V → IsDomain (X.presheaf.stalk x) ∧
  UniqueFactorizationMonoid (X.presheaf.stalk x)` renders exactly the boundary
  hypothesis. Both domain and unique factorization are included; no property
  of the local rings at points inside V is required. I read the Mathlib
  UniqueFactorizationMonoid definition and its explanation that the conjunction
  with IsDomain means unique factorization domain.
- `∃ Dext : X.IdealSheafData, Dext.IsInvertible ∧ Dext.comap V.ι = D` renders
  existence of the extended effective Cartier divisor and the exact equality
  of restrictions. I read Mathlib IdealSheaf/Functorial.lean: `comap` is the
  kernel of the pullback closed immersion, and `comapIso` identifies its
  subscheme with that fibre product. Consequently this equality is equality
  as closed subschemes with all multiplicities, not just equality of supports.
