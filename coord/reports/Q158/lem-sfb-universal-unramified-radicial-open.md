## Original proof, preserved in full for the next workers

### Original step 1

First $f$ is locally of finite type, without any assumption on $q$. Indeed, at $a\in A$ choose affine opens $W\subset B$, $H\subset q^{-1}(W)\subset C$, and $G\subset f^{-1}(H)\subset A$ containing $p(a),f(a),a$, respectively. By [lem-weil-pbc-lft-affine-pairs] for $p$, $\mathcal O_A(G)$ is generated by finitely many elements over $\mathcal O_B(W)$. The same elements generate it over $\mathcal O_C(H)$, since the first ring map factors through $\mathcal O_C(H)$ by $q\circ f=p$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-lft-pointwise-criterion] to $f$.

Cited statements: lem-weil-pbc-lft-affine-pairs, lem-weil-pbc-lft-pointwise-criterion

### Original step 2

We will use the following elementary consequence of the existing point description. For $r:D\to B$, $h:T\to B$, and points $d\in D,t\in T$ over the same $b\in B$, [lem-weil-common-extension-fields] gives embeddings of $\kappa(d)$ and $\kappa(t)$ in one field over $\kappa(b)$ (the residue-field maps are those of [def-weil-residue-field]). The kernel of the resulting unital map $\kappa(d)\otimes_{\kappa(b)}\kappa(t)\to M$ is prime: a product maps to zero in a field exactly when a factor does. That prime and [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-points] give a point of $D\times_B T$ projecting to the prescribed pair $(d,t)$. This uses no closedness, flatness, or finiteness of $h$.

Cited statements: lem-weil-common-extension-fields, def-weil-residue-field, lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-points

### Original step 3

Apply [lem-trf-unramified-locus-base-change] to $f$ and let $U\subset A$ be its open unramified locus. For $h:T\to B$, the universal property [def-pc-fibre-product] identifies $f_T$ with the base change of $f$ along $C\times_B T\to C$. Thus it is locally of finite type by [lem-weil-pbc-lft-base-change], and its unramified locus is the inverse image of $U$. A morphism is unramified exactly when its entire source is its unramified locus: one direction is the union defining the locus; for the other, the unramified source neighbourhoods cover the source and their unramified affine presentations together give the affine cover in [def-weil-unramified-morphism]. Hence $f_T$ is unramified exactly when no point of $A\times_B T$ projects into $A\setminus U$.

Cited statements: lem-trf-unramified-locus-base-change, def-pc-fibre-product, lem-weil-pbc-lft-base-change, def-weil-unramified-morphism

### Original step 4

Set $B_0=B\setminus p(A\setminus U)$, with its open subscheme structure. It is open because $A\setminus U$ is closed and $p$ is a closed map. For arbitrary $h:T\to B$, the condition at the end of step 3 is equivalent to $h(T)\subset B_0$. In fact, a point of $A\times_B T$ outside the inverse image of $U$ supplies a bad $a$ and $t$ with $p(a)=h(t)$. Conversely, for such a bad $a$ and $t$, the common-field and fibre-product argument of step 2 supplies a point above both. By [lem-weil-pbc-morphism-factors-through-open-subscheme], $h(T)\subset B_0$ is equivalent to the unique factorization through $B_0$. Thus $B_0$ represents precisely unramifiedness of $f_T$.

Cited statements: def-weil-open-immersion, lem-weil-common-extension-fields, def-weil-residue-field, lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-points, lem-weil-pbc-morphism-factors-through-open-subscheme

### Original step 5

Put $A_0=A\times_B B_0$, $C_0=C\times_B B_0$, $f_0:A_0\to C_0$, and $D=A\times_C A$. For any $r:E\to B$, the open subscheme $r^{-1}(B_0)\subseteq E$ [def-weil-open-immersion], with the map to $B_0$ given by [lem-weil-pbc-morphism-factors-through-open-subscheme], has the universal property of $E\times_B B_0$ [def-pc-fibre-product]: a morphism $T\to E$ together with a morphism $T\to B_0$ over $B$ is the same as a morphism $T\to E$ whose image lies in $r^{-1}(B_0)$, by the uniqueness clause of that factorization. So $A_0$, $C_0$ and $D\times_B B_0$ are the open subschemes $p^{-1}(B_0)$, $q^{-1}(B_0)$ and $p_2^{-1}(B_0)$, and their maps to $B_0$ are the restrictions of $p$, $q$ and $p_2$. By [def-pc-fibre-product], $D_0=A_0\times_{C_0}A_0$ is canonically $D\times_B B_0$, compatibly with both projections and the diagonal. The restrictions $p_0:A_0\to B_0$ and $p_{20}:D_0\to B_0$ are closed maps: for a closed map $r:E\to B$ and a closed subset $F$ of $r^{-1}(B_0)$, write $F=F'\cap r^{-1}(B_0)$ with $F'$ closed in $E$; then $r(F)=r(F')\cap B_0$, which is closed in $B_0$. Apply this to $p$ and $p_2$. No assertion that a closed map stays closed after an arbitrary base change is being used.

Cited statements: def-pc-fibre-product, def-weil-open-immersion, lem-weil-pbc-morphism-factors-through-open-subscheme

### Original step 6

The identity test $B_0\to B_0$ in step 4 shows that $f_0$ is unramified. Consequently its diagonal $\delta:A_0\to D_0$ is an open immersion by [def-weil-unramified-morphism]. Let $J=\delta(A_0)$, an open subset of $D_0$ by [def-weil-open-immersion], and let $E=D_0\setminus J$. This is a closed subset. Since $p_{20}$ is closed, $V=B_0\setminus p_{20}(E)$ is open in $B_0$, hence in $B$; equip it with the open subscheme structure. This is the required candidate.

Cited statements: def-weil-unramified-morphism, def-weil-open-immersion

### Original step 7

Fix $h:T\to B_0$. The fibre-product universal property [def-pc-fibre-product] identifies $D_0\times_{B_0}T$ with $A_T\times_{C_T}A_T$ and the base change of $\delta$ with $\delta_T$. It is an open immersion by [lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-base-change]. Its image is exactly the inverse image of $J$ under $D_0\times_{B_0}T\to D_0$: identify $A_0$ with the open subscheme $J$ using [lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-iso-onto-image]; the inverse-image open subscheme has the same universal property as $J\times_{D_0}(D_0\times_{B_0}T)$, because a map to it is a map to $D_0\times_{B_0}T$ whose composite lands in $J$, with its unique factorization furnished by [lem-weil-pbc-morphism-factors-through-open-subscheme]. Therefore $\delta_T$ is surjective on points exactly when the inverse image of $E$ is empty.

Cited statements: def-pc-fibre-product, lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-base-change, lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-iso-onto-image, lem-weil-pbc-morphism-factors-through-open-subscheme

### Original step 8

The inverse image of $E$ in $D_0\times_{B_0}T$ is empty if and only if $h(T)\subset V$. A point in that inverse image projects to $e\in E$ and $t\in T$ with $p_{20}(e)=h(t)$, showing failure of containment. Conversely, if containment fails, choose $e\in E$ and $t\in T$ with this equality; exactly as in step 2, take a common extension of $\kappa(e),\kappa(t)$ over $\kappa(h(t))$ and the prime kernel of their tensor map to that field. The point description gives a point above $(e,t)$, which lies in the inverse image of $E$. Containment is equivalent to factorization $T\to V$ by [lem-weil-pbc-morphism-factors-through-open-subscheme].

Cited statements: lem-weil-common-extension-fields, def-weil-residue-field, lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-points, lem-weil-pbc-morphism-factors-through-open-subscheme

### Original step 9

By [def-weil-radicial-morphism], applied to $f_T:A_T\to C_T$, universal injectivity is equivalent to surjectivity of its diagonal $A_T\to A_T\times_{C_T}A_T$. Steps 7 and 8 therefore say, for every $h:T\to B_0$, that $f_T$ is universally injective exactly when $h$ factors through $V$. For arbitrary $h:T\to B$, if $f_T$ is unramified and universally injective, step 4 first factors $h$ uniquely through $B_0$ and then the preceding equivalence factors it through $V$. Conversely, a factorization through $V\subset B_0$ gives unramifiedness by step 4 and universal injectivity by the diagonal equivalence. This proves the claimed universal property for every scheme $T$.

Cited statements: def-weil-radicial-morphism
