## Findings and changes

The imported item was a sound statement with no proof. Its generality matters: neither finite dimension nor finite generation of M is assumed. I retained that statement, including the empty A and zero-Lie-algebra cases. Milne, Section 23h, printed pp.503–504, 508–511, supplies the Lie-theoretic and highest-weight context but does not state this general criterion. I read those local PDF pages. The proof is therefore marked proof_origin.by=agent.

The crucial reusable input already exists as lem-sfb-integrable-sl2-reflection-automorphism. Its statement concerns the adjoint action on a possibly infinite-dimensional Lie algebra, not an arbitrary module. I do not silently apply it to M. Step 2 explicitly constructs the Lie algebra g semidirect M with M an abelian ideal. Step 3 checks local nilpotence on this direct sum, applies the actual statement to that Lie algebra, and restricts its weight-space isomorphism to the invariant ideal M.

The remaining argument uses no longest-element theorem, invariant positive-definite form, Weyl chamber theorem, complete reducibility, or infinite-dimensional sl2 classification. It averages the simple-root height over the finite subgroup generated by the simple reflections. The average vanishes on every simple root because it is invariant under the reflection negating that root. This gives a lower height bound, which combines with the prescribed integral upper cones to bound each nonnegative integer coefficient. The coefficients range in an explicit finite box. The finite basis argument then proves finite dimension.

All seven steps have fewer than 2000 characters. The semidirect-product verification is an explicit expansion of the representation identity. The averaging step is three linear equalities on a basis; the coefficient bound is an explicit finite-set argument. These are steps, not new nodes. The non-elementary reflection theorem and root structure are cited as existing statements. No mathematical content of the item has been moved into a renamed lemma.

## Review by Q02

I reviewed the delivery-errors B–E requirements against the node, cited statements and local sources. There are no new statement-only nodes needing independent source or duplicate audits. The item's statement introduces its Lie algebra, representation, ordinary weight spaces, finite upper set and local-nilpotence hypotheses, and its existing statement dependencies define the Lie and semisimple notions and the root data. Those dependencies remain appropriate.

Step 1 uses only the hypotheses. For empty A every weight space is zero, and the given direct sum is zero. For rank zero the stated simple-root basis implies h-dual is zero, so M is its single finite-dimensional zero-weight space; I did not assume an absent positive rank.

Step 2 cites def-wf-lie-algebra, whose statement explicitly gives the representation identity r([x,y])=r(x)r(y)-r(y)r(x). Expanding the displayed semidirect bracket yields its Jacobi identity by precisely that identity. This defines a genuine Lie algebra and identifies M as an abelian ideal. No semidirect-product existence theorem is hidden here.

Step 3 cites the full statements of lem-sfb-split-semisimple-root-decomposition and lem-sfb-integrable-sl2-reflection-automorphism, both read in full. The former states the normalized triples and nilpotence of each adjoint root operator, so its hypotheses hold for the given e_i,f_i. The module hypotheses give local nilpotence on the other direct summand; maxima of annihilating exponents give local nilpotence on L. The latter permits infinite-dimensional L, needs no weight decomposition, and explicitly states the reflected weight-space isomorphism. Every exponential and inverse preserves M, so restricting to its intersection with an L-weight space gives exactly the ordinary module weight space. This avoids the frequent C4 error of taking a module theorem from a statement only about the adjoint representation.

Step 4 cites the root-decomposition node for the finite full root-reflection group. The simple-reflection subgroup is therefore finite, without needing the additional theorem that simple reflections generate the full group. Inverses of the generators equal themselves by alpha_i(h_i)=2. Successive applications of step 3 prove invariance of the weight support. The upper bound for height is the displayed sum of nonnegative coefficients, with C chosen nonnegative even if all A-heights are negative.

Step 5 uses only steps 3–4 and linearity. Right multiplication permutes the finite subgroup, its averaged height is reflection invariant, and each simple root is negated by its own reflection. Over Q this forces zero on the basis. In the sum for a weight, the identity term is isolated and each of the d-1 remaining terms is at most C, giving the claimed lower bound. No crystallographic lattice condition on the arbitrary weights is assumed here.

Step 6 combines that lower bound with the item's integer-cone hypothesis, bounding each n_i between 0 and a common integer N. The finite product box and its image explicitly prove support finiteness; mere boundedness of a rational subset would not suffice. Step 7 uses the given finite dimensions of the individual weight spaces and the ordinary direct sum to form a finite spanning union of bases. It does not replace ordinary weights by generalized weights or assume that M is cyclic.

Tested small and limiting cases: A empty, M zero, rank zero, the rank-one irreducible sl2 module of highest weight m (height range [-m/2,m/2] in simple-root coordinates), reducible root systems, repeated/direct sums with finite weight multiplicities, rational upper weights not in the root lattice, and an upper set whose heights are all negative. In the last case the inequalities correctly rule out nonzero support. Infinite direct sums of trivial modules illustrate why finite-dimensional weight spaces are needed: their support is finite but their zero-weight space violates the hypothesis. The Verma-module example violates lowering local nilpotence, so it is not a counterexample. No correction of the statement was necessary.
