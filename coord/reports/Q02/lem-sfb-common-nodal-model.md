## Arguments for the three additional statement-only nodes

Component comparison (old step 2): finite-presentation over the Noetherian DVR gives Noetherianity. Regular local rings are domains, so distinct irreducible components do not meet; finitely many components are open and closed. Flatness makes pi a nonzerodivisor, excluding vertical components. Each component's generic open is therefore nonempty, integral and dense, and a generic isomorphism uniquely matches these generic components. The relative curve dimension yields dimension-two integral regular surfaces (also explicitly supplied for the T_i by regularization); their clopen structure maps are projective and thus proper. The base S is Noetherian. Apply 0C5S to each matched pair and the specified dense generic-open isomorphism. Every intermediate surface is proper over S because point blowups are projective. A reduced closed centre has closed singleton image under this proper map, so it maps to the closed point, not the nonclosed generic point of S. Consequently every blowup is the identity generically. Finite disjoint unions give the desired componentwise comparison. This retains both the component argument and the special-centre argument that were dropped in the returned proof.

Finite-complement conductor descent (old step 9): apply 0BFP(1) and (2) on each regular surface component. The codimension-one local rings are DVRs by lem-sc-regular-dim1-dvr. Thus the closed complement of the maximal isomorphism open contains only codimension-two points. On a Noetherian dimension-two surface such a closed subset has dimension zero, hence finitely many points, all closed. Since q_K is an isomorphism, they are special. Finite blowup centres of r map to finitely many closed special points of C under the proper intermediate maps; r also takes the finite exceptional set to closed special points. Their images under the finite C -> X remain closed special points, giving D and Z. Off D the map h is an isomorphism. For V=X minus Z, restriction gives an isomorphism W_V -> C_V that identifies the two copies of B_V and their map to N_V. The open-restriction pushout properties identify V and p^{-1}(V) as pushouts of isomorphic diagrams; their unique induced map is an isomorphism. In particular this argument works at persistent horizontal nodes where C -> X alone is not an isomorphism. The existing exceptional-curve-count node may supply the regular-surface finite-set subclaim when this node is later split, but does not give the conductor descent.

Proper-chain pinching connectedness (old step 10): composition of proper surjections with nonempty geometrically connected fibres has the same property. After a geometric base change, the inverse image under the upper map of a connected intermediate fibre is connected: a partition into nonempty clopen subsets would have disjoint closed images, because a connected upper fibre lies in one piece; properness makes images closed, and surjectivity makes them cover the intermediate fibre. This contradicts connectedness. Induction handles the finite chain. For p, off N the geometric fibre is a geometric fibre of h because both conductor complements are unchanged. ### Original step 1

### Original step 2

Among point-blowup sequences $q_1:W\to T_1$ equipped with an $S$-map $q_2:W\to T_2$ inducing the fixed generic identification, choose one of minimum length, summing lengths over components.
