I read the source's complete argument around the item, not just its list of three constructions. In 0C4A I checked separately the split product of two power-series rings and the nonsplit separable quadratic power-series ring. The common substitution X=zt, Y=t gives the single algebra calculation used here. The image is F+tL[[t]]: expansion gives a constant in F and positive coefficients in L, and conversely each such series is f(t)+zt g(t), the image of f(Y)+Xg(Y). This explains why the new integral-closure statement is a smaller intermediate algebra fact; the item's projective comparison is still proved in its own steps. For the new quadratic-completion statement I read 0C4D (3)=>(2) and 0C49 (3), including the coefficient-field section. Their separability hypothesis is supplied by the item. The source allows the residue field to differ from k, exactly the case missing from the existing rational node.

Step 1 uses only the new separable-node completion statement, which supplies reducedness, Noetherianness, essential finite type and dimension one as well as the presentation. The explicit three-vector test for a nonzero leading quadratic coefficient is valid even over F_2. Step 2 uses the statement of `lem-trf-curve-normalization-completion`, whose hypotheses are now all available; it supplies finiteness and the actual canonical embedding into completed total fractions. The new quadratic-series node states the substitution and wedge image, not merely abstract isomorphism of the rings.

Step 3 invokes the complete-module quotient theorem with M=A and the finitely generated maximal ideal; its statement gives every finite quotient and its canonical projection. Taking kernels therefore identifies the graded pieces and multiplication, as required by the associated-graded definition. I wrote the exact formula for every positive power of the wedge maximal ideal. Step 4 uses the full standard-chart clause of `def-nagata-affine-proj`, which really does state the affine degree-zero homogeneous localization. I checked the entire Proj lies in this chart by the square-zero positive ideal modulo t; I did not assume that the nonsplit case has two rational points.

Step 5 cites both the completion's faithful flatness and the module injection theorem. Faithful flatness alone is not silently treated as injectivity on arbitrary modules. The quotient-tensor identity used for B/fB is given explicitly from the tensor universal property. This proves mB=fB and the regularity of f before any fraction a/f^n is used. Step 6 verifies all relations in the degree-zero localization, including changes modulo m^(n+1) and extra denominator powers, so the canonical map is defined over the original residue field. Step 7 gives the two inverse tensor-quotient maps and checks on leading coefficients that the displayed map is an isomorphism; it does not infer a canonical map from an arbitrary abstract isomorphism.

Step 8 computes the transition from f to g as multiplication by the explicit unit g/f, then applies that same formula to a local-ring isomorphism. This is the naturality clause. Step 9 gives the explicit inverse to evaluation at distinct quadratic roots; irreducibility gives the other field case, and b^2-4ac nonzero is the separability condition also in characteristic two. For the comparison with the actual geometric completed local ring, I added a citation to `lem-trf-separable-closed-point-formal-presentation`: its base-change conclusion applies to every presentation, so it applies to the quadratic one just constructed and to the embedding of the residue field determined by the chosen geometric point.
