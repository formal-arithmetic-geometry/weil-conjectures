Step 5 uses the existence/root property in def-weil-algebraic-closure, the minimal-polynomial kernel property in def-weil-pbc-separable-algebraic-extension, and exactly the one-generator separability statement of lem-trf-simple-separable-field-extension. A product of distinct linear factors has no repeated roots, and its divisors have none; equivalently the minimal polynomial is coprime to its derivative. Thus the separable-generation hypothesis is verified, not inferred from a bare definition of flatness or separability.

Step 6 uses lem-sfb-splitting-field-finite-normal for finite dimensionality and normality; I read its statement and Tag 09HU, lemma-splitting-field. Both hypotheses hold: all p displayed roots are in E(b) and one of them is b. With step 5 this permits the fixed-field lemma. The map tau to tau(b)-b is a homomorphism because prime-field elements are fixed, and it is injective because L is generated by b. These are written calculations, not an unnamed comparison theorem.

Step 7 uses the fixed-field conclusion already established in step 6 and the monic quotient basis it explicitly cites. If b is not in E, some automorphism moves it; a nonzero prime-field translation has p distinct multiples, so its powers fill the entire possible image and force the degree p. The quotient map has no kernel by the same root/division calculation as step 4, then equal dimensions give an isomorphism. The fixed-field lemma applies to L, not to a quotient ring that has not yet been proved a field.

Step 8 cites the monic basis for bounded representatives. The displayed interpolation polynomial gives a preimage of every tuple, while the root bound gives injectivity. Hence evaluation is an algebra isomorphism, with no uncited Chinese remainder theorem hidden in the step. The reverse implication in the final iff is checked using a coordinate projection, not simply assuming the cases exhaust all possibilities. For a=0 it gives the p distinct prime-field roots and E^p, including p=2. Over F_2, a=1 gives the field F_4; over F_3, a=1 has no root and gives a degree-3 field. For an algebraically closed E every a has a root, so only the product case occurs. The product cannot be a field when p>=2.
