## New nodes

`lem-sfb-arrangement-generic-broken-line` is a lemma about any finite affine hyperplane arrangement in a finite-dimensional real vector space. It gives density of regular points and a two-segment path in any convex open set, avoiding all intersections of distinct hyperplanes. It is not a root-system assertion. Its source records the supporting chamber context of Milne C.8--C.10, pp.609--610, and identifies the statement as an agent supporting lemma rather than a verbatim assertion of that text. A proof plan for the future worker is as follows. A finite union of proper affine subspaces cannot contain an open set: choose for each such subspace a nonzero affine linear equation vanishing on it, multiply the equations, and use the fact that a nonzero real polynomial cannot vanish on an open box (induction on the number of coordinates, using the finite-root property in one variable). For regular endpoints x,y, exclude each hyperplane and, for each nonempty intersection A of two distinct hyperplanes, the affine spans of A with x and with y. These spans have dimension at most dim(E)-1 because A has codimension at least two and x,y do not belong to A. Choose z in U outside this finite union. If [x,z] met A, z would belong to the excluded span of x and A; likewise for [z,y]. Neither segment has an endpoint in a hyperplane, and an affine linear function along a segment has at most one zero unless identically zero; a zero between nonzero endpoint values forces opposite signs. This supplies the stated finite crossing sequence. In dimensions zero and one there are no nonempty intersections of distinct hyperplanes to exclude. The finite avoidance and crossing package is substantially more than a two-line elementary consequence, so it is exposed as one genuine tool node.

`lem-sfb-chamber-face-parabolic-residue` states, for any reduced crystallographic real root system with base D, that the chambers whose closures contain a point q of the J-face are precisely the W_J translates of the fundamental chamber. It also explicitly identifies the vanishing roots as R intersect span(J), so the main proof can determine the size of J. The finite, spanning, positive-definite, reduced, crystallographic and same-sign base hypotheses are all written in the statement. The source is again Milne's chamber context C.8--C.10 and C.19--C.22, with an explicit notice that this face refinement is agent supplied. A proof plan is to restrict the roots to R_J=R intersect span(J); same-sign expansions show that J is a base and that precisely these roots vanish at q. Roots outside span(J) keep their nonzero signs in a sufficiently small neighbourhood of q. The local chambers there are therefore the chambers of R_J in the normal directions, extended by the directions along the face. Simple-reflection generation identifies its reflection group with W_J, and the existing chamber theorem applied to R_J gives exactly the claimed orbit; the W_J generators fix q. This is a local parabolic description for every J, not connectivity along a marked wall or a restatement of either clause of the item.
