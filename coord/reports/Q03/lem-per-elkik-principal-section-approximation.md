## Review by Q03

I read Elkik's original PDF, II Lemma 2 on pp.561–562, Lemma 3 on pp.562–564, and Lemma 4 with its proof on pp.564–566. The item is Lemma 4's approximation assertion, with no need for the additional preservation-of-the-open conclusion. Noetherianity is the paper's standing assumption. In particular neither the paper nor this statement requires t to be regular. The conormal-chart node is the construction of Lemma 3 together with the ambient-neighbourhood equations paragraph in Lemma 4: the symmetric conormal algebra has an augmentation, its smooth open has constant relative dimension, and after adjoining zero variables the conormal has constant free rank on every affine subset of that open. A basis lifts to finitely many equations near that affine subset; shrink in the ambient polynomial space and clear powers of the defining function to obtain sJ contained in (f). Multiplying s by an element which is a unit on D(h) preserves that open. The maximal minors generate the unit ideal there by the smooth conormal criterion. This is a genuine geometric lemma, rather than an elementary step hidden in the item's proof. The Tougeron node states exactly Lemma 2: arbitrary ideal I, n > 2h, errors in I^n, all maximal minors containing I^h, and a corrected solution modulo I^(n-h). Both new statements introduce every object and have empty proof/proof_deps.

I opened each cited node and checked its statement against the use:

1. The henselian definition explicitly gives uniqueness of idempotent lifts for every finite algebra, so applying it to A/m proves t lies in every maximal ideal. The Jacobson completion node states faithful flatness under precisely the Noetherian and radical hypotheses just established. The contraction node explicitly includes injectivity and contraction of every extended ideal. The completion-mod-power node gives both surjectivity and the exact kernel t^q C. These are the four citations, and they justify all completion comparisons used later. The nilpotent case is the stabilized inverse limit itself.
2. The Noetherian definition says every ideal is finitely generated, so it applies to the ideal T of all power-torsion elements. A common annihilator exponent kills T by its finite generators. The Artin–Rees node states the needed inclusion for any submodule of a finite module; take M=A and N=T, then q >= k+e makes the intersection zero. No regularity of t is used. I read Stacks algebra.tex Tag 00IN, lemma-Artin-Rees, at line 12187.
3. The new conormal-chart node explicitly gives B -> D -> B with composite identity and a fixed polynomial presentation. Therefore u composed with the retraction extends the formal section, and the relation ri=1 proves its restriction returns u. I do not assume the symmetric conormal algebra is smooth globally.
4. The affine-spectrum definition supplies the closed/open ideal language. The radical node says the radical is the intersection of primes, which gives the single power t^alpha in the evaluated ideal; the resulting expression uses only finitely many summands by the definition of an ideal generated by a set. The completion-mod-power node lifts the finite list of coefficients. The completion definition realizes C as compatible sequences, so the displayed finite geometric sums really give the inverse of 1+tz. This avoids assuming C is local or importing an uncited completeness theorem. The chosen h is in H, and its value is t^alpha times a unit; those two clauses respectively prove D(h) is inside W_D and contains the whole punctured section.
5. The new chart node states sJ subset (f), the equality of the principal opens and the unit-ideal property for the maximal minors. The radical node gives both t^d in (s(a)) and t^g in the evaluated minors ideal. These certificates are fixed before any precision is selected. No individual minor is claimed invertible globally, and no claim is taken from the proof of a cited node.
6. The completion projection and contraction statements give lifts and convert the residual C-ideal containments into A-ideal containments. The Jacobson one-plus-units node states precisely the criterion needed to divide by 1-t^(L-g)z_0. Polynomial congruence follows by expanding monomials. I marked this certificate manipulation elementary because it is only the displayed subtraction and factorization from those statements; it invokes no further lifting result. I read Tag 0AME, lemma-contained-in-radical, at algebra.tex line 3555.

The conclusion has precision L-g, so R=L-g is the precision used in the last step. A correction lemma for complete rings would not have sufficed here.
