Put $k=\mathbf Q$, $S=\operatorname{Spec}k$, and let $\mathcal I$ be the quasi-coherent ideal of $C$ in $P$ [def-sbc-closed-immersion-ideal-sheaf]. Apply [lem-pur-smooth-pair-local-coordinates] to the two smooth $S$-schemes $C,P$. Near each point of $C$ it gives an open $V\subseteq P$, integers $a,b\geq0$, and an etale map $h:V\to\mathbf A^{a+b}_k$ for which $C\cap V$ is the scheme-theoretic inverse image of $\mathbf A^a_k$ and $\mathcal I|_V$ is generated by the last $b$ coordinates. On $P\setminus C$ the ideal is the unit ideal. Hence $\mathcal I$ is locally generated by finitely many sections, which is exactly finite type for this ideal sheaf. The closed immersion $C\hookrightarrow P$ has finite presentation: locally it is a quotient by these finitely many equations; its affine closed-immersion description supplies quasi-compactness and quasi-separatedness [def-weil-closed-immersion].

Cited statements: def-sbc-closed-immersion-ideal-sheaf, lem-pur-smooth-pair-local-coordinates, def-weil-closed-immersion.

### Original step 2 → lem-trf-smooth-pair-blowup-local-models

For a chart $h:V\to\mathbf A^{a+b}_k$ of step 1 put $A=k[x_1,\ldots,x_a,y_1,\ldots,y_b]$ and $J=(y_1,\ldots,y_b)$. The map $h$ is flat by [def-weil-etale-morphism]. Apply [thm-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change] with its comparison [def-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change-map] to $h$ and to the open immersion $V\hookrightarrow P$ (an open restriction is flat because its local-ring maps are isomorphisms). The inverse-image assertion in step 1 identifies the ideals being pulled back, giving
$$b_P^{-1}(V)\simeq\operatorname{Bl}_{C\cap V}V\simeq V\times_{\mathbf A^{a+b}_k}\operatorname{Bl}_{J}(\operatorname{Spec}A),$$
where $b_P:P^+\to P$ is the blowup morphism. Thus the first projection to the model blowup is a base change of the etale map $h$.

Cited statements: def-weil-etale-morphism, thm-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change, def-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change-map.

### Original step 3 → lem-trf-coordinate-subspace-blowup-charts

Suppose $b>0$. By [prop-nagata-affine-blowup-chart-cover] the model blowup has charts $\operatorname{Spec}(A[J/y_i])$. By [def-nagata-blowup-chart-ring], this chart ring is the subalgebra of $A_{y_i}$ generated by $A$ and $y_j/y_i$. The map
$$k[x_1,\ldots,x_a,y_i,t_j\ (j\ne i)]\longrightarrow A_{y_i},\qquad t_j\longmapsto y_j/y_i$$
has precisely that image: the other original coordinates satisfy $y_j=y_i t_j$. It is injective, since a monomial $x^\alpha y_i^e\prod_{j\ne i}t_j^{\beta_j}$ maps to the Laurent monomial $x^\alpha y_i^{e-\sum\beta_j}\prod_{j\ne i}y_j^{\beta_j}$, and these exponents recover $(\alpha,e,\beta)$ uniquely. Consequently every chart is $\mathbf A^{a+b}_k$. If $b=0$, then $J=0$; apply the same covering statement using the generator $0$. Its chart ring $A[0/0]\subseteq A_0=0$ is the zero ring, so this blowup is empty. This also covers centers containing a whole open component.

Cited statements: prop-nagata-affine-blowup-chart-cover, def-nagata-blowup-chart-ring.

### Original step 4 → lem-trf-smooth-pair-blowup-local-models (etale base change) and lem-trf-coordinate-subspace-blowup-charts (smoothness)

Each nonempty model chart of step 3 is isomorphic over $k$ to $\mathbf A^{a+b}_k$, hence smooth over $k$ by [sbc-lem-affine-space-smooth] and the isomorphism assertion (c) of [lem-weil-pbc-smooth-restrict-open-subschemes]; the same assertion transports smoothness along the isomorphism $b_P^{-1}(V)\simeq V\times_{\mathbf A^{a+b}_k}\operatorname{Bl}_J(\operatorname{Spec}A)$ of step 2. The inverse image of each such chart in $b_P^{-1}(V)$ is etale over that chart by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-scheme-base-change] and step 2. An etale map is smooth by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-smooth-zero-dimensional-fibres], and its composite with the smooth model projection is smooth by [lem-trf-smooth-composition]. These inverse images cover $b_P^{-1}(V)$, so [lem-weil-pbc-smooth-local-on-source] proves that $b_P^{-1}(V)$ is smooth over $k$. When $b=0$ this is the empty scheme; the same source-locality statement allows an empty covering and gives smoothness as well.

Cited statements: sbc-lem-affine-space-smooth, lem-weil-pbc-etale-scheme-base-change, lem-weil-pbc-etale-smooth-zero-dimensional-fibres, lem-trf-smooth-composition, lem-weil-pbc-smooth-local-on-source, lem-weil-pbc-smooth-restrict-open-subschemes.

### Original step 5 → assembly step 2

Over $P\setminus C$, [thm-nagata-global-blowup-complement] identifies the blowup with that open subscheme of $P$. It is smooth over $k$ by the restriction and isomorphism assertions of [lem-weil-pbc-smooth-restrict-open-subschemes]. The opens $b_P^{-1}(V)$ from step 1 together with $b_P^{-1}(P\setminus C)$ cover $P^+$, since the corresponding opens cover $P$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-smooth-local-on-source] and step 4 to conclude that $P^+$ is smooth. In particular if $C=\varnothing$, the complement assertion identifies the entire blowup with $P$; no point of the center is needed.

Cited statements: thm-nagata-global-blowup-complement, lem-weil-pbc-smooth-restrict-open-subschemes, lem-weil-pbc-smooth-local-on-source.

### Original step 6 → assembly step 3

The ideal $\mathcal I$ is quasi-coherent and of finite type by step 1, so [lemma-nagata-blowup-proper] makes $b_P:P^+\to P$ proper. By [def-weil-proper-morphism] it is separated and of finite type. Composing its finite-type structure with the given finite-type morphism $P\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ gives a finite-type morphism $P^+\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ by [lem-sfb-finite-type-composition-base-change](1). This uses no properness of $P$ over $k$.

Cited statements: lemma-nagata-blowup-proper, def-weil-proper-morphism, lem-sfb-finite-type-composition-base-change.

### Original step 7 → assembly step 4

Separatedness follows directly by factoring the diagonal over $k$ as
$$P^+\longrightarrow P^+\times_P P^+\longrightarrow P^+\times_kP^+.$$
The first arrow is a closed immersion because $b_P$ is separated, by [def-weil-proper-morphism]. The second is the base change of the closed diagonal $P\to P\times_kP$, hence a closed immersion by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change]. Their composite is a closed immersion: in the affine quotient description of [def-weil-closed-immersion], the two surjections of rings compose to a surjection, and the two closed embeddings of spaces compose to a closed embedding. Thus the displayed composite diagonal is closed, proving that $P^+\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is separated.

Cited statements: def-weil-proper-morphism, lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change, def-weil-closed-immersion.

### Original step 8 → assembly step 5

Let $X\hookrightarrow P$ be reduced and put $U=P\setminus C$. The open $X_U=X\times_PU$ is reduced: its local rings are the corresponding local rings of $X$, so [def-pc-reduced-scheme] applies. By [thm-nagata-global-blowup-complement] the open subscheme
$$T=(X\times_PU)\times_U(P^+\times_PU)\subseteq X\times_PP^+$$
is isomorphic to $X_U$, and hence reduced. Its scheme-theoretic closure is the strict transform by [def-nagata-strict-transform]; the finite-presentation center condition was checked in step 1. Apply [lem-trf-reduced-scheme-image] to the inclusion of this reduced open $T$ into $X\times_PP^+$. It says that its scheme-theoretic closure is the reduced induced closed subscheme on its topological closure. Therefore the strict transform is reduced. The projection $X\times_PP^+\to P^+$ is a closed immersion by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change], so this is also the strict transform viewed inside $P^+$: its closure in the fiber product is closed in $P^+$, and any closed subscheme of $P^+$ containing $T$ pulls back to one containing $T$ in the fiber product; the minimal-closure properties therefore agree. The argument allows $T=\varnothing$ (for example $X\subseteq C$), and nowhere assumes $C\subseteq X$ or a stratum condition.

Cited statements: def-pc-reduced-scheme, thm-nagata-global-blowup-complement, def-nagata-strict-transform, lem-trf-reduced-scheme-image, lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change.
