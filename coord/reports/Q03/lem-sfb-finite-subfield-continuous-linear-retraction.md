Continuity of a linear functional and a scalar t with 0<|t|<1 give a finite constant C>0 with |f(w)|≤C‖w‖: choose δ>0 such that ‖w‖<δ implies |f(w)|<1, and scale each nonzero w by a power of t that brings its norm into [|t|δ,δ). Order all linear extensions satisfying this same bound by restriction; a chain has a well-defined bounded union. For an extension on U and v outside U, its possible value c at v must satisfy |c-f(u)|≤C‖v-u‖ for every u in U. These closed scalar balls are pairwise nested: the distance between two centres is at most C‖u-u'‖≤max(C‖v-u‖,C‖v-u'‖). They have a common point: fix one ball, intersect all the others with it, and use its compactness and the finite intersection property. Define g(u+av)=f(u)+ac. If a≠0, the ball condition for -u/a gives |g(u+av)|≤C‖u+av‖; if a=0 the old bound applies. Thus a maximal bounded extension has domain V, by the maximality principle. It is continuous by the bound. This establishes that the new statement has the right hypotheses even when U is not closed and V is not complete.
