Original step 1 (source and route retained; its relevant subargument is now a statement-only dependency):

First check the ambient scheme of the chosen compactification. Properness makes $p$ finite type by [def-weil-proper-morphism], so $\overline X$ is Noetherian by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian]. Choose a finite Noetherian affine cover $Y=\bigcup_i\operatorname{Spec}R_i$. By [def-weil-finite-type-morphism], each inverse image has a finite affine cover $\operatorname{Spec}B_{ij}$ with a surjection $R_i[t_1,\ldots,t_{r_{ij}}]\twoheadrightarrow B_{ij}$. The affine-cover dimension formula [lem-pc-dimension-affine-open-cover] and [lem-pc-scheme-dimension-local-rings] give $\dim R_i\leq D$ for some finite $D\geq0$. By [lem-sf-noetherian-polynomial-dimension], $\dim R_i[t_1,\ldots,t_{r_{ij}}]\leq D+r_{ij}$. Under a surjection of rings a strict chain of primes in the quotient pulls back to a strict chain of primes, so $\dim B_{ij}\leq D+r_{ij}$. Applying the same affine-cover formulas to $\overline X$ proves that it has finite dimension. These arguments apply to nonempty schemes; an empty scheme already has dimension $-\infty$.
There is also a uniform bound $e=\max(\{0\}\cup\{r_{ij}\})$ on all geometric fibres of $p$. Indeed, for a geometric point $\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to Y$, choose a chart containing its image. By [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra], the pullbacks of its source charts are the spectra of $B_{ij}\otimes_{R_i}\Omega$. They cover that fibre and are quotients of $\Omega[t_1,\ldots,t_{r_{ij}}]$: the images of the chosen generators still generate, since every pure tensor is a polynomial in them with coefficients in $\Omega$. A field has only the primes $(0)$ and only the ideals $(0),(1)$, so its dimension is zero and it is Noetherian. The polynomial-dimension theorem, the quotient-chain argument and the affine-cover dimension formulas give dimension at most $e$ for the geometric fibre. Empty charts or fibres contribute $-\infty$ and cause no exception; if $Y$ is empty all three schemes are empty and take $e=0$. Finally $\ell$ is invertible on $\overline X$: at any point the local ring map from $Y$ carries an inverse of $\ell$ to an inverse.
