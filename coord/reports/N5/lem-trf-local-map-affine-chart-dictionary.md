Step 3 uses G-ring invariance for R_r=O_{S,s} and finite-type ascent only after exhibiting a finite generating family for C over R_r. The disjointness of the denominators and p follows from Step 2. Step 4 uses localization Noetherianity with the complement of P, the G-ring definition at every contracted prime Q_0, completion ring-equivalence with the corresponding maximal ideals, regular-map invariance with its commuting-square hypothesis, and finally G-ring invariance. I read each of these five cited result/definition statements as well as the localization definition. The equality of the square in regular-map invariance follows from the canonical completion-map compatibility asserted in the completion ring-equivalence node. Thus no claim about regularity is inferred from an arbitrary unnormalized ring isomorphism. The remaining argument about twice-localized fractions is elementary: elements outside P are already inverted, and subsequently inverting the complement of Q in C_P inverts exactly the complement of Q_0 in C. This proves localization permanence here instead of creating another node for a short deduction from the cited results. No excellence hypothesis enters.

Step 5 uses the affine etale characterization expressly included in def-weil-etale-morphism; the separately cited etale-composition-cancellation statement gives both open-immersion etaleness and composition. The spectrum chart inclusions are supplied by lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-spec-points.

Step 6 uses only spectrum functoriality and the definitions of a local stalk map and prime localization. The quotient squares follow from the already proved stalk-map square; applying them to every coordinate proves equality of the maps on inverse limits.
