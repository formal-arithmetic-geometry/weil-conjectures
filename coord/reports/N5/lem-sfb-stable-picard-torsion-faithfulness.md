For step 1 I opened the statement of lem-sfb-stable-relative-picard-kummer, not merely its proof. It supplies the smooth separated group algebraic space, the degree-zero-on-each-component interpretation, the semiabelian fibres and functoriality for isomorphisms. The base Spec(k) is locally Noetherian, so its family hypotheses hold. Over this single algebraically closed field the fibre is the entire Picard space. Its torus is split because a separable closure of k is k itself. I use none of the node's smooth-family Kummer comparison: no smoothness of C is assumed.

For step 2 I opened lem-sfb-stable-isom-finite-unramified. Its explicit finite unramified Isom scheme applies to C and C over any k-scheme, hence to the field base. A finite scheme over an algebraically closed field has finitely many k-points; repetition and cancellation of powers give an actual positive integer r with a^r=id. Pullback functoriality from step 1 gives u^r=id. Neither finiteness of a group nor this elementary powers argument requires r prime to the characteristic.

For step 3 I checked the new semiabelian Hom theorem applies to the very algebraic space given in step 1, rather than silently using an abelian-variety theorem on a nonproper space. Its statement explicitly supplies a finite integral basis for End and identifies its ring identity with id. This is a substantial independent input: the standard proof sends a homomorphism to its torus and abelian-quotient maps, embeds Hom into the product of their finite free Hom groups, and uses Hom(A,T)=0 for injectivity. Torus-to-abelian vanishing ensures this map is defined. The two vanishings follow from the affine-image and proper-function arguments in the sources read above. A subgroup of a finite free abelian group is finite free. These facts will be expanded when that new node is split; they are not smuggled into the item's step.

For step 4 I checked that the new torsion-divisibility node has exactly the hypotheses in force, including algebraic closure and invertibility of n. The underlying justification is that [n] on a semiabelian object is finite etale surjective for invertible n, obtained from its torus and abelian quotient. The kernel is therefore reduced, so vanishing on all its k-points is vanishing on the kernel scheme. Quotient factorization gives a homomorphism, not just a function on points, and uniqueness follows from surjectivity. This is not the false assertion that every homomorphism of groups of k-points is algebraic.

For step 5 I opened lem-sfb-finite-free-ring-congruence-torsion and matched all four hypotheses: finite integral basis, u^r=1, u=1+nb, and n at least three. Its ring can be noncommutative, so no commutativity of End is being assumed. The source's root-of-unity argument takes place over characteristic zero in an integral lattice even though the original curve can have positive characteristic.

For step 6 I read Deligne–Mumford Theorem 1.13 and its proof through Lemmas 1.14–1.17. Its Pic^0 is exactly the ordinary group in the new node; it includes reducible stable curves and has no tameness hypothesis. The new theorem is applied only after step 5 establishes identity on all Pic^0 classes. Thus no finite torsion claim was substituted for the theorem's stronger hypothesis. Each step's uses has a verbatim excerpt in citations.json and proof_deps is exactly their union.

For small cases, the Hom theorem gives integer matrices for two split tori and reduces to Milne 10.15 for two abelian varieties; zero torus rank, zero abelian dimension and the trivial group give valid rank-zero cases. For divisibility on G_m, a homomorphism is an integral power and killing mu_n for invertible n says exactly that this power is divisible by n; n=1 is the identity factorization. Excluding additive groups matters: End(G_a) would not be finite free in positive characteristic. For the item n=2 would fail, as a smooth hyperelliptic involution acts by -1 on Pic^0 and hence trivially on 2-torsion; the n>=3 restriction is necessary. Elliptic tails and reducible compact-type curves remain in scope, with no replacement of their generalized Picard object by the Jacobian of a smooth curve.
