### Original step 1

All coordinates below denote biproduct components, so the calculations are identities of morphisms of module sheaves. Termwise products exist by [det-pe-products-derived]. The product universal property [def-weil-category-product] defines $T_N$ and gives $dT_N=T_Nd$, since each $t_n$ is a chain map. Hence $s_N=1-T_N$ is a chain map. The cone and shift definitions [def-weil-abstract-derived-category] give the stated reordered differential; its square is $(0,(ds_N-s_Nd)v)=0$. For a strict map $f:N\to N'$, put $F=P(f)$. The compatibility in [def-weil-inverse-system], checked after each projection, gives $Fs_N=s_{N'}F$. Consequently $\operatorname{diag}(F,F)$ commutes with the displayed differential. Products preserve identities, composition and addition of maps because these equations hold after every projection. The diagonal formula therefore defines the asserted additive functor $H$.

### Original step 2

Suppose $f_n$ is a quasi-isomorphism for every $n$. The last clause of [det-pe-products-derived] makes $F=\prod_nf_n$ a quasi-isomorphism. Apply [lem-pe-roos-coordinates-quasi-iso] with $V=P(N)$, $V'=P(N')$, $L=H(N)$, $L'=H(N')$, and its endomorphisms chosen to be $-s_N$ and $-s_{N'}$. Its required differential $(dx,sx-dy)$ is exactly $(dv,-du-s_Nv)$, and its required diagonal map is exactly $H(f)$ by step 1. Thus $H(f)$ is a quasi-isomorphism. The auxiliary lemma allows arbitrary chain endomorphisms; it imposes no surjectivity, boundedness or convergence condition on this tower.

### Original step 3

The first clause of [det-pe-products-derived] identifies $QP(N)$, with its actual localized projections, as the product of $(QN_n)_n$. Thus $Q(s_N)$ is the one-minus-transition map of [def-weil-derived-limit]. By [def-weil-abstract-derived-category], the cone triangle $QP(N)\xrightarrow{Q(s_N)}QP(N)\to Q\operatorname{Cone}(s_N)\xrightarrow{-Qp}QP(N)[1]$ is distinguished. Inverse rotation using (TR2) of [def-weil-triangulated-category] gives $QH(N)\to QP(N)\xrightarrow{Q(s_N)}QP(N)\to QH(N)[1]$. Its first map is the projection $(v,u)\mapsto v$: inverse rotation negates the shifted boundary $-p$. Its last map is induced by $w\mapsto(0,w)$ into $H(N)[1]$. This is the required Roos triangle, hence $QH(N)$ is a derived limit by the cited definition.

### Original step 4

Fix $r\in\mathbf Z$ and write $\epsilon=(-1)^r$. Under the termwise product identification $P(N[r])=P(N)[r]$, the differential of $H(N[r])$ is $(\epsilon dv,-\epsilon du-s_Nv)$ and that of $H(N)[r]$ is $(\epsilon dv,-\epsilon du-\epsilon s_Nv)$, using [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]. The map $\sigma_{N,r}(v,u)=(v,\epsilon u)$ has inverse with the same formula. Both $\sigma d$ and $d\sigma$ equal $(\epsilon dv,-du-\epsilon s_Nv)$, so it is a chain isomorphism. It commutes with diagonal product maps, proving naturality. For $r=0$ it is the identity, and applying the comparisons for $r$ and $s$ successively multiplies the second coordinate by $(-1)^r(-1)^s=(-1)^{r+s}$. This proves coherence under the usual reindexing of iterated shifts. The product identifications here are the unique projection-preserving ones of [def-weil-category-product].

### Original step 5

For a strict tower map $f:N\to N'$, write $A=P(N)$, $B=P(N')$, $F=P(f)$, $S_A=s_N$ and $S_B=s_{N'}$. Products of the two summands in $\operatorname{Cone}(f_n)^q$ identify $P(\operatorname{Cone}(f))^q=B^q\oplus A^{q+1}$: the map takes the two component projections, and its inverse pairs them at each $n$; their composites are identities after each product projection [def-weil-category-product]. The cone differential is $(b,a)\mapsto(db+Fa,-da)$ and its transition endomorphism is $\operatorname{diag}(S_B,S_A)$. Consequently the differential on $H(\operatorname{Cone}(f))$ is
$$D(b,a,b',a')=(db+Fa,-da,-db'-Fa'-S_Bb,da'-S_Aa).$$
The cone formula [def-weil-abstract-derived-category] gives
$$D'(b,b',a,c)=(db+Fa,-db'-S_Bb+Fc,-da,dc+S_Aa)$$
on $\operatorname{Cone}(H(f))$. Hence $\Phi(b,a,b',a')=(b,b',a,-a')$ satisfies $\Phi D=D'\Phi$, both sides being $(db+Fa,-db'-Fa'-S_Bb,-da,-da'+S_Aa)$. Its inverse is $(b,b',a,c)\mapsto(b,a,b',-c)$. This proves the asserted chain isomorphism without invoking an unstated commutation-of-cones lemma.

### Original step 6

A strict commutative square between $f$ and $g$, with maps $\alpha$ on the sources and $\beta$ on the targets, acts on the four source coordinates as $(P\beta,P\alpha,P\beta,P\alpha)$. On the reordered target coordinates it acts as $(P\beta,P\beta,P\alpha,P\alpha)$. Permutation and multiplication by $-1$ commute with these maps; thus $\Phi$ is natural. The cone inclusion sends $(b,b')$ to $(b,0,b',0)$ before $\Phi$, and to $(b,b',0,0)$ afterwards, exactly the cone inclusion for $H(f)$. For the cone boundary convention $-p$ in [def-weil-abstract-derived-category], $H(-p)$ sends $(b,a,b',a')$ to $(-a,-a')$ in $H(N[1])$. The shift comparison of step 4 sends this to $(-a,a')$ in $H(N)[1]$, exactly $-p\Phi$. This proves preservation of the boundary after the shift comparison, including its sign.

### Original step 7

Given compatible $j_n:M\to N_n$, define $J:M\to P(N)$ by the product universal property [def-weil-category-product]. Since $t_nj_{n+1}=j_n$, every projection of $s_NJ$ is zero, so $s_NJ=0$. Hence $d(Jm,0)=(Jdm,0)$, proving that $\eta_j:m\mapsto(Jm,0)$ is a chain map by [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]. If $g:M\to M'$ and a strict $f:N\to N'$ satisfy $f_nj_n=j'_ng$, then $P(f)J=J'g$ after every projection. The diagonal formula for $H(f)$ now gives $H(f)\eta_j=\eta_{j'}g$. This is the asserted naturality of the augmentation.

### Original step 8

For shifted augmentations, $\sigma_{N,r}\eta_{j[r]}$ and $\eta_j[r]$ both send $m$ to $(Jm,0)$ after reindexing, because $(-1)^r0=0$. For cone augmentations, let $g:M\to M'$ and $f:N\to N'$ satisfy $f_nj_n=j'_ng$. The induced levelwise cone map sends $(m',m)$ to $(j'_nm',j_nm)$ by the cone formula [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]. Its zero-second-coordinate augmentation to $H(\operatorname{Cone}(f))$ is $((J'm',Jm),(0,0))$. Step 5 sends this to $((J'm',0),(Jm,0))$, exactly the cone map induced by $\eta_{j'}$ and $\eta_j$. Together with the inclusion and boundary identities of step 6, this proves the final augmentation compatibility for cones and shifts. All calculations are identities of the specified maps, rather than choices of morphisms completing triangulated diagrams.
