For the presentation node I checked the construction independently. Write A=(k[X]/H)_p (a local localization is localization at the preimage of its maximal ideal), put R=k[X]_{p'}, and map it onto A. Choose finite k-algebra generators of a finite-type model of B and lift them as the new Y variables in R[Y]. Localize R[Y] at the inverse image of m_B to obtain S. The resulting map S→B is surjective: every element of B is a fraction in the chosen k-generators with denominator mapping outside m_B. Its restriction to R is the prescribed composite through A, since the original map is local. Polynomial flatness and localization make R→S flat; R and S are localizations of finite polynomial rings over k. F is the localization of (R/m_R)[Y] at the induced prime, hence regular local, and is a quotient of S, hence essentially finite type over k. This also checks compatibility when either variable list is empty. No completed presentation or separable coefficient-field section is used.

For the fibre-ideal node I checked both directions beyond the source citation. A regular sequence generating K is quasi-regular; 0CEP preserves that under the closed-fibre base change because T/K is A-flat. In the Noetherian local closed fibre the image is proper and quasi-regular equals regular. Conversely flatness of T/K identifies K/mK with its image in T/mT. Lift a regular generating sequence of that image to K; Nakayama on the finite T-module K modulo these lifts makes them generate K (mT is in the maximal ideal). Then 00MG makes the lifts a regular sequence. This is a genuine algebra lemma with a flat quotient assumption; flatness of T alone would be insufficient. It is left statement-only to its next worker.

Edge cases: A=B=k (empty sequences), identity on k[ε]/(ε²), k→k[x,y]_(x,y)/(xy), and a purely inseparable finite field extension all satisfy the assertion and the auxiliary hypotheses. For the nested ideal input, I=J=0 and I=0,J=(x²) in a regular local power-series ring test the empty and hypersurface cases. J=m_R S with I=0 over R=k[t]_(t) would give S/J killed by t and is excluded by the flat-quotient hypothesis, as required. For the fibre input K=0 and T=A[x]_(m,x), K=(x²) work, whereas T=A and K=m for a nonfield A is deliberately excluded because T/K is not A-flat. Zero rings are excluded by nonzero local rings; C cannot become zero because the map is local.
