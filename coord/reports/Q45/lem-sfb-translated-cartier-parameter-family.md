For the boundary prerequisite I read divisors.tex Tag 0BCW, including its locally factorial affine-complement criterion, varieties.tex Tag 056S on smooth regularity and more-algebra.tex Tag 0AG0 on regular local UFDs. Finite type over a field supplies Noetherianity, integral supplies a nonempty reduced irreducible space, and smoothness supplies regular stalks; these meet the criterion for the prescribed nonempty affine open. The conclusion is existence of a divisor, not an asserted global defining function. The empty divisor is permitted.

For the chart prerequisite I read morphisms.tex Tag 054L (local etale coordinates), Tag 02NW (finiteness after shrinking near finite generic fibres), Tag 04MH (finite flat locally presented pushforward and rank), and Tag 02GL (etale fibres over fields), alongside the actual chart shrinking in 0BF7. An etale chart of a nonempty open of an integral smooth X has open nonempty image in affine space; it is dominant and quasi-finite. Shrinking the affine-space base makes its full inverse image finite, retaining etaleness, affineness, nonemptiness and density in X. The connected integral base makes its positive locally constant rank a single integer n. Over algebraically closed k its degree-n etale fibres split into n distinct k-points. This is why a finite flat chart alone would be insufficient.

For the reused function-norm prerequisite I read divisors.tex Tag 0BD2 and the function-norm discussion of section Tag 0BCX, including the determinant construction and the argument that a zero at y forces a zero of its norm at p(y). I also read morphisms.tex Tag 02KD for finite-locally-free base change. Determinants glue on free charts; their restriction, multiplicativity, scalar-power and base-change identities apply to arbitrary finite locally free morphisms, without a domain or characteristic assumption. Positive rank gives nonempty residue-field fibres and hence surjectivity. For the nowhere-dense prerequisite I read the full proof of morphisms.tex Tag 03HX. It really states finite maps preserve nowhere density with no integrality or flatness hypothesis; closedness follows from finiteness. I did not substitute a general closed-map assertion, which would not suffice.

The chart explicitly supplies that V is a nonempty finite-type k-scheme; the Jacobson statement says closed points of V are dense, and the residue-field statement uses the actual algebraically closed base k to make such a point k-rational. Hence the entire fibre lies in the good open and Step 3 proves the requested covering clause. All uses have verbatim quotations in citations.json.
