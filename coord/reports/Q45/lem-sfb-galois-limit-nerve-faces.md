### STATEMENT-DISPUTED: base-vertex notation and coefficient action

The original sentence says that `xg` means precomposing a base geometric map by `Spec g`. That is ambiguous until the domain/codomain of `x` is fixed, and with a base scheme point `t:Spec B -> Spec Omega` it has the wrong type: `Spec g` acts on its codomain, so it must be postcomposed. The tensor formula forces the unambiguous ring-point convention `x:Omega -> B`, `xg=x o g`, hence `tg=Spec(g) o t`. This also fixes multiplication as `(gh)(a)=g(h(a))`. To see why order matters, take a separable closure of Q with an S3 Galois subextension and automorphisms restricting to distinct transpositions tau=(12), g=(23). For the base ring map x=tau the tensor formula gives tau o g at the next vertex; precomposing the corresponding scheme map by Spec(g) instead gives g o tau, and these differ on that subextension.

The first face is semilinear on the structure ring: `d0*(f)(g1,...)=g1(f(g2,...))`. In degree one its restriction to Omega is `a -> (g -> g(a))`; the last face is `a -> (g -> a)`. For k=R, Omega=C and g conjugation, these differ at a=i. The original final cocycle sentence therefore cannot be an unconditional untwisted assertion for arbitrary structure-ring coefficients. For example, change a basis by b=1+i and set c(g)=b^{-1}g(b)=-i. This is the usual semilinear rank-one descent cocycle: c(g)g(c(g))=1, whereas c(g)c(g)=-1 != c(1). The corrected statement retains the general pulled-back descent equation epsilon02=epsilon01 o epsilon12 and spells out that c(gh)=c(g)c(h) is the constant-coefficient-fibre convention. This is the convention the parent uses: its matrices have values in the external coefficient field A, on which changing the base geometric vertex acts trivially. No target or unit statement is changed, and no other node is edited.

The original statement is retained verbatim in the sandbox's `original-statement.txt` for review. The statement's tensor and scheme identifications, finite-map factorization, ordered interior faces and unit degeneracies are all retained.
