## Retained child proof sketch (report only)

This argument is preserved for the next stage-1 worker; the delivered child has no proof fields populated.

1. Every $\kappa_X$-module sheaf is flat by [lem-exc-constant-field-module-flat], applied to the small pro-étale site with its usual sheafifications. This is sheaf flatness; the section rings of $\kappa_X$ need not be fields.

2. Assume the cohomology bounds. For a representative $C^\bullet$ of $B$, put $P=\tau_{\ge a}\tau_{\le b}C$. By [def-kun-truncations] the canonical zigzag is a quasi-isomorphism, and $P$ has terms only in $[a,b]$, including when $a=b$. Its terms, including the boundary kernel and cokernel, are flat by step 1. By [lem-pc-bounded-above-flat-kflat-site], $P$ is K-flat. The definition [def-weil-derived-tensor] computes $B\otimes^L N$ by $P\otimes N[0]$, whose degree-$i$ term is $P^i\otimes N$ and is zero outside $[a,b]$. This gives the Tor bound.

3. Conversely test the Tor condition against $N=\kappa_X$. On a K-flat representative $Q$ of $B$, $Q\otimes\kappa_X[0]$ is isomorphic to $Q$ degree by degree by $x\otimes c\mapsto cx$, inverse $x\mapsto x\otimes1$. These maps commute with the differentials. Hence $B\otimes^L\kappa_X\simeq B$ and the cohomology bounds follow. Taking the existence of some interval gives the final equivalence.
