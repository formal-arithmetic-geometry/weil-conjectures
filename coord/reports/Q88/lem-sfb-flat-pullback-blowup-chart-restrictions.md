I read the node's entire statement against 0804/0805 and 02GL. Flatness, a quasi-coherent ideal and the actual extended ideal are the hypotheses needed for the graded Rees equivalence. The finite-type condition is retained, although the cited base-change interfaces are more general. The last clause uses both etaleness and that the original centre is the reduced closed point. In particular it does not infer a reduced singleton fibre from flatness alone, or identify kappa(x) and kappa(y).

Step 1 cites def-nagata-flat-pullback-rees-map for the degreewise canonical map and lemma-nagata-flat-pullback-rees for its graded equivalence. It cites def-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change-map for the exact chart-preserving construction named in the statement and thm-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change for the isomorphism over Y. Finally lem-nagata-affine-proj-scalar-extension explicitly states both the inverse image of D_+(f) and its homogeneous-fraction map. Applied with f=at of degree one, it really gives the chart assertion, not just an abstract isomorphism. These five statements were opened, and their exact wording is quoted in citations.json. Flatness is used in the Rees equivalence; the scalar-extension theorem allows any scalar map.

Step 2 cites lem-nagata-homogeneous-localization-scalar-extension for the actual degree-zero localization tensor isomorphism. Its statement explicitly says no flatness or regularity of the denominator is needed. The explicit theta formula comes from def-nagata-homogeneous-away-to-chart; the homomorphism property comes from lemma-nagata-homogeneous-away-to-chart, and its bijectivity from the separately opened injective and surjective nodes. Thus it does not extract a stronger fraction formula from the mere chart-cover theorem or from an uncited proof. Composing these maps with the step-1 graded equivalence gives the formula for every degree n, including n=0 for coefficient/structure compatibility and n=1 for the requested generators.

Step 3 uses exactly the statements of lemma-nagata-extended-ideal-affine-chart and lemma-nagata-chart-extended-ideal-principal: the first says which ideal is generated, the second that it is (a). Thus equality of ideals follows from the closed subscheme, not just equality of supports. The final use is a restriction of the already established comparison.
