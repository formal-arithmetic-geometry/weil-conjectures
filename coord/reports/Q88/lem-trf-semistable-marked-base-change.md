For each step I opened the full statement of every cited node. The checks were as follows.

1. The flat-base-change node is for arbitrary scheme base change. Clause (3) of the qcqs-permanence node explicitly includes finite presentation, rather than just local finite presentation. The closed-immersion node is applied to D→X along X'→X, with the iterated fibre product identified by its universal property. Both structural maps are checked separately.
2. The dimension node is the pointwise fibre-dimension equality of Tag 02FY, not just equality of the total dimensions of affine schemes. The other two dimension statements convert pure component dimension to constant local dimension and back. Their locally finite type and locally Noetherian assumptions hold on finite-type field fibres. The finite-type-over-Noetherian node supplies the Noetherian hypothesis; no claim that the original base is Noetherian occurs.
3. The generic-point assumption gives exactly the residue-field map needed to express the new generic fibre as a field extension of the old one. Only the smooth clause of the proper/smooth base-change node is used; properness is not assumed. The etale-base-change statement supplies the marking clause.
4. The nodal-family-fitting statement gives openness and the equality of the nonsmooth sets after arbitrary base change. Its hypotheses match the original semistable curve. This step does not read nodality of the new curve out of a statement that only compares singular subschemes; nodality is proved in Steps 5–8 instead.
5. Completed-local-ring comparison is a direct combination of the existing flat-local-completion isomorphism and the etale-prime-generates-maximal assertion. I checked the equal-residue-field hypothesis at closed rational points over the algebraically closed field, and the Noetherian hypothesis on affine field charts. Etaleness supplies flatness through its definition. This is why no general etale-completion node was created.
6. The local-model node supplies all three etale arrows and a point over the original nonsmooth point. The etale-base-change theorem applies to each arrow. The field-fibre decomposition node proves existence of a rational lift over the algebraically closed field after tensoring the original nonempty separable fibre. No rational lift over the original arbitrary residue field is assumed.
7. Smooth descent is applied to an open smooth neighbourhood mapping onto its open image: the image is open by flat-lfp openness, and the restricted map is flat, surjective and lfp. The cited descent node does not require quasi-compactness. The separate etale-smooth and smooth-relative-dimension nodes make a lift over the smooth part of uv=a smooth. The explicit invertible-coordinate computation forces a nonsmooth point to be the origin with a=0.
8. The inverse-limit monomial computation identifies the completion of the polynomial local ring directly. The completion-quotient node then applies to its quotient by (uv). The two completed-local-ring comparisons are those verified in Step 5. This proves the actual completed geometric local-ring condition, not merely an abstract singular-locus condition. Etaleness of the new marking and its containment in the pulled-back smooth locus give the remaining clauses.
9. The algebraic-over-algebraically-closed-subfield lemma supplies the exact hypothesis of the geometric-irreducibility-of-a-field-extension node. The component-bijection node is applied both to C and to each component individually; this proves that the entire pullback of each component is irreducible, which its bare bijection formulation alone would not say. The two faithfully-flat nodes supply surjectivity. Geometric reducedness over the perfect field supplies the equality of reduced component schemes.
10. I checked the finite-presentation hypotheses for the component and the projective line before using morphism descent. The argument descends both inverse maps and then both inverse equations; it cites the equality-stage construction and the eventual-equality theorem, as well as morphism descent. The stage is a nonzero finitely generated subalgebra of the extension field, so it has a maximal ideal whose residue field is k by the cited closed-point statement. Specialization proves reflection of being P1. This three-operation application of existing descent statements is retained as an elementary step, not a new node duplicating their consequences.
11. The marking is unramified by the etale definition; the singular subscheme is unramified by the fitting statement. The field-fibre decomposition gives rational points, and quasi-compactness of their closed subschemes in a finite-type Noetherian curve makes the sets finite. Each point has exactly one scalar extension. The point/component incidence is a fibre product, so its nonemptiness is unchanged. I count distinct points on a component, as the item requires, not branches on its normalization.
12. A rational component contracted before extension maps constantly to a rational point. Using the affine-spectrum dictionary on the two standard projective-line charts, compatible ring maps land in k[t] intersect k[t^-1]=k, so they glue to a constant morphism. No uncited general Hom-to-affine assertion is needed. In the reverse direction surjectivity of the source projection forces a singleton image downstairs. Together with Steps 9–11 this preserves the exact forbidden-component predicate. No descent of a modification or of a chosen isomorphism is claimed.
13. I checked the algebraic-closure embedding node: the extension being embedded is algebraic, while the containing field is algebraically closed. The geometric fibre over a new point is the scalar extension of the old chosen algebraic-closure fibre along such an embedding. This addresses the difference between the definition's chosen algebraic closure and a larger geometric point.
14. The locality proof separates pointwise flat/lfp conditions, quasi-compactness and its application to the diagonal, and the closed-immersion/etale locality theorem. The latter is applied to the cover of the curve for the marking immersion. Every fibre and fibre morphism is literally unchanged on a base open containing its point. This gives the geometric clauses and stability, with no appeal to properness.
