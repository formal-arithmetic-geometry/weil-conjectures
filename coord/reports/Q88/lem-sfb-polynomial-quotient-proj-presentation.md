### Input encoding and clause-by-clause correspondence

The ideal and its quotient are represented by their equivalent affine-basis presentation.
`B : RelativeProjAlgebra U` is the quasi-coherent graded quotient sheaf and
`q : (RelativeProjectiveSpace.algebra U n).affineData ⟶ B.affineData` is its quotient map.
`hq` is surjectivity on every affine open. An affine-section map in this category is a
coefficient-preserving graded ring map, natural for restriction; it is not an arbitrary
map to an unrelated algebra. The quasi-coherent kernel is precisely the given ideal J,
and the first isomorphism theorem identifies its quotient with B. Conversely a
quasi-coherent homogeneous J and S/J give this same presentation. This changes the
coordinates used for the input data, not the hypotheses or the mathematical objects.
`RelativeProjectiveSpace.algebra` is the recorded symmetric/free polynomial sheaf, with
its canonical `affinePolynomialEquiv`, rather than a second definition of that sheaf.
The quasi-coherence built into B is exactly that required by the recorded relative-Proj
input: a quasi-coherent homogeneous ideal has a quasi-coherent quotient, and a kernel
of a map of these quasi-coherent sheaves is quasi-coherent.
