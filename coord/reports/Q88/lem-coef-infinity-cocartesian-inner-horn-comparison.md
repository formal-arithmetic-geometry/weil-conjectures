## Prism comparisons (old Step 4)

For the base case $N=2,i=1$, apply the cocartesian-arrow projection to $p$. The fixed base edge $0\to1$ defines a simplicial map $\mathcal C_0\to Z$: every vertex belongs to $Z$ by the lift hypothesis and $Z$ is full. Its pullback is a trivial Kan fibration; choose vertices and then lift successive boundaries to obtain a section, hence $h:\Delta^1\times\mathcal C_0\to\mathcal C$ with source the inclusion and every vertical edge cocartesian. Put $D=\mathcal C_{12}$, $A=(\Delta^1\times\mathcal C_0)\amalg_{\{1\}\times\mathcal C_0}\mathcal C_1$, and $B=(\Delta^1\times\mathcal C_0)\amalg_{\{1\}\times\mathcal C_0}D$. To obtain the two comparisons without a new lemma, first regard $\mathcal C_{01}$ over $\Delta^1$ and then regard $\mathcal C$ over $\Delta^1$ by the collapse $0\mapsto0,1,2\mapsto1$. Both maps are cocartesian fibrations. For the latter, an outer-horn problem with first edge $h(x):x\to F(x)$ projects to an ordered simplex in $\Delta^2$: in dimension two its last index is at least $1$, and in higher dimensions the adjacent edges are already specified. Thus its original $p$-cocartesian filler solves this problem. These give all nonidentity base-edge lifts, and identity edges supply the degenerate lifts: they are equivalences by degenerate triangle witnesses, so the equivalence outer-horn lemma fills their absolute outer horns; the filler projects to the required simplex since the simplex base is determined by its vertices. After taking opposites and reversing the order of the two base vertices, these are cartesian fibrations, with terminal fiber $\mathcal C_0^{\mathrm{op}}$. By the explicit mapping-simplex formula, $A^{\mathrm{op}}$ and $B^{\mathrm{op}}$ are the $n=1$ mapping simplices of the chains $\mathcal C_1^{\mathrm{op}}\leftarrow\mathcal C_0^{\mathrm{op}}$ and $D^{\mathrm{op}}\leftarrow\mathcal C_0^{\mathrm{op}}$ respectively, with transition $F=h|_{\{1\}}$ followed by inclusion. The maps induced by $h$ are identities on both vertex fibers and carry the distinguished marked edges to the cartesian opposites of $h(x)$. The universal mapping-simplex theorem, with $T=\Delta^1$, therefore gives categorical equivalences $A^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal C_{01}^{\mathrm{op}}$ and $B^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}$. Taking opposites preserves categorical equivalences directly from the testing definition: $\operatorname{Fun}(K^{\mathrm{op}},Q)\cong\operatorname{Fun}(K,Q^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{op}}$, and $Q$ ranges over all quasicategories. Hence $A\to\mathcal C_{01}$ and $B\to\mathcal C$ are categorical equivalences. The map $A\to B$ is a monomorphism, being the pushout of $\mathcal C_1\hookrightarrow D$. Pushing out $A\to\mathcal C_{01}$ along it gives $B\to\mathcal C_{01}\amalg_{\mathcal C_1}D=H_1$, a categorical equivalence by left properness. Its composite with $H_1\to\mathcal C$ is $B\to\mathcal C$, so two-out-of-three proves the base case.

## Collapse filtration (old Step 5)

For $i>1$, use strong induction on $N\geq3$, with $N=3,i=2$ proved in Steps 6–7. For $N>3$, let $c:\Delta^N\to\Delta^3$ send indices below $i-1$ to $0$, $i-1$ to $1$, $i$ to $2$, and indices above $i$ to $3$. Order the nondegenerate simplices on which $c$ is surjective in nonincreasing dimension, with $\sigma_1=\Delta^N$, and remove successively each $\sigma_j$ and its face $\sigma_j^0$ omitting $i$, including degeneracies, to form $K_j$, $0\leq j\leq m$. This corrects the source's inconsistent word 'nondecreasing'. Every proper coface of a removed $\sigma_k$ belongs to the same surjective family and has already been removed. A proper coface of its partner omitting $i$ is either a coface of $\sigma_k$ or the partner of a larger surjective simplex. Thus $K_j$ is a simplicial subset. Among the codimension-one faces of $\sigma_j$, the face omitting $i$ is removed. A different face which is surjective belongs to the family in smaller dimension and is not yet removed; a different face which is not surjective still contains $i$ and cannot be a removed partner. All proper faces of the omitted face lie in these retained faces. Therefore $K_j\cap\sigma_j=\Lambda_{p_j}^{n_j}$, where $p_j$ is the position of $i$ in $\sigma_j$, and $K_{j-1}=K_j\cup\sigma_j$. Degreewise inverse image makes this union/intersection square a pushout. Surjectivity forces a vertex below $i-1$ and one above $i$, so $1<p_j<n_j$ and the preceding vertex is exactly $i-1$. For $j>1$, $n_j<N$, and the original cocartesian lifts restrict to the indicated pair in this face. Induction and left properness therefore give $p^{-1}K_m\to p^{-1}K_1=H_i$ a categorical equivalence. A remaining simplex is precisely one whose $c$-image omits some vertex other than $2$; hence $K_m=c^{-1}\Lambda^3_2$. To check that an edge over $i-1\to i$ is $cp$-cocartesian, consider any prescribed outer-horn lifting problem with this first edge. In dimension two the last original vertex index is at least $i$, because its $c$-image is at least $2$ and $c^{-1}(2)=\{i\}$. In higher dimensions the horn already contains every adjacent edge, so its projected original vertex indices are ordered. They determine a unique simplex of $\Delta^N$, whose restriction is the original projected horn. The original $p$-cocartesian lifting property supplies a filler over it; composing its base with $c$ gives exactly the prescribed $\Delta^3$ simplex, since these have the same vertices. Also $cp$ is inner. Apply Steps 6–7 to $cp$, and two-out-of-three gives $H_i\to\mathcal C$.
