## Baseline seven-step proof retained verbatim

### Old step 1

Assume that $M$ is Mittag-Leffler in the tensor-product sense. Fix $i$ and put $N=\prod_{s\in I}M_s$. Applying [lem-trf-mittag-leffler-hom-systems] to the given finitely presented directed colimit with this single target module $N$ gives an index $j\ge i$ such that, for every $k\ge j$, the images of $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M_j,N)\to\operatorname{Hom}_R(M_i,N)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M_k,N)\to\operatorname{Hom}_R(M_i,N)$ agree. The target is therefore chosen before any component $M_j$ is used; this uses precisely the tensor-product Mittag-Leffler hypothesis [def-trf-mittag-leffler-module] required by that node.

Uses: lem-trf-mittag-leffler-hom-systems, def-trf-mittag-leffler-module

### Old step 2

For each $k\ge j$, identify maps into the product $N$ with their component maps. Projecting the equality of image sets from Step 1 along $N\to M_j$ gives equality of the images of $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M_j,M_j)\to\operatorname{Hom}_R(M_i,M_j)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M_k,M_j)\to\operatorname{Hom}_R(M_i,M_j)$. The identity of $M_j$ maps by precomposition with $f_{ij}$ to $f_{ij}$, so it is the precomposition image of some $h_k:M_k\to M_j$, and hence $h_k\circ f_{ik}=f_{ij}$. Thus this relation is available for every $k\ge j$, exactly as required for the subsequent stage-kernel argument.

Uses: lem-trf-mittag-leffler-hom-systems

### Old step 3

Let $L$ be any $R$-module and let $x\in\ker(f_i\otimes_R\mathrm{id}_L)$. By the elementwise description of the directed colimit after tensoring, [lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-modules-elementwise] applied to the colimit of the tensor system gives an index $k\ge i$ such that $(f_{ik}\otimes_R\mathrm{id}_L)(x)=0$. Choose a common upper bound $k'\ge k,j$; functoriality of the transition maps carries the vanishing to $(f_{ik'}\otimes_R\mathrm{id}_L)(x)=0$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-modules-elementwise, lem-weil-pbc-tensor-commutes-with-colimits, def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product

### Old step 4

Using the map $h_{k'}$ from Step 2 and functoriality of tensor products [def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product], we have $(f_{ij}\otimes\mathrm{id}_L)=(h_{k'}\otimes\mathrm{id}_L)\circ(f_{ik'}\otimes\mathrm{id}_L)$. Thus the element $x$ is killed by $f_{ij}\otimes\mathrm{id}_L$. Since $L$ and $x$ were arbitrary, $\ker(f_i\otimes\mathrm{id}_L)\subseteq\ker(f_{ij}\otimes\mathrm{id}_L)$ for every $L$, so $f_{ij}$ dominates $f_i$.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product

### Old step 5

Conversely, assume that for every $i$ there is $j\ge i$ with $f_{ij}$ dominating $f_i$. To prove the tensor-product Mittag-Leffler condition, fix a set $A$, a family of $R$-modules $(Q_\alpha)_{\alpha\in A}$, and an element $x$ in the kernel of the canonical comparison $\theta_{M,(Q_\alpha)}:M\otimes_R\prod_\alpha Q_\alpha\to\prod_\alpha(M\otimes_RQ_\alpha)$. By [lem-weil-pbc-tensor-commutes-with-colimits], the tensor system $M_i\otimes_R\prod_\alpha Q_\alpha$ has colimit $M\otimes_R\prod_\alpha Q_\alpha$; the elementwise colimit criterion [lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-modules-elementwise] therefore represents $x$ as $(f_i\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\prod Q_\alpha})(z)$ for some $i$ and $z\in M_i\otimes_R\prod_\alpha Q_\alpha$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-tensor-commutes-with-colimits, lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-modules-elementwise, def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product

### Old step 6

Choose $j\ge i$ so that $f_{ij}$ dominates $f_i$. For each $\alpha$, let $z_\alpha$ be the image of $z$ under the canonical projection $M_i\otimes_R\prod_\beta Q_\beta\to M_i\otimes_RQ_\alpha$. The $\alpha$-component of $\theta_{M,(Q_\beta)}(x)=0$ says $(f_i\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Q_\alpha})(z_\alpha)=0$. Domination then gives $(f_{ij}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Q_\alpha})(z_\alpha)=0$ for every $\alpha$.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product

### Old step 7

Because $M_i$ is finitely presented, [lem-trf-finite-presentation-tensor-products] identifies $z$ with the family $(z_\alpha)_\alpha$ under the canonical comparison. The coordinatewise vanishing in Step 6 consequently implies $(f_{ij}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\prod Q_\alpha})(z)=0$. Since $f_i=f_j\circ f_{ij}$, we obtain $x=(f_i\otimes\mathrm{id})(z)=0$. Thus every kernel of $\theta_{M,(Q_\alpha)}$ is zero, for every family, so $M$ is Mittag-Leffler by [def-trf-mittag-leffler-module].

Uses: lem-trf-finite-presentation-tensor-products, def-trf-mittag-leffler-module, def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product
