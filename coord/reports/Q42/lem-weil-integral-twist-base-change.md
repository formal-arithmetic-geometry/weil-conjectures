### Old step 1

Put $A=\widehat{\mathbf Z}_{\ell,S}$ and $L_H=\mathcal O_H(1)$. Clauses 2 and 3 of [lem-pur-twist-sheaf-locally-free] give $T$ locally free of rank one over $A$ and $R_H\cong A\otimes_{(\mathbf Z_\ell)_S}(\mathcal O_H)_S$. The defining formula of [def-coefficient-twists-detailed] therefore gives $i_H:T\otimes_A R_H\cong L_H$: after the latter substitution its map is $t\otimes(z\otimes a)\mapsto zt\otimes a$, with inverse $t\otimes a\mapsto t\otimes(1\otimes a)$. Both composites are identities by the balanced tensor relations. All maps are sheaf maps, since the sectionwise formulas commute with restriction and hence extend through the sheafification defining tensor [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product]. The inclusion of integer rings induces $R_E\to R_F$ in this presentation, by $z\otimes a\mapsto z\otimes a$ with $a$ mapped to $\mathcal O_F$; it is compatible with towers. This is also the canonical map of the completed coefficient sheaves [def-weil-completed-integral-coefficients]: composing with each finite quotient gives the inclusion followed by that quotient, which characterizes the map into the inverse limit.

Uses: lem-pur-twist-sheaf-locally-free, def-coefficient-twists-detailed, def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product, def-weil-completed-integral-coefficients.

### Old step 2

Through $i_E$ and $i_F$, define $c_1:L_E\otimes_{R_E}R_F\to L_F$ by $(t\otimes a)\otimes b\mapsto t\otimes\iota_{EF}(a)b$. Its inverse sends $t\otimes b$ to $(t\otimes1)\otimes b$.
