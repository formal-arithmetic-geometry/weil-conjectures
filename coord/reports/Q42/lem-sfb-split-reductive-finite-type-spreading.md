## Review by Q42

I checked this split against delivery-errors B--E with the item, the new statement, and all cited statements open. For step 1, the affine-limit node explicitly identifies the all-affine limit with the spectrum of the ring colimit. The proof verifies that the index is a set, directed and nonempty, and that the colimit really is K; it does not assume K is algebraic over k. Laurent coordinates keep all of M, including its central directions.

For step 2, I checked that the algebra descent node gives exactly descent of maps between fixed finitely presented algebras and equality of maps. I used its equality clause for the finite group and torus-homomorphism diagrams. Tensor powers meet the finite-presentation hypothesis by the tensor-product node. The spectrum definition states the ring-map/affine-scheme-map correspondence and functoriality; the algebra-tensor node states the pushout universal property, giving the affine products. The initial algebra is defined from finitely many presentation coefficients directly. No Hopf theorem stated only over a field is applied over the subalgebra. Step 3 proves surjectivity of the torus coordinate map with the finite Laurent generators and actual preimages; it does not simply assert that a closed immersion spreads, nor confuse a fibrewise inclusion with a closed subgroup over the base.

In step 4, the smoothness node's qcqs, affine-transition and local-finite-presentation hypotheses hold for the affine models, and the limit map is the given smooth group. Only after this descent is the new Corollary 3.1.11 node applied. I read its source pp.88--89 and verified that its conclusion includes connected geometric fibres on the whole base. The new node is self-contained, statement-only, and strictly an input to the construction rather than a restatement of the item's existence clause. No Noetherianity, flat transitions or injective transitions are falsely imposed on this generic theorem.

In step 5, the field-extension node is applicable because the fraction-field group is already reductive, hence smooth connected affine finite type. It gives maximality first over that fraction field and then over its algebraic closure; the openness node specifically permits a *closed* split torus and concerns geometric maximality. The domain has generic point, so a principal open D(f) can be chosen within the supplied neighbourhood. Localization is by a nonzero f; adjoining f inverse preserves the required finite-type subalgebra of K.

In step 6, I checked the Noetherianity input and the exact non-Noetherian limitations of the relative-tangent node: our subalgebra is Noetherian by the cited finite-type theorem. That tangent node explicitly gives finite support, finite projectivity, and base change for full torus weights, not just their differentials. I spelled out why finite projectivity gives finite presentation before using module descent. During the small-case check I corrected an intermediate draft's assertion that zero always belongs to weight support: for the trivial group the zero summand is zero. The final wording only asserts that the nonzero weight support is Phi.

For step 7, the module node supplies descent of maps between fixed finitely presented modules and eventual equality of maps. I use both parts to keep the specified isomorphism K to each simple-root line, including 1 mapping to the *given* X_alpha. Descending an unspecified line isomorphism alone would not establish pinning compatibility. For non-simple roots any basis is sufficient. Unwanted weights form a finite set from step 6, so all vanish at one common stage. Tangent-weight base change identifies the descended modules with those of the actual new group, and the already established geometric properties persist on pullback.

In step 8 I read the arbitrary-base root-line/coroot statement: it says precisely that the zero weight equals Lie(T), that the geometric roots are the nonzero weight lines, and that a root line of rank one has a relative canonical coroot whose monomial exponent is locally constant and whose formation commutes with arbitrary base change. The final subalgebra is still a domain, so Spec(A) is connected (all nonempty opens contain its generic point). Each coroot exponent is therefore constant and can be identified by its K-value. No Lie-character differential is used to detect roots or coroots in positive characteristic. Step 9 uses the definition and pinned base-change theorem only for the final assembly and identification; every structural condition has already been verified.

Small cases checked: K=k; arbitrary transcendental K/k; a pure torus with empty roots and a nonzero central lattice; the rank-zero trivial group; a root set not spanning M; and residue characteristic 2 or 3. The finite lists may be empty and the Laurent presentation for rank zero is the base ring. The new limit lemma also allows nonflat and noninjective transitions and nilpotent base rings; its source is genuinely that general. There is no assumption that rank-one projective modules are globally free before step 7. No step exceeds 2000 characters or hides a separate comparison lemma.
