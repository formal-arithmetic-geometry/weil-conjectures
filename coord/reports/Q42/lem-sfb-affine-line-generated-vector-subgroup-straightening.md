## Review by Q42

I read Conrad Proposition 1.15 on printed p.9 alongside the item's statement and every proof step. The source assumes smoothness but not connectedness of the given subgroup, uses the separable closure rather than an algebraic closure, and produces a group automorphism, all of which the item preserves. A family whose images generate is enough: the induction never requires every possible affine-line morphism to be a generator or the chosen family to be Galois stable. I did not replace the source by a perfect-field assertion.

For the equation prerequisite I read Corollary 1.8 and Propositions 1.6--1.7 on printed p.5. The ambient group is the actual coordinate Ga^n, H is smooth of dimension n-1, and P is its defining equation as a scheme, not only on rational points. For an additive polynomial, each partial derivative is its constant linear coefficient; separability is precisely that at least one is nonzero. Thus the node's extra explicit derivative clause is part of the source's separability conclusion, not an added hypothesis. For n=1 this includes finite smooth subgroups, such as the zeros of t^p-t, not just the trivial subgroup. Nonreduced alpha_p is excluded by smoothness, as it must be.

For the axis prerequisite I read Lemma 1.11 on printed pp.6--7, Lemma 1.12 and Lemma 1.13 on printed p.7. This is not the item's codimension-one clause renamed: f is any additive homomorphism, with no smoothness or generation hypothesis on its kernel. A nonconstant curve over the separable closure supplies a nonzero principal-part zero in every coordinate system by comparing the highest coefficient of the composed polynomial. Lemma 1.12 descends such a zero from the Galois extension; Lemma 1.11 then performs degree-decreasing triangular additive coordinate changes over k. The conclusion that f composed with alpha ignores one coordinate is exactly assertion (3). I included n=1 separately: a nonzero one-variable polynomial cannot annihilate a nonconstant polynomial, by leading-degree multiplication, so f=0. The zero map works in every dimension. For the projection f(x,y)=x, swapping axes satisfies the assertion. The hypothesis is essential over imperfect fields: a wound kernel has no such nonconstant curve over a separable closure; I did not infer the conclusion merely from positive kernel dimension.

For Step 1 I opened the statements of def-weil-closed-immersion and def-weil-polynomial-ring. They supply a quotient ideal and polynomial coordinates; the elementary leading-term calculation then rules out a nonconstant curve into a proper subgroup of Ga. All constants are zero because the family is pointed. Minimality, followed by injectivity of scalar extension on vector spaces, proves the scheme-theoretic zero subgroup. This also tests the empty family and N=0. No use of geometric points or reducedness is hidden here.

For Step 2 I opened lem-sf-noetherian-polynomial-dimension and lem-wf-affine-dimension-drop. The first applies to a field as a Noetherian ring of dimension zero; the second permits any ground field and any proper closed subset of an irreducible affine variety. The nonzero ideal has proper support because the polynomial ring is a domain. These statements give exactly N and the bound N-1; they do not require k to be algebraically closed.

For Step 3 I checked that the new equation node gives a scheme-theoretic kernel, then that the new axis node accepts a nonconstant scheme morphism over K, not only a Ga homomorphism.
