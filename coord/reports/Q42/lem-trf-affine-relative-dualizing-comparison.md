## Review by Q42

I reopened each cited statement, the item, and the new node, and checked the sources above against their hypotheses. The item agrees with 0E2U for the given pair, specialized to the separated locally Noetherian setting stated by the item. It asserts no construction of a pair. Its boundedness assumption is relative Tor dimension, and the proof never replaces this with absolute Tor dimension over A.

Step 1 uses `lem-weil-pbc-flat-morphism-affine-criterion` and `lem-weil-pbc-lfp-affine-opens-finite-presentation`, whose statements apply to every compatible affine pair, not just a cover. `pqf-lem-affine-open-locnoeth` supplies Noetherianity for the actual V. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian` applies first to A over R, then to B over A. The generators 1⊗t_i give the second finite-type hypothesis explicitly. This corrects the former unjustified shrinking and definition-only citations.

Step 2 uses `lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra`, including its specified coprojection formulas. Since V→S is a monomorphism, U×_V U equals U×_S U. I checked the actual diagonal B→A is multiplication and that its inverse image of D(b) is D(μ(b)). The sections A_{μ(b)} are exactly those given by `lem-weil-pbc-spec-module-tilde-sections`, including the fraction restriction maps. Thus this step proves the pushforward identification rather than citing a definition of a pushforward as a theorem about it.

Step 3 uses `def-pur-pseudo-coherent-complex` only for its local finite-free condition, `lem-sc-pseudo-coherent-qcoh` for quasi-coherent cohomology, then `lem-pc-affine-qcoh-derived-equivalence` and `lem-pc-affine-pseudo-coherent-criterion` for M and its bounded-above finite-free representative. `def-pc-ring-pseudo-coherent-complex` names the resulting ring property. The equivalence is applied after its quasi-coherence hypothesis is verified.

Step 4 uses precisely the new criterion's local-finite and inverse-image-test clauses. I read all of 0DHY: it imposes no flatness, boundedness, Noetherianity or pseudo-coherence assumption, and its second clause globalizes the interval by quasi-compactness of Spec(A). The additional inverse-image-test formulation follows from the stalk test 09U9: every module T over O_{V,v} is the stalk at v of the direct image of T from the one-point ringed space. At u over v, inverse image has that same stalk T, so these tests see all relative stalk tensors. This also validates the item's formulation in terms of inverse images of O_S-modules. Extending an O_V-module by open direct image justifies restricting the base. Testing N=R then supplies bounded cohomology of M. This is the only new node, with no proof hidden in its statement and no claim of being a Mathlib leaf.

Step 5 uses both the affine and open clauses of `lem-pc-affine-derived-pullback-tensor-tilde`. `lem-weil-pbc-flat-module-base-change` supplies flatness of B as an A-module through the first factor (the second copy of A is the base-changed flat R-module). `lem-pc-module-bounded-flat-kflat` makes B[0] a legitimate flat/K-flat tensor model. `lem-weil-pbc-flat-tensor-exact` preserves cycles, boundaries and their quotient, yielding H^i(M_1)=H^i(M)⊗_A B. The lower bound needed later is therefore available.

Step 6 uses the surjection μ(a⊗1)=a to show A is cyclic over B. `lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-pseudocoherent-cohomology-criterion` applies to A[0] over the already Noetherian B: cohomology is zero except for the finite module in degree zero. `def-pc-ring-pseudo-coherent-complex` then supplies the bounded-above finite-free representative. The argument needs no regular immersion or finite projective dimension.

Step 7 uses `lem-sc-derived-internal-hom-restrict-open` with the open W in Y. `lem-sc-affine-pseudo-coherent-rhom-qcoherent` has exactly the needed first-argument finite-projective and second-argument bounded-below conditions; Steps 5 and 6 verify them, and `lem-weil-pbc-free-module-projective` provides the free-to-projective implication. This is the existing node's affine RHom statement, not the weaker finite-module Ext sheaf statement.

Step 8 uses the fully faithful affine equivalence to obtain the actual class ξ_U. It checks which multiplication map is carried to which by transposition, rather than choosing an arbitrary isomorphism of Hom objects. The tensor comparison is `lem-pc-affine-derived-pullback-tensor-tilde`; evaluation and the finite-free model use `lem-sc-derived-hom-finite-free-model`, `lem-sc-derived-hom-tensor-total`, and the explicitly normalized family of `def-pc-canonical-derived-tensor-hom-bijection`. I checked the two transposes: both are multiplication A⊗_B^L A→A followed by ξ_U, with componentwise evaluation h⊗x→h(x). Thus the given isomorphism, not merely its isomorphism class, is reflected back to the algebraic multiplication map.

Step 9 reuses the stated open compatibilities, basic-open fraction maps and finite-free Hom/evaluation models. The finite-free first argument and bounded-below second argument leave finitely many summands in each Hom degree, so restriction commutes with the actual comparison maps and their differentials. Scalar multiplication commutes by the displayed evaluation identity, and first-variable actions use the functorial Hom construction. I checked this for an arbitrary smaller affine pair using its product open in W. The proof does not use an invalid assertion that equality of arbitrary derived morphisms can be detected locally.

For the new criterion I tested R=A, R→0 (empty source), M=0, shifted free complexes, a nonflat map Z→F_2, and an unbounded direct sum of shifts. The finite-relative-Tor condition correctly fails for complexes with cohomology in arbitrarily many degrees; no hidden boundedness hypothesis was introduced. For the item I checked the empty chart and the identity morphism, and that A cyclic over B does not falsely imply projectivity or regularity of the diagonal.
