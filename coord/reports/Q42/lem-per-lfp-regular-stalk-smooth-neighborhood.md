I reviewed the node files with the Stacks passages above open, and read every statement cited by the five proof steps. In step 1, `def-weil-pbc-g-ring` explicitly defines a regular ring map to be flat with geometrically regular fibres at every prime. Applying this at the maximal ideal of the source stalk is just an instance of that definition, so only this step is marked elementary. The stalk map is local, but nothing in this step enlarges its flatness to nearby points.

In step 2, `lem-sbc-scheme-fibre-stalk-quotient` states the surjection from the source stalk and identifies its kernel with the base maximal ideal times that stalk. `lem-weil-pbc-fibre-ring-localization-quotient` gives an isomorphism of B-algebras and its formula on pure tensors. At the maximal ideal of the local ring A, the denominators are already units, so its localization is B. The composite identification respects k = A/m: a residue class of a in A maps to the class of its image in B by both routes. This checks the actual scalar structure needed for geometric regularity, rather than using an arbitrary ring isomorphism. Tensoring that k-algebra isomorphism with each finite purely inseparable extension identifies the rings whose regularity is in the definition. The step cites a theorem for each of its two non-elementary identifications.
