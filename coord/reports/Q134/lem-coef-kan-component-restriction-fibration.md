I read the item's statement, the chapter and unit records, the ancestor's blueprint proof paragraph 2, and the online Rezk PDF at Section 12.2 and Lemma 13.9. The auxiliary assertion is valid for arbitrary small simplicial sets with a Kan fibration; neither source nor target needs to be Kan. I checked each clause of the unchanged statement: the map exists precisely under the stated inclusion of component subsets, its fiber is a strict degreewise intersection, the intersection is saturated for fiber-edge equivalence, and both the restricted fibers and component subcomplexes of a Kan complex satisfy all horn fillers.

For step 1 I opened def-weil-pbc-simplicial-set and read its statement defining simplicial operators, natural transformations, and simplicial subsets. The vertex formula for an operator proves closure without a further lemma: restricting a simplex to vertices after an order map selects the corresponding original vertices. Restricting along the two-vertex order map gives the edge which identifies their component classes. Applying a natural transformation preserves that edge; the hypothesis (pi_0 p)(S) contained in R is used exactly to obtain the target restriction. This is the only step using the simplicial-set definition node, and its quoted sentence says exactly what is used.

For step 2 I opened lem-coef-kan-quillen-right-proper and read its statement, not its proof. It explicitly identifies Kan fibrations with the right lifting property for all positive-dimensional horn inclusions. That characterization is the sole nontrivial supplied interface used here. I form a square for the original p, fill it, choose a vertex of an included face, and use edges in the filled simplex to place every filler vertex in S. Factoring the filler and checking both equations require only degreewise inclusion. I specifically checked n=1: it is false that every horn contains all vertices, so the proof uses just one horn vertex and the newly supplied filler edge. This avoids that tempting false argument. The clause for arbitrary n follows by the same explicit edge restriction, without any connectivity theorem or model-category base-change lemma.

Step 3 cites no node because it writes the degreewise pullback and its universal property directly. In degree n the fiber condition is p(x)=y_n, not merely that p(x) has the component of y. I checked all operators preserve this equation by naturality. The union of components is the inverse image T of S under pi_0(X_y)->pi_0(X); equality with (X_y)[T] is immediate from the stated vertex condition. I checked that the proof never assumes that this map on pi_0 is injective: different fiber components can belong to the same ambient component.

Step 4 again uses only the horn and fibrant-object clauses of lem-coef-kan-quillen-right-proper, with the same verbatim citation. A horn in the strict fiber, together with the constant simplex at y, is a lifting square for the already proved restricted fibration. Its filler remains strictly over y, so lies in the identified fiber. The same construction gives the Kan property of the ambient fiber if desired. There is no assumption that the base is Kan and no hidden stability-of-pullback theorem.
