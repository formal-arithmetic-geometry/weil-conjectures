## Source error

In Proposition 20.24 the printed argument has inconsistent initial-component subscripts. More substantially, after proving that the k-th power of the tail times the remaining module element lies in Q, the last paragraph claims the unpowered initial product lies in Q. Homogeneity gives instead the k-th power of the tail's initial component times the initial module component. The unpowered inference is false: in the standard graded ring K[t], for Q=(t^2), tail t and module element 1, the squared product belongs to Q but the unpowered product does not. The stated proposition remains true. Steps 4--6 retain the exponent k, use the hypothesis on the homogeneous pair (u_t,c^k), and conclude c^{kh} belongs to J. This repairs the proof without changing any hypothesis or conclusion. The proof also avoids the printed dependence on a nilradical ideal by giving an explicit exponent for a finite sum of elements having powers in J.
