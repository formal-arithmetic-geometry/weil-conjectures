## Review by NG

I checked the argument against the Stacks passages named above and read each cited node's statement, not merely its proof or title. There are no new nodes whose sources need review. The statement's affine A and B and the assumption on every quasi-compact open of X suffice; neither X nor a is treated as quasi-compact or separated.

Step 1 uses lem-weil-pbc-affine-opens-basis for an affine neighbourhood of every image point, and lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs solely for quasi-compactness of each chosen affine. Their statements say exactly these facts. Continuity pulls this covering back to L; its given finite-subcover property supplies the finitely many W_j. Restricting any open covering of their finite union and taking the union of finite subcovers proves that union quasi-compact. Applying the hypothesis then makes W quasi-affine. This is a direct finite-cover argument, not a hidden geometric lemma. I deliberately did not apply the existing finite-affine-cover-of-a-qc-open theorem to p(L): the image need not be open.

Step 2 cites def-pc-fibre-product for the universal property that identifies W times_A B with W times_X Z; the pairs and their compatibility are written explicitly. The statement of lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-base-change gives the open immersion of this pullback into Z. Its image is the inverse image of W: the open restriction represents exactly maps factoring through W. Since L's underlying subset is contained in that open, restricting the structure sheaf proves L is its open subscheme. def-weil-open-immersion states precisely the required topological and structure-sheaf criterion. This use is valid for open immersions; no false assertion about arbitrary immersions factoring from point-set containment is used.

Step 3 uses the forward implication of lem-a1-quasi-affine-target-locality with the singleton affine cover {A}; its only required preimage is W, already quasi-affine. The statement of lem-trf-quasi-affine-base-change applies to this morphism along the arbitrary b, with no finite-presentation condition, and gives exactly T to B quasi-affine. Taking the affine open B itself in def-nagata-quasi-affine-morphism gives T quasi-affine. These hypotheses are all in force. The off-unit node is a generic black-box tool already specified by the item's source, declared in split_report.tool_uses; it replaces no unit step.

Step 4 uses def-weil-open-immersion only for composition of actual open-subscheme identifications: an open of an open is open, and sheaf restriction is transitive. Embedding T in an affine H and restricting to L produces the required embedding; L's quasi-compactness is the original hypothesis, not a consequence of every open in a quasi-affine scheme being quasi-compact. Thus this is a few-line elementary argument, not a new lemma or an application of a missing quasi-affine theorem.

I tested L empty (choose n=0, W empty, whose quasi-affineness follows from the same hypothesis), X empty, the zero-ring affine base, identity base change, and unrestricted possibly non-quasi-compact X. The proof never assumes intersections of affine opens are affine, or that p(L) is open. No step exceeds 2000 characters; the complete assembly is 2073 characters.
