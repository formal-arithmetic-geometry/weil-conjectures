## Source error

The item statement is retained, including the full non-smooth locus, all parameters, arbitrary base fields and the image-closure conclusion. For dimension zero of the vector space, generation forces Y empty; the proof treats this explicitly, interpreting P(V) as empty when V=0. There is no added positive-dimensionality or flatness hypothesis.

The source is Stacks varieties.tex, Tag 0FD6, `lemma-bertini`, read in full (including its use of 0B2L and its final omitted first-order detail). Its flatness paragraph proves flatness only over a selected open U but then states an unqualified equivalence between smoothness of q at (x,v) and smoothness of H_v at x. This equivalence is false outside that open. For example Y=A^1 with the sections 1,t of O gives the immersion (1:t) into P^1. The family is a+bt=0. At the zero coefficient vector, H_0=A^1 is smooth, whereas q is not smooth at (a,b,t)=(0,0,0): the images of the regular parameters a,b are -bt,b, and after quotienting by a, multiplication by b kills the nonzero class of t. Thus this local map is not flat. The source's asserted equivalence `H_v singular iff the local equation lies in m_x^2` also fails for the identically zero equation: H_0=Y is smooth. The dimension estimate itself is true.

I repair precisely this inference, keeping the same first-order-kernel route: if the relative differential of F is nonzero at a point of its zero scheme, the relative hypersurface criterion gives smoothness of q. Consequently a nonsmooth point has F=dF=0. Only containment in that linear incidence kernel is needed for an upper bound. The calculation works over residue fields directly, so there is no need to transport the non-smooth locus through an algebraic closure. This is recorded with proof_origin.by=agent because it repairs the literal source argument. No unit/chapter route or target statement has been changed.

An initial candidate node for first-order generation was removed before delivery: the existing conormal sequence supplies its non-elementary content, and the explicit calculation with e_i and e_j-f_j e_i fits in Step 3. This avoids an elementary extra node.

Its statement introduces W,S,M,s,Z,z,f and the residue differential, and contains no proof. Applying this to the finitely many irreducible components gives both stated inequalities. No irreducibility or properness assumption is silently imposed on X or Y. Its statement is self-contained and has no proof sketch.

Step 1 uses exactly the definitions of an invertible sheaf and generating sections: a zero stalk cannot be free of rank one over a nonzero local ring. Thus an empty generating set excludes every point. The empty-space dimension convention handles Y empty and r=0.

Step 2 uses `lem-wf-affine-noetherian-components` for the Noetherian topology of polynomial affine charts, `lem-kun-finite-union-noetherian` for their finite union, and `lem-sfb-noetherian-space-compact-subsets` for quasi-compactness of the immersion image. I read their statements: the first includes finite-type algebras over a field, the second allows arbitrary subspaces, and the third gives every subset a finite subcover. The immersion definition is a closed followed by an open immersion, hence identifies Y with its locally closed image. The smoothness definition includes local finite presentation. Finite-type and zero-scheme definitions then give finite generation of every displayed quotient ring. The finite covers and quotients are explicitly written, not an application of a theorem about a different scheme. A closed reduced locus is defined affine-locally by a radical quotient, so does not require reducedness of any fibre.

Step 3 uses `lem-weil-pbc-smooth-differentials-free-rank-fibre-dimension` exactly for affine local freeness and equality of its rank with local fibre dimension. For the structure morphism to Spec k, that fibre is Y itself. Reapplying its rank statement at each point identifies the rank d throughout the chosen open. The conormal node `lem-pur-conormal-sequence-smooth-immersion` supplies surjectivity on residue differential fibres for a closed immersion with smooth source; the locally closed immersion is factored through its open ambient neighbourhood before this application. The coordinate interpretation comes from the existing projective-by-sections definition. The calculation `e_i -> (1,0)` and `e_j-f_j(y)e_i -> (0,df_j(y))` supplies first-order generation explicitly, over every residue field, including inseparable ones. Neither a theorem only about rational cotangent spaces nor the source's omitted detail is cited as if proved.

Step 4 uses clause (2) of `lem-pc-proper-base-change`, which really states smooth base-change stability; it does not take that fact merely from the base-change definition. The new hypersurface node says exactly nonzero relative df gives smoothness at z. The differential-sheaf definition identifies affine differentials by their universal derivations; treating x_j as constants over k[x] gives the displayed relative derivative. The zero-scheme definition supplies F=0. All hypotheses of the hypersurface node hold for the ambient smooth projection, in every characteristic. Taking the contrapositive gives only containment in K_U, which is valid even where q is non-flat. I do not use the false converse discussed above.

Step 5 uses the residue rank proved in Step 3, not a presumed vector-bundle kernel construction. Solving the invertible minor is elementary linear algebra over kappa(y), and identifies the fibre scheme by its polynomial quotient, not just its set of rational points. The polynomial-dimension leaf states the exact formula over a Noetherian ring, and the spectrum-dimension node identifies the resulting ring dimension with topological dimension. A field is Noetherian (only two ideals) and has dimension zero; zero variables are allowed. The new dimension node applies to the finite-type affine U and K_U with n=r-d-1 >=0 and dim U=d. The closed-locus chain argument gives the dimension bound for the subspace Sigma over U; this does not assume scheme-theoretic factorization of an arbitrary nonreduced map through a set-theoretic locus.

Step 6 writes the local-to-global chain argument explicitly and applies the first clause of the new dimension node to the finite-type scheme Sigma and its k-morphism into affine space. This node states dimension of the closure of the image, so no constructibility or properness inference is hidden. The polynomial/spectrum leaves give dim A^r=r, and hence the image closure is proper. There is no need to infer a closed image for q itself.
