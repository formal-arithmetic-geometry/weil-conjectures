## Statement clarified: STATEMENT-DISPUTED

The imported statement calls the morphism supplied by the completed-map definition a *specified strict tower* morphism. At the statement interface, however, lem-weil-pbc-tower-compatibility only asserts existence, recovery by evaluation, and transition compatibility. Those conditions do not characterize a lift. Its proof uses a specific extra construction: reduce the ambient mate and conjugate by projection-formula isomorphisms. Using that construction only from the proof of a cited node would violate C3. I have made it explicit in this item's statement, leaving its hypotheses, corner types, comparison directions, naturality and final equality intact.

The construction is now: realize the corner targets on the ringed diagram sites, take H and H' to be the actual counit-defined projection-formula transformations on those sites, and set v'=H' R(e) H^{-1}. This is the same transported realization used in the supplied tower-compatibility proof (steps 3 and 4), with canonical derived corner isomorphisms. It selects that lift; it makes no assertion about arbitrary lifts having the same evaluations. Its finite evaluations are exactly the witness-transported arrows by the existing finite-level rectangle. No witness compatibility or new hypothesis was imposed.

The obstruction to the tempting alternative argument is explicit. In inverse sequences of vector spaces over a field k, let S1 be k only at level 1 and S2 be k only at level 2, all other levels zero. The sequence 0 -> S1 -> M -> S2 -> 0, with M2=M1=k and transition identity, is exact and nonsplit: a putative section S2 -> M must have level-2 component identity, whose transition to level 1 is nonzero, contradicting its zero level-1 component. It gives a nonzero derived morphism S2 -> S1[1], although every evaluated morphism is zero (Ext^1 over k vanishes). Thus strict derived morphisms cannot be identified solely by evaluation. This is a counterexample to that inference, not a counterexample to the intended normalized equality.
