Choose the affine charts $V=\operatorname{Spec}B\subset T$ and $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset S$ in the hypothesis around the images of $t,s$. That definition identifies their spectra with the stated inverse limits. Functoriality says both rings are strictly henselian local, their structural maps $j_S,j_T$ send closed points to the images of $s,t$, and the canonical map is $\operatorname{Spec}h$ for a local homomorphism $h:H_S\to H_T$ with $j_S\operatorname{Spec}h=uj_T$. Both structural maps factor through the chosen affine charts: their inverse images of these opens contain the closed point of the respective local spectrum, and an open containing the maximal ideal of a local ring is the whole spectrum. Indeed a basic neighbourhood $D(f)$ of that point has $f$ outside the maximal ideal, hence $f$ is a unit and $D(f)$ is the whole spectrum. Let $a:A\to H_S$ and $b:B\to H_T$ be the resulting ring maps. The displayed structural equality gives $ha=b\circ(A\to B)$: both corresponding spectrum maps factor through the open $U$, whose inclusion is a monomorphism; uniqueness of the ring map inducing an affine scheme morphism then gives the equality. Thus $h$ is an $A$-algebra map for precisely these structural algebra structures.

Apply the affine-chart ind-etaleness lemma to $(S,s,U,H_S,a)$ and $(T,t,V,H_T,b)$, with the identity spectrum identifications. Its chart contains the respective geometric point and its required structural-map equality is exactly the factorization in step 1. It gives $A\to H_S$ and $B\to H_T$ ind-etale. Since $A\to B$ is ind-etale by hypothesis, stability under composition makes $A\to H_T$ ind-etale. Now $H_S,H_T$ are both ind-etale $A$-algebras, and $h$ is the $A$-algebra map checked in step 1. The relative-map clause of ind-etale stability therefore makes $h$ ind-etale, hence weakly etale. This uses no finite-presentation hypothesis on $B$ itself.
