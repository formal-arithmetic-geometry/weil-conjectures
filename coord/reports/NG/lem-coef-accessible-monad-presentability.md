Step 1 cites the small-product clause of `lem-coef-accessible-pie-closure`. Its statement really gives accessibility for a small family of varying accessible factors, not just finite products or functor categories with a constant target. Each factor is accessible directly from the item's definition, and its small Hom sets give a small Hom product because S is small in the same universe. No common presentability cardinal is assumed.

Step 2 cites the full universal-property statement of `def-weil-pbc-category-colimit`, which I opened. The two-line componentwise factorization of a cocone proves all small colimits of the product; it is not inferred from a definition of sheaf colimits or from an unrelated existence theorem. Accessibility from Step 1 plus these colimits is exactly the definition of local presentability in the item. This argument includes S empty and J empty.

Step 3 cites the complete-accessible characterization for completeness of the now locally presentable base, and the inserter clause for the accessible category of arrows TA -> A and its accessible forgetful functor p. Both facts are in the cited statements. T is accessible by the exact filtered-colimit hypothesis in the item; identity preservation is immediate. The morphism condition is f a = b T(f), with the same direction as the item's algebras.

Step 4 uses the equifier clause of the closure node, not its proof. I checked both natural transformations have source and target p, so the equifier theorem applies to accessible functors. The displayed naturality equation verifies that a eta_A is natural on this inserter. The full subcategory imposes the unit law while retaining exactly the existing algebra-map condition.

Step 5 uses the same equifier clause with source T^2 q and target q. The proof spells out both naturality calculations and accessibility of the composite functors. The regular cardinal can be enlarged beyond the finitely many existing preservation/existence cardinals; a diagram filtered for the enlarged cardinal is filtered for each smaller one. No assertion that an accessible functor preserves presentable objects at the original cardinal is needed. The full equifier adds a T(a) = a mu_A and leaves all morphisms unchanged, so its category is exactly the category in the item. This is the substantive construction missing from a mere citation of the accessible-monad theorem.

Step 6 is an elementary universal-property construction and cites no additional theorem. I checked the cone compatibility before forming a:TL -> L, both algebra equations after all limit projections, and the algebra equation for the unique mediator. Joint monicity follows from uniqueness of maps into a limiting cone, so it requires no extra exactness or limit-preservation theorem. The calculation supplies the entire argument, including the terminal/empty limit; it does not silently assume T preserves limits. Although several formulas are written out for auditability, they are only the limit universal property applied to the two algebra identities.

Step 7 cites exactly the complete-accessible implication of `lem-coef-accessible-completeness-characterization`. I checked its hypothesis of accessibility is supplied by the inserter and both equifiers, and its completeness hypothesis is supplied by the calculation, not inferred from an unspecified monadicity theorem. Algebra Hom sets are subsets of already small base Hom sets. The cited statement gives cocompleteness as well as local presentability. The proof never claims T creates arbitrary colimits or that the same accessibility cardinal works throughout.

Small cases checked: an empty product is the terminal category and all formulas still apply; the identity monad forces a = 1 by the unit law and recovers the base category; a constant terminal monad has the expected terminal algebra category, without needing the monad to preserve coproducts. For the general PIE theorem, a small discrete accessible category has arbitrary small products still accessible after increasing the cardinal; this exposes why one must not fix the original cardinal. An equifier may be empty; the empty category is accessible but not complete or cocomplete, so the characterization does not incorrectly declare it locally presentable. The terminal category satisfies all three characterization conditions. No empty-diagram case or smallness hypothesis was removed.
