## Original argument preserved for review

The following is the original live 4391-character proof in full. Its work is assigned to the two new nodes as described above; it is retained here as an audit trail, not as proof steps on the new nodes.

Original Step 1: Use the enlarged etale ringed-site model for $X$ and $Y$. Write $\mathcal C_X,\mathcal C_Y$ for its sites and temporarily write $F$ and $\mathcal O_X$ for their extensions. The continuous functor defining $f$ is $v_f(V)=X\times_YV$, with its canonical ring map. Thus the hypotheses about a morphism of ringed sites in the localization theorem hold, without a representability hypothesis on $f$. Scheme objects need not be preserved by $v_f$; this is why we use the enlarged sites. The extension theorem interprets sections on an etale space as compatible sections on scheme charts.

Original Step 2: For any etale $u:Z'\to Z$, the model identifies its ringed topos with localization at $Z'$. The definition of restriction gives $u^{-1}\mathcal O_Z(T\to Z')=\mathcal O_Z(T)=\mathcal O_{Z'}(T)$, with identical restrictions and ring operations. For modules, the tensor pullback therefore reduces to $\mathcal O_{Z'}\otimes_{\mathcal O_{Z'}}(M|_{Z'})$. The maps $a\otimes m\mapsto am$ and $m\mapsto1\otimes m$ are inverse: $1\otimes am=a\otimes m$ is the tensor balancing relation. These maps commute with restrictions and hence induce inverse maps of the sheafified tensors. They are natural in $M$. This proves that the tensor pullback along an etale map is precisely restriction, including its module structure.

Original Step 3: The family $\{g:Y'\to Y,\operatorname{id}_Y:Y\to Y\}$ is jointly surjective and etale: the identity is etale and its image contains every point of $Y$. Apply [lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers] to this family and the arbitrary morphism $f:X\to Y$. Each member of the pulled-back family is etale; in particular $h:X'=X\times_Y Y'\to X$ is etale. This does not assume that $g$ itself is surjective. Regard $Y'$ as an object of $\mathcal C_Y$ and $X'=v_f(Y')$ as an object of $\mathcal C_X$. The enlarged-site model identifies the two localized ringed sites with those of $Y'$ and $X'$. On an object $V\to Y'$ the localized functor is $(X\times_YV\to X\times_YY')$. This is the functor for $f'$: the isomorphism $X\times_YV\cong X'\times_{Y'}V$ sends $(x,v)$ to $((x,g_V(v)),v)$, with inverse forgetting the $Y'$ coordinate, where $g_V:V\to Y'$ is the given structure map. The maps are inverse by the fibre-product defining equations. Their maps on regular functions are the same pullbacks on every scheme chart, so the localized ring map is also the canonical one for $f'$.

Original Step 4: Apply the direct-image comparison of the ringed-site localization theorem to $v_f$ at $Y'$. Using the preceding identifications gives $(f_*F)|_{Y'}\cong f'_*(F|_{X'})$ as module sheaves. More explicitly, for an etale scheme $V\to Y'$, its component is the identity $F(X\times_YV)=F|_{X'}(X\times_YV\to X')$, after the fibre-product identification of step 3. Both are the equalizer of the two restrictions from the sections on an etale scheme chart $W\to X\times_YV$ to those on $W\times_{X\times_YV}W$. The enlarged-site model supplies this equalizer even when $X\times_YV$ is not a scheme. The scalar action on both sides is the same pullback of a regular function on $V$ to those charts. For a map $V_1\to V_2$ over $Y'$, both restriction maps use $X\times_YV_1\to X\times_YV_2$; for $F\to H$, both use the induced maps of these equalizers. The identities therefore form the asserted canonical natural module isomorphism.

Original Step 5: The pullback comparison of the same ringed-site localization theorem gives $(f^*G)|_{X'}\cong f'^*(G|_{Y'})$. Here the localization pullbacks are restrictions by step 2, and their ring maps agree with the original ones by step 3. To specify the comparison, use the common ring map from $(f h)^{-1}\mathcal O_Y$ to $\mathcal O_{X'}$, equal to that for $g f'$. Both paths identify with $\mathcal O_{X'}\otimes_{(f h)^{-1}\mathcal O_Y}(f h)^{-1}G$. On local tensors the first identification is $a\otimes(b\otimes m)\mapsto a\,h^\sharp(b)\otimes m$; the second is $c\otimes(d\otimes m)\mapsto c\,f'^\sharp(d)\otimes m$. In each case the inverse is $e\otimes m\mapsto e\otimes(1\otimes m)$, and the balancing relation proves both inverse identities. These formulas are read locally before sheafification, so they do not assert that every section of a sheaf tensor product is globally a pure tensor. The ring maps commute with restriction, and the formulas leave the section $m$ unchanged; hence the comparison is natural in $G$ and multiplies exactly the scalar coefficients specified in the statement.
