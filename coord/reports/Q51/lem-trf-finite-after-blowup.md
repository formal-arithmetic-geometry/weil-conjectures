Step 1: I opened `sk-lem-nagata-boundary-divisor` and used precisely its admissible blow-up and schematically dense inverse open. `lemma-nagata-blowup-proper` supplies properness. `def-weil-proper-morphism` supplies finite type (hence quasi-compactness) and separatedness; `lem-pc-separated-quasi-separated` supplies quasi-separatedness; clause (2) of `lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence` then supplies qcqs of the new base. `lemma-nagata-admissible-blowup-over-open` states the required isomorphism over the preserved open, which makes its inverse image quasi-compact. Step 2: I read the current `blueprint_update` of `def-nagata-strict-transform`: this is the scheme image of the open above the complement of the blow-up center, as a closed subscheme of the base change. I also read `lemma-nagata-scheme-image-restrict-open`, whose statement gives restriction as closed subschemes, and `lemma-nagata-admissible-blowup-over-open`. Their combination proves the restriction claim, rather than assuming it from a definition. The remaining checks are elementary: a finite-type ideal on an affine has finitely many generators and its complement is the finite union of their principal opens, so this open immersion is quasi-compact; after restricting to the preserved open it is the identity, with zero kernel. Thus its scheme image is the whole restricted ambient scheme. This short argument applies separately to both blow-ups. I corrected the provisional source tag for quasi-compact scheme image to 01R8; 01RE is a different lemma.

Step 3: Clauses (a)--(c) of `lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence` explicitly cover composition, base change and closed immersions. They make the first strict transform proper. I read the whole statement of the existing `lem-trf-flattening-by-admissible-blowup`: its base qcqs, quasi-compact open, finite-type quasi-separated map and flat locally-finitely-presented restriction are all verified before applying it. It supplies a finite-type quasi-coherent admissible center and flat, finitely presented strict transform. No result from its proof is used.

Step 4: The same three properness permanence clauses apply to the second strict transform. `lemma-nagata-admissible-blowup-preserves-density`, in both its stored statement and current update, supplies density of the inverse open: Step 1 provided a quasi-compact schematically dense open and Step 3 a finite-type ideal that is the unit ideal there. `def-nagata-scheme-theoretically-dense-open` supplies the injective structure-sheaf restriction. On a nonempty affine missing the open, this would inject a nonzero ring into zero; this explicitly proves topological density. Step 2 gives finiteness on that open and Step 3 gives flatness and local finite presentation. These are every hypothesis of the new 0B48 node, so it gives finiteness. I removed a provisional, incorrect 0806 reference from this step's source place: 0806 is the blow-up universal property, whereas density here is taken from the cited density node.

Step 5: I opened `def-pc-finite-locally-free`. Its definition is exactly finite plus flat plus locally of finite presentation, so this is a definition-level step using the preceding results. No constant rank assumption or implicit finite-flat criterion is required.
