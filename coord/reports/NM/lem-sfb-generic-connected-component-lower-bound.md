All non-elementary uses are existing statements and every occurrence has an exact quote in citations.json. All four steps are shorter than 2000 characters.

Step 1 uses the full fibre homeomorphism stated in def-pc-scheme-fibre, not just the fibre product definition. The maximal-component node explicitly supplies a connected component through every point and contains every connected set through that point. I wrote out why intersecting components coincide (their union is connected), why each component is closed (a clopen separation of its closure restricts to a separation of the dense component), and why taking the ambient closure has exactly the original trace in the fibre. These are elementary arguments from connectedness and subspace closure; no Noetherian openness claim is used. The finite number n is supplied by the hypothesis, not by an uncited finiteness theorem. The n=0 case is handled before choosing any component or point.

Step 2 uses precisely the closed-cover-spread statement, with C the finite union of the ambient closures and D empty; its binary form is therefore enough. For each pair it uses precisely the avoidance statement with the closed set C_i intersect C_j. I verified the generic trace is empty from Step 1. Both tools require only locally finite type and quasi-compactness, which are exactly the two clauses of finite type in the item's definition. Their conclusions are on V intersect closure({eta}); because eta is generic in all S this is just V. Finite intersections of the resulting neighbourhoods still contain eta. In particular I did not apply an empty-generic-fibre theorem to an arbitrary open complement, which could fail to be quasi-compact over a non-Noetherian base.

Step 3 uses the closed-subset-nonemptiness statement with each C_i, which is closed and meets the generic fibre because A_i is nonempty. I opened that node and its live Lean statement/docstring as an additional check: no reducedness, separatedness, Noetherianity or initial flatness is required. Its source is the same generic-flatness/openness argument used in 055D. I take this result from its statement only. Its implementation and proof are not being used as an extra conclusion.

Step 4 proves the counting conclusion explicitly. Every closed piece is clopen because the other finitely many pieces cover its complement. A connected component through a chosen point cannot cross that clopen separation. The components selected in different pieces must therefore be distinct; the fibre homeomorphism transports them to scheme fibres. The pieces need not themselves be connected. This avoids an unjustified assertion of constancy or exactly n components in special fibres.
