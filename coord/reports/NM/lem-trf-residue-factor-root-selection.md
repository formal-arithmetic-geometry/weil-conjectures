## Review by NM

I read the item against the final residue paragraph of Stacks 00UF and read the whole 09HN proof and the 09HL splitting convention for the new node. The item has exactly the residue-field polynomial data that 00UF uses; the mathematical statement remains unchanged. The new node introduces its field, both polynomials, divisibility and the meaning of splitting within its statement. Its two statement dependencies actually define fields and polynomials. It has no proof or proof dependencies, because it is a searched and verified Mathlib leaf. Its source specifies tags and labels, and its Mathlib match specifies exact declaration, file, and read lines. There is no hidden root-selection lemma, no statement-only node with a proof sketch, and no new definition.

For Step 1 I opened `def-pc-field-extension` and `def-weil-polynomial-ring`. The former explicitly supplies the injective field map. The latter explicitly describes polynomials as coefficient sequences and defines degree and leading coefficient. A nonzero constant is a unit by the field inverse, so the nonunit irreducible has a nonzero positive-degree coefficient. Injectivity preserves this coefficient. This coefficient argument is elementary and does not use an unquoted scalar-extension theorem.

For Step 2 I opened `lem-pur-polynomial-field-pid`. Its statement supplies BOTH the domain property and that every ideal in k[t] is principal. I use the latter only. The proof explicitly identifies J as all A f_1+B g and verifies its ideal property, so membership of 1 gives the displayed two-term identity without an unstated theorem about finite ideal spans. Writing J=(d), f_1=d e, and using irreducibility gives either d or e a unit. If e is a unit, d=f_1 e^{-1} and d dividing g forces f_1 dividing g, which is excluded. Thus d is a unit and 1 belongs to J. The only non-elementary input is the cited PID statement; no generic Bézout node, coprime predicate, or minimal polynomial assertion is silently used.

For Step 3 I opened `lem-weil-coefficientwise-extension-injective` and the new leaf. The existing statement explicitly gives both the ring homomorphism and injectivity of coefficient extension, not just a definition of its coefficients. It therefore supplies f_L=(f_1)_L H_L and nonvanishing of f_L from f nonzero. The new leaf actually states splitting of an arbitrary divisor of a nonzero split polynomial; it is applied over F=L, NOT over k. Its three hypotheses are the given factor list, this nonvanishing, and the mapped divisibility. It supplies a full scalar-times-linear-factor expression for (f_1)_L; it is not being used for a root-selection conclusion that it does not state.

For Step 4 I opened `def-weil-polynomial-root`, whose statement says that evaluation is the unique ring homomorphism extending the coefficient map and sending t to the chosen element. Nonconstancy forces the auxiliary factor list to be nonempty. Evaluating at one of its beta_j kills one factor, then f=f_1 H shows f(beta_j)=0. Evaluation of the original prescribed factor list and the field's no-zero-divisor property force beta_j=alpha_i for some i. The finite-product zero argument is written as induction and uses no separability or distinctness. This is a few-line calculation from the displayed factorizations and evaluation definition, not a new lemma.

For Step 5 the same opened evaluation definition gives the ring homomorphism used on A f_1+B g=1. It yields B(alpha_i) g(alpha_i)=1 at the SAME index from Step 4. Thus g(alpha_i) is nonzero. There is no field descent assertion or identification of a prime ideal hidden in this step. Every citation in all five steps has its verbatim statement in `citations.json`; no proof of another node is used.

I checked the hypotheses and small cases explicitly. For the item, I empty means f_L=1, so the hypothesized nonconstant divisor cannot occur; Step 4 detects this as 0=1 rather than excluding the empty case in the statement. Repeated roots, for example f=t^2, f_1=t, g=1 over any field, cause no problem. In characteristic p, f_1=t^p-u over F_p(u) can split with repeated roots after adjoining a p-th root; the proof still works because it never uses separability or L-irreducibility. For f_1=t-a, the chosen value is a and nondivisibility is the asserted nonzero evaluation. A nonzero constant g is allowed; g=0 is incompatible with f_1 not dividing g, since every polynomial divides zero. The leaf is tested with nonzero constant P (and empty factor family), unit divisors Q, Q=P, repeated-root P, and arbitrary characteristic. Its exclusion of P=0 is essential (every polynomial divides zero, but may fail to split), and is present. Q=0 is impossible when Q divides P nonzero. Scalars in the factorization therefore cannot create a false zero-divisor case.
