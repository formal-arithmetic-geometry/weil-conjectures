The first theorem assumes a locally Noetherian target and locally finite type morphism; a field is Noetherian because it has only two ideals. Step 2 uses 047M's assertion about every local ring, not just its assertion at the identity. The localization node actually gives an inclusion-preserving prime bijection, and the affine-component node actually identifies components with minimal primes. A prime strictly below a prime contained in p remains contained in p, which checks the minimality argument in both directions. The open-intersection node explicitly gives an injective map on components meeting the open; the component-cover node explicitly supplies existence. These yield uniqueness of a component at every point, hence disjointness. No geometric point was incorrectly treated as a rational point.

Step 3 takes local finiteness from step 1 and disjointness from step 2. Removing the finitely many other closed components gives a neighbourhood inside Z because the quoted component-cover theorem covers every point of that neighbourhood. The elementary clopen-separation argument identifies Z with C. The 047M node explicitly states geometric irreducibility of the reduced induced scheme on Z. The reduction definition describes its sheaf, and the reduction theorem explicitly says its map remains a homeomorphism after every base change. Since Z is open, its reduced induced sheaf is the nilradical quotient of the open restriction. This checks the transfer of geometric irreducibility to the original open scheme C without discarding nilpotents. I checked the hypotheses of the reduction theorem on C itself.

Step 4 uses `lem-sc-irreducible-product-over-field`, whose actual hypothesis is geometric irreducibility of BOTH factors; step 3 supplies this. It does not assume that a product of arbitrary connected spaces is enough. Multiplication's image contains e via (e,e); inversion fixes e; inverse images of clopen separations prove connectedness of these images. The open-factorization node gives existence AND uniqueness for every morphism whose set image lies in the open, not the invalid analogous assertion for arbitrary closed subschemes. Uniqueness verifies the group diagrams after composing with the inclusion. `def-sc-group-scheme` gives exactly those diagrams.

This is the local finite type plus quasi-compact definition of finite type. It never infers that an arbitrary open subset of a finite-type scheme is automatically quasi-compact.

Step 6 cites the open-base-change theorem, rather than treating that fact as part of the definition. Apply it to C and to its complementary open. Their images are the complementary inverse images under the projection, so they form a clopen decomposition. The definition of geometric irreducibility supplies connectedness after ANY field extension, including inseparable ones. The base-change definition supplies the induced unit. The two set inclusions identifying the component are written separately; equality as open schemes then follows because both have the same restricted structure sheaf.
