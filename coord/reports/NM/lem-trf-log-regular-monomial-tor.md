## Argument retained for lem-trf-log-chart-strict-local-tor

The following is the entire original first step, preserved so its future worker loses no argument:

Fix a geometric point x of Spec A and write A_x for the local ring and A_x^{sh} for its strict henselization. Let F be the face of P consisting of elements whose images in A_x are units, put P_F={p-f}, and put P_x=P_F/F^{gp}. The face-sharpening statement [lem-trf-fs-face-sharpening] gives the fine saturated sharp quotient and the splitting P_F≅P_x⊕F^{gp}; the sharp-chart statement [lem-trf-log-regular-sharp-chart] supplies an etale-local chart b:P_x→A_x^{sh} inducing the characteristic identification. Apply [lem-trf-log-chart-face-tor-comparison] to the localized chart α:P→A_x^{sh}. It explicitly supplies the localization at F, the chosen section, the unit homomorphism u with α∘s=u·b, and the change-of-rings identification, carrying the ideal generated by H to the image ideal H_x. Thus, for R=Z[P] and I=Z[H], it identifies Tor^R_1(R/I,A_x^{sh}) with Tor^{Z[P_x]}_1(Z[P_x]/Z[H_x],A_x^{sh}). The latter vanishes by [lem-trf-kato-tor-independence-chart].

Clarification checked against the actual cited statements: `lem-trf-fs-face-sharpening` gives the free finite-rank group $P_x^{gp}$ but does not literally assert a splitting. Each lift of $q$ in $P_x$ lies in $P_F$: choose any monoid representative of $q$ and observe that the difference is in the unit subgroup $F^{gp}$. Restrict the section to $P_x$; together with the units it gives $P_F \cong P_x\oplus F^{gp}$. This is the elementary argument implicit in the old first step, not an additional unproved assumption. The characteristic identification follows from the chart pushout in `def-trf-fs-log-blowup`. The chosen sharp chart comes from an actual pointed etale neighbourhood by `lem-trf-log-regular-sharp-chart`, so the sharp Kato theorem applies; one need not assert log regularity anew for an unrelated chart on Spec of the stalk. The comparison node explicitly handles the localization, section, unit homomorphism and coefficient twist; it gives an isomorphism of Tor groups with precisely the two ring actions in use. These four existing supporting nodes carry the lower-level tasks; the new node carries their arbitrary-chart application at a geometric point. It has no global descent or tensor-injectivity conclusion.

## Argument retained for lem-trf-tor-strict-henselian-detection

The following is the entire original second step:

For the same R and I, let x lie over the prime $\mathfrak p$ of A, so $A_x=A_{\mathfrak p}$ is the local ring of Spec A at the image point. It is Noetherian: Spec A is locally Noetherian, so $A_x$ is a localization of a Noetherian ring $D$ of an affine open, and every ideal of a localization $S^{-1}D$ is the extension of an ideal of $D$ (Stacks 00FN; elementary). Hence [lem-sc-strict-henselization-dvr], applied to Y=Spec A and the geometric point x, says that the canonical map $\iota:A_x\to A_x^{sh}=\mathcal O^{sh}_{\operatorname{Spec}A,x}$ (the same strict henselization of [def-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-limit] used in Step 1 and in [lem-trf-kato-tor-independence-chart]) is a flat local homomorphism, so it is faithfully flat by [lem-pc-local-flat-faithful-flat-reflects-surjective]. The R-algebra structure of $A_x^{sh}$ through $\iota$ is the localized chart of Step 1. Apply [lem-trf-tor-flat-base-change] to $R\to A_x\to A_x^{sh}$ to obtain
$$Tor^R_1(R/I,A_x)\otimes_{A_x}A_x^{sh}\cong Tor^R_1(R/I,A_x^{sh}).$$
The right side is zero by Step 1, so [lem-sc-faithfully-flat-tensor-zero] gives Tor^R_1(R/I,A_x)=0. The coefficient-localization statement [lem-trf-tor-localization-on-spec] identifies the localization of Tor^R_1(R/I,A) at the prime $\mathfrak p$ with Tor^R_1(R/I,A_{\mathfrak p})=Tor^R_1(R/I,A_x)=0; since every prime of A is the image of a geometric point, the global Tor module is zero.

This task abstracts that argument to any R-module M and any n >= 0, with the same locally Noetherian scheme and the same geometric strict henselization. It also states the composed coefficient-localization and flat-base-change isomorphism, rather than suppressing either comparison. The local ring is a localization of a Noetherian affine-neighbourhood ring; ideals of that localization extend ideals of the original ring (the elementary argument in old step 2). Thus `lem-sc-strict-henselization-dvr` supplies a flat local map for the actual geometric strict henselization, and `lem-pc-local-flat-faithful-flat-reflects-surjective` supplies faithful flatness. The full statement of `lem-trf-tor-flat-base-change` applies to the fixed base R, with coefficient map A_p -> B_x, and its reflection consequence agrees with `lem-sc-faithfully-flat-tensor-zero`. `lem-trf-tor-localization-on-spec` gives both the preceding coefficient-localization isomorphism and detection of zero at every prime. Every prime has a geometric point by taking an algebraic closure of its residue field. The reverse direction of the equivalence follows from the very same comparison after localizing a zero module. Thus no finite generation or flatness of A over R is silently assumed.
