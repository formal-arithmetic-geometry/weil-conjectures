## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported item asserted that the tensor comparison belongs to $D^b_c(X,A_{E,n})$ for all bounded constructible $M,N$. This is false, even for identity pullback on a point. Take $E=\mathbf Q_\ell$, $n=2$, $A=\mathbf Z/\ell^2$, a separably closed field $k$ of characteristic different from $\ell$, $X=Y=\operatorname{Spec}k$, $f=1$, and $M=N=\mathbf F_\ell$ in degree zero. These are bounded constructible. The free resolution of $\mathbf F_\ell$ over $A$ has $A$ in every nonpositive degree, differential multiplication by $\ell$, and augmentation $A\to\mathbf F_\ell$. It is exact in negative degrees since the kernel and image of multiplication by $\ell$ are both $(\ell)$. Tensoring with $\mathbf F_\ell$ makes every differential zero. Thus $\operatorname{Tor}^A_i(\mathbf F_\ell,\mathbf F_\ell)=\mathbf F_\ell$ for every $i\geq0$. The derived tensor is not bounded. The same constant-sheaf resolution gives the counterexample on the pro-etale point.

The corrected finite assertion is the canonical comparison in the **ambient unbounded** category, for arbitrary derived inputs. In particular it still applies to all the original finite inputs; it does not make a boundedness claim about their output. The integral, rational and algebraically closed assertions and their coherences are retained. This is the statement of Stacks Tag 07A4, not an extra finite-Tor hypothesis added to its theorem. The unit already uses finite-Tor coefficients for its own purity conventions; its statement and route are unchanged. The current definition `def-weil-derived-pullback-tensor-hom` explicitly warns that finite operations are ambient and does not claim that they preserve $D^b_c$.

The only current direct citer is `def-weil-frobenius-coefficient-twists`. Its clause 5 uses the comparison only for $E$ and $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$; those clauses are unchanged and still supply that definition's displayed map. No citer is edited.
