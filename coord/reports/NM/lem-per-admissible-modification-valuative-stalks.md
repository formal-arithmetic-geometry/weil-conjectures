Step 1 uses the existing presentation for the coefficient colimits and exchange and the new local-ring node for a conservative subfamily, rings B,C, nonunit regular generators and their I-valuative property. Step 2 applies the abstract ring statement twice. Its principal-ideal hypothesis is exactly what Step 1 supplies; it does not assume that the original A is a domain or a valuation ring. Its conclusions give P,Q, the henselian rings S,S', valuation rings V,W, the residue-field identifications and the topologies. The phrase henselian valued field here means that its valuation ring is henselian, so it uses exactly the stated conclusion. Nontriviality follows from the explicitly nonzero nonunit images of the generators.

Step 3 takes from the completion statement precisely its isomorphisms B/phi^n = C/phi'^n. The inverse-image equality u^{-1}(Q)=P follows by testing zero in every displayed quotient, which constructs the injective map of valuation quotients and then their fraction fields. It also retains the quotient identifications after dividing by P,Q. No assertion about a field extension is imported from a new node.

Step 4 is elementary relative to Steps 2–3: the written calculation chooses a lift of c modulo phi'^(n+r) to approximate c/phi'^r and computes the inverse images of the neighbourhood ideals. This proves both density and the induced field topology, including denominators. It does not appeal to conservativity. The balls form a basis because Step 2 explicitly gives the phi-adic valuation topology and V[1/phi]=K.

Step 5 uses the existing `prop-weil-pbc-gabber-pair-comparison`, whose statement gives all q and torsion abelian coefficients without Noetherian, excellence, invertibility-of-order or constructibility assumptions. Its two pairs are (S,PS) and (S',QS'), not (B,phi B) and (C,phi' C): Step 2 identifies their maximal ideals and says both local rings are henselian. Define G_t by the actual inverse image along Spec K -> Spec S, and restrict the same sheaf for every q. The local annihilation condition is retained by inverse image, so the comparison applies to E and E'. This is an existing generic tool, declared in split_report.tool_uses.

Step 6 uses the new local-ring node only for the intermediate exchange pullback along S -> S'. It writes the square of localization and quotient homomorphisms on an element b/phi^r. The composition and naturality clauses of the existing `lem-weil-pbc-cohomology-pullback-functorial` make the cohomology square commute. Conjugating by the restriction isomorphisms gives the field pullback, with the coefficient sheaf identified by that same square. Thus compatibility is an argument, rather than an assertion that two calculations transport. Functoriality is the second generic tool declaration. The conservative family was supplied before the pointwise constructions; there is no spurious final use of conservativity.

Small cases: for a complete henselian discrete valuation ring B and a uniformizer phi, P=0, V=B and S=Frac(B), so the ring and cohomology calculations have their expected form. A henselian rank-two valuation ring with phi in the smaller positive Archimedean class tests P nonzero: the theorem uses the quotient B/P, not B itself as the valuation ring of the resulting field. For example use the composite valuation on k((x))((y)), with phi=x, where P consists of elements of positive y-value and the valuation quotient is k[[x]]. The identity comparison tests that density and all exchange maps reduce to the identity. Unit or nilpotent I gives empty closed-fibre blowup systems in the relevant degenerate cases, so the conservative family can be empty; no invalid nontrivial field is constructed at such a point. In a nonempty chart, regularity and the nonunit condition exclude phi=0, nilpotent phi and an invertible phi. The zero coefficient sheaf and q=0 satisfy the same maps and formulas. No higher-rank valuation is silently treated as a DVR: only the quotient's explicitly stated phi-adic topology is used.
