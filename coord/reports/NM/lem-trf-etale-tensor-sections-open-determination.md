Step 1 uses `def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category` for sectionwise module actions and semilinear restrictions and `def-weil-pbc-etale-site` for covers. An open cover of an open subscheme of V is a jointly surjective etale family over the corresponding object, so gluing G on that cover gives exactly gluing G_u. This is a direct instance of the definitions, not a general change-of-topology theorem. The etale object u remains arbitrary.

Step 2 uses `lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-module-pullback-restriction` for an O_A-linear, unit-compatible kappa; `lem-sc-etale-qcoherent-presheaf-functorial` for the specific rho and normalized c, not merely existence of some pullback iso; `new-etale-tensor-open-section-iso` for its explicitly canonical open comparison; `new-etale-tensor-open-section-naturality` for the restriction equation of that comparison; and `lem-sc-left-adjoint-composition-iso-associativity` for the displayed three-map composition identity. I read each statement. The naturality node refers to the iso node's particular e, and the iso node specifies the unit-compatible comparison, so I invert it rather than substituting an unspecified additive isomorphism. Associativity is applied to T,V,A,B with right-adjoint compositions the ordinary direct images; these agree sectionwise on every open, so the required right-adjoint equality holds. Pulling back the natural c past the unit is ordinary naturality, explicitly noted. Thus the composite e is compatible with the prescribed rho. All scheme-module maps involved are linear over functions on A; additivity alone would not suffice.

Step 3 uses only the definition of module sheaf morphism, with the compatibility calculation from step 2. I wrote the naturality square as an equality of the three actual composites. This forms a morphism a of Zariski modules, to which the scheme adjunction can legitimately apply. I do not assume P_M is an etale sheaf or use the parent topology comparison to manufacture a.

Step 4 again uses precisely the unit equations of the open comparison and normalized-composition node. I checked the two factorizations have the same source A and the same composite to T; the arrow v is an arrow of the small etale category because its source and target are etale over T and it is a T-morphism. The component eta^u_W lands in N(u^{-1}W), not in global sections of V. The text explicitly keeps that preimage and computes both sides in Gamma(A,u_A* M). The right-hand kappa_W is evaluated on all W, and the left-hand kappa for j_A is evaluated on A. This avoids the wrong-object and unnormalized-comparison errors in E4/E5.

Step 5 cites `lem-pc-module-pullback-adjunction`, whose actual statement gives a natural bijection from Hom(u* M,G_u) to Hom(M,u_*G_u) and defines the sectionwise pushforward and its scalar action. Its image is the unit followed by pushforward of a. I wrote the resulting formula on each W and each m; naturality of theta along v and the given hypothesis, rather than an unproved assertion that an identification “transports”, give equal adjuncts. No extra flatness, injectivity, or quasi-coherence is used.

Step 6 uses equality of sheaf-module morphisms componentwise and cancellation of an isomorphism. On the whole-open scheme one may have a canonical isomorphism rather than definitional equality with V; naturality along that isomorphism is stated before cancellation, so the proof does not depend on a representation of the whole open subscheme. Equality of all components is equality of a natural transformation, including its already specified arrow squares; it is not an extra functoriality theorem.

I tested empty T, empty etale V, empty W, zero M and zero G. Empty opens have zero section modules and the same comparison isomorphisms; there is no nonemptiness assumption. I tested the logical roles of M and G by allowing M non-quasi-coherent and V non-affine/non-separated: the argument uses only sheaf module pullback and adjunction on opens, so neither an affine section tensor formula nor finite generation is smuggled in. All morphisms are module-presheaf maps; dropping linearity would invalidate the adjunction construction, and the statement correctly retains it.
