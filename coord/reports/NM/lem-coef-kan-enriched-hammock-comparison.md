## Findings and changes

The original statement is correct and was preserved, including the constant marking, the three specified functors, the strict square and the universe convention. There is no STATEMENT-DISPUTED. The original proof was empty. A naive use of pair-homotopy-invariance on the ordinary vertex category would fail its mappingwise-equivalence hypothesis; the split does not make that inference.

The degree-k category of simplicial sets is not assumed to be a model category. Instead its ordinary relative localization is compared with that of simplicial sets by the explicit functor T_k sending X to X times the standard k-simplex and a parameterized arrow f to (f,parameter). Its natural transformation e from the identity to c_k T_k is not marked. The proof deliberately verifies its invertibility after localization using the marked projection c_k(pi_X) and the equation c_k(pi_X)e_X = identity. Mistaking the projection for a natural transformation on the degree-k category would be false; only e is asserted natural there. Projections are natural on the ordinary composite T_k c_k.

The Kan replacement is enriched, not merely a functorial ordinary replacement. Its formula uses the canonical finite-product comparisons of Ex-infinity and the unit on the parameter simplex; the formula is checked against parameter operators, composition and unit naturality.

The only new lemma is a general change-of-marking result. It states no Kan-model comparison, none of a,b,j, and no strict square. In the application the proof first calculates the identity-marked hammock category explicitly and separately proves that weak maps of Kan complexes are invertible in the component category. The new node is thus an independent localization tool, not a renamed clause of this item. The other comparisons and the strict square are proved in the item.
