Hypotheses and objects, clause by clause:

- Four arbitrary commutative rings: `{A : Type u} {C : Type v} {B : Type w}
  {D : Type z}` with all four `[CommRing ...]`. The universes are independent.
- The two ring maps out of A: `[Algebra A C] [Algebra A B]`; every commutative-ring
  homomorphism determines exactly such an algebra structure. `[Algebra B D]` gives the
  specified B-algebra D. No A-algebra structure on D or extra tower is assumed.
- Nonnegative d,r and the displayed finite basis/list: `{d r : ℕ}`,
  `e : Module.Basis (Fin d) A C`, `k : Fin r → C`, `K : Ideal C`, and
  `hK : K = Ideal.span (Set.range k)`. Neither d nor r is required positive.
- T is literally `C ⊗[A] B`. The root `id` application substitutes the fixed
  `Algebra.TensorProduct.rightAlgebra` for the lambda's instance argument. It is beta
  reduction, not a hypothesis or quantification over alternative algebra structures.
  Thus `θ : C ⊗[A] B ≃ₐ[B] B × D` is precisely the stated B-algebra isomorphism with
  the B-action through the second factor. No extra map f or compatibility hypothesis
  is introduced; the node's proof constructs f when needed.
- J is `Ideal.map (includeLeftRingHom : C →+* C ⊗[A] B) K`: by Ideal.map this is the
  ideal generated by all `k ⊗ 1` with k in K, rather than only a B-module image.
- H is `splitComplementIdeal θ.toRingEquiv K`, the ideal image of K under the composite
  of includeLeftRingHom, θ and RingHom.snd. This is exactly the ideal generated by the
  second components in the statement.
- q_A is `(Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ A K).toLinearMap`. q_B is the real RingHom composite
  `splitComplementQuotient θ.toRingEquiv K`, with quotient codomain D/H. The conclusion
  `Function.Surjective ...` renders the displayed two-headed arrow q_B; q_A is Mathlib's
  canonical surjective ideal-quotient map.
- `splitTensorRelations e k (j,i)` is exactly `(k_j ⊗ 1)(e_i ⊗ 1)`.
  `splitComplementRelations ... none` is exactly `θ.symm (1,0)` and the `some (j,i)`
  values are exactly the w_ji. Option gives a finite indexed list with the one extra
  epsilon entry; it allows duplicates of vector values, as the source does.

Conclusions, clause by clause:

1. `LinearMap.ker (Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ A K).toLinearMap = Submodule.span A
   (Set.range (fun a : Fin r × Fin d ↦ k a.1 * e a.2))` is the exact A-kernel equality.
2. `J.restrictScalars B = Submodule.span B (Set.range (splitTensorRelations e k))`
   is the exact B-module equality for J.
3. `(RingHom.ker q_B).restrictScalars B = Submodule.span B {θ.symm (1,0)} ⊔
   J.restrictScalars B` means ker(q_B) = B epsilon + J. Supremum of submodules is their
   sum, not a direct sum assertion.
4. The next equality with `Submodule.span B (Set.range (splitComplementRelations ...))`
   gives precisely the span of the union of epsilon and the w_ji.
5. `Module.FinitePresentation A (C ⧸ K)` and
   `Module.FinitePresentation B (D ⧸ splitComplementIdeal ... K)` are ordinary module
   finite presentations, not finite presentation as algebras.
6. For C/K the canonical surjection from A^d is
   `(Ideal.Quotient.mkₐ A K).toLinearMap.comp e.equivFun.symm.toLinearMap`.
   Its `presentationFittingIdeal 0` equals the ideal span of the range of
   `t : Fin d → Fin r × Fin d ↦ Matrix.det (fun i l ↦
   e.repr (k (t i).1 * e (t i).2) l)`.
   Thus rows are exactly the coordinates of an ordered d-tuple of k_j e_i.
7. For D/H, `splitComplementPresentation θ.toRingEquiv e K` is the linear map from B^d
   sending its standard basis vectors to q_B(e_i tensor 1). Its definition uses
   `Pi.basisFun.constr`, so there is no arbitrary chosen map. Since θ is B-linear,
   this is q_B composed with the canonical scalar-extended basis parametrization.
   Its zeroth presentation ideal equals the span of determinants indexed by
   `t : Fin d → Option (Fin r × Fin d)`. Coordinates are
   `(e.baseChange B).repr (TensorProduct.comm A C B (...))`, which are exactly the
   coordinates in v_i=e_i tensor 1: baseChange uses 1 tensor e_i, and comm flips factors.
8. The last two `.FG` assertions state finite generation of both zeroth ideals.
   These are the node's presentation-independent I_0(C/K) and I_0(D/H), expressed using
   the two displayed canonical rank-d surjections. This uses the recorded definition
   `LinearMap.presentationFittingIdeal` and its independence theorem directly, not a
   second definition of Fitting ideals. No finite-module witness or arbitrary
   presentation is added as an assumption. The other existing interface,
   `LinearMap.fittingIdealZero`, chooses an arbitrary finite free surjection and is
   identified with these ideals by the same independence theorem; it is not redefined.
9. Tuple indices are arbitrary functions, not injections: repetitions are allowed.
   At d=0 each tuple domain is Fin 0 and has exactly one function, including when the
   relation index is empty. Matrix.det on the empty matrix is 1. Thus the empty
   determinant convention is retained by the actual Matrix.det definition and the
   determinant-ideal equations, rather than excluded or replaced by an empty set.
10. There is no Noetherian, flatness, nontriviality, domain or positive-rank hypothesis.

  d>0 the first determinant family is empty and its ideal is zero, as for a free
  positive-rank module. Repeated epsilon rows give zero determinants when d>1.
