### Former step 1: lem-trf-split-exterior-comparison-localization

Projectivity of $W$ lifts $\mathrm{id}_W$ through the surjection $p$, giving an $R$-linear $s:W\to V$ with $ps=1$. The map $t:U\oplus W\to V$, $(u,w)\mapsto i(u)+s(w)$, is an isomorphism: given $v$, the element $v-s(pv)$ is in $\ker p=\operatorname{im}i$, so there is a unique $u$ with $i(u)=v-s(pv)$; this gives its inverse. Localizing these mutually inverse formulas preserves the splitting, so no assertion about preservation of an arbitrary exact sequence is needed. Choose finite standard free covers for $U$ and $W$. Their pairwise products give a common finite standard cover: if $1=\sum a_jf_j=\sum b_kg_k$, then $1=\sum_{j,k}a_jb_kf_jg_k$. On each member $A=R_h$, the localized modules $U_h,W_h$ have finite bases of lengths $r,s$, and $t_h$ gives a concatenated basis of $V_h$ of length $r+s$.

Inputs read: def-weil-pbc-projective-module, lem-weil-pbc-finite-projective-standard-free-cover.

### Former step 2: lem-trf-projective-determinant-chart-descent

First make precise the determinant modules used in the statement. For a finite projective $E$, choose a finite standard free cover and, on a member $D(f_a)$ where $E_{f_a}$ has basis length $n_a$, take $L_a=\bigwedge^{n_a}_{R_{f_a}}E_{f_a}$. It is free of rank one by the exterior-power basis theorem. Exterior powers commute with localization. Thus on a nonempty overlap these lines identify by the exterior power of the identity of $E_{f_af_b}$. The two basis lengths agree there: if $n_a<n_b$, the basis theorem would make $\bigwedge^{n_b}E_{f_af_b}$ both zero and free of rank one, forcing $R_{f_af_b}=0$; interchange $a,b$ for the other inequality. On a zero overlap all modules and maps are zero and the identification is unique. The induced exterior map sends a wedge to the wedge of the images: it is obtained by applying the map to every factor of the tensor algebra, which preserves the relations $x\otimes x$. Consequently identity and composition of these maps follow on pure wedges, which span. The transition isomorphisms therefore satisfy the cocycle condition, including empty overlaps.

### Former step 3: lem-trf-projective-determinant-chart-descent

On $X=\operatorname{Spec}R$, form the associated module sheaves $\widetilde{L_a}$ on the standard opens of the preceding step. Their restrictions are the corresponding localized modules, so the transition isomorphisms give sheaf gluing data. Glue these sheaves by the module-sheaf gluing theorem, obtaining $\mathcal D_E$. It is quasi-coherent by the affine-local definition, since every $\widetilde{L_a}$ is quasi-coherent. Put $D_E=\Gamma(X,\mathcal D_E)$. The affine tilde/Gamma counit identifies $\widetilde{D_E}$ with $\mathcal D_E$, and the standard-open section formula gives $(D_E)_{f_a}\cong L_a$. Since these are free of rank one, the standard-cover finite-projectivity criterion makes $D_E$ finite projective and locally of rank one. Define $\det_R(E)=D_E$. Two covers have the common refinement by pairwise products; the transition identifications there give mutually inverse glued sheaf maps, hence mutually inverse module maps on global sections. Their cocycle equations show independence of refinements. This is exactly the construction by local top wedges stipulated in the statement, rather than an additional choice of determinant object.

### Former step 4: lem-trf-split-exterior-comparison-localization

On a simultaneous free chart $A=R_h$, let $t_h:U_h\oplus W_h\xrightarrow{\sim}V_h$ be the splitting isomorphism of step 1. Invert the top-exterior direct-sum isomorphism and follow it by $\bigwedge^{r+s}t_h$. This gives an isomorphism $\gamma_h:\bigwedge^rU_h\otimes_A\bigwedge^sW_h\to\bigwedge^{r+s}V_h$. Here $\bigwedge^{r+s}t_h$ and the map induced by $t_h^{-1}$ are inverse, since their composites fix every spanning pure wedge, directly from the tensor-algebra quotient construction. The direct-sum theorem characterizes the inverse on arbitrary pure wedges; consequently $\gamma_h$ sends $(u_1\wedge\cdots\wedge u_r)\otimes(w_1\wedge\cdots\wedge w_s)$ to $i(u_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge i(u_r)\wedge s(w_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge s(w_s)$. Thus this is an actual map of exterior powers and an isomorphism, not merely a prescription on chosen generators.

Inputs read: lem-sc-exterior-top-direct-sum, def-weil-pbc-exterior-algebra.

### Former step 5: lem-trf-split-exterior-comparison-localization

For arbitrary lifts $\widetilde w_j$ of $w_j$, exactness on the split chart gives $\widetilde w_j=s(w_j)+i(a_j)$ with $a_j\in U_h$. Expand the displayed wedge by multilinearity. Every term except the one using all $s(w_j)$ contains at least $r+1$ factors in $i(U_h)$. Expand these factors in a basis of the free rank-$r$ module $U_h$; each resulting summand repeats a basis vector, so alternation makes it zero. Hence the displayed formula agrees with $\gamma_h$ for all lifts, and choosing a different splitting gives exactly the same map. This works for arbitrary tuples $u_j,w_j$, not just basis tuples. The vanishing uses alternation $x\wedge x=0$, so it remains valid in characteristic two.

### Former step 6: lem-trf-split-exterior-comparison-localization

The construction on each chart uses the canonical direct-sum exterior map and the specified maps $i,p$; the splitting has just been eliminated. It therefore has no remaining dependence on either chosen basis. Explicitly, expand any replacement tuple in the original basis. Multilinearity and alternation expand both the input wedges and the output wedge with identical coefficients (a term with repeated indices vanishes, and interchanging two indices changes the sign in both expressions). Thus the same linear map satisfies the formula in the replacement bases. For further localization $A\to B$, the exterior-power localization theorem sends each pure wedge to the wedge of its images, and tensor localization sends a pure tensor to the tensor of its images. The two localized maps therefore have the identical formula on pure tensors of pure wedges, which span, so they agree. In particular, on every overlap of the standard cover the two maps $\gamma_h$ agree under the transition identifications of the determinant modules.

### Former step 7: lem-trf-projective-determinant-chart-descent

Write $P=D_U\otimes_R D_W$ and $Q=D_V$. By tensor localization and the determinant identifications of step 3, the local maps are isomorphisms $P_h\to Q_h$, and step 6 says that they agree on overlaps. For $x\in P$, their values on $x/1$ form a compatible family in the $Q_h$. The standard-cover exactness theorem gives a unique $y\in Q$ with these values; define $\gamma(x)=y$. Uniqueness gives additivity and $R$-linearity. It also shows that the localization of $\gamma$ is $\gamma_h$, first on $x/1$ and then on every fraction $x/h^e$. Apply the same argument to the compatible inverses $\gamma_h^{-1}$ to obtain $\delta:Q\to P$. Both $\delta\gamma$ and $\gamma\delta$ localize to the identity on the finite standard cover. Injectivity in the cover exactness theorem makes them the identity globally. This proves the required canonical isomorphism. Refining the cover produces the same map, since its localized values are prescribed by the same formula and that theorem also gives uniqueness.

Inputs read: lem-weil-pbc-localization-tensor-product, lem-weil-pbc-cover-module-exact.

### Former step 8: lem-trf-projective-determinant-chart-descent (determinant zero), lem-trf-split-exterior-comparison-localization (empty wedges, zero ring), and the localization/descent clauses of both

For $E=0$ use the empty basis: its local determinant is $\bigwedge^0(0)=R$ with generator $1$, and all transition maps are identities, so $D_0=R$ canonically. If $r=0$, injectivity and exactness identify $V_h$ with $W_h$ and the formula is the ordinary empty-left-wedge identification $A\otimes\det(W_h)\to\det(V_h)$. If $R=0$, every unital $R$-module is zero; all asserted maps are the unique isomorphism of zero modules. Finally, the localization calculation of step 6 holds after localizing any finite cover, since the images of its elements still generate the unit ideal. Uniqueness in step 7 therefore proves that the global isomorphism commutes with further localization as asserted.

### Additional socle comparison needed by the existing definition

On a free chart, expand an exterior-algebra element in its basis indexed by subsets. Multiplication by each basis vector annihilates exactly the terms already containing that vector. Hence the simultaneous annihilator is precisely the full top-wedge line, including degree zero. For a finite projective E choose finitely many generators. Under exterior localization an element of the localized socle has a denominator; its products with these finitely many generators are zero after localization. Clear their finitely many annihilation denominators simultaneously. The resulting numerator lies in the original socle, which proves surjectivity of its localization into the localized socle; equality is inherited from exterior localization. The free-chart socle/top-wedge identification follows. The sheaf-glued line of former steps 2--3 is therefore canonically the existing socle module, because the identifications agree with their common exterior-algebra inclusions on overlaps. This work belongs to the chart-descent node; it is not claimed to be proved by a definition alone.
