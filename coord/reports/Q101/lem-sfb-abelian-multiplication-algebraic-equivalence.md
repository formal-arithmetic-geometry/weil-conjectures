I checked the unchanged item against Milne AV I Remark 10.19, printed p.50, and read Corollary 5.4 and its actual recurrence proof, pp.22–23. This proves n squared on the Neron-Severi quotient for arbitrary invertible modules. Symmetry and ampleness are not required of N, and no integer is required to be invertible in the base field. I read the new node's sources in I section 8 as detailed above: Proposition 8.4(b) is precisely invariance under every translation, and the subsequent Pic^0 construction realizes these classes by connected smooth families. This is the one non-elementary comparison absent from the existing statements, not a clause of the item's n-multiplication claim.

Step 1 cites the actual tensor-pullback node, whose statement gives closure under pullback and tensor, distribution over tensor, and composition. Tensoring a generating equivalence family with a fixed line and dualizing it explicitly proves the congruence needed for the quotient G. The algebra in line classes, including dual cancellation locally in rank one, is elementary. The pullback under the constant identity map is the constant one-dimensional fibre line, and choosing a basis makes it trivial. Thus c_0 is genuinely zero; this does not assume any property of a nonzero multiplication isogeny.

Step 2 uses the statement of polarization-calculus, not its proof: it explicitly states phi_{[-1]^*N}=phi_N and that phi_L(a) is the isomorphism class of t_a^*L tensor L inverse. Equality at each a is therefore an isomorphism of the two difference lines. Their tensor quotient is t_a^*D tensor D inverse by the tensor-pullback node; cancelling gives t_a^*D isomorphic to D. The base is algebraically closed, as both polarization-calculus and the next criterion require. No claim about scheme-theoretic kernels or reduction to closed points is needed.

Step 3 applies exactly the new node's implication to this actual homogeneous line D on the same A over the same field. It yields algebraic triviality in the specified smooth-connected-family sense, so c_{-1}=c_1. It holds for an arbitrary invertible module on an abelian variety over any field. The four sum maps after x maps to ([r]x,x,-x) are r+1, r-1, 0 and r; tensor-pullback composition gives exactly the displayed identity. The constant factor is trivial by step 1. I checked the sign of the c_{r-1} term and used c_{-1}=c_1 only after step 3. This is Milne's equation (2), passed to algebraic-equivalence classes.
