Step 1 takes constant-rank neighbourhoods from the degree node and actual affine bases from the principal-basis-cover node, after restricting to each degree piece; neither requires a Noetherian base. The fibre-product/base-change identities are written out on test schemes. Step 2 takes universal closed containment from the new locus node. The source equalizer is a closed subscheme because it is the pullback of the separated diagonal; the quotient calculation spells out this base-change fact. The locus therefore gives exactly the diagonal, not just its underlying closed point set.

Step 3 takes precisely the quotient and universal representing property of the coefficient node; finite generation uses finite I, whereas finite presentation also uses finite K. Step 4 uses scheme coproducts and the finite-affine-coproduct spectrum node for finite unions. It uses 05XR on the actual fibre over each scheme test of H_J to W, and 05XQ on the pullback of the surjective open cover of Z over the finite family. Finiteness ensures affine, quasi-compact preimages of affine test opens, so only finitely many summands are needed. These statements say exactly the section existence and etaleness used here. No separation assumption on Z enters. Step 5 applies the flat-lfp-surjective source-descent node to the coproduct of the H_J; etaleness supplies flatness and local finite presentation. Step 6 cites the affine-opens finite-presentation theorem twice and the finite-coproduct lfp theorem, then the full jointly-surjective-family locality statement 036N. This avoids assuming a finite product of presented algebras without justification.

Step 7 uses the smoothness and formal-smoothness definitions exactly as stated for affine first-order thickenings. Both base-changed tests are affine by the finite-morphism permanence node, and the square-zero ideal remains square zero after base change. Step 8 invokes only the algebraic-space existence statement of the existing restriction node. The universal open containment formula makes each affine restriction an open subspace; the universal-homeomorphism definition gives the one-point field fibre needed to prove coverage. The finite affine cover exists because Y over the affine finite-family source is quasi-compact. The affine-test node supplies the precise notion and locality of quasi-compactness for intermediate spaces.

Step 9 uses finite-etale trivialization on constant-rank pieces, quasi-compact base change for the scheme factors, and the affine-test node for the intermediate space factors. Finite products are covered by products of finitely many affine charts over an affine base. Step 10 applies the generic-factorization node only over affine integral reduced pieces of the Noetherian base, and checks all its hypotheses. Step 11 is the ordinary Noetherian closed-subset induction written out, including the empty case, reduction, finitely many irreducible components, shrinking to quasi-compact generic opens, and the finite-union subcover argument. It uses no dimension bound and never infers quasi-compactness of a total space merely from its individual fibres.
