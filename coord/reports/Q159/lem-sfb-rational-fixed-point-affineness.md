For the future split of rational-fixed-section-jets, here is the local argument that its source abbreviates. Work first on an inversion-stable open neighbourhood W of e contained in V. For u in W(k), alpha_u and alpha_(u inverse) are inverse germs at P: their compositions agree with the identity on Y, and hence near P by integrality and separatedness. Equality in the group-law diagrams follows in the same way, on the open sets where the compositions exist. For any g in G(k), choose v with v and gv in W (equivalently use W intersect gW; after choosing a consistently oriented factorization g = u v inverse). Such an intersection is nonempty because the smooth connected group is irreducible. The composition of the two regular germs at P represents alpha_g. Varying the first factor in an open of W gives a representative on an open neighbourhood of (g,P), not just a pointwise map. The largest domain therefore contains every (g,P). Closed points detect the complement in G times {P}, so it contains that entire subscheme. The resulting morphism on the section is the constant P: both maps agree on its original open V and G is reduced. This argument does not assert an action on all of X.

Now on the domain containing the entire section, the ideal of the section is preserved, as a morphism of ideal sheaves, because alpha fixes the section scheme-theoretically. Its (n+1)-st power is preserved as well. Thus restriction to G times X_n factors through X_n; all these products lie in the domain because nilpotent thickening preserves support. The action laws hold on the thickening: the corresponding maps near G times G times {P} represent the same rational map on an integral source; the identity follows likewise. The finite local quotient is a finite-dimensional nonzero k-algebra: choose a finite set of generators of the maximal ideal of the Noetherian local ring, then the successive quotients m^j/m^(j+1) are spanned by their degree-j monomials over R/m = k. Inverse pullback gives the homomorphism convention, with inverse supplied by group inversion. Quotients intertwine the representations. The ordinary scheme kernel is closed; the compatibility makes the kernels descend. This uses neither smoothness of X nor affineness of G. Its future split will have to cite the generic Noetherian, density, and kernel inputs; this report is not claiming those facts are elementary item steps.

For constant-family-jet-equality, the useful geometric argument is as follows. The equalizer F of f and g is a closed subscheme of E because Z is separated. On affine parameter and X charts near a point of T times {P}, its ideal lies in every power of the ideal of the constant section, since it vanishes on all X_n. Local Krull intersection (00IQ, with 00IP as used in its proof) annihilates this ideal in the stalks along the section; coherence gives an open neighbourhood W of the entire section on which f = g. Such a W is schematically dense in the constant family T times X, including nilpotents in T. One way to justify this last input when the lemma is split is the associated-point formula for the flat projection to T: X is geometrically integral over the algebraically closed field, so every associated point in each fibre is its generic point. W meets every fibre nontrivially and hence contains those associated points. This is why neither mere topological density nor reduction of T is used. Then W is schematically dense in E, and separatedness gives f = g on E. Alternatively, on an affine integral chart A containing P, the maps A to R and R to the inverse system R/m^n are injective; scalar extension by k-vector spaces and a finite-coefficient argument give the same formal detection before localization. This is a real geometric lemma, not just 00IQ applied without the needed density argument. The item's step 3 cites this entire explicitly stated lemma.
