I read Conrad's Theorem 3.2.6 proof on pp.94-95 rather than using Corollary 3.2.7 to assume a torus etale locally, which would be circular here. I checked the proposed inputs against Theorem 2.3.1 and Remark 2.3.5 on pp.67-71, and the p.95 conjugacy argument. Theorem 2.3.1 treats subgroups of multiplicative type, so a supplied split torus is within its hypotheses without reductivity or maximality. Its quotient is quasi-affine, not necessarily affine. Remark 2.3.5 explicitly says the normalizer quotient map is smooth. The sheaf quotient is by right cosets; N need not be normal in G, and X is not asserted to be a group. The new conjugacy input uses only the part of Theorem 3.2.6's proof after it explicitly says that only smoothness and affineness of G will be used. That argument assumes both tori already exist and works by finite-presentation descent, approximation and complete-local conjugacy; it does not invoke the parameter-scheme theorem as a black box. It handles geometric maximality, not maximality only among tori over the original ring.

For step 1 I opened the full statements of lem-pc-fpqc-descent, def-sbc-closed-immersion-ideal-sheaf, lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-ideal-closed-subscheme, and lem-pc-closed-imm-base-change. The first is an equivalence including full faithfulness, not just existence of a descended module. The latter two provide the actual closed scheme and the flat-pullback identity of its ideals; these cannot be replaced by a definition of closed immersion alone. The vanishing of the multiplication into the quotient verifies the ideal condition, and the same faithful descent checks the Hopf operations and their equations. I also checked the covering axioms and the representable fpqc descent statement used to check uniqueness of group morphisms. Relative tori are defined to split on an fppf covering with ranks allowed to vary, so their condition is local by composing the splitting coverings. If a descended geometric torus had a strictly larger torus, that strict inclusion would persist after a faithfully flat field extension and contradict maximality on a lifted geometric point. This makes the geometric-maximality check explicit.

For step 2 I read the existence clause in lem-sfb-reductive-borel-pair-structure (there exists a split maximal torus), the entire geometric-torus-fppf-lift statement (an affine flat locally finitely presented neighborhood and a marked lift), and the second, split-torus clause in reductive-torus-fibre-openness.
