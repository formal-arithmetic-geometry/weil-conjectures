## Review by Q159

I read the item against Temkin Proposition 3.3.5, especially the first paragraph and the source-side shrinking paragraph on printed p.25, and Lemma 2.1.4 on p.8. I read BMT Definition 7.2, the proof of Theorem 7.4 (image meets the smooth locus), and Theorem 7.5 (rational approximation of arbitrary characteristic-zero varieties). The coefficient lemma is strictly a theorem about a field and its coefficient subrings, with no singular pairs, Cartier divisor or fibre morphism. In BMT's field case, replacing models by schematic images gives subrings of k; their maps to k remain regular. Between finite-type rational models regular transitions are smooth. The diagram must be selected with the smooth transition order; all smooth rational subalgebras ordered by arbitrary inclusion would be an unjustified stronger assertion. The new statement expressly avoids that claim. The smooth-image lemma isolates Lemma 2.1.4's local cancellation plus finite-presentation smoothness. A flat local map is faithfully flat; the composite regularity therefore descends at every point of the image. Taking the union of the resulting smooth neighborhoods gives W. No global surjectivity or finite presentation of p is used.

For step 1 I opened the statements of the coefficient-system, finite-type-to-finite-presentation, fp-algebra-descent and tensor-colimit nodes. The descent node really gives an algebra model and the tensor isomorphism; the tensor node gives element representation and zero detection, with the common-universe restriction, which the proof explicitly enforces. I checked Stacks 00FP and 05N9 (algebra.tex lemma-Noetherian-finite-type-is-finite-presentation and lemma-colimit-category-fp-algebras). A field is Noetherian directly from its two ideals. Lifting the chosen equation requires only its finite coefficient list, not a new descent theorem.

For step 2 I read the exact smooth-base-change-and-composition and smooth-implies-flat statements, 00T4, 00TD and 00TA. The finite-type-Noetherian node is used over Q and over k, not over a possibly non-Noetherian arbitrary ring. Localization-Noetherian covers the fibre localization, and the finite-type-Noetherian node covers its quotient as a zero-generator finite-type algebra. The smooth-colimit-regular node, 07EP in more-algebra.tex, requires Noetherian fibre rings; those are explicitly supplied. The scheme regularity definition says precisely flatness and geometric regularity of the fibres.

For step 3 I checked 00IK's finite generation of the kernel in a finite module, 00HD's preservation of exactness under tensor by a flat module, and 05LI's finite-list vanishing in a tensor colimit (opened in algebra.tex). These nodes actually state the required finiteness, exactness and vanishing, rather than only definitions. N need not be finite as a module over the coefficient ring: it is finite over A_a, so killing its A_a-generators kills its tensor base change. Flatness is over R_a, where the transitions are smooth and the projection to k is regular. This corrects the tempting but invalid shortcut that a non-zero-divisor automatically descends along an arbitrary coefficient model.

For step 4 I read the characteristic-zero separability node, the regular-over-perfect-field-smooth node and its source in varieties.tex Tag 0B8X, the fixed-map smooth-colimit descent node, and 0C0B/0CAQ in algebra.tex. The source ring for this application is a fixed R_a and the fixed algebra is A_a[1/pi_a]; it is finitely presented. Its limit base change is the smooth punctured k-variety. Smooth descent yields a stage, and smooth base change and composition give all later punctured rational models smooth. Smooth-over-field-regular-local, 00TT, then gives the required absence of singularities away from the divisor. No assertion about openness of a singular locus in an arbitrary nonexcellent scheme is smuggled in.

For step 5 I opened the affine-transition limit node (01YX/01YW in limits.tex), the same smooth base-change node and the same 07EP node. Quotients commute with this colimit by the displayed ring-map universal property, not an uncited limit theorem. Powers extend exactly since the equation is chosen. All thick fibre rings remain Noetherian, including the zero ring. Fibre projections therefore meet 07EP's hypotheses. This does not assume that truncation itself is a regular closed immersion.

For step 6 I read the full statement of finite-presentation-algebra-factors-filtered-colimit (00QO and 07C3), and algebra.tex's proof by lifting generators and relations. I use it on E, not on an algebra over the wrong coefficient field. Every characteristic-zero scheme morphism fixes Q because it fixes integers and their inverses. The equations t=SU and S=tV encode both containments of ideals; equality of the descended S with the distinguished source generator is separately forced using the zero-detection statement of the tensor node. The target index a remains fixed throughout. At m=1 both distinguished ideals can be zero; U,V can then be zero and the argument still works.

For step 7 I opened regular-composition (07QI), checked that its Noetherian composite-fibre hypothesis follows from the Noetherian source fibre algebra, and applied the new smooth-image statement only to locally Noetherian schemes and a finitely presented finite-stage map. I read the local regularity and permanence counterparts 07C0 and 07NT in more-algebra.tex, and the pointwise smoothness criterion 01V9 in morphisms.tex. The open W in the closed thick fibre is lifted to an open U of the ambient model by the subspace topology; the map factors through an OPEN containing its image, so the known failure of factorization into an arbitrary locally closed subscheme is irrelevant. Ideal equality restricts to W. No lift of g from the thick fibre to a map of ambient pairs is claimed.

For step 8 I checked finite products, idempotent equations and the same finite-presentation/zero-detection statements. A map from a source component need not land in one target component; the product-algebra argument handles the resulting clopen decomposition and descends its finite idempotent equations. The zero ring handles an empty union and the empty open handles an empty fibre. For k=Q the coefficient system may be constant. For a unit pi all fibres are empty and the punctured open is all of X, which is regular by hypothesis. For a nonreduced test A=k[x,e]/(e^2,xe) with pi=x, pi is a zero divisor, so the item correctly excludes it; A=k[e]/(e^2) with pi=1 is excluded by the singular-locus condition. The proof never adds reducedness as a hypothesis.
