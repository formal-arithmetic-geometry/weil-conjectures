### lem-sfb-continuous-qbar-lisse-heart-closure

Returned step 1:
The scalar ring is $\Lambda(*)=\Omega$. A finitely presented vector space has a finite basis, so $R_T\otimes_\Omega V\cong R_T^r$, and conversely $R_T^r=R_T\otimes_\Omega\Omega^r$. Thus finite constant local models are precisely lisse modules, including rank zero [sk-def-sfb-constructible, def-sfb-lisse-coefficient-module]. The underlying space of $X$ is Noetherian by [lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-scheme-closed-subsets-minimal]. Each locally closed subspace is Noetherian (intersect a descending chain of closed sets with the subspace), and has finitely many irreducible components by [lem-sc-noetherian-finite-irreducible-components]. These assertions also apply to every stratum used below.

Returned step 2:
Verify the two coefficient hypotheses of [lem-zl-t-admissible-dualizable-truncations] for the actual all-continuous ring. Every ideal of the field $\Omega$ is zero or $\Omega$, hence is finitely presented; every finitely presented $\Omega$-module is finite free, hence has a length-zero finite projective resolution. For every extremally disconnected profinite $P$, the section ring $\Gamma(P,\Lambda)=C(P,\Omega)$ is an $\Omega$-vector space. A choice of basis identifies its tensor functor with a direct sum of copies of the identity, so it is flat over $\Omega$. This is t-admissibility as explicitly defined in the cited statement; it does not assert that continuous maps factor through a finite subfield. On any stratum $T$, that statement therefore identifies the cohomology of every dualizable object with finite constant local models. Its geometric hypothesis holds by step 1.

Returned step 3:
Let $u:L\to M$ be a morphism of lisse $R_T$-modules on a stratum. By [lem-zl-condensed-dualizable-local-perfect-models], $L[0]$ and $M[0]$ are dualizable: on their respective trivializing covers they are scalar finite free complexes in degree zero. Derived scalar extension gives the same complexes since they are free. The cited statement makes their cone dualizable. The explicit cone convention of [def-weil-abstract-derived-category] represents this cone by $L\xrightarrow{u}M$ in degrees $-1,0$, so its cohomology is $\ker u$ in degree $-1$, $\operatorname{coker}u$ in degree zero, and zero otherwise. Step 2 implies that these two sheaves have finite constant local models; by step 1 they are lisse. Thus kernels and cokernels are closed without any finite-field descent.

Returned step 4:
For a short exact sequence $0\to L\to E\to M\to0$ with lisse ends on $T$, first trivialize both ends on a common pro-etale cover.
