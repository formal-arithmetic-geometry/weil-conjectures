## Independent argument for the additional statement-only lemma

This is the argument the future splitter should use. It is kept in the report rather than hidden in the new statement or installed as a proof on a new node.

Write V(K)=|K|_Q. The pinched-resolution node supplies p_K:V(K)->K, the explicit theta_C:V(M_x)->A(a,x), and the evaluation counit epsilon_T:V(Sing_Q T)->T. It supplies weak equivalences for p and theta, and for epsilon when T is Kan. All three are actual specified maps, with their degree-zero formulas, rather than unspecified equivalences between objects. Its current closure is independent of this item.

First compute locally in the coherent nerve of the simplicial category of Kan complexes, without its full comparison theorem. A right-pinched n-simplex from X to Y is an enriched functor C[J^n]->Kan taking the initial collapsed face to X and the terminal vertex to Y. Since the endomorphisms on the collapsed initial face are identities, its only variable mapping-set map is Q^n->Fun(X,Y). This gives the exact identification Map^R_S(X,Y)=Sing_Q Fun(X,Y), compatible with every simplex operator by precomposition. For a Kan target T, the comparison T->Sing_Q T sends an n-simplex t:Delta^n->T to t composed with p^n:Q^n->Delta^n. Call it zeta_T. On a coend generator [t,q], epsilon_T V(zeta_T) takes the value t(p^n(q)), exactly p_T. Hence epsilon_T V(zeta_T)=p_T. Both epsilon_T and p_T are weak equivalences. Naturality p_{Sing_Q T} V(zeta_T)=zeta_T p_T and two-out-of-three show that zeta_T is a weak equivalence. Thus the inverse mapping comparison is represented by the span Sing_Q T <- V(Sing_Q T) -> T, with arrows p and epsilon, and NOT by the cyclic coherent-nerve-comparison theorem. For T=Fun(X,Y), Kan fibrancy follows from the existing independent Kan-functor-space theorem lem-cbc-kan-mapping-homotopy-invariance, applied to the identity of X. With X=point this supplies exactly the point-evaluation comparison used by the cocone criterion, including its K=point clause.

Now apply H to a right-pinched simplex tau:J^n->C. Put B=Fun(H(a),H(x)). Under the preceding exact identification, its image is Q^n -> A(a,x) -> B, the first arrow induced by C[tau], the second the enriched action defining H. Adjunction over all simplices gives b_x=epsilon_B V(H_*):V(M_x)->B. Evaluation at eta gives, ON EACH COPY OF Q^n, the map

Q^n -> A(a,x) -> Sing|A(a,x)|,

because postcomposing the identity of a in A^top is strictly the identity on the enriched Hom and the action from A is its singular-realization unit. Therefore ev_eta b_x=u_{a,x} theta_C as actual simplicial maps on the coend, not just on vertices.

It remains to match this with the actual cocone, which was missing in the returned proof. Its first transformation is the constant point eta and its second is H applied to the pinch prism. In the enriched nerve, their composite is represented by adjoining the arrow eta before each of the variable arrows Q^n->B. The three edges of this parameterized triangle are eta, that arrow, and its evaluation on eta. The subset-poset construction of C[Delta^m] uses union for composition; inserting the fixed first arrow therefore commutes with images of subsets under every monotone map. Its 02 face on each Q^n is exactly ev_eta b_x, while its 01 and 12 faces are the two transformations defining lambda_x. Consequently it is a comparison of THIS composite cocone with the evaluated enriched-action family. This identifies its point-map class e_x by [e_x][p_M]=[ev_eta b_x]=[u][theta]. It is natural with all the Q^n relations and all additional simplicial parameters; no objectwise choice or identification of two Kan models is used. This is the local nerve/evaluation calculation described before HTT Proposition 2.2.4.1, specialized to the defined postcomposition functor.

Finally theta and u are weak equivalences, by the pinched-resolution node and the realization-singular-unit node. The span p_M is also a weak equivalence. In the homotopy category of simplicial sets the computed class e_x is therefore invertible; equivalently, its Kan-space representative is a weak equivalence (the elementary Whitehead/model-category characterization of equivalences of Kan spaces). For a direct proof at the subsequent split level one can use the existing Kan Whitehead and Kan mapping-space homotopy-invariance lemmas, after replacing the coend by a Kan complex; these are independent of this item. The higher-dimensional assertion of the new lemma is the exact compatibility above, not an assertion inferred from this last invertibility test. Nothing about transformations from H was used, so this is strictly prior to the Yoneda argument.

The retained point-cocone node must use the same local Q-counit calculation for its 06QR input. Its later proof should not cite the broad coherent-nerve-comparison node. For the copower assertion itself, Kerodon 03F6 reduces the universal property of the actual cocone to precomposition Fun(Y,Z)->Fun(K,Z); the point-map comparison just computed identifies that test with e_lambda. Applying the ordinary Kan-space homotopy-invariance/Whitehead criterion gives exactly the claimed weak-equivalence condition. This is a proof of a general spaces/cocone fact, not a hidden Yoneda assumption.
