## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: unrestricted ambient blowup histories are not the domain of the embedded Hilbert--Samuel invariant. For an explicit example take X=P=A^2_Q and blow up the coherent ideal (u^2,v^3). The u^2-chart has coordinate ring Q[u,v,t]/(u^2 t-v^3), singular at (0,0,0): its local dimension is 2 and its maximal ideal has three independent generators modulo its square. Thus the new ambient scheme need not be smooth. With X=P its strict transform is the same blowup. Merely calling every ordinary blowup sequence a history does not supply the smooth ambient input for the claimed local construction.

The corrected domain consists of initial segments of the maximum-stratum construction and their restrictions and smooth ambient pullbacks, deleting empty-center stages. Those are precisely the histories used by clauses (1)--(4) and by the parent lem-trf-embedded-resolution-ambient-comparison. The other hypotheses and conclusions are retained. No parent file was changed. The corrected text is in the item's statement; original-statement.txt preserves the original for comparison. This is a source-intent repair, not a different resolution route: ILO VIII 2.3.11 starts with the admissible embedded algorithm and VIII 2.3.13 corrects its synchronization.

I read ILO VIII 2.3.1, 2.3.4--2.3.5 and 2.3.10--2.3.13, printed pages 91--94, from the local PDF extracted in this sandbox. For the geometric node I read Stacks more-morphisms.tex lemma-blowup (0FUT) and its affine coordinate model, and divisors.tex lemma-blowing-up-projective (02NS). Reduced strict transform follows from its defining closure: a function whose power vanishes on a reduced open vanishes on that open, so the closure ideal is radical. All new nodes are self-contained statements introducing the objects they use; no node is a clause-only relabeling of the item.

Step 1 cites the dimension-profile statement for its explicitly defined head, finite range and regularity test. The common-shift argument is written out: first differing coefficients stay first differing coefficients under partial summation or finite difference. This removes dependence on dim X; it does not claim the raw global-dimension-dependent head itself has equal values after a smooth pullback.

Step 2 uses the presentation node's maximal-order, codimension-zero and embedding-independent equivalence class, then the engine's explicit leading entry 1 and equivalence-invariant tail. These statements do say what is needed; merely citing a definition of marked ideal would not suffice. The scheme transforms and ideal transforms are deliberately different, connected by equality of test sequences. The regular locus is assigned bottom so exceptional cleanup is not continued there.

Step 3 uses finite ranges and upper semicontinuity from the profile and engine, the cosupport/stratum correspondence from the presentation, and the engine's smooth closed maximum center. I opened lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme: its clause (3) gives uniqueness of the reduced ideal, so the local centers really glue as schemes. Closedness is checked first in the maximum head stratum and then in X, not inferred from local smoothness. The generic-point argument is given explicitly to get nowhere density from reducedness. Regularity alone would not imply that condition.

Step 4 uses finite-type-algebra-noetherian for affine coordinate rings, reduced-induced-closed-subscheme for quasi-coherence of the ideals, and noetherian-submodule-finitely-presented for finite generation of ideals and kernels. Those are the actual statements I read. The use of regular-perfect-field-smooth is legal over Q and finite type; it imposes no extra dimension or integrality hypothesis. The new smooth-center-blowup theorem applies because this center is smooth, closed, and finite type. The strict maximum decrease is obtained from the profile's nonincrease and the engine's strict decrease on a fixed head; coherence is not assumed merely from the definition node.

Step 5 applies the profile pointwise with s equal to the dimension of the local fibre ring. This matters at nonclosed points; s is not a globally fixed relative dimension. The local presentation and engine statements explicitly include smooth comparison, so the tail comparison is covered. The cited lem-pc-proper-base-change clause (2) says smoothness is stable under arbitrary base change. Thus the pulled-back center is smooth/reduced, and the reduced-induced-closed-subscheme clause (3) gives equality of ideals. Set equality alone was not used as ideal equality. Empty inverse images give the unit ideal, and deletion adds no local exceptional hypersurface.

Step 6 uses the intrinsic-history clause of the presentation statement and the equivalence clause of the engine, not their unrecorded proofs. The hypotheses retain earlier ordered center ideals even for identity intrinsic blowups, matching exactly the item. Equality of functions gives equality of maximum subsets, and reduced-induced uniqueness gives the scheme/ideal comparison. Restriction/pullback compatibility is checked on the same local data, rather than asserted by an unexplained transport.

Step 7 uses the no-infinite-head-drop assertion of the profile; this is not inferred from the arbitrary lexicographic order. After finitely many drops, the same maximum-head presentation is being resolved; the engine's coefficient-dimension induction and monomial termination exclude an infinite tail, and the presentation theorem says empty cosupport removes that head. The four new lemmas are logically smaller inputs: local marked presentations, a general controlled-ideal algorithm, head normalization, and blowup geometry. Termination of these particular functions is proved in this step; it is not obtained by inventing a well-foundedness assertion for V.
