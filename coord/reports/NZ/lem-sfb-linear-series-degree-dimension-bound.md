## New auxiliary: proof and search assessment

For lem-sfb-residue-valuation-product-dimension, the source is the agent reconstruction needed for Morrison's Clifford line. For its local algebra I read Stacks `algebra.tex`, Tag 00PD, lemma-characterize-dvr: nonzero elements have a unit-times-uniformizer-power expression, with the resulting integer valuation extended to the fraction field. This is only a source for DVR structure, not a claim that Stacks states the product bound.

Here is the complete auxiliary argument. Choose a uniformizer t and write v for its integer order. Nonzero elements of k are units of R, so v is trivial on k*. For any nonzero finite-dimensional k-subspace T of K, choose a finite basis. The minimum valuation of those basis vectors is a lower bound on the valuation of every nonzero linear combination, and is attained by a basis vector; denote it m. The leading-coefficient map T -> R/m_R = k is x |-> (t^(-m)x mod m_R). It is a nonzero k-linear map, hence surjective with kernel of codimension one. Every nonzero vector in its kernel has valuation strictly larger than m. Inducting on dim T, the kernel has a basis of strictly increasing valuations; adjoining a vector with valuation m gives such a basis of T. The base dim T=1 is immediate.

Apply this to V and W, choosing bases v_1,...,v_r and w_1,...,w_s with strictly increasing orders alpha_i and beta_j. The products
v_1 w_1, v_2 w_1, ..., v_r w_1, v_r w_2, ..., v_r w_s
have strictly increasing orders
alpha_1+beta_1 < ... < alpha_r+beta_1 < alpha_r+beta_2 < ... < alpha_r+beta_s.
They are linearly independent: in a nontrivial linear relation the term with smallest order is unique, has a coefficient of order zero, and cannot be cancelled by higher-order terms. There are r+s-1 such products in P. Finally all rs basis products span P by expanding products of linear combinations, so P is finite-dimensional. This proves the exact new statement. The valuation-adapted-basis induction is substantial enough to remain a separate unsplit lemma, rather than being hidden in the item's Clifford step.
