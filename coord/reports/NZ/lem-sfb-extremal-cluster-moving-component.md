## Source argument for the new node

I read Stacks `varieties.tex`, Tag 0B40 (`lemma-check-invertible-sheaf-trivial`), including its proof expressing a nonzero line section as an effective Cartier divisor, as well as the degree-of-divisor argument in the same file. The new node is explicitly an agent-formulated corollary, not a purported verbatim Stacks theorem. It is the moving-point geometry used before the source Lemma 3.3 Step 1.

Here is its prospective proof, kept out of the statement-only node. Normalize the reduced integral component Gamma. This is a proper smooth integral curve over the algebraically closed field, and pullback of restricted sections is injective. Remove the fixed divisor of the pulled-back linear series; the resulting invertible module M is generated by the image of V. Its degree is positive: if its degree were zero, any nonzero section would trivialize it by 0B40, whereas the proper integral curve has only scalar regular functions, contradicting the image dimension at least two. Every nonzero M-section therefore has a zero. Enlarge the finite removed set to contain the inverse image of Z and the non-isomorphism locus of the normalization. At each removed point, evaluation on M is nonzero, so its kernel in V is a proper linear subspace. Include also the kernel of the entire restricted series (or simply {0} as well). For a section outside these finitely many proper subspaces all its moving zeros avoid the removed set. A zero descends at a point where normalization is an isomorphism, hence gives the claimed zero of the original section outside Z. This works with base points, singular components, reducible X, and arbitrary characteristic; no separability of a pencil or Bertini assertion is used.
