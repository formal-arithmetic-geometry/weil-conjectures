## Review by NZ

I read Temkin §§3.2.2–3.2.6 and the statement and proof of Proposition 3.3.1 and Corollary 3.3.2, together with §§3.3.1–3.3.2. These distinguish the nonradical GR ideal from the radical singular locus, define the conductor by a power inclusion, assert that it decreases under base change and smooth extension, and give the section-lifting theorem with n>r and agreement only modulo n. The four new nodes keep precisely those roles. The conormal definition is a real construction, with its presentation independence left as a theorem in the calculus node. Taking the intersection over all finite generating lists is set-sized. The section theorem explicitly requires the parameter to act injectively on both base and algebra, as required by principal invertible ideals. The parameter-bound node makes the source's removal of bad nonzero fibres explicit by q(0) nonzero; it also records flatness over k[t], needed in the tensor-product step. Over the PID k[t], the torsion is supported at finitely many nonzero points because the parameter acts injectively; these points can be removed along with the nonsmooth nonzero fibres. Finite presentation follows from finite type over a Noetherian ring. This is a supporting lemma, not a hidden assertion about every original point of X.

I also read Stacks algebra.tex, lemma-variant-local-criterion-flatness (Tag 00ML), more-algebra.tex lemma-henselization (0A02), lemma-henselization-flat (0AGU), and lemma-henselization-Noetherian-pair (0AGV), and the node statement of lem-weil-pbc-flat-local-criterion-nonzerodivisor. Its finite-module hypothesis is met by the target local ring as a rank-one module over itself; the parameter lies in the source maximal ideal precisely at the distinguished fibre. I read morphisms.tex lemma-smooth-at-point (01V9) through the existing criterion statement. For Step 1 I checked the parameter-bound and ideal-calculus statements: the former provides a single q and r depending on A and its chosen generator, the latter provides the inclusion under the smooth algebra A_* to A'_*. The definition of smooth morphism supplies stability under base change by retaining the prescribed base coordinate in each lifting problem. The bound is chosen before u, Y, m, or a. No conductor of Y is used.

For Step 2 I checked the cancellation argument directly. The pair condition gives (s)=(sv)+(s^N); division by s is allowed because s is a non-zero-divisor. Since r is at least one and m>r, N is at least three, so v is a unit on V(s). Localization at v changes no thickened fibre. The source parameter is lifted using a at the full precision N, so the approximate section in Step 3 is k[t]-linear at that precision. A lift chosen only from a modulo m would not suffice. This corrects the ambiguous parameter choice in a loose reading of the source proof.

For Step 3 I opened every cited existing node. pair-henselization provides a small filtered diagram and henselianness, flat-quotients gives all powers, and noetherian-completion supplies Noetherianity. Smooth-ring-map-flat, flat transitivity and flat-module-base-change state exactly the three flatness implications used. Finite-type-algebra-noetherian and finite-type-noetherian-finite-presentation supply the finiteness hypotheses. The tensor-test definition of flatness preserves multiplication by rho. Thus the bounded section theorem applies to the actual algebra C=A'_* tensor H; no claim that C is finite as a module, or etale, is made. Its retraction modulo rho^N is written explicitly and m>r is the theorem's strict inequality.

For Step 4 I checked finite-presentation-algebra-factors-filtered-colimit: it assumes a small filtered category of commutative algebras, allows any finitely presented source, and factors the algebra map. This is the actual diagram given by pair-henselization, with all structural k[t]-maps compatible. I did not use filtered-colimit-factorization-descent, which only descends polynomial factorizations. Etale-algebra-flat and flat-iso-mod-ideal-powers show that the chosen etale stage already has every required quotient, rather than using an assertion from the proof of the henselization node. The induced stage-to-H quotient map is an isomorphism by comparison with the common B/J^t, which proves the required agreement. I also used the two existing Noetherian/finiteness leaves here to check D is Noetherian and finite presentation over A'_*, supplying the hypotheses for the next local criterion.

For Step 5 I checked the nonzero-divisor local flatness criterion and the smooth-at-point fibre criterion against their exact hypotheses, including Noetherian local rings, finiteness of the module over the target local ring, and local finite presentation. The special-fibre morphism is the base change of a, so smooth and flat; localizing its source and target gives the quotient local map required by the flatness criterion. The ordinary fibre over a point containing pi is literally its fibre inside the special-fibre morphism. This is why smoothness follows without assuming smoothness of X or Y over k. Each neighbourhood contains its chosen point of the closed fibre; their union contains all of that fibre. No point away from the fibre is claimed to remain.

For Step 6 I checked the maps preserve the ideals exactly, not only their supports: the descended map sends pi to rho and rho generates J after shrinking. The quotient isomorphisms survive both shrinkings because the supports of all positive thickenings equal the closed fibre. Etaleness and smoothness on these open restrictions follow from their affine/local definitions. The composition identity follows from the equality in Step 4 after composing with the inverse of g_m. Empty distinguished fibres, including unit ideals, are covered by taking Z empty, so no nonemptiness assumption was added.
