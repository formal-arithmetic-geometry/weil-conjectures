I initially bundled a flat base-change comparison with the new embedding statement. On review it is a short consequence of the canonical map and the section isomorphism, so I removed that clause from the new node and put its argument into step 5. For an affine L inside W, the base-change square is Spec of C -> Gamma(L) tensored with A; the restriction map on E_0 is the same map by the formula c tensor a -> pullback(c) a in the sections node. These squares glue over the affine open cover of W. Thus the new embedding node states 0API itself, and no additional comparison lemma is hidden or created. The first dry run also flagged the earlier diagram's TeX row separator adjacent to a letter as a doubled command; the revised statement needs no diagram and removes that ambiguity.

For step 1 I opened the statements of lem-a1-ind-quasi-affine-permanence, lem-weil-pbc-scheme-coproduct-family and lem-galois-fpqc-descending-types-morphisms. The reduction explicitly requires arbitrary base change, finite coproduct stability (including m=0) and effectivity for flat surjective affine covers. The first is the new permanence statement, the second follows by checking P on the disjoint components, and steps 2--7 establish the third. The reduction is applied only after that case is proved. I did not assume that a general singleton fpqc cover is a quasi-compact morphism: 02W3 is what permits passage to affine covers.

For step 2, permanence gives absolute ind-quasi-affineness and quasi-separatedness because X is affine. The affine-morphism node says exactly that a map between affine schemes is affine. Thus inverse images of affine target opens are affine, and the singleton covering condition follows directly from the fpqc definition and surjectivity. The invariant-neighbourhood node requires V quasi-separated and p flat surjective affine, all now verified. To check that node against Groupoids: the two source/target maps of the cartesian descent groupoid are base changes of p, so are flat and quasi-compact. First 0APB gives an invariant open W around v contained in some quasi-compact Q; then 0APA applied to Q makes its saturation E an intersection of quasi-compact opens, and constructing-invariant-opens makes it invariant. This gives W subset Q subset E, with exactly the two invariances stated. No openness of a general flat map is assumed.

For step 3, the sections lemma explicitly covers inverse-image sheaves on arbitrary intersections in a quasi-separated scheme. It supplies both section identifications, their functoriality and their iterated flat compatibility, rather than requiring E itself to be a scheme. The flat base-change node supplies the extra flat projection. I wrote out the reversed-composition equality for comorphisms on the triple product, with indices 02, 01 and 12. This is the 'details omitted' in the source; it follows because each section identification is the canonical pullback-times-scalar map, so pulling a section twice agrees with pulling it along the composite. The cocycle is for algebras over A tensor A, not merely abstract ring isomorphisms.

For step 4, I opened the affine fibre-product statement, the contravariant spectrum definition, the affine-morphism statement and the affine-effectivity theorem. The spectrum functor reverses the just-verified comorphism equality back to the scheme cocycle. The theorem's required structural map is affine, its cover is fpqc as verified in step 2, and its datum is the one just built. It yields a scheme Z_0 with the actual datum-compatible isomorphism, which is all the next step needs. No affineness of Z_0 is inferred from a theorem that does not state it.

I checked the whole base-change square on each affine open L of W using the exact projection maps in the affine fibre-product node and the explicit formula in the sections node. Restriction of sections commutes with the comorphism of phi by its definition, and W is invariant by step 2. Thus theta carries one whole inverse-image open onto the other. This is a routine affine computation carried by the step, not a new statement-only base-change clause.
