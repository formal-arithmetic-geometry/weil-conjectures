For the subgroup node I read Exercise 2.4.3(iii) and the final torsion argument in Theorem B.3.2(2). Here is why no flatness assumption is being smuggled in. For a closed K in T, every K[n] is a closed subgroup of the finite group T[n], so its coordinate algebra C_n is finite over A. The identity section splits C_n=A plus its augmentation module. The latter is a finite A-module, and reduction to k is zero when K_k=1; Nakayama makes that augmentation module zero, so K[n]=1. In every geometric fibre, closed subgroups of a split torus are diagonalizable; a nonzero finitely generated character group has nontrivial n-torsion group scheme for some n. Thus all fibres of K are trivial. Finally the augmentation ideal Q of A[K] is finite over the Noetherian finite-type coordinate algebra A[K]. The counit splits A[K]=A plus Q as A-modules, so Q tensor kappa(p)=0 at every base point p. Localizing at a prime q of A[K], Nakayama for Q_q and the ideal p A[K]_q gives Q_q=0. Therefore Q=0 and K=1. This argument uses neither fibrewise flatness nor the false inference that a nonproper closed subscheme is determined by one fibre. The new node is the independent closed-subgroup claim needed by the item, not its closed-immersion clause relabelled.
