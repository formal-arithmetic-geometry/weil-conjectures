## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported sentence identifying a single distinguished triangle with the truncation exact couple is ill-typed. The triangle is a diagram of three derived-category objects and their boundary; def-sp-truncation-couple instead defines two bigraded objects D and E in the target abelian category, obtained by applying Rv and H* to a family of triangles. These are different kinds of mathematical data. In particular, for A = B = Ab, v = identity and K = Z[0], the triangle at n = 0 is 0 -> Z[0] -> Z[0] -> 0 in D(Ab), whereas the couple has E^{0,0} = Z and E^{p,q} = 0 for the other bidegrees. The two constructions are related as the cited definition says; they cannot be identified as objects of the same type.

Repaired the sentence to say that the successive triangles produce the couple after Rv and cohomology are applied, in exactly the bounded-below/Grothendieck/left-exact setting that def-sp-truncation-couple specifies. Bounded-below is a restriction on that spectral-sequence application, not on the truncation or telescope definitions. The canonical triangle and every original construction are retained.

Also made explicit that complexes here have abelian coefficients, including R_T-module coefficients in the unit's applications; plain sheaves of sets do not have the kernels, cokernels, additive differentials and derived cohomology implicit in the imported sentence. The formulas are stated for any abelian category, so this clarification excludes no intended coefficient ring. Filled the missing boundary terms of the good truncations, the missing final object of the successive triangle, and the shift endomorphism of the telescope. Countable coproducts and all sheaf colimits mean small diagrams in the relevant chosen universe. A cone in a bare triangulated category is chosen, not a canonical functorial object. This agrees with Stacks 090Z and does not change the intended homotopy-colimit notion.

## Review by NZ

I read the master blueprint node, the chapter and the Kunneth unit, then Stacks homology.tex Tag 0118 (section-truncations), in particular its cochain-complex half. It fixes the direction: stupid >= is a subcomplex and stupid <= a quotient; good <= has ker(d^n) at the boundary and good >= has coker(d^{n-1}). I checked these against the definitions of abelian category and cochain complex/cohomology in def-sp-abelian and def-weil-abstract-derived-category, and against def-sbc-standard-tstructure-derived-category, which gives the standard t-structure, not a boundedness assumption on all objects. Kernels and cokernels are taken in the abelian category of etale sheaves or coefficient modules of def-weil-pbc-etale-sheaves; def-weil-pbc-etale-site supplies the small etale site. The elementary local calculation gives H^n(tau<=E) = ker(d^n)/im(d^{n-1}) and H^n(tau>=E) = ker(d^n)/im(d^{n-1}); away from the boundary it retains or kills exactly the stated degrees. Maps commuting with d induce maps of these kernels and quotients. No extra lemma is concealed here and no new statement-only node is needed for this calculation.

For the triangle clause I opened lem-weil-pbc-truncation-triangle and read its full statement. It explicitly supplies tau<=n-1 K -> tau<=n K -> H^n(K)[-n] -> (tau<=n-1 K)[1] for any abelian category and integer n. Stacks derived.tex Tag 08J5 (remark-truncation-distinguished-triangle) gives that precise triangle with a = n-1. The statement of def-sp-truncation-couple really does apply Rv and cohomology and stipulates bounded-below complexes and left exact additive functors between Grothendieck abelian categories. The statement of def-weil-pbc-hypercohomology-filtrations uses these triangles in its cartesian-square bounded-below setup. I corrected the misleading identification rather than using either definition as a theorem about unbounded spectral-sequence convergence. For the telescope I read derived.tex Tag 090Z (definition-derived-colimit). Its endomorphism is 1 minus the transition shift. I checked def-weil-triangulated-category: its TR1 axiom says every morphism is the first arrow of a distinguished triangle, so it allows a cone for 1-s once B exists.
