## Review by NZ

I read Milne, Algebraic Groups (2017), printed pp.149--150 and 154--155: Proposition 8.2, Definition 8.3, Proposition 8.6, Theorem 8.26, Theorem 8.27 and Note 8.30. The item is the algebraically closed specialization with existence only; it does not require uniqueness or base-change compatibility from Note 8.30. Smoothness of L is retained. I also read the product construction and isomorphism theorem at pp.111--112, Lemma 6.41 and Proposition 6.42 at p.134, and the dimension discussion and irreducibility at p.17. Those are the source of the first new node. A smooth proper closed subgroup of equal dimension cannot be a nontrivial nilpotent thickening: both groups are reduced, the larger one is irreducible, and a proper closed subset has smaller dimension. The product construction uses the image of the semidirect product; quotient and extension closure in Proposition 8.1 makes that product affine, smooth and connected. This is a real lemma, not an elementary rewrite of the item.

For the second new node I read Theorem 5.14 (p.102 and its proof p.105), Propositions 5.24--5.25 and Corollary 5.26 (pp.104--105), Proposition 1.62 and its proof (p.24), Theorem 5.55 and Proposition 5.59 (pp.113--114), and Proposition 8.1 (p.149). They give representability with kernel, smooth quotient, the smooth quotient morphism for smooth kernel, and normal inverse images. The inverse image of an affine subgroup is an extension of two affine groups; it is affine by 8.1, smooth by 1.62 and connected by 5.59. The new node includes finite presentation and the base-change covering clause explicitly, avoiding an unstated covering-permanence lemma in the assembly proof. The third new node is precisely Theorem 8.26 over the already algebraically closed field, with pseudo-abelian spelled out rather than introduced as an undefined name. Its conclusion is properness, not the original decomposition theorem. I did not split any of the new nodes, and none contains a hidden proof in its statement.
