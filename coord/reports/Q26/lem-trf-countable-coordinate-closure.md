### Image-support work item: lem-trf-countably-generated-image-support

This worker owns the enumeration of generators, finite-image-support countability coding, and extension of the generator bound by linearity.

Old step 1: For each $j\in J$, choose a generating sequence $(g_{j,n})_{n\in\mathbf N}$ of $N_j$. This is possible from the given at most countable generating subset by listing it and inserting zero in unused positions; for the zero module the constant zero sequence suffices. Write $\iota_j:N_j\to M$ for the canonical inclusion of [def-weil-pbc-direct-sum-modules]. Thus each $x\in N_j$ is a finite sum $\sum_{n\in E}r_n g_{j,n}$, with $E\subseteq\mathbf N$ finite and $r_n\in R$. The choice is of generators, not an enumeration of all elements of $N_j$.

Old step 2: For each $j,n$, put $S_{j,n}=\operatorname{supp}(f(\iota_j(g_{j,n})))\subseteq J$. Each $S_{j,n}$ is finite because its defining vector belongs to the direct sum [def-weil-pbc-direct-sum-modules]. Put $F_j=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbf N}S_{j,n}$. This set is at most countable: choose a finite listing of each $S_{j,n}$, and assign an element of $F_j$ the pair $(n,r)$ of its first occurrence (least $n$, then least position $r$ in that listing). This is an injection into $\mathbf N^2$, which is enumerated by successive finite diagonals. Empty supports contribute no entries.

Old step 4 (generator portion is this node; the finite-sum use remains parent step 3): For every $k$, $f(M_{A_k})\subseteq M_{A_{k+1}}$. Indeed, for $j\in A_k$ and $x=\sum_{n\in E}r_n g_{j,n}\in N_j$, linearity gives $f(\iota_j(x))=\sum_{n\in E}r_n f(\iota_j(g_{j,n}))$. Outside $F_j$ every coordinate of every vector on the right is zero. Hence this vector is supported in $F_j\subseteq A_{k+1}$. Every $y\in M_{A_k}$ is the finite sum $\sum_{j\in\operatorname{supp}(y)}\iota_j(y_j)$ by [def-weil-pbc-direct-sum-modules]. Applying $f$ and the preceding calculation to each term proves that $f(y)$ is supported in $A_{k+1}$.

The same argument applies to h:L→M: enumerate a countable generating subset, pad by zero (also for L=0), define F as the union of supports of h(g_n), use finite listings and first-occurrence (n,r) codes, then expand every x as a finite linear combination. For each coordinate outside F every h(g_n) is zero there; linearity makes h(x) zero there. No assertion enumerates L or R.

### Successor/exhaustion work item: lem-trf-countable-successor-coordinate-exhaustion

This worker owns stage recursion and both countability codings, plus the finite-support maximum-stage argument.

Old step 3: Define subsets of $J$ by ordinary recursion on $\mathbf N$: $A_0=C$ and $A_{k+1}=A_k\cup\bigcup_{j\in A_k}F_j$. They are increasing and at most countable. For the countability induction, the base is the hypothesis on $C$. Given an injection $a:A_k\to\mathbf N$ and the injections $b_j:F_j\to\mathbf N$ of Step 2, an element $t\in\bigcup_{j\in A_k}F_j$ is coded by $(a(j),b_j(t))$, choosing among its possible $j$ the one with least $a(j)$. Distinct elements have distinct codes. Adjoining $A_k$ uses one additional binary label. Diagonal enumeration of pairs, and then the binary label, puts this set into $\mathbf N$. This proves the successor claim even if $A_k$ is empty.

Old step 5: Set $D=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbf N}A_k$. Then $C=A_0\subseteq D$. To verify countability explicitly, choose injections $a_k:A_k\to\mathbf N$ supplied by Step 3. Send $j\in D$ to $(k,a_k(j))$, where $k$ is its least membership stage. This is injective, and enumeration of $\mathbf N^2$ by finite diagonals proves that $D$ is at most countable. These choices and the recursion are all over sets: $J$ is a set and the stage set is $\mathbf N$.

Old step 6: Let $y\in M_D$. If $y=0$, then $f(y)=0\in M_D$. Otherwise its support is finite by [def-weil-pbc-direct-sum-modules]. For each index of that support choose its least membership stage in $A_k$, and let $k$ be the maximum of these finitely many natural numbers. Since the $A_k$ are increasing, $y\in M_{A_k}$. Step 4 gives $f(y)\in M_{A_{k+1}}\subseteq M_D$. This proves the required invariance. Every countability bound used only generators and finite coordinate supports; none counted the possible coefficients in $R$.

For its standalone statement the last step stops at y∈M_Ak, rather than asserting invariance: the linear endomorphism belongs only to the parent. The additional closure clause F_j⊆D follows from j∈A_k implying F_j⊆A_(k+1); it is a direct consequence of the same recursion. For an empty finite subset take k=0. For a nonempty finite subset take the maximum of least membership stages. The two work items respectively carry roughly half the old proof, with only the original finite direct-sum assembly left in the parent.
