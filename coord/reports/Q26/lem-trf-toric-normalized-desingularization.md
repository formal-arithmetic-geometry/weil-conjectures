### Original step 1: lem-trf-normalized-resolution-on-normal-inputs

First work on the larger family of all reduced finite-type $\mathbf Q$-schemes, so that products with acting tori will also be in the domain. Fix one ordinary resolution rule $R$ from [lem-trf-smooth-functorial-ordinary-resolution]. For a normal member $S$, write $R(S)=(S_i,I_i)$ and put $N_0=S$, $N_i=S_i^\nu$ for $i>0$, with centers $K_i=I_i\mathcal O_{N_i}$. By [lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus], $N_{i+1}\to N_i$ is exactly $B_{K_i}(N_i)$. Each center is coherent, nonempty and nowhere dense: the normalization maps are surjective and birational, so their inverse images of the nonempty nowhere dense ordinary centers have these properties. The terminal scheme is $S_n$, because the ordinary terminal scheme is regular and its normalization is itself. Denote this finite normalized tower by $R^\nu(S)$.

Inputs: lem-trf-smooth-functorial-ordinary-resolution, lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus.

Source: literature/illusie-laszlo-orgogozo-travaux-de-gabber_arXiv-1207.3648.pdf, 2.2.4(ii), 2.2.6, 2.3.3(i), 2.3.9; here the initial S is normal.

### Original step 2: lem-trf-normalized-resolution-on-normal-inputs

For a smooth map $T\to S$ between normal members, compare $R(T)$ with the ordinary pulled-back tower by [lem-trf-smooth-functorial-ordinary-resolution]. Normalize its levels. Smooth comparison in [lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus] identifies every normalized level with the pullback of $N_i$ and every center with $K_i\mathcal O$. All levels now are normal, including level zero. Thus if an ordinary pulled-back ideal is the unit ideal, its normalized blowup is the identity and can really be deleted. Removing these stages gives exactly $R^\nu(T)$ with the other extended ideals in their original order. Applying the identity and composition comparisons of the two cited nodes proves the corresponding comparisons for $R^\nu$. This argument uses normality of the starting schemes, not an assertion that normalization of an empty blowup is always the identity.

Inputs: lem-trf-smooth-functorial-ordinary-resolution, lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus.

Source: literature/illusie-laszlo-orgogozo-travaux-de-gabber_arXiv-1207.3648.pdf, 2.2.9, 2.3.4 and 2.3.12(i); explicit check of contraction on normal inputs.

### Original step 3: lem-trf-conductor-extension-of-normalized-resolution

For an arbitrary reduced finite-type $X$, let $D_X$ be the conductor ideal of [lem-trf-conductor-smooth-base-change], and set $P(X)=B_{D_X}(X)$. The center is nowhere dense, and [lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus] makes $P(X)$ normal and of finite type. Define $H(X)$ by the one stage $P(X)\to X$, followed by $R^\nu(P(X))$ from Step 1. If $D_X$ is the unit ideal, omit that first stage: the conductor criterion says that $X$ is then normal, so $P(X)=X$ and omission is valid. Otherwise $V(D_X)$ is nonempty. Hence $H(X)$ is in every case a finite normalized tower with nonempty nowhere dense centers and a regular last scheme. The empty scheme gives the trivial tower. This preliminary conductor stage is used only to handle the initial nonnormal input; it need not equal the finite normalization morphism.

Inputs: lem-trf-conductor-smooth-base-change, lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus.

Source: literature/illusie-laszlo-orgogozo-travaux-de-gabber_arXiv-1207.3648.pdf, 2.2.3--2.2.9, 2.3.9--2.3.12; agent refinement for the initial nonnormal level, using Stacks 07T8 as stated in the conductor node.

### Original step 4: lem-trf-conductor-extension-of-normalized-resolution

Let $h:Y\to X$ be any smooth map between reduced finite-type schemes. By [lem-trf-conductor-smooth-base-change], the first extended ideal is $D_Y=D_X\mathcal O_Y$. By [lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus], $P(Y)=P(X)\times_XY$ and $P(Y)\to P(X)$ is smooth. If either first stage has unit ideal, the corresponding scheme is normal by the conductor criterion, so that stage is an identity. Step 2 now compares the remaining towers on the normal schemes $P(Y)$ and $P(X)$. Together these comparisons give $H(Y)=(h^*H(X))^c$, retaining the ordered ideals. Their identity and composition laws follow first for the conductor stage from the two cited nodes, and then for the tails from Step 2; successive deletion keeps the same ordered subsequence. If $h$ is surjective, each map of pulled-back levels is surjective, being a base change of $h$, so every nonempty zero scheme of a center has nonempty inverse image. Thus no stage is deleted for a surjective $h$.

Inputs: lem-trf-conductor-smooth-base-change, lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus.

Source: literature/illusie-laszlo-orgogozo-travaux-de-gabber_arXiv-1207.3648.pdf, 2.3.4, Remark 2.3.5(i), Lemma 2.2.9; initial conductor comparison is the agent refinement.

### Original step 5: lem-trf-smooth-functorial-tower-torus-equivariance

By [lem-trf-toric-realization-finite-type], all the required $\mathbf Q[M]$ and their finite disjoint unions are reduced finite-type schemes. Define $\mathcal F(X)=H(X)$ on this family. Steps 3--4 give every tower and smooth-comparison clause of the statement, for arbitrary smooth scheme morphisms and not just monomial morphisms. To obtain equivariance without unsynchronized componentwise choices, fix one torus of a connected component; the same node says it is a smooth finite-type group. Let it act as the identity on all other components. Then $T\times X$ is reduced and finite type and projection $p:T\times X\to X$ is smooth and surjective by [lem-trf-toric-realization-finite-type]; the action $a$ is also smooth and surjective, since $(t,x)\mapsto(t,tx)$ is an automorphism with inverse $(t,x)\mapsto(t,t^{-1}x)$ and $a$ is projection after this automorphism. Step 4 gives identical uncontracted towers $p^*H(X)=H(T\times X)=a^*H(X)$, including their ordered centers.

Inputs: lem-trf-toric-realization-finite-type, lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus.

Source: literature/illusie-laszlo-orgogozo-travaux-de-gabber_arXiv-1207.3648.pdf, 2.3.16--2.3.17 and 3.2.19; use the rule on the larger family to accommodate T times X.

### Original step 6: lem-trf-smooth-functorial-tower-torus-equivariance

Induct on the length of $H(X)$. At level zero, the equality of the first ideals in the two towers of Step 5 says $p^*J_0=a^*J_0$, so $J_0$ is invariant in exactly the sense of [def-trf-monoscheme-toric-realization]. The smooth normalized-blowup comparisons of [lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus] identify their next schemes with $T\times X_1$ and $X_1\times_{X,a}(T\times X)$. Their canonical identification followed by the first projection defines the lifted action $a_1:T\times X_1\to X_1$. The unit and associativity diagrams are lifts of the original action diagrams: the two maps in each diagram agree by the final-object uniqueness in the normalized-blowup universal property, applied to the normal schemes $X_1$ and $T\times T\times X_1$ and the pulled-back invertible center. These maps dominate the relevant components; smooth pullback and the birational blowup maps preserve their generic points. Thus $a_1$ is an action. At level $i$, the same ordered-tower comparison gives $p_i^*J_i=a_i^*J_i$; repeat the construction and uniqueness argument to obtain the action on $X_{i+1}$. The induction includes every retained center, so the chosen component torus acts throughout and preserves all its ideals. Repeating for each component proves the final clause.

Inputs: def-trf-monoscheme-toric-realization, lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus.

Source: literature/illusie-laszlo-orgogozo-travaux-de-gabber_arXiv-1207.3648.pdf, Corollary 2.3.17 proof, using 2.1.11 and the normalized universal property 2.2.4(i); unit and group-law check made explicit.
