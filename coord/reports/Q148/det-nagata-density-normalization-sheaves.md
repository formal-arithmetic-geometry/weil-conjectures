## Argument extracted into the new node

Under the canonical internal/sectionwise equivalence, an action morphism from the sheafified tensor corresponds to the bilinear action on sections; on arrows the equivalence keeps the underlying abelian-sheaf map. Applying the lax-monoidal direct image to an action and restricting scalars along the mate of alpha gives exactly the sectionwise direct-image action. This verifies the direct-image comparison on objects and arrows. The internal Hom transposition in `def-sfb-module-upper-lower-adjunction` consists of scalar extension adjunction followed by the monoidal inverse-image adjunction. On sectionwise modules these are precisely the two transpositions of Stacks 03D7. Thus the two left adjoints and their Hom bijections agree through the canonical comparison; both unit and counit are identified, with the same underlying sheaf adjunction because alpha is invertible.

For an open immersion, `sk-def-sfb-module-lower-shriek` supplies the linearization of abelian extension by zero using its projection formula and coefficient transport. Its module Hom transposition is the restriction of the same abelian open adjunction. After applying E it is the sectionwise left adjoint of the same restriction functor. Uniqueness of left adjoints with this Hom normalization identifies the extensions and the open units and counits. Direct-image composition is sectionwise composition; inverse-image composition is its normalized adjoint comparison. Consequently the mate that defines open-extension base change is carried to the same mate, with the same units, counits and pullback-composition comparison. No existence or invertibility of an arbitrary base-change comparison is asserted: the new node identifies the transformations, and the parent separately cites the sectionwise invertibility theorem. Additivity follows because the equivalence is the identity on underlying linear maps; it preserves kernels and cokernels by their universal properties, hence exactness. These comparisons are the non-elementary argument removed from the long parent step; they are not an auxiliary form of the closure conclusion.
