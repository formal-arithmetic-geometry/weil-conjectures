The qcqs tool actually states that quasi-separated source implies quasi-separated morphism, so the argument does not take a relative property from an absolute definition alone.

Step 2 is the substantive argument, not an invocation of a new version of the item. lem-sc-irreducible-components-cover states that every point is in a component, and lem-sc-scheme-sober supplies its generic point. I read topology.tex lemma-irreducible (004W) and schemes.tex lemma-scheme-sober (01IS). The image generization is elementary continuity; maximality of the two target components and uniqueness of their generic points force the image of the chosen generic point to be the fixed s. This is exactly where maximal dominance is used. lem-trf-finite-residue-point-closed-in-fibre states closedness in the whole scheme fibre under a finite residue extension, not merely an isolated-point assertion or the converse. I checked its text and Stacks 01TE/01TF in morphisms.tex. def-pc-scheme-fibre includes the homeomorphism to the set-theoretic fibre with its subspace topology (Stacks schemes.tex lemma-fibre-topological, 01K1), so the specialization remains a specialization inside that fibre. A closed point has no other specializations. The resulting equality puts every fibre point among the finite set from Step 1. Nothing about the number of closed points on a positive-dimensional scheme is assumed.

Step 3 applies lem-weil-pbc-generic-finite-morphism-open-neighborhood literally: its hypotheses are quasi-compact, quasi-separated, locally finite type, Noetherian target, and finite fibres over every component generic point. Steps 1 and 2 provide all of them. I read Stacks 02NW, label lemma-generically-finite, including (1) equivalent to (4); no separatedness assumption is hidden. This node is a generic spreading tool, and the item's residue-field-to-finite-fibre argument remains in Step 2.

Step 4 is marked elementary because the only substantive input is the cited finite component set. I checked that components are closed by def-weil-irreducible-components. A component's generic point is in no other component: otherwise its closure would be contained in another maximal component. Removing the finitely many other components gives the open W used in the proof; density of V and the definition in def-sc-generic-point complete the argument. This proves every generic point lies in V, without incorrectly assuming a dense set meets an arbitrary closed subset.

Step 5 uses exactly clause (a) of lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence, stability under arbitrary base change (Stacks morphisms.tex lemma-base-change-finite, 01WL). The canonical point map factors through V because s belongs to V, and the fibre-product universal property gives the required identification. def-pc-scheme-fibre states the fibre's actual scheme and structure map. This proves finite as a scheme morphism, not just finiteness of the underlying point set.

Step 6 uses def-weil-finite-integral-morphism to make the finite fibre an affine spectrum of a finite-dimensional algebra. The quotient by a prime is again finite-dimensional by taking the images of a vector-space generating set, and it is a domain by primality. The existing Mathlib leaf lem-weil-pbc-finite-dimensional-domain-is-field therefore applies even for an inseparable residue extension; it needs no reducedness of B. For residue fields I read the last sentence of Stacks schemes.tex lemma-points-fibre-product (01JT), and the full statement of lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-point-residue-field: it expressly gives a field isomorphism compatible with both input fields. At a scheme fibre the tensor factor kappa(s) cancels, so its unique prime is zero. This justifies the extension identification, which the separate points-only node would not have justified.
