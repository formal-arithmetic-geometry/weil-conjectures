## Review by Q148

I read Milne's PDF at printed pp.164–166 (9.2, its proof and 9.3/9.6), pp.166–171 (9.9, 9.11 and 9.13–9.15), pp.188–193 (10.6–10.7, 10.11, 10.14 and 10.19–10.23), pp.376–377 (the additive preliminaries and exercise 17.74), and p.87 (faithful representation). The main construction follows the additive analogue of the reconstruction argument: for each representation the semisimple and nilpotent parts give tensor DERIVATIONS, so 1+ε times those parts give tensor endomorphisms over the dual numbers. Using multiplicative Jordan tensor compatibility here would be wrong; the new linear node states tensor SUM compatibility explicitly. The existing lem-sfb-affine-group-tensor-reconstruction is exactly the general R version of 9.2. It was found in the completed source-place search, so the initially proposed reconstruction node was removed before final validation; the narrower lem-sfb-tannaka-rational-point-reconstruction does not cover a nonreduced coefficient algebra. The tangent calculus is 10.6–10.23 without imposing the characteristic-zero hypotheses of the existing bracket node. The item proof_origin is agent because 17.74 itself has no proof in the source.

For step 1 I opened the complete statements of the faithful-linear-representation and new tangent-calculus nodes. The former supplies a closed immersion with injectivity on every algebra and after every field extension, and the latter supplies precisely the dual-number matrix formula. Thus equality of differential matrices implies equality of points over k[ε]/ε², hence equality of tangent vectors. Faithfulness on k-points alone would not suffice. The same proof works for every closed immersion because its affine coordinate map is surjective, as explicitly explained in step 7.

For step 2 the linear compatibility node gives unique commuting parts, naturality for any linear intertwiner, and the tensor-sum laws. I verified equivariance actually supplies the needed intertwiner by comparing ε coefficients. I wrote the tensor differential calculation: the cross term has ε² and vanishes. The tangent-calculus matrix formula is the only group-specific input here. Both families vanish on the trivial representation by linear uniqueness; the zero representation causes no exception.

For step 3 I checked all three hypotheses against the reconstruction node's complete statement: every equivariant linear map, every tensor pair, and the unit. The sum law turns into the multiplicative tensor law over R=k[ε]/ε². The reconstructed elements reduce to the identity because their faithful matrices reduce to I. This uses the faithful-representation node on k-points after reconstruction, and its use on dual-number points is distinct. The tangent-calculus node then turns their ε coefficients back into the stated differential images. No claim about arbitrary abstract Lie representations enters.

For step 4 the tangent-calculus node gives both linearity and bracket preservation. The commuting matrix parts therefore imply sum equality and bracket zero through the already proved injective differential. The linear node's uniqueness proves uniqueness in one faithful representation, hence in the tangent space. For step 5 the chain rule from the same calculus node applies to the composite r'∘f, a rational representation of H; a faithful representation of H' exists by the existing faithful-representation node. No assumption that df is injective is made or needed.

For step 6 I checked finite type for the tangent scalar-extension isomorphism and perfectness for both constructions. The linear node expressly states perfect-field extension compatibility. A faithful representation stays faithful after extension; its matrix parts identify the constructed K-parts with the extensions of the k-parts by injectivity. There is no invalid assumption that every K-representation is defined over k.

For step 7 I read def-weil-closed-immersion, whose affine formulation says a closed immersion is induced by a surjective ring homomorphism. That gives the algebra-valued injection, including dual numbers over the algebraic closure. Algebraically closed fields are perfect because their irreducible polynomials are linear. Linear uniqueness identifies the pure semisimple or pure nilpotent matrix parts with zero in the other slot. Injectivity then kills the corresponding tangent part. For step 8 the calculus node identifies scalar extension of the differential with the extended matrix. The previous construction over the algebraic closure gives semisimplicity or nilpotence in every extended rational representation. Geometric semisimplicity is the definition in the item; nilpotence descends because extension of a matrix power is its entrywise extension and k→k̄ is injective. This last descent is an explicit elementary entry calculation, not an uncited descent theorem.

I tested the new linear statement on V=0, A=0, a scalar matrix, a single nontrivial Jordan block, and tensor sums with coinciding eigenvalue sums in characteristic p: eigenvalue collisions do not change uniqueness, and no division by factorials occurs. For the group statements I tested the trivial group, G_a, G_m, α_p and μ_p. No smoothness is needed; α_p has its expected nilpotent tangent images and μ_p its scalar semisimple tangent images. The Frobenius map on G_a has zero differential, illustrating why arbitrary homomorphisms need not preserve nonzeroness and why the proof uses only faithful differentials for detection. Imperfect k is used only in the geometric clause, after extension to k̄; the proof never asserts a k-rational decomposition there.
