Step 2 uses only the restricted-series definition. All m_i are chosen from M but form a basis only after passing to the fraction field F, which exists by the item hypotheses. There are finitely many such elements and countably many monomials for finite n, so their coefficients form a countable set. The often hidden claim that the generated coefficient field is of countable type is written out: k[D] is already a field because b^p is in k and b^{-1}=b^{p-1}/b^p; its countable monomials span it. Its metric closure is complete inside k_1 and is a field by the explicit inverse estimate. Thus the dense-span hypothesis used later is actually established. Neither k nor its underlying point set has to be countable.

Step 3 cites lem-trf-tate-integrally-closed, whose statement I opened in full: for every complete nontrivially valued nonarchimedean field and every d>=0, the restricted-series ring is a domain and contains every fraction satisfying a monic polynomial over it. This establishes M subset S for the whole module, not just its finite chosen basis. The node is in the same unit, so no off-unit tool-use declaration is necessary.
