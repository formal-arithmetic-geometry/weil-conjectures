The displayed sheaf map h_U -> F sends a morphism v to F(v)(sigma); naturality gives the stated formula on every germ. This is a direct cone/germ construction from the cited statements, with its equality calculation written out, not an appeal to an unrecorded theorem classifying topos arrows. In particular it does not use only the underlying relation t specializes to s.

Step 2 applies clause (1) of `lem-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-functoriality`: the statement explicitly supplies strict henselian locality, the closed point and the canonical Omega_s-valued lift. `def-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-structural-map` supplies its structural morphism and `def-pc-fibre-product` the cartesian square. `lem-weil-pbc-surjective-base-change` gives q surjective for an arbitrary base change. `def-weil-proper-morphism` really defines universal closedness using *all* base changes, so q is closed immediately; no finite-presentation assumption or separate proper-base-change theorem is being smuggled in.

Step 3 uses the same base-change-surjectivity theorem on Spec Omega_t -> B, hence W_a is nonempty and supplies z. Clause (1) of `lem-pc-algebraic-closure-exists-unique` supplies the algebraic closure of kappa(z). `def-weil-geometric-point` explicitly identifies residue-field maps with geometric points. Its statement therefore gives both the upstairs geometric point and the field embedding from Omega_t; no rational-point theorem or algebraic closure inside the original field is assumed.

Step 4 spells out the closed-set argument from 01KC: q(cl{x}) is closed and contains q(x), hence contains the closed point of the local spectrum. The prime-in-maximal-ideal calculation proves the required specialization on B. The same proper definition already gives closedness, so no new Zariski lifting lemma is needed. Having chosen y, the fibre product P is formed over the *residue field of the closed point of B*. Both maps to B factor through that same point; the fibre-product universal property identifies the two versions. The one-point map Spec Omega_s -> Spec kappa(s0) is surjective, and `lem-weil-pbc-surjective-base-change` therefore guarantees a point w of P. Algebraic closure existence and the residue-field description of geometric points then give Omega'_s containing the original Omega_s and the **equality** q y_W = s_B r_s. This avoids the invalid step of choosing an unrelated algebraic closure of kappa(y) and asserting it contains Omega_s. These elementary fibre-product and residue-field manipulations are written in the step itself.

Step 5 applies `lem-weil-pbc-geometric-specialization-strict-local-lift` to W with the already chosen x_W, y_W and x specializing to y. Its exact statement provides a map from the specified Spec Omega'_t into W_(y_W) with the specified composite x_W. It does not claim uniqueness, which is not used here. The strict-local limit projections and the stalk colimit then give the displayed germ formula, and their compatibility proves well-definedness. This is for sheaves of sets; I did not cite an abelian-only specialization definition for a statement about set topoi. Step 6 uses exactly the **set-sheaf** theorem `lem-weil-pbc-sets-strictly-henselian-open-sections-stalk`, with U=T=B and the closed geometric point s_B r_s. The point remains over the maximal ideal after field enlargement, which is its only point hypothesis.
