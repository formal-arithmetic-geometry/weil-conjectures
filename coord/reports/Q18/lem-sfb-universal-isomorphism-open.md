I read spaces-more-morphisms.tex, lemma-where-isomorphism (05XD), and both preceding input statements and proofs, lemma-where-surjective-flat (05XC) and lemma-where-closed-immersion (05XA). I compared each new locus statement hypothesis by hypothesis with those sources. The surjective-flat lemma needs p flat, locally finitely presented and closed, and q locally finite type and closed; it does not need separatedness of q. The closed-immersion lemma needs p locally finite type and universally closed and q separated; it does not assume p flat. Both source conclusions quantify over arbitrary base changes, which is the essential point here. Neither new lemma is a clause asserting that the same map becomes an isomorphism, and neither new statement contains a proof.

For the Mathlib leaf I read morphisms.tex, lemma-characterize-flat-closed-immersions (04PW), including its final universal factorization assertion and proof, and descent.tex, lemma-flat-surjective-quasi-compact-monomorphism-isomorphism (06NC), including its proof. I read FlatMono.lean:25–31: the Mathlib declaration requires flatness, quasi-compactness, surjectivity and monomorphism. The last two additional instance requirements are met by any closed immersion: ClosedImmersion.lean:69 supplies IsPreimmersion, Preimmersion.lean:65 supplies Mono; ClosedImmersion.lean:151 supplies IsAffineHom, and Affine.lean:58 supplies QuasiCompact. Thus the match really is a one-line application with inferred instances. I did not use the tempting isIso_of_isClosedImmersion_of_surjective alternative, which requires a reduced target absent here. The precise declarations, files and lines are recorded in mathlib_match.how.

For Step 1 I opened the statements of def-weil-proper-morphism, def-sbc-locally-finite-presentation and def-weil-finite-type-morphism. The first actually defines universal closedness for every base change, so taking the identity gives closedness. The second provides finite polynomial presentations locally, so forgetting their finitely many relations gives finite generation as in the third. These are short definitional arguments, not uncited permanence theorems. Step 2 cites the complete new 05XC statement and verifies its five assumptions using the original hypotheses and Step 1. Step 3 cites the complete new 05XA statement and verifies its three assumptions in the same way. Step 4 uses exactly the statement of def-weil-open-immersion, which identifies the sheaf with its restriction to an open; I spelled out factorization by restricting the continuous map and sheaf map, and then used the elementary intersection-of-images condition. Step 5 cites the ordinary flat-closed-immersion criterion in its forward direction. In the reverse direction the cited definitions of surjectivity, flatness and closed immersion give those three properties directly for an isomorphism (homeomorphism, invertible stalk maps, invertible sheaf map). The previous two universal equivalences then force factorization, including on nonreduced test schemes. Every uses entry has its verbatim statement in citations.json; no step reaches into another node's proof. All non-elementary steps are below 2000 characters.

I tested the statements and argument on empty bases/test schemes, identity maps, and maps over a field. For the identity, both loci are the whole base. For the empty source and nonempty finite target over a field, the closed-immersion locus is the whole base and the surjective-flat locus is empty. The fold of two field-valued points onto one point is flat and surjective but not a closed immersion; its first locus is full and the second empty. The inclusion of one point in two points is a flat closed immersion but not surjective; the roles are reversed. The nilpotent closed immersion Spec(k) into Spec(k[e]/(e^2)), both over Spec(k), is surjective and closed but not flat as a morphism between these schemes: it must not be declared invertible merely from its points. It is excluded by the surjective-flat locus. This confirms why flatness in the final criterion and the arbitrary-test-scheme conclusions in both new nodes must be retained. None of these cases requires a reduced base, noetherianity, projectivity, or flatness of q.
