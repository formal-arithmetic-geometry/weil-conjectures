## Statement correction

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported sentence "Every matrix entry is a regular derivative of some c_{i gamma}, divided by a factorial" omits the constant monomial remainder columns. For example, take N=Spec(Q), one transverse variable z, c_{1,1}=2 and all other coefficients zero, with diagram generated by 1. At a the series is 2z. In degree zero the division matrix is the 1-by-1 remainder matrix (1), whereas every derivative of the c's is 0 or 2 (there are no coordinate derivations). Thus its entry 1 is not one of the asserted derivatives. With an empty vertex list there are not even any c's, but the degree-zero remainder matrix still has entry 1.

I corrected only that clause to "Every matrix entry is 0, 1, or a regular derivative ... divided by a factorial", which is exactly the finite coefficient-matrix construction intended in BM 7.7 and the completion of 9.6. I also made the implicit hypothesis explicit that k is a field of characteristic zero. The order, neighbourhood, division, finiteness, support and residue-extension conclusions are retained.

## Review by Q18

I checked the split against delivery-errors B--E with the sources and every cited statement open. The item proof has six actual arguments, not a single citation to a renamed assertion. Its one new node is statement-only, introduces the field, variable lists, vertices, supports, degrees and both indexed bases, and is strictly algebraic and smaller than the item. It has no proof, proof dependencies or concealed proof inside its statement. I searched before creating it, including outside this chapter and in the whole imported blueprint. All item steps are under 2000 characters.

Step 1 uses lem-trf-diagonal-taylor-faithful-flatness for existence, coordinate values, constant evaluation and extension compatibility of the centered Taylor map at every scheme point. It uses lem-trf-etale-coordinate-derivations-jacobian for regular commuting derivatives and their comparison with formal differentiation. Both statements explicitly include nonclosed points or arbitrary coordinate-valued Taylor maps. Differentiating the coefficient and taking its constant term gives the factorial coefficient formula; characteristic zero justifies division by factorials. I did not use the false assertion that the ordinary completed local ring at a nonclosed point has all the relative variables.

Step 2 is the direct order estimate: a local homomorphism carries a maximal-ideal power into the corresponding variable-ideal power; every summand has degree at least d_i on S. The specific constant-in-W coefficient c_{i,alpha_i}, nonzero at a, gives the reverse bound after its inversion. No theorem about semicontinuity or an infinitely indexed open intersection is used. Taking the derivative of order zero is allowed by the stated choice of order witness.

Step 3 uses def-trf-supported-homogeneous-division for the precise indexed columns and their supports, and lem-trf-initial-diagram-stabilization for the finite bounds separately applicable to restricted degree prefixes. I verified that the latter statement explicitly includes both prefixes and restriction to essential variables. Every square matrix is written over the one ring of regular functions, with a row-column bijection from the first-vertex partition. At a, removing complementary variables leaves the first monomial and its nonzero coefficient. In ascending exponent order no column has entries before its assigned monomial; the ordinary determinant permutation formula then gives the nonzero product of diagonal entries. The constant remainder columns are exactly why the statement correction was necessary.

Step 4 uses the statement of lem-weil-endomorphism-invertible-iff-det-nonzero, an existing Mathlib leaf, applied after explicitly identifying the two finite index sets. Thus it provides invertibility, not merely a definition of determinant. It then uses the all-degree conclusion of lem-trf-initial-diagram-stabilization over the residue field of b. Monotonicity, finite ordered vertices and the prefix/essential restriction hypotheses are those of the item. Only finitely many degrees and prefixes occur before applying stabilization; no openness outside S is falsely asserted for initial forms. The matrices have meaningful regular formulas everywhere, but they represent the initial forms where the order bound has already been established.

Step 5 uses exactly the two basis statements of the new lemma, not its future proof. The full diagram is the product with the unrestricted complementary exponent set because every vertex is zero in those coordinates; the same first-vertex assignment proves the product formula for quotient supports. I checked H_i(0,V)=h_i and the actual total degree d_i before applying it. The new lemma requires no unsupported inference that first exponents remain the same at nearby points.

Step 6 is elementary bookkeeping of the finite inversion list, empty matrices and scalar extension. A residue-field embedding maps every determinant and its inverse to the corresponding determinant and inverse, so no new open test appears. Empty vertex families give monomial bases, zero essential variables give zero positive-degree spaces, and degree-zero vertices give a nonzero scalar generator. These cases are explicitly allowed in the definitions and the new lemma.

For the new lemma I read all of BM Lemma 7.7, including the induction for (c) to (a), and checked it degree by degree. At fixed total degree, filter columns and monomials by total U degree. Replacing H(U,V) by H(0,V) changes a column only in strictly larger U degree. The diagonal blocks are the restricted basis matrices, repeated for each U monomial, so finite back substitution gives the forward implication. Conversely, specializing U=0 yields spanning of the restricted space from the full basis; the first-vertex partition gives the same finite number of indexed columns and monomials, so it is a basis. This argument is recorded here as source understanding, not inserted as a proof into the new unsplit node. It works over any field, without monotonicity. I tested H(U,V)=V+U and vertex 1, the empty list, a zero-degree vertex, zero restricted generators (where both conditions fail if that column occurs), m=0, and r=0. Scalar extension preserves each finite matrix inverse. A degree-t basis alone would not suffice to lift degree t without lower-degree bases, so the new statement correctly assumes the basis property in every degree.

The verbatim quotations in citations.json were checked against the actual statement text. The only off-unit proof tool is the existing determinant Mathlib leaf, allowed as a tool without replacing this unit's route. No circular dependency is introduced: the coordinate and completed-diagonal nodes have no dependency on this item, and stabilization does not depend on the new comparison or the item.
