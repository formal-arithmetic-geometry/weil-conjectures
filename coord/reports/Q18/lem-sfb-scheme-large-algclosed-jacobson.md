## Source error

The proof of Stacks Tag 0479 (`varieties.tex`, `lemma-make-Jacobson`) writes the maximum of the family of cardinal bounds obtained from an arbitrary affine cover. An infinite family need not have a maximum: the family of cardinals aleph_n for n in N is a counterexample to that set-theoretic assertion. This is a defect of the proof's bound selection, not of the theorem. Step 1 instead applies the existing Mathlib leaf `lem-coef-small-family-regular-bound` to the disjoint union of all affine coordinate rings, obtaining a cardinal strictly larger than every ring cardinality. It then uses one extension whose cardinality is at least this bound. Thus the strict inequality needed by 00FU holds simultaneously on every affine chart. No source statement or target statement is weakened, and no unit-level route changes.

## Supporting-node arguments for the next workers

The cardinal-enlargement node is a field construction independent of schemes. For an index set T of cardinality lambda, take the fraction field F of the polynomial domain k[X_t : t in T] and then an algebraic closure K of F, whose existence is 09GT. The variables are distinct in the polynomial ring (compare their monomial coefficients), the polynomial ring injects into its fraction field, and F injects into its algebraic closure. Hence T injects into K. The composite k -> F -> K supplies the required extension structure. All these constructions remain small in the fixed universe. Merely taking an algebraic closure of k would fail, for example with k finite and lambda uncountable; the transcendental enlargement is essential. This construction is not the literal assertion of 09GT, and its source field explicitly says it is an agent consequence of that theorem. The node itself carries a statement only, with no proof or proof dependencies, as required for a new unsplit node.

The cardinal-generator Nullstellensatz node is exactly the more general algebra input 00FU, rather than an affine version of the item. For finite I its source uses the ordinary Nullstellensatz. For infinite I, the polynomial ring has vector-space dimension |I|; the inverses 1/(t-c), for c in the base field, obstruct a transcendental element in a maximal quotient. The Jacobson conclusion follows by the source's prime-localization argument. That future split must cite the linear dimension obstruction 00FT and the prime-localization criterion, rather than repeating them without nodes. The present delivery states and searches 00FU but does not split it. Its quotient A/m formulation is justified because at a maximal ideal the residue field is precisely the quotient field.

The Jacobson node has two independent, general-purpose criteria, not either clause of the item's conclusion: one ring/spectrum equivalence and one open-cover equivalence for arbitrary topological spaces. It is a Mathlib leaf with the source lines actually read. It has no proof and needs no subsequent split.

For step 1 I opened `def-pc-scheme`, `lem-coef-small-family-regular-bound` and the new cardinal-extension statement. The scheme notion supplies affine charts; the existing cardinal lemma explicitly applies to an arbitrary small family of sets and bounds its tagged disjoint union strictly. The strict inequality |A_i| < lambda <= |K| is the hypothesis later needed in 00FU. This is where I corrected the source's nonexistent maximum. For step 2 I opened the complete statement of `lem-weil-pbc-affine-chart-base-change-iso`, not merely its title. Its W, V, A, B hypotheses hold here with W = Spec k, V the chosen affine chart, T = Spec K, A = K and B = A_i. It explicitly identifies the chart over the base change with Spec(K tensor_k A_i) and identifies the second projection with the map from K. Thus the K-structure used by the Nullstellensatz is the actual one needed in the conclusion. The elementary pure-tensor calculation exhibits a generating family indexed by the underlying set of A_i; no finite-generation claim is used. Both the Jacobson conclusion and algebraicity of maximal quotients are explicit clauses of the new 00FU statement, with the strict cardinal hypothesis already checked.

For step 3 I compared both clauses of `lem-sfb-jacobson-spectrum-open-cover` to what is taken from them. The first converts each chart ring's Jacobson property to its spectrum's density statement; the second glues those statements on the inverse-image cover. This is exactly density of globally closed points in each closed subset of U_K, rather than just density in the whole space or density of points closed only in some chart. Arbitrary covers and nonseparated schemes are allowed by these topological declarations.

For step 4 I read `def-weil-affine-spectrum` and `def-weil-residue-field`. The step supplies its own missing elementary identifications: a singleton is closed in the chart iff its prime is maximal, restricting an open does not change a stalk, and localization of a field quotient at nonzero elements changes nothing. In particular it never infers that a point closed in an arbitrary open is closed in the whole scheme. It starts with a globally closed u, so the direction used is valid. The residue-field isomorphism respects K because step 2 identifies the structural maps. No finite-type Zariski lemma is substituted for algebraicity.

For step 5 I read `def-weil-algebraic-closure` and `def-pc-field-extension`. The former defines algebraic closedness by polynomial roots; factoring a polynomial follows by degree induction, using successive root division. Evaluation in a field shows that an algebraic element of kappa(u) is the image of an element of K. The latter statement explicitly supplies injectivity of the structural field map. These give bijectivity of that particular map, not merely an abstract field isomorphism. This elementary argument avoids creating a redundant rational-residue-field node; the existing finite-dimensional extension lemma is intentionally not cited, since the input here is algebraicity without an a priori finite-dimensional bound.
