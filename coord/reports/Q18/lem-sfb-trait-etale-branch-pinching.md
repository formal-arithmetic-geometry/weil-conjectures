## Review by Q18

I read the item and each cited statement with its proof step open, and checked the sources above. I kept the original hypotheses and every conclusion. In Step 1, the projective-ample node gives an ample invertible sheaf over an arbitrary affine base, not only over a field; the projective-space properness and closed-immersion properness nodes give the properness actually needed. The finite-map node gives finite fibres, so over the two-point spectrum of a DVR the entire branch scheme has finitely many points. The ample finite-set affine-neighbourhood node therefore verifies precisely hypothesis (3) of 0E25.

In Step 2 the new theorem supplies the quotient space, simultaneous affine charts, cartesian square, complement and universal property. I did not use integrality as if it already implied finiteness: the explicit lifted-generator calculation proves that the affine map C to Y is finite because D is finite over E. This calculation works even if the branch schemes are disconnected. Step 3 uses the same new theorem's separatedness and locally-finite-type clauses with a Noetherian base; the surjective-base-change and proper-base-change statements say exactly what the inverse-image-of-a-closed-set proof needs. This proves universal closedness explicitly, rather than citing the scheme-theoretic-image node, which has a different conclusion. The locally-finite-type/finite-presentation equivalence applies over the Noetherian DVR. Quasi-compactness and separatedness give finite presentation in the project's convention.

In Step 4 I checked that A, E, D are coordinate rings of open subschemes of the given flat schemes, so they are R-flat even if those opens are not finite over R. Their product is torsion free and P is a submodule; the PID torsion-free/flat statement applies because a DVR is a local PID. For special-fibre base change the exact sequence ends in D, which is R-flat. The cited flat-quotient tensor lemma, not just the definition of flatness, gives the necessary injection after arbitrary tensoring. The displayed kernel identity uses the actual coordinate maps and therefore agrees on overlaps. No invalid claim that arbitrary closed pushouts always commute with nonflat base change is made.

In Step 5 I replaced an initially considered Galois-set equivalence citation by the more direct existing etale-fibre decomposition, which states the disjoint-union-of-finite-separable-fields description explicitly. Over an algebraically closed field all these fields are the base field. Finite module generators persist under tensoring; fixed-relative-dimension smooth permanence gives etaleness under base change. The closed-immersion base-change node and smooth base-change clause verify that the two distinct points being identified remain smooth closed points of the fibre. The field pinching lemma is applied with reference scheme Spec Omega and empty markings. Its assumptions are all present, and its statement explicitly gives semistability and the completed ring at a two-point fibre. The multipointed-curve definitions include pure dimension one and allow disconnected and empty markings. Thus this is not an unrecorded connectedness assumption.

In Step 6 the existing affine nonvanishing-section statement applies to every point of the finite set B, not just to rational or closed points. A section nonzero in a rank-one residue fibre is a local frame; this elementary local calculation produces U_s. The finite-map closedness node makes W open. The cartesian square identifies the whole inverse image of N with B, so W contains N. In Step 7 I checked the gluing maps on the overlap of W and Y minus N, including their inverse; the module-sheaf gluing theorem gives M, local frames give invertibility, and the two stated pullback identifications agree. This proves a^*M=L and does not assume arbitrary descent of line bundles along a finite map.

Step 8 uses 0B5V for a specified invertible sheaf with ample pullback. Both schemes have already been proved proper over the Noetherian DVR. I did not use the normal-target existence-of-an-ample-bundle theorem: Y is typically not normal. The affine-base ample/very-ample node supplies an immersion into finite projective space. Proper-to-separated applies since that projective space is proper and hence separated. An immersion is left cancellable by its open/closed factorization, and the exact proper-monomorphism/closed-immersion equivalence (04XV) gives a closed immersion. In Step 9 the new theorem states the open-restriction property verbatim; a pushout in all schemes supplies arbitrary compatible maps, not merely S-maps.

Small cases checked: empty B and N give Y=C and P=A; two points on one smooth component create a self-node; points on two different components create a node joining them; disconnected initial fibres remain allowed; nonsplit degree-two branches are dealt with before passing to geometric fibres; characteristic two does not require division by two or a trace splitting; a pre-existing node is unchanged because B lies in the relative smooth locus. The special fibre is a nonflat base change and is covered by the flat-last-term exact sequence. Nonexcellent DVRs cause no problem: no normalization finiteness or regularity of completion is assumed.
