## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported assertion that equality after an exceptional-intersection test forces equality at its image is false, even for a reduced cusp. It is removed, while the Hilbert bound and boundary regularity are retained. This is a correction to the source's intended test calculus, not an added hypothesis.

Take X=V(y^2-x^3) in A^2_Q, with initial maximum H0(n)=2n+1. Blow up its origin, an admissible center for an actual Hilbert presentation. On the x-chart put x=e, y=ev. The strict transform is v^2-e=0, a smooth curve. The newly born boundary is e=0. Add an independent line coordinate t, appending t=0 to the boundary. At the origin of this smooth surface, the extended Hilbert function is binomial(n+2,2), strictly below L=Lambda H0=(n+1)^2 (already 3<4 at n=1). Now blow up the intersection e=t=0 in the smooth ambient chart and take the prescribed ordinary pullback. On its t-chart put t=u, e=uw. The pullback of X is v^2-uw=0. At u=v=w=0 its associated graded ring is Q[u,v,w]/(v^2-uw), so its cumulative function is (n+1)^2=L. Thus a dropped level returns. All points are rational closed points, so no transcendence-degree convention is involved. This sequence starts with empty boundary. The exceptionality test is allowed regardless of cosupport. On the returned equality germ the two boundary equations u,w ARE regular: quotienting by u gives Q[v,w]/(v^2), and w is injective there. This example separates the false clause from the surviving regularity assertion.

The same example explains why the certificate node's assertion that saturation cannot restore a drop cannot be used for a NONFLAT ordinary boundary pullback: substitution e=uw precedes saturation and can restore the level. The delivered proof makes no such inference.
