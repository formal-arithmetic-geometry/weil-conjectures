## Statement dispute and corrections

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the new detail omits the quasi-compactness required by its own referenced refinement definition. The actual current documents are main-20261015/sk-def-nagata-sketch-data/def-nagata-compactification.md (open immersion, no quasi-compactness), def-nagata-dense-compactification.md (global scheme-theoretic dominance, injectivity only when quasi-compact), and det-nagata-definition-refinement.md (both j,j' explicitly quasi-compact). Thus the detail's phrase “so h is a refinement” does not follow from its object hypotheses. Its imported proof also uses injectivity without checking the hypothesis under which the new density definition supplies it.

Explicit test: let A=k[x_1,x_2,...], S=Spec(A), and X=union_{n>=1} D(x_n). Take both ambient spaces to be S, both structure maps to be id_S, both open maps to be X→S, and h=id_S. The ambient structure maps are proper. A is a domain and X contains the generic point, so restriction on every nonempty affine open is injective; in particular X is scheme-theoretically dominant. But X→S is not quasi-compact. Indeed a finite collection of the D(x_n) misses a prime containing precisely those finitely many variables and not another variable. Therefore the displayed family of principal opens has no finite subcover. This is a dense compactification under the updated definition, while it is outside the object hypotheses of det-nagata-definition-refinement. No change to h or its inverse-image equality supplies the missing quasi-compactness.

Repair: made j and j' quasi-compact explicitly, with the meaning written inline, and retained all three conclusions. This is the setting required by the refinement definition and by the unit's finite-type route; it restores the hypothesis formerly supplied by the old compactification definition. The current unit _section.md concerns separated finite-type f over qcqs S, so this repair does not remove any case used by that unit. I did not edit the definitions, unit metadata, or target nodes. This is a reported missing-hypothesis correction, not a silent weakening of a target.

The imported proof's first paragraph and the section-and-density route remain. Step 1 uses the existing graph-factorization theorem instead of repeating it without citations. Step 2 recognizes the restriction as a proper base change, supplying separatedness for the same section argument. Step 4 now checks quasi-compactness before using the injective sheaf map. The five steps do not introduce a new node for any clause of the item or for the elementary sheaf-restriction calculation.

For comparison, Stacks morphisms.tex, “Scheme theoretic closure and density”, explicitly warns that global scheme-theoretic closure equal to the whole scheme need not mean density on all opens without quasi-compactness. I read its example A=k[x,z_1,z_2,...]/(x^n z_n), U=Spec(A)\V(z_1,z_2,...), and its quasi-compact-density and sheaf-injectivity lemmas.
