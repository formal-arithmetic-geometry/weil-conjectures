## Argument available for the new lemma

This is supporting material in the report, not a proof embedded in its unsplit node. The representation is a morphism, so its upper-unipotent orbit is a polynomial. Compare full diagonal characters over the Laurent polynomial test algebra: the coefficient of z^i has diagonal weight -q+2i, hence belongs to V_i and all other coefficients vanish. Evaluate at z=0 to obtain v_0=v. Compare coefficients in x_+(s)x_+(t)v=x_+(s+t)v to obtain x_+(s)v_i=sum_j binom(i+j,j)s^j v_(i+j). No factorial cancellation occurs.

For Weyl reversal, in the Laurent polynomial algebra use the determinant-one matrix identity

`w x_+(t) = x_+(-t^{-1}) h(-t^{-1}) x_-(t^{-1})`.

The last factor fixes v. The middle factor multiplies v by (-t)^q. Thus the right side acting on v is sum_i (-1)^(q+i)t^(q-i)v_i. The left side is sum_i t^i w(v_i). Comparing Laurent coefficients yields w(v_i)=(-1)^i v_(q-i). Finally conjugate the upper formula by w, using w x_+(s) w^{-1}=x_-(-s), to get the lower formula, including the binomial coefficient binom(q-i+j,j). These are comparisons of free polynomial/Laurent monomials, valid over every commutative ring and for zero vectors. A basis of orbit coefficients is never asserted. This is a genuine general representation lemma; the new node says nothing about reductive groups, tangent brackets, root-module generators or signed rank-two vectors.
