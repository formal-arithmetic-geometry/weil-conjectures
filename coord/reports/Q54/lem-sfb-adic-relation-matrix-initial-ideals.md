I checked that argument directly: for a in I^r M with g'(a) in I^n N and r<n-c, congruence gives g(a) in I^(r+c+1)N. The bound for g supplies a1 in I^(r+1)M with g(a1)=g(a). Exactness gives a-a1=f(z). When r>=c the bound for f lets z be chosen in I^(r-c)L; when r<c an arbitrary z suffices. Consequently (f-f')(z) lies in I^(r+1)M in both cases. Subtracting f'(z) replaces a by an element of I^(r+1)M without changing g'(a), using g'f'=0. Starting at r=0 and repeating finitely many times reaches r=n-c. This is a genuine lemma about division bounds, rather than a clause of the initial-ideal equality.
