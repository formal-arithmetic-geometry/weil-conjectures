The original four proof-step texts from the current repository are preserved verbatim below. The algebra child owns Step 3's R-module colimit construction from representatives/eventual equality, tensor-colimit assembly, pure-tensor computation and upgrade to an algebra isomorphism. ### Original step 1

Write the given intersection as $E=\bigcap_{i\in I}U_i$ with each $U_i$ quasi-compact open. Replace $I$ by the nonempty family of all finite intersections of the $U_i$; then the resulting family has the same intersection and is directed by reverse inclusion. Since inverse image commutes with arbitrary intersections, $E_T=\bigcap_i g^{-1}(U_i)$, and each $g^{-1}(U_i)$ is quasi-compact because $g$ is affine (it is the base change of $\operatorname{Spec}T\to\operatorname{Spec}R$).

### Original step 2

Apply [lem-a1-sections-intersection-structure-colimit] to $X=Y$ with the family $(U_i)$ and to $X=Y_T$ with the family $(g^{-1}(U_i))$, which is again directed by reverse inclusion and has intersection $E_T$ by Step 1. Clauses (a) and (b) identify $\Gamma(E,\mathcal O_Y|_E)$ and $\Gamma(E_T,\mathcal O_{Y_T}|_{E_T})$, through the restriction maps $\rho_i$ and $\rho^T_i$, with the filtered colimits of the section rings over $U_i$ and over $g^{-1}(U_i)$, as sets, abelian groups and rings. The quasi-separatedness hypothesis needed for the second application holds because $Y_T\to Y$ is affine, hence quasi-separated, and $Y$ is quasi-separated, so $Y_T$ is quasi-separated; every $g^{-1}(U_i)$ is a quasi-compact open of $Y_T$ by Step 1.

### Original step 3

Put $A_i=\Gamma(U_i,\mathcal O_Y|_{U_i})$, $B_i=\Gamma(g^{-1}(U_i),\mathcal O_{Y_T}|_{g^{-1}(U_i)})$, $C=\Gamma(E,\mathcal O_Y|_E)$, $C_T=\Gamma(E_T,\mathcal O_{Y_T}|_{E_T})$; all are $R$-algebras through $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}R$, and the restriction maps are $R$-algebra maps. By clauses (a), (b) of Step 2, $C$ with the $R$-linear maps $\rho_i$ is the colimit of the $A_i$ in $R$-modules: given compatible $R$-linear $\mu_i:A_i\to M$, the map $u(\rho_i(a))=\mu_i(a)$ is well defined by (b) and directedness, defined everywhere by (a), and $R$-linear because $r\rho_i(a)=\rho_i(ra)$; uniqueness is (a). The same holds for $C_T$ and the $B_i$. For every $i$, apply [lem-weil-pbc-flat-base-change-global-sections-qcqs] to the quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme $U_i$ over $\operatorname{Spec}R$ (quasi-separated as an open of $Y$) and the flat morphism $\operatorname{Spec}T\to\operatorname{Spec}R$; the fibre product $U_i\times_{\operatorname{Spec}R}\operatorname{Spec}T$ is the open subscheme $g^{-1}(U_i)$ of $Y_T$, so it gives a ring isomorphism $\varphi_i:A_i\otimes_RT\to B_i$, $a\otimes t\mapsto g^\sharp(a)\,t$. Since pullback of sections along $g$ commutes with restriction, $\varphi_k\circ(\mathrm{res}\otimes1)=\mathrm{res}\circ\varphi_i$ for $U_k\subseteq U_i$ (check on pure tensors), so the $\varphi_i$ form an isomorphism of directed systems of $R$-modules. By [lem-weil-pbc-tensor-commutes-with-colimits], $C\otimes_RT$ with the maps $\rho_i\otimes1$ is the colimit of the $A_i\otimes_RT$. Hence there is a unique $R$-linear bijection $\Phi:C\otimes_RT\to C_T$ with $\Phi\circ(\rho_i\otimes1)=\rho^T_i\circ\varphi_i$. For $c=\rho_i(a)$ this gives $\Phi(c\otimes t)=\rho^T_i(g^\sharp(a)t)=g^\sharp(c)\,t$, since the comorphism of $g|_{E_T}:(E_T,\mathcal O_{Y_T}|_{E_T})\to(E,\mathcal O_Y|_E)$ commutes with the restrictions; by (a) every $c$ is of this form. So $\Phi$ is the displayed map, and by [lem-weil-pbc-algebra-tensor-pushout-universal-property] (2), (3), applied to the $R$-algebra maps $g^\sharp:C\to C_T$ and $T\to C_T$, it is the unique $T$-algebra homomorphism with that pure-tensor formula; being bijective, it is an isomorphism of $T$-algebras.

### Original step 4

All maps used above are the canonical restriction, pullback, and tensor maps. A scheme isomorphism carrying the indicated opens commutes with restriction and pullback, so the stagewise isomorphisms and their filtered-colimit assembly are natural. For a second flat affine base change, the two stagewise maps both send $c\otimes t\otimes s$ to the pullback of $c$ multiplied by the two scalars; tensor-product associativity identifies the source, and functoriality of pullback identifies the target. Thus the canonical isomorphisms commute with iterated base change and with tensor-product associativity.
