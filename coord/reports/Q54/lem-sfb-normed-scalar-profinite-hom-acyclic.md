### Original step 1 → lem-sfb-profinite-free-hom-resolution-evaluation

The sectionwise tensor right adjoint is exactly the slice sheaf Hom of [def-pur-sheaf-hom], by [lem-pc-sheaf-modules-tensor-closed]; thus this is the transported internal Hom. The abelian sheaf category is Grothendieck, with additive closed tensor, by [lem-weil-pbc-sheaf-tensor-closed] applied to $\mathcal A=\operatorname{Ab}$. The internal module category is consequently Grothendieck by [lem-sfb-modobj-grothendieck], so [lem-sp-grothendieck-enough-injectives] supplies a nonnegative injective resolution $A\to I^\bullet$. An exact equivalence preserves injectivity, since its inverse carries monomorphisms to monomorphisms and its Hom bijections carry the injective lifting property to the same property. We may therefore compute in sectionwise $A$-modules.

### Original step 2 → lem-sfb-profinite-free-hom-resolution-evaluation

For an $A$-module $N$ and a profinite $S$, the definition [def-pur-sheaf-hom] gives $\mathcal Hom_A(A[P],N)(S)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A|_S}(A[P]|_S,N|_S)$. A map on the right is specified by the image of the universal generator: it is a section $x\in N(S\times P)$. Explicitly a generator over $U\to S$ given by $p:U\to P$ is sent to $(U\to S\times P)^*x$, and a linear combination $\sum_i a_i[p_i]$ is sent to $\sum_i a_i(U\to S\times P)^*_ix$. Conversely evaluating on the tautological $P$-coordinate of $S\times P$ recovers $x$. These operations are inverse, natural in $N,S,P$, and respect sheafification by the defining universal property of the free internal module $A[P]$. Thus the complex $J^\bullet=\mathcal Hom_A(A[P],I^\bullet)$ has $J^n(S)=I^n(S\times P)$, with the same differentials. At $N=A$ this gives $C(S\times P,F)$, and scalar multiplication uses the $S$-coordinate. By derivation in the second variable using the chosen nonnegative injective resolution, $J^\bullet$ represents the required derived Hom. The comparison map on $S$ is $A(S\times P)[0]\to I^\bullet(S\times P)$ induced by the resolution augmentation.
