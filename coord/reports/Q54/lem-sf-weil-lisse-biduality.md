### Returned step 1

Work with the actual adic ring sheaf $R$ of the statement. The native module-object description and sectionwise $R$-modules have the same data: an action $R\otimes_{\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell}M\to M$ is exactly a compatible sectionwise $R(U)$-action; conversely each such action is balanced over constant $\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$-scalars, hence gives that tensor map. Morphisms agree because $R$-linearity includes linearity over constant scalars. The native relative tensor and the sectionwise sheaf tensor both represent the same balanced bilinear maps. Apply [lem-pc-sheaf-modules-tensor-closed]: its right adjoint is the slice-Hom sheaf of [def-pur-sheaf-hom]. Uniqueness of the right adjoint identifies the native internal Hom used by `WeilSheaf.dual` with this sheaf, preserving evaluation. Explicitly the adjunction in both descriptions sends a bilinear map $b$ to $y\mapsto(x\mapsto b(x,y))$, so their evaluation is $(x,\lambda)\mapsto\lambda(x)$. This argument applies on every slice and uses the ring $R$, not a discrete constant coefficient sheaf.

### Returned step 2

For every site object $U$ and $x\in M(U)$, define $\delta_M(x)$ to be the map $D(M)|_U\to R|_U$ whose component on $V\to U$ sends $\lambda$ to $\lambda(x|_V)$. This is $R|_U$-linear, commutes with restriction, and is linear in $x$, so gives a global module map $\delta_M:M\to D^2(M)$. The slice-Hom definition [def-pur-sheaf-hom] also gives $D(M)|_U=D_{R|_U}(M|_U)$ by identifying $(\mathcal C/U)/(V\to U)$ with $\mathcal C/V$; the same holds for the double dual and for $\delta_M$. For a module map $a:M\to N$, evaluation gives $D^2(a)\delta_M=\delta_Na$, since both sides applied to $x$ and then to $\lambda\in D(N)$ give $\lambda(a(x))$.

Cited statements: def-pur-sheaf-hom. Source: literature/stacks-project/sites-modules.tex, Tag 03EM, lemma-internal-hom, and Tag 0E8H, lemma-internal-hom-restriction; evaluation immediately preceding lemma-internal-hom..

### Returned step 3

Take a trivializing covering $\{U_i\to\bar X\}$ for $M$. Clause (1) of [lem-weil-proetale-weakly-etale-localization] identifies the pro-etale site of $U_i$ with the slice. Clause (1) of [lem-sfb-proetale-object-inverse-image-restriction] identifies inverse image of the underlying abelian sheaf with restriction. The coefficient identification here is also literal restriction: both rings on a further object $V$ consist of the same locally finite functions on $V$. Thus the native module pullback trivializations are precisely the slice trivializations used below. On its $i$th member put $S=R|_{U_i}$ and choose a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ of $M|_{U_i}\cong S^r$. On every further slice, a linear functional $\lambda$ is uniquely $\sum_j\lambda(e_j)e_j^*$, so $D(M)|_{U_i}\cong S^r$ with dual basis $e_j^*$. In particular the dual is lisse on the same cover, so it is a valid input to the second `WeilSheaf.dual`. For a section $\eta$ of $D^2(M)$ on this slice, set $v=\sum_j\eta(e_j^*)e_j$. Then $\delta_M(v)(\lambda)=\sum_j\eta(e_j^*)\lambda(e_j)=\eta(\lambda)$, while this formula applied to $\delta_M(x)$ returns $x$. It is compatible with further restriction. Thus it gives a two-sided inverse of $\delta_M|_{U_i}$. When $r=0$ both objects are zero and the empty sum gives the inverse.

### Returned step 4

Forget the $R$-action on $\delta_M$ and apply [lem-sfb-sheaf-cover-local-isomorphism] to its underlying abelian sheaves and the objects $U_i$. Its covering hypothesis holds: base-changing the chosen cover to any object $V$ gives a covering sieve of arrows with domains mapping to some $U_i$, and the sieve of all such arrows is larger and therefore covering. Smallness is arranged by [def-weil-size-convention]. Step 3 gives the required isomorphisms of all slice restrictions, so the underlying map is invertible. Its inverse $h$ is $R$-linear: $\delta_M(h(a\eta))=a\eta=\delta_M(a h(\eta))$ and injectivity gives $h(a\eta)=a h(\eta)$, with additivity and restriction compatibility inherited from the inverse sheaf map. Hence $\delta_M$ is an isomorphism of $R$-modules. This also covers an empty scheme or a zero-rank chart.

### Returned step 5

It remains to check the specific Weil structures, not just the underlying modules. Since $T$ reindexes slices along the automorphism $g^{-1}$, the slice-Hom formula [def-pur-sheaf-hom] identifies $TD(M)$ with $\mathcal Hom_{TR}(TM,TR)$, and $T\delta_M$ is the same evaluation formula over $TR$. Write $s:TR\xrightarrow{\sim}R$ for the coefficient identification and $a=\theta:TM\xrightarrow{\sim}M$. Unpacking the dual structure in the statement gives $b:TD(M)\xrightarrow{\sim}D(M)$, $b(\mu)=s\circ\mu\circ a^{-1}$. The next dual structure is $c:TD^2(M)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^2(M)$, $c(\zeta)=s\circ\zeta\circ b^{-1}$. On every slice, for $v$ a section of $TM$ and $\mu$ a section of $TD(M)$, one has
$$c(T\delta_M(v))(b\mu)=s(\mu(v))=(b\mu)(av)=\delta_M(av)(b\mu).$$
Since $b$ is an isomorphism, these evaluations prove $c\circ T\delta_M=\delta_M\circ a$. This is exactly the morphism condition of [def-weil-endofunctor-fixed-pair-category]. No cover invariant under Frobenius is required.

Cited statements: def-pur-sheaf-hom, def-weil-endofunctor-fixed-pair-category. Source: lean/WeilConjectures/WeilSheaf/Dual.lean, dualStr and dual, lines 208-235: inverse Weil structure in the first variable and coefficient structure in the second; agent expansion of their double application..

### Returned step 6

Let $h=\delta_M^{-1}$. From $\delta_M a=cT(\delta_M)$, multiplying on the left by $h$ and on the right by $T(h)$ yields $aT(h)=hc$. Thus $h$ too is a morphism of fixed pairs [def-weil-endofunctor-fixed-pair-category]. Its two inverse identities are those of the underlying module isomorphism, since the forgetful functor is faithful. Therefore $\delta_M$ and $h$ form an isomorphism $P\cong(P^\vee)^\vee$ of lisse Weil sheaves, which supplies the nonempty isomorphism asserted in the item.
