## Review by Q54

I read BLR's statements and proofs, rather than treating Corollary 4 as a black-box node. For the bound node, I checked the proof on printed pp.66–67: after splitting the smooth generic fibre by dimension, its Jacobian minors generate the unit ideal after inverting the uniformizer, so a fixed power of the uniformizer bounds each section's minimum minor valuation. Finitely many dimensions and affine charts give one bound. This needs neither excellence nor a bound on rational points in the generic fibre. For the filtration node I read Lemma 3.3/4 and the canonical-partition paragraphs of 3.4: separable evaluation detects functions on the reduced closure, hence geometric reducedness; smoothness and generic freeness give the dense open, and the successive complements terminate. The coherent-module generalization just replaces the module of relative differentials in that construction; it does not assert a locally free sheaf on a nonreduced closure.

For the blowup node I checked 0804 and 0806 in the local Stacks source and BLR 3.2: since the ideal contains pi, its pullback to a DVR section is either the unit ideal or (pi), so is invertible and has a unique lift to the blowup. In the latter case pi generates the pulled-back ideal and selects the pi chart. Each chart is a subalgebra of B[1/a]; when B is R-torsion-free so is the chart, and torsion-free modules over the PID R are flat. Finite generators give finite-type charts; properness is the finite-ideal blowup theorem. None of these arguments assumes that X is integral or smooth, or that the centre is reduced.

Step 1 cites the actual algebraic/geometric strict-henselization comparison, the geometric strict-local-ring DVR theorem, and the maximal-ideal theorem. Their statements give the particular A and the particular ideal pi A needed later. The PID-flatness theorem is applied only after the quotient B_0 has been embedded in B[1/pi]. Each original section kills the pi-power torsion because pi remains nonzero in A. The quotient is canonical under restriction, so it gives a global closed subscheme, not just unrelated affine models.

Step 2 uses exactly the per-section zero criterion and the new uniform bound. The existing criterion has flatness and local finite type, both now established. Step 3 uses the finite-type-over-Noetherian theorem for the finite-type models over the Noetherian DVR and the relative-differential theorem for quasi-coherence and finite generation. On Noetherian affine charts the modules are finitely presented; the filtration therefore applies. The new filtration is a statement of the density and free/smooth open assertions actually used, not a citation to their proof.

Step 4 checks all hypotheses of the existing permissible-dilatation estimate: the scheme is flat with smooth generic fibre, Z_t satisfies the evaluation-kernel condition, and U_t is smooth with locally free restricted differentials. The blowup node, not the group-dilatation definition, identifies the lift with the correct chart. Thus the estimate really is about the same lifted section and differential module. Step 5 uses the filtration's last-part separation and the blowup's isomorphism off the centre. The support invariant states why outer induction modifications affect only the last part; it is not an assumption of the induction. Step 6 spells out the locality argument for the second induction: away from Z_t all remaining section germs and coherent-module restrictions are unchanged, so the earlier membership tests persist and at most t-1 parts remain. New exceptional points that are not reductions of remaining sections cannot create a new partition part. Every later centre is nonsmooth, preserving the sections already made smooth.

Step 7 uses the bound to start the construction and the blowup node at every stage to obtain the bijection on A-points. The zero criterion and the elementary local-spectrum argument put the entire section in the smooth open, not just its closed point. Step 8 uses clause (b) of the open-restriction smoothness theorem, which really permits changing the target from Spec K to Spec R through its generic open. The Noetherian open-subscheme theorem supplies quasi-compactness, and finite generation is then verified on finitely many principal affine opens. The diagonal argument gives separatedness. No properness assertion about the final smooth open is made.

I tested the empty scheme, an empty set of sections, an already smooth model (both inductions terminate immediately), and Y=Spec(R/(pi)) (the explicit generic closure is empty and there are no A-sections). The basic nonzero-defect example xy=pi^2 with section (pi,pi) has defect one and its pi chart xy=1 has a smooth lifted section; this tests that it is the full special-fibre centre blowup and the pi chart, not a group translation, that the estimate uses. The filtration allows imperfect k, using separable points rather than assuming a perfect residue field. The statements and proof also allow disconnected and non-equidimensional generic fibres.
