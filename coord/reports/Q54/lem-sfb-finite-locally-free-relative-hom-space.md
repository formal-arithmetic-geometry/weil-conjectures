## Supporting arguments retained for the new unsplit nodes

These are explanatory report material, not proofs inserted into statement-only nodes.

For Tag 05Y6 I read `more-morphisms.tex`, `lemma-hom-from-finite-free-into-affine`, including the complete polynomial construction and the omitted-details warning. If B has basis e_j and C has presentation R[x_i]/(f_k), form P=R[a_ij]. Send x_i to sum_j a_ij tensor e_j in P tensor_R B, write each image f_k as sum_j c_kj tensor e_j, and take M=Spec(P/(c_kj)). Every R'-algebra map C to R' tensor_R B determines unique coefficients alpha_ij; it factors through C precisely when all evaluated c_kj vanish. Thus it determines a unique point of M, and conversely. The coefficient constructions commute with R'-algebra homomorphisms. Affine tests cover every scheme test, so maps and inverse maps glue by the sheaf property. This proves the natural bijection for all tests. Arbitrarily many x_i and f_k are allowed; m=0 gives M=T. This is a construction of an affine Hom scheme, rather than a restatement of the arbitrary algebraic-space target item.

For Tag 05XR I read `criteria.tex`, `lemma-space-of-sections`, both proof paragraphs. In the separated case a section's image is an open finite part of W, and the section is recovered as the inverse to that part's isomorphism with Z. The finite-open-parts algebraic space and the open isomorphism locus give representability and etaleness. In the general case use a separated scheme atlas W' of W; the section-space comparison has scheme-test fibres given by sections of the pulled-back separated etale map. These fibres are algebraic and etale by the first case, and local sections from 05XQ give surjectivity; bootstrap then proves algebraicity and etaleness. This is a special-target section-space theorem, not the full arbitrary-target Hom theorem. It assumes no separation condition on q; the new node's representability hypothesis on q is sufficient for our applications to the target atlas.

For Tag 05XQ I read `criteria.tex`, `lemma-surjection-space-of-sections`, including its finite-neighbourhood argument. Choose a separated scheme atlas of W. At each point u, there are finitely many z_i over u; choose w_i over them. Etale localization of the base splits off the relevant finite parts for Z and W simultaneously, with purely inseparable residue extensions over the new base point. Remove the complementary closed finite image to retain the selected parts of Z. The selected finite etale maps W_i to Z_i have a single point in the relevant fibre and equal residue fields: their residue extension is both separable and purely inseparable. They are isomorphisms after shrinking near those points; closedness of Z over the base permits a further base shrink ensuring the whole selected Z_i is in that neighbourhood. Their inverses assemble to the required section. Taking the coproduct of these etale neighbourhoods gives a surjective etale U' to U. This theorem requires only finiteness of Z to U; it does not require flatness and does not assert algebraicity of the section functor.
