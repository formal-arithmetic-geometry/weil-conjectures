## Original returned argument, retained verbatim

### Original step 1

The finite-type-algebra Noetherian theorem [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian] applied to $R\to P$ makes $P$ Noetherian: it is generated as an $R$-algebra by the $n$ displayed variables. The given surjection makes $A$ a cyclic $P$-module. Indeed, for $a\in A$ choose $p\in P$ mapping to $a$; then $a=p\cdot1$. Thus $A[0]$ is a bounded complex of finitely generated $P$-modules.

### Original step 2

By [def-weil-pbc-flat-ring-map], the degree-zero complex $A[0]$ is a bounded-above complex of flat $R$-modules (zero terms are flat since their tensor functor is zero). By [lem-pc-module-bounded-flat-kflat] it is K-flat. By [lem-pc-source-kflat-module-tensor-model], for any $R$-module $L$ the derived tensor $A\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}L$ is represented by $(A\otimes_RL)[0]$, so it has cohomology only in degree zero. This proves the additional finite-Tor assertion over $R$, with interval $[0,0]$. To express the same information using nonnegative Tor, apply [lem-pc-flat-resolution-tor-comparison] to the nonnegative chain complex consisting of $A$ in degree zero and zero elsewhere, augmented by its identity to $A$. Its tensor with $L$ has zero homology in every positive degree; hence $\operatorname{Tor}^R_j(A,L)=0$ for $j>0$.

### Original step 3

Apply [lem-pc-polynomial-tor-bound] with base ring $R$, polynomial module $A$ and $d=0$. Step 2 gives its vanishing hypothesis for every base module, with exactly the first-variable Tor convention in that node. It follows that $\operatorname{Tor}^P_j(A,N)=0$ for every $P$-module $N$ and every $j>n$. This is the polynomial-extension homological bound underlying Tag 068X; neither a regularity nor a local complete-intersection assumption on $A$ is used.

### Original step 4

By [lem-weil-pbc-finite-free-resolution-noetherian], applied to the Noetherian ring $P$ and cyclic module $A$ of Step 1, choose an augmented finite-free resolution $F_\bullet\to A$. Put $F_j$ in cochain degree $-j$ and zero in positive degrees, keeping the differentials. Exactness of the augmentation gives $F^\bullet\simeq A[0]$. Its terms are flat by [lem-sc-affine-standard-cover-finite-free-local-fp-flat], so [lem-pc-module-bounded-flat-kflat] makes $F^\bullet$ K-flat. The tensor model [lem-pc-source-kflat-module-tensor-model] computes $A\otimes_P^{\mathbf L}N$ by $F^\bullet\otimes_PN$. By [lem-pc-flat-resolution-tor-comparison] its degree $-j$ cohomology is $\operatorname{Tor}^P_j(A,N)$ for $j\ge0$ (the cochain indexing is exactly the reversal of the chain indexing). Therefore Step 3 gives vanishing below $-n$; vanishing above zero holds because the representing complex has zero terms there. Hence $A[0]$ has Tor amplitude in $[-n,0]$ over $P$.

### Original step 5

Apply [lem-weil-bounded-finite-tor-perfect] over $P$ to $A[0]$ and the interval $[-n,0]$. Step 1 verifies Noetherianity, boundedness and finite generation, and Step 4 gives its derived-tensor vanishing for every $P$-module. Thus there is a finite-projective complex $E^\bullet$ supported in $[-n,0]$ isomorphic to $A[0]$ in $D(P)$, which is perfectness in the sense of [def-pc-perfect-ring-complex]. The isomorphism induces $H^0(E)\cong A$ and $H^i(E)=0$ for $i\ne0$. Since $E^1=0$, compose $E^0\to E^0/\operatorname{im}d^{-1}=H^0(E)$ with this isomorphism to obtain an augmentation $E^0\to A$. It is surjective with kernel $\operatorname{im}d^{-1}$; in every negative degree vanishing cohomology gives exactness, by [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]. The augmented finite complex is the required bounded resolution by finitely generated projective $P$-modules. This includes $n=0$, when the model has only a degree-zero term.
