### Original step 1 → lem-sfb-slice-scalar-hom-chain-adjunction

Work throughout with the underlying sheaves of abelian groups and their given module actions. On the localized site $\mathcal C/W$, restriction of scalars is sectionwise restriction along $R|_W\to S|_W$. The tensor-restriction isomorphism identifies $(EA)|_W$ with $S|_W\otimes_{R|_W}(A|_W)$ and sends every sheafification-unit image of $s\otimes x$ to that same generator. It respects the $S|_W$-action: multiplication by a local scalar agrees on each presheaf generator, hence agrees on the sheafifications by their universal property. These identifications apply separately to every term of any complex. All generator calculations below are made over every object of every slice, followed by the sheafification universal property; they do not assert that every section of a sheaf tensor is a tensor of global sections.

### Original step 2 → lem-sfb-slice-scalar-hom-chain-adjunction

For module sheaves $A,B$ over any one of these slices, an $R$-linear sheaf map $h:A\to UB$ gives the sectionwise $S$-linear presheaf map $s\otimes x\mapsto s h(x)$ by the ordinary module scalar-Hom bijection. Restrictions commute with it since $(s h(x))|_Z=s|_Z h(x|_Z)$. Sheafification therefore extends it uniquely to $EA\to B$. Conversely $g:EA\to B$ gives $x\mapsto g(1\otimes x)$. The composites are identities: on $A$ this is $1h(x)=h(x)$; on the tensor presheaf it is $s g(1\otimes x)=g(s\otimes x)$, and equality passes uniquely to its sheafification. This proves the normalized slice adjunction used below, including its inverse formula, directly from the cited module bijection and sheafification. Precomposition, postcomposition, and further restriction commute with both formulas by substitution.

### Original step 3 → lem-sfb-slice-scalar-hom-chain-adjunction

For each degree $i$, the $R$-sheaf $S\otimes_R A^i$ and $UE(A^i)$ have the same underlying abelian sheaf: both are the abelian sheafification of $W\mapsto S(W)\otimes_{R(W)}A^i(W)$. Module sheafification supplies the actions extending those on this presheaf; after restriction the two $R$-actions coincide. The identity on that presheaf induces mutually inverse $R$-linear maps by uniqueness of sheafification. This is $\nu_A^i$ and has the required value on generators. It commutes with the differential, since either composite sends $s\otimes x$ to $s\otimes d_Ax$, and is natural by the identical calculation with a map in place of $d_A$.

### Original step 4 → lem-sfb-slice-scalar-hom-chain-adjunction

This uses products of the coordinate bijections, not any preservation of products by $E$.

### Original step 5 → lem-sfb-slice-scalar-hom-chain-adjunction

For a homogeneous family $g$ of degree $n$, the $i$-coordinate of $\theta(dg)$ evaluated at $p$ equals $d_N(g^i(1\otimes p))-(-1)^n g^{i+1}(1\otimes d_Pp)$, since $d_{EP}(1\otimes p)=1\otimes d_Pp$. ### Original step 6 → lem-sfb-scalar-hom-chain-evaluation

Write $B=\mathcal Hom_R^\bullet(P,J)$. On a slice and in degrees $n,i$, the expression $(s,f,p)\mapsto s\otimes f^i(p)$ gives an $R$-balanced map in $(s,f)$ and an $R$-linear sheaf map in $p$. Indeed $\varphi(r)s\otimes f^i(p)=s\otimes r f^i(p)=s\otimes(rf)^i(p)$, and $f^i(rp)=r f^i(p)$. The tensor universal property followed by module sheafification gives, for each $i$, a map $S\otimes_R B^n\to\mathcal Hom_R(P^i,S\otimes_RJ^{i+n})$. Compatibility with slice restriction follows by restricting $s,f,p$ in the displayed formula. The product universal property assembles these maps into $\alpha_S^n$. Evaluating its differential at $p$ gives $s\otimes(d_J f^i(p)-(-1)^n f^{i+1}(d_Pp))$, which is $\alpha_S(s\otimes df)^i(p)$. Hence $\alpha_S$ is the asserted chain map. No exchange of tensor and product is used.

### Original step 8 → lem-sfb-scalar-hom-chain-evaluation

The differential of $\sigma_q(s\otimes f)$ evaluated at $t\otimes p\in EP^i$ is $st[d_Nq^{i+n}(1\otimes f^i(p))-(-1)^n q^{i+n+1}(1\otimes f^{i+1}(d_Pp))]$. If another $S$-linear map has the stated values, then for each local $s,f$ all its product coordinates agree with $\sigma_q$ on the tensor presheaf of $EP^i$; they agree on its sheafification. They then agree for every presheaf generator of $E B^n$, so again agree on its sheafification. This proves uniqueness.

### Original step 11 → lem-sfb-scalar-hom-chain-coefficient-coherence

For coefficient compatibility, take a commuting square $a:R\to R'$, $b:S\to S'$, $\varphi':R'\to S'$ with $b\varphi=\varphi'a$. Put $P'=R'\otimes_RP$, $J'=R'\otimes_RJ$, $N'=S'\otimes_SN$. The canonical iterated-extension identifications send $s'\otimes(r'\otimes p)$ to $s'\varphi'(r')\otimes p$; the inverse inserts $1\otimes p$. Their relations and inverse equations follow from the tensor universal property and descend by sheafification. They give $q'$ by $q'(s'\otimes(r'\otimes j))=s'\varphi'(r')\otimes q(1\otimes j)$. The canonical Hom comparison sends $f$ to the family $f'(r'\otimes p)=r'\otimes f(p)$ (the construction in step 7 with $q$ the identity); analogous comparisons extend $S$-linear $g$ to $g'(s'\otimes z)=s'\otimes g(z)$. On generators, $\theta(g')$ and the extended $\theta(g)$ both send $1\otimes p$ to $1\otimes g(1\otimes p)$. For $\sigma$, both orders of change of coefficients send the images of $s\otimes f$ and $t\otimes p$ to $b(s)b(t)\otimes q(1\otimes f(p))$. For $\alpha$ and $\nu$ both orders send a tensor to that same tensor in the common extension. Linearity supplies arbitrary additional $R'$- and $S'$-scalars, and the two successive sheafification uniqueness arguments supply all sections. Thus the coefficient diagrams commute; no Hom comparison is claimed invertible. A tower is the same calculation with nested tensors; both bracketings multiply scalars in the same order, by associativity. If the target $N'$ is supplied with a compatible semilinear map instead of chosen as $S'\otimes_SN$, compose these equations with that map. This also includes compatible changes of $q$.
