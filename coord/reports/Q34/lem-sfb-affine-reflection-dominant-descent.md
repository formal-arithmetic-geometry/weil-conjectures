## Proof material for the new unsplit node

Write shifted weights U=u+rho, V=v+rho. Suppose U=V-q beta with q=c_beta(V)-np>=0. Fix alpha simple and abbreviate a=c_alpha(V), b=c_alpha(U). Simple reflection preserves positive roots other than alpha: a root beta distinct from alpha has another positive simple coefficient, unchanged by reflecting across alpha. For beta!=alpha, conjugation sends the edge V -> U to an edge s_alpha V -> s_alpha U with positive root s_alpha beta and the SAME nonnegative integer n. The equality follows directly from the stated coroot compatibility, and its difference is q s_alpha beta>=0.

There are four sign cases. If a,b>=0 retain the edge. If a,b<0 use its conjugate. If a<0<=b, use s_alpha V -> V -> U; the first edge is at level 0. If b<0<=a and beta!=alpha, use V -> s_alpha V -> s_alpha U, again starting at level 0. These paths have at most two edges. Cases with a=0 or b=0 are included because reflection fixes the wall.

The remaining beta=alpha case cannot have a<0, since a>=np. If b>=0 the edge is retained. Otherwise b=2np-a<0. If n=0, folded endpoints coincide. For n>=1, put h=floor(a/p), epsilon=a-hp with 0<=epsilon<p. Then a>2np implies h>=2n. Use the two affine reflections along alpha at levels hp and (h-n)p. Starting with pairing a, the intermediate pairing is hp-epsilon and the final pairing is a-2np=-b, exactly the pairing of s_alpha U; all other directions are unchanged. The first lowering condition is a>=hp. The second is hp-epsilon>=(h-n)p, since np>=p>epsilon. Both levels are nonnegative multiples of p. The second reflection applied to the intermediate point is V-np alpha=s_alpha U. This proves the advertised at-most-two-edge bound, including non-prime p. This local calculation is strictly smaller than the item and never invokes its conclusion.

I checked this identity by finite exact-integer enumeration (`check_small_cases.py` in the sandbox) for shifted weights with simple-coroot coordinates in [-5,5] and p=1,2,3,5: A1 49, A2 3174, B2 4824, G2 9548, and A1 x A1 2050 edge/fold cases all passed. The routine enumerates the roots with their coroots and searches paths of length at most two; it is a mathematical sanity check, not Lean validation. Rank zero has no simple alpha, so the local statement is vacuous. The rank-one crossing case is essential: merely conjugating its root would reverse positivity, so I kept its separate two-level calculation.
