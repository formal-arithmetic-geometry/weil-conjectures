### Hypotheses and coefficients

1. The two positive finite stages, with m ≤ n, specialize `Λ` and `I : Ideal Λ`:
   Λ = O_E/(varpi_E^n) and I is the ideal generated by the image of varpi_E^m.
   The quotient Λ/I is canonically O_E/(varpi_E^m), because (varpi_E^n) is
   contained in (varpi_E^m). This is the coefficient homomorphism of the node,
   not an assumed flat homomorphism. Finite coefficient stages are Noetherian,
   so the source's `[IsNoetherianRing Λ]` is satisfied.
2. `T` is the implicit `X : Scheme.{u}`. Both source theorems are valid for every
   scheme, so in particular for every T where ell is invertible; no additional
   geometric hypothesis is required. Their general form does not alter the node.
3. The etale category is
   `DerivedCategory (Sheaf X.smallEtaleTopology (ModuleCat.{u+1} Λ))`;
   the pro-etale category replaces the topology with `Scheme.ProEt.topology X`.
   These are module-valued sheaves on precisely the two sites in the node.
4. The bounded constructibility assumption is `_hK : DerivedCategory.boundedConstructible
   (ModuleCat.{u+1} Λ) X K` in each respective namespace. The pro-etale upstream
   predicate is expressed by etale origin; the recorded intrinsic constructibility
   dependency uses stratified locally constant finite stalks and the ringed-site
   module encoding. These are different Lean presentations, not definitional equality;
   this attachment retains the upstream presentation already attached to the item.
   No claim of a new Lean transport between these presentations is made here.
5. The flat model has `P : CochainComplex (...) ℤ` and `e : K ≅ DerivedCategory.Q.obj P`.
   In the etale theorem `hP : IsBoundedFlatComplex Λ a b P` unfolds to
   `(∀ q, q < a ∨ b < q → IsZero (P.X q)) ∧ ∀ q, ObjectProperty.flat (P.X q)`.
   The pro-etale theorem states exactly this conjunction as `hP`.
   The orientation of `e` is immaterial since the node writes P ≅ K.

### Every conclusion

Write P' = `((Sheaf.quotSMulTop _ I).mapHomologicalComplex _).obj P`.
This is termwise P/I P, equivalently P tensor_Λ Λ/I over the quotient ring.

1. The first conclusion is `IsBoundedFlatComplex (Λ ⧸ I) a b P'` on the etale
   side, and its explicit vanishing-and-flatness conjunction on the pro-etale side.
   It contains BOTH vanishing for every q outside [a,b] and flatness of every term
   over the NEW coefficient ring Λ/I, not merely flatness over Λ.
2. The second conclusion gives `Nonempty (K_m ≅ DerivedCategory.Q.obj (...))`,

   It distinguishes the quotient-module model from its underlying Λ-module object;

### Edge cases

- m = n gives I = 0, hence the identity coefficient reduction up to the canonical
  quotient isomorphism. No strict inequality is assumed.
- The smallest positive coefficient stage m = 1 is included. A zero stage is not
  a positive finite coefficient stage; the general upstream theorem also handles it.
- a = b gives a one-degree model. If a > b, the support condition forces every
  term to be zero, and the generic reduction theorem is still valid.
- P = 0, K = 0 and the empty scheme are not excluded.
- Nonflat quotient maps are included: flatness is asserted for the reduced terms
  over the quotient ring, never for Λ → Λ/I itself.
- Site objects/morphisms retain the original scheme-size convention; the upstream
  ModuleCat coefficients are in universe u+1. The finite coefficient ring may be
  canonically ULifted to that size. No site universe equality is imposed.
