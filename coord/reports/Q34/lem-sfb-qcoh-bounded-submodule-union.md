For every citation I opened the node and read its actual statement; the quotes in `citations.json` copy those statements exactly. Here is what each step takes from them and why the hypotheses apply.

1. **Covers and uniform cardinal.** `lem-weil-pbc-affine-opens-basis` states the basis property for every scheme, so it applies inside arbitrary open intersections. `lem-coef-small-family-regular-bound` bounds a small family of small sets; the cover and overlap indices are such sets. The disjoint union includes the original cover index as well as the overlap indices. Neither lemma requires quasi-compactness. The symmetric choice is made before the module is introduced, so the resulting cardinal depends only on X.
2. **Restriction and finite supports.** `lem-weil-pbc-module-restriction-open-immersion-colimits` clause (2) preserves quasi-coherence under restriction. `lem-weil-pbc-module-pullback-affine-sections` explicitly identifies the canonical tensor-to-sections map and states naturality. I apply it to the identity and nested affine opens; `def-pc-module-pullback` explicitly provides the identity-pullback isomorphism. `def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product` explicitly says that pure tensors generate, which supplies finite expressions for restricted sections. No finite generation of the ambient modules or finiteness of their rings is assumed.
3. **One closure step.** `lem-sfb-infinite-cardinal-square` states the infinite-cardinal multiplication identity in the ordinary sheaf universes. The displayed recursive rule is the actual construction, and the indexing triples and finite supports are counted explicitly. Induction has the seed family as its base and the written cardinal estimate as its successor step; this is not an appeal to an unrecorded closure theorem.
4. **Countable closure.** The same cardinal leaf bounds the union by countable stages times kappa. The finite-support containment follows at the very next stage of the written recursion. The equality of spans uses the same affine chart in each direction because the covers were chosen symmetrically. The source does not require a regular cardinal for this construction, although choosing one in step 1 is harmless.
5. **Actual affine subobjects.** `lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction` has the mapping property, unit and quasi-coherent counit, not merely full faithfulness. `lem-weil-pbc-spec-module-tilde-sections` supplies quasi-coherence of the associated module sheaf. `lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-module-cokernel-image` states both quasi-coherence of an image and equality of its affine sections with the module-map image. These are exactly the properties used to obtain H_i and H_i(U_i)=N_i. Both source and target of the image map are quasi-coherent, as required.
6. **Overlap compatibility.** The cited affine section comparison is natural for the actual inclusion H_i into F, so the spans are identified inside the same ambient M_ijk. The affine adjunction has a natural invertible counit on both quasi-coherent subobjects, giving equality of subsheaves from equality of their included module sections. The final passage from affine overlap charts to the entire intersection is explicitly the sheaf membership axiom, not an assumption that the intersection itself is affine.
7. **Gluing and generation.** `lem-weil-pbc-glue-sheaves-of-modules` states gluing for sheaves AND for morphisms, which allows the inclusions to glue. Equality as subobjects gives the cocycle. The displayed range sheaf and its sheaf check ensure this is an actual subsheaf. `pqf-def-coherent` defines quasi-coherence affine-locally, so H_i on the cover suffice. The chosen sections have at most kappa elements and generate their affine associated sheaves; hence their germs generate the stalks, exactly as in the item.
8. **Smallness and nonemptiness.** `def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category` gives inherited module structures/restrictions, so the subset code determines an actual subsheaf. `def-weil-size-convention` fixes V-small sites and the universe of filtered colimits. The product of powersets is a set in that universe, and taking a subset preserves smallness. The zero subsheaf is a literal witness with empty generators. This step is elementary; it does not invoke a well-poweredness theorem without a node.
9. **Directedness.** `lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-module-direct-sum` supplies quasi-coherence of the two-summand source. `lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-module-cokernel-image` supplies quasi-coherence of its image in F. The meaning of image in that node is local image, so the stalkwise generator argument is valid even though the sum map need not be surjective on all open section sets. The infinite-cardinal leaf bounds the union of the two generating families on a common refinement. This proves directedness for all bounded-generated subobjects, not just the ones constructed from the chosen cover.
10. **Exhaustion and colimit.** The preceding construction accepts a singleton seed, so every affine section occurs in one bounded-generated subobject. `lem-pc-scheme-modules-grothendieck-abelian` explicitly states that all small colimits exist in ALL module sheaves; it is not a statement about QCoh generators, so there is no circular use of the parent. `def-weil-pbc-category-colimit` gives the canonical map. `lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-module-colimit-affine-sections` applies because the index is now a small nonempty directed poset and every object is quasi-coherent; it states both quasi-coherence of the colimit and representation of every affine section at a stage. An element in the kernel has a stage representative already zero in its actual subsheaf. I do not claim that arbitrary-open sections commute with this colimit.
11. **The canonical isomorphism.** The affine tilde adjunction supplies its natural counit for both quasi-coherent modules, so the actual comparison c is locally the associated map of the already proved section isomorphism. The morphism-gluing statement supplies the inverse; equality of its local composites is checked using its uniqueness clause. Finally the colimit universal property is transported along this particular isomorphism, keeping the specified inclusions.
