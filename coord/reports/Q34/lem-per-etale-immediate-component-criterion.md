I read ILO XIV 2.1.3 on printed pp. 209-210, including the paragraph immediately before it. That paragraph permits replacement by the reduced local closure and states the flat-component generic-point fact; the proof then uses discreteness to identify a point over the integral generic point with the generic point of its component. I checked the actual statements of every node used, not just their titles or proofs. There are no new statements requiring a separate source review.

Step 1 uses lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme, whose clauses (1)-(2) give the reduced closed structure and its affine radical ideal, read against schemes.tex Tag 01J3, label lemma-reduced-closed-subscheme. For a point closure that radical ideal is its prime, as the written V(p) argument verifies. lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization(c) explicitly identifies the stalk with the prime localization (Tag 01HV, lemma-spec-sheaves), and lem-weil-pbc-spec-local-ring-canonical-morphism(1)-(2) supplies the image of the closed point and the identity on its stalk (01J6, lemma-morphism-from-spec-local-ring, and 01J7, lemma-specialize-points). The spectrum and fraction definitions used in the remaining argument are included in uses. No assumption that an intersection of affine opens is affine is made; only one affine open containing x is chosen. Every such open contains y by specialization. The quotient R/p is a nonzero domain, and the written fraction argument proves its localization is a nonzero domain.

Step 2 applies lem-exc-affine-strict-local-ring-comparison exactly to an affine open of W and to the whole affine Z. I read its complete statement and etale-cohomology.tex Tag 04HX, label lemma-describe-etale-local-ring, whose proof reduces both descriptions to the same local ring and separable residue closure. The closed-point localization A_m=A is justified by the affine-stalk lemma and by all denominators being units. Thus the same A and the same K inside the given Omega can be used, including when Omega is larger than a separable closure. The cited algebraic strict-henselization definition supplies the model being chosen. The comparison's structural-map and geometric-evaluation clauses give a local isomorphism compatible with W and the identity on K. I checked clause (3) of lem-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-functoriality, which says uniqueness for exactly these structural and residue conditions; the proof uses it to establish canonicity instead of assuming that an arbitrary abstract isomorphism is the desired map.

Step 3 uses the statement of lem-trf-closed-immersion-strict-localization-cartesian, which explicitly states the canonical fibre-product isomorphism and the surjective ring map with extended-ideal kernel. I read algebra.tex Tag 05WS, label lemma-quotient-strict-henselization, and its surrounding quotient statement. The needed hypothesis is a closed immersion, established for W in Step 1, and the same geometric point factors through W because its ideal vanishes in kappa(x). The affine characterization in def-weil-closed-immersion (01QO) makes the inverse image a closed embedded subspace. The written closure argument then needs no separate comparison theorem: a closure of a point in a closed subspace stays in that subspace and its induced topology is unchanged. This is the precise reason the ambient closure is identified, without claiming that the component is ambient.

Step 4 uses lem-sbc-affine-strict-henselization-structural-flat for exactly the canonical structural ring map of Spec A; its statement has no finite-type or Noetherian hypothesis. The flatness source is more-algebra.tex 07QM(1), label lemma-dumb-properties-henselization, which I read. lem-sc-scheme-sober explicitly supplies a generic point for every irreducible component (schemes.tex 01IS, lemma-scheme-sober), and lem-sc-flat-going-down states the prime inclusion lifting used (algebra.tex 00HS, lemma-flat-going-down). The proof writes why maximality of an irreducible component makes its generic prime minimal, and then why going-down forces its contraction to be zero. It does not infer domination from flatness without a node.

Step 5 uses lem-sc-irreducible-components-cover to put t in a component, and the exact generic-point clause of lem-sc-scheme-sober. Step 4 establishes that both t and the component generic point map to y.
