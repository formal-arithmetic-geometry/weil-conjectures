## Review by Q147

I read Marot pp.104–107, in particular the standing finite-field-extension assumptions above Lemma 1.1 and the full proof of Proposition 2.1. The item is its Noetherian-normalization special case. Lemma 1.1 says exactly that A'/p' is finite over A/p when A/p is Japanese. The new node retains R Noetherian and a domain, L finite over Frac R, T the full integral closure, P prime, and p its contraction. It does not incorrectly infer finiteness of the full normalization from an individual quotient. Marot's proof uses a finite subalgebra and a residue-field finiteness input; those are genuinely non-elementary, so I leave the whole Lemma 1.1 as the new statement-only node rather than claim it is elementary.

For Step 1 I opened the statements of integral closure, adic-complete Jacobson radical, integral-algebra radical ascent, and Jacobson Krull intersection. The first supplies integrality; the second places J in every maximal ideal of R; the third explicitly transfers this to JT in T; the fourth applies to the Noetherian ring T and the one-generated T-module T. This proves separatedness without assuming T finite over R. I read Stacks 05GI (`lemma-radical-completion`) and 00IQ (`lemma-intersection-powers-ideal-module`) and checked the Jacobson hypothesis before applying Krull. Equality (JT)^n = J^nT is the expansion of ideal products. This replaces the source's harder Lemma 1.3 only because Noetherianity of T is an explicit item hypothesis.

For Step 2 I read the complete statement of the existing primary-decomposition leaf: it gives finitely many primary ideals in any Noetherian commutative ring and defines primaryness. I use it in T, not in R, and explicitly prove that their radicals are prime and that the radical of their finite intersection is the intersection of those radicals, by taking the maximum of finitely many exponents. Those elementary deductions require no unquoted minimal-prime theorem. Noetherianity, as stated in its definition node, gives finite generation of the ideal r. The family cannot be empty because Step 1 proves JT proper.

For Step 3 the quoted Nagata definition gives Japanese prime quotients, exactly the needed clause. I verified p_i contains J and that p_i/J is prime, and wrote the quotient identification. The new Lemma 1.1 statement applies to every prime P_i, not just minimal primes, and gives T/P_i finite over R/p_i. Lifting coefficients gives finite generation over R; no finite residue-field assertion is smuggled into this step.

For Step 4 the cited Noetherian submodule lemma states that a submodule of any finite R-module is finite. I checked R is Noetherian (an original hypothesis), constructed the diagonal map with kernel r from Step 2, and exhibited finite product generators by coordinate insertion. Thus the cited theorem applies to the image of T/r in a finite module, exactly as its statement says. I read Stacks 00IK (`lemma-Noetherian-basic`), second assertion. No separate finite-product lemma or injection theorem is needed for these explicit generator and kernel calculations.

For Step 5 I wrote the induction rather than appealing to a blanket devissage: monomials in finitely many ideal generators give r^n/r^(n+1) finite over T/r; products with the R-generators give finite generation over R; lifting generators across T/r^(n+1) -> T/r^n and adjoining kernel generators proves the induction step. Each is a direct finite-generation argument and elementary. There is no application of the existing `finite-middle-of-exact` node, which concerns finite cardinality of abelian groups and would be the wrong theorem.

For Step 6 I opened the fg-ideal radical-power node and Stacks 00IM (`lemma-Noetherian-power`): its first ideal must be finitely generated, which r is, and it must lie in the radical of the second, which is equality here. Thus it yields a positive t with r^t in JT. Quotienting the explicit finite generators from Step 5 gives the needed finite R/J-module. For Step 7 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-finite-over-complete-ring` and read Stacks 031D (`lemma-finite-over-complete-ring`): the three hypotheses are completeness of R, separation of T, and finiteness modulo J, supplied respectively by the original hypothesis, Step 1 and Step 6. Its statement concludes exactly the item's module finiteness and does not require completeness of T. This generic tool is declared in `split_report.tool_uses`; it packages the end of Marot's proof, without replacing the unit's route.

Small cases were checked: J=0 reduces the argument to the zero ideal radical in the domain T; the empty generator family for r=0 is handled in Step 5. For a field R, the only proper ideal is zero and T=L is finite. The new lemma with P=0 and R Japanese recovers the defining N-2 finiteness, and with L=K it remains valid for a nonnormal base. A purely inseparable finite field extension is admitted throughout. A nonlocal complete domain also satisfies the Jacobson argument because no maximal-ideal completeness was used. The zero-ring case is excluded by the domain hypotheses, not silently discarded from a theorem about all rings. No step exceeds 2000 characters; none hides a separate comparison lemma.
