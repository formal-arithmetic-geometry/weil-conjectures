Step 3 uses the new nodal theorem only under the induction hypothesis U in all dimensions at most d-1. The input is normal complete local, d>=2; the output M_a is explicitly integral excellent Noetherian, its pair is log regular, and the marked inverse image has a Cartier equation. Its output e_a is proper birational with integral regular Noetherian source, and its boundary contains the inverse image of B_a. A proper map has closed image; since birationality gives a dense open in that image, e_a is surjective. Properness supplies finite type, the cited composition lemma makes R_a finite type over X, and the function fields compose. Thus the singleton is a generator in the same ordinary alteration category, and the quoted Grothendieck-topology transitivity axiom composes the covering families. Step 5 checks the difference between containment in a boundary and equality of support. Dominance between integral schemes keeps a nonzero Cartier equation nonzero in the function field and therefore in every local domain where it is evaluated. If V(f) is contained in V(T) for T the product of the boundary parameters, then T is in the radical of (f), so T^N is a multiple of f. Thus f is a unit after T is inverted, exactly the hypothesis/conclusion of the cited boundary factorization lemma. The resulting monomial has reduced support a subset of the coordinate divisors, globally a subset of the finitely many regular boundary components. Every intersection of this subset is one of the original regular intersections of the right codimension. This is the elementary sub-boundary argument from the cited statements, not a new theorem node. The unit equation and r=0 give the empty boundary correctly.
