Step 1 uses the actual two clauses of `rem-pc-regular-normal`: its statement gives both regular implies normal and the finite pairwise disjoint clopen integral decomposition for a Noetherian normal scheme. Both hypotheses are in the item. `def-weil-open-immersion` identifies the structure sheaf with the ambient restriction; taking stalks immediately preserves regularity. Thus I did not infer integrality from mere reducedness or connectedness.

Step 2 uses `lem-sc-scheme-sober` for the component's generic point and only the integral clause of `lem-sc-rational-functions-generic-stalks`, which explicitly says its generic stalk is a field. It applies to the nonempty integral component, which has just one irreducible component. The open-immersion definition identifies that field with the ambient stalk. `def-weil-flat-morphism` supplies flatness of the stalk over R if the generic point mapped to the closed point; `lem-trf-flat-module-torsionfree-domain` explicitly says multiplication by any nonzero base element is injective on a flat module over a domain. R is a domain and pi is nonzero by `def-sc-discrete-valuation-ring`. The local homomorphism sends pi to the field's zero maximal ideal, contradicting injectivity on a nonzero ring. The elementary PID argument shows the DVR has only its generic and closed points; `def-pc-scheme-fibre` gives the precise correspondence between points over the generic point and points of $T_K$. This establishes horizontality without silently invoking openness of flat morphisms.

Step 3 uses closedness in `def-weil-proper-morphism` on the already closed subset $T_i$, so no unstated proper-composition theorem is needed. Its image contains the generic point and therefore the closed point. `lem-per-locally-noetherian-closed-specialization` explicitly gives a point closed in the ambient T, which is important: a point closed only in an arbitrary affine chart would not suffice. Noetherian T is locally Noetherian. Its specialization stays in the two closed subsets $T_i$ and the special fibre. It is consequently closed in that fibre, exactly the hypothesis for the assumed dimension two. The open-immersion definition transfers the local ring, and `lem-pc-scheme-dimension-local-rings` explicitly identifies the requested topological dimension with the supremum of all local dimensions, in both finite and empty cases. This supplies both inequalities, rather than asserting all components have dimension two merely from the upper bound.

Step 4 works in an affine open of the ordinary reduced special fibre. The intersection of a component with this open is still a component: the proof in the step uses irreducibility and maximality, and needs no extra dimension or finiteness theorem. `lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-components-minimal-primes` says exactly that affine components correspond to minimal primes. `def-pc-reduced-scheme` includes reducedness of rings of sections. `lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization` clause (c) identifies the actual stalk with localization at that prime. `lem-weil-pbc-minimal-prime-reduced-localization-field` explicitly states that this localization is a field when the ring is reduced, with no assumption of irreducibility of the entire special fibre. `def-weil-residue-field` then gives the residue-field identification. No integral-Cartier-divisor theorem is applied to a reducible fibre.

Step 5 uses `def-weil-regular-scheme` for the ambient stalk's Noetherian local property and the normal-domain conclusion already obtained from `rem-pc-regular-normal`. Flatness and the torsion-free lemma apply to this stalk at a point over the closed point, so pi has nonzero image. The statement of `lem-sbc-scheme-fibre-stalk-quotient` explicitly gives the surjective stalk map, its kernel $\mathfrak m_R A$, and the quotient isomorphism for the ordinary fibre. The DVR definition gives $\mathfrak m_R=(\pi)$. Step 4 makes that quotient a field, hence $(\pi)$ is the unique maximal ideal of the local ring A. `lem-sc-principal-ideal-dvr` requires exactly a Noetherian local domain with a principal nonzero maximal-ideal generator; all four conditions have now been established.
