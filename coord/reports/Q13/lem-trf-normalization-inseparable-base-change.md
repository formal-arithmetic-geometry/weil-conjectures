Original step 1 (866 characters):

Write $X_i=(C_{k_i})_{\mathrm{red}}$. Because each $k_i/k$ is purely inseparable, [lem-pc-homeo-pure-insep] makes $C_{k_i}\to C$ a homeomorphism on underlying spaces. Since $C$ is integral, each $C_{k_i}$ is irreducible and its reduction $X_i$ is integral (reduction preserves the underlying space and removes nilpotents, by [def-weil-pbc-scheme-reduction]). Apply [lem-weil-pbc-reduced-algebraic-normalization-field-change] to the reduced finite-type $k_i$-scheme $X_i$: its componentwise normalization is finite and surjective and is an isomorphism over a dense open normal locus $U_i\subseteq X_i$. As $X_i$ is integral, this componentwise normalization is the given $N_i$, so $\nu_i$ is finite and $N_i|_{U_i}\cong U_i$. The normalization has normal-domain local rings by the integral-closure normality statement [lem-sbc-integral-closure-finite-normal-product].

Original step 2 (2221 characters):

Put $Y=N_1\times_{\operatorname{Spec}k_1}\operatorname{Spec}k_2$. The geometric-normality hypothesis (with $L=k_2$) says that every local ring of $Y$ is an integrally closed domain, so $Y$ is reduced and normal; the homeomorphism $Y\to N_1$ from [lem-pc-homeo-pure-insep] (applied to the $k_1$-scheme $N_1$) makes $Y$ irreducible and nonempty, hence integral [def-pc-integral-scheme]. By [lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence](a) the base change $\nu_1\times\mathrm{id}:Y\to X_1\times_{k_1}k_2$ is finite. Since $X_1\to C_{k_1}$ is a closed immersion that is the identity on underlying sets, its base change $X_1\times_{k_1}k_2\to C_{k_2}$ is a closed immersion which is bijective, so $Y\to C_{k_2}$ is finite (over an affine $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq C_{k_2}$ the ring of $Y$ is a finite module over a quotient of $A$, hence over $A$). As $Y$ is reduced, $Y\to C_{k_2}$ factors uniquely through $X_2=(C_{k_2})_{\mathrm{red}}$; over $V$, $X_2\cap V=\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak n)$ with $\mathfrak n$ the nilradical, the $A$-algebra structure of the ring $B$ of $Y|_V$ kills $\mathfrak n$ because $B$ is reduced, and $B$ is finite over $A/\mathfrak n$; thus $p:Y\to X_2$ is finite. It is surjective because $\nu_1$ is surjective (step 1) and $Y\to N_1$, $X_2\to X_1$ are the homeomorphisms of [lem-pc-homeo-pure-insep]. Let $U_2\subseteq X_2$ be the open set corresponding to $U_1$ under the homeomorphism $X_2\to X_1$; it is dense open. Base change of the isomorphism $N_1|_{U_1}\cong U_1$ over $U_1$ gives $Y|_{U_2}\cong U_1\times_{k_1}k_2$ over $C_{k_2}$, where $U_1\times_{k_1}k_2$ is the open subscheme of $X_1\times_{k_1}k_2$ on the set $U_2$. That open subscheme need not a priori be reduced, but it is isomorphic to the open subscheme $Y|_{U_2}$ of the reduced scheme $Y$, so it is reduced; a reduced open subscheme of $X_1\times_{k_1}k_2$, whose underlying space is that of $C_{k_2}$, equals the open subscheme of $(X_1\times_{k_1}k_2)_{\mathrm{red}}=(C_{k_2})_{\mathrm{red}}=X_2$ on the same set [def-weil-pbc-scheme-reduction] (the two reductions agree because $X_1\times_{k_1}k_2\to C_{k_2}$ is a bijective closed immersion). Hence $p$ restricts to an isomorphism $Y|_{U_2}=p^{-1}(U_2)\cong U_2$.
