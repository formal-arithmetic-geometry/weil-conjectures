## Issues corrected in the split

The imported node had a statement but no proof. I retained its hypotheses and all conclusions. A proof from coherence alone would be invalid: a coherent topos need not make every subobject of a coherent object coherent. The split first proves the ascending-chain assertion by the source's maximal-point argument and two-coordinate Noetherian induction, then applies coherent approximation to an arbitrary subsheaf.

I expanded the source's generic stabilization inference. Only F and A_N are made locally constant on the chosen open rectangle; the infinitely many A_n need not have a common trivializing stratification. If a section of A_n at a special point generalizes into A_N, the locally constant inclusion A_N into F transports that membership back. The chosen generization stays in the open rectangle. Maximal points removed by the rectangle are not assumed to remain there.

The induction is on closed pairs of the original Noetherian X and S, including empty pairs. A closed base piece is identified with V_(Z_T/T) by the new closed-base lemma, rather than silently identified with V_(Z/S). The complements are two strictly smaller pairs. I added existing citations for well-foundedness of closed subsets, finite irreducible-component decompositions, and composition/base-change permanence. The finite-presentation claim for a closed immersion is elementary here: locally R/I with I finitely generated by Noetherianity. This is written explicitly, rather than quoted without hypotheses.

The set/module conversion cites both existing criteria. The proof does not assume that finite locally constant set structures automatically carry a constant module structure without the existing module coherence theorem. Arbitrary quotients are handled only after arbitrary kernels have been proved constructible; the local cokernel theorem alone would not give arbitrary quotients.
