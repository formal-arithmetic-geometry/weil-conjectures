### Old step 1

An integral curve is nonempty and irreducible, hence connected, so the hypotheses of [lem-sc-smooth-connected-curve-local-rings] hold for both $X$ and $Y$. At the generic point the local ring is the function field; at every other point it is a DVR with that fraction field. Its explicit formula $z=ut^n$ implies that either $z$ or $z^{-1}$ lies in the local ring; a field has the same property. Thus all local rings of $X$ are valuation rings. We will also use that on either curve every nongeneric point is closed and that sections on a nonempty open inject into the function field.

Dependencies: def-pc-integral-scheme, lem-sc-smooth-connected-curve-local-rings

### Old step 2

For either curve $C$, choose a nonempty affine chart $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ from a finite-type affine cover. The ring $A$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra by [def-weil-finite-type-morphism], and [lem-sc-integral-function-field-affine-fraction] identifies $k(C)$ with $\operatorname{Frac}A$. If $A=k[a_1,\ldots,a_r]$, its fraction field is $k(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$, since this field contains every fraction and conversely contains $A$. Hence $k(C)/k$ is finitely generated. Apply [lem-sc-closed-irreducible-dimension-trdeg] to the irreducible closed subset $C$ of $C$ itself: its dimension is one, and its generic residue field is its generic local ring, so $\operatorname{trdeg}_k k(C)=1$.

Dependencies: def-weil-finite-type-morphism, lem-sc-integral-function-field-affine-fraction, lem-sc-closed-irreducible-dimension-trdeg

### Old step 3

Let $f:X\to Y$ be nonconstant. If $f(\eta_X)\ne\eta_Y$, step 1 says $y=f(\eta_X)$ is closed. Since every point of $X$ specializes from $\eta_X$, continuity gives $f(X)\subseteq\overline{\{y\}}=\{y\}$. Choose an affine chart containing $y$ from a finite-type affine cover. By clause 1 of [lem-wf-affine-nullstellensatz-vanishing], $\kappa(y)=k$. The point inclusion $\operatorname{Spec}k\to Y$ is a closed immersion: on any affine neighbourhood containing $y$ it is the quotient by its maximal ideal, and on an affine open missing $y$ it is the empty closed subscheme, as in [def-weil-closed-immersion]. Since $X$ is reduced, [lem-weil-pbc-reduced-source-factors-through-closed-subscheme] now factors $f$ through this $k$-point, contradicting nonconstancy. Therefore $f(\eta_X)=\eta_Y$.

Dependencies: lem-sc-smooth-connected-curve-local-rings, def-pc-integral-scheme, def-sc-generic-point, lem-wf-affine-nullstellensatz-vanishing, def-weil-closed-immersion, lem-weil-pbc-reduced-source-factors-through-closed-subscheme

### Old step 4

The map of local rings at $\eta_X$ is therefore a map $f^*:k(Y)\to k(X)$. It is a $k$-map because $f$ is a $k$-morphism, and a map of fields is injective by [def-pc-field-extension]. Explicitly, it sends the germ of a section $a$ near $\eta_Y$ to the germ of its pullback near $\eta_X$. This also identifies the composite $\operatorname{Spec}k(X)\to X\xrightarrow{f}Y$ with the generic-point map specified by $f^*$.

Dependencies: def-sc-function-field, def-weil-residue-field, def-pc-field-extension, def-weil-scheme-over-base, def-weil-affine-spectrum

### Old step 5

For any $k$-embedding $\varphi:k(Y)\hookrightarrow k(X)$, put $E=\varphi(k(Y))$ and $L=k(X)$. By step 2 and [lem-kun-transcendence-degree-tower], $1=1+\operatorname{trdeg}_E L$, so $L/E$ is algebraic. If $L=k(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$, then $L=E(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$ since $k\subseteq E\subseteq L$. Each $a_i$ is algebraic over $E$, so [lem-weil-pbc-algebraic-generators-finite-extension], applied with base field $E$ and ambient field $L$, says this generated subfield is finite-dimensional over $E$. It is all of $L$, hence $[L:E]<\infty$ in the sense of [def-pc-field-tower]. In particular this applies to every $f^*$. No separability is required.

Dependencies: lem-kun-transcendence-degree-tower, lem-weil-pbc-algebraic-generators-finite-extension, def-pc-field-tower

### Old step 6

Conversely, fix a $k$-embedding $\varphi:k(Y)\hookrightarrow k(X)$ and a nonempty affine chart $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq Y$. Compose its germ injection $A\hookrightarrow k(Y)$ with $\varphi$. By the affine-spectrum correspondence this defines $h:\operatorname{Spec}k(X)\to V\subseteq Y$ over $k$. Its image is $\eta_Y$, since the kernel of $A\to k(X)$ is zero. The map induced on the generic local rings is $\varphi$: on a fraction $a/b$ it is $\varphi(a)/\varphi(b)$. This construction uses only the fraction-field description of [lem-sc-integral-function-field-affine-fraction] and the functoriality in [def-weil-affine-spectrum].

Dependencies: lem-sc-integral-function-field-affine-fraction, def-weil-affine-spectrum

### Old step 7

Apply [lem-sfb-valuation-stalk-proper-target-extension] to $X$, $Y$ and the map $h$ in step 6. Integrality is a hypothesis, all local rings of $X$ are valuation rings by step 1, and $Y$ is proper over $k$ by hypothesis. We obtain a $k$-morphism $F:X\to Y$ whose restriction to $\operatorname{Spec}k(X)$ is $h$. Thus $F(\eta_X)=\eta_Y$ and $F^*=\varphi$. In particular $F$ is nonconstant. Suppose $F=x\circ c$ with $c:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ and a $k$-point $x:\operatorname{Spec}k\to Y$. Then $x$ has image $F(\eta_X)=\eta_Y$, and by [def-weil-residue-field] it induces a $k$-embedding $k(Y)=\kappa(\eta_Y)\hookrightarrow k$. So $k(Y)/k$ would be algebraic, and [lem-kun-transcendence-degree-tower] would give $\operatorname{trdeg}_kk(Y)=0$, contradicting step 2.

Dependencies: lem-sfb-valuation-stalk-proper-target-extension, lem-sc-smooth-connected-curve-local-rings, def-weil-residue-field, lem-kun-transcendence-degree-tower

### Old step 8

Suppose $f,g:X\to Y$ induce the same $k$-embedding. Their composites with $i_X:\operatorname{Spec}k(X)\to X$ coincide: take an affine neighbourhood of $\eta_Y$, and use the affine-spectrum correspondence to identify both maps with the same homomorphism on its coordinate ring. Let $e:E\to X$ be their equalizer, the pullback of $\Delta_{Y/k}$ along $(f,g):X\to Y\times_kY$. The diagonal is a closed immersion since $Y/k$ is proper, and so is $e$ by base-change permanence. Since $f i_X=g i_X$, the fibre-product universal property factors $i_X$ through $E$. Hence the closed image $e(E)$ contains $\eta_X$ and equals $X$. As $X$ is reduced, [lem-weil-pbc-reduced-source-factors-through-closed-subscheme] supplies $s:X\to E$ with $e s=\operatorname{id}_X$. Since $f e=g e$ by the equalizer construction, composing with $s$ gives $f=g$.

Dependencies: def-weil-affine-spectrum, def-pc-fibre-product, def-weil-proper-morphism, lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence, def-weil-closed-immersion, def-sc-generic-point, def-pc-integral-scheme, lem-weil-pbc-reduced-source-factors-through-closed-subscheme

### Old step 9

Steps 4 and 7 show that every embedding occurs, and step 8 shows that it occurs only once; hence $f\mapsto f^*$ is bijective. For the identity map a germ $a_\eta$ is sent to itself, so $(\operatorname{id}_X)^*=\operatorname{id}_{k(X)}$. For $f:X\to Y$ and $g:Y\to Z$, both send generic point to generic point, so does $g\circ f$, which is therefore nonconstant by the residue-field argument at the end of step 7 (applied to $Z$). For a germ represented by $a$ on a neighbourhood of $\eta_Z$, pullback along the composite is pullback along $g$ followed by pullback along $f$, by composition of morphisms of locally ringed spaces. Taking germs gives $(g\circ f)^*(a)=f^*(g^*(a))$ for every $a\in k(Z)$, as asserted.

Dependencies: def-pc-scheme, def-weil-pbc-module-stalk, def-weil-residue-field, lem-kun-transcendence-degree-tower
