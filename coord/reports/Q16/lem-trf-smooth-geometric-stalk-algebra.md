## Review by Q16

I reviewed the split against sections B--E of `coord/delivery-errors.md`, opening every cited node's statement, rather than relying on its name or on something in its proof. I read the underlying Stacks source passages: 04HX in `etale-cohomology.tex`; 00FN, 00HC, 00HI, 05WS, 08HV, 00TT, 00ON, 00OP, 00OQ and 00T7 in `algebra.tex`; 07QM, 06LJ, 06LN and 06LK in `more-algebra.tex`; 01VB and 01VF in `morphisms.tex`; and the affine fibre-product description in `schemes.tex`. I also read the smooth/geometrically-regular comparison 038X in `varieties.tex`, and the source context ILO Expose VI 1.8 and Proposition 1.9, printed pages 70--71. The item is an agent-assembled algebraic input to ILO's log-regularity argument, so its proof origin is explicitly `agent`; I do not claim ILO states this bundled lemma verbatim. No SGA source is used. There are no new nodes needing separate source verification.

1. **Noetherian local rings.** `pqf-def-locnoeth` supplies Noetherian affine neighbourhoods. `lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization` supplies the ordinary stalk localization, and `lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian` applies to those Noetherian coordinate rings. The statement of `lem-exc-affine-strict-local-ring-comparison` supplies an actual compatible isomorphism to the algebraic strict henselization, including the residue-evaluation kernel. `lem-sc-strict-henselization-noetherian` then applies because the ordinary local ring has just been shown Noetherian. Neither noetherianity nor residue evaluation is inferred from the definition of a colimit.

2. **The smooth model over the strict-local base.** `lem-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-functoriality` supplies the factorization of the geometric point through the local spectrum and the uniqueness of the local ring map with its specified residue inclusion. The common field is the original algebraically closed geometric field, as required by `def-weil-geometric-point`; no change to an unrelated separable closure is made. `def-pc-fibre-product` supplies the lifted point in W. Clause (2) of `lem-pc-proper-base-change` really is smooth base-change stability without properness; I checked this in the node's statement. `lem-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-affine-chart-ind-etale` applies to the structural ring map with the actual structural spectrum equality. `lem-trf-ind-etale-strict-local-invariance` explicitly includes arbitrary base changes of a strict localization. The affine comparison verifies localness of the structural map through the ordinary local ring at w and its strict henselization. This proves that the isomorphism with B preserves the intended A-action, rather than merely producing an abstract ring isomorphism.

3. **Flatness.** `lem-pc-smooth-flat` states flatness of smooth scheme maps; `def-weil-flat-morphism` defines that by precisely the local ring map A to C at the closed base point. The affine comparison and `lem-sc-strict-henselization-faithfully-flat` supply the second, canonical flat local map C to its strict henselization. `lem-pc-module-flat-transitive` states the module transitivity needed for their composition. The local map in the item agrees with it by step 2 and functoriality. No Noetherian or regular assumption on C is needed here, so the argument can legitimately be reused for the coordinate section.

4. **Regularity of the actual closed fibre.** The affine fibre-product statement and the affine characterization in `def-weil-closed-immersion` identify the fibre immersion by the ideal m_A D. `lem-trf-closed-immersion-strict-localization-cartesian` explicitly states that the induced strict-local ring map is surjective with that extended ideal as kernel; it does not merely promise existence of some quotient algebra structure. This is why F is the strict local ring of the closed fibre Z. Smooth base change makes Z smooth over the field kappa=A/m_A. `def-sbc-smooth-morphisms` includes the affine-local smooth-ring characterization, so `lem-weil-pbc-smooth-over-field-regular-local` gives a regular, hence Noetherian, ordinary local ring of Z. Only then is `lem-sc-strict-henselization-regular` used, with the affine comparison. Imperfect base residue fields cause no problem: the ring is smooth over the field, not merely regular before field extension.

5. **Arbitrary I in m_A.** The flat-module quotient statement supplies quotient flatness over A/I directly. `lem-weil-pbc-quotient-local-structure` supplies nonzero local quotients and their maximal ideals. I wrote out inverse images of those maximal ideals to prove the induced map local, and wrote the iterated quotient identifying its closed fibre with F. The inclusion I in m_A is used both for nonzero local quotients and for this fibre equality; it is not omitted.

6. **The coordinate section and its base quotient.** The actual quotient of the polynomial chart sends selected coordinates to zero and retains the others; the inverse maps on polynomial generators commute with affine-base restriction. This proves the identification with affine (r-k)-space in `def-nagata-relative-affine-space`. `sbc-lem-affine-space-smooth` states smoothness of this projection. The two affine-space conventions are the same polynomial chart construction; no comparison theorem about a different geometric object is assumed. Vanishing under the evaluation map from step 1 makes the given geometric point factor through this closed subscheme. The closed-immersion theorem supplies B_K=B/J_K with its structural A-action. The already-established argument of steps 2--3 now gives its flat local map from A; there is no circular appeal to the item itself. The same quotient-flat and quotient-local statements give the reduction modulo I. Noetherianity of a quotient is proved by lifting each ideal and descending finite generators, so no extra Noetherian-quotient node is hidden. The explicit iterated quotient proves the asserted closed-fibre formula.

7. **Polynomial models for F and F_K.** The affine fibre-product description over kappa identifies the closed fibre of affine r-space with Spec P. The point prime q contains every selected coordinate because those coordinates vanish under its evaluation, not because q is assumed to be the origin. The affine comparison identifies F with the strict henselization of P_q. Applying the closed-immersion theorem to P/L gives the strict henselization model for F_K with the correct quotient map. `lem-weil-pbc-polynomial-ring-field-regular-ring` states both Noetherianity and regularity of all prime localizations, including nonmaximal primes. Consequently `lem-sc-strict-henselization-regular` applies to both models. No assumption that q is maximal or that the geometric field equals a residue field is inserted.

8. **Dimension at the prime.** I checked the exact statement of `lem-weil-pbc-flat-height-formula`: it applies to a flat map of Noetherian rings at any prime and its contraction. Here D=kappa[t_i:i in K] and P are Noetherian by the polynomial regular-ring theorem, and smooth polynomial extension plus the smooth-ring-flat theorem supply flatness D to P. The contraction of q is exactly the coordinate-origin maximal ideal n of D, because all selected variables vanish and every nonzero field constant is a unit. `lem-weil-pbc-polynomial-ring-origin-regular-local` gives dim D_n=k, with no hypothesis on the prime in P. The denominator maps explicitly identify the local closed-fibre quotient with (P/L) at q/L. `lem-sc-strict-henselization-dimension` states preservation of local Krull dimension for any local ring; it applies to the two models in step 7. Thus the formula is the required local dimension formula, not the dimension of an entire affine space or the topological dimension at a point.

I checked edge cases r=0; K empty; K all coordinates; I=0; I=m_A; a singular/nonreduced local base such as k[epsilon]/(epsilon^2); and nonclosed points on a coordinate section. For example, with r=2, P=kappa[t1,t2], q=(t1) and K={1}, the local dimensions are 1 and 0, even though the remaining affine line has global dimension 1. This checks that step 8 uses local rings, not global fibre dimensions. If K is all coordinates, q is the coordinate origin and the quotient fibre is a field. For K empty the claimed dimension equality is an identity. A coordinate not vanishing at the geometric point is deliberately excluded from K; otherwise its quotient would be the zero ring, so this hypothesis is essential and is used.
