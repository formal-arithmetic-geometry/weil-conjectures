Step 1 uses the Nagata and Japanese definitions to check the field case directly. In `algebra.tex`, Tag 0335 lists fields in clause (1); I did not confuse that with its clause (3), which is the integers. The essentially-finite-type and finite-type definitions supply exactly a localization of a quotient of a finite polynomial ring. The polynomial-Nagata statement is iterated from the field; the finite-over-Nagata statement explicitly includes quotients, and the localization-Nagata statement explicitly covers arbitrary multiplicative subsets. Thus these existing statements give both Nagata and Noetherian, with no excellence, perfection or separability hypothesis smuggled in.

Step 2 uses the finite-minimal-primes statement and the reduced-Noetherian total-fractions statement, which explicitly supplies both the canonical product of fields and vanishing of each localized minimal prime. Its hypotheses were established in step 1 and are present in the item. The kernel and fraction-field identifications are written out. The quotient clause of finite-over-Nagata gives the properties of each domain quotient. Locality and dimension one ensure that every minimal prime is strictly below the maximal ideal, so each component really has dimension one; a zero-dimensional component is not silently fed to the Dedekind lemma.

Step 3 reads the N-2 condition from the Nagata definition and specializes the Japanese-domain definition to the degree-one field extension. This supplies the finite modules before applying the finite-algebra-is-integral statement. The cited one-dimensional integral-closure statement gives a Dedekind domain for a Noetherian domain in a finite extension of its fraction field, all verified here. Step 5 uses the finite-extension-completion statement only after finiteness of B and Noetherianity of A have been proved. Step 6 applies Noetherian completion flatness, whose statement gives flatness for every ideal of a Noetherian ring. The flat-map definition itself explicitly states preservation of injections by tensor product, so it really supports R embedding in C; this is not a definition used as a substitute for an unstated exactness theorem. Nilpotents then vanish by that injection. The completion-dimension statement also explicitly says that the completion is Noetherian local.
