Step 2 uses the full finite-constant-model meaning from `sk-def-sfb-constructible`, rather than assuming M is locally free. `lem-sfb-integral-quotient-resolution-normalization` explicitly supplies flatness of B over discrete constant A and injectivity of powers of pi on B for *every scheme*, without Noetherianity or invertibility of ell on the scheme. `lem-sfb-constant-finite-free-presentation-extension` explicitly takes a specified scalar short exact sequence to the same matrix over B, with the specified cokernel identified with the scalar model. Step 3 uses the exact cone formula, cohomology formula and quasi-isomorphism meaning in `def-weil-abstract-derived-category`. With that convention Cone(e) lies in degrees -2,-1,0 and has first differential *minus* pi^n. The inclusion of K_n[2] is a strict chain map and gives all its cohomology. The induced transition is pi on the left coordinate, identity on the middle coordinate and the actual quotient map on the right coordinate; hence the inclusions intertwine the kernel transitions. This proves an isomorphism of derived objects, not merely a list of unrelated per-degree isomorphisms. The cited `lem-sp-slice-restriction-strict-derived-limit` explicitly states exactness of restriction, so the calculation survives on slices. This is a written elementary cone computation, not a new lemma that restates a clause of the item.

On an infinite disjoint union carrying models A/(pi^i), the exponents have no common bound, but each slice argument still works; the proof never asserts a global pro-zero condition.
