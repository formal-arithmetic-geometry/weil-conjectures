### Returned step 1

The schemes $X,Y$ are integral by [def-pc-curve]. By [lem-sc-scheme-sober] they have unique generic points $\eta_X,\eta_Y$. The dimension convention of [def-pc-curve] is the supremum of local-ring dimensions [def-pc-dimension]; [lem-pc-scheme-dimension-local-rings] identifies it with topological Krull dimension. Thus $Y$ has topological dimension one, and every local ring of $Y$ has dimension at most one.

Cited nodes: def-pc-curve, lem-sc-scheme-sober, def-pc-dimension, lem-pc-scheme-dimension-local-rings

### Returned step 2

The point $f(\eta_X)$ must equal $\eta_Y$. Indeed, put $z=f(\eta_X)$. If $z\ne\eta_Y$, its closure is a proper irreducible closed subset of $Y$, by uniqueness of generic points [lem-sc-scheme-sober]. For any $w\in\overline{\{z\}}$ different from $z$, uniqueness of generic points also makes $\overline{\{w\}}\subsetneq\overline{\{z\}}$. This would give a length-two chain $\overline{\{w\}}\subsetneq\overline{\{z\}}\subsetneq Y$, contradicting [def-kun-topological-krull-dimension] and Step 1. Therefore $\overline{\{z\}}=\{z\}$. Continuity of the map underlying $f$ [def-weil-locally-ringed-space-morphism] implies $f(X)\subseteq\overline{\{f(\eta_X)\}}=\{z\}$: the inverse image of this closed set contains $\eta_X$, hence its closure $X$. This contradicts the two-point image hypothesis. Consequently $f$ sends generic point to generic point, and its image is dense.

Cited nodes: lem-sc-scheme-sober, def-kun-topological-krull-dimension, def-weil-locally-ringed-space-morphism

### Returned step 3

The field $k$ is Noetherian [def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring]: its only ideals are $(0)$ and $(1)$. Its spectrum is the one-point affine scheme [def-weil-affine-spectrum], so it is quasi-compact and locally Noetherian [pqf-def-locnoeth]. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian] to $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}k$, using finite type from [def-pc-curve]. This makes $Y$ locally Noetherian. For $y\in Y$ choose an affine open from the Noetherian affine cover in [pqf-def-locnoeth], with coordinate ring $R$. The germ map identifies $\mathcal O_{Y,y}$ with a localization of $R$ by [lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization]. By [lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian], $\mathcal O_{Y,y}$ is Noetherian.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring, def-weil-affine-spectrum, pqf-def-locnoeth, lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian, def-pc-curve, lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization, lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian

### Returned step 4

Fix $x\in X$, set $y=f(x)$, $A=\mathcal O_{Y,y}$ and $B=\mathcal O_{X,x}$. The ring $A$ is a normal local domain by the hypothesis [def-sbc-normal-dominant-galois]; Steps 1 and 3 give Noetherianity and dimension at most one. If its dimension is one, [lem-sc-normal-dim1-dvr] makes $A$ a discrete valuation ring, hence a principal ideal domain by [def-sc-discrete-valuation-ring]. If its dimension is zero, its maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ must be zero: otherwise the prime chain $(0)\subsetneq\mathfrak m$ contradicts [def-weil-pbc-krull-dimension-ring]. Every nonzero element is then a unit by [lem-weil-pbc-local-ring-unit-criterion], so $A$ is a field and again a principal ideal domain. These are the only cases since a nonzero local domain has the prime $(0)$ and dimensions are suprema of nonnegative integers.

Cited nodes: def-sbc-normal-dominant-galois, lem-sc-normal-dim1-dvr, def-sc-discrete-valuation-ring, def-weil-pbc-krull-dimension-ring, lem-weil-pbc-local-ring-unit-criterion

### Returned step 5

Write $K_Y=\mathcal O_{Y,\eta_Y}$ and $K_X=\mathcal O_{X,\eta_X}$. They are fields, and the germ maps $\rho_y:A\to K_Y$, $\rho_x:B\to K_X$ are injective; also $B$ is a domain, by [lem-sc-integral-scheme-stalks-in-function-field], applied separately to the two integral schemes. Step 2 gives the unital ring map $u=f^\sharp_{\eta_X}:K_Y\to K_X$, which is injective: if $a\ne0$ and $u(a)=0$, applying $u$ to $aa^{-1}=1$ gives $0=1$. For the stalk map $\varphi=f^\sharp_x:A\to B$, one has $\rho_x\varphi=u\rho_y$. To check this equality, represent an element of $A$ by $s_y$ on an open $V\ni y$; both sides are the germ of $f^\sharp_V(s)$ at $\eta_X\in f^{-1}(V)$, by [def-weil-locally-ringed-space-morphism] and the germ description [def-weil-pbc-module-stalk]. If $\varphi(a)=0$, this equality and injectivity of $u\rho_y$ give $a=0$. Thus $\varphi$ is injective.

Cited nodes: lem-sc-integral-scheme-stalks-in-function-field, def-weil-locally-ringed-space-morphism, def-weil-pbc-module-stalk

### Returned step 6

Regard $B$ as an $A$-module via $\varphi$. If $a\in A$ is nonzero and $ab=0$ for $b\in B$, then $\varphi(a)b=0$ in the domain $B$. By Step 5, $\varphi(a)\ne0$, hence $b=0$. This is precisely torsion-freeness in the hypothesis of [lem-weil-pbc-pid-torsion-free-module-flat]. Since $A$ is a principal ideal domain by Step 4, that node gives flatness of $B$ as an $A$-module. No finite generation of $B$ over $A$ is needed.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-pid-torsion-free-module-flat

### Returned step 7

The point $x$ was arbitrary. Step 6 therefore proves that every map $\mathcal O_{Y,f(x)}\to\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is flat. By [def-weil-flat-morphism], this says exactly that $f$ is flat.

Cited nodes: def-weil-flat-morphism
