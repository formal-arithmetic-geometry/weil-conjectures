I read the statement and proof of ILO VIII 3.3.14--3.3.15, printed pp.109--110, and checked each of the item's hypotheses: fs is sufficient, quasi-compactness supplies the finite chart cover, locally Noetherian is allowed throughout this use, and monoidal torsion freeness is exactly the source's hypothesis on the characteristic groups. For `def-trf-fine-fan-blowup` I read VIII 3.1.2--3.1.10 and 3.1.13--3.1.26, printed pp.98--102. Its spectra use monoid prime ideals, including the empty prime, not ring prime ideals. Sharpening removes the group of units, so smooth fan stalks are N^r, without a Z^s factor. Saturated ideal charts use P[I-a] in the same group completion and then sharpening; the ordered centers remain part of a tower. Contraction deletes whole-monoid centers, not all principal ideals. The latter distinction is needed because a proper principal ideal can also have identity blowup.

For `lem-trf-functorial-fan-desingularization` I read VIII 3.2.19, Theorem 3.2.20, the paragraph combining it with Theorem 3.1.27, and Remark 3.2.21, printed pp.102 and 106. The finite cover is the quasi-compact restriction of the etale chart construction used in 3.3.15.

For `lem-trf-strict-fan-chart-local-comparison` I read the paragraph beginning "Choose a point y" in the proof of 3.3.15, and VIII 3.1.8(ii)/3.1.10(ii). With a strict f, both chart stalk maps are isomorphisms, so their comparison is an isomorphism, a valid specialization of the source's smooth lambda. Shrinking to the inverse images of the affine localizations gives two homomorphisms into the sharp monoid sheaf. It supplies the substantive chart-independence input used below.
