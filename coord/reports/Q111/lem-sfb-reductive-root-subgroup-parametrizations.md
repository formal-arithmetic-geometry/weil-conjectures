## Review by Q111

I read Milne Theorem 21.11 and its proof on printed pp.428--430 and paragraph 21.19, formula (135), on printed p.431. These use torus characters rather than their differentials. That distinction matters in characteristic 2, and is preserved throughout this split. The item's reductivity, smoothness, connectedness, affineness, finite type and split maximal-torus assumptions all match the existing rank-one node. Its statement is not silently restricted to characteristic zero or to rational points.

For step 1 I opened lem-sfb-reductive-rank-one-reflections and copied its actual sentence asserting dim g_alpha = 1 and Q alpha intersect Phi = {alpha,-alpha}. These are exactly the two inputs used. Nonzero weights on the positive rational ray therefore reduce to alpha alone; no assertion about differentials is used. The rank-one node does not depend on the present item in its current transitive statement/proof dependency closure, so its reuse introduces no cycle.

For step 2 I read Proposition 16.64 on printed pp.348--349, including both containment clauses and the following uniqueness sentence, and Proposition 16.62 on printed pp.346--347. The new ray node states those integration and comparison facts with its own objects and hypotheses. I required every comparison subgroup K to be smooth and connected: this avoids the false extension to finite etale subgroups with zero tangent space. The positive ray has no trivial character, so Proposition 16.62 gives unipotence. Applying the new node uses H=G, the existing split torus T acting by conjugation, and eta=alpha, which is a nonzero character. The two inclusions really are scheme inclusions, not a conclusion from equal sets of rational points. I copied the integration/comparison statement into citations.json. I then read Milne paragraph 14.58 on printed p.298 and Proposition 1.37 on printed p.18: the latter identifies tangent dimension with dimension for a smooth group. This justifies the second new node's tangent-dimension formulation of the one-dimensional unipotent classification. Algebraic closedness supplies perfectness. Its citation is the actual asserted Ga isomorphism, not just the definition of unipotence.

For step 3 I read paragraphs 14.59--14.60 on printed p.298 and the statement of def-sc-gm-ga, which specifies the affine polynomial and Laurent coordinate descriptions and functors of points. The step gives the missing universal calculation: work over the domain F[T], use the polynomial inverse to force degree one, and use preservation of the identity to remove the constant coefficient. The linear coefficient is the tangent action and hence the root character. This proves a polynomial identity over F[T], so every substitution F[T] -> A preserves it, even when A has nilpotents. It is not an inference from A-points being dense, and it does not assert that Aut(Ga_A) is just A-units for an arbitrary nonreduced A. The calculation applies to any chosen group-scheme isomorphism u, rather than only the parametrization supplied in step 2. Polynomial degree multiplication over a domain and evaluation on dual numbers are the elementary calculations in this step.

For step 4 I read the cocharacter and pairing-two clauses of lem-sfb-reductive-rank-one-reflections together with its definition of the integer pairing. Evaluating that definition at the given cocharacter directly gives s squared on all algebras; I am not replacing this with a differential pairing. No additional coroot existence lemma is introduced.

I tested the new ray statement when the ray contains no weights: its tangent sum is zero and the subgroup is the identity. When S has rank zero there is no eta and the statement has no instance. In Ga with a positive nontrivial torus weight the whole group is obtained; with two positive weights on one ray, the tangent sum includes both, so the statement does not claim to integrate arbitrary isolated tangent lines (Frobenius graphs are not a counterexample). The connected/smooth requirements exclude finite etale subgroups and alpha_p from the comparison/classification misuse. The classification is tested on Ga in characteristics zero and p; wound forms over imperfect fields and nonsmooth infinitesimal groups are outside its hypotheses. For the item, G=T gives an empty root system, so the conclusion is vacuous. For SL2 in characteristic 2, the torus character alpha remains nontrivial although its differential may vanish, and the cocharacter formula remains s squared. Finally the universal-coordinate argument works after substitution into F[epsilon]/(epsilon squared).

Both new nodes contain statements only, introduce all objects, use single TeX escapes and cite the definition nodes for the notions they name. They are genuine generic inputs; neither moves the whole item or its separate clauses to replacement nodes. All four proof steps are below 2000 characters.
