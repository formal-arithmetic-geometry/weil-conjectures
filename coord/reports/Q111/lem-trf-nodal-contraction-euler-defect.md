## New nodes

`lem-trf-nodal-subcurve-boundary` states only local subcurve geometry, with no contraction or Euler conclusion. It follows Stacks Tag 0E38, label lemma-closed-subscheme-nodal-curve, whose proof examines the two completed branches. Its hypotheses are reduced finite-type pure curves and a nodal neighbourhood of Y; applying the source on that neighbourhood is sufficient since the intersection with the complementary closure lies in Y. Closure commutes with restriction to an open; in a reduced curve the schematic closure of the complementary components is reduced. Its intersection is reduced, with residue fields k because k is algebraically closed. The source gives smoothness on Y at the boundary, and exchanging the two complementary subcurves gives smoothness on Z. Properness, connectedness and a map to another curve are not required. The case Y=X gives empty boundary; one branch of a transverse node gives one reduced point; retaining both branches leaves a node of Y and removes that point from the boundary.

`lem-trf-nodal-curve-normalization-euler` retains its earlier statement and actual search records. Its source is Stacks Tag 0C1R, lemma-prepare-delta-invariant, and Tag 0CBW, lemma-split-node. For an algebraic reduced proper curve the finite normalization exact sequence has punctual quotient. At a split node the completed normalization is k[[u]] direct-sum k[[v]] and the original ring is pairs with equal constant term; the quotient is k, of length one. Thus each internal node, including a self-node, subtracts one from the sum of normalized-component Euler characteristics. Each smooth proper connected normalization has H^0=k, H^1 dimension g_i and no cohomology above degree one, so its Euler characteristic is 1-g_i. This proves the stated formula without any contraction or boundary data. These explanatory proof notes are in this report, not in the unsplit node's statement/proof fields.
