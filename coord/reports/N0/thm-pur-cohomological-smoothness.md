These give precisely the hypotheses of the ordinary input and of the comparison-class theorem. Step 3 uses the actual statement of `thm-asd-smooth-tensor`: it allows every ordinary-etale complex, identifies its particular canonical mate, and states that the shifted dualizing object is a locally constant free rank-one sheaf. Step 4 is the elementary inverse-line construction, not a new lemma. `def-pur-sheaf-hom` really defines Hom on slices. In a trivialization, evaluation and its inverse are the displayed scalar multiplication and unit insertion. `lemma-weil-pbc-geometric-stalk-conservative` applies to the underlying abelian sheaf morphism, which is enough to reflect its module isomorphy. I use stalks only to test an isomorphism of sheaves. Flatness follows by testing a kernel after a cover that makes the tensor operation the identity; the single-term flat line and its shift then compute their derived tensor directly. The derived tensor-pullback statement provides the monoidal comparison for the evaluations. The transported inverse has finite Tor amplitude, so invertibility meets the exact definition of `def-pur-co-smoothness`, rather than existence of an inverse in some larger unspecified category.

Step 5 uses the exact pullback comparison, the derived tensor-pullback comparison, the projection-map definition, and the proper projection/exchange coherence statement. I checked that the latter assumes a commutative ringed-topos square, not a Cartesian one: the comparison square of sites therefore satisfies its hypotheses. `lem-cbc-extension-zero-projection-formula` supplies the actual canonical open-factor map. Restricting it to the open gives the same tensor formula and outside gives zero, which accounts for the open part of the comparison. `fix-prop-projection-formula` states invertibility and naturality on the finite constructible/finite-Tor inputs used here.
