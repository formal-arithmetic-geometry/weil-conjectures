### Former step 1: lem-coef-finite-reduction-ctf-model (unit/omega specialization stays in parent step 1)

First work on the étale site. The constant sheaf $A_X$ is constructible with Tor interval $[0,0]$: tensoring with the free rank-one coefficient sheaf is the identity, so this is the definition [def-weil-finite-tor-amplitude]. By the Hom-complex definition [def-pur-derived-internal-hom], $R\mathcal Hom_A(A_X,\omega_{X,A})=\omega_{X,A}$ (the Hom complex from the degree-zero unit to a K-injective representative is that representative). Apply [lem-weil-verdier-preservation-strata] to $A_X$ to obtain $\omega_{X,A}\in D^b_{ctf}(X_{\mathrm{Et}},A)$. The same result applied to $K$ gives $D_{X,A}K\in D^b_{ctf}(X_{\mathrm{Et}},A)$; this also permits applying the eventual comparison to $D_{X,A}K$.

We also check that the reduced inputs used in the cocycle remain in the required category. For any $T\in D^b_{ctf}(X_{\mathrm{Et}},A)$ with Tor interval $[h,j]$, the constructible variant of [lem-sf-flat-model] gives a bounded flat constructible model $P$ in $[h,j]$ (its finite-type-over-$k$ hypothesis applies to $X$). A bounded flat complex is K-flat: tensor with an acyclic complex and filter the total complex by the finitely many degrees of $P$; each successive quotient is acyclic by flatness. Thus, since derived change of rings is computed on K-flat complexes [lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings] (2), $T_B$ is represented by $P\otimes_A B$. On every stratum trivializing $P^i$, its coefficient extension is the constant finite $B$-module $P^i_{\bar x}\otimes_A B$, so each term is constructible. The kernel and cokernel clauses of [thm-constructible-serre], applied to its differentials, make its cohomology constructible. Each term is $B$-flat because $(P^i\otimes_A B)\otimes_B N=P^i\otimes_A N$ and restriction of $B$-module sheaves to $A$-module sheaves is exact. The same finite filtration proves $P\otimes_A B$ K-flat; tensoring it with a degree-zero $B$-module sheaf has terms only in $[h,j]$. Consequently $T_B\in D^b_{ctf}(X_{\mathrm{Et}},B)$ with that interval. Apply this to $T=K,\omega_{X,A},D_AK$, and repeat for $B\to C$ when needed below.

### Former step 4: lem-coef-finite-bidual-transpose

The double-dual map $\delta_K$ is the transpose of evaluation with tensor symmetry by [lem-weil-duality-pairing]. Under the tensor--Hom adjunction, which [thm-coef-finite-hom] respects, both arrows in
$$d_{D_AK,A,B}\circ S(\delta_K)=D_B(d_{K,A,B})\circ\delta_{K_B}$$
have source $K_B$ and target $D_B(S(D_AK))$. To compute the left transpose, apply Step 3 with input $D_AK$, then precompose with $S(\delta_K)\otimes1$. This gives $u\circ S(\operatorname{ev}_{D_AK}\circ(\delta_K\otimes1_{D_AK}))$. The definition of $\delta_K$ as the evaluation transpose says exactly that $\operatorname{ev}_{D_AK}\circ(\delta_K\otimes1)=\operatorname{ev}_K\circ\tau_{K,D_AK}$, where $\tau$ is tensor symmetry. For the right transpose, precomposition in internal Hom is adjoint to precomposition in its tensor argument. Hence transposing $D_B(d_K)\circ\delta_{K_B}$ gives $\operatorname{ev}_{D_BK_B}\circ(\delta_{K_B}\otimes d_K)$, which by the same defining equation for $\delta_{K_B}$ equals $\operatorname{ev}_{K_B}\circ(d_K\otimes1)\circ\tau_{K_B,S(D_AK)}$. Step 3 with input $K$ equates these transposes; injectivity of the adjunction bijection proves the equation. Here $D_AK$ is in the required domain by Step 1. Since $d_K$ is an isomorphism, this equation is exactly compatibility with double duals after identifying $S(D_A^2K)$ with $D_B^2(K_B)$ using $D_B(d_K)^{-1}\circ d_{D_AK}$. On K-flat models scalar change is termwise tensor with $B$ placed in degree zero: on homogeneous tensors of degrees $i,j$, symmetry is $x\otimes y\mapsto(-1)^{ij}y\otimes x$ both before and after scalar change. Thus the symmetry used in these two transposes has the same sign.

Original cited nodes: lem-weil-duality-pairing, thm-coef-finite-hom.
