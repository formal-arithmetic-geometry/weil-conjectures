The ordinal node has a concrete independent proof: a monotone map A into J with its new greatest element extends uniquely over the new last element of A by sending that last element to infinity. Restriction and extension are inverse, commute with precomposition in A and postcomposition in D, and therefore give the stated adjunction. The empty ordinal has exactly one arrow into each object of D. This does not require a classification or colimit theorem.

For the prism node, the two ordinary transformations are actual maps on each finite ordinal: original-element inclusion and inclusion of its last element. Naturality is equality of their values on each element, since every D-arrow fixes the greatest element and every j-arrow preserves original elements. Taking the nerves of these ordinary transformation functors gives maps of nerve products with the interval; postcomposition with the given coherent functor gives the full prisms. The empty component of the original-element inclusion is the unique map from the empty ordinal to the singleton, hence contravariantly the original augmentation. The last-element inclusion is the image under U of the unique arrow from the empty object of D, so its opposite gives exactly the specified final-object cocone, whose empty component is the identity. Restriction to nonempty ordinals gives the first-vertices and last-vertex maps stated. This supplies the proof route for the two future one-level splits without embedding a proof in their statement-only records.
