STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the phrase “the natural map” between derived limits of arbitrary towers of objects in an ordinary triangulated category is not defined by the stipulated Roos-triangle notion. Stacks `derived.tex`, Tag 08TC (`definition-derived-limit`) explicitly says that the isomorphism between such limits is non-unique; the node `def-weil-derived-limit` makes the same restriction. There is no chosen derived-limit functor on these ordinary towers in that interface.

A concrete failure of cone uniqueness already occurs in `D(Ab)`. For the zero morphism `A -> B`, with `A = Z[0]` and `B = A[1]`, a fiber triangle has `H = A direct-sum B[-1]`, map `H -> A` the first projection and connecting map `B -> H[1]` the second inclusion. The distinct automorphisms `(a,b) -> (a,b)` and `(a,b) -> (a,b+a)` both fix these structure maps and the two vertices `A,B`. Thus specifying the square on those vertices does not specify a unique fiber comparison. A coherent/strict derived-limit construction could provide additional functorial data, but none is supplied in the item's hypotheses.

I repaired the wording exactly as the already reviewed parent `sk-lem-formal-closed-support-ii` does: applying the functor to any Roos triangle gives a Roos triangle of the image tower. Moreover every comparison completing the product identifications is invertible. All seven functors, all small products, fibers and every inverse sequence are retained. No new geometric or boundedness hypothesis is added, and no case is dropped. This is a precision correction to the intended assertion, not a replacement by the mere existence of an unrelated object isomorphism.
