## Review by Q37

I read Temkin Lemma 5.1 and both paragraphs of its proof in the primary online PDF, as well as the local Stacks `curves.tex` statement and proof of `lemma-locus-where-nodal` (Tag 0DSC), the adjoining geometric definition of nodal fibres and the nodal-family equivalence. The new nodal-open statement is the pure-relative-dimension-one specialization of 0DSC, with the geometric completed-ring meaning written out. It assumes flatness and local finite presentation, exactly the hypotheses needed for that source-open theorem. Its statement contains no proof sketch. Smooth fibres, an empty fibre and a family with a cuspidal fibre give the expected nodal opens; a cusp point is excluded. The arbitrary-base-change clause is part of 0DSC, not inferred merely from openness.

For the component-data node I read Temkin's weighted-incidence-graph paragraph. I extracted only a nonempty generic open and stated the information the numerical test actually needs: arithmetic genus of each contracted reduced component, self-node count, and distinct marked or ambient-singular point count. Marking points and intersections with noncontracted components must be retained as decorations; self-nodes are loops and count once as special points. The numerical statement is a refinement to be proved by the future worker using the cited generic component/incidence and genus machinery. It assumes an integral Noetherian base, proper pair maps, and semistability of every geometric source fibre. It imposes no generic-isomorphism hypothesis, since that hypothesis is not used by the component/incidence argument and would generally be lost on the closed strata where the item applies this lemma. A component contracted to a point is proper even though the entire source curve need not be proper. The graph node explicitly supplies this properness and the finiteness needed for its multiset. Its role is a generic geometric input, not a disguised stable-locus theorem.

I opened the full statement of every node cited in the seven item steps and checked the following uses against those statements; `citations.json` copies the actual relevant text without reconstructing its TeX.

1. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian` applies to both structural maps, which are finitely presented and hence finite type. `lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-scheme-closed-subsets-minimal` supplies the Noetherian topological property on these schemes and the base. The new nodal-open lemma supplies exactly the equivalence between avoiding its closed complement and having only ordinary nodes in the geometric fibre. No nodality of the entire family is assumed here.
2. `lem-sbc-flat-fibre-etale-neighbourhoods` gives the whole-fibre etale criterion for the marking, including empty fibres. `lem-trf-smooth-at-point-fibre-criterion` identifies the smooth-neighbourhood union in the total curve with smoothness at the corresponding fibre point. Both maps are flat and locally finitely presented as required. Unions of these neighbourhoods are open by their explicit definition; I do not use an uncited abstract etale-locus theorem. The two closed bad-marking subsets are then written out.
3. `def-weil-constructible-subset` supplies constructibility of the closed bad loci on the Noetherian total spaces and the Boolean operations. `lem-weil-pbc-locally-noetherian-quasi-separated` checks the target hypothesis of Chevalley. `lem-weil-pbc-chevalley-constructible-image` applies separately to the curve and marking structural maps: both are quasi-compact and locally finitely presented, with qcqs target. Taking the complement of their images proves the semistable assertion. This step does not treat a merely fibrewise proper source as a proper family.
4. The constructible-subset definition gives finitely many locally closed subsets of the semistable set. `lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme` gives the reduced structure in a containing open. `lem-weil-pbc-immersion-base-change-lft` gives local finite type of the resulting immersion. `lem-sfb-noetherian-space-compact-subsets` makes every inverse image of an affine open quasi-compact, and the finite-type/Noetherian node then proves each resulting base Noetherian. The first, explicitly nonsemistable clause of `lem-trf-semistable-marked-base-change` is sufficient to retain the multipointed curves after every restriction; `lem-weil-pbc-proper-base-change` retains the two proper maps. Radical quotients and open restrictions keep the residue fields, so the geometric fibres and their predicates are the original ones. No stronger statement about base changes of modifications is taken from either citation.
5. The induction claim, empty case and inductive step are explicit. Closed-subset minimality makes the induction well-founded. The finite-component node gives finitely many irreducible components; the reduced-induced node and `def-pc-integral-scheme` make each an integral Noetherian base. The new generic-data node therefore applies. Deleting intersections with other components leaves nonempty opens. Their complements form a finite closed union, so the residual set is strictly smaller and the induction hypothesis applies. This is an ordinary closed-subset induction, not an unproved assertion that a generic open already gives constructibility on the whole base.
6. The generic-data node gives properness of each contracted component. The statement of `lem-trf-nodal-normalization-adjunction` explicitly says that only its self-nodes are singular on the reduced component, and intersections with another component are smooth on that component. Thus zero self-nodes gives a smooth proper connected curve. `lem-trf-genus-zero-smooth-proper-line` then identifies it with the projective line if its structure-sheaf H1 has dimension zero. Conversely `lem-asd-projective-line-genus-zero` explicitly supplies smoothness and H1 vanishing for the projective line. These are the two directions needed for the numerical test, rather than an uncited use of the converse of genus-zero classification.
7. Constancy of the finite multiset makes the explicitly stated exceptional-component test constant. The selected pieces are locally closed in the original base, and their finite union is constructible by the constructible-subset definition. This last step is elementary set selection and transitivity of locally closed inclusions; it adds no geometric assertion.

I tested the data statement and the final criterion on a constant identity map (empty contracted multiset), an unmarked nonproper smooth curve with the identity map, a contracted rational tail or bridge with one or two special points, a smooth contracted elliptic component, and an irreducible rational nodal component. The last has a self-node, so it is not a projective-line component even if it has at most two distinct special points; the separate self-node count prevents the false classification that branch counting would invite. Empty marking causes no bad marking points. Components appearing only on special fibres are permitted: the generic open need not include those fibres, and the induction handles their closed locus. The empty closed subset is only the induction base, not an illicit application of the integral generic-data lemma to an empty scheme.
