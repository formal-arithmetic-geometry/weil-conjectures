### Original Step 1

Work at a finite coefficient field whenever a construction involves finitely many objects or arrows. In the common-stage category every object and arrow is represented at some finite extension, and equality means equality after further extension [def-weil-algebraically-closed-coefficients]. In particular a Weil structure, its inverse and their inverse equations can be represented over one finite field $E$: first represent the two arrows, then enlarge $E$ to make both inverse equations hold. This does not require an integral lattice stable under Frobenius. The computations below are natural in rational arrows; their common-stage versions use the specified scalar comparisons in the pullback and compact-support functors.

### Original Step 4

For the assertion about closed points, factor $h_0$ as a closed immersion into an open subscheme of $X_0$. An open immersion identifies the local rings at corresponding points [def-weil-open-immersion]. For a closed immersion, the local ring at the source point is the target local ring modulo the stalk of its defining ideal, by the affine quotient description in [def-weil-closed-immersion]. Its maximal ideal is the image of the target maximal ideal; quotienting by it gives the same residue field [def-weil-residue-field]. Thus $\kappa(x)\cong\kappa(z)$ over $k_0$. Since $z$ is closed in the finite-type scheme $W_0$, [lem-kun-closed-point-field](a) makes this a finite extension of $k_0$; applying the converse of the same lemma on $X_0$ shows that $x$ is closed. In particular $r=[\kappa(z):k_0]=[\kappa(x):k_0]$ and both residue fields have $q^r$ elements.

### Original Step 11

To make explicit the assertion about exact functors of Weil sheaves, kernels and cokernels of a Weil-equivariant map are computed in the constructible heart. The exact automorphism pullback $G_T$ preserves their universal properties; thus their kernels and cokernels acquire induced invertible Weil structures, and their inclusions and projections are equivariant. For example, the kernel structure is the unique map $G_T\ker u\to\ker u$ induced by $G_T\ker u\to G_T\mathcal F\xrightarrow{\theta}\mathcal F$; applying the same construction to $\theta^{-1}$ gives its inverse. The identical argument applies to cokernels. The underlying exact functors $h^*$ (Step 2) and $j_!$ (Steps 7--8), with the natural structures of Steps 2 and 9, preserve these universal kernel and cokernel maps. Hence they preserve short exact sequences of constructible Weil sheaves. This is a direct universal-property calculation in the fixed-pair category [def-weil-weil-sheaf-complex], not an assertion of a triangulated structure on the category of Weil complexes.
