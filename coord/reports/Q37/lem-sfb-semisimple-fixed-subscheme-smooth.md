Step 1 uses only the new cyclic-closure lemma. I reopened its statement: it explicitly gives a closed subgroup G, smoothness, linear reductivity and density of the powers, exactly what the step takes. The item's hypothesis defines semisimplicity by the identical representation criterion. The restricted action is the composite with the inclusion G into H; substitution proves both action identities without an extra theorem. Affineness and finite type are part of the lemma's ambient setting, and a closed subscheme of an affine finite-type scheme has a quotient finite-type coordinate algebra. No connectedness of G is assumed or used.

Step 2 uses no black box: it proves by induction that being fixed by s gives being fixed by each nonnegative power; applying the inverse automorphism gives negative powers. The reverse implication selects power 1. These arguments are on X(A) for an arbitrary commutative k-algebra A, commute with changes of A, and use the given action laws, so the specified closed equalizer represents the exact common-fixed functor required in step 3. In particular, I did not assume the fixed scheme reduced, and did not infer a scheme identification from point sets. This is the only elementary step.

Step 3 uses only the new dense-fixed lemma. I reopened its statement and compared each input: algebraically closed k; G smooth affine finite type and linearly reductive from step 1; Gamma dense in G(k); the original X smooth affine finite type; the restricted action; and the given closed subscheme F identified functorially in step 2. Its conclusion states exactly that F is smooth over k. citations.json contains literal substrings of those statements, not paraphrases or proof-internal claims. Each non-elementary step names its independent theorem input, and proof_deps is their exact union. All three steps are shorter than 2000 characters; no long inline comparison hides another lemma. Both new nodes inherit the item's unit, so no off-route tool use is introduced and no dependency can point back to the item.

I tested the edge cases s=1 (G is trivial and F=X), finite order prime to the characteristic (G can be disconnected and F is the corresponding tame cyclic fixed scheme), infinite order (the closure can have a torus part), trivial actions, disconnected smooth X, and an empty fixed scheme. In characteristic p, the smooth reduced cyclic closure cannot be a nonreduced mu_p; k-points have no nontrivial p-power roots of unity. A unipotent element is excluded by the semisimplicity hypothesis. For comparison, Milne 13.13(a),(b), p.257, gives nonsmooth fixed loci when linear reductivity is absent; the new theorem retains that necessary hypothesis. The dense-fixed result includes a trivial dense subgroup only for a trivial smooth group, and makes no false claim about arbitrary nonreduced groups.
