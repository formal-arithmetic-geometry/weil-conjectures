## Review by Q37

I read Hironaka's original pp.168–173 for the order algebra and Lemma 17, and pp.176–181 for Lemma 20, especially (20.4)–(20.12). I also read ILO printed p.94 (PDF p.100), VIII 2.3.13; it motivates the historical resolution ancestor but does not prove either new lemma. No SGA source is used. There is no Stacks tag for these two Hironaka assertions, and none is invented.

The first new statement is the leading-relation conclusion of Lemma 17: write theta_j=delta(phi_j), take its t''=e, and use the hypothesis that the coefficient orders are at least e-d_j and the weighted sum has order greater than e. Its completed coefficient factors can be replaced by elements of L modulo the finitely many relevant powers of N; that replacement preserves every displayed higher-order remainder. The coefficients C_vj are homogeneous forms in the order algebra, and the relation is an actual polynomial relation sum C_vj phi_j=0, not just an initial relation. Indices with negative homogeneous degree are zero. Terms of total degree zero cannot be needed since every d_j is positive. This node does not require minimality or normal flatness. The field is arbitrary, including imperfect fields, because Lemma 17 uses the order-algebra presentation and its coefficient-basis descent; I did not silently replace the point by a rational or separable point.

The second new statement is exactly the first original-ring lifting after (20.6), before the divided chart formula (20.7). The assumptions are the regular parameter presentation, normal flatness of gr_I R over T/P, and a full tangent standard basis with f_i in P^d_i. The source says the P-initial ideal is generated by the P-initial forms of the f_i and that its intersection with the maximal-ideal multiple of the ambient graded ring is the maximal-ideal multiple of that initial ideal. Applying the degree-n part gives the coefficients in M P^(n-d_i); the remainder stays in the original ideal and is in P^(n+1). This is a single P-adic coefficient layer, without a chart or the finite-jet intersection conclusion. Reducedness is not used in that source assertion and is not added. In particular n=0 has no coefficients and is automatic from a subset P; the case a=0 has no possible nonempty full minimal family and is vacuous as stated.

For Step 1 I opened def-nagata-blowup-chart-ring and verified its exact statement: the chart is the subalgebra of R[1/x0] generated by the divided ideal. Thus x0 is invertible in the containing localization, multiplication on the subalgebra is injective, and this remains so on its localization. This proves every divided element of a lands in a'. The displayed monomial expansion proves division and the residue formula, rather than citing the definition for an unstated theorem. The identity M T'=H follows directly from the parameter equations. The quotient T'/H=L is an explicit hypothesis of the item. Lifting N^v into M'^v follows by lifting finitely many products of v elements of N. The triangular equality follows in both directions by induction. These are elementary calculations, not new lemmas.

For Step 2 I checked every input to the second new node: the chosen homogeneous polynomial syzygy has total degree h; u=sum c_j f_j belongs to a and P^h, and the syzygy makes its M-degree-h class zero, hence u is in M^(h+1). All original full-family assumptions of that node occur verbatim in the item. Its remainder belongs to a intersect P^(h+1), which is needed for Step 1 to divide it once more. Coefficients p_i/x0^(h-d_i) are in H by explicit expansion, not merely in the chart. The conclusion is only H a', and so does not assume what the item is proving.

For Step 3 I checked the first new node's hypotheses on the active subfamily d_j<=e. The assumed phi_j(1,t) in N^d_j applies to all j<=b. Each phi_j is nonzero because it is a finite-order initial form. The inactive terms are in N^(e+1), so the required active sum vanishes to that order. Coefficients have the required lower bounds by the induction invariant. No minimality of the active subfamily is needed. The conclusion used is its coefficient-wise improvement by homogeneous polynomial syzygies, and the quote in citations.json is exactly that formula.

For Step 4 I checked that h_v>=d_j for every nonzero C_vj, so no negative denominator exponent occurs; the fractions lie in the chart by Step 1. The lifts A_v may be arbitrary here: no convergence or infinite coefficient-field expansion is used in the assembly. The syzygy effect is H a' by Step 2. Replacing g by q changes it by HJ because every earlier correction w_j is in HJ. The only use of finite-jet generation is the explicit inclusion H a' subset HJ+H M'^d subset HJ+M'^(d+1). This preserves both the represented class modulo HJ+M'^d and the condition that the represented element is in H+M'^d. Inactive indices are zero in the syzygies and already satisfy the next bound. Thus the induction is established from e=1, not merely invoked.
