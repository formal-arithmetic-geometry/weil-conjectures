## Review by Q37

I checked the two conclusions against Temkin's residue-factorization paragraph and then checked each step against its cited statements. The imported generality is valid: instead of borrowing separable closedness or divisibility of value groups from the surrounding proposition, the item assumes exactly the scalar normalization needed for every nonzero factor. I did not use the source's later separable perturbation. The statement and its mathematical scope remain unchanged.

Step 1 is an explicit restriction calculation. For a constant c, both c and its inverse belong to R precisely when both belong to O; this proves equality of the two unit tests. Consequently the kernel of reduction restricted to R is its own maximal ideal, so k embeds in E. R is itself a valuation subring because the valuation alternative restricts to K. No general residue-field base-change theorem or definition of a scheme point is used. This corrects the imported residue-field dependency mismatch.

Step 2 uses precisely the Noetherian conclusion of `lem-sc-p1-polynomial-noetherian`, the integral-domain conclusion of `lem-pur-polynomial-field-pid`, and the finite irreducible-product conclusion of `lem-trf-factorization-exists`. These statements introduce the ring and hypotheses they require; K is a field here, so the first two apply, and their conclusions verify both hypotheses of the third. I opened all three node files. The extra monic normalization is written out using the polynomial coefficient multiplication rule, quoted from `def-weil-polynomial-ring`: the top coefficient of a product cannot vanish over a field, units therefore have degree zero, and each irreducible can be multiplied by its inverse leading coefficient without changing irreducibility. This is a few-line calculation, not an unmentioned UFD theorem. Existence of a factorization suffices; no uniqueness is used.

Step 3 cites `def-weil-rational-function-field` for its actual statement that every rational function is a quotient of polynomials and that the polynomial ring embeds in that field. This is the right interface; `def-pc-field-extension` alone would not say it. A unit u is nonzero, so both numerator and denominator are nonzero and all their evaluated factors are nonzero. The item's assumption, applied to these finitely many factors, gives the normalizing scalars. I checked the identity c=(a product(pi))/(b product(rho)) and solved it for c to prove c is an O-unit. Step 1 then proves c is an R-unit. This is the needed justification for reducing c into k. All factors being reduced are O-units, so products and inverses survive reduction. No integrality of the unnormalized factors or of their scalars is assumed.

Step 4 uses the quotient definition already present in the item: every nonzero residue lifts to an element outside the maximal ideal, hence to a unit. This completes the generation argument, including the zero residue. I included the explicit ratio of two normalizing scalars to show that even fixed independent choices for each irreducible produce the same field; the quotient belongs to R-units by Step 1. This is elementary and introduces no support lemma that merely restates the item.

Step 5 uses the complete subfield assertion of `def-weil-relative-algebraic-closure`, including closure under inverses and containment of the base field. This is an assertion in that node's statement and is backed by Stacks 09GI; it is not taken from a hidden proof or from a definition that only names an object. If all normalized residues were algebraic, that subfield would contain the generated field E.
