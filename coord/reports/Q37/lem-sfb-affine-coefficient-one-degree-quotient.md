Here is an explicit counterexample to the original statement. Fix k=F_p. Let V be the direct sum of countably infinitely many copies of k, and form the commutative square-zero extension

A=k[t]⊕V,  (f,v)(h,w)=(fh,f(0)w+h(0)v).

Thus V²=0, tV=0, and ann_A(t)=V is not finitely generated: multiplication on V factors through A→k, so finitely many elements generate a finite-dimensional subspace. Take D=Z, S=k, E=k[t], the evident E→A, and r=-1. These satisfy the original finite-type and Noetherian hypotheses; S is deliberately not Z-flat.

For every k-algebra B appearing here, the coefficient dg algebra C_B=k⊗_Z R_B is naturally modeled by B[ε], |ε|=-1, ε²=0, dε=0. To see both the model and the augmentation, use the Koszul dg algebra Z[e], |e|=-1, de=p, resolving k. There is a zigzag

k⊗R_B ← Z[e]⊗R_B → Z[e]⊗B=B[ε].

Both arrows are quasi-isomorphisms, since the tensor factor kept fixed is Z-K-flat. Both augment to B by ε↦0, and the zigzags commute with B→B'. Hence ordinary B-complexes retain zero ε-action. For E'→A a k-algebra map, extension from E'[ε] to A[ε] of an ordinary E'-complex G' is A⊗^L_{E'}G', with zero ε-action. Indeed A[ε]=A⊗_{E'}E'[ε], E'[ε] is E'-free, and derived extension/restriction base change gives exactly this tensor. This compatibility is essential; it is not merely an identification of cohomology groups.

Over C_A=A[ε] choose the finite semi-free left dg module P on generators x,y,z in degrees 1,0,-1 with

dx=dy=0,  dz=εx+ty.

Order the cells x,y,z. This is a perfect object. The coefficient Leibniz rule gives d(εz)=-tεy, and ε²=0 makes d²=0. Direct calculation gives

H¹(P)=Ax,
H⁰(P)=(Ay⊕Aεx)/(ty+εx)≅A,
H⁻¹(P)=Aεy/tAεy≅A/tA,
H⁻²(P)=ann_A(t)εz,

and no cohomology above degree 1. The degree-zero relation is [εx]=-t[y].

Let G be the bounded free E-complex with z in degree -1, y in degree 0, differential z↦ty, together with a free summand Ex in degree 1. It is bounded coherent. Its realization is the ordinary A-complex N₀=A⊗_E G with zero ε-action; no higher tensor correction occurs because G is E-perfect. Let K be the ordinary A-complex (A/tA)y in degree 0 and Ax in degree 1, zero differential. Define gamma:G→Res_E K by z↦0, y↦1 mod t, x↦x. Let s:P→N₀ send the generators to their namesakes and send ε-multiples to zero. It is a dg chain map because dz maps to ty. The normalized counit composite is u:P→K, x↦x, y↦1 mod t, z↦0. Thus H^i(s) is surjective for i>r (at i=0 it is A→A/tA; at i=1 it is the identity), and H^-1(u) is onto the zero module. Associated sheaves on Y=Spec k give exactly the data of the original item.

Suppose its conclusion held. Any finite type E-algebra E' is a Noetherian k-algebra. A bounded coherent G' over E' has a bounded-above finite-free resolution. Consequently its realization N=A⊗^L_{E'}G' is pseudo-coherent over A and has zero ε-action. The asserted maps give P→N→K with composite u and P→N surjective on cohomology in every degree ≥-1. Surjectivity makes H^i(N)=0 for i>1. At degree 1, the composite Ax→H¹(N)→Ax is the identity, and the first arrow is onto, hence H¹(N)=A. At degree 0, H⁰(N) is generated by the image of y, and t[y]=-[εx] maps to zero because ε acts trivially on N. The composite to H⁰(K)=A/tA is the quotient A→A/tA. Hence H⁰(N)=A/tA. At degree -1, H⁻¹(P) is generated by εy, whose image is zero; surjectivity therefore forces H⁻¹(N)=0.

Since H¹(N)=A is projective and N has no higher cohomology, its top truncation splits: a representative of the generator gives A[-1]→N splitting N→H¹(N)[-1]. Thus M=N⊖A[-1] is pseudo-coherent, lies in degrees ≤0, has H⁰(M)=A/tA, and H⁻¹(M)=0. A bounded-above finite-free representative for M can be taken to have zero terms above degree 0 (split off its exact finite positive tail). Its degree 0,-1,-2 terms then exhibit A/tA as a module of type FP₂: the first two kernels in a finite-free partial resolution are finitely generated, because exactness at degree -1 identifies that kernel with the image of the finite module in degree -2. Compare this partial resolution with the standard finite presentation A --t--> A → A/tA →0. Schanuel's relation identifies their second syzygies up to finite projective summands. It follows that ann_A(t) is finitely generated. This contradicts ann_A(t)=V.

The counterexample uses only the surjectivity at r required by the original statement. I make no claim about adding injectivity at r as an alternative repair. The actual correction used is the separately proved flatness hypothesis.
