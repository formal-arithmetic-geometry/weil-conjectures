Old step 1: Write $f:U\to T$ for the structure map. It is locally of finite type by [lem-weil-pbc-lqf-locally-finite-type], and separated by [lem-weil-pbc-affine-source-morphism-separated], since $U$ is affine. Local quasi-finiteness means in particular that $f$ is quasi-finite at the chosen $u$. Therefore $u$ is an isolated point of the scheme-theoretic fibre over $p=f(u)$ by [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-finite-at-point-isolated]. These are exactly the hypotheses needed for the isolated-point splitting theorem.

Cited statements: lem-weil-pbc-lqf-locally-finite-type, lem-weil-pbc-affine-source-morphism-separated, lem-weil-pbc-quasi-finite-at-point-isolated.

Old step 2: Apply [lem-weil-pbc-etale-splits-off-isolated-points-technical] to $f$, $p$, and the singleton list $(u)$. It gives an elementary etale neighbourhood $(S_1,p_1)\to(T,p)$ and a decomposition $U\times_TS_1=V\amalg W_1$ into open and closed subschemes with $V\to S_1$ finite and a point $v\in V$ over both $u$ and $p_1$. In particular the induced map $\kappa(p)\to\kappa(p_1)$ is an isomorphism. The order of the fibre-product factors is changed using the universal property [def-pc-fibre-product].

Cited statements: lem-weil-pbc-etale-splits-off-isolated-points-technical, def-pc-fibre-product.

Old step 3: Choose an affine open neighbourhood $T'\subseteq S_1$ of $p_1$ and denote the same point by $p'$. Its inclusion is etale, so $T'\to T$ is etale by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation]. The residue field at a point is unchanged on an open subscheme [def-weil-open-immersion], hence $\kappa(p)\to\kappa(p')$ is still an isomorphism. Pulling back the decomposition gives $U\times_TT'=U'\amalg W$, where $U'=V\times_{S_1}T'$ is finite by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence](a). The point $v$ lies in this inverse image and gives $u'\in U'$ over $u$ and $p'$. The open neighbourhood exists because schemes are locally affine [def-pc-scheme].

Cited statements: def-pc-scheme, def-weil-open-immersion, lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation, lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence.

Old step 4: Put $U_0=U\times_TT'$, $R_0=R\times_TT'$, and write $s_0,t_0:R_0\to U_0$ for the pulled-back projections. Define $R'$ to be the induced open subscheme with underlying subset $s_0^{-1}(U')\cap t_0^{-1}(U')$, and let $W'$ be its induced open complement. Both inverse images are open and closed, so their intersection and its complement are open and closed. Thus $R_0=R'\amalg W'$. For every $T'$-scheme $H$, a map $H\to R_0$ factors through $R'$ exactly when its two composites to $U_0$ factor through $U'$, and the factorizations are unique. By [def-pc-fibre-product] this identifies $R'$ with $U'\times_{U_0,s_0}R_0\times_{t_0,U_0}U'$ as schemes, with the stated projections. This also identifies $R'$ with the pullback of $j:R\to U\times_TU$ along $U'\times_{T'}U'\to U\times_TU$.

Cited statements: def-weil-open-immersion, def-pc-fibre-product.

Old step 5: The map $R'\to U'\times_{T'}U'$ is a closed immersion: its pullback square with $j$ was identified in step 4, so apply [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change]. It is finite by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-finite]. The projection $U'\times_{T'}U'\to U'$ is a base change of the finite map $U'\to T'$, hence finite; its composite with $U'\to T'$ is finite. Composing once more with $R'\to U'\times_{T'}U'$ proves $R'\to T'$ finite. The base-change and composition assertions here are precisely the finite-morphism clauses of [lem-trf-finite-radicial-basechange]; they do not require any radicial hypothesis.

Cited statements: lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change, lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-finite, lem-trf-finite-radicial-basechange.

Old step 6: The maps $s_0,t_0:R_0\to U_0$ are base changes of $s,t$, hence etale by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-scheme-base-change]. Since $R'\hookrightarrow R_0$ is an open immersion, the composites $R'\to R_0\rightrightarrows U_0$ are etale by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation](1),(2). Each factors through the etale open immersion $U'\hookrightarrow U_0$; part (3) of the same node then gives that the factors $s',t':R'\to U'$ are etale.

Cited statements: lem-weil-pbc-etale-scheme-base-change, lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation.

Old step 7: For a $T'$-scheme $H$, maps $H\to U_0$ over $T'$ identify with maps $H\to U$ over $T$, and similarly for $R_0$, by [def-pc-fibre-product]. Thus $R_0(H)$ is an equivalence relation on $U_0(H)$ by the hypothesis. The open immersion identifies $U'(H)$ with a subset of $U_0(H)$ [def-weil-open-immersion]. Step 4 says exactly that $R'(H)$ is the restriction of that relation to this subset. Reflexivity holds because $(a,a)$ is related for each $a\in U'(H)$; symmetry holds because reversing a related pair still has both entries in the subset; transitivity holds because if $(a,b)$ and $(b,c)$ are related then so is $(a,c)$ and both endpoints remain in the subset. This proves the required equivalence-relation assertion for every $H$, completing all the conclusions.

Cited statements: def-pc-fibre-product, def-weil-open-immersion.
