### Original step 1

First establish the Noetherian and coherence hypotheses. A field is Noetherian: its only ideals are zero and the unit ideal, both finitely generated. Thus the one-point affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}k$ is Noetherian. Properness includes finite type, so [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian] makes $X$ Noetherian. The structure module is quasi-coherent and locally generated by $1$, and $\mathcal N$ is quasi-coherent and locally free of rank one. By [pqf-lem-coherent-ft-locnoeth] both are coherent. The same reasoning applies to any proper $k$-scheme below. Tensoring a coherent module by $\mathcal N$ preserves coherence: on a trivializing open of $\mathcal N$ the tensor is that coherent module itself. Euler characteristics of all these coherent modules on proper schemes are defined by [lem-pc-euler-characteristic-well-defined].

Existing inputs: def-weil-field, def-weil-proper-morphism, def-weil-affine-spectrum, pqf-def-locnoeth, lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian, def-sc-invertible-module, pqf-lem-coherent-ft-locnoeth, lem-pc-euler-characteristic-well-defined.

Source: literature/stacks-project/morphisms.tex — Tag 01T6, lemma-finite-type-noetherian; coherent.tex Tag 01XZ, lemma-coherent-Noetherian; hypotheses for varieties.tex Tag 0AYW

### Original step 2

By [lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-finitely-many-components] there are only finitely many irreducible components, hence finitely many of dimension one. Give each such component $C_i$ its reduced induced structure using [lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme]. It is reduced and irreducible, hence integral by [def-pc-integral-scheme]. Its dimension is one by its selection, and [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-proper] makes it proper over $k$. By [lem-weil-pbc-scheme-sober] it has the unique generic point $\xi_i$ specified in the statement. The affine description of the reduced induced structure is the quotient by the radical ideal of the component, which is prime because the component is irreducible.

### Original step 3

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-coherent-filtration-integral-subschemes] to the coherent module $\mathcal O_X$ on the Noetherian scheme $X$. Obtain a finite filtration $0=\mathcal F_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal F_s=\mathcal O_X$ by coherent submodules, with quotients $\mathcal Q_a=\mathcal F_a/\mathcal F_{a-1}\cong(j_a)_*\mathcal I_a$, where $j_a:Z_a\hookrightarrow X$ is an integral closed subscheme and $\mathcal I_a\subseteq\mathcal O_{Z_a}$ is a nonzero coherent ideal. Every $Z_a$ is proper over $k$ by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-proper], and therefore Noetherian by Step 1. The coherent filtration is the common device used below to calculate both the degree and its generic multiplicities.

Existing inputs: lem-weil-pbc-coherent-filtration-integral-subschemes, lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-proper.

Source: literature/stacks-project/coherent.tex — Tag 01YF, lemma-coherent-filter, applied to the structure module; agent-written devissage of varieties.tex Tag 0AYW

### Original step 4

Every $Z_a$ has dimension zero or one: a chain of irreducible closed subsets of $Z_a$ is a chain in $X$. If $\dim Z_a=1$, its underlying set must be an irreducible component of $X$. By [lem-sc-irreducible-components-cover] the irreducible set $Z_a$ lies in some irreducible component $T$ of $X$, which is closed. If $Z_a\neq T$, it lies properly inside $T$, and a strict chain $T_0\subsetneq Z_a$ witnessing dimension one would give $T_0\subsetneq Z_a\subsetneq T$, contradicting $\dim X\leq1$. Hence its underlying set is some $C_i$. An integral scheme is reduced, so uniqueness in [lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme] identifies $Z_a$ with $C_i$ over $X$. If $\dim Z_a=0$, then every point has closure equal to $Z_a$: a smaller nonempty irreducible closed closure would give a chain of length one. Uniqueness of generic points then says that $Z_a$ is a single point.

Existing inputs: def-kun-topological-krull-dimension, def-weil-irreducible-components, def-pc-integral-scheme, lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme, lem-weil-pbc-scheme-sober, lem-sc-irreducible-components-cover.

Source: literature/stacks-project/varieties.tex — Tag 0AYW, reduction to integral one-dimensional supports; elementary chain argument from the definition of dimension

### Original step 5

Fix $i$. Take stalks of the filtration at $\xi_i$ using [lem-weil-pbc-module-stalk-exact]. By [lem-weil-pbc-closed-pushforward-stalks], $(\mathcal Q_a)_{\xi_i}=0$ if $\xi_i\notin Z_a$. If $\xi_i\in Z_a$, closedness implies $C_i=\overline{\{\xi_i\}}\subseteq Z_a$; maximal irreducibility of $C_i$ implies $Z_a=C_i$ as sets, hence as reduced closed subschemes by Step 4. By [lem-weil-pbc-integral-scheme-ideal-generic-stalk], $(\mathcal I_a)_{\xi_i}=\mathcal O_{C_i,\xi_i}=\kappa(\xi_i)$. Its pushforward stalk is this residue field regarded as a module over $R_i=\mathcal O_{X,\xi_i}$. It has length one: since $j_a$ is a closed immersion [def-weil-closed-immersion], $\mathcal O_X\to(j_a)_*\mathcal O_{C_i}$ is surjective, so by [lem-weil-pbc-module-stalk-exact] and [lem-weil-pbc-closed-pushforward-stalks] the stalk map $R_i\to\mathcal O_{C_i,\xi_i}=\kappa(\xi_i)$ is surjective and the action factors through $R_i\twoheadrightarrow\kappa(\xi_i)$, so its only submodules are zero and itself. Repeated use of [lem-weil-pbc-length-additive-short-exact], starting with the zero stalk, gives
$$m_i=\operatorname{length}_{R_i}R_i=\#\{a:Z_a=C_i\}.$$
This proves finiteness. It also proves positivity: $R_i$ has the nonzero residue field $\kappa(\xi_i)$, so its length is not zero. Thus every multiplicity in the statement is a finite positive integer.

### Original step 6

For a coherent module $\mathcal G$ on $X$ write $D(\mathcal G)=\chi(X,\mathcal N\otimes\mathcal G)-\chi(X,\mathcal G)$. Tensoring the filtration sequences by the finite locally free module $\mathcal N$ is exact by [lem-sc-ext-tensor-exact]. Both the original and tensor sequences consist of coherent modules, by Step 1. Apply [lem-pc-euler-additive] to each pair of sequences and subtract to obtain $D(\mathcal F_a)=D(\mathcal F_{a-1})+D(\mathcal Q_a)$. Since $D(0)=0$, finite induction gives
$$\deg_k(\mathcal N)=D(\mathcal O_X)=\sum_{a=1}^s D(\mathcal Q_a).$$
Here $\mathcal N\otimes\mathcal O_X\cong\mathcal N$, with the map $n\otimes f\mapsto fn$, and degree is [def-pc-degree-line-bundle].

Existing inputs: def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product, lem-sc-ext-tensor-exact, lem-pc-euler-additive, def-pc-degree-line-bundle.

Source: literature/stacks-project/varieties.tex — Tag 0AYW, tensoring the coherent filtration and Euler characteristic additivity; lemma-euler-characteristic-additive; coherent.tex Tag 01YF

### Original step 7

For the closed immersion $j_a$ there is a canonical isomorphism
$$\mathcal N\otimes(j_a)_*\mathcal I_a\cong(j_a)_*(j_a^*\mathcal N\otimes\mathcal I_a).$$
Indeed, the map sends $n\otimes s$ to $j_a^*n\otimes s$. On an open set where $e$ generates $\mathcal N$, both sides identify with $(j_a)_*\mathcal I_a$ and the map is the identity under $e\otimes s\mapsto s$. Replacing $e$ by $ue$, for a unit $u$, multiplies both identifications by the same unit, so these local inverses agree and glue. This proves the comparison here without any flatness assumption on $j_a$.

### Original step 8

The cohomology in the Euler characteristics is computed in $\mathcal O$-modules; by [lem-sc-zariski-module-abelian-cohomology-agree], on $Z_a$ and on $X$ it agrees, naturally in module maps, with the cohomology of the underlying abelian sheaves. Apply [lem-sc-cohomology-closed-immersion-pushforward] to the underlying abelian sheaves of $\mathcal I_a$ and $j_a^*\mathcal N\otimes\mathcal I_a$. Its hypotheses hold because a closed immersion identifies the underlying space with a closed subset. The cohomology isomorphisms are $k$-linear: their naturality applied to multiplication by every $b\in k$ makes them commute with scalar multiplication. Their finite-dimensional cohomology groups and finite Euler sums are justified by Step 1. Combining them with Step 7 yields
$$D(\mathcal Q_a)=\chi(Z_a,j_a^*\mathcal N\otimes\mathcal I_a)-\chi(Z_a,\mathcal I_a).$$

### Original step 9

Suppose $\dim Z_a=1$, so $Z_a=C_i$ for a unique $i$. The pullback $\mathcal N|_{C_i}$ is invertible by [lem-weil-pbc-invertible-pullback-tensor](1). The nonzero coherent ideal $\mathcal I_a$ has generic stalk equal to $\kappa(\xi_i)$ by [lem-weil-pbc-integral-scheme-ideal-generic-stalk], and thus generic rank one. The integral proper dimension-one scheme $C_i$, invertible module $\mathcal N|_{C_i}$ and coherent module $\mathcal I_a$ meet all hypotheses of [lem-trf-curve-euler-tensor-generic-rank]. Its formula, with $r=1$, and Step 8 give
$$D(\mathcal Q_a)=\deg_k(\mathcal N|_{C_i}).$$
This uses no smoothness, normality, or algebraic closedness of $k$.

### Original step 10

If $\dim Z_a=0$, Step 4 makes $Z_a$ a one-point scheme. A Zariski trivializing neighborhood of its unique point is all of $Z_a$. Since $j_a^*\mathcal N$ is invertible by [lem-weil-pbc-invertible-pullback-tensor](1), choose a generator $e$ globally on $Z_a$. The maps $e\otimes s\mapsto s$ and $s\mapsto e\otimes s$ give inverse isomorphisms $j_a^*\mathcal N\otimes\mathcal I_a\cong\mathcal I_a$. Consequently the two Euler characteristics in Step 8 agree, so $D(\mathcal Q_a)=0$.

Existing inputs: def-sc-invertible-module, lem-weil-pbc-invertible-pullback-tensor.

### Original step 11

Group the finite sum of Step 6 by its integral supports. By Steps 9 and 10, only the $Z_a=C_i$ contribute; by Step 5 there are exactly $m_i$ such factors. Thus
$$\deg_k(\mathcal N)=\sum_a D(\mathcal Q_a)=\sum_{i=1}^t\#\{a:Z_a=C_i\}\deg_k(\mathcal N|_{C_i})=\sum_{i=1}^t m_i\deg_k(\mathcal N|_{C_i}).$$
If $t=0$, every filtration factor has dimension zero and its contribution is zero. For $X=\varnothing$, the structure module is zero and the Euler sums are zero; one can take the length-zero filtration. Hence the asserted empty-sum convention holds in all the stated edge cases.
