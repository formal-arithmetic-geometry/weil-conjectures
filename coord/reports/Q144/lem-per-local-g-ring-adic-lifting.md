I checked the statement against Kurano–Shimomoto (5.1) and Appendix A, and then against the original Rotthaus Theorem 3. I independently read the source locations named for each of the four new nodes; each is a strictly smaller input and each introduces all its rings, primes, maps, topologies and exceptional cases.

Steps 1 and 12 cite the full finite-domain criterion: its statement really permits a finite algebra map with kernel. Step 1's other citation states completeness of every finite module over an adically complete Noetherian ring, with the required module finite and J^n=I^nB. This passes completeness to B without claiming that localizing B preserves it. Step 12 applies the criterion only after checking EVERY finite domain B and every generic prime of its full semilocal completion.

In step 2 I opened the finite-integral, maximal-contraction/dimension, radical-power, completion-finite-extension, Noetherian-completion and local-completion nodes. Each factor's maximal ideal and Noetherianity have their own cited nodes.
