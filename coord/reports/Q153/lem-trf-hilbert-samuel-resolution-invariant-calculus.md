### Old step 1 → lem-trf-normalized-resolution-word-gluing

For a local ring at $x$ form $h_x$, $q_x$ and $c_x$ as in [lem-trf-hilbert-samuel-smooth-dimension-profile], and use the normalized head $A_x=(c_x,\Delta^{q_x}h_x)$. For a fixed input choose an integer $D$ at least its dimension. The common shift $\Lambda^D$ is strictly increasing in lexicographic order: if $f$ and $g$ first differ at $n$, their partial sums first differ there with the same sign. Its inverse $\Delta$ has the same property. Thus comparing normalized heads is exactly comparing the modified heads with this common $D$. Changing the dimension of a different input never enters this comparison. The cited statement gives finite image, upper semicontinuity and detection of regularity. Keep the normalized head, not $D$, as part of the synchronization value.

### Old step 3 → lem-trf-normalized-resolution-word-gluing (finite image/closedness) and lem-trf-resolution-word-maximum-center-descent (etale descent/nowhere density)

On a finite affine cover, the heads and their tail words have finite images, so their union is finite. At a maximum head, its presentation identifies its stratum with the relevant marked-ideal cosupport. Upper semicontinuity of the head makes the maximum head closed; upper semicontinuity of the tail on that closed stratum makes the full maximum $T=\{\nu_H=m\}$ closed in $X_H$. Let $M_H$ be its reduced induced closed subscheme [lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme]. Each point of $T$ lies under a point of an etale neighbourhood $U\to X_H$ carrying a presentation; by Step 2, $\nu_H$ on $U$ is the word of that presentation, and the marked-ideal engine makes the preimage of $T$ the underlying set of a smooth closed center $C_U$ transverse to the exceptional divisors. The base change $M_H\times_{X_H}U\to M_H$ is etale [lem-weil-pbc-etale-scheme-base-change], so $M_H\times_{X_H}U$ is reduced [etale-proposition-etale-reduced] with the same underlying set as $C_U$, hence equals $C_U$ by the uniqueness clause (3). Now $C_U$ is smooth over $\mathbf Q$, so its local rings are regular [lem-trf-smooth-geometrically-normal]. Etale maps are flat [def-weil-etale-morphism], so each local ring of $M_H$ maps flatly and locally to a regular local ring of $C_U$ and is regular [lem-weil-pbc-flat-under-regular]. Thus $M_H$ is a nonempty regular closed subscheme when $X_H$ is singular. No maximum greater than $\bot$ meets the regular locus. Every generic point of a reduced scheme has a field as its local ring: localization at a minimal prime is reduced with only its zero prime, so each nonzero element is invertible. Thus a closed subset of singular points contains no irreducible component and hence no nonempty open subset. This proves the claimed nowhere density.
