### Old Step 1 -> lem-pur-retagging-coordinate-orientations

The retagging changes only the two structural maps to \operatorname{Spec}k; it leaves the underlying schemes, the underlying morphism f, and every fibre product of these schemes unchanged. In particular the relative diagonal X\to X\times_Y X is the same closed immersion on both sides. Its regular-immersion description and normal bundle are therefore the same relative tangent bundle by [lem-pur-diagonal-regular-immersion]. The deformation-to-the-normal-cone construction is also literally the same Rees algebra: [def-pur-dnc-section-family] defines it from the ideal of the section and the underlying fibre product, and [lem-pur-dnc-section-geometry] identifies its two fibres, coordinates, punctured trivialization, and base-change maps. The identity on the underlying schemes consequently identifies the diagonal section invariant and the normal-bundle section invariant, including all pullback maps used in the deformation.

Cited nodes: lem-pur-diagonal-regular-immersion, def-pur-dnc-section-family, lem-pur-dnc-section-geometry.

### Old Step 2 -> lem-pur-retagging-coordinate-orientations

Steps 2 and 3 work at the finite level $R=\mathbf Z/\ell^m$ with $m\geq1$, i.e. $E=\mathbf Q_\ell$, the coefficient stage at which [det-sp-orientation-charts] and [det-sp-orientation-vector-bundle] are stated; Step 4 passes to an arbitrary $A_{E,n}$. At this level the diagonal orientation is, on each etale coordinate chart, the composite of the normalized diagonal-to-normal-bundle comparison and vector-bundle purity, as specified by [det-sp-orientation-charts]. The deformation comparison is characterized uniquely by being the identity on the standard coordinate pair, and is compatible with etale localization and base change by [thm-pur-dnc-invariance]; the coefficient-transport statement [lem-pur-dnc-coefficient-transport] gives the same normalization for the finite coefficient $R$ and records compatibility with the Tate-line comparison. On the normal bundle, [cor-pur-vector-bundle-purity] gives the canonical integration-normalized map, and [det-sp-orientation-vector-bundle] identifies it with the diagonal orientation. By Step 1 the diagonal, its conormal/normal bundle, the DNC family, and the standard coordinate charts are identical after retagging. The identity of coefficient sheaves on the identified small sites also fixes the normalized Tate line and its transition maps. Thus the two finite orientation composites agree on every identified coordinate chart.

Cited nodes: thm-pur-dnc-invariance, lem-pur-dnc-coefficient-transport, cor-pur-vector-bundle-purity, det-sp-orientation-vector-bundle, det-sp-orientation-charts.

### Old Step 3 -> item assembly, Step 2

Smoothness supplies an etale coordinate cover by [sbc-thm-etale-local-normal-form]. On every chart $U\to\mathbf A^d_Y$, [det-sp-orientation-charts] says that the restriction of the diagonal orientation is the affine-space orientation. Step 1 identifies the chart, its coordinate adjunction, and its integration map after retagging, so the two restrictions agree by Step 2. To compare these as derived morphisms, compose the difference $(\Pi_{f^{\langle\sigma\rangle}}-\Pi_f)$ on the right with $\Pi_f^{-1}$, the inverse of the isomorphism supplied by [thm-pur-diagonal-dualizing-object], and remove the common shift $[2d]$; the result is an endomorphism of the degree-zero coefficient sheaf $R_X(d)$. The degree-zero full-faithfulness statement [lem-weil-pbc-derived-single-fully-faithful] identifies such derived morphisms with ordinary sheaf maps. These local sheaf maps agree on overlaps, and the sheaf Hom gluing statement [lem-pur-sheaf-hom-cover-gluing] gives a unique global equality. This proves $\Pi_{f^{\langle\sigma\rangle}}=\Pi_f$ in the finite derived category with coefficients $R=\mathbf Z/\ell^m$, for every $m\geq1$, with the displayed orientation direction (the inverse of the dualizing-object map) preserved.

### Old Step 4 -> lem-pur-retagging-coefficient-tower-transfer

Now let $A=A_{E,n}=\mathcal O_E/\varpi_E^n$ be arbitrary. Since $\ell\in\varpi_E\mathcal O_E$, one has $\ell^nA=0$, so $A$ is a finite commutative ring receiving the unital map from $R=\mathbf Z/\ell^n$. By [det-sp-finite-general-ring](2), the orientation obtained by running the diagonal and deformation construction directly with $A$-coefficients, which is $\Pi_f$ at $A$ by [thm-pur-diagonal-dualizing-object] and [def-pur-smooth-purity-map], equals the composite of [det-sp-finite-general-ring](1): $c_{R,A}(R_Y)^{-1}:\omega_{f,A}\xrightarrow{\sim}(\omega_{f,R})_A$ followed by the scalar extension $(\Pi_{f,R})_A$ of the $R$-orientation. The same holds for $f^{\langle\sigma\rangle}$. Scalar extension $(-)_A=-\otimes^L_{R}A$ is formed on the same underlying small sites with the same constant sheaves, so Step 3 at $m=n$ gives $(\Pi_{f^{\langle\sigma\rangle},R})_A=(\Pi_{f,R})_A$. The comparison $c_{R,A}(R_Y)$ is, by [det-sp-reduction-exceptional], the unique morphism $c$ with $\varepsilon_A(A_Y)\circ Rf_{!,A}(c)=(\varepsilon_R(R_Y))_A\circ b(\omega_{f,R})^{-1}$. The identifications of the statement preserve the counits $\varepsilon_R,\varepsilon_A$ (they are the counit-preserving adjunction isomorphisms), and $b$ is built from the coefficient exchanges for $Rp_*$ and $j_!$ of the same underlying compactification $f=pj$, so it is the same map. The defining equation of $c$ is therefore the same for $f$ and $f^{\langle\sigma\rangle}$, and uniqueness gives $c^{\langle\sigma\rangle}_{R,A}=c_{R,A}$. Composing, $\Pi_{f^{\langle\sigma\rangle}}=\Pi_f$ at every finite stage $A_{E,n}$.

Cited nodes: det-sp-finite-general-ring, det-sp-reduction-exceptional, thm-pur-diagonal-dualizing-object, def-pur-smooth-purity-map.

### Old Step 5 -> lem-pur-retagging-coefficient-tower-transfer

For the coefficient tower, [det-sp-tower-gamma](1) states that its orientation is the compatible family whose level $n$ is the finite diagonal orientation with coefficients $\mathcal O_E/\ell^n$. If $\ell=u\varpi_E^e$ in $\mathcal O_E$, then $(\ell^n)=(\varpi_E^{en})$, so each tower level is $A_{E,en}$ and Step 4 applies at index $en$.
