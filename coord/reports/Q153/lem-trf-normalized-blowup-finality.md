Their composites prove exactly the object's normality and generic-point claim. This does not require finite normalization, excellence, or an assumption on Y.

For step 2, thm-nagata-blowup-principalizes-center explicitly gives local nonzerodivisor generators for the extended ideal; the coherent ideal is of finite type as required. lem-sfb-normalization-affine-total-fractions gives the actual subring of Q(A) describing the inverse image of each affine open; reducedness and finitely many components were established in step 1. A regular element a is invertible in Q(A), hence cannot kill a nonzero element of its integral-closure subring. Extension of (a) along successive ring maps remains the ideal generated by the image of a. This is a short calculation from the two statements and the definition of effective Cartier ideal, so it is an elementary step, not a new preservation lemma. I specifically checked that flatness of normalization was not used. A nonflat pullback would in general destroy a Cartier generator; the displayed inclusion into Q(A) is what justifies this particular pullback.

For step 3, the final-object assertion of thm-nagata-blowup-universal-property supplies g and uniqueness for the canonical extended ideal. Its exact closed project signatures and current blueprint statement confirm that there is no finite-type, qc or normality restriction on the source of this ordinary mapping property. The other citation explicitly says the isomorphism over U and the generic-point bijection used to locate g(eta). Every generic point of S lies in U by our hypothesis; therefore g(eta) is the generic point in the isomorphic open, not a point in an exceptional fibre. Continuity and the two stated closure inclusions prove dominance of the component. I wrote this argument out rather than silently assuming the ordinary lift is maximally dominating.

Composing the produced factorization with b gives the required S-morphism. For step 5, the ordinary final-object assertion first makes both composites to B equal to g; then the normalization node's uniqueness assertion applies with that fixed g. Uniqueness over S is not mistakenly inferred merely from uniqueness over B.
