## Proof and source

Read `literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex`, Definition Tag 00IE (`algebra-definition-value-group`, lines 11987ff), including the preceding ordered quotient construction, Lemma Tag 00IF (`algebra-lemma-properties-valuation`), and Lemma Tag 00PD (`algebra-lemma-characterize-dvr`, lines 29104ff). They supply the valuation conventions, not the conclusion of this item. The complete proof here is agent-written and labelled accordingly.

For a nonzero finite-dimensional subspace U, take a finite spanning family e_i and fractions e_i=a_i/b_i. The product q of the nonzero denominators satisfies qU contained in R, because all scalar coefficients are in R. Thus all nonzero orders in U are at least -nu(q). A nonempty integer subset bounded below has a least member; choose u of least order n. Every x/u is in R. Apply the residue map and the inverse of the given scalar-residue isomorphism to obtain a linear functional lambda:U -> k with lambda(u)=1. Every nonzero element w in its kernel has w/u in the maximal ideal, hence is not a unit; since its order is nonnegative and not zero, nu(w)>n. The explicit vectors e_i-lambda(e_i)u span the kernel, proving its finite dimensionality without an unrecorded subspace-finiteness lemma. Rank-nullity then gives dimension d-1. Induction supplies its increasing-order basis. Appending it after u gives a basis of U: x=lambda(x)u+(x-lambda(x)u) proves spanning, and applying lambda to a relation proves independence. Dimension zero has the empty basis. This is an induction on dimension, not an iteration of approximate cancellation or an appeal to completeness.
