## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported coefficient divides by the inseparable residue degree, whereas the fixed transition in def-per-potential-dualizing-complex already divides by that degree. The canonical coniveau summand uses the unscaled localization boundary under the normalization equivalence. If a is the degree, it is epsilon res(b) sp_V(e). Since the definition fixes sp_S=a^{-1}sp_V, the coefficient relative to sp_S must be a res(b), not a^{-1}res(b). The previous formula differs by a squared degree; a convention-dependent sign cannot repair it.

A small geometric test exhibits the issue: let k be an imperfect separably closed field of characteristic 2, choose t in k not a square, take S=Spec k[[u,v]], and take the curve v^2-tu^2=0. Its normalization is Spec k(sqrt(t))[[u]], with purely inseparable closed residue degree 2; its generic field is unchanged. Take Lambda=F_7. The old two inverse-degree factors multiply the actual boundary by 2^{-2}=2 in F_7, which is neither 1 nor -1. For the smooth curve v=0 in the same S the degree is 1, fixing the same convention sign. Regular purity provides nonzero top classes here, so the discrepancy is detected. This example checks the normalization issue; the proof does not use purity at the closed point of the general singular S.

I replaced 'divide' by 'multiply', explicitly writing rho_y=a_y res_y. The cokernel assertion, all hypotheses, and the image subgroup are retained. This is faithful to the source's last paragraph: it asserts the generated subgroup and does not specify the erroneous inverse-degree coefficient.
