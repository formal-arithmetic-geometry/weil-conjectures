## New nodes

`lem-sfb-symbol-borel-cutoff` concerns functions on one Euclidean phase space with fixed spatial support, not operators on arbitrary bundles. The operator item still needs fixed-atlas localization, a correction recurrence, continuity, realification, and the finite error identity. It is not a renamed clause of the item.

The intended subsequent proof follows the cutoff construction in Demailly 3.7. Choose a smooth theta zero on the unit ball and one outside the ball of radius two. For each j>=1, Leibniz gives each of the first j weighted derivatives of theta(epsilon xi)a_j a bound C_j epsilon in symbol order r-j+1. Choose epsilon_j tending to zero and C_j epsilon_j <= 2^(-j). The sum a_0 + sum theta(epsilon_j xi)a_j is locally finite in frequency and preserves the fixed x support. For a truncation L, its finite low-frequency corrections have bounded frequency support and every symbol order; its j=L term has order r-L; the remaining derivative tails have geometric bounds in order r-L, after discarding finitely many indices for each derivative. This gives every required uniform estimate without assuming analytic convergence of the unmodified series. This construction is not placed in the new node's statement or proof.

`lem-sfb-symbol-common-atlas` is a single-operator, one-order approximation statement. It contains no decreasing sequence and no Borel summation. It explicitly allows nonclassical S_(1,0) symbols, so it does not assume the existence of a homogeneous principal symbol.

For its later proof, use a fixed finite partition of unity chi_i and larger input cutoffs psi_i. An input defining term has a kernel smooth away from the diagonal, as repeated frequency integration by parts shows. Thus chi_i A(1-psi_i) has smooth kernel. On the remaining overlaps change coordinates in the oscillatory kernel: f(x)-f(y)=H(x,y)(x-y), with H(x,x)=Df(x) invertible, and substitute the transposed linear frequency variable near the diagonal. Smooth frame changes and the determinant factor produce a matrix amplitude with the same order bounds on the compact overlaps. Replace its y argument by x. Taylor's first remainder has a factor y-x; integration by parts in frequency moves this factor into one frequency derivative of the amplitude, giving an operator of order s-1. This gives a left symbol in the fixed chart, after multiplying by a fixed spatial cutoff equal to one near supp chi_i; the discarded part has separated supports and smooth kernel. Extend that symbol by zero from its fixed compact chart support K_i. Sum finitely many overlap pieces. All identities are identities of the kernel/transposed maps. This argument does not use asymptotic summation and is strictly smaller than the item. The course's fixed partition discussion motivates this supporting lemma; the general-symbol coordinate comparison and error-order formulation are agent-supplied rather than attributed to a verbatim theorem of Demailly.

`lem-sfb-localized-symbol-distribution-action` is a single-symbol analytic realization statement. Continuity is not inferred merely from the definition's requirement that a pseudodifferential operator be continuous. Its later proof estimates the transpose on tests, integrating by parts in x enough times to make frequency integrals and each y derivative integrable. Both spatial cutoffs are retained. The input cutoff gives the fixed compact support of the transpose; every output test seminorm is bounded by finitely many input test seminorms. For a bounded test set S its image under the transpose is bounded, so p_S(Q(a)u)=p_(Q(a)^t S)(u) proves strong-dual continuity. The symbol's oscillatory formula is real-linear by testing and taking the cutoff limit; transposition gives the same finite-linear-combination equality on all distributions. The single-term representation then gives the claimed order.
