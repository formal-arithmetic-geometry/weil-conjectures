## Review by Q63

I checked the item against the full proof of Tag 0DEN, rather than inferring the forward direction from exactness alone. The item is not restricted to K-flat complexes having flat terms: in the source the flat-term hypothesis belongs only to a chosen resolution. All three new statements match their source roles. The local realization node isolates the finite-presentation construction in 0DEN after the source's finite-limit replacement, including the lifted slice point and natural restriction comparison. The restriction node follows 0E8K with the exactness of the localization inverse image made explicit. The universal-acyclicity node is precisely the sufficient criterion of 0E8F, specialized to commutative rings; it is not an incorrect equivalence asserting that every K-flat complex is universally acyclic.

Step 1 uses lem-trf-geometric-inverse-image-exact for exact module inverse image with transported scalars. Its statement covers arbitrary geometric morphisms, not only morphisms presented by site functors. It uses lem-pur-cochain-pullback-tensor-coherence for the ordinary total-complex comparison, whose statement explicitly allows arbitrary unbounded complexes and gives signed symmetry. Applying it with the identity map on the pulled-back ring identifies its pullback with p^{-1}; the action-unit formula is written in the step. The cohomology calculation uses exactly the kernel/image/cokernel definition in def-weil-abstract-derived-category. This avoids an unstated assertion that exactness alone preserves K-flatness.

Step 2 uses lem-trf-topos-localization-site-presentation with A terminal, which actually states existence of a small finite-limit subcanonical site; a definition of a topos would not suffice. Exact inverse image and the signed total-tensor comparison apply to both directions of the equivalence. The step writes why these functors are inverse on module structures and why every acyclic test complex transports both ways. Thus the original site is not assumed to have limits, and equivalence-invariance of K-flatness is proved here rather than hidden in a new lemma. This is a short application of the cited comparisons, not a new assertion about arbitrary pullbacks.
