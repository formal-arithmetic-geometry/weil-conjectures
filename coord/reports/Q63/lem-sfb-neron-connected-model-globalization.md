## Review by Q63

I read BLR 10.1 Proposition 9 (b)⇒(a) and its reference back to Proposition 7 in the primary scan. The former first constructs the connected model when it is supplied only on a dense open; our hypothesis already gives B globally, so that subargument is unnecessary. I checked the finite-type versus lft distinction explicitly: only each chart is quasi-compact, never the union. I read Stacks limits.tex lemma-glueing-near-closed-point (Tag 0BPA), the full proof and lemma-glueing-near-point-properties (0EY3), and the property descent statements lemma-descend-smooth (0C0C), lemma-descend-separated-finite-presentation (01ZQ), lemma-descend-open-immersion (0EUU), lemma-descend-closed-immersion-finite-presentation (01ZP). These give the new lemma with arbitrary complement scheme, not merely a modification of the base. In the Noetherian specialization every arrow between finite-presentation objects is locally of finite presentation, so smooth/open descent and locally-finite-type closed descent apply. U is quasi-compact because S is Noetherian. No claim of an fp global N is made.

Step 1 uses qcqs-over-affine exactly for quasi-compactness of B and its local restriction; separated-affine-open-intersection makes intersections of finite unions of affine opens quasi-compact. Smooth-restrict-open-subschemes supplies smoothness of W. Qcqs-morphism-permanence supplies quasi-compactness over the affine base and finite-presentation base changes. Closed-immersion-base-change supplies the restriction of the diagonal. The generic puncture computation uses dimension one and a nonzero element of the maximal ideal; it does not assume a finite residue field, a principal maximal ideal, or a split component. W consists of arbitrary quasi-compact open neighbourhoods of the entire neutral subgroup, so the argument avoids needing a geometric component to descend over its residue field.

Step 2 uses the new lemma's equivalence, unique lifting of arrows, and smooth/separated/open-immersion detection verbatim. B and W really are fp objects by Step 1. Intersections and unions of finitely many W's remain eligible. The fibre-product comparison is itself fp, by fp base-change and the affine-local algebra of fp morphisms; the existing Mathlib leaf lem-weil-pbc-finite-presentation-base-change-composition supplies exactly tensor-product base change and composition of finitely presented algebras (also cited in Step 5); the pair of inverse comparisons lifts by full faithfulness. Thus the overlap identification is proved, not assumed.

Step 3 cites scheme-gluing with the actual cocycle data and checks both its intersection equation and triple transition equation: same-point charts sit in their union chart, and mixed-point intersections reduce to the distinguished B. Scheme-morphism-gluing gives the base map. I added the existing smooth-local-on-source leaf rather than inferring this theorem from a definition alone. Step 4 uses only morphism-gluing existence and uniqueness to identify the restricted covers; another closed point's chart is B after localization, because its exceptional point is absent. The inverse maps glue on exactly the same opens.

Step 5 checks separatedness rather than assuming it survives arbitrary gluing. Closed-point patching detects closed immersion for the overlap-to-product fp maps. Same-point overlaps use the local Néron model's closed diagonal, with closed-immersion-base-change. Different-point overlaps are graphs; separated-base-change makes the projections separated, and section-separated-closed-immersion states precisely that their sections are closed. Closed-finite-etale-zariski-local is used only for its closed-immersion clause on the cover of the diagonal's target. The source of the diagonal over each chart product is the specified overlap by the gluing theorem.

Step 6 uses local-ring-spec-limit-affine-neighbourhoods only for its actual limiting cone statement, restricts to the cofinal principal neighbourhoods, and uses limit-fibre-products to identify X localized at s with their inverse limit. Affine-scheme-qcqs checks the hypotheses on the stage test schemes. Pointed-section-stage-descent applies to the section of N×_S X→X, locally fp by smoothness and fp base-change. Its target need not be quasi-compact, exactly as that cited statement allows. This proves extension to an honest open neighbourhood, rather than calling a map over D_m a map on an open neighbourhood.

Step 7 uses locally-finite-type-limit-map-equality on a small directed system of affine restrictions: transitions are affine, stages quasi-compact, and N→S locally finite type. Local Néron uniqueness gives its equality-on-the-limit hypothesis. The smaller neighbourhoods cover every closed point of the overlap and its generic point; if the overlap has only its generic point, equality is already prescribed. Scheme-morphism-gluing then supplies both existence and uniqueness. For arbitrary smooth X the affine opens stay smooth by smooth-restrict-open-subschemes; affine covers of their intersections reduce comparison to the affine uniqueness just proved. This avoids any separatedness or quasi-compactness hypothesis on the smooth test scheme.

Step 8 takes only smooth-base-change clause (2) from lem-pc-proper-base-change, and smooth-composition from lem-trf-smooth-composition. These ensure every product used as a test object is smooth. Generic group laws extend by the mapping property and their equations follow from its uniqueness, so no group operations are falsely assumed to be defined on arbitrary W charts. The supplied group identifications over each D_m make B an open subgroup with exactly the neutral geometric fibres; the generic fibre is connected too. Hence B=N^0. I checked the trivial group, a base with only one closed point, empty affine test schemes, unbounded local component groups, and infinitely many maximal ideals. The patching lemma also covers a field base with U and V empty (the equivalence reduces to its T factor), and empty local or complement objects with compatible empty puncture. I found no statement dispute.
