## Split audit (delivery-errors B--E)

1. Step 1 chooses finite generators `b_i` of the finite-type algebra `B` and
   representatives `p_i/u_i` for their images in `U^{-1}P`.  This is exactly
   what the cited finite-type and localization definitions provide.  The
   finite choice is valid even when `W` and `U` are not finitely generated.
2. Step 2 forms `A = S tensor_R P` and
   `Q = A[t_i]/(u_i t_i-1, b_i tensor 1 - (1 tensor p_i)t_i)`.  The cited
   base-change finite-presentation leaf gives `A` finite presentation over
   `S`; adjoining finitely many variables and quotienting by the displayed
   finite ideal gives `Q` finite presentation over `S`.  The relations are
   well-typed after viewing `p_i,u_i` in `A` through `1 tensor P`.
3. Step 3 applies the localization universal property twice.  The map
   `Q -> U^{-1}P` sends `t_i` to `1/u_i`; both relation checks are explicit.
   Localizing at the image `V` of `U` is legitimate because every element of
   `V` is a unit in `U^{-1}P`.
4. Step 4 checks both composites.  The reverse composite fixes `1 tensor P`
   and each `t_i`; it fixes the `B`-image because the `b_i` generate `B` over
   `R`, then fixes inverses of elements of `W` by uniqueness of inverses, hence
   all of `S = W^{-1}B`, then `A`, `Q`, and the localization.  This is a
   genuine inverse check, not a use of a proof hidden in a cited node.

The new node is strictly smaller than the parent limit-tower statement: it
only supplies the localized cancellation/presentation construction and does
not restate the limit system or its module conclusions.  All non-elementary
facts used by the steps have cited nodes; the localization universal property,
finite-presentation base change/composition, and finite-generated-ideal
quotient are existing Mathlib leaves or existing project nodes.  The split is
not a relabeling or a cycle.

## Edge cases

The construction still works when there are no generators (`n=0`), no
relations, or when a multiplicative set contains `0` (then the corresponding
localization is the zero ring).  If `u_i=0`, the target localization is the
zero ring, and the relation `u_i t_i=1` likewise makes `Q` zero; otherwise a
zero denominator cannot represent an element.  Thus no extra hypotheses are
being used.
