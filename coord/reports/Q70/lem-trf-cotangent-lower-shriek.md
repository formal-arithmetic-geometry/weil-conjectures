### Returned step 1

The polynomial formulas define the three functors: writing $P=A[S]$ and $Q=P[T]$ gives $Q=A[S\amalg T]$ and $Q\otimes_P B=B[T]$. A functor between these presheaf sites is continuous and cocontinuous, since covers are generated by identities. Precomposition preserves kernels and cokernels objectwise; apply the cited derived-adjunction theorem with the constant ring $\underline R$. It supplies each $Lg_{i,!}^R\dashv g_i^{-1}$ with its canonical counit. For the variable rings, precomposition gives respectively $P,Q,Q\otimes_P B$ and the identity structural map; their augmentations and the coefficient map $B\to C$ are the specified algebra maps. Thus these structural rings are distinct from the constant coefficient ring used in the counit calculations.

### Returned step 2

Fix $V\in\mathcal C_i$. The comma formula computes $(Lg_{i,!}^Rg_i^{-1}K)(V)$ by the bar complex on $\mathcal I_V=(V\downarrow u_i)$, with coefficient $K(u_iU)$ at $(U,a)$. These are precisely the components of the counit, so their quasi-isomorphisms prove the presheaf counit is an isomorphism.

### Returned step 3

For $i=1$, define $s_1(P\to B)=(P\to B,P\to C)$. The ring maps $P\xrightarrow{1}P$ and $P\to Q$ define a natural arrow $U\to s_1u_1U$ in the opposite category, whose image under $u_1$ is the identity.
