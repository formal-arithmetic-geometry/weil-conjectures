## Review by Q70

I read Milne Theorem 1.45 with its proof, Corollaries 1.46–1.47, Aside 1.55, and the book's conventions (printed pp.3–4). In Chapters 1–8 algebraic groups are not required to be affine and nilpotents are allowed; these hypotheses match the item. The new node is precisely the independent closed-point-subgroup realization result, not the monoid-to-subgroup argument. It is stated over an arbitrary field because Theorem 1.45 is; the application uses the item's algebraically closed field. The statement gives the reduced closure itself and the restricted scheme morphisms, so the final step does not borrow a construction hidden in a proof. Its proof is intentionally left empty, as required for a new unsplit node. The supplementary construction reference is Stacks 01J3, schemes.tex label lemma-reduced-closed-subscheme, whose statement and proof I read; no SGA source is used.

For step 1 I opened def-weil-finite-type-morphism: its equivalent finite affine-cover formulation supplies finitely generated coordinate algebras. I opened lem-wf-affine-noetherian-components: it states exactly the descending-closed-chain property for each affine spectrum. I opened lem-kun-finite-union-noetherian: it applies to arbitrary subspaces, so open affine charts are allowed and no affineness of G is inferred. This establishes Noetherianity on all of |G|, where the later chain lives.

For step 2 I opened def-sc-group-scheme, which gives actual scheme morphisms and the unit/inverse/associativity diagrams, not just an abstract group on rational points. Composing the proposed translations with translations by inverses gives identities; their underlying maps are homeomorphisms. The two successive closure operations are justified by homeomorphisms carrying closure to closure. This elementary step does not assume joint continuity on the product of the point spaces, or that products of closures commute with closure.

For step 3 I checked the same group and Noetherianity citations again. Every translate is closed in the same ambient |G|, and the chain is decreasing because L_a(Z) is contained in Z. Cancellation of the stabilized equality uses the ambient inverse translation, so it does not presuppose the missing inverse morphism on H. The point mapping to 1 is the rational inverse a^{-1}; equality of rational points follows from their identification with residue-field-k points. Thus S really is inverse-stable before the realization theorem is invoked. This is the essential monoid argument and remains in the item's proof.

For step 4 I checked the full statement of the new realization node and of lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme. The former supplies all three restricted morphisms. The latter supplies the exact radical vanishing ideals, reduced closed support and uniqueness needed to identify K with H, not just existence of some closed subscheme. The density argument is in the item: M intersect U is dense in Z intersect U for every open U, and zero loci are closed. Hence the ideal in the imported statement is exactly the ideal of Z_red; no containment of supports is mistaken for equality of scheme structures.

I tested the item with M={1}, with finite constant groups, with positive powers of a point of G_m, with a nonaffine abelian variety, and with nonreduced finite groups such as alpha_p. The identity-only closure is the reduced identity subgroup; positive powers are handled by the same translate chain; nonaffine and disconnected cases require no change; nilpotents in G do not enter the argument about its reduced closed support. For the new node the trivial subgroup over a nonperfect field still gives Spec k, and the whole rational-point subgroup is only claimed to recover its reduced closure, not all of a group with nondense rational points. The empty subgroup is excluded by the identity condition. No argument uses a characteristic restriction, smoothness, commutativity or a dense open affine.
