## Audit findings and corrections

The phrase “adjunction-mate comparisons for these squares” must not be read as saying that the small-restriction inverse-image square commutes. There is a canonical transformation
`f'_s^{-1} i_g^{-1} -> i_{g'}^{-1} f_b^{-1}`, and it can be noninvertible even though its direct-image mate is an isomorphism. For example take `S=Spec k`, `g=id`, `f:A^1_k -> Spec k`, and the additive big sheaf of regular functions: restriction to the small site of the point followed by pullback gives the constant sheaf k, whereas restriction over A^1 gives its regular-function sheaf and contains the nonconstant coordinate. The existing statement does not assert invertibility of that inverse-image transformation; steps 7–8 make the distinction explicit. By contrast the big localization square has identical composite inverse images. After composition with `pi_S^{-1}`, the first transformation and the comparison square do give the ordinary small-site inverse-image composition isomorphism, with the adjunction normalization checked in step 8. No false commuting-restriction-square shortcut is used.

The argument proving injective preservation is restricted to abelian sheaves. Stacks 0758 explicitly gives a counterexample for structure-sheaf modules. The proof therefore factors arbitrary small restriction through big localization and `pi_*`, rather than assuming a theorem for arbitrary ringed-topos pullback.

The unit route is `sk-i-curve-zero`, inside the fixed trace-formula chapter. This item is a generic tool below the normal-cone specialization's first-descent branch, not the key node realizing the unit.
