For `lem-trf-hilbert-samuel-near-normal-cone`, I read CJS's definitions of normal flatness and permissibility and Theorems 2.2--2.3,2.14 online. Characteristic zero makes every residue field perfect and makes the directrix an actual linear translation subspace, giving the polynomial factor over k. Normal flatness identifies the normal-cone fibre with the tangent cone modulo the tangent space of the centre. Nearness puts y in the projectivization of the remaining directrix; hence its dimension e is positive. Smoothness of the centre supplies regularity, and the normalized H-constancy gives Bennett's numerical normal-flatness condition: if eta is its local generic point and d=dim(O_C,x), trdeg_Q kappa(eta)=trdeg_Q kappa(x)+d, so cancellation of Lambda gives h_x=Lambda^d h_eta. Likewise trdeg_Q kappa(y)=trdeg_Q kappa(x)+delta, hence equality at y gives the raw near condition Lambda^delta h_y=h_x. Lambda is injective by taking successive differences. These conversions do not use any conclusion about components. In particular, no circular appeal is made to CJS's differently normalized H or its Theorem 2.10(3). The lemma has no rationality assumption on y and does not silently replace a scheme point by a k-rational point.

CJS phrases permissibility with no component contained in the centre. Here that condition is forced locally whenever there is a near point: if a component through x lies in the smooth centre, the centre is locally irreducible at x and must equal that component locally. Its generic point is a reduced generic point of X, so its local ring is a field. H-constancy then equates h_x with the smooth centre's local Hilbert--Samuel function after cancelling Lambda. The quotient O_X,x -> O_C,x induces surjections modulo every maximal-ideal power of equal length, hence is an isomorphism on each such quotient; Krull intersection gives zero kernel. Thus X=C near x, whose blowup has empty inverse image there, contrary to y. This verifies that the source's convention does not add a hypothesis or exclude the item's allowed cases.

For `lem-trf-normal-cone-directrix-minimal-primes`, I read the actual Stacks `algebra.tex` statements and proofs at labels `lemma-intersect-powers-ideal-module-zero` (00IP), `lemma-free-fibre-flat-free` (00MH), and `lemma-reduced-ring-sub-product-fields` (00EW), checking the tag mapping. The node is an agent synthesis, not attributed to any one of them. Here is the feasibility argument, kept in the report rather than in the unsplit node. Fix p and a homogeneous element in ker(q_p) in degree n. Right exactness of tensor and the explicit quotient filtration show that it is represented by the initial form of f in p intersect I^n. If its image in k[T_1,...,T_e] were nonzero and e>0, infinitude of k permits choosing a vector at which this homogeneous polynomial is nonzero. Change the linear T-coordinates so that this vector is the T_1-axis. Its initial form in B[T] then has nonzero scalar coefficient of T_1^n and all other terms have smaller T_1-degree. It is monic up to a scalar, so multiplication by it is injective on B[T], even when B has zero divisors.

Each G_j is a finite A-module because I is finitely generated, and flat as a direct summand of G. It is finite free over the Noetherian local A (00MH with R=S=A; zero modules are harmless). The multiplication map G_j -> G_{j+n} has injective reduction modulo the maximal ideal of A. A full-rank minor is therefore a unit, giving injectivity before reduction. The initial form of f is regular on G. The I-adic filtration is separated by 00IP since I is proper in the local Noetherian R. If fg=0 with g nonzero, take the finite I-adic order of g; its nonzero initial form contradicts this regularity. Therefore f is a nonzerodivisor, contrary to f being in a minimal prime of the reduced R by 00EW(3). This proves the kernel containment. If e=0, the target k is concentrated in degree zero and q_p is the identity there, so the assertion is automatic. This argument does not assume that the quotient associated-graded ring is flat over A.
