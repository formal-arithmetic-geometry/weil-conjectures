## Review by Q70

I checked this as the author, against delivery-errors B–E with the node statements and the source passages open. The available human source gives the pointwise perverse condition; the expanded equivalence and shifted Hom calculation are my argument. Its exact claim uses finite commutative coefficients and bounded-below complexes on ordinary small etale sites. No statement dispute was found.

Step 1 uses the statement of `lem-pur-etale-supported-kernel-adjunction`: it gives the actual supported-sheaf identification, the exactness of closed direct image, and its adjunction, not just a definition. It is stated for abelian sheaves. The step therefore writes out why naturality restricts the bijection to scalar-linear maps. `lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-colimits-forgetful` explicitly says coefficient forgetting is faithful and exact, so exactness is legitimately detected there.

Step 2 uses `prop-weil-pbc-inverse-image-modules-exact` for exact inverse image and its module adjunction, `lem-sp-right-adjoint-preserves-injectives` twice, and `lem-weil-pbc-dplus-injective-representative` for genuine bounded-below injective complexes. These statements ensure both factors of the sheaf support functor preserve module injectives. I checked that this does not claim that an injective module sheaf has injective underlying abelian sheaf.

Step 3 cites the definition of derived supported sections and the injective-representative theorem. It proves the composite identities on those same injective complexes, using the pointwise equality of kernels when the smaller support is contained in the larger. This is the support-transitivity needed later; it is not borrowed from a theorem with excellent-scheme or prime-power restrictions. The equality depends only on the complement, which also justifies using reduced supports in Step 12.

Step 4 cites the representative theorem's stronger clause (1), which permits a representative with terms zero strictly below the cohomological lower bound. The displayed kernel calculation then proves the bottom formula for any left exact functor. This is a few-line calculation, not an uncited spectral-sequence edge theorem. The same argument applies to the sheaf support functor, supported global sections, and ordinary global sections.

Step 5 uses the statement of `lem-weil-pbc-etale-restriction-injectives`, which explicitly covers modules over a constant ring and preserves injectives under etale restriction, and exact inverse image from the module theorem. The restriction comparison for the support functor is proved sectionwise on the localized small site. Thus no arbitrary base-change assertion has been smuggled into the etale restriction step.

Step 6 checks the affine, Noetherian and ind-etale hypotheses required by Step 8. I read the definition of etale morphisms and the local finite-presentation definition, and the statements on finite-type Noetherian algebras, localization of Noetherian rings, Noetherian strict henselization, and the ind-etale affine chart of a strict localization. A finite affine cover of the affine source suffices to verify it is Noetherian. Only the strict-localization map from an affine scheme is used in the affine ind-etale theorem; no arbitrary etale map into a nonseparated scheme is assumed to be affine.

Step 7 uses the actual maximal-ideal and residue-field conclusion of `lem-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-triples`. Every element outside the prime is a unit in this local ring. A prime lying over the support contracts to a prime between its ideal and the selected minimal prime, and must therefore be the latter; it then contains the entire maximal ideal of the strict henselization. This proves that the inverse-image support is the closed point, even if the inverse-image subscheme has nilpotents. The closed subset, rather than a claimed reduced base-change identification, is all the kernel needs.

Step 8 uses the full statement of `lem-per-ind-etale-closed-support-base-change`, which gives the supported comparison for arbitrary bounded-below finite-ring complexes without constructibility and explicitly names its closed direct-image comparison. I checked its coefficient and Noetherian assumptions against Step 6. `lem-trf-ind-etale-strict-local-invariance` explicitly gives the strict-localization isomorphism for an etale map, so the costalk on an auxiliary etale scheme really is a costalk of the original complex.

Step 9 takes the first possible nonzero cohomology degree, using the fixed global lower bound of K. It does not choose a generic point of the support of an arbitrary nonconstructible sheaf. The chosen section has a closed zero-complement by `lem-sc-etale-section-zero-open`; the same statement says its germ is nonzero at every point of that complement. `lem-sc-noetherian-closed-subset-finite-components` and `lem-sc-scheme-sober` provide the component and its generic point. Since the sheaf is supported on the original Z, its section support maps into S.

Step 10 pulls this particular section back, keeping a nonzero germ by the explicit germ-compatible statement of `lem-weil-pbc-inverse-image-stalk-germ`. Exact module pullback preserves the lower bound and cohomology. Step 7 makes the section supported at the closed point, and Step 4 now gives a nonzero bottom supported class. The ind-etale support comparison and strict-local invariance cited again here give precisely the original geometric costalk. The support nesting used in Step 3 is verified by the fact that the chosen generic point lies in Z. This is the contradiction to (1), not an appeal to the item itself.

Step 11 is just derived global sections of the sheaf-support bound, with the same representative theorem and Step 4. Thus vanishing of local-cohomology sheaves really yields the asserted global supported groups.

Step 12 reverses the detection: at a first nonzero sheaf degree the bottom formula on an affine etale object turns a nonzero section into a prohibited supported class. The reduced induced closed-subscheme theorem supplies the required reduced Z_W; Step 3 guarantees its underlying support gives the same kernel. The source of W -> X is affine, so it is one of the test objects in (2). Its support still maps into S. This is a local sheaf argument, not a claim that arbitrary higher-cohomology classes glue as sections.

Step 13 is the only place specialization stability of S is needed: the closure of x in an affine neighbourhood is contained in S. It is given the genuine reduced closed structure by the cited theorem. The supported base-change statement identifies the pullback sheaf-support complex with closed-point supported cohomology, whose lower bound then survives derived sections by Step 4. Every geometric point over x is allowed, so (1) is obtained with its full quantifier.

Step 14 uses the injective representative and `lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-hom-derived` to compute Hom in the unbounded derived category; the latter explicitly also describes D+ as a full subcategory. The exact etale module adjunction and its injective-preservation statement give the termwise Hom comparison. The supported adjunction is the one already extended to linear maps in Step 1. Evaluation at 1 identifies Hom from the rank-one constant module with sections, even when that constant module is not projective in the sheaf category. Chain maps from the source placed in degree m modulo homotopy compute H^m of this Hom complex, giving the sign [-m] as stated. Naturality follows from these actual natural adjunction and evaluation maps.

Small-case checks: empty S permits only empty supports and both sides vanish; the zero complex is immediate; empty Z gives a zero kernel; Z=V reduces to ordinary sections. On a point the two conditions both say the complex is concentrated in degrees greater than a, and Hom from the shifted rank-one constant module computes the same cohomology. For a section with reducible closed support, localization at a chosen component's generic point discards the other components and the prime calculation in Step 7 still applies. For arbitrary nonconstructible sheaves the entire sheaf support need not be closed, which is exactly why the proof uses a single section. The least-degree argument works with any integer lower bound and requires no bound on Krull dimension. Nilpotents do not affect a restriction kernel with a fixed open complement.
