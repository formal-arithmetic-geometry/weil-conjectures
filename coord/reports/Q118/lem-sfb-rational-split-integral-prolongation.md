## Source error

In the paragraph of Conrad's proof of Proposition 6.3.15 crossing printed pp.228--229, the displayed choice n_c = exp_c(1) is a typographical error if exp_c means the root homomorphism introduced in that same sentence: an element of U_c normalizes U_c and cannot conjugate it to U_{-c}. Already in SL_2 over Q the upper-unitriangular matrix with upper entry 1 preserves the upper root subgroup. The required representative is the three-factor word x_c(1) x_{-c}(-1) x_c(1). Direct matrix multiplication gives [[0,1],[-1,0]], whose conjugation sends the upper entry z to lower entry -z. The two new root-cell statements use that explicit word, and the envelope identities extend it from the rational group over every test algebra. This is a correction of this intermediate notation, not a change of the item's statement or a departure from the construction.

## Review by Q118

I read Conrad Section 6.3 beginning with Lemma 6.3.1 (pp.212--213), the setup and positive-root integral laws (pp.217--220), the birational law and strictness construction (pp.223--227), and the fibre and affineness arguments (pp.227--229). For the envelope node I extracted only the construction before reductivity: finite presentation is finite type over the Noetherian base Z, and the product chart contains the identity. The rank-one identity is equation (6.3.5); torus conjugation is the full character identity, not a differential. I checked that the argument still allows several irreducible components of the root system. Its torus and root maps are initially immersions, exactly as the source cautions; they were not declared closed before affineness.

For the field criterion I read the whole proof of Proposition 6.3.15: the coroots generate the torus, hence commutative quotients vanish; the linearity theorem then gives affineness; torus centralizers and dynamic root groups rule out a nonzero unipotent radical. The hypotheses state all the cell and opposite-root identities that this reasoning uses, including a genuine Weyl representative. Reducedness of the root system excludes other roots proportional to c in the codimension-one centralizer. The criterion includes the source's simply connected coroot-generation hypothesis, so it does not silently apply that generation argument to a non-simply-connected torus. Geometric labels are fixed by the monomial torus, its full character weights and the rank-one relation. For the affineness node I read Proposition 6.3.16: the relative adjoint map has finite geometric kernels; the group homomorphism affineness theorem over a Dedekind base gives the claim. This is a separate global geometric criterion, with no reductive-existence conclusion.

Step 1 uses exactly the new envelope statement, including its generic isomorphism and specified torus. Step 2 uses exactly the field criterion and relative affineness statements. The dense product cell is geometrically irreducible, hence makes the whole fibre irreducible and connected. The existing multiplicative-type monomorphism lemma says closed immersion into an affine finitely presented group, so it applies only after affineness has been proved. Step 3 is the elementary differential of the displayed open product chart, with the coaction computed by the stated conjugation formula; it uses only that chart and the definition of a split realization. The integral root lines have the actual coordinate tangent generators.

In step 4 I checked an extra hypothesis of the lattice lemma: the reflection group is finite. This follows from the finite Weyl group and reflection realization for the given rational group's algebraic closure, explicitly cited. The lattice lemma gives j(alpha)=(alpha,0), not just some finite-index embedding. Its coroot formula is precisely what is needed later. Rational realization is used only for the semisimple simply connected datum on its weight lattice, and the construction of steps 1--3 applies to that rational group. The dual of the weight lattice is the coroot lattice, so that datum satisfies the stronger hypothesis used in those steps.

Step 5 uses the affine permanence and smooth tensor/base-change statements for the product, with finite presentation following by adjoining the two finite lists of generators and relations. A torus has no unipotent subgroup by the unipotent-to-multiplicative statement. I checked the connectedness hypothesis of the subdirect-product theorem separately: the split torus is geometrically connected because its Laurent algebra remains a domain after field extension, and geometrically-connected-product-components identifies the components of its product with those of the connected fibre of S. The subdirect-product theorem then proves reductivity of this already smooth connected product. I added the product clause of central-isogeny-borel-root-compatibility, applied to the identity isogeny, to justify the root and coroot product labels rather than assuming them from a definition.
