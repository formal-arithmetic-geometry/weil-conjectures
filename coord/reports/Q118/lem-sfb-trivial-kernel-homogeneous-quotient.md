## Review by Q118

I read Milne, *Algebraic Groups* (the local 2017 PDF), Definition 5.20 and Theorem 5.28 on pp.104-105, Proposition 5.31 and the first proof of Theorem 5.34 on pp.106-107, and Appendix B Lemma B.36 and Theorem B.37 on pp.604-606. I also opened the Stacks source of 06PH (`bootstrap.tex`, `lemma-quotient-free-action`), the dense scheme open theorem 086U (`decent-spaces.tex`), and the largest schematic open 03JH (`spaces-properties.tex`). The final proof uses these existing nodes as statements; it does not use constructions hidden only in their proofs. I read all twenty cited node statements, retaining their text in `citations.json`.

1. The first step uses exactly the group-scheme definition and the stated kernel fibre product. Equality of two images forces their ratio through the trivial kernel on every test scheme. Inversion in the right multiplication action is necessary for it to be a left action; I checked the order of the factors explicitly. This step is elementary and creates no injectivity lemma.
2. The representable-scheme algebraic-space definition supplies the actual sheaf associated to a scheme. `lem-pc-flat-field` applies to the structure map of H, not to an arbitrary morphism between schemes. `pqf-lem-lft-fp-locnoeth` applies because Spec k is locally Noetherian and H is finite type. `lem-sfb-free-flat-group-space-quotient` requires only a free action and flat lfp group, not a closed subgroup. Its statement gives both the quotient and the isomorphism of the kernel pair. Reparametrizing its pair `(g i(h)^{-1}, g)` gives the required `(g, g i(h))`; no assertion about reduced points is substituted.
3. The same quotient node states fppf local sections and that F is an fppf sheaf. I wrote the local lifts, overlap equality, independence, naturality and action laws, rather than assuming sheafification preserves an unstated product. This step defines the action even while F is merely an algebraic space.
4. `lem-sfb-space-qcqs-permanence` says that a map from a quasi-compact space to a quasi-separated space is quasi-compact. Here its source is G times H and its target is the separated scheme G times G. `lem-sfb-space-fppf-covering-calculus` supplies the base-change/composition covering q times q, and `lem-sfb-space-quasicompact-fppf-local` supplies descent of quasi-compactness of the diagonal along it. I checked that the pullback is precisely the kernel-pair map and that these statements need no separatedness of F. This corrects the tempting but circular claim that its diagonal was already closed.
5. `lem-sfb-representable-lft-fppf-source-descent` applies to algebraic-space morphisms and concludes local finite type from G, since q is surjective flat lfp. The topology node states continuity of all morphisms, which makes F a quasi-compact point space. The permanence node then proves quasi-compactness of the structure map to the affine field spectrum. This checks both parts of finite type, instead of identifying a topologically quasi-compact source with a quasi-compact morphism without checking the target.
6. The dense-open node assumes a quasi-compact relative diagonal, already verified. The largest-schematic-open node has no separatedness assumption and explicitly states that the union of all scheme opens is itself a scheme open. Its maximality proves translation invariance and inclusion under field extension. These are applications of its statement, not of a hidden gluing construction.
7. `lem-weil-pbc-zariski-lemma` makes the residue of a maximal ideal in a finite-type affine algebra a finite extension; no perfectness or separability is imposed. `lem-sfb-finite-extension-open-exhaustion` is applied to the finite-type scheme G over k1, not to the algebraic space F. Both required hypotheses are checked: rational points lie in the opens by translation from g0, and the opens increase under extension by step 6. The covering-calculus node supplies base-change surjectivity, and the four-property node supplies finite-type base change. Only after this step is F_K called a scheme.
8. The closed-point-field criterion applies to Y because it is a finite-type scheme over K. Its rational point has residue field exactly K, even if K is imperfect. The residue-point node explicitly equates topological closedness with a closed residue-field immersion. The kernel-pair formula identifies its scheme fibre with H_K. Pulling back its ideal proves that i_K is closed. The closed-immersion descent node accepts the finite flat lfp surjective cover G_K to G; a finite field basis verifies those covering hypotheses. This is where closedness is first obtained, rather than assumed.
9. The existing homogeneous-quotient node assumes separated finite-type group schemes and a closed subgroup. Those hypotheses now all hold, and its statement supplies a finite-type scheme and a flat surjective finitely presented quotient map with the full all-test-scheme right-coset relation. I checked its independent dependency closure before using it.
10. The comparison of X with F is written in both directions using local lifts and their common kernel pair. Both are fppf sheaves: F by the free-action quotient theorem and X by the representable-scheme algebraic-space construction. The big fppf-site definition makes the flat surjective finitely presented scheme pullback of p a cover. I corrected an early draft that invoked fpqc sheaf descent for arbitrary fppf local covers without separately explaining the refinement. The delivered text uses their fppf sheaf property directly. The action morphism is then obtained by evaluating the natural pointwise action on the universal point, and p(1) gives its base point.
11. The final step is a direct specialization of the existing quotient theorem's exact formula on every scheme T. It checks the direction `a^{-1}b`, rather than `ab^{-1}`, and retains all requested clauses.
