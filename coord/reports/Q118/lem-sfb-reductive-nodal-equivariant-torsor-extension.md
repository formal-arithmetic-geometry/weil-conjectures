## Review by Q118

I read the main paper's Section 2 Claim 2 in full, including the passage from a reductive torsor to a linear torsor plus section. Its proof uses only regularity in dimension two, extension of vector bundles, and an affine quotient; completeness and algebraic closure first appear in the subsequent Claim 3. I also read Section 3's proof of Theorem 1.3, (1) implies (2), which explains the same linear-quotient argument for general bases. I did not import that theorem as a replacement for this item's argument.

For lem-sfb-nodal-surface-flat-hartogs I checked the regularity assertion in Claim 2 against the explicit ring: A[s,t]/(st-pi) embeds in Frac(A)[s,s^-1] with t=pi/s. Away from (s,t) the two charts are Laurent polynomial rings over A. At the missing point (s,t) is the maximal ideal and (0) < (s) < (s,t) is a prime chain; its two generators give dimension exactly two and regularity. The Laurent equalizer gives B = B_s intersection B_t inside B_st: in the s^d basis, B_t has coefficient pi^-d A, so the intersection coefficient is A for d >= 0 and pi^-d A for d < 0, exactly B. Tensoring the short exact equalizer at its first two terms with any flat B-algebra preserves it. Principal localization proves the sheaf assertion on affine charts of T. Locally trivializing two bundles then gives Hom extension entry by entry. An affine target is relative Spec of an algebra, so its sections are algebra maps into the structure sheaf and have the same extension property. These expansions check the flat-base clauses, including non-Noetherian T; the new node records them as a real input, rather than silently taking arbitrary base-change Hartogs from a Noetherian-only node.

For lem-sfb-regular-surface-vector-bundle-extension I opened literature/stacks-project/divisors.tex at lemma-reflexive-S2-extend (Tag 0EBJ) and lemma-reflexive-over-regular-dim-2 (Tag 0B3N), reading their statements and proofs. The first gives an equivalence for coherent reflexive modules when the boundary local rings have depth at least two; the second identifies those modules with finite locally free ones on a regular scheme of dimension <= 2. The regular boundary here has local dimension two, so this is the finite-locally-free consequence of those two tags. The existing 0EBJ citer is a finite-etale-algebra model, not this statement. The dimension bound is essential: this new node does not assert vector-bundle extension in dimension three.

For lem-sfb-dvr-reductive-linear-affine-quotient I checked the Dedekind paragraph of Milne VIII Aside 9.4: a finite subrepresentation of the regular representation containing algebra generators is torsion-free over a Dedekind domain and hence projective, giving a linear closed embedding. Over a DVR the finite projective module is free. I used only that paragraph, not the later general-base splitting claim. I checked Alper Corollary 9.7.7: the ambient GL_n is reductive and the subgroup H is flat, finitely presented and separated; smoothness gives flatness, finite type over the Noetherian DVR gives finite presentation, and affineness gives separatedness. Thus reductivity of H makes GL_n/H affine. The quotient is a sheaf quotient and its torsor map remains such after any base change. I checked that n can be positive even when H is trivial. The node introduces the group-object and quotient conventions explicitly and does not rely on a field-only group definition to supply missing hypotheses.

I checked the orientation of the right actions: (p,l)h=(ph,i(h)^-1 l), and the recovered reduction is the inverse image of the quotient section. The quotient E/H is locally a twist of the affine L/H; its affineness is included explicitly, so no uncited descent theorem is needed in the item's assembly. This statement is a generic reduction-of-structure-group tool, not a clause asserting extension across a puncture.
