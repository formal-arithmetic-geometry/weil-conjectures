## Review by Q118

I read the unchanged item against Stacks 0328 and the actual statements and proofs of 05GG, 05GI and 00PD in algebra.tex, checking the tags against tags/tags. The source uses a flat p-adic input to obtain regularity; the present statement instead assumes regularity directly, and Step 4 gives the missing completion argument for that more general input. This is an agent-written generalization with its argument spelled out, not an assertion that 0328 literally states the item. I read delivery-errors B--E whole and checked the following uses with all four cited node files open.

Step 1 applies lem-weil-pbc-adic-completion-mod-ideal to the principal ideal I=tS. It requires finite generation only, not Noetherianity: I is generated by the single t. Its statement explicitly supplies all of the surjectivity, first-coordinate kernel, algebra isomorphism, canonical-map compatibility and ID-adic completeness that the step takes from it. def-weil-pbc-adically-complete-ring supplies the literal inverse-limit object of the item. Since m is the unique maximal ideal, S/tS is a nonzero field, so the quotient cannot accidentally be zero.

Step 2 uses that same quotient statement and lem-weil-pbc-adic-complete-jacobson-radical. The latter explicitly states that every maximal ideal contains the complete ideal. Completeness is already known for ID, so it is applied to D and ID, not to S. Because D/ID is a field, ID is itself maximal; any maximal ideal containing it equals it. This is exactly locality, and the known completeness is then for the maximal ideal in the statement.

Step 3 is elementary cancellation in S and coordinate evaluation in the definition of completion. I checked both exclusions: t=0 contradicts injectivity on nonzero S, while t a unit contradicts the properness of m. The separate calculation t=t^2s implies 1=ts is what proves the image of t is nonzero already modulo t^2. This does not assume that S injects into D.

Step 4 is an elementary quotient calculation, not an appeal to flatness of completion. The representative a_(n+1) exists by the definition of the quotient; regularity gives ta_(n+1)=t^(n+1)b implies a_(n+1)=t^n b. Compatibility of the inverse-limit coordinates then gives x_n=0 for every n. This proves regularity of the completed generator without any Noetherian hypothesis.

Step 5 uses only the completed ring's separatedness, included explicitly in def-weil-pbc-adically-complete-ring, and the completeness clause of the completion-mod-ideal statement. A nonzero element has a finite last membership exponent in the descending powers: the least excluded power is positive, and subtracting one gives the stated r. The factor y cannot belong to the maximal ideal, otherwise x would belong to the next power. Locality from Step 2 says y is a unit; equivalently an inverse modulo ID lifts because ID is in the Jacobson radical. No general valuation or Krull intersection theorem is used here, and no domain property is used before it is proved.

Step 6 uses the factorization already established and cancellation from Step 4. Every power of the regular element is regular by composing its multiplication map. If two nonzero factorizations multiply to zero, cancel that power and the two unit factors, yielding 1=0, impossible since D surjects onto the nonzero field S/tS. This proves the domain clause, and the nonzero element in its proper maximal ideal proves that D is not a field. def-sc-discrete-valuation-ring is the definition being assembled, not a substitute for the argument.

Step 7 is the elementary least-natural-exponent argument for ideals. The set of exponents of nonzero elements of a nonzero ideal is nonempty and has a least member. The chosen element is a unit times t-hat^r, so its ideal equals t-hat^rD, and every other element has exponent at least r and belongs to it. This proves every ideal principal, not just the maximal ideal. I then apply the exact local-domain/principal-ideal/not-field definition from def-sc-discrete-valuation-ring. Completeness and the canonical residue identification are the exact clauses already proved in Step 1, not consequences inferred from being a DVR.

I tested the hypothesis boundaries on a field (its maximal ideal can only have generator zero, which is not regular in a nonzero field), on S=k[t]_(t), and on a complete DVR with a generator multiplied by a unit. I also checked the nonseparated and nondomain input S=A semidirect-product K, where A=k[[t]], K=k((t)), and K is a square-zero ideal. Here t acts bijectively on K and regularly on A, the maximal ideal is tS, and S/t^nS=A/t^nA, so the completion is A; the proof correctly never needs injectivity of S to D. Dropping regularity admits S=k[t]/(t^2), whose completion is not a DVR, so Step 4's hypothesis is essential. S=0 is explicitly excluded. No conclusion about a field residue's characteristic or perfectness is used.
