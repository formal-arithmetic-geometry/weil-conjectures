## Review by Q118

I checked the statement against Theorem 1.13 itself: its Picard group is the ordinary group of invertible sheaves of degree zero on EACH component, not a total-degree-zero group, and the theorem is for arbitrary stable curves over an algebraically closed field. Neither the characteristic nor the order of the automorphism is restricted. The item's definitions of stability and normalization degree agree with this. I retained every statement hypothesis and all existing mathematical metadata.

For step 1 I opened the statement of the new component-gluing lemma: its restriction map is surjective onto the product over the integral components, and its equivariance explicitly includes permutation of factors. That supports the singleton-support argument proving a component with nontrivial Picard zero is fixed. I also opened def-sfb-stable-pointed-curve; it supplies connectedness, projectivity, reducedness and the nodal hypothesis needed here. This is why I did not simply cite the old full-normalization sequence: a rational normalization with self-nodes can have nontrivial component Picard zero.

For step 2 the new gluing node explicitly says that an integral component with trivial Picard zero is P^1. The stable-curve definition explicitly counts special points on its normalization. For a component actually isomorphic to P^1 there are no self-nodes, so these special points are exactly its intercomponent edges. This elementary argument proves the graph's low-degree fixed-vertex hypothesis; I did not create a node restating that clause of the item.

For step 3 I checked both the injectivity and equivariance of the graph-kernel map in the gluing statement. The proof writes out separation of a nonzero lattice vector by a coordinate character: it works even when k is an algebraic closure of a finite field, because for each fixed nonzero integer n there are elements outside the finite set of n-th roots of unity. The graph lemma is applied to a loopless multigraph, retaining parallel edges, with identity on the integral cycle lattice and the exact low-valency hypothesis established in step 2. No general graph statement was applied to a graph with unremoved self-loops.

For step 4 I read the integral-normalization node's precise assertion: it supplies the smooth connected normalization, unique lift, identity on normalized Picard zero, and pointwise fixation of the preimages of self-nodes. It does NOT assert curve faithfulness, so the remaining genus cases genuinely remain to prove. The intercomponent nodes are already fixed by step 3, and each is a smooth point of the reduced component; it has a unique normalized preimage. This gives exactly the special points used below.

For step 5 I opened sk-thm-picard-main, lem-pc-rational-points-dense and lem-sfb-curve-polarized-jacobian-faithfulness. The first gives a smooth separated Picard-zero variety with the k-point dictionary, the second gives density because k is separably closed, and the third gives a group-scheme automorphism induced by pullback and its injectivity for genus at least two. Since that theorem needs identity of scheme morphisms, I supplied the closed-equalizer and reduced-ideal argument, rather than treating equality on ordinary line bundles as equality of functors without proof. These are generic smooth-Picard and density tools, declared in split_report.tool_uses; no change to the unit route is intended.

For step 6 the low-genus statement supplies exactly the two facts used: in genus one a Picard-trivial automorphism with a fixed point is identity, and in genus zero fixing three distinct points is enough. The stable-curve definition supplies one or three DISTINCT special points, respectively; step 4 fixed each of them individually. In step 7 I wrote out the reduced-ring descent argument: normalization is surjective on points and functions on a reduced curve inject into the product of its component function fields. This closes the proof as equality of scheme automorphisms, including all component intersections.

I checked small cases: a smooth curve gives a one-vertex graph, for which the graph lemma is harmless; an irreducible rational curve with two self-nodes has genus two and four normalized branches, and its Picard zero is NOT discarded as trivial; a rational component joined by three parallel edges is a degree-three vertex, and permuting edges is detected by the two-dimensional cycle lattice; an elliptic tail requires its attaching point to eliminate translations; a tree has zero cycle lattice but its low-degree fixed vertices force the graph automorphism to be identity; the empty graph is allowed in the generic combinatorial input, though not as a connected nonempty stable curve. In characteristic two, inversion on k^* is still distinct from identity (choose an element of order three), so the ordered-branch statement does not silently lose branch swapping. Rational genus-one self-nodal components are handled by the genus-zero normalization case with branch and attachment points, not by assuming they are smooth elliptic curves.
