Applying [lem-weil-pbc-localization-base-change] to $D\to D_g$ shows that $H$ and $T$ are the away localizations of $F$ and $E$ at the respective images of $g$. By [lem-weil-pbc-spec-localization-away-basic-open], $\operatorname{Spec}T$ is the open $D_E(\bar g)$ containing $Q$. The open-subspace local-dimension theorem therefore gives $\dim_{Q_g}\operatorname{Spec}T=n$ at the corresponding point $Q_g$. In particular $T\ne0$.

### Former step 4

### Former step 5

### Former step 6

Among the finitely many nonzero coefficients $a_I$ choose $b=a_J$ such that $a_I/b\in A$ for every $I$. Here is the finite induction that gives this choice without a rank or discreteness assumption: start with one coefficient $b$; for the next nonzero coefficient $a$, either $a/b\in A$ and keep $b$, or $b/a\in A$ and replace $b$ by $a$. Previously admitted coefficients $c$ then satisfy $c/a=(c/b)(b/a)\in A$. Thus $h=b^{-1}f$ has all coefficients in $A$ and its $J$-coefficient is $1$. Its image in $H$ is zero. Since $B$ is a domain containing $A$, [lem-weil-pbc-generic-fibre-localization-domain-irreducible] applied to $A\to B$ shows that $B\to H$ is the localization of $B$ at the nonzero elements of $A$; this map is injective: an element killed in the localization is killed by some nonzero $s\in A$, hence was zero. Therefore $\alpha(h)=0$ in $B$.

### Former step 8

The prime $Q$ was arbitrary, so every point of $\operatorname{Spec}E$ has local dimension $d$. The dimension-as-supremum-of-local-dimensions theorem [lem-weil-pbc-dimension-supremum-local-dimensions] gives global dimension $d$; this is a nonempty supremum because $E\ne0$: [def-weil-pbc-krull-dimension-ring] states that absence of prime ideals is equivalent to being the zero ring. The spectrum/ring comparison then gives $\dim E=d=\dim F$. For equidimensionality, [lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-components-minimal-primes] writes each component as $V(\mathfrak p)$ for a minimal prime $\mathfrak p$ of $E$. At the point $\mathfrak p$, this is the unique component containing it: a component $V(\mathfrak r)$ through it has $\mathfrak r\subseteq\mathfrak p$, hence $\mathfrak r=\mathfrak p$ by minimality. The dimension-at-point/components theorem now identifies the dimension of that component with $\dim_{\mathfrak p}\operatorname{Spec}E=d$. This proves every clause.
