### lem-sfb-separable-cover-finite-coordinates

Former step 1:

Write $\Omega=k^s$. By [lem-weil-pbc-separable-closure-exists-galois], $\Omega/k$ is Galois. By [lem-weil-pbc-finite-set-in-finite-galois-subextension], every finite subset lies in a finite Galois intermediate field $E$. Thus these fields form a nonempty directed set (include $k$, and apply the same assertion to finite generating sets of two fields), and their union is $\Omega$. The finite separable stages are finite etale by [lem-kun-algebraic-separable-smooth]. Hence $k\to\Omega$ is ind-etale, so weakly etale by [lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability]. It is faithfully flat by [def-pd-separable-closure]. Both spectra are affine and the unique point is covered, so the singleton $v$ is a covering by the affine-open criterion of [def-weil-pbc-proetale-site].

Former step 2:

For a finite Galois $E/k$ inside $\Omega$, put $Q_E=\operatorname{Gal}(E/k)$. The algebra isomorphism in [lem-exc-finite-galois-tensor-decomposition](1), base changed along $E\hookrightarrow\Omega$ in its first factor, gives $\Omega\otimes_k E\cong\Omega^{Q_E}$, with $a\otimes b\mapsto(a\sigma(b))_\sigma$. Here base change and the finite product identification are direct consequences of the tensor universal property [lem-weil-pbc-algebra-tensor-pushout-universal-property]: apply a map to each coordinate and use the finitely many coordinate idempotents for its inverse. Iterating gives $\Omega\otimes_k E^{\otimes_k q}\cong\Omega^{Q_E^q}$. Reindex by successive increments: its coordinate at $(g_1,\ldots,g_q)$ is
$$a_0\otimes\cdots\otimes a_q\longmapsto a_0\,g_1(a_1)\,(g_1g_2)(a_2)\cdots(g_1\cdots g_q)(a_q).$$
For $q=0$ this is the identity of $\Omega$. Restriction from $E'$ to $E$ makes these maps compatible, as is seen by this formula on each pure tensor.

### lem-sfb-galois-limit-nerve-faces

Former step 3:

Pass to the directed union of the fields $E$ simultaneously in all $q$ variable factors. Tensor commutes with these colimits by [lem-weil-pbc-tensor-commutes-with-colimits]; the diagonal system suffices since any finite list of stages has a common upper bound. Consequently the coordinate ring of the $(q+1)$-fold fibre product is $\varinjlim_E\Omega^{Q_E^q}$. The fibre product of affine spectra over $\operatorname{Spec}k$ is the spectrum of the tensor product by [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra] (with the projections of [def-pc-fibre-product]), applied $q$ times, and the multiplication of the tensor ring is that of [lem-weil-pbc-algebra-tensor-pushout-universal-property]. Taking spectra converts this colimit into the inverse limit of the finite disjoint unions by [lem-weil-pbc-spec-colimit-limit]. Finally [lem-exc-galois-krull-finite-limit] identifies $G_k$ with $\varprojlim_E Q_E$ as a topological group, so this is precisely $(\operatorname{Spec}\Omega)[G_k^q]$ in the sense of [def-sfb-profinite-test-objects]. One can use these quotients to compute that object: a continuous map from $G_k^q$ to a finite discrete set is constant on a finite cover by cylinder neighbourhoods; a common upper bound of their finitely many stages makes it factor through $Q_E^q$. Thus these finite quotients refine every finite continuous quotient, as required for the inverse-limit description.

### lem-sfb-separably-closed-lisse-split-trivialization

Former step 6:

The one-point scheme $\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$ is w-contractible in [det-pe-wlocal-strict-contractible]: its sole point is closed, its component space is the one-point extremally disconnected profinite space, and its local ring is $\Omega$. This field is strictly henselian under [def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology]: its maximal ideal is zero, so the idempotent lifting condition for every finite algebra is the identity, and its residue field $\Omega$ is separably closed. Apply [det-pe-wcb-clopen-partition] to a lisse trivializing covering from [def-sfb-lisse-coefficient-module]. A finite clopen partition of this nonempty one-point scheme has exactly one nonempty member, namely the whole scheme. The supplied arrow into one member of the covering is therefore a section $s$ of that covering arrow $u$.

Former step 7:

Let $u:U\to\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$ be the covering member of step 6 with its section $s$. Lisseness gives an isomorphism of $R_A|_{U}$-modules $L|_U\cong(R_A|_U)^{\oplus n}$ on the localization at $U$ in the sense of [def-pur-localized-ringed-site]; module objects are compared with sectionwise module sheaves, with the same sections and restrictions, by [def-weil-pbc-internal-abelian-sheaf-module-equivalence]. The section $s$ is a morphism of $(\operatorname{Spec}\Omega)_{\mathrm{proet}}$, since $u\circ s=1$. By the relocalization clause of [def-pur-localized-ringed-site], restricting along $s$ carries $L|_U$ to $L|_{\operatorname{Spec}\Omega}$ and $R_A|_U$ to $R_A|_{\operatorname{Spec}\Omega}$, sectionwise the identity, and carries the isomorphism to an isomorphism. Since $\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$ is the final object of its own site, the localization at it is the whole site and these restrictions are $L$ and $R_A$. Finite direct sums of sheaves of modules are computed sectionwise, so restriction keeps them. Therefore $L\cong R_{A,\Omega}^{\oplus n}$, including $n=0$, with no condition on $A$ beyond those in the statement.

### lem-sfb-ringed-site-free-matrix-scalar-extension

Former step 8:

Compute maps using the sectionwise module category equivalent to internal modules by [def-weil-pbc-internal-abelian-sheaf-module-equivalence]; the size and sheafification conventions are those of [def-weil-pbc-proetale-site]. A map $R_A^{\oplus n}\to R_A^{\oplus m}$ is determined by the images of its $n$ global standard basis sections. Its image on any object sends $(a_j)_j$ to $\sum_j a_j\,s_j|_U$, by linearity and restriction compatibility. Conversely this formula defines a module morphism for every tuple of global sections $s_j\in\Gamma(\Omega,R_A^{\oplus m})$. Finite sums of module sheaves are also finite products, sectionwise, so step 5 at $q=0$ identifies this group with $(A^m)^n=M_{m\times n}(A)$. Composition is matrix multiplication by the same formula. If $n=0$ there are no columns; if $m=0$ every column is zero. In either case both sides are singleton zero groups.

Dependencies: def-weil-pbc-internal-abelian-sheaf-module-equivalence, def-weil-pbc-proetale-site.
Source: literature/hemo-richarz-scholbach-constructible-sheaves_arXiv-2305.18131.pdf — Example 3.1; elementary finite-free module calculation using the section-action equivalence.

Former step 10:

For the ordinary internal extension of scalars of [sk-def-sfb-change-of-scalars], the balanced map $R_B\otimes_{R_A}R_A\to R_B$ sends $b\otimes a$ to $b\varphi(a)$ and has inverse $b\mapsto b\otimes1$; the two compositions are identities by the balancing and unit relations. The same formulas on $n$ summands give $R_B\otimes_{R_A}R_A^{\oplus n}\cong R_B^{\oplus n}$ (the extension is a left adjoint and preserves finite sums). A matrix $T=(t_{ij})$ sends $1\otimes e_j$ to $\sum_i\varphi(t_{ij})e_i$, hence extends to the entrywise matrix $\varphi(T)$. On a Cech object the coefficient-unit morphism is exactly the ring map from step 9. These are sheaf tensor identities, established by the coequalizer universal property; they require no formula identifying tensor products of sections with sections of tensor products. For $n=0$ the extension preserves the zero object. No limit of Cauchy sequences, completion, or coefficient-algebraic-closure argument occurs in any step.

Dependencies: sk-def-sfb-change-of-scalars.
Source: literature/hemo-richarz-scholbach-constructible-sheaves_arXiv-2305.18131.pdf — Example 3.1 and the ordinary internal coequalizer definition of scalar extension; agent explicit balanced-map calculation.
