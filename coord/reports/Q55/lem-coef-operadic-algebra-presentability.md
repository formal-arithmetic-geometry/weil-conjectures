## Offline account of the mathematical inputs

This section paraphrases the retained sources and spells out the agent arguments. Each input has its own complete statement in a delivered node; these paragraphs do not replace any step's node citations.

**Small operad base.** GH Section 3.2 (pp.606–608) constructs the categorical pattern on Delta-op which marks inert arrows and requires the inert Segal cones to be relative limits. The resulting model category of marked simplicial sets over that pattern is combinatorial; its fibrant objects are precisely the ordinary nonsymmetric operads with their inert arrows marked. Passing to its underlying infinity category gives a presentable category. Objects here are small marked simplicial sets in the fixed universe: the word “small” is essential. This is the retained base node's assertion, not a proof from the definition of presentability.

**Conditional active-comma criterion.** GH A.2.10 is on printed p.703, not p.696. Pushing a diagram of tuples forward along the active arrow to [1] tensors its entries. A colimit of this tensor diagram which is stable under tensoring on either side is an operadic colimit: its universal property remains a colimit after insertion into an arbitrary surrounding ordered tensor word. In A.4.3–A.4.5 (pp.705–706), these operadic colimits over O_act×_(P_act)(P_act)_{/b}, for each target color b, are exactly the existence criterion for a free P-algebra generated by an O-algebra. Substitution of operations produces the P-structure; the operadic universal property produces its coherences and the mapping-space adjunction. The support node states this conditional criterion for a target which need not be cocomplete or presentable. It includes the empty tensor as the unit. For ordinary operads the zero-fiber equivalence required by the generalized theorem is automatic, since both zero fibers are contractible. In the item, smallness of O and P makes every indexing category small, and presentable monoidality provides the colimits and their tensor stability. Thus step 6 actually verifies the criterion instead of just renaming the unrestricted extension clause.

**Generalized monadicity.** GH Proposition 3.4.5 identifies algebras for the inert/trivial generalized operad with Fun(M_[1],V). The trivial inclusion is unchanged over [0], and the Kan hypothesis on M_[0] is exactly the hypothesis used in A.4.6 for its free extension. Restriction to colors detects equivalences: the Segal condition reduces every object over [n] to its inert components. Sifted colimits of algebras are computed on colors because the indexing shape is sifted and a finite tensor word commutes with those colimits; the needed comparison from the diagonal indexing category is cofinal. This is the content of A.5.4 (p.709). The coherent free/forgetful adjunction is monadic as in A.5.6 (pp.709–710). The new input records the monadic comparison and sifted-colimit preservation, not fixed-operad presentability; the target V need only be monoidal and compatible with small colimits.

**General coherent-monadic presentability.** GH A.5.8–A.5.9 (p.710) supply the general mechanism. A monadic algebra has the simplicial free bar resolution associated with the adjunction. Finite coproducts of free objects are free on the corresponding coproduct of generators. Taking the geometric realization of the degreewise coproduct of free resolutions gives finite coproducts of arbitrary algebras. Sifted colimits together with finite coproducts give all small colimits. If C is presentable, choose a sufficiently large regular kappa making it kappa-presentable. Preservation of sifted colimits by U includes kappa-filtered colimits. By adjunction, F carries kappa-compact generators to kappa-compact objects. Free resolutions express arbitrary objects as colimits of these, and closure under kappa-small colimits gives a small kappa-compact generating subcategory. This proves presentability without imposing an artificial common rank. The other clause is the general small-functor-category presentability theorem Fun(K,V), with K small; its HTT source remains WANTED. It is needed here because M_[1] need not be a discrete set or a space. Step 2 applies both clauses with K=M_[1].

**Marked sections and limits, independently of symmetry.** Pull back p:E→S to T. For a J-diagram of sections, take its limit at each vertex t in the fiber E_g(t). An arrow a:t→t' supplies maps from g(a)_! of this candidate limit into every value at t'; these come by composing g(a)_! of the limit projections with the diagram's structure arrows. The limit at t' gives a map from g(a)_! of the candidate at t into that limit. The universal property of these relative limits supplies these maps coherently on simplices of T; this is the relative-limit/right Kan extension construction, not a choice of unrelated arrows. Testing a cone of sections against another section reduces its mapping-space universal property to the fiberwise ones, so these pointwise limits are limits of all sections. If a is marked, every structure arrow in the diagram is cocartesian. Its comparison on the limiting values is then an equivalence because g(a)_! preserves J-limits. Hence the new section again carries a to a cocartesian edge. The full subcategory of marked sections is closed under these limits, and evaluation at every vertex detects them. This argument uses an arbitrary cocartesian fibration and does not use Fin-star, symmetric groups or an operad structure on S. It also explains why transports on unmarked arrows need not preserve limits: one only needs the canonical comparison toward the limiting target, not its invertibility. This is the agent-formulated supporting theorem, with HA Section 3.2.2 as comparison and the general relative-limit source still WANTED.

For the item, E=V-tensor and S=Delta-op. The [n]-fiber is V^n. Every inert transport is a projection to the consecutive indicated entries and therefore preserves limits, including the projection to the terminal zero-fiber. A marked section is exactly an algebra. Its inert color components determine its value at every vertex; values over [0] lie in a terminal category. Thus the fiberwise criterion reduces to evaluations on colors, and restriction to the inert subcategory creates the same limits. Precomposition of algebras carries a limiting color diagram to a limiting color diagram, even when colors change. This proves the fixed generalized-operad limit and restriction clauses directly.

**Cocartesian lifts from adjunctions.** The Cartesian unstraightening identifies the mapping space over a base map v with the fiber mapping space into v* of the target. For the unit A→u*u_!A, the canonical comparison into the homotopy pullback of the cocartesian mapping square is a map of spaces over Map_Opd(P,Q). Its map on fibers over v is exactly the adjunction equivalence Map_AlgP(u_!A,v*C)≃Map_AlgO(A,u*v*C). The existing fiber-detection lemma applies over the identity of this base, and the existing horn/mapping-square criterion proves that the unit edge is cocartesian. No hypothesis that precomposition by u is an equivalence of base mapping spaces is used.

**Tensor adjunction and continuity.** GH Corollary 3.2.6, Remark 3.2.12 and the proof of Lemma 3.6.8 (pp.608–610,619–620) use the categorical-pattern product as a left Quillen bifunctor. Its derived tensor C-odot-O preserves colimits separately, and its right adjoint is Alg_O(Q). The literal product C×O need not satisfy the operad Segal condition; the support node specifies marked product followed by operadic fibrant replacement. Markings are equivalences in C paired with inert edges in O. The tensor-adjunction equivalence tested on all C shows that Alg_(colim O_j)(V) is the limit of Alg_Oj(V), with index J-op. The proof uses the existing Yoneda/mapping-space limit and colimit criterion, not a bare assertion that restriction is continuous. Nested universes let a small operad be tested against the generally large operad V-tensor.

**Total presentability and projection accessibility.** GHN v3 Definition 10.1 and Theorem 10.3 (pp.29–32) concern a Cartesian fibration over a presentable base, with presentable fibers and right-adjoint restrictions, whose classifying functor takes sufficiently filtered colimits in the base to limits of fibers. The proof first obtains colimits in the total category using the left-adjoint transports. To prove accessibility it restricts to a small compact-generating subcategory of the base, chooses a common larger rank for its fibers and transports, and realizes the extended total category as an accessible subcategory of a presheaf category satisfying a small set of limit conditions. Restriction of presheaves is accessible. Pulling this construction back along the accessible presentation of the base proves both accessibility of the total category and of its projection; together with colimits this proves presentability. The node explicitly states the continuity hypothesis as G(colim_I b)≃lim_(I-op)G(b_i), avoiding ambiguity in “filtered limits” for a functor with opposite-domain variance. It does not assume the whole presentable base is small. Step 9 supplies the four hypotheses from steps 5, 2, 6 and 8 respectively. This is a general fibration theorem, not a renamed operadic total-presentability clause.
