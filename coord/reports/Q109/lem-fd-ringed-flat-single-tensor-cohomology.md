Step 1 uses exactly the signed direct-sum formula in `def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves` and additivity in each variable in `lem-pc-sheaf-modules-tensor-closed`. Projection and inclusion of the (n,0) summand are inverse, and the second differential is zero. This is elementary for every integer n, even with C unbounded: only one summand survives. The explicit maps f^n tensor u show this particular identification is simultaneous in both variables.

Step 2 uses the abelian assertion of `def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category`, exactness from `def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules`, and the full statement of `lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-complex-cohomology`. That statement gives both the isomorphism and its compatibility with cycle inclusions and cohomology projections. It therefore supplies precisely the canonical comparison needed later, rather than an arbitrary abstract isomorphism. It is the non-elementary cohomology input and is marked accordingly.

Step 3 does not infer coefficient naturality from a statement which mentions only complex naturality. It uses that statement's canonical cycle-quotient characterization to prove coefficient naturality. Tensor bifunctoriality makes 1 tensor u a natural transformation. Its cycle square is fixed by cancellation of a monic cycle inclusion. After precomposition with the epic cycle projection, the required cohomology equation is exactly naturality of 1 tensor u for Z to H^i(C). Cancellation proves the equation. This is a direct kernel/cokernel argument, checked also against the Mathlib natural-transformation identity; no new non-elementary lemma is concealed. In particular u need not be invertible or preserve an additional structure.

Step 4 applies the already proved cohomology formula to an arbitrary acyclic A. Vanishing in all integer degrees is precisely the K-flat definition cited there. The zero sheaf is flat because its tensor functor is zero and exact, and the degree-zero term is the given flat L. Thus the flat-term hypothesis of the derived-tensor descent node is verified before Step 5. The quasi-isomorphism assertion uses the definition through the maps H^i(f), not a bounded spectral sequence.

Parts (2) and (3) of the Mathlib leaf `lem-weil-pbc-localization-universal-property` give respectively existence/invertibility of the descended transformation and equality of the two coefficient-naturality composites.
