## Original returned proof (verbatim, preserved for the two future workers)

Old step 1 (assigned above):

By [def-pc-perfect-ring-complex], choose a bounded cochain complex $P$ of finitely generated projective $B$-modules, zero outside $[a,b]$, and an isomorphism $QP\cong C$. To apply the ringed-site canonical-map statement below, use the one-object site with only its identity morphism and bottom topology, as in [def-pc-perfect-rhom-base-change-map] for $B\xrightarrow{1}B$. A module sheaf on this site is precisely its single $B$-module of sections, and a morphism is precisely a linear map. Evaluation at that object and the constant one-object construction are inverse functors; their formulas identify kernels, tensor products and Hom with the ordinary module operations. They consequently identify complexes, quasi-isomorphisms, their localizations and the derived Hom defined using K-injectives. Write $H$ for the product total Hom complex [def-sc-module-total-hom-complex].

Old step 2 (assigned above):

Put $E=H(P,B[0])$. In degree $r$ the product defining $H$ has only one possibly nonzero factor, namely $E^r=\operatorname{Hom}_B(P^{-r},B)$; projection to it and insertion of zero in all other coordinates are mutually inverse. Its differential is $d_E^r(\phi)=(-1)^{r+1}\phi d_P^{-r-1}$, whose square is zero since $d_P^2=0$. Thus $E$ vanishes outside $[-b,-a]$. By [lem-sf-finite-projective-dual] every $E^r$ is finitely generated projective. Applying [lem-pod-projective-hom-evaluation-model] to $P,B[0]$ identifies $QE$ with $R\operatorname{Hom}_B(QP,B)$, and hence with $R\operatorname{Hom}_B(C,B)$. The definition [def-pc-perfect-ring-complex] now proves the first conclusion.

Old step 3 (assigned above):

Check the ordinary-representative hypotheses of [lem-sfb-ringed-site-duality-maps]. Both $P$ and $E$ are bounded above and projective termwise, so [lem-pod-projective-hom-evaluation-model] applies to each, with arbitrary target. In particular the two maps
$$H(P,B[0])\longrightarrow H(P,I(B[0])),\qquad H(E,B[0])\longrightarrow H(E,I(B[0]))$$
are quasi-isomorphisms. Indeed for either first complex $T$, naturality of the canonical comparison identifies the localization of $H(T,B[0]\to I(B[0]))$ with $R\operatorname{Hom}_B(QT,Q(B[0]\to I(B[0])))$, conjugated by those comparison isomorphisms. The latter is invertible because the resolution map is a quasi-isomorphism, which localization inverts. A complex map whose localization is invertible is a quasi-isomorphism, as its induced cohomology maps are then invertible; this is the cohomology factorization in [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]. Here $I$ is the fixed K-injective resolution of [def-pur-derived-internal-hom], and its Hom complex is exactly the specified derived Hom. Thus $QH(E,B[0])$ represents the double derived dual, with the particular representative identifications required by the canonical-map node. That node's bidual clause requires no K-flatness assumption.

Old step 4 (assigned above):

Under Step 3's comparisons, [lem-sfb-ringed-site-duality-maps] represents the canonical bidual map by
$$\delta_P:P\longrightarrow H(E,B[0]),\qquad x\longmapsto(\phi\longmapsto(-1)^{p|\phi|}\phi(x)),\quad x\in P^p.$$
Only $|\phi|=-p$ contributes, so its degree-$p$ component is $(-1)^p$ times ordinary evaluation $P^p\to(P^p)^{**}$, an isomorphism by [lem-sf-finite-projective-dual]. The sign is necessary for the chain-map equation: for $\phi\in E^{-p-1}$ one has $d_E\phi=(-1)^p\phi d_P$, and hence
$$(d_{H(E,B)}\delta_P(x))(\phi)=-(-1)^p\delta_P(x)(d_E\phi)=(-1)^{p+1}\phi(d_Px)=\delta_P(d_Px)(\phi).$$
All other Hom components are zero. Multiplying this chain-map equation by the degreewise inverses shows that they form an inverse chain map. Therefore $\delta_P$ is a chain isomorphism, and its localization is invertible.

Old step 5 (assigned above):

The canonical bidual map of [lem-sfb-ringed-site-duality-maps] is natural in its input, and the representative comparisons of [lem-pod-projective-hom-evaluation-model] are natural in both variables. For $e:QP\xrightarrow{\sim}C$ their naturality square is $b_C e=R\operatorname{Hom}_B(R\operatorname{Hom}_B(e,B),B)b_{QP}$. The right factor $b_{QP}$ is invertible by Step 4, and the other two factors are images of isomorphisms under functors. Solving this equality for $b_C$ proves that the actual canonical evaluation $C\to R\operatorname{Hom}_B(R\operatorname{Hom}_B(C,B),B)$ is invertible. The zero ring, zero complex, and an odd shift of a finite projective module obey the same formulas: in odd degree the component evaluation has the unit sign $-1$, rather than the unsigned evaluation.
