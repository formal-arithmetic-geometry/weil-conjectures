### Old step 1, assigned to lem-sfb-coefficient-diagram-regular-scalars

Write $F=h^{-1}$ for inverse image on abelian sheaves, $H=h^*$ for module pullback, and $\alpha=\alpha_h$. For $a\in R$, let $t_a:\Lambda\to\Lambda$ be multiplication by the section $a$ in the condensed ring: explicitly $t_a=\mu_\Lambda\circ(a\otimes1)\circ\lambda_\Lambda^{-1}$, with $a:\mathbf1\to\Lambda$. Put $t_{a,W}:\Lambda_W\to\Lambda_W$ for multiplication by its canonical scalar image, for $W=U,V$. The transported multiplication and unit in the pulled-back coefficient ring give $p_W^*(t_a)=t_{a,W}$: substitute the displayed formula into the strong monoidal comparison. The comparison $c:Fp_U^*\cong p_V^*$ is natural in all condensed abelian sheaves, and $c_\Lambda=\alpha$. Its naturality square for $t_a$ therefore gives $\alpha\circ F(t_{a,U})=t_{a,V}\circ\alpha$. This uses the canonical scalar maps, rather than arbitrary sections of $\Lambda_U$.

### Old step 2, assigned to lem-sfb-coefficient-diagram-regular-scalars

The formula for transport of module actions identifies $\operatorname{mapMod}(F)(\Lambda_U)$ with the regular module over $T=F\Lambda_U$: both actions are $F(\mu_{\Lambda_U})$ preceded by the tensor comparison. Hence $H\Lambda_U=S\otimes_T T$, where $S=\Lambda_V$ has right $T$-action $b\cdot x=b\alpha(x)$. Apply the mixed-bimodule right unitor with $P=S$, left ring $S$, and right ring $T$. It gives the canonical regular-module isomorphism $e_1:S\otimes_T T\cong S$ with $e_1\circ q=\mu_S\circ(1\otimes\alpha)$ and inverse $b\mapsto q(b\otimes1_T)$. The ring-homomorphism property of $\alpha$ gives the commuting actions required here. All tensor and coequalizer hypotheses hold for abelian sheaves: their sheaf tensor product preserves colimits in both variables, and the ambient abelian category has coequalizers. The expression $q(b\otimes x)$ denotes the map from $S\otimes T$ into the balancing coequalizer, not a claim that sections of a sheafified tensor product are all simple tensors.

### Old step 3, assigned to lem-sfb-finite-colimit-matrix-cokernels

By the module adjunction $H\dashv h_*$ and preservation of colimits by a left adjoint, $H$ preserves the initial object, each finite coproduct, and every coequalizer. Only finite small indexing categories are needed. In the abelian module categories the initial object is zero, so $H(0)=0$ up to its unique isomorphism, and $H$ preserves zero morphisms because they factor through zero. Define $e_r:H(\Lambda_U^r)\cong\Lambda_V^r$ by the coproduct comparison and $r$ copies of $e_1$. It is characterized by $e_r H(\iota_{j,U})=\iota_{j,V}e_1$. For each $i$, one also has $\pi_{i,V}e_r=e_1H(\pi_{i,U})$: precompose with every $H(\iota_{j,U})$ and use $\pi_i\iota_j=\delta_{ij}$ and preservation of zero. Equality follows from the coproduct universal property. This includes $r=0$, where $e_0$ is the unique isomorphism of zero objects.

### Old step 4, assigned to lem-sfb-coefficient-diagram-regular-scalars

The definition of extension of scalars sends a map $f$ to the map induced by $1_S\otimes F(f)$. For $f=t_{a,U}$, precompose both $e_1H(t_{a,U})$ and $t_{a,V}e_1$ with the balancing coequalizer map $q:S\otimes T\to S\otimes_T T$. The first composite is $\mu_S\circ(1\otimes(\alpha F(t_{a,U})))$; step 1 makes it $\mu_S\circ(1\otimes(t_{a,V}\alpha))$. This equals $t_{a,V}\circ\mu_S\circ(1\otimes\alpha)$ by associativity and commutativity of multiplication in $S$. Thus the second composite agrees. Uniqueness in the coequalizer universal property gives $e_1H(t_{a,U})=t_{a,V}e_1$.

### Old step 6, assigned to lem-sfb-finite-colimit-matrix-cokernels

Let $q_U:\Lambda_U^n\to Q_U$ and $q_V:\Lambda_V^n\to Q_V$ be the cokernel projections. A cokernel is the coequalizer of its map and zero. By step 3, $H(q_U)$ is therefore a cokernel of $H(A_U)$. The matrix square of step 5 gives $q_Ve_nH(A_U)=0$ and $H(q_U)e_n^{-1}A_V=0$. The two cokernel universal properties give unique maps $u:H(Q_U)\to Q_V$ and $v:Q_V\to H(Q_U)$ satisfying $uH(q_U)=q_Ve_n$ and $vq_V=H(q_U)e_n^{-1}$. Thus $u$ is the required isomorphism, and its defining equation is precisely compatibility with the cokernel projections.
