## What was wrong and what changed

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the original phrase "invertible locally constant of rank one" did not distinguish a finite locally constant sheaf from a line over completed integral coefficients or its rational/common-stage image. This is the same coefficient ambiguity already repaired in `prop-weil-twist-preserves`, whose precise statement is now used. For a concrete test, take $S=\operatorname{Spec}\overline{\mathbf F}_q$ and $E=\mathbf Q_\ell$. On the pro-etale object given by the inverse limit of the finite disjoint unions indexed by $\mathbf Z/\ell^j$, the completed coefficient unit has the compatible residue-coordinate section, hence the continuous identity function $\mathbf Z_\ell\to\mathbf Z_\ell$. That function is not locally constant. The discrete constant coefficient sheaf only gives locally constant functions on this object. Thus calling the completed unit or its rank-one twist a discrete constant coefficient sheaf would change the object. The corrected statement retains the source's line assertion in its intended coefficient-dependent meaning: finite locally constant rank one, completed integral locally free rank one, and invertible images in the two field categories. No hypothesis on $X$, $K$ or $M$ is added and no interchange conclusion is weakened.

The first server dry run rejected the inherited opening "In the setting of" under B2. I repaired it by explicitly introducing the two primes, finite field and its closure, coefficient extension and residue exponent, the six-functor datum, its separated finite-type scheme $X$, and the coefficient-category domains of $K$ and $M$. The word perfect has the same local bounded finite-projective meaning as the source. In particular $K$ is the perfect object in the displayed finite-Tor coefficient category of the original datum; this is not an additional geometric assumption. The residue exponent is named $N$ to avoid shadowing the shift.

The shift parameter is now $r\in\mathbf Z$, separate from the fixed positive finite-coefficient exponent in the datum. The map written in the statement goes in the opposite direction from the evaluation-transpose map; the statement now explicitly names it as the inverse, as the current main-20261015 unit proof does. Step 3 proves that this inverse exists and identifies it with the perfect-duality composite, rather than asserting merely the existence of some isomorphism.
