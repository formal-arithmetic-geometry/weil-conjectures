## Replacement calculation and proof of the finite helper

The id lem-per-rdp-double-line-tail-persistence is retained, but its statement has been replaced by a finite chart calculation, with no infinite tower hypothesis or eventual-parameter conclusion. It assumes no rationality or Gorenstein property. Its three parts are local algebra, usable without a resolution theorem. It is not a relabelled termination assertion. The following is my argument for that helper; its node itself remains statement-only as required for a new unsplit node.

For part (1), start with gr S=k[U,V,W]/(W^2) and an actual equality w^2=J in m^3. On the u-chart put t=v/u-lambda and z=w/u, using any lift of lambda. Divide the equality by u^2 in the common fraction field. It becomes z^2=uP. The local maximal ideal is (u,t,z). Since the successor still has embedding dimension three, P has zero residue: otherwise this equality would eliminate u in the cotangent space. The quadratic relation is therefore Z^2-U times the linear part of P. Its restriction at U=0 is Z^2. If it is a scalar square, normalizing the coefficient of Z^2 shows it is (Z+dU)^2 for some d in the actual residue field; there is no T term in that linear square. Choose a lift delta of d and set omega=z+delta u. The exact equality is still omega^2=uG. Its initial quadratic is W_omega^2, so uG is in the third power of the maximal ideal. Multiplication by U is injective in degrees zero and one of k[U,T,W_omega]/(W_omega^2). Taking initial terms of G consequently gives G in the second power. This establishes the claimed exact divisibility, not merely congruence modulo degree four.

For part (2), expand F of the relation w^2=uF in degree-two monomials with coefficients in S. The restriction at W=0 has coefficients a,b,c, hence H=U(aV^2+bUV+cU^2). In the u-chart at the multiple zero [1:lambda], substitute v=u(lambda+t), w=uz. Dividing by u^2 gives z^2=u times the substituted quadratic expansion of F. Every old coefficient with zero residue belongs to m S', which is contained in u S'. Thus its contribution has an extra u. The term in t^2 without an extra u has coefficient a. Since a!=0, the multiple zero of the degree-two polynomial a t^2+b t+c is a double zero, and recentering at lambda eliminates its constant and linear terms in the residue field. As in part (1), the tangent square is (Z+dU)^2, so a shift z to omega=z+delta u gives omega^2=uF' with F' in m'^2. This shift can change the U^3 and U^2 T coefficients of the binary cubic, but cannot change U T^2: the shifted terms either contain omega or at least two factors u. Thus H'=U(aT^2+b'UT+c'U^2). The exceptional ideal in this chart is (u).

For part (3), a=0. Expand F in quadratic monomials, retaining coefficients with zero residue. In the v-chart substitute u=vr, w=vz, and put G=F/v^2 in the chart algebra. The exact identity is z^2=vrG. At [0:1] we have r=z=v=0. The residue of G is a=0, so G is in m'. Its linear part is b r+d z+e v for some d,e in k: the coefficient of r is b, while zero-residue old coefficients contribute multiples of v. Hence z^2 is in m'^3, the unique tangent quadratic is Z^2, and its binary cubic is VR(bR+eV). Set L=G. If b!=0, v is a suitable distinguished coordinate and the VR^2 coefficient is b. If b=0 and the next binary cubic is nonzero, e!=0; use r instead, with the exact equality z^2=r(vL). In either choice the parenthesized factor belongs to m'^2. This retains all cubic terms created by higher old coefficients.

These arguments work in characteristic 2 and mixed characteristic with residue characteristic 2. They use the assumption that the tangent quadratic already is a square in the actual residue field, to find d; they never infer squareness after a field extension and never divide by 2. The exact identity (z+delta u)^2=z^2+2 delta u z+delta^2 u^2 is used as written. The extra mixed-characteristic terms change only coefficients containing u, and the argument preserving U T^2 still applies. In part (3), Z^2 is obtained without any shift at all. No branch-curve theorem valid only when 2 is invertible is being applied.
