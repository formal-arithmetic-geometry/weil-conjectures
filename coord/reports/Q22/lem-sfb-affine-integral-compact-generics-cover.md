## Argument for the new retraction lemma

Let R be reduced and Ann(a)=e_a R with e_a idempotent. Every prime p contains a minimal prime q (the usual Zorn argument, intersecting descending chains of primes). If q is minimal, R_q is a reduced local ring with only the prime qR_q; its nilradical is zero, hence qR_q=0. Consequently a in q implies a/1=0, so some s outside q satisfies sa=0. As s is a multiple of e_a, e_a is outside q. Conversely ae_a=0 implies that a and e_a cannot both be outside q. Thus for a minimal q, a is in q if and only if e_a is outside q.

If distinct minimal primes q,q' lay below the same p, choose a in q but outside q'. Then e_a is outside q and in q', so 1-e_a belongs to q. Both e_a and 1-e_a would belong to p, a contradiction. There is therefore a unique minimal q=r(p) below p. If e is any idempotent, e belongs to p if and only if it belongs to q: one implication is q contained in p, and for the other, e outside q implies 1-e in q contained in p, which excludes e from p. Hence

`a outside r(p) iff e_a in r(p) iff e_a in p iff 1-e_a outside p`.

This proves the inverse-image formula in the statement. The D(a) form a basis of the spectrum topology, and D(a) intersect G form a basis for G, so that formula proves continuity. If p is minimal, uniqueness makes r(p)=p. The points in G are precisely generic points of the irreducible components: the closure of a prime q is V(q), and the irreducible components of an affine spectrum are the V(q) for minimal primes. This is the standard affine dictionary described at the start of Hochster section 4, p.775. These arguments also handle the empty spectrum.

This lemma is a genuine intermediate result about a general class of rings. It constructs a specified continuous retraction and an inverse-image formula, whereas the item constructs an integral covering of a possibly nonreduced input affine scheme. It is not the item or a renamed existence assertion.
