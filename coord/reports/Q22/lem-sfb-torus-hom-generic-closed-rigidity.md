## Sources and mathematical checks for the two new inputs

For lem-sfb-split-local-special-kernel-lifting I read Conrad's Remark B.1.2, describing subgroups of a split diagonalizable group over a field as quotients of the character group, and Corollary B.2.7, which applies to an arbitrary target group scheme, not merely an affine or smooth target. This distinction matters because the item's target can have a proper abelian part. I also read Stacks algebra.tex, Tag 00IP, label lemma-intersect-powers-ideal-module-zero, and its proof, for the following local consequence. This consequence is formulated by the agent and is not claimed to be the verbatim statement of a printed theorem.

Here is a verification of that consequence, for the next worker. The special kernel is closed in the split torus because the target is separated. Its field-level diagonalizable description supplies a subgroup L of Z^r with kernel D_k(Z^r/L), including the specific inclusion into the original torus. Put M=Z^r/L and H=D_R(M), with the explicit group-algebra quotient inclusion. The restriction u|H is trivial modulo m. For every n≥1, apply Corollary B.2.7 over R/m^n with its nilpotent ideal m/m^n: u|H is trivial modulo m^n. Since the target is separated, the equalizer with the identity-valued map is a closed subscheme of H; write its ideal as I⊂R[M]. Its full pullback over R/m^n means I⊂m^n R[M]. A member of R[M] has finitely many coefficients. Each of those coefficients lies in every m^n and is zero by Tag 00IP applied to the finite R-module R. Hence I=0 and u|H is trivial over R. This uses Krull intersection coefficientwise on R, not on the generally infinite R-module R[M]. Thus the new node is true without a reducedness, completeness, excellence, smoothness-of-target or invertibility-of-torsion assumption. No SGA text was used.

For lem-sfb-relative-torus-monomorphism-closed I read the statement and direct proof of Conrad Theorem 5.3.5. A torus is a special case of its reductive source; the target there is explicitly separated and of finite presentation and need not be affine or smooth. The proof reduces properness to DVRs, passes to the schematic closure with affine generic fibre, invokes Raynaud affineness (Conrad offers Prasad–Yu Proposition 3.1 as a non-SGA reference), embeds the resulting affine flat group in GL_n, and uses torus structure there. This theorem remains a genuine unsplit input, not a claim that an arbitrary scheme monomorphism is closed. The source requirement is finite presentation, not merely local finite presentation; Conrad explicitly warns of counterexamples to the latter. We retain finite presentation. The future worker must continue the non-SGA source route, rather than opening the alternate SGA references printed in Conrad.

Small cases tested: r=0 makes both the lifted subgroup and the source torus the identity. Over a field the lifted kernel is precisely the original diagonalizable kernel. The map [n]:G_m→G_m has lifted kernel μ_n, including μ_p in residue characteristic p; this shows why geometric-point injectivity and prime-to-characteristic torsion tests alone would be insufficient. The constant identity embedding has M=0. For a split torus mapping to an abelian scheme, the local kernel lift can be the whole torus; the lemma does not assert generic injectivity. Nonreduced Noetherian local rings are allowed in the lifting node: Krull intersection still holds and the coordinate argument uses no reducedness. For the monic-torus theorem, the identity, diagonal embedding G_m→G_m², and rank-zero identity section satisfy the conclusion; [n] with n>1 fails its monomorphism hypothesis as a group functor even when its geometric-point kernel is invisible. A torus over a non-Noetherian base is allowed in this second node because Conrad's theorem is stated over an arbitrary scheme.
