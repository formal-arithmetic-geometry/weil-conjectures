I corrected the marked-initial-vertex node's source to label its combinatorial assertion as an agent consequence of the actual generators. Its proof is as follows. Induct on (n), first completing the first (n-1) spine edges to the face (Delta^{\{0,\ldots,n-1\}}). From that face and the final edge, for every nonempty subset (S\subseteq\{0,\ldots,n-2\}), in increasing cardinality, attach the simplex on (S\cup\{n-1,n\}) along its inner horn missing vertex (n-1). The face omitting (n) is in the initial face; every other included face removes an element of (S) and has already been attached (the empty subset gives the final edge). The missing face adds (S\cup\{n\}), so these attachments produce the full underlying simplex, with the spine edges marked. These are pushouts of the flat inner-horn generators; pre-existing domain markings remain marked. In increasing interval length, for every (i<j-1), apply the triangle-marking generator to the face ((i,j-1,j)): its two consecutive edges are already marked, and it adds exactly the marking of ((i,j)). Thus the marked spine inclusion is coanodyne. The initial vertex attaches the successive spine edges using the marked (1)-horn generator and then uses this spine inclusion; (n=0) is the identity. Finally the parameter extension is the pushout product of either inclusion with (T^\flat\to(T')^\flat), using the opposite of the existing marked-anodyne pushout-product theorem.
