## Original reviewed proof retained

1. Put $A=\mathcal O_E$, $\pi=\pi_E$, $P_n=A/\pi^n$, $\kappa=P_1$, and $R_T=\mathcal O_{E,T}$ for each scheme $T$. The standing schemes and morphisms are those of [sk-def-sfb-dc-setting]. In particular they are Noetherian, as recorded in [sk-lem-integral-models]. By [det-prop-sfb-qlbar-colimit-x-integral-limit], $R_T=\varprojlim_n(P_n)_T$ canonically as a ring sheaf. Thus the completed coefficient sheaves in [prop-weil-pbc-direct-image-limits] are exactly these pulled-back coefficient sheaves, not the constant sheaves with value $A$. The coefficient datum applies: [lem-weil-l-adic-extension-integers-dvr] makes $A$ a discrete valuation ring, hence Noetherian since every ideal is principal, and [lem-weil-pbc-standard-quotients-perfect] makes each $P_n$ finite and perfect over $A$. Also $|\ell|_E=\ell^{-1}<1$, so $\ell\in\mathfrak m_E=(\pi)$ by [def-weil-l-adic-coefficient-extension]; consequently $\ell\kappa=0$. For the admissible untwisted coefficients $\Lambda=\mathcal O_E$ and $P_n$, [sk-prop-sfb-cartesian-coefficients] (1) gives the canonical ring isomorphism $\alpha_u:u^{-1}\Lambda_T\xrightarrow{\sim}\Lambda_{T'}$ for every morphism $u:T'\to T$. Its final paragraph says that module pullback is exact and canonically naturally isomorphic to the lift of $u^{-1}$, with the $\Lambda_{T'}$-action transported along $\alpha_u^{-1}$. We use this natural identification, including the transported scalar action; we do not use the theorem's Tate-twist or finite-extension freeness assertions. The operations in [sk-def-sfb-module-derived-pushpull] are therefore the ordinary ringed-topos derived operations on these same sheaves; throughout, $u^*=Lu^*$ and all tensors are uncompleted derived tensors.

2. For $L\in D(\operatorname{Mod}(R_T))$ write $L_n=L\otimes^L_{R_T}(P_n)_T$, as an object with its $P_n$-action. If $L\in\Dbc(T,\mathcal O_E)$, [sk-lem-integral-models] (2) says that $L$ is derived complete and $L_1$ is bounded constructible and pulled back from the étale site. Its finite Tor dimension follows from the same clause's formulation using $\Dc$: $\operatorname{Res}L\in\Dc$ has first reduction of finite Tor dimension. The comparison of [det-prop-integral-criterion-constant-coefficients] (3) identifies that reduction with $L_1$ in $D((\kappa)_T)$. Thus $L_1\in\Dctf(T,\kappa)$. Conversely, a derived complete $L$ with bounded constructible classical $L_1$ belongs to $\Dbc(T,\mathcal O_E)$ by [sk-lem-integral-models] (2). By [det-prop-integral-criterion-reductions], the reductions $L_n$ are bounded constructible in one interval independent of $n$, and this is the bounded constructible derived-complete integral category used by the coefficient-reduction tools below. Apply these assertions to $K$ now, and to the other constructible integral inputs below.

3. Apply [prop-weil-pbc-direct-image-limits] with $(A,I)=(\mathcal O_E,(\pi))$ and the morphism $p$. It gives, for every $n\geq1$, the functorial canonical projection-formula comparison
$$\rho_{p,n}:(Rp_*K)\otimes^L_{R_Y}(P_n)_Y\xrightarrow{\sim}Rp_*(K_n),$$
compatible with the quotient transitions, and identifies the completion map with
$$Rp_*K\xrightarrow{\sim}Rp_*R\varprojlim_nK_n\xrightarrow{\sim}R\varprojlim_nRp_*K_n.$$
Here completeness of $K$ is step 2. These comparisons prove derived completeness of $Rp_*K$ in the sense of [sk-def-pe-derived-completion], and $n=1$ gives the reduction isomorphism in clause (1). The finite reduction is represented by the two-term resolution $[R_T\xrightarrow{\pi^n}R_T]$, hence by $\operatorname{cone}(\pi^n:L\to L)$, by [det-pe-derived-nakayama-resolution]. This identifies the coefficient quotient used here with the cone quotient used in the blueprint; the comparison is the specified canonical map supplied by the cited direct-image theorem.

4. Step 2 gives $K_1\in\Dctf(X,\kappa)$, with $\kappa$ finite and killed by $\ell$ as checked in step 1. Apply [det-thm-sfb-proper-base-change-finite] (1): $Rp_*K_1\in\Dctf(Y,\kappa)$, so it is bounded constructible. It is classical by [sk-thm-pe-constructible-comparison], which applies to the discrete Noetherian field $\kappa$ on the qcqs scheme $Y$. In particular it is pulled back from a bounded constructible étale complex. Step 3 identifies this complex with $(Rp_*K)_1$ as an $R_Y$-complex; since step 2 places $K$ in the bounded integral domain, the direct-image reduction comparison of [lem-fix-operation-reduction] makes this an identification in $D((\kappa)_Y)$ of $(Rp_*K)_1$, with its $\kappa$-action, with the $\kappa$-coefficient direct image $Rp_*K_1$ to which the finite theorem was applied. The converse criterion in [sk-lem-integral-models] (2), applied to the complete object $Rp_*K$, now proves $Rp_*K\in\Dbc(Y,\mathcal O_E)$. This completes clause (1). Classicality here uses constructibility; torsion coefficients alone would not imply it.

5. Let $C$ be any derived $\pi$-complete complex on the scheme in question and suppose $C_1=0$. By [det-pe-derived-nakayama-resolution] the coefficient filtration gives a distinguished triangle $C_1\to C_{n+1}\to C_n\to C_1[1]$ for every $n\geq1$. Starting with $C_1=0$, if $C_n=0$, both outside terms vanish and the triangle gives $C_{n+1}=0$. Induction proves $C_n=0$ for every $n$. The canonical completeness isomorphism of [sk-def-pe-derived-completion] gives $C\simeq R\varprojlim_nC_n=0$. This proves clause (2) without a boundedness assumption on $C$.

6. For the cartesian square of clause (3), with $Y'$ a standing scheme and $g$ a morphism of the setting, $p'$ is proper by [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence] (b). Hence $X'$ is again a standing scheme: $p'$ is separated and of finite type, so the composite $X'\to Y'\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is separated and locally of finite type by clause (e) of the same lemma and quasi-compact as a composite of quasi-compact maps, and $g'$, $p'$ are $k$-morphisms. By [sk-lem-integral-models] (3), $g'^*K$ is bounded constructible integral; step 4 for $p'$ gives $Rp'_*g'^*K\in\Dbc(Y',\mathcal O_E)$. Step 4 for $p$ followed by the same pullback assertion gives $g^*Rp_*K\in\Dbc(Y',\mathcal O_E)$. For the projection map, the same lemma's pullback and tensor assertions give $p^*M\in\Dbc(X,\mathcal O_E)$, $K\otimes^Lp^*M\in\Dbc(X,\mathcal O_E)$ and $(Rp_*K)\otimes^LM\in\Dbc(Y,\mathcal O_E)$; step 4 gives $Rp_*(K\otimes^Lp^*M)\in\Dbc(Y,\mathcal O_E)$. All four endpoints are derived complete by [sk-lem-integral-models] (2). In particular no completion of the displayed inverse images or tensors is needed.

7. Write $\beta_K:g^*Rp_*K\to Rp'_*g'^*K$ for the canonical ambient exchange. It is the unit--pullback-comparison--counit composite of [def-weil-pbc-ambient-ringed-exchange]; the coefficient identifications in step 1 make the square a square of ringed topoi. Apply [lem-fix-operation-reduction] on the bounded integral domains checked in step 6. Its comparisons identify the reductions of the endpoints with $g^*Rp_*K_1$ and $Rp'_*g'^*K_1$, respectively (all operations on the latter objects now have coefficient $\kappa$). They preserve the units and counits used to form exchange, as its statement explicitly asserts. The pullback comparison also reduces to the same comparison, since both routes are the reassociation of the inverse-image and scalar-extension tensors for $p\circ g'=g\circ p'$. Thus reducing the defining composite term by term gives
$$ (\beta_K)_1=\eta_{p',\kappa};\ Rp'_{*,\kappa}(\theta_{\square,\kappa});\ Rp'_{*,\kappa}g'^*_{\kappa}(\varepsilon_{p,\kappa}),$$
under those endpoint identifications, where semicolons mean composition in the written order. This is exactly $\beta_{K_1}$, the canonical finite-coefficient exchange, not just some morphism between isomorphic endpoints.

8. The canonical projection map $\alpha_{K,M}:Rp_*K\otimes^LM\to Rp_*(K\otimes^Lp^*M)$ is, by [def-weil-pbc-projection-formula-map], the adjoint of
$$p^*(Rp_*K\otimes^LM)\xrightarrow{t_p}p^*Rp_*K\otimes^Lp^*M\xrightarrow{\varepsilon_K\otimes1}K\otimes^Lp^*M.$$
The reduction comparisons of [lem-fix-operation-reduction] identify its source and target with
$$Rp_*K_1\otimes^L_{\kappa}M_1,\qquad Rp_*(K_1\otimes^L_{\kappa}p^*M_1).$$
Here the tensor comparison is the balancing isomorphism $(U\otimes^L_{R_T}V)\otimes^L_{R_T}\kappa_T\simeq U_1\otimes^L_{\kappa_T}V_1$; inverse image uses the corresponding scalar-extension comparison. The cited lemma preserves tensor associativity, units and counits. Therefore the reduction of the displayed adjunct is $(\varepsilon_{K_1}\otimes1)t_{p,\kappa}$, and reduction of the unit used to take its adjunct is the finite-coefficient unit. The reduced map is exactly $\alpha_{K_1,M_1}$.

9. By step 2, $K_1\in\Dctf(X,\kappa)$ and $M_1\in\Dctf(Y,\kappa)$. All square hypotheses and properness are the ones already checked in step 6, and $\kappa$ is a finite commutative ring killed by $\ell$. Hence [det-thm-sfb-proper-base-change-finite] (2) makes $\beta_{K_1}$ an isomorphism and its clause (3) makes $\alpha_{K_1,M_1}$ an isomorphism. Steps 7 and 8 identify these with the first reductions of the actual integral maps.

10. Take the cone of each integral map. The endpoints are complete by step 6, so the cones are complete by the cone-closure assertion of [det-pe-derived-nakayama-first-reduction]. Derived reduction is exact: using the functorial two-term cone resolution in [det-pe-derived-nakayama-resolution], reduction of a mapping cone is the mapping cone of the reduced map. Step 9 therefore makes the first reduction of each cone zero. Apply the vanishing proved in step 5 to each cone. Both cones vanish, so both integral maps are isomorphisms, proving clause (3).
