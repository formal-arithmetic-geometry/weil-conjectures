## Mathematical details retained for the new unsplit nodes

### Local slice

An inert map from [n] to [1] is exactly one of the n consecutive-interval projections rho_i. Every edge and higher simplex in the fiber A over [1] projects to a degenerate base simplex. Therefore the degreewise simplicial set of arrows from x to A is the disjoint union of the exact lift categories over the different rho_i; the label cannot change on any simplex. The map C to the inert base is the pullback of the operad inner fibration, so these lift categories are quasicategories. The inert lift alpha_i stays cocartesian on restriction to the inert base, and the existing cocartesian-composition-initiality statement makes it initial in its exact lift category. Now apply the existing relative-limit-initial-components theorem: its hypotheses are precisely a disjoint union of small quasicategories with selected initial objects and an arbitrary inner fibration D to B. Its existence assertion keeps both the entire prescribed boundary and the base cone exact, and its second assertion tests any specified cone. This proves both clauses of the helper without assuming completeness. At n=0 there are no maps to [1], so both indexing sets are empty and the existing theorem explicitly includes this case.

### Recognition and arbitrary inert edges

For a functor H preserving inert edges, each color cone is a cone of cocartesian projections. The monoidal definition gives its mapping-space product condition. The relative-cone constant-test theorem turns that into the diagram-level relative limit condition: the upper-right mapping space is a product because the indexing set is discrete; base mapping spaces in the simplex category are discrete, and the fiber over each fixed base map is the monoidal product equivalence. The local-slice reduction then gives the full local Kan cone.

Conversely, a relatively right Kan extended H gives relatively limiting color cones by the particular-cone reduction. A monoidal tuple of the same target colors has a relatively limiting cone by the preceding argument. The two relative universal properties give mutually inverse comparison maps over the identity of [n], fixing the cone boundary: the space of comparisons over that identity data is contractible. Thus the comparison of apices is a fiber equivalence and H of each chosen color lift is cocartesian, by the mapping-space criterion. Other choices of source color lift differ by target equivalences by cocartesian-composition-initiality, so their H-images are also cocartesian.

Let a:x to y be any inert edge over rho:[n] to [m], and let b_j be the target color projections. Their composites b_j a are inert color lifts by the composition statement. Choose a target cocartesian lift v:H(x) to z over rho and factor H(a) as t v with t in the [m]-fiber, using the object case of pointwise-cocartesian-transport. Choose cocartesian projections delta_j:z to z_j. Factor H(b_j)t through delta_j as theta_j:z_j to H(d_j). The two arrows delta_j v and H(b_j a) are cocartesian over the same base map with the same source; the initial-lift comparison makes theta_j an equivalence. Under the Segal equivalences determined by delta_j and H(b_j), these theta_j are exactly the components of t. Hence t is an equivalence, and H(a) is cocartesian. Conversely if H(a) is cocartesian, its comparison with v is an equivalence and all these components are equivalences. For m=0 the fiber is equivalent to a point and this same argument has no components. This supplies the explicit comparison asserted by the helper. The three conditions define equal full subcategories, so no claim about transformations is obtained merely from a bijection on objects.
