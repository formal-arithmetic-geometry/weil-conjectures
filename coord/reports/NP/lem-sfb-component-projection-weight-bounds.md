## Review by NP

I read Morrison's Section 3.2 proof plan at printed p.14 and its weight conventions in the preceding Hilbert setup, then the actual book argument of Gieseker Proposition 1.0.3 at printed pp.30--37. The book chooses the same zero/one flag, defines the spaces of monomials with at least m-j factors in W, injects them into a complementary primary subscheme, imposes a point-ideal power at a boundary point, and sums the dimensions. The source's semistability step occurs after that calculation and is not used. The item and all children are self-contained about their schemes, field, embeddings, degree and powers. All children are statement-only. The source paths are declared wanted above and proof_origin is agent because the local documents are absent and two child formulations are generalized from the book's fixed setup.

Step 1 uses exactly the bounded-weight node's dimension-one and eventual P(m) assertions, the new span lemma's inequalities (including e positive), and the primary-complement lemma's finite generic length, Hilbert slope and restriction injection. I opened every one of those statements. The existing linear-series bound has the unwanted genus>=2 hypothesis and was not substituted. The connected finite-type curve cannot have an isolated zero-dimensional irreducible component: finiteness follows on a projective finite-type scheme over a field, and any point component meeting another is contained in it. Thus the chosen irreducible component is one-dimensional, as required by the children.

Step 2 checks W is nonzero separately for e=1 and e>=2 in the multiple case, and for e<=3 in the non-rational-normal case. These checks are essential: connectedness alone would not supply a distinct component. Nondegeneracy then excludes support equal to Y. The primary-complement statement explicitly supplies a boundary point on a one-dimensional complementary component, so the point-bound child's hypotheses hold. The projective-Hilbert-polynomial dimension node explicitly says the degree of the polynomial is the dimension and that its leading coefficient is positive; this makes delta>0. Generic reducedness makes the generic Artinian local ring a field, of length one. No nodality, normality of X, linear normality or completeness of its embedding is used.

Step 3 cites the exact primary-separation injection and the point section bound, plus the ideal-sheaf and ideal-power definitions read in def-sbc-closed-immersion-ideal-sheaf and def-sbc-ideal-sheaf-powers. A product of at least t forms vanishing on Y lies in I_Y^t; at the chosen reduced point of C every such factor lies in I_P. This uses inclusion of ideals, not the false assertion that set-theoretic containment forces scheme factorization. The resulting injection bounds the dimension of the actual monomial span, so no unproved surjectivity onto an ideal-power space is needed. The constants are fixed for this curve, not claimed uniform in a family.

Step 4 is elementary finite-dimensional linear algebra: greedily pick spanning monomials one weight at a time, and compare any other monomial basis by its numbers of vectors below each threshold. The summation identity is written explicitly. Only Q-1 exceptional terms occur and each is O(m); summing the displayed linear ranks gives (delta-1/2)m^2+O(m). The bounded-weight statement supplies w_F(m)=a_F m^2+O(m) with the same convention. Thus the coefficient is mu*e+1/2, not twice that coefficient (Gieseker/Morrison sometimes denote twice it by e_F). Step 5 only substitutes mu>=2 or mu=1 and the span inequalities. Full verbatim statements of every node cited in each step are in citations.json; no citation takes material only from a dependency's proof.
