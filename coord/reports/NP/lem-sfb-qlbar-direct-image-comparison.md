### Retained step 2

Let $I_E$ be the poset of finite fields $E\subseteq F\subseteq\overline{\mathbf Q}_\ell$. The tail module-colimit theorem says precisely that this is nonempty and directed and that the inclusion maps $q_{F,Z}:F_Z\to B_Z$ exhibit $B_Z=\varinjlim_{I_E}F_Z$ in $R_Z$-module sheaves, for $Z=X,Y$. The universe in step 1 makes this a small diagram. Cartesian coefficients identify $f^{-1}R_Y$ with $R_X$, $f^{-1}F_Y$ with $F_X$, and $f^{-1}B_Y$ with $B_X$; they make $L$ exact. Compatibility of coefficient maps with inverse image identifies $Lq_{F,Y}$ with $q_{F,X}$. Each $F_Z$ is finite free over $R_Z$, hence its tensor functor is exact; the algebraic-closure flatness theorem gives the same exactness for $B_Z$.

Uses: `lem-sfb-qlbar-tail-module-colimit`, `sk-prop-sfb-cartesian-coefficients`, `sk-def-sfb-coefficient-change`, `lem-sfb-subfield-flat`, `lem-sfb-qlbar-flat`.

### Retained step 3

Choose an integer $a$ and a representative $K^\bullet$ for $C$ with $K^r=0$ for $r<a$. This is possible by the standard t-structure characterization of $D^{\ge a}$. Exact scalar extension is its own derived functor, so $M_F=F_X\otimes_{R_X}K^\bullet$ represents $F_X\otimes^L_{R_X}C$ and has the same lower bound $a$. Tensor preserves the coefficient colimit, term by term, so $\varinjlim_F M_F=B_X\otimes_{R_X}K^\bullet$, with the actual maps $q_{F,X}\otimes1$; it represents $B_X\otimes^L_{R_X}C$. The same calculation on any representative $J^\bullet$ of $A=R_fC$ gives $\varinjlim_F(F_Y\otimes_{R_Y}J^\bullet)=B_Y\otimes_{R_Y}J^\bullet$. Filtered colimits in module sheaves are exact: the forgetful functor preserves them and is exact, and filtered colimits of abelian sheaves are exact. Exact functors commute with cohomology, so for each $q$ this last identification gives
$$H^q(B_Y\otimes^L_{R_Y}A)=\varinjlim_F H^q(F_Y\otimes^L_{R_Y}A).$$

### Retained step 4

For an $R_Y$-module $P$ in degree zero let
$$\delta_P:P\otimes^L_{R_Y}R_fC\longrightarrow R_f(LP\otimes^L_{R_X}C)$$
be the projection map with the factors interchanged by symmetry. Its adjunct is the canonical pullback/tensor comparison followed by $1\otimes\varepsilon_C$. The ringed projection-mate theorem makes this natural in $P$ and says that $\delta_{R_Y}=1$. For $P=F_Y$, choose a finite free identification $F_Y\cong R_Y^{\oplus d}$ and pull it back to $F_X\cong R_X^{\oplus d}$. Additivity identifies the source and target with $A^{\oplus d}$ and $R_f(C^{\oplus d})$. On each coprojection, naturality of $\delta$ identifies its map with the corresponding coprojection followed by $\delta_{R_Y}=1$. Thus $\delta_{F_Y}$ is the finite-biproduct comparison for the additive functor $R_f$, and is invertible. No infinite-sum assertion about $R_f$ is used in this calculation.

### Retained step 5

Apply the pro-étale bounded-below direct-image continuity theorem of the route (unit of bounded continuity) to the sheaf of rings $R_X$ (the section-ring sheaf of step 1) and to the actual diagram $(M_F)_{F\in I_E}$ of step 3. Its hypotheses hold: $f$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated, $I_E$ is small and filtered, every $M_F$ is a complex of $R_X$-modules vanishing in degrees below the common bound $a$, and its termwise colimit is $B_X\otimes_{R_X}K^\bullet$ with the maps $q_{F,X}\otimes1$. The theorem gives that the canonical map $\varinjlim_FR^qf_*M_F\to R^qf_*(B_X\otimes_{R_X}K^\bullet)$ is an isomorphism of sheaves on $Y_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}$. Here $f_*$ lands in $f_*R_X$-modules; $R_f$ is the derived functor of restriction along $R_Y\to f_*R_X$ composed with $f_*$, computed on the same K-injective resolutions, and restriction is exact and leaves underlying sheaves unchanged. So the comparison is the underlying map of the $R_Y$-linear map
$$\varinjlim_F H^q(R_f(F_X\otimes^L_{R_X}C))\longrightarrow H^q(R_f(B_X\otimes^L_{R_X}C)),$$
which is therefore an isomorphism for every $q$. Naturality of the projection map in $q_{F,Y}$ gives the actual cocone equation
$$\delta_{B_Y}\circ(q_{F,Y}\otimes1)=R_f(q_{F,X}\otimes1)\circ\delta_{F_Y}.$$
Consequently, under the cohomology-colimit identification of step 3, $H^q(\delta_{B_Y})$ is the composite of the colimit of the isomorphisms $H^q(\delta_{F_Y})$ from step 4 and the displayed continuity isomorphism. It is an isomorphism for every $q$, so $\delta_{B_Y}$ is an isomorphism in $D(\operatorname{Mod}(R_Y))$.

Uses: `det-pe-bounded-continuity-direct-image`, `lem-weil-pbc-ringed-projection-mate-exact`, `lem-weil-pbc-unbounded-derived-iso-cohomology`.

### Retained step 6

It remains to identify the specified map, rather than an unspecified isomorphism between its endpoints. Write $U_Z$ for restriction from $B_Z$ to $R_Z$, $T_Z=B_Z\otimes^L_{R_Z}-$, and $\gamma_C:T_YA\to Rf_{*,B}T_XC$ for the map in the statement. By its adjunct formula, $d\circ U_Y\gamma_C$ has $L\dashv R_f$ adjunct obtained by restricting the defining $B$-coefficient adjunct of $\gamma_C$. $$L(B_Y\otimes^L_{R_Y}A)\ \cong\ B_X\otimes^L_{R_X}LA\ \xrightarrow{\,1\otimes\varepsilon_C\,}\ B_X\otimes^L_{R_X}C.$$

The adjunction bijection therefore gives $d\circ U_Y\gamma_C=\delta_{B_Y}$. By step 5 this is invertible, and $U_Y$ reflects isomorphisms; hence $\gamma_C$ is invertible.
