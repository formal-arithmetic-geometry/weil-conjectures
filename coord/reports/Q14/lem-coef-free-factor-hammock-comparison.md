## Returned Step 4 retained for provenance

Fix a vertex $x$ of an oriented tree $T$ and a finite signed word $w$. First suppose $T$ is finite. We prove $NZ_w(x,x)$ weakly contractible by induction on the number of edges, using the diagram categories of [def-coef-zigzag-homotopy-calculus]. For the singleton tree every object and arrow of $Z_w(x,x)$ is the constant identity diagram, including when $w$ is empty, so its nerve is a point. Otherwise choose a leaf $l\ne x$, with neighbour $a$, and let $T'$ be obtained by deleting $l$ and its incident edge. Such a leaf exists: a maximal simple path in a finite tree has two leaf endpoints. Let $i:C_{T'}\to C_T$ be inclusion and let $r:C_T\to C_{T'}$ send $l$ to $a$, its incident generating edge to $1_a$, and every other generator to itself. Freeness extends this to a functor, with $ri=1$. If the edge is $l\to a$, it is the sole nonidentity component of a natural transformation $1\Rightarrow ir$; if it is $a\to l$, it defines $ir\Rightarrow1$. Naturality holds on that edge and on all unchanged generating edges, hence on every path. Applying $i,r$ and this transformation at every vertex of a fixed-word diagram gives functors between $Z_w^{C_{T'}}(x,x)$ and $Z_w^{C_T}(x,x)$, a retraction, and a transformation from one composite to the identity in the stated orientation. All endpoint components are identities because $l\ne x$; all vertical components lie in the distinguished category $C_T$. By [lem-coef-nerve-natural-homotopy-equivalence], their nerves are homotopy equivalent. Induction proves the finite-tree assertion.

For arbitrary small $T$, index by the nonempty directed poset of finite subtrees $S\subseteq T$ containing $x$. The union of two such subtrees is again a finite subtree because they intersect at $x$. For each $S$ the preceding induction gives $NZ^{C_S}_w(x,x)\to\Delta^0$ a weak homotopy equivalence. A simplex of $NZ_w$ has finitely many arrows, and every arrow is a finite generating path. All their edges, together with the finitely many paths connecting their vertices to $x$, lie in a finite subtree. Thus $NZ^{C_T}_w(x,x)$ is exactly the degreewise filtered colimit of these nerves; there are no extra identifications because subtree inclusions are faithful and injective on objects. Apply [lem-coef-filtered-colimit-weak-equivalences] to these collapse maps and the constant point diagram. The indexing category is small, nonempty and filtered, so its constant point colimit is a point. Hence $NZ^{C_T}_w(x,x)$ is weakly contractible.
