### Original step 4

The inverse of $L[2d]$ can be constructed without a new geometric theorem. Put $L^\vee=\mathcal Hom_A(L,A_X)$, using [def-pur-sheaf-hom]. On an etale cover trivializing $L$, evaluation $L\otimes_A L^\vee\to A_X$ is $A\otimes_A A\to A$, with inverse $a\mapsto1\otimes a$. These maps are compatible with restriction; the evaluations are isomorphisms by [lemma-weil-pbc-geometric-stalk-conservative], applied to underlying abelian sheaves. Tensoring by either line preserves injections: on the same cover it is tensoring by $A$, hence the original injection, and kernels vanish if their restrictions vanish. Thus both lines are flat. They are constructible because they are locally constant with finite rank-one stalks over the finite ring $A$. The tensor of these one-term flat complexes computes derived tensor; shifting the factors by $2d$ and $-2d$ cancels the shifts. Hence
$$W_f\otimes_A^L L^\vee[-2d]\cong A_X\cong L^\vee[-2d]\otimes_A^LW_f.$$
Apply the exact comparison $V_X$ and its monoidal isomorphism [lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-derived-tensor-pullback]. The two transported lines are still locally constant free rank one: pull back the same trivializing cover. They are flat by the same local injection argument. Consequently $M=V_XL^\vee[-2d]$ is bounded constructible and of finite Tor amplitude, and the two evaluations identify $\omega_f\otimes_A^LM$ and $M\otimes_A^L\omega_f$ with $A_X$. This is invertibility in precisely the sense of [def-pur-co-smoothness], including the required finite-Tor inverse.
