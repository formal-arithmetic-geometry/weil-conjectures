### Returned step 4 — lem-trf-chosen-nodal-fibres-universal

First verify the hypotheses of [lem-trf-nodal-family-fitting-singular] and [lem-trf-nodal-family-etale-local-model], which quantify over every algebraically closed field, whereas semistability is a condition on the chosen geometric fibres $Y_{\bar t}$, $\bar t:\operatorname{Spec}\overline{\kappa(t)}\to T$. Let $\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to T$ be any geometric point, over $t\in T$. By [lem-weil-pbc-algebraic-extension-embeds-algebraically-closed] the chosen algebraic closure $\overline{\kappa(t)}$ embeds into $\Omega$ over $\kappa(t)$, and associating fibre products identifies $Y\times_T\operatorname{Spec}\Omega$ with the base change of $Y_{\bar t}$ to $\Omega$. The scheme $Y_{\bar t}$ is locally of finite presentation, hence locally of finite type, over $\overline{\kappa(t)}$ by clause (3) of [lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence], and its nonsmooth points have completed local ring $\overline{\kappa(t)}[[u,v]]/(uv)$; so [lem-trf-node-algebraically-closed-extension] gives completed local ring $\Omega[[u,v]]/(uv)$ at every nonsmooth point of $Y_\Omega$. It supplies an open relative smooth locus $U\subset Y$ and says that the nonsmooth locus after every base change is the inverse image of $Y\setminus U$. Taking complements gives exactly $U'=U\times_TT'$, including the assertion that the relative smooth locus commutes with base change.

### Returned step 9 — lem-trf-geometric-components-projective-line-descent

To compare the chosen algebraic-closure fibres in the definition with larger algebraically closed fibres, let $k\subset L$ be algebraically closed fields and let $C$ be a finite-type $k$-scheme. By [lem-weil-pbc-algebraic-over-algebraically-closed-subfield], $k$ is algebraically closed in $L$; [lem-zl-field-extension-algebraically-closed-geometric] makes $\operatorname{Spec}L$ geometrically irreducible over $k$. [lem-sc-product-irreducible-components-bijection] gives a bijection of irreducible components after scalar extension, both for $C$ and separately for each reduced component $R\subset C$. In particular each $R_L$ is irreducible: it is nonempty and has exactly one irreducible component. Since $k$ is perfect, [lem-kun-geometric-reduced-perfect] says $R_L$ is reduced. Thus $R\mapsto R_L$ identifies the reduced component schemes themselves.

### Returned step 10 — lem-trf-geometric-components-projective-line-descent

For a reduced component $R$ of such a finite-type scheme, suppose $R_L\cong\mathbf P^1_L$. The component $R$ is closed in a finite-type Noetherian scheme, hence quasi-compact and quasi-separated (intersections of affine opens are Noetherian opens and quasi-compact). Both $R$ and $\mathbf P^1_k$ are of finite presentation over the field $k$ (use finite-type Noetherian affine charts). Write $L$ as the directed union of its finitely generated $k$-subalgebras. Apply [lemma-nagata-morphism-descends] to the isomorphism and its inverse, then [def-nagata-morphism-equality-stage] and [lemma-nagata-morphism-eq-eventually] to the two inverse equations. At one common stage $A\subset L$ this gives inverse $A$-morphisms $R_A\rightleftarrows\mathbf P^1_A$. The nonzero finite-type $k$-algebra $A$ has a maximal ideal; [lem-weil-pbc-closed-point-residue-field-base] identifies its residue field with $k$. Pulling the two inverse morphisms back to this point gives $R\cong\mathbf P^1_k$. Conversely such an isomorphism pulls back to one over $L$, with projective-space base change supplied by [lemma-nagata-projective-space-base-change-pullback]. This uses only the two cited morphism-descent assertions, their equality assertion, and specialization.

### Returned step 11 — lem-trf-nodal-marking-special-point-incidence

For a semistable marked geometric curve $(C,F)$ over $k$, its marking and its singular subscheme are finite disjoint unions of $k$-points: use [lem-trf-unramified-field-fibre-decomposition] for the etale marking and for the unramified singular subscheme supplied by [lem-trf-nodal-family-fitting-singular], applied to $C\to\operatorname{Spec}k$. Its hypotheses hold for every algebraically closed extension $\Omega$ of $k$: $C_\Omega$ is pure of dimension one by Step 2 and nodal by [lem-trf-node-algebraically-closed-extension]. They are quasi-compact closed subschemes of the finite-type Noetherian curve, so the disjoint decompositions have finitely many terms. Scalar extension replaces each $\operatorname{Spec}k$ by one $\operatorname{Spec}L$. Step 4 identifies the pulled-back singular locus, and a marking is pulled back as a closed subscheme. Therefore on corresponding components $R,R_L$ the number of distinct points in the union of marking and singular locus is unchanged. Intersections are fibre products of the same closed subschemes, so a given rational point belongs to $R$ if and only if its unique lift belongs to $R_L$. This counts points on the component, as distinct points of the component; it does not count branches of the normalization.

### Returned step 12 — lem-trf-projective-line-contraction-scalar-extension

For a morphism of the geometric pairs and a corresponding component $R$, contraction is unchanged by $k\subset L$. If $R\cong\mathbf P^1_k$ and its image is a point, that point is $k$-rational (evaluate at a rational point of $R$); the morphism is constant. Indeed choose an affine neighbourhood $\operatorname{Spec}B$ of its image. On the two affine charts of [def-sc-p1-standard] its maps are ring maps $B\to k[t]$ and $B\to k[t^{-1}]$, by [def-weil-affine-spectrum]. They agree in $k[t,t^{-1}]$, whose two subrings have intersection $k$. Thus both chart morphisms factor through the same rational point and glue to the constant morphism. Its scalar extension is constant too. Conversely if $R_L$ is contracted, projection of its image and the surjectivity $R_L\to R$ from Step 9 show that the image of $R$ has one point. Together with Step 10 and Step 11, every exceptional contracted $\mathbf P^1$ component after extension would give exactly such a component before extension, with the same number of special points. Thus a fibre with no exceptional component remains without one.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-field-extension-base-change-faithfully-flat, lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-surjective-spectrum, def-sc-p1-standard, def-weil-affine-spectrum.
Source: literature/stacks-project/curves.tex; Temkin geometric exceptional-component condition; algebraically closed field extension.

### Returned step 14 — lem-trf-structural-fibrewise-open-locality

Finally let $(T_i)$ be an open cover of $T$. Flatness and local finite presentation are pointwise affine-local conditions. Quasi-compactness is local on the target by [lem-pc-quasi-compact-local-on-target]; apply the same lemma to the diagonal to check quasi-separatedness locally, using that an open cover of $T$ pulls back to an open cover of the diagonal target. Thus finite presentation is local on $T$. Closed immersion and etaleness are local on the target by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-finite-etale-zariski-local], applying closed-immersion locality over the inverse-image cover of the curve. Every point of $T$ belongs to a $T_i$, and its fibre, marking and fibre morphism are identical to those of the restricted pair. Hence pure dimensions, nodes and the no-exceptional-component condition are local as well; the marking's inclusion in the smooth locus is pointwise. These arguments allow empty marking and nonproper, nonseparated curves. They assert nothing about schematic density or preservation of modifications.

Cited nodes: def-weil-flat-morphism, def-sbc-locally-finite-presentation, lem-pc-quasi-compact-local-on-target, lem-weil-pbc-closed-finite-etale-zariski-local.
Source: literature/stacks-project/curves.tex; Open-cover locality of the defining conditions.
