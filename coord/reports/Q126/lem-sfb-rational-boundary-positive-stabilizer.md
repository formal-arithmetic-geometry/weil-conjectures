Step 1 uses normal-divisorial-completion for integrality of G, a normal proper X0 and its boundary, domain-laws for the rational extension of left multiplication, and divisor-model for a modification unchanged over the dense open, a divisor of dimension d-1, and the open W. Those are all explicit conclusions in the cited statements. Normalization alone would not produce W, so this is a genuine geometric input, stated separately. The new completion statement is for smooth connected separated nonproper finite-type varieties over algebraically closed k, so it applies to G. The model statement requires G integral, obtained from the completion lemma, and smoothness, already a hypothesis. It is more general than this item because its dense open can carry any regular action and it asserts no stabilizer. The modification is required to be an isomorphism over that entire open, so the conclusion still embeds all of G.

Step 2 uses the model's actual open W, not just a birational rational map without a common domain. I opened the Jacobson node: it explicitly gives density of closed points in every closed subset of a locally finite-type scheme over a field, which applies to nonempty W itself. I opened the residue-field-base node: for each closed point it identifies the residue field with algebraically closed k. Thus the chosen pair really lies in G(k) times D(k). The domain-laws node explicitly gives existence of (g0 inverse times g0) acting on P from the two successive actions; its unit clause then identifies the value with P. Merely equality of rational maps on generic points would not suffice.

Openness of V is the inverse image of the open domain along the fixed-point section; the fibre F is the scheme-theoretic fibre of this morphism over the rational point.
