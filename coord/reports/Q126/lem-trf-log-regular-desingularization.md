The definition asserts no existence theorem. I re-read the current DAG node def-trf-log-regular-pair after its update: its statement now explicitly introduces the geometric point in the chart clause, identifies the strict local ring and geometric stalk, and cites Krull dimension in the final formula. I replaced the step-1 citation quote with that exact current statement; no proof text or hypothesis was changed. For the monoidal tower, VIII 3.3.14--3.3.15 gives free final characteristic stalks, using the fan construction from 3.2.19--3.2.21; the auxiliary toric calculation is over Q and imposes no characteristic-zero restriction on V. For the comparison, VIII 3.4.6(i) is the underlying normalized-blowup identification; VIII 3.3.11 with VI 1.8 gives preservation of log regularity. Over the triviality locus the log ideal is already principal, so the universal property gives the identity; outside it a local ring homomorphism cannot turn a nonunit into a unit, so a nontrivial characteristic remains nontrivial. For the final criterion I read VI 1.4 and 1.7, expressly invoked by VIII 3.4.4 and 4.1.1.

Step 1 uses the existing pair definition verbatim for the dense open, fs structure and dimension condition; the new general definition uses that identical condition. The cited finite-type-algebra-noetherian lemma applies to every affine F-algebra in a finite affine cover because the field is Noetherian. This verifies precisely the compactness and Noetherian hypotheses of the tower lemma, rather than assuming excellence or characteristic zero.

Step 2 checks the torsion-freeness hypothesis directly: if ng=0, saturation places both g and -g in the sharp monoid and hence g=0. The tower statement cited gives exactly the finite list of saturated log blowups and the free final stalks used here. It does not give regular underlying schemes, and the proof does not take that from it.

Step 3 cites the affine chart cover and the definition A[I/a] as a subalgebra of A_a, not an opaque blowup definition. I checked their complete statements. For a domain, each nonempty chart ring lies in its fraction field; the charts have a common generic point and therefore glue to an integral scheme. The normalization definition is an integral closure inside this same field, so the integral-domain argument applies again. This is a few-line elementary chart argument, also confirmed against Stacks Tag 02ND, divisors.tex, lemma-blow-up-integral-scheme. I kept it as a step, not a new lemma node. No existence, finiteness or properness theorem is inferred merely from the normalization definition.

Step 4 sets up its induction before using it. The current stage is integral finite type and log regular, so the one-step comparison has every stated hypothesis. Its statement supplies the nonzero finite-type quasi-coherent ideal, the log regularity, the normalized ordinary blowup and the precise triviality locus. I opened all seven cited statements: the blowup-proper lemma applies to this finite-type quasi-coherent ideal; properness includes finite type; finite-type composition applies without additional separatedness hypotheses; normalization-exists assumes an integral scheme finite type over a field, which has just been proved for B_i; finite morphisms are proper; and proper morphisms compose. The comparison and these statements prove the inductive claim with no appeal to the full resolution conclusion. In particular I did not apply the existing normalization finiteness node before verifying integrality and finite type, nor did I infer finiteness merely from existence of normalization.

Step 5 uses only the established induction and the free final characteristic monoids. The local-criterion node states all of regularity, density, the reduced effective Cartier boundary and the etale-local parameter description used here. Its triviality locus is p^{-1}U, so equality of the reduced inverse boundary with that divisor is by equality of the complementary closed subsets. The composition node supplies properness; the length-zero tower gives the identity, for which properness is directly checked from the properness definition.

I tested the statements on the trivial log structure and Z empty (r=0, unit equation for the empty effective Cartier divisor), on a smooth scheme with an already normal crossings boundary (possibly no blowups), on the toric node xy=z^2 with its toric boundary (the original characteristic need not be free), and on a normal crossings divisor with a self-intersecting global component (the final criterion promises etale branches, not strict global components). For the general tower, strata need not be regular: making the characteristic free alone does not resolve those strata; the item obtains regularity only after its log-regularity induction. An empty log ideal is explicitly excluded so a blowup cannot discard the scheme and vacuously satisfy the conclusion. Nonempty finite log-ideal generators map to nonzero functions in the divisorial integral case.
