## Preserved arguments for the new workers

These are the five old steps verbatim from the repository baseline; they are retained here so the re-split does not discard or blur any of the former proof. Steps 2-3 are assigned to etale-chart-ideal-lift; step 5 to free-quotient-fitting-power; steps 1 and 4 remain parent assembly.

### Former step 1

By [lem-weil-pbc-zero-locus-eq-iff-radical-eq], $\sqrt J$ is the intersection of the prime ideals containing $J$. Every such prime contains the image $a_B$ of $a$, since $V(J)\subset V(aB)$. Thus $a_B\in\sqrt J$, and the definition of the radical in that same statement gives an integer $m\ge1$ with $a_B^m\in J$. Fix this exponent before any localization.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-zero-locus-eq-iff-radical-eq.

### Former step 2

Fix a prime $\mathfrak q\subset B$. The standard-etale localization [lem-weil-pbc-etale-locally-standard] supplies $g\notin\mathfrak q$, a monic $f\in A[T]$, and an $A$-algebra isomorphism $\theta:B_g\xrightarrow{\sim}C_h$, where $C=A[T]/(f)$ and $h\in C$. Its displayed algebra $A[T][Y]/(f,Yh-1)$ identifies with $C_h$: send $T$ to its class and $Y$ to $h^{-1}$, with inverse induced by the quotient map, in which $Yh=1$. By [lem-weil-pbc-principal-localization-open-immersion], $\operatorname{Spec}B_g\to\operatorname{Spec}B$ is an open immersion; [lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation](1)-(2) makes its composite to $\operatorname{Spec}A$ etale. Consequently $\operatorname{Spec}C_h\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is etale under $\theta$. Put $d=\deg f$. The monic-quotient theorem [lem-weil-pbc-monic-quotient-free] gives the free $A$-basis $1,t,\ldots,t^{d-1}$ of $C$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-etale-locally-standard, lem-weil-pbc-principal-localization-open-immersion, lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation, lem-weil-pbc-monic-quotient-free.

### Former step 3

Let $j_1,\ldots,j_r$ generate $J$. For each $i$, write $\theta(j_i/1)=c_i/h^{e_i}$ with $c_i\in C$ and $e_i\ge0$, using the fraction description [def-weil-ring-localization-away]. Since $h^{e_i}$ is a unit in $C_h$, these fractions and the images of the $c_i$ generate the same ideal, namely $\theta(JB_g)$. Write $a_C$ for the image of $a$ in $C$ and set $K=(c_1,\ldots,c_r,a_C^m)\subset C$. Step 1 and $A$-linearity of $\theta$ give $a_C^m/1\in\theta(JB_g)$. Hence $K$ is finitely generated and $KC_h=\theta(JB_g)$. Moreover $a_C^m(C/K)=0$, since $a_C^m\in K$.

Uses: def-weil-ring-localization-away.

### Former step 4

Use the basis of Step 2 to fix the surjection $\pi:A^{\oplus d}\cong C\twoheadrightarrow C/K$, and put $I=I_0(\pi)$. The hypotheses of [lem-trf-etale-fitting-trick] hold: $C$ is finite free, $\operatorname{Spec}C_h\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is etale, and $K$ is a finitely generated ideal of $C$. That statement gives a finitely generated ideal $I\subset A$ and a finitely generated ideal $L\subset C_h$ such that $IC_h=(KC_h)L$. Set $J'=\theta^{-1}(L)\subset B_g$. Its generators are the inverse images of generators of $L$, and Step 3 converts the equality to $IB_g=(JB_g)J'$.

Uses: lem-trf-etale-fitting-trick.

### Former step 5

Finally, relative to the basis of Step 2, each vector $a^m e_i\in A^{\oplus d}$ belongs to $\ker\pi$ by Step 3. The $d$ rows $a^m e_i$ form the diagonal matrix $a^m1_d$. Its determinant is $a^{md}$: in the Leibniz formula [def-weil-pbc-matrix-determinant] only the identity permutation can contribute a nonzero product, giving $d$ diagonal factors. The kernel-minor definition [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change] therefore puts $a^{md}$ in $I$. If $d=0$, that definition instead gives $I=A$ and $V(I)=\varnothing$. If $d>0$, then $md\ge1$, so a prime containing $I$ contains $a^{md}$ and hence $a$ by primality. Thus $V(I)\subset V(a)$ in both cases, completing all the required conclusions for the chosen $\mathfrak q$.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-matrix-determinant, lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change.
