## Preserved arguments for the three unsplit workers

### Original step 1: item assembly

Put $X'=X\times_TT'$ and let $r:X'\to X$ be the projection. By [lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers] and [lem-trf-space-chartwise-etale-permanence], $r$ is representable etale. The open inclusion $V'\to X'$ is representable etale: on every scheme base change it is an open immersion, hence etale by clause (1) of [lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation]. Thus $q:V'\to X$ is representable etale by the same algebraic-space permanence statement. Since $T'$ is affine, the given factorization of $V'\to T'$ is a quasi-compact open immersion into an affine scheme, using clause (2) of [lem-weil-pbc-affine-morphism-permanence]. That affine scheme is quasi-compact by [lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs], so $V'$ is quasi-compact. Apply [lem-a1-space-etale-open-image] to $q$ to obtain the quasi-compact open subspace $W\subseteq X$ with $|W|=q(|V'|)$. It remains to prove that this particular $W$ is a scheme.

### Original step 2: item assembly

For each integer $n\geq0$, let $P(n)$ assert the claimed schematicity for every diagram in the statement whose affine etale map $a$ has fibre degrees bounded by $n$ [def-weil-pbc-fibre-degree-bound]. These quantify over all schemes and diagrams, so they may be applied with a new target. The map $a$ is locally quasi-finite: every etale scheme chart of $T'$ has etale composite to $T$ by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation], so [lem-a1-etale-space-locally-quasi-finite], applied to the scheme $T'$ as an algebraic space and then its identity chart, gives this. Its source $T'$ is quasi-compact by [lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs]. Hence [lem-weil-pbc-lqf-qc-source-bounded-fibres] supplies such an $n$. We prove $P(n)$ by strong induction. If $n=0$, every fibre is the spectrum of the zero algebra, hence empty; a point of $T'$ would lie in a nonempty fibre. Thus $T'=\varnothing$, $V'=\varnothing$, and $W$ is the empty scheme. In the remaining steps fix $n\geq1$ and assume $P(m)$ for $m<n$.

### Original step 3: lem-a1-affine-etale-diagonal-complement-degree

Let $T''=T'\times_TT'$ with projections $\pi_0,\pi_1$. It is affine by [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra]. The map $a$ is affine, hence separated, by [lem-weil-pbc-morphism-affine-schemes-affine] and clause (3) of [lem-weil-pbc-affine-morphism-permanence]; separated means its diagonal is closed [def-weil-proper-morphism]. Its diagonal $\delta:T'\to T''$ is also open, since etale implies unramified [def-weil-etale-morphism], and [def-weil-unramified-morphism] defines unramified using the open diagonal. Therefore $T''=\delta(T')\amalg T^*$ as open and closed subschemes. The complement $T^*$ is affine: on $T''=\operatorname{Spec}R$ its clopen subset is $D(e)$ for an idempotent $e$ by [lem-weil-pbc-spec-clopen-idempotent], represented by $\operatorname{Spec}R_e$ by [lem-weil-pbc-principal-localization-open-immersion]. The restriction $b=\pi_1|_{T^*}:T^*\to T'$ is etale, since $\pi_1$ is a base change of $a$ and the inclusion of $T^*$ is open; use [lem-trf-space-chartwise-etale-permanence] and [lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation].

### Original step 4: lem-a1-affine-etale-diagonal-complement-degree

The fibre degrees of $\pi_1:T''\to T'$ are bounded by $n$ by [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-degree-base-change]. For any field-valued point $\operatorname{Spec}K\to T'$, [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-degree-bound-field-points] makes its fibre a finite $K$-scheme of degree at most $n$. The clopen decomposition of step 3 pulls back to the disjoint union of the diagonal section $\operatorname{Spec}K$ and the fibre of $T^*$. A finite scheme over a field is affine; its coordinate algebra therefore decomposes as $K\times C$, where $C$ is the finite-dimensional algebra of the complementary fibre. The vector-space identity $\dim_K(K\times C)=1+\dim_K C$ gives $\dim_K C\leq n-1$. The converse direction of [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-degree-bound-field-points], applied to $b$, now bounds every fibre degree of $b$ by $n-1$. This calculation works over every field without assuming that residue fields are algebraically closed.

### Original step 5: lem-a1-affine-etale-diagonal-complement-degree

If $n=1$, the previous calculation forces $T^*=\varnothing$ (a point of $T^*$ would give a nonempty fibre). Hence $\delta:T'\to T''$ is an isomorphism. The fibre-product universal property then says $a$ is a monomorphism: any pair of maps with equal composites through $a$ gives a map to $T''$, which factors uniquely through $\delta$, so the two maps agree. The etale definition [def-weil-etale-morphism] supplies flatness and local finite presentation. By [lem-pe-flat-lfp-monomorphism-open], $a$ is an open immersion. Its base change $r:X'\to X$ is an open immersion: each scheme test is a base change of $a$, hence is an open immersion by [lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-base-change]. Thus the composite open inclusion $V'\to X$ identifies $W$ with the scheme $V'$. This proves $P(1)$. We may now assume $n>1$.

### Original step 6: lem-a1-etale-saturation-quasi-affine-pullback

Write $X''=X\times_TT''$, $X^*=X\times_TT^*$, and let $p_i:X''\to X'$ be induced by $\pi_i$. Put $Z=p_0^{-1}(V')\cap X^*$. As a space over $T^*$ this is the scheme $T^*\times_{\pi_0,T'}V'$. Its map to $T^*$ is quasi-affine by [lem-trf-quasi-affine-base-change]; the base change here uses $\pi_0$, whereas $X^*\to X'$ uses $\pi_1$. The space $Z$ is open in $X^*=T^*\times_{b,T'}X'$, because it is the pullback of the open $V'\subseteq X'$ along the other projection. By [lem-a1-space-separated-lqf-permanence], $X'\to T'$ is separated and chartwise locally quasi-finite. The map $b:T^*\to T'$ is etale between affine schemes and its fibre degrees are bounded by $n-1$. All hypotheses of $P(n-1)$ therefore hold for $(T',T^*,X',Z)$. It gives a quasi-compact open scheme $H\subseteq X'$ with $|H|=p_1(|Z|)$.

### Original step 7: lem-a1-etale-saturation-quasi-affine-pullback

Apply the base-change image clause of [lem-a1-space-etale-open-image] to $q:V'\to X$ and $r:X'\to X$. It identifies the inverse image $P=W\times_XX'=T'\times_TW$ with the image open of $V'\times_XX'\to X'$. The latter source is $p_0^{-1}(V')\subseteq X''$, mapping by $p_1$. On the diagonal component of $X''=X'\amalg X^*$ its image is $V'$, and on the complementary component its image is $H$ by step 6. The inclusion-preserving open-subspace correspondence in the same lemma therefore gives $P=V'\cup H$. This is a Zariski covering by two open schemes: after a map from any scheme, the two open pullbacks cover that scheme, since their point sets do. The sheaf $P$ is an algebraic space, hence an fppf sheaf by [def-trf-algebraic-space], and in particular a Zariski sheaf. Apply [lem-pc-schemes-criterion] to the two open subfunctors $V',H\subseteq P$ to obtain a scheme representing $P$. Its underlying space is quasi-compact, as a union of two quasi-compact open subsets.

### Original step 8: lem-a1-etale-saturation-quasi-affine-pullback

Apply [lem-a1-space-separated-lqf-permanence] to $W\subseteq X$ and to base change by $T'\to T$. It makes the scheme map $P\to T'$ separated and locally quasi-finite. The target $T'$ is quasi-separated by [lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs], and $P$ is quasi-compact by step 7, so [lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence], clause (1), makes $P\to T'$ quasi-compact. By [lem-weil-pbc-lqf-locally-finite-type] it is locally of finite type. The four hypotheses of [lemma-nagata-quasi-affine-morphism-of-quasi-finite] now hold for this scheme map, so $P\to T'$ is quasi-affine. No Noetherian, finite-presentation, or finiteness assumption on $X$ has been inserted.

### Original step 9: lem-a1-open-image-quasi-affine-scheme-descent

Let $B\subseteq T$ be the open image of $a$, supplied by [lem-a1-space-etale-open-image] applied to schemes. Then $p:T'\to B$ is surjective etale. It is also affine: $T'\times_TB=T'$ since $a$ factors through $B$, and affineness follows by base change from the affine map $a$ using [lem-weil-pbc-affine-morphism-permanence]. The sheaf $W$ factors through $B$: its point image in $T$ lies in $B$, since $|W|=q(|V'|)$ and $V'\to T$ factors through $T'$. Thus $W\cap(X\times_TB)$ and $W$ have the same point subset of $X$, and the open-subspace correspondence of that same image lemma identifies them. For every affine open $C\subseteq B$, $p^{-1}(C)$ is affine and surjects onto $C$. With flatness and local finite presentation from [def-weil-etale-morphism], this verifies the singleton fpqc-covering condition [def-weil-pbc-fpqc-covering] and the fppf-covering condition [def-pc-fppf-site]. The base $B$ need not be affine.

### Original step 10: lem-a1-open-image-quasi-affine-scheme-descent

Regard $W$ over $B$ as the sheaf assigning to $Q\to B$ the $S$-morphisms $Q\to W$ compatible with the maps to $B$. This is an fppf sheaf: compatible maps to $W$ glue uniquely because $W$ is an algebraic space [def-trf-algebraic-space], and equality of their composites to $B$ is also local. Its pullback along $p$ is represented by $P=T'\times_BW=T'\times_TW$, the scheme of step 7. Apply [lem-trf-fppf-sheaf-representability-descent-data] to the fppf covering $\{p:T'\to B\}$. It gives a scheme descent datum on $P/T'$. On the double overlap its comparison is the natural interchange of $P\times_BT'$ with $T'\times_BP$ representing the same sheaf $T'\times_BW\times_BT'$; on the triple overlap both composites keep the same section of $W$ and the same three base coordinates. This is its cocycle. The statement of the cited node, rather than a claim that fpqc is a site, supplies both this datum and the criterion that its effectivity is equivalent to representability of the sheaf $W$ over $B$.

### Original step 11: lem-a1-open-image-quasi-affine-scheme-descent

The map $p:T'\to B$ is an fpqc covering by step 9, and $P\to T'$ is quasi-affine by step 8. Apply [lem-trf-quasi-affine-fpqc-effective-descent] to the datum of step 10. It is effective. The representability-if-effective clause of [lem-trf-fppf-sheaf-representability-descent-data] now represents $W$ by a scheme over $B$, hence over $S$. We have proved $P(n)$ under the induction hypotheses. Strong induction and the bound from step 2 prove schematicity for the original diagram. Together with step 1 this is exactly the quasi-compact open image asserted in the statement.
