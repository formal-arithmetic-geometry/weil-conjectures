## Statement corrections — STATEMENT-DISPUTED

The imported maximal-order assertion lacks a regular-point exception. Take X=Z=Spec Q, a minimal smooth envelope of a regular zero-dimensional germ. A codimension-zero ideal is zero (infinite order, and full cosupport for every positive marking) or the unit ideal (order zero, empty cosupport). Neither has order equal to a positive finite marking throughout the required nonempty cosupport. The corrected statement asserts codimension-zero maximal order at singular points. At regular points it retains the genuine presentation (Z,Z,0,1) and explicitly does not claim finite order for zero. This repairs a small-case overstatement, rather than assuming away all regular points.

The source's smooth-pullback assertion concerns presentation/test equivalence. Its numerical levels must shift: for Spec Q and its product with A^1, the closed-point functions are respectively 1 and n+1. I made the shift H -> Lambda^r H explicit, locally on constant relative-dimension pieces, instead of asserting numerical invariance or synchronization across different dimensions. This follows BMT section 6.3 and preserves the intended local functoriality claim.

The old sentence made strict transform appear compulsory for every test operation. BM Theorem 9.4 distinguishes an admissible blowup from an exceptional-intersection test: in the latter its formal-coordinate calculation gives the total pullback and ordinary coefficient pullback (and in this special situation the strict transform agrees). I stated the two operation rules separately. This avoids imposing an unrelated strict-transform formula in test histories. I also stated what boundary is appended for a line product and that equivalence is agreement of all finite test sequences, not just equality of initial zero sets.

The item uses the extended Hilbert--Samuel function at arbitrary scheme points, not completion dimension with the transcendence-degree summation omitted. Its statement now cites the existing residue-field and transcendence-degree definitions. None of these corrections changes a locked Challenge target.
