## Original returned argument (retained verbatim)

### Old step 1

For $eN\geq j$, the identity $\ell^N=u^N\pi^{eN}$ implies $(\ell^N)\subseteq(\pi^j)$: explicitly $\ell^N=\pi^j u^N\pi^{eN-j}$. Hence reduction of representatives defines $O/(\ell^N)\to O/(\pi^j)$ and thus $B_N\to D_j$ on the coefficient sheaves of [sk-def-sp-coefficient-towers]. Its composite with $R\to B_N$ is $R\to D_j$. For $N\geq N_0$ and $j\geq j_0$ whenever the relevant quotient maps exist, all composites send the class of $x$ to the class of $x$; the quotient squares commute.

### Old step 2

Let $P$ be any K-flat $R$-complex, in particular the flat-term representative in the statement. For an acyclic $B_N$-complex $L$, exact restriction of scalars [lem-pur-sheaf-ring-derived-coefficient-adjunction] makes $\operatorname{Res}L$ acyclic. The natural sheaf tensor cancellation of [lem-sfb-scalar-tensor-cancellation-independent], applied in each bidegree and summed with the total differential, identifies $\operatorname{Res}\operatorname{Tot}(L\otimes_{B_N}(P\otimes_R B_N))$ with $\operatorname{Tot}(\operatorname{Res}L\otimes_R P)$. The latter is acyclic by [def-pc-kflat-complex]. Exact restriction detects zero cohomology, since it retains the underlying sheaf and all underlying maps. Thus $P\otimes_R B_N$ is K-flat over $B_N$.

### Old step 3

Apply [lem-sfb-successive-derived-quotient-normalization] to $R\to B_N\to D_j$. Its comparison is an isomorphism in the target category $D(D_j)$, not merely after forgetting coefficients. Choose a flat-term K-flat representative using [lem-pc-kflat-resolution]; [lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings] and the preceding step compute both successive extensions on it. The comparison and inverse are the ordinary chain maps $(x\otimes b)\otimes d\mapsto x\otimes\bar b d$ and $x\otimes d\mapsto(x\otimes1)\otimes d$. They are $D_j$-linear. The cited normalization gives independence of the representative, naturality in $K$ and in the composable ring maps, and agreement of iterated comparisons. Together with the quotient squares in step 1 this proves compatibility with further quotient maps in both indices.

### Old step 4

Put $H_N=K\otimes_R^L B_N$. The assumed Tor interval and [def-weil-proetale-tor-amplitude], tested on the degree-zero unit module $B_N$, give $\mathcal H^q(H_N)=0$ outside $[a,b]$, since $H_N\otimes_{B_N}^LB_N\cong H_N$. Thus the constructible cohomology assumption makes $H_N$ an object of the bounded finite-Tor constructible category appearing in [det-sp-scalar-extension-functor]. Fix $j\geq1$ and take $N=j$; then $eN\geq j$ because $e\geq1$. Both $O/\ell^N$ and $O/\pi^j$ are finite commutative rings killed by $\ell^N$. Clause (2) of that existing node applies to their quotient map on the arbitrary scheme $T$, giving constructible cohomology and Tor amplitude in exactly $[a,b]$ for $H_N\otimes_{B_N}^LD_j$. Transfer these properties through the $D_j$-linear comparison of step 3 to $K\otimes_R^LD_j$. Testing its Tor interval on $D_j$ itself also proves the asserted cohomology vanishing. The invoked clause has no flatness or finite-projective-dimension requirement on the coefficient map and no invertibility or completeness hypothesis.
