## Work instructions for the extracted tasks

For the local-algebra task, use the existing closed-fibre Noetherianity statement and nonzerodivisor statement. If m_R is not associated, take h regular on L, choose n≥1 with h^n in the image of q, and lift h^n to g∈q. Apply 0GSG to g:N→N, whose target is A-flat and whose closed-fibre map is injective. For each localization use the explicit rule: if g(n/u)=0 then some further denominator v has vgn=0, hence vn=0 and n/u=0. A nonzero element of P annihilated by m_T is then killed by g, a contradiction. This has its own module hypotheses and does not use the parent theorem.

For the stalk model, take a finite type affine chart about x with finite module representing F. Its localizations identify B and N and the special quotients R and L. The prime q comes from y as a generalization of x and contracts to zero in A. Primes containing q+m_A B correspond to points of the closure's closed fibre generalizing x; x is component generic, so the only such prime in Spec B is m_B. This proves the radical equality. Localizing first by A\{0} and then by q_K identifies the generic-fibre ring and module stalks. These identifications preserve maximal ideals and annihilator witnesses, giving both association equivalences. In particular the model asserts no association conclusion without the separate algebra task.

For the integral valuation-scheme dimension task, every nonempty affine chart W=Spec D contains η, so A→D is injective. The generic chart is the localization of a domain by nonzero elements of A; these charts give an integral generic fibre with generic η. At a special component generic point choose such W. The closed fibre of W is nonempty, so apply 00QK to D. Its component E∩W is obtained by the existing open-component injection lemma, and its dimension equals dim E by locally algebraic dimension constancy. The generic chart is a nonempty open of the irreducible generic fibre, hence has the whole generic fibre's dimension. The spectrum/ring dimension comparison identifies the affine generic dimension in 00QK with that dimension. Finally isolate E near its generic point using local finiteness of components; other components do not contain that point. The dimension of this open equals its local dimension. This retains all the chart and local-dimension work of original steps 1, 2, 3 and 9.

For the residual-component task, scalar extension by a field is flat, so flat going-down (00HS) sends component generic points to component generic points. On affine rings R→R⊗_k L, over a minimal prime p of R, a prime q is minimal precisely when its image after localization at R\p and quotient by p is minimal: every smaller prime contracts into p, hence to p, and the localization/quotient prime correspondences preserve the order of these primes. The localized quotient is κ(p)⊗_k L, with no separability assumption. A closed immersion identifies the closure of its point with the same closure in the ambient scheme; if the latter is an ambient component it is also a component of the closed subscheme. This is a scheme-level result with a genuine flat going-down direction, not a duplicate of local-dimension invariance.

## Original ten-step argument, retained verbatim for handoff

### Original step 1

The integral closed subscheme $Y\hookrightarrow X$ is locally of finite type by [lem-weil-pbc-immersion-base-change-lft](2), so $Y\to S$ is locally of finite type by [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence](e). These assertions remain true after arbitrary base change by [lem-weil-pbc-lft-base-change], and hold also for the integral closed subschemes $Y'$ used below. In particular all fibres and field-extended fibres used below are locally of finite type over their fields, hence locally Noetherian by [pqf-lem-locnoeth-lft]. The spectrum of a field is Noetherian since its only ideals are zero and the whole field. No Noetherianity of $S$ is asserted.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-immersion-base-change-lft, lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence, lem-weil-pbc-lft-base-change, pqf-lem-locnoeth-lft.

### Original step 2

For each of these locally algebraic fibres, at a component generic point $z$ one has $\dim_z Z=\dim\overline{\{z\}}$. Indeed [lem-sc-locnoeth-components-locally-finite] gives a neighbourhood meeting only finitely many components; remove the other components, none of which contains $z$, to obtain an open neighbourhood contained in its component $D$. On this neighbourhood the ambient space and $D$ have the same topology. [lem-sc-dimension-locally-algebraic] gives $\dim_zD=\dim D$, proving the assertion from the neighbourhood definition of dimension. Apply this in particular to $x\in Y_s$ to obtain $\dim_xY_s=\dim C$. Also $Y_t$ is irreducible with generic point $y$: on affine charts it is obtained from the domain defining $Y$ by inverting the nonzero functions on the integral image closure of $t$.

Uses: lem-sc-locnoeth-components-locally-finite, lem-sc-dimension-locally-algebraic.

### Original step 3

Apply [lem-trf-valuative-fibre-component-lift] to $f,y,s,x$. Retain its square $X'=X\times_SS'$, valuation base $S'=\operatorname{Spec}A$, points $t',s',y',x'$ and integral closed subscheme $Y'=\overline{\{y'\}}$. Put $K=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ and $k=A/\mathfrak m_A$. Write $C'$ for the component of $Y'_{s'}$ with generic point $x'$. The generic fibre $Y'_{t'}$ is the component of $Y_t\times_t t'$ with generic point $y'$: on affine charts taking the prime defining the closure of $y'$ and then localizing in $A\setminus\{0\}$ recovers the prime of that component.

Uses: lem-trf-valuative-fibre-component-lift.

### Original step 4

Put $\mathcal F'=(g')^*\mathcal F$. It is quasi-coherent and finite type by [lem-weil-pbc-module-pullback-quasi-coherent] and [lem-weil-pbc-module-pullback-finite-type]. The stalk formula [lem-weil-pbc-module-stalk-pullback] and pointwise scalar extension [lem-pc-pullback-stalk-relative-flat-extension], applied with $M=\mathcal F_x$, give that $\mathcal F'_{x'}$ is $A$-flat; here $\mathcal O_{S',s'}=A$. Since $y'$ is generic in a component of $Y_t\times_t t'$, it is generic in its fibre over the generic point $y$ (localizing at $y$ preserves minimal primes). The residual fibre is $\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(t')\otimes_{\kappa(t)}\kappa(y))$. As $y$ is relatively associated, [lem-trf-relative-assassin-base-change] therefore gives $y'\in\operatorname{Ass}_{X'/S'}(\mathcal F')$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-module-pullback-quasi-coherent, lem-weil-pbc-module-pullback-finite-type, lem-weil-pbc-module-stalk-pullback, lem-pc-pullback-stalk-relative-flat-extension, lem-trf-relative-assassin-base-change.

### Original step 5

Prove $x'\in\operatorname{Ass}_{X'/S'}(\mathcal F')$ by contradiction. Set $B=\mathcal O_{X',x'}$, $N=\mathcal F'_{x'}$, $R=B/\mathfrak m_AB$ and $L=N/\mathfrak m_AN$. Choose a finite type affine $A$-chart around $x'$ and an affine module presentation there. By [lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization] and [lem-weil-pbc-spec-tilde-stalk-localization], $B$ is a localization at a prime of that finite type algebra and $N$ is a finite $B$-module. The local map $A\to B$ is thus essentially of finite type. The same localization formulas on the closed-fibre chart and [lem-weil-pbc-module-stalk-pullback] identify $R=\mathcal O_{X'_{s'},x'}$ and $L=(\mathcal F'_{s'})_{x'}$. Thus $R$ is Noetherian by step 1 and $L$ is finite. If $x'$ is not relatively associated, the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_R$ is not associated to $L$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization, lem-weil-pbc-spec-tilde-stalk-localization, lem-weil-pbc-module-stalk-pullback.

### Original step 6

Let $\mathfrak q\subset B$ be the prime corresponding to $y'$, which specializes to $x'$. In the local spectrum the closure of $y'$ is $V(\mathfrak q)$. Its closed fibre at $x'$ has just one prime, since $x'$ is the generic point of its fibre component and localization keeps only primes contained in $\mathfrak m_B$. Consequently $\sqrt{\mathfrak q+\mathfrak m_AB}=\mathfrak m_B$, or $\sqrt{\overline{\mathfrak q}}=\mathfrak m_R$ for the image ideal in $R$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-nonzerodivisor-if-maximal-not-associated] to the Noetherian local ring $R$ and finite module $L$: obtain $h\in\mathfrak m_R$ acting injectively on $L$. Some power $h^n$, with $n\ge1$, lies in $\overline{\mathfrak q}$, so choose $g\in\mathfrak q$ with image $h^n$ in $R$. Its multiplication on $L$ is injective as a composite of $n$ injective maps. This is the source's prime-avoidance conclusion, obtained here from the existing nonzerodivisor lemma and the radical equality.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-nonzerodivisor-if-maximal-not-associated.

### Original step 7

Apply [lem-trf-valuation-finite-module-injectivity] to multiplication $g:N\to N$. The two $B$-modules are finite, the target is $A$-flat by step 4, the map $A\to B$ is local essentially of finite type, and its reduction is injective by step 6. Hence $g$ acts injectively on $N$. It remains injective after both localizations used to reach the generic-fibre stalk at $y'$: if $g(n/u)=0$, the fraction criterion [def-weil-pbc-module-localization] supplies a further denominator $v$ with $vgn=0$; injectivity gives $vn=0$ and thus $n/u=0$. By the affine stalk and pullback formulas of step 5, this localized module is $(\mathcal F'_{t'})_{y'}$, over $(B\otimes_AK)_{\mathfrak q}=\mathcal O_{X'_{t'},y'}$. But $y'$ is relatively associated, so this stalk has a nonzero element annihilated by its maximal ideal. The element $g\in\mathfrak q$ maps into that maximal ideal and kills it, contradicting injectivity. Therefore $x'$ is relatively associated.

Uses: lem-trf-valuation-finite-module-injectivity, def-weil-pbc-module-localization, lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization, lem-weil-pbc-spec-tilde-stalk-localization, lem-weil-pbc-module-stalk-pullback.

### Original step 8

By [lem-trf-relative-assassin-base-change], the associated point $x'$ maps to $x\in\operatorname{Ass}_{X/S}(\mathcal F)$ and is generic in the residue-field fibre over $x$ of $X'_{s'}\to X_s$. This same residual fibre occurs for $Y_s\times_s s'\to Y_s$, since $x\in Y_s$ and a closed immersion does not change its residue field. Because $x$ is a component generic point of $Y_s$, $x'$ is a component generic point of $Y_s\times_s s'$: on affine charts, a prime properly contained in the prime of $x'$ would contract to a prime contained in the minimal prime of $x$, hence to that same prime; after localizing at $x$ it would contradict minimality in the residue-field fibre. Thus the closure of $x'$ in $Y_s\times_s s'$ is an irreducible component. It equals $C'$ topologically because $Y'_{s'}$ is a closed subscheme of this base change and contains $x'$.

Uses: lem-trf-relative-assassin-base-change.

### Original step 9

Choose a finite type affine $A$-chart $W=\operatorname{Spec}D\subset Y'$ containing $x'$. Its coordinate ring is a domain and $A\to D$ is injective: every nonempty open of the integral scheme $Y'$ contains $y'$, whose image is the generic point of $\operatorname{Spec}A$, so a nonzero element of $A$ cannot vanish in $D$. The fibre $W_{s'}$ is nonempty. Apply [lem-trf-valuation-domain-fibre-dimensions]: every component of $W_{s'}$ has dimension $\dim(D\otimes_AK)$. In particular $C'\cap W$ is such a component by [lem-sc-components-open-injection]. By [lem-sc-dimension-locally-algebraic], its dimension is $\dim C'$, while the nonempty open $W_{t'}$ of the irreducible $Y'_{t'}$ has dimension $\dim Y'_{t'}$. The affine spectrum/ring comparison [lem-weil-pbc-spec-topological-krull-dimension-ring] identifies $\dim W_{t'}=\dim(D\otimes_AK)$. Hence $\dim C'=\dim Y'_{t'}$.

### Original step 10

Apply [lem-sc-fibre-dimension-after-base-change] to $Y\to S$ at $y'$ over $t'$: $\dim_{y'}(Y_t\times_t t')=\dim_yY_t$. Step 2 identifies the left side with $\dim Y'_{t'}$ and the right side with $\dim Y_t$, since $y'$ is generic in its component and $Y_t$ is irreducible. The same base-change lemma at $x'$ over $s'$ gives $\dim_{x'}(Y_s\times_s s')=\dim_xY_s$. By step 8, $x'$ is a component generic point of this entire base change, so step 2 identifies its local dimension with $\dim C'$. Combining these equalities with steps 2 and 9 gives $\dim_xY_s=\dim C=\dim C'=\dim Y'_{t'}=\dim Y_t$. Together with step 8 this is the required conclusion.
