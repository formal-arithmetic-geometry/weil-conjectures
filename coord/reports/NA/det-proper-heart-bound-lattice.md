## Statement scope: STATEMENT-DISPUTED

The imported standalone wording said only “In the situation of det-proper-heart-bound-torsion.” That cited statement itself omitted the standing separated finite-type scheme setting. Read literally for arbitrary schemes, the source's Completeness inference “write a=i∘u with u quasi-compact open” is invalid. For example, let R be the product of countably many copies of a field and let U be the union of the disjoint principal opens D(e_n) in Spec(R), where e_n is the nth coordinate idempotent. This open is dense: any nonempty principal open D(f) contains a coordinate point where f is nonzero. It is not quasi-compact: the displayed cover has no finite subcover. Its closure is all of Spec(R), so the factorization through its closure has a non-quasi-compact open part. The source also applies Tag 09BM, which requires the complementary open to be retrocompact; it is not a theorem about arbitrary closed immersions without that condition.

The item now explicitly restores the inherited separated finite-type k-scheme setting of `sk-def-sfb-dc-setting` (positive characteristic different from ell). This is the scope of the chapter's restricted six-functor construction and of the integral-model theorem used in the source. Within this setting X, S and the closure of S are Noetherian, so the two quasi-compactness conditions hold. This is a correction of the suppressed standalone scope, not a claim to have disproved the vanishing conclusion for every possible arbitrary-scheme interpretation. The conclusion, the strict range q>2d+1, and the objects p,V,a,L0 are retained. The proof does not put V or X_V in the finite-type setting: they may be non-Noetherian.

The coefficient ring throughout is the pulled-back integral coefficient sheaf, identified with the completed coefficient sheaf in `sk-lem-integral-models`. It is never replaced by the discrete constant sheaf of O_E. On X_V the argument uses the restricted ring sheaf itself, which suffices for the generic site theorem and agrees with the restriction convention in the statement.
