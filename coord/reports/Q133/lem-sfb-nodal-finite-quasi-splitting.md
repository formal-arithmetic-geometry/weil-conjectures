### Original step 1: lem-sfb-nodal-branch-quasi-split-spreading

For a finitely presented nodal family $g:C\to V$, write $D_g$ and $B_g$ for its singular subscheme and its two-branch cover. The branch-cover lemma identifies $(B_g)_v$ with the inverse image of the singular points in $C_v^\nu$, and $B_g\to D_g$ has two geometric points over each geometric node. The finite etale field-algebra decomposition identifies its finite branch fibre with a product of its finite separable residue fields. Thus $C_v$ is quasi-split exactly when $(B_g)_v$ is a disjoint union of rational points. Indeed rationality of both branches is part of the definition; conversely a rational branch above $x$ forces $\kappa(v)\to\kappa(x)$ to be an isomorphism since its composite with $\kappa(x)\to\kappa(b)$ is an isomorphism. This criterion includes the empty branch scheme.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-nodal-branch-double-cover, lem-weil-pbc-etale-algebra-over-field-product.

Source: Definition preceding Lemma 5.7, pp.614--615; relative branch cover from the cited Stacks assertion.

### Original step 2: main item Step 1 (arbitrary base change)

First prove the base-change clause, independently of the alteration. For $V'\to V$ and $v'\mapsto v$, put $F=\kappa(v)$ and $L=\kappa(v')$. The branch-cover identifications give $(B_{g_{V'}})_{v'}=(B_g)_v\times_F L$. If $C_v$ is quasi-split, this is a disjoint union of copies of $\operatorname{Spec}L$, because $F\otimes_F L=L$. Step 1 now makes the singular points and both normalization branches $L$-rational. This argument is local at each fibre and does not assume that $V'$ is Noetherian or that normalization of the total space commutes with base change.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-nodal-branch-double-cover.

Source: Base-change preservation used in Theorem 5.9 proof, p.617; explicit branch-scheme calculation.

### Original step 3: main item Step 2 (geometric preparation and generalized induction)

The base $S$ is Noetherian: a field is a Noetherian ring and finite type over a Noetherian scheme is Noetherian. It has finite topological dimension by the finite-type dimension lemma. The same holds for every intermediate normal integral projective finite-type $k$-base. Regard $f$ as a semistable multipointed curve with empty marking. Its base changes remain flat finitely presented nodal families by the semistable base-change lemma; projectivity is preserved by base change, and a projective morphism is proper. Consequently the branch scheme is finite over each intermediate base. We will prove the assertion more generally for such families by induction on the dimension of the closure of their non-quasi-split locus, without using smoothness of their generic fibres.

### Original step 4: lem-sfb-nodal-branch-quasi-split-spreading

We need the spreading claim of Lemma 5.7. Let $T$ be any integral reduced closed subscheme of an intermediate base $V$, with generic point $\xi$, and suppose $C_\xi$ is quasi-split. Restrict to a nonempty affine open $T_0\subset T$. The finite scheme $B_g\times_V T_0$ is affine, and is finitely presented over $T_0$ by finite-over-locally-Noetherian finite presentation. By Step 1 its generic fibre is isomorphic to the generic fibre of $\coprod_{j=1}^n T_0$, including $n=0$. Apply generic-fibre isomorphism spreading to these two affine finitely presented $T_0$-schemes. It gives a nonempty open $U\subset T_0$ on which the branch scheme is $\coprod_{j=1}^n U$. Step 1 proves that every fibre over $U$ is quasi-split.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-nodal-branch-double-cover, pqf-lem-finite-fp-locnoeth, lem-sbc-generic-fibre-iso-spread-base-open.
