### Original step 1

Put $V_X=\prod_nX_n$ termwise. By [lem-weil-proetale-countable-products-exact], evaluation at $s$ identifies $\Gamma(V_X)^i$ with $\prod_n\Gamma(X_n^i)$, with the actual product projections. In these identifications, the $n$-th component of $\Gamma(s_X)$ is $\Gamma(w_n)\operatorname{pr}_{n+1}$, hence $\Gamma(1-s_X)=1-s_{\Gamma X}$. Exact sections [lem-weil-point-proetale-sections-exact] are additive and preserve binary biproducts. Define $c_X^i$ by these identifications on the two summands $V_X^{i-1}\oplus V_X^i$. Both summand identifications are invertible, so $c_X^i$ is invertible.

Uses: lem-weil-proetale-countable-products-exact, lem-weil-point-proetale-sections-exact.

### Original step 2

Under $c_X$, the differential applied to $(b,a)$ is $(-\Gamma(d)b-(1-s_{\Gamma X})a,\Gamma(d)a)$ on either side, so the componentwise isomorphisms are a chain isomorphism. For a strict tower map $w:X\to Y$, put $W=\prod_nw_n$. Strict compatibility gives $(1-s_Y)W=W(1-s_X)$. The diagonal map $(b,a)\mapsto(Wb,Wa)$ therefore commutes with the displayed differentials; its identity and composition laws hold on both components. Evaluating this map at $s$ gives precisely the diagonal map built from $\prod_n\Gamma(w_n)$, proving $c_Y\Gamma H(w)=H(\Gamma w)c_X$. This uses the signed cone coordinates and strict cone-map construction of [def-weil-strict-roos-model].

Uses: def-weil-strict-roos-model.

### Original step 3

The source sheaf-module category has exact countable products by [lem-weil-proetale-countable-products-exact]. The target module category does too: products are Cartesian products with componentwise action, and a product of surjections is surjective by choosing a lift in each coordinate. Apply [lem-weil-derived-limit-products-core] in both categories. Thus the termwise products used above represent products of the entire complexes in their derived categories, with no boundedness or Mittag--Leffler assumption. The shifted cone triangle of $1-s$ is distinguished by [def-weil-abstract-derived-category], so it exhibits $H(X)$ and $H(\Gamma X)$ as the derived limits of their whole towers in the sense of [def-weil-derived-limit].

Uses: lem-weil-proetale-countable-products-exact, lem-weil-derived-limit-products-core, def-weil-abstract-derived-category, def-weil-derived-limit.

### Original step 4

Its exact-product hypothesis was checked in Step 3. Its descent assertion says that the strict cone construction inverts levelwise quasi-isomorphisms of tower complexes. ### Original step 6

Specialize [lem-weil-finite-free-completed-scalar-comparison] to the identity extension $L=E$, $Y=s$, $K=N$. Its completeness hypothesis is exactly the derived completeness assumed here, and it gives a specified natural isomorphism $\chi_N:N\to H(P_\bullet)$, independent of the resolution. ### Original step 7

Write $\iota_{g,N}:Lg^*N\xrightarrow{\sim}H(C_\bullet)$ for the representative comparison in the defining integral pullback of [def-weil-derived-pullback-tensor-hom]. Define $\beta_N=c_{C_\bullet}\Gamma(\iota_{g,N})$. This is an isomorphism, and on the chosen model $H(C)$ its sectionwise map is exactly $c_C$, the identity under both product identifications. The comparison $\iota_{g,N}$ is the comparison with the explicit complex supplied by [lem-pur-integral-operations-well-defined], whose statement gives descent to functors and independence of resolutions with these very comparison maps. Uses: def-weil-derived-pullback-tensor-hom, lem-pur-integral-operations-well-defined.

### Original step 8

The identity-extension maps $\chi_N$ are natural for derived morphisms and independent of resolutions by [lem-weil-finite-free-completed-scalar-comparison]. The completed pullbacks and their representative comparisons have the same properties by [lem-pur-integral-operations-well-defined]. Combine these two assertions with the localized naturality of $c$ from Step 4. Explicitly, for a derived morphism $f:N\to N'$, let $D(f)$ and $B(f)$ denote the maps on the evaluated reduction and pullback towers induced by those descended constructions. Composition of the two naturality squares gives
$$H(D(f))\alpha_N=\alpha_{N'}\Gamma(f),\qquad H(B(f))\beta_N=\beta_{N'}\Gamma(Lg^*f).$$
For a change of flat-term K-flat resolution the same equations apply with the canonical comparison maps, so the two definitions agree under those maps. Naturality for an inverse quasi-isomorphism follows by multiplying the chain-map square by its inverses, and hence holds for every derived morphism. This proves independence and naturality with the specified strict-cone comparisons throughout.

Uses: lem-weil-finite-free-completed-scalar-comparison, lem-pur-integral-operations-well-defined.
