### Original step 1 (1358 characters)

Fix an arbitrary scheme $T$ with a map $T\to Y$. Put $X=F\times_YT$, $T_i=Y_i\times_YT$, and $X_i=X\times_TT_i\simeq F_i\times_{Y_i}T_i$. By [lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers], $X$ is an algebraic space, and the base-changed families cover $T$ and $X$. The $T_i$ are schemes: the maps $Y_i\to Y$ are representable etale maps. Their projections to $T$ form a jointly surjective etale family, and $X_i$ is a scheme since $F_i,T_i,Y_i$ are schemes. These assertions use the scheme-test definition of etale maps of algebraic spaces in [def-trf-algebraic-space] and associativity of sheaf fibre products. Let $N_i$ be the ordinary pullback of $M_i$ to $X_i$, invertible by [lem-weil-pbc-invertible-pullback-tensor]. By [lem-trf-scheme-relative-ample-base-change], $N_i$ is ample on the inverse image of every affine open of $T_i$. On $X_i\times_XX_j$, pull back the restriction identifications of $M$ on $F_i\times_FF_j$. Functoriality of tensor pullback [def-pc-module-pullback] makes these isomorphisms satisfy identity and triple-overlap cocycles. By [lem-trf-algebraic-space-invertible-descent] they descend to a line module $N$ on $X$. If $T\to Y$ is etale, $N$ is canonically the restriction of $M$: that restriction has the same local identifications, and the full faithfulness in the same descent lemma glues the identity maps and their inverses.

Supporting nodes: def-trf-algebraic-space, def-pc-module-pullback, lem-trf-scheme-relative-ample-base-change, lem-trf-algebraic-space-invertible-descent, lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers, lem-weil-pbc-invertible-pullback-tensor.

### Original step 3 (1018 characters)

By [lem-trf-space-section-algebra-etale-base-change], $\mathcal A=\bigoplus_{d\geq0}(f_T)_*N^{\otimes d}$ is a quasi-coherent graded algebra on the scheme $T$. Let $\psi:f_T^*\mathcal A\to\bigoplus_{d\geq0}N^{\otimes d}$ be its evaluation homomorphism. The relative Proj existence theorem [lemma-nagata-relative-proj-exists] supplies the scheme $P=\operatorname{Proj}_T(\mathcal A)$. Apply [lem-trf-space-line-module-proj-map] to obtain an open $U=U(\psi)\subseteq X$ and a map $r:U\to P$. For each $i$, the section-algebra lemma identifies $\mathcal A_i=(T_i\to T)^*\mathcal A$ with $\bigoplus_{d\geq0}(f_i)_*N_i^{\otimes d}$ and identifies the restricted $\psi$ with the evaluation map $\psi_i$. In degree $d$ this is precisely the restriction comparison $(f_T)_*N^{\otimes d}|_{T_i}\simeq(f_i)_*N_i^{\otimes d}$; it sends a section to its restriction, so evaluation sends that section to the same local section of $N_i^{\otimes d}$. Thus these are the canonical maps to which the scheme ampleness criterion applies.

Supporting nodes: lem-trf-space-section-algebra-etale-base-change, lemma-nagata-relative-proj-exists, lem-trf-space-line-module-proj-map.

### Original step 4 (862 characters)

For each $i$, [lem-trf-scheme-ample-canonical-proj-criterion] applies to $(f_i,N_i)$ and its canonical algebra from Step 3: it gives $U(\psi_i)=X_i$ and an open immersion $r_i:X_i\to P_i=\operatorname{Proj}_{T_i}(\mathcal A_i)$. By [lem-trf-space-line-module-proj-map], $U\times_TT_i=U(\psi_i)=X_i$ and $P_i\simeq P\times_TT_i$, with $r_i$ equal to the base change of $r$. The complement of $U$ is empty: the maps $X_i\to X$ cover, and its inverse image in each $X_i$ is empty. Therefore $U=X$. The maps $P\times_TT_i\to P$ are a jointly surjective etale scheme family, being base changes of $T_i\to T$; the scheme covering base-change lemma [lem-weil-pbc-etale-covering-base-change] verifies this. Apply [lem-trf-space-open-immersion-etale-local] to $r:X\to P$: its base changes are the open immersions $r_i$. Hence $r$ is an open immersion and $X$ is a scheme.

Supporting nodes: lem-trf-scheme-ample-canonical-proj-criterion, lem-trf-space-line-module-proj-map, lem-weil-pbc-etale-covering-base-change, lem-trf-space-open-immersion-etale-local.

### Original step 5 (1102 characters)

Regard $N$ now as an ordinary invertible module on the scheme $X$, using the scheme-site interpretation of [lem-trf-algebraic-space-invertible-descent].
