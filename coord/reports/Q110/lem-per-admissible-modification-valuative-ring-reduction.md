Every step is shorter than 2000 characters. All elementary steps are explicit ideal, unit, fraction or ordered-group calculations, rather than a named theorem relabelled elementary.

Step 1 uses only the principal-ideal hypothesis: writing both generators as multiples of a regular generator and cancelling it forces one coefficient to be a unit in the given local ring. The chosen exclusion from a power rules out the wrong coefficient. Step 2 uses the resulting power divisibility to prove the properness and primeness of P; cancellation of powers is justified by the given nonzerodivisor. It separately checks nonzero and nonunit image of phi. Step 3 proves P=aP, including the cancellation for each n; it does not assume that the ring B is a domain or reduced. Step 4 uses this saturation to compute all nonunits in S, so the maximal ideal is actually PS. Inverses are checked as fraction equations with localization injective by regularity.

Step 5 cites only the statement of `def-trf-valuation-ring`, which defines the domain/fraction-inverse alternative being established. The argument proves comparability of two generators in B, then reduces it to the already proved domain V. Step 6 proves the kernel and quotient identification with explicit fractions and proves that the localization of V is its fraction field; it does not identify B itself with a valuation ring.

Step 7 cites `lem-weil-pbc-integral-over-henselian-pair`, whose statement says exactly that (V,m_B V) is henselian when B→V is integral. The quotient is integral by the displayed monic equation. The equality of that ideal with m_V comes from Step 2. Thus every hypothesis of the citation is present, and the desired local henselian conclusion follows from the named definition.

Steps 8–9 handle the part not supplied by quotient permanence. I checked the scaling G(X)=phi^l F(X/phi^k) with l sufficiently large and l≥k; this makes G a polynomial in B[X], G(b) in P and its derivative d outside P. The earlier saturation gives G(b)=d²c with c in P. Step 9's only cited theorem, `lem-weil-pbc-henselian-local-root-lifting`, explicitly states both directions of equivalence for monic polynomials. Q is monic of degree N, reduces to Z^(N−1)(Z+1), and has the simple residue root −1. Its root z is a unit. Substitution t=c/z gives H(t)=c z^(−N)Q(z)=0, and then G(b+dt)=0. The correction is in P, so after dividing by phi^k it lies in PS and preserves the prescribed residue root. Applying the converse monic-root criterion to the already local ring S proves its henselianity. This is a direct finite polynomial calculation, not an invocation of an uncited Newton convergence theorem; no completion or excellence hypothesis enters.

Step 10 cites the valuation-ring definition for the associated valuation notation and writes out the order convention and the cofinality proof. The quotient value group is ordered by divisibility; well-definedness is immediate from multiplying representatives by units, totality is the proved divisibility alternative, and the valuation sum inequality follows because V is closed under addition. Every nonnegative value has a representative x in V, which divides a power of phi. Passing from a weak inequality to a strict inequality uses delta>0 and one extra power. This distinction is needed for the stated balls v(x)>gamma. Both inclusions of the neighbourhood bases are written explicitly, not asserted by a topology slogan.

I checked small cases: for a complete DVR B=k[[t]], phi=t^e (e≥1), P=0 and S=Frac(B); the construction works even when phi is not a uniformizer. For the rank-two composite valuation ring of k((x))((y)), taking phi=x gives P=y k((x))[[y]], V=k[[x]], S=k((x))[[y]], so the nonzero-prime case and topology on the separated quotient are essential. A nonreduced test is B=k[[t]]⋉k((t)) with square-zero second summand and phi=(t,0); then P is that summand and S=k((t))[epsilon]/(epsilon²). Every ideal containing a power of phi has the required regular principal generator, and the proof uses cancellation only outside P, so this example is covered. The reciprocal-polynomial calculation also works when c=0 and in characteristic 2 (−1=1, still a simple root). A field, zero ring, unit phi or nilpotent phi is excluded by the original hypotheses, rather than silently used as an admissible case. No argument assumes phi-adic separatedness of B.
