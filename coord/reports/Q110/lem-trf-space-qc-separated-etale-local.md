### Original step 1

Suppose $f$ is quasi-compact and separated. For every affine scheme $Z\to Y_i$, transitivity of fibre products gives $(X\times_YY_i)\times_{Y_i}Z\cong X\times_YZ$; its quasi-compact atlas is provided by the hypothesis on $f$. Thus each $f_i$ is quasi-compact. The diagonal of $f_i$ is the pullback of $\Delta_f:X\to X\times_YX$ along $Y_i\times_Y(X\times_YX)\to X\times_YX$. On every scheme test, this is a scheme base change of a closed immersion; [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change] makes it a closed immersion. Hence each $f_i$ is separated as well.

### Original step 2

For separatedness in the converse, assume every $f_i$ is separated. Put $B=X\times_YX$ and test its representable diagonal on any scheme $T\to B$. The fibre $D=X\times_BT$ is a scheme. The composite $T\to B\to Y$ gives schemes $T_i=T\times_YY_i$ forming a jointly surjective etale family over $T$ by [lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers]. Regard this as a cover of the terminal object in $T_{\mathrm{et}}$. It is fpqc by [lem-weil-pbc-fpqc-finer-than-etale]. Transitivity identifies $D\times_TT_i\to T_i$ with the scheme test along $T_i\to B\times_YY_i$ of the diagonal of $f_i$, since $B\times_YY_i\cong(X\times_YY_i)\times_{Y_i}(X\times_YY_i)$. It is therefore a closed immersion. Apply [lem-pc-fpqc-descend-closed-immersion] to the scheme morphism $D\to T$ and the fpqc family $(T_i\to T)_i$. Thus $D\to T$ is a closed immersion for every test $T$, which is exactly separatedness of $f$.

### Original step 3

Assume now that every $f_i$ is quasi-compact, and fix an affine scheme $Z\to Y$. Set $A=X\times_YZ$. By [lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers], the fibre products $Z_i=Z\times_YY_i$ are schemes and $(Z_i\to Z)_i$ is a jointly surjective etale family. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-etale-covering-affine-refinement] to this family over the affine scheme $Z$. It gives finitely many affine opens $W_a\subseteq Z_{i(a)}$ with $(W_a\to Z)_a$ etale and jointly surjective. Each $W_a$ has a map to $Y_{i(a)}$ through $Z_{i(a)}$. Quasi-compactness of $f_{i(a)}$, tested on this affine $W_a$, supplies a quasi-compact scheme $U_a$ and a surjective etale atlas $U_a\to (X\times_YY_{i(a)})\times_{Y_{i(a)}}W_a\cong A\times_ZW_a$. In particular every $U_a$ now has its required map to $A$.

### Original step 4

The composites $U_a\to A\times_ZW_a\to A$ form a jointly surjective etale family. Indeed, for any scheme $R\to A$, put $R_a=R\times_ZW_a$. The family $(R_a\to R)_a$ is an etale cover by [lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers]. The scheme $U_a\times_{A\times_ZW_a}R_a$ is canonically $U_a\times_AR$ and is etale-surjective over $R_a$, as a base change of the chosen atlas. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-etale-covering-composition] to these two levels of scheme covers: $(U_a\times_AR\to R)_a$ is an etale cover. This verifies representability, etaleness and covering surjectivity on every scheme test. Hence $U=\coprod_aU_a\to A$ is an etale surjection. The scheme $U$ is quasi-compact: restrict an open covering to each of the finitely many quasi-compact $U_a$, choose finite subcovers, and take their finite union [def-weil-pbc-quasi-compact-scheme]. This gives the required quasi-compact atlas of $A$. Since $Z\to Y$ was arbitrary, $f$ is quasi-compact. Together with the preceding steps this proves the equivalence.
