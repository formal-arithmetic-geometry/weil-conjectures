I read the online Numdam text of Marot Lemmas 1.1 and 1.2, printed pp.104–105, including footnote (1), and the application of Lemma 1.2 inside Proposition 2.3, printed p.107. For the new node I checked footnote (1), which attributes precisely the general Krull-domain criterion to Nishimura; the inline definition of Krull agrees term for term with the definition in the existing Mori–Nagata node. In particular its height-one local rings are normal and Noetherian, it is their intersection in its fraction field, and the height-one primes containing a nonzero element are finite. The new node has only a self-contained statement, definitions as stmt_deps, empty proof and proof_deps, null proof_origin, and all required searches. It does not hide a proof in its statement.

Step 1 cites def-weil-pbc-integral-closure for monic equations and the integral subring, lem-sc-finite-generated-integral-subring for finiteness of the explicitly generated B, and lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-noetherian for its Noetherianity. I opened all three statements: they assert exactly these facts. The basis scaling is written out with coefficients a_i^(m_i-j)c_ij; one common denominator for the coefficients makes each lie in R. The field equality Frac(B)=L follows because all basis elements x_i=y_i/a_i belong to Frac(B), which already contains K. This uses no primitive-element theorem, so remains valid for inseparable extensions. B is a domain because it is a subring of L.

Step 2 cites the integral-closure definition and lem-weil-pbc-integral-transitive for both inclusions between the normalization of B and T, then the exact statement of lem-trf-normalization-integral-closure-krull. I explicitly checked the latter only covers normalization in the base's own fraction field; it is applied to B and Frac(B)=L, not incorrectly to R and L. The two old normalization nodes are used only through their statements, not through any construction buried in a proof. Their dependencies do not refer to this item or its adic parent.

Step 3 is elementary and has no external theorem hidden behind Cohen–Seidenberg: a nonzero z in P has a least-degree monic integral equation whose constant coefficient is nonzero, by cancellation in L. That coefficient belongs to P intersect R. Height one guarantees P is nonzero; no assumption on the height of its contraction is made. The coefficient belongs to R, so the all-nonzero-primes Japanese assumption actually applies. I checked the degree-one case separately: a zero constant coefficient would force z=0. The source's narrower associated-prime hypothesis is not silently substituted into our proof.

Step 4 cites lem-trf-almost-finite-residue-field-degree with C=T. I opened its full statement and checked it allows any integral subring in a finite fraction-field extension, without assuming C module-finite or Noetherian. Thus it supplies finite Frac(T/P)/Frac(R/q), not merely algebraicity. The Japanese definition then supplies finite U over R/q. Reducing each monic equation explicitly proves T/P embeds as a submodule of U. I opened lem-weil-pbc-surjective-image-noetherian, lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-submodule-finite, and lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-noetherian: their statements respectively give Noetherian R/q, finite T/P, and Noetherian T/P.
