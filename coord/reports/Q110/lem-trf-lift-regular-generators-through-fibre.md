For Step 1 I opened lem-trf-regular-ideal-residue-basis-prescribed-generator and read its entire statement and split. It explicitly supplies the residue basis of an ideal generated by a regular sequence in the maximal ideal, with no extra Noetherian hypothesis. Taking its R=S, I=J, z=y is legitimate; the specified residue-field isomorphism identifies its scalar field with k. Only its first assertion is used, not its prescribed-element conclusion or its proof.

Step 2 uses no black box: applying beta and beta inverse proves spanning and independence, and surjectivity of the quotient I to I/mI gives representatives. I explicitly quantified over any basis and any lifts afterward, so existence of a convenient basis does not weaken the universal assertion.

For Step 3 I opened lem-weil-pbc-nakayama-local-generators. Its statement gives exactly generation from residue spanning for a finite module over a local ring. Here the finite module is the ideal I, finite by hypothesis. Ideal extension is checked by applying c to a finite sum expressing each element of I; the reverse inclusion follows from f_i in I. Membership of every x_i in n follows from locality and I contained in m.

For Step 4 I opened lem-pur-local-generating-tuples. Its statement supplies a GL_r(S) matrix for any two length-r generating tuples of a finite module over a nonzero local ring, without freeness or independence. Both x and y generate J and J is finite. Reducing the matrix and its actual inverse gives inverse matrices over B=S/J; no determinant or unquoted local-unit theorem is needed.

For Step 5 I opened lem-pur-regular-sequence-graded-polynomial. It explicitly gives the canonical B-polynomial to associated-graded isomorphism for the regular tuple y, with its variable formula. The substitution alpha and its inverse from P inverse are specified on every variable; composing gives the identity because the coefficient matrices multiply to the identity. Equality theta_x=theta_y alpha is checked on coefficients and variables. This is a routine polynomial substitution, not an implicit regular-sequence change-of-generators theorem.

For Step 6 I opened lem-pur-quasi-regular-noetherian-local. Its hypothesis is precisely the canonical polynomial-to-graded isomorphism, not mere freeness of the conormal module. Step 5 supplies that stronger hypothesis. S is nonzero Noetherian local, the x_i are in its maximal ideal, and their generated ideal is J, so its regularity conclusion applies directly. No conclusion about regularity in R is taken.

I checked r=0 separately: y empty implies J=0, beta forces I/mI=0, and Nakayama forces I=0; empty regularity uses S nonzero. For r=1, R=S=k[t]_(t), I=(t^2), any basis lift differs from a unit multiple of t^2 by mI and still generates; a lift in mI is excluded by the basis hypothesis. For r=2 the identity map on k[u,v]_(u,v) with I=(u,v), including swaps, shears and arbitrary higher-order changes, has the stated invertible substitution. When S is a field, J must be zero and only r=0 can occur. A nonflat example c:k[u,v]_(u,v) to k[v]_(v), I=(v), still satisfies beta and the conclusion; flatness is not smuggled into the argument.
