I read Temkin Definition 4.2.2 and the entire proof of Lemma 4.2.3, which prescribe successive schematic closures, as well as Lemma 4.2.1 for pushing forward centers and Lemma 4.2.5 for flat compatibility. The local Stacks proofs were read in morphisms.tex (01R8, 056B, 081I), divisors.tex (02OS, 02NS, 0805, 080E, and the effective-Cartier density argument 07ZU), and coherent.tex (01Y1). There are no new-node sources to check.

For step 1 I opened thm-nagata-global-blowup-complement: it states the isomorphism off the center, precisely what disjointness of the inserted center from U permits. I explicitly state the induction invariant and its base.

For step 2 I opened lemma-nagata-scheme-image-ideal-qc: it provides the actual quasi-coherent kernel, factorization, schematic density for quasi-compact maps, and topological closure of the image. The locally closed immersion in use is quasi-compact because its source is Noetherian. Coherence of its ideal is proved inline from finite generation of submodules over Noetherian rings, rather than hidden in an uncited scheme theorem. I opened thm-nagata-global-blowup-proper, def-weil-proper-morphism and lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian: respectively these give properness for finite-type ideals, finite type as part of the definition of properness, and the resulting Noetherian source. Coherence gives the first hypothesis before the properness theorem is used.

For step 3 I opened lem-sfb-scheme-image-flat-base-change and thm-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change, including the statement dependencies defining bc_g. The former explicitly gives equality of closed subschemes and of the extended kernel ideals for every quasi-compact map; the latter gives the blowup comparison isomorphism. Open immersions are flat. These two statements give exactly the induction step, not just a comparison of supports. Canonical uniqueness follows by repeating the same prescribed kernel construction, and not by assuming arbitrary ambient automorphisms are trivial.

For step 4 I opened lemma-nagata-strict-transform-ambient-closed: it states that the intrinsic blowup is the ambient strict transform under the closed immersion. The other cited statement, lemma-nagata-admissible-blowup-preserves-density, explicitly permits finite composites and assumes a quasi-compact schematically dense open and a finite-type center ideal trivial on it. At each stage the transform is Noetherian and W_U is open and quasi-compact; the initial density is the hypothesis on C_m and every later density follows by this statement. This proves that the final transform has exactly the closure kernel K_m. When an inserted center is already a subscheme of the transform its pullback ideal is its own center ideal, giving the claimed intrinsic iterated blowup.

For step 5 I opened lem-trf-reduced-scheme-image: it states the scheme image of any reduced scheme is the reduced induced structure on its image closure. The restricted old center is reduced, so this directly gives the replacement. The argument makes no assumption of ambient reducedness.

For step 6 I checked the properness hypotheses for all ambient maps, using the same finite-type ideal theorem as in step 2. Composing closed blowup maps remains closed. The image of a dense part under a continuous closed map has closure equal to the image of the whole center, so old and new images agree. More explicitly, if a generic image point were outside U, its irreducible component would be contained in the closed complement of U. A nonempty open in that component avoiding the other finitely many components would then miss the dense image of the U-part, a contradiction. The inverse image of these generic points therefore lies in the common U-part; the two successive restriction injections (closure density, then the given generic-point density) give the asserted schematic density. Every non-elementary geometric input here is either properness or the image/density statement of the cited image interface; the remaining closure and injection arguments are written out.

For step 7 the same flat-image statement explicitly identifies ideals with their extensions. At every stage the map to the original stage is a base change of the given flat morphism, so its flatness hypothesis holds. The quasi-compact immersion hypothesis was checked in step 2. Simultaneous induction therefore identifies every blowup and every ordered ideal, including empty stages. I specifically did not infer reducedness under arbitrary flat pullback: that implication is false, for example for an inseparable field extension, and is not needed for compatibility of this construction.
