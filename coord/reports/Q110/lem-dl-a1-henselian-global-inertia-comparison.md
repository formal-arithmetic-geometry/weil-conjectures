### Original step 1

Write $H=A^{\mathrm h}$, $R=A^{\mathrm{sh}}$ and $T=B_{\mathfrak q}$. The separable closure $\Omega/K$ is algebraic and separably closed. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-integral-closure-algebraic-extension-normal] to $A\subset K\subset\Omega$: $B$ is a normal domain with fraction field $\Omega$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-localization-normal-domain] to obtain the embedding $T\subset\Omega$, normality of $T$, and $\operatorname{Frac}(T)=\Omega$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-gabber-quotient-localizations] with normal domain $B$, ideal $(0)$ and prime $\mathfrak q$; it says precisely that $T$ is strictly henselian. Its residue field is $E=B/\mathfrak q$, because $\mathfrak q$ is maximal. This is algebraic over $\kappa=A/\mathfrak m$: reducing a monic equation for any $b\in B$ gives an algebraic equation for its class in $E$. The map $A\to T$ is local since $\mathfrak q\cap A=\mathfrak m$.

### Original step 2

The universal property [def-weil-pbc-henselization-at-prime], applied to the local map $A\to T$ and the henselian ring $T$, gives a unique local $A$-map $h:H\to T$. By [lem-trf-henselization-prime-ind-etale], $A\to H$ is ind-etale, since $A=A_{\mathfrak m}$. By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-colimit-normal-local-domain], $H$ is a normal domain and $A\to H$ is injective. Here the base really is normal as a ring: $A$ is a normal domain, and each prime localization $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is a normal domain by [lem-weil-pbc-localization-normal-domain] applied with $S=A\setminus\mathfrak p$. Thus both the local-colimit hypothesis and the base-normality hypothesis of that node hold.

### Original step 3

Put $H_K=(A\setminus\{0\})^{-1}H=K\otimes_AH$. It is a nonzero domain, since $A\hookrightarrow H$ and $H$ is a domain. By [lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability] it is ind-etale, hence weakly etale, over $K$. By [lem-weil-pbc-weak-etale-field], every finitely generated $K$-subalgebra is finite etale. Each such subalgebra is a domain; [lem-weil-pbc-etale-algebra-over-field-product] identifies it with a product of finite separable fields, necessarily a single field since it is nonzero and has no nontrivial idempotents. Thus every nonzero element of $H_K$ has its inverse there, and every element is separable algebraic over $K$. Consequently $H_K=\operatorname{Frac}(H)=K^{\mathrm h}$. Composing $h$ with $T\subset\Omega$ extends to a unital map $H_K\to\Omega$, as all nonzero elements of $A$ stay nonzero; a unital map from a field is injective. This proves that $h$ embeds $H$, and identifies $K^{\mathrm h}$ with a subfield of $\Omega$.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability, lem-weil-pbc-weak-etale-field, lem-weil-pbc-etale-algebra-over-field-product.

### Original step 11

Identify $\kappa^{\mathrm{sep}}$ with its image $j(\kappa^{\mathrm{sep}})\subset E$. The algebraic extension $E/\kappa^{\mathrm{sep}}$ is purely inseparable: by [lem-weil-pbc-separable-first-purely-inseparable], it is separable followed by purely inseparable, and its separable part is just $\kappa^{\mathrm{sep}}$ by [lem-pc-sepclosed-finite-sep-trivial], applied to the finite field generated by each separable element. Restriction of the residue action on $E$ therefore defines a homomorphism $r:D\to\operatorname{Gal}(\kappa^{\mathrm{sep}}/\kappa)$. ### Original step 15

The subgroup identifications have the claimed topology. The Krull neighbourhood basis at the identity of an automorphism group of an algebraic field extension consists of pointwise stabilizers of finite subsets of its top field. For finite $S\subset\Omega$, the stabilizer in $\operatorname{Aut}_{K^{\mathrm h}}(\Omega)$ is exactly $D\cap\{g\in G:g|_S=1\}$, and the stabilizer in $\operatorname{Aut}_{K^{\mathrm{sh}}}(\Omega)$ is exactly $I\cap\{g\in G:g|_S=1\}$. Hence the intrinsic Krull topologies are the subspace topologies. Also $\Omega/K^{\mathrm{sh}}$ is separable algebraic by [lem-weil-pbc-separable-closure-exists-galois], so these are indeed its usual absolute Galois groups.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-separable-closure-exists-galois.

### Original step 16

For continuity of $r$, let $e/\kappa$ be a finite Galois subextension of $\kappa^{\mathrm{sep}}$, choose field generators $a_1,\ldots,a_s$ of $e/\kappa$, and lift them to $z_1,\ldots,z_s\in R$ using the surjective residue map of Step 4. The open subgroup $D\cap\{g:g(z_i)=z_i\text{ for all }i\}$ is contained in $r^{-1}(\operatorname{Gal}(\kappa^{\mathrm{sep}}/e))$. The latter is a subgroup, so it is a union of cosets of that open subgroup, hence open. These stabilizers of finite Galois residue subextensions form a Krull basis. This proves continuity. The residue action of the henselian field $K^{\mathrm h}$ is the action of its absolute Galois group on this separable residue closure, computed in the integral closure $C=T$ of Step 9. By Steps 10-12 its kernel is precisely $I\subset G$. The construction started with an arbitrary compatible residue embedding $j$; uniqueness in Step 5 identifies every compatible embedding in the statement with that construction for its own residue map. Thus the last assertion holds for any chosen compatible embedding as well.
