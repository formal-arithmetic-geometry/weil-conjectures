## Review by Q110

I read Tag 07RN (`limits.tex`, lemma-approximate), including the induction and final gluing paragraph, against the unchanged statement of the item. The source allows an arbitrary supplied finite-type inverse system on the prescribed open, not just subrings of an atlas. The construction preserves that system after an order-preserving cofinal refinement. For the finite-subring input I read Tag 01Z7 and its generator/denominator proof. The strengthened marking is exactly the same construction: for the given h_l choose generators x_lq of R localized at h_l, represent each as y_lq/h_l^N, and include the y_lq, h_l and the finite seed in A. Then all generators and 1/h_l belong to A localized at h_l; injectivity follows from A being a subring. The specified localizations therefore agree, and the map on Q is an open immersion with precisely the marked union as image. Its statement introduces R, Q, h_l, E and A and cites the definitions it uses, with no proof embedded in the statement.

For the pullback-ring input I read Tag 01Z9 and More on Algebra Tag 01Z8 (`more-algebra.tex`, lemma-diagram-localize). The latter has no surjectivity hypothesis: localization preserves the displayed fibre-product exact kernel, giving B'_h = B_g times over R_s(g) with R'_f. When B_g maps isomorphically to R_s(g), this is R'_f by projection. Consequently the principal opens glue to the exact union stated. This is why the existing surjective fibre-product spectrum node cannot be cited for this step. This new node also has no proof steps or proof sketch inside its statement.

For the quasi-affine-stage leaf I read Mathlib `AffineTransitionLimit.lean` lines 986–991: the cofiltered system has compact, quasi-separated stages, affine transitions, and quasi-affine cone point, and it concludes existence of one quasi-affine stage. These are precisely the leaf's hypotheses. It does not conclude a whole tail by itself. For the other leaf I read Mathlib `QuasiAffine.lean` lines 46–49, 93–100, 115–150: an affine map over a quasi-affine target has quasi-affine source, the canonical maps are open immersions, the indicated square is cartesian, and the range equality is explicit. Each clause has its own declaration and line in `mathlib_match`. The source Tag 07RN uses this property when descending its principal opens; Mathlib supplies its exact generic form. An open-immersion-only square from another unit is insufficient. The corrected step now cites this leaf rather than silently using a theorem mentioned only in prose.

Step 1 uses the affine-open basis to choose a finite chart list by compactness. I checked the quasi-separated affine-cover criterion: affine opens of an open subscheme are affine opens of X, so its criterion gives quasi-separatedness of every intermediate union. This was added as an explicit citation. The zero-chart base is the supplied system itself. Step 2 uses the finite-type-over-Noetherian statement, including its explicit Spec Z clause, and the locally-Noetherian quasi-separatedness statement to get qcqs V_i. The intersection lemma supplies qc T; open descent supplies qc T_i, the inverse-image equality and the limit. The affine-cover criterion gives their quasi-separatedness, so the quasi-affine-stage leaf applies. The affine-morphism permanence clause then gives every later T_i quasi-affine. Each tail inclusion is cofinal by the common-upper-bound argument written there. No intersection of two arbitrary affine opens is assumed affine.

Step 3 uses exactly the section-colimit statement's lifting and eventual-zero clauses. The fibre-product ring colimit proof handles equality at different indices by first choosing a common upper index. Step 4 takes a finite subcover of the existing principal-open basis. The canonical embedding node gives the open image, the principal-affinization isomorphism and the affine-section localization statement identify B_g with R_s(g) through their actual restriction maps. Section descent lifts the finitely many functions. The affine-limit statement makes the limit projection affine; the new cartesian-square leaf identifies inverse images of the stage canonical images. Thus the two qc opens of Spec R_i really have equal inverse images in Spec R, and the equality clause of open descent applies. All later canonical images are inverse images by that same square. This repairs the otherwise hidden comparison in the source's “by Lemma descend-opens.”

Step 5 uses the pullback-ring lemma with the equalities and localization isomorphisms proved in step 4, then the finite-subring lemma with the marked h_l. The finite-type definition is applicable to T_i: refine a finite affine cover of V_i by finitely many principal opens inside its qc open T_i; the rings are generated by the original generators and one inverse. The basis and compactness justify that refinement. Enlarging a marked subring preserves its localized image, since injectivity holds inside B_i and surjectivity already holds for the smaller subring. Step 6 is the explicit set of all such subrings, not a choice assumed compatible with transitions. The size convention permits this set of subrings. Upper bounds contain images of finitely many generators; the colimit's relations are supplied by step 3. Neither injective transitions in the original system nor surjectivity to the global limit is assumed.

Step 7 uses the scheme-gluing statement for the two charts with exact intersection, and separately uses morphism gluing for maps out of their union. The morphism-into-affine bijection checks agreement on T by the equality A to R. The stated preimage-of-basic-open formula verifies the inverse images of both charts; the finite-type definition then applies to the finite affine cover. Step 8 uses that same morphism-gluing uniqueness and basic-open preimage formula for the transitions. Retaining h_l proves both chart inverse-image identities. For the affine-chart restriction, the affine-local-target criterion applies to the single whole affine target; for V_k to V_i it uses the given affine transition. Refining the target's two charts therefore proves affineness globally. These are the precise statements in the cited nodes, not an uncited gluing universal property inferred from the existence of a glued scheme.

Step 9 writes the cofinal-cone argument directly; the affine-limit statement applies to the rings whose colimit was proved in step 6. Step 10 again uses the affine-limit node's open-restriction clause, rather than assuming a colimit of schemes commutes with this gluing. It identifies both inverse-image chart limits and their overlap, then uses morphism gluing and uniqueness for the inverse comparison. Step 11 is finite induction with composition of the cofinal index maps; it checks the original prescribed opens and their transition maps survive each extension.
