## Source error

STATEMENT-DISPUTED (the online source's preliminary cover definition only; not the item): the extracted text preceding Lemma 10.1 requires all vertices together with a union of consecutive interval faces, and then claims that the spine is the smallest cover. Those conditions alone are insufficient. In F(5), the disjoint union H of the faces on [0,1] and [2,5] contains every vertex but omits edge [1,2]. These faces satisfy even the PDF's printed bound 0 <= i_lambda <= k_lambda - 1: their pairs (i_lambda,k_lambda) are (0,1) and (2,3); both also satisfy i_lambda+k_lambda <= 5. (The original F(3) example failed that printed bound and was replaced during second reading.) Take V to be the degreewise discrete nerve of the discrete category on two objects. It is Reedy fibrant (maps between discrete simplicial sets are Kan fibrations) and strictly Segal. Map(F(5),V) has two elements whereas Map(H,V) has four; restriction is not a weak equivalence. Thus that literal cover formulation cannot be the fact used here. The new lemma states explicitly G(m) contained in H, retaining the intended interval-union argument. Every application in step 2 verifies this hypothesis. No target, unit, or item statement has been weakened.

For the next worker: the new lemma is provable by induction on m. The full-simplex case is identity and m=1 is identity. For a proper interval union H containing the spine, start with G(m) and attach its maximal interval faces, all of dimensions less than m. The intersection at each attachment is a union of consecutive interval faces containing the smaller spine (isolated vertex intersections add nothing). Apply the lower-dimensional claim to that intersection. SM7 makes the mapping restriction a trivial fibration, whose pullback remains trivial. Hence Map(H,V) to Map(G(m),V) is a weak equivalence. Map(F(m),V) to Map(G(m),V) is the assumed Segal comparison; two-out-of-three proves the claim. This explanation is in the report, not a proof embedded in the new statement-only node.
