The theorem applies to arbitrary index sets, all repeated tuple intersections, every nonnegative degree and quasi-coherent coefficients, so the hypotheses proved in Step 1 suffice. The definition specifies the actual comparison as the beta augmentation followed by the inverse alpha augmentation. The proof locates those augmentations on the two axes and describes the edge map by the same formula. I checked the sign when swapping resolution and Cech indices: multiplication by (-1)^(ab) changes the two displayed total differentials into each other and fixes both axes. This small component calculation is elementary; a second comparison-identification node would duplicate the existing comparison construction. This step uses the general theorem as a tool, while Step 2 retains the blueprint's affine-vanishing/spectral-sequence argument.

It is natural for every module-sheaf morphism, hence for multiplication by each global section, which is exactly the scalar action on the section complexes and on derived cohomology. Writing that equation yields global-section linearity, and substitution of the image of a in A yields base-ring linearity.
