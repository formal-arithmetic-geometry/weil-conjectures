It presents the action category as modules over $p_K^{-1}A$ on the small action topos, identifies $p_K^*$ with trivial inflation, and identifies its ordinary tensor and unit comparisons with the identity on coefficient tensors and on $A$. In particular the symmetry is the signed cochain symmetry, rather than an unsigned tensor symmetry.

### Original step 3

The transpose of $e:I\to FI$ is the unit comparison $LI\to I$, hence the identity under the prescribed identification.

### Original step 4

The shifted adjunction sends $x$ to the adjoint $a:S_p\to FI$ of $f$; shift compatibility in step 2 identifies this with the specified $\Phi_p$. Likewise write $g=y[-q]$ and $b=\Phi_q(y)$. ### Original step 5

We now compute this tensor of arrows without interchanging $f$ and $g$. For any derived object $Z$ define $\kappa_{Z,q}:Z\otimes^LS_q\to Z[-q]$ by extracting the second shift and then applying the shifted right unitor. The natural signed shift comparisons exist on all derived morphisms by [lem-weil-pbc-derived-tensor-shift-descent], not merely on chain maps between chosen resolutions. On the degree-zero unit $I$, $\kappa_{I,q}$ is the left unitor $\lambda_{S_q}:I\otimes^LS_q\to S_q$: their representative formulas are both multiplication of the ring section, since the first-factor degree is zero. These unitor formulas are those of [lem-sf-ringed-derived-closed-symmetric-monoidal]. Bifunctoriality and left-unitor naturality, followed by naturality of $\kappa$ at $f:S_p\to I$, give
$$m(f\otimes g)=g\lambda_{S_q}(f\otimes1)=g\kappa_{I,q}(f\otimes1)=g f[-q]\kappa_{S_p,q}.$$
This is a three-equality calculation with arbitrary derived arrows; in particular it does not assume that a map from the unit in the action category is represented by an ordinary chain map.

### Original step 6

Both $\kappa_{S_p,q}r_{p,q}$ and the ordinary identification $S_{p+q}=S_p[-q]$ are maps between the same shifted copies of the regular unit. Compute them on the single nonzero tensor summand $A\otimes_A A$ in total degree $p+q$. By [lem-weil-pbc-total-tensor-integer-shifts], extracting the second shift $-q$ on a first factor concentrated in degree $p$ contributes $(-1)^{pq}$; first-shift extraction is unsigned. The fixed comparison $r_{p,q}$ is the shifted-unit identification made from these same signed comparisons. Thus its contribution is also a sign depending only on the integers and the fixed extraction order. Their composite is $t_{p,q}$ times the ordinary shift identification, for $t_{p,q}\in\{1,-1\}$. This computation is valid after localization by [lem-weil-pbc-derived-tensor-shift-descent]. All possible signs here are products of $(-1)^{pq}$: the unit is concentrated in degree zero and only the two shifts are extracted. Hence $t_{p,0}=t_{0,q}=1$. No scalar, group element, or representative of $f,g$ enters this computation.

### Original step 7

Substitute step 6 into the last equality of step 5 and precompose with $r_{p,q}$. The adjoint of the cup product from step 4 is $t_{p,q}g\circ f[-q]$. Shifting this map by $p+q$ gives exactly $t_{p,q}y[p]\circ x$: indeed $f[-q][p+q]=x$ and $g[p+q]=y[p]$. Additivity of the adjunction bijection therefore gives
$$\Phi_p(x)\smile\Phi_q(y)=t_{p,q}\Phi_{p+q}(y[p]\circ x).$$
No symmetry was used to reverse the arrows. Degree zero has the unsigned unitors, giving both asserted zero-degree normalizations and the identity-to-unit assertion of step 3. Every construction applies to the zero ring: its unit object and all maps are zero, while the integer sign is still the same prescribed sign. For the trivial group, the invariant-inclusion adjunction is the identity adjunction, so the same calculation applies. The argument works for all integer shifts and hence for the required $p,q\geq0$.
