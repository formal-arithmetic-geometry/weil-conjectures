## Argument handoff: generic regular model

The task includes both integer regularity and the characteristic-zero finite image model. Its proof should use the existing statements lem-weil-pbc-z-localizations-dvr, lem-weil-pbc-field-regular-ring, def-weil-regular-scheme, and def-weil-pbc-finite-purely-inseparable-extension. The map from the integers into L is injective, hence isomorphic onto its explicitly chosen image B. This identifies B with the regular integer ring, rather than claiming an unknown existential model. The zero ideal proves nonemptiness, and the whole spectrum is open. The following are the full old arguments:

### Returned Step 1

First check Noetherianity and regularity of $\mathbf Z$. By [lem-weil-pbc-z-localizations-dvr] it is Noetherian, its primes are $(0)$ and $(p)$ for rational primes $p$, its localization at $(0)$ is $\mathbf Q$, and its localization at $(p)$ is a one-dimensional Noetherian local ring with principal maximal ideal. The field case is regular local by [lem-weil-pbc-field-regular-ring]. In the other case one generator generates the maximal ideal and the dimension is one, which is exactly the regular-local condition of [def-weil-regular-scheme]. Thus every prime localization is regular local, so $\mathbf Z$ is regular.

### Returned Step 3

For the J-2 criterion, first take the prime $(0)$. Its residue field is $\mathbf Q$ by [lem-weil-pbc-z-localizations-dvr]. A finite purely inseparable extension $L/\mathbf Q$ is trivial by the characteristic-zero clause of [def-weil-pbc-finite-purely-inseparable-extension]. Identify $L$ with the image of $\mathbf Q$ under its structure map and take $B$ to be the image of $\mathbf Z$ in $L$. This is a $\mathbf Z$-subalgebra generated by $1$ as a module, and all elements of $L$ are fractions of elements of $B$. Step 1 gives regularity of all prime localizations of $B\cong\mathbf Z$. Thus the whole $\operatorname{Spec}B$ is a nonempty regular open subset, nonempty because $(0)$ is prime.

## Argument handoff: closed residue model

The task includes the actual residue-field identification, not only the assertion that Z/pZ is a field. It uses lem-weil-pbc-z-localizations-dvr, def-weil-pbc-finite-purely-inseparable-extension, and lem-weil-pbc-field-regular-ring. For literal algebra maps, B=L means equality of the image subring with the full subring, not an equality of types. From us+vp=1 one has a/s-au=avp/s, so the sign in the inherited Bezout calculation is correct. The statement is independent of the generic-model task.

### Returned Step 4

Now take a nonzero prime $(p)$, as classified in [lem-weil-pbc-z-localizations-dvr]. The residue field $\kappa((p))=\mathbf Z_{(p)}/p\mathbf Z_{(p)}$, the maximal ideal being $(p)\mathbf Z_{(p)}$ by [lem-weil-pbc-z-localizations-dvr], is $\mathbf F_p=\mathbf Z/p\mathbf Z$. Indeed, for an integer $s$ not divisible by $p$, Bezout gives $u,v$ with $us+vp=1$; hence $a/s-au=avp/s\in p\mathbf Z_{(p)}$ for every integer $a$, so $\mathbf Z\to\kappa((p))$ is surjective, and its kernel is a proper ideal containing $p$, hence equal to $p\mathbf Z$ since $p$ is prime. In particular every element of $\kappa((p))$ is an integer multiple of $1$, and $p=0$ in it. Let $L/\mathbf F_p$ be finite purely inseparable. By [def-weil-pbc-finite-purely-inseparable-extension], for $x\in L$ there are $n\ge0$ and $a\in\mathbf F_p$ with $x^{p^n}=a$. In characteristic $p$, the binomial expansion gives $(z+w)^p=z^p+w^p$ since $p$ divides the intermediate binomial coefficients. It follows by iteration that the $p^n$-power map is additive, and it fixes $1$, hence every integer multiple of $1$, hence every $a\in\mathbf F_p$. Consequently $(x-a)^{p^n}=0$ and $x=a$, since $L$ is a field. Thus the extension is trivial. Take $B$ to be the image of $\mathbf F_p$ in $L$. It is the image of $\mathbf Z$, hence finite as a $\mathbf Z$-module generated by $1$; its fraction field is $L=B$. By [lem-weil-pbc-field-regular-ring], its whole spectrum is regular, and it is nonempty because the zero ideal of a field is prime. These are the required witnesses at every nonzero prime.
