### Original step 1

Source: literature/stacks-project/sites.tex; Tag 03A1 (lemma-special-equivalence), including prescribed epimorphic covers; finite-limit closure and module/slice identifications written here

Cited node statements to reuse: lem-weil-pbc-site-sheaf-sets-limits-sectionwise, lem-weil-pbc-sheaf-subcategory-comparison, lem-sf-exact-functor-derived-triangulated.

Choose a small site $\mathcal S$ presenting $\mathcal T$. Starting with its sheafified representables and $1,U,V$, close a set of chosen objects under finite limits, by adjoining the limits of all finite diagrams at each of countably many stages. Such limits exist by [lem-weil-pbc-site-sheaf-sets-limits-sectionwise]; each stage is a set since the sheaf category has set-valued Hom sets. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-sheaf-subcategory-comparison] to the resulting full subcategory $\mathcal E$, retaining the epimorphic families $\{C\times U,C\times V\}\to C$. They are epimorphic because $U\cup V=1$, and local surjectivity is preserved by pullback. This gives a small subcanonical site with $1,U,V,W$ represented by its objects. Under the nerve equivalence, a ring or module is sent to its sheaf of generalized sections $C\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}(C,-)$; its operations are the induced operations on these sections. The inverse equivalence recovers the operations, since an equivalence preserves finite products and their diagrams of identities. Thus the ring and module categories are identified, including the slice categories as follows. A sheaf $F$ on $\mathcal E/Z$ corresponds to the sheaf $H(C)=\coprod_{s:C\to Z}F(C,s)$ over $h_Z$, with projection $(s,x)\mapsto s$. This is a sheaf: a matching family first glues its $s$ coordinates in $h_Z$, then glues its $x$ coordinates in $F$ on the localized site. Conversely take the fibre over $s$ of a sheaf $H\to h_Z$; the two constructions are inverse sectionwise. Ring and module operations are the same operations in each fibre, identifying modules over the restricted ring in both descriptions. These are additive exact equivalences: their inverse functors preserve kernels and cokernels by their universal properties. They induce derived equivalences by [lem-sf-exact-functor-derived-triangulated], so it suffices to work on $\mathcal E$ and its localized sites.
