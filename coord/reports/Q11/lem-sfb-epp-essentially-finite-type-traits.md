I checked the statement of every node cited below and copied it verbatim into citations.json. In particular the integral-closure localization node actually states the equality of the localized closure with the closure after localization; it is not just a definition of localization. The field perfectness and separating-basis nodes do not claim arbitrary residue fields are algebraic: they apply only after finite generation or algebraicity has been checked.

1. The normalization-structure node supplies Noetherian branch rings, maximal contractions, local DVRs and finite generation. I use case (b) only for the original essentially finite type D to T and with Noetherian lower closure; for P=F that closure is D. The separable-field-extension definition explicitly includes transcendental separability, which is necessary here.
2. The weak perfect-residue Epp node applies because characteristic zero makes the lower residue field perfect and Step 1 gives finite generation. The normalization-structure node supplies finite lower residue extensions and finitely generated upper residue extensions. The perfect-field separating-basis node applies in characteristic zero, and separably-generated-separable identifies its conclusion with the separability notion used for strong solutions. No residue-finiteness assertion is inferred merely from essential finite type T/D.
3. The perfect-residue tower node gives a DVR D' and a perfect residue field; its integral closure is therefore Noetherian even though P/F may be infinite. The normalization-structure node then gives finitely many V_a with finitely generated residue fields. Weak Epp applies independently to these DVR pairs WITHOUT assuming V_a is essentially of finite type over D'. The finite-extension case of the structure node gives finite residue extensions after N_a/P. Algebraic-extension-perfect therefore applies to the lower residues, and finite generation over these residues follows from the finite upper residue extensions and the previous finite generation. The separating-basis and separably-generated-separable nodes now supply the missing separability. This is where weak solutions become strong; it is not a permanence claim for weak solutions.
4. Common-extension-fields provides embeddings over the common base; compositum-degree-bound gives finite binary composita. Multiplying finite spanning sets verifies transitivity of finite degree. The new base-change statement assumes index one and separable residue, exactly what Step 3 proved, and allows every finite extension, including inseparable ones. I checked the normalized objects: after tensoring a quotient by a nil ideal its kernel is still a nil ideal, so passing to reduction gives the same algebra. The integral-transitive node and the integral-closure-localization node identify the iterated local normalizations. Thus the permanence statement really applies to the branches of the common compositum, not to an unrelated tensor algebra. The finitely many fields need not be separable or Galois.
5. The normalization-structure statement gives a Noetherian lower closure for the finite extension P_1/P (use D' to D' if a DVR pair is desired); transitivity identifies this with the closure over D. Its case (b), still applied to D to T, gives the upper Noetherian closure and finite generation. Localization-of-closure identifies each branch with Step 4's pairs. The new finite-stage-descent node has these algebraic solution properties as hypotheses, so it is not the initial Epp assertion relabelled. It produces a finite subextension of P_1/F only after all those hypotheses have been proved. Algebraicity of the tower follows by putting the finitely many coefficients of each algebraic equation into a finite subextension.
6. For each arbitrary further M/F_1, the structure node supplies actual DVR branches and maximal contractions. The new local base-change node takes the strong solution at F_1 as hypothesis, so its application is justified. I checked the same reduced-tensor, transitive-integral and localized-closure identifications as in Step 4; this expands the details omitted by 0GLR without creating a circular solution-goes-up assertion. The resulting equality is with the actual maximal ideal of (B_M)_q. No completion, excellence or field separability is inserted.
7. The structure node gives finite generation of the residue field k_q over k_M. Clause (2) of the existing separable-field-extension definition can therefore be applied to the whole extension; it yields the separably-generated hypothesis required by the existing separably-generated-tensor-reduced node. That node explicitly allows every reduced base algebra, so a field Omega qualifies. It states reducedness of exactly the tensor product in the item.

I tested the new inputs on the identity DVR extension and trivial base extension. For a nonperfect characteristic-p residue field the tower allows infinitely many p-basis elements and does not assert finite degree; with perfect residue the identity tower works. For D=F_p[[t]] and T=F_p[[u]], t maps to u^p, the required base extension may be inseparable and the reduced tensor algebra, rather than the unreduced tensor algebra, is indispensable. The permanence node requires index one initially, so it is not wrongly applied before Epp. For T=D[s] localized at the prime generated by a uniformizer, the residue extension is k(s)/k, genuinely transcendental; this checks that algebraic separability or algebraic formal unramifiedness was not silently substituted. Finite-stage descent assumes an existing algebraic solution and finitely generated residue fields, and does not assert descent for arbitrary infinite residue extensions.
