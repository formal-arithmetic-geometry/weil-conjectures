### Old step 1 → lem-a1-affine-split-morphism-positive-rank-pieces

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-finite-locally-free-degree-locally-constant] to $s:R\to U$. Write $d(u)=\dim_{\kappa(u)}\Gamma(R\times_{s,U}\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(u),\mathcal O)$. Existing tools: lem-weil-pbc-finite-locally-free-degree-locally-constant.

### Old step 2 → lem-a1-affine-split-morphism-positive-rank-pieces

The identity satisfies $s\circ e=\mathrm{id}_U$. Thus each fibre of $s$ has a $\kappa(u)$-point: $e$ base-changes to a section of that fibre over $\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(u)$. Evaluation at this point gives a unital algebra map from its coordinate algebra to $\kappa(u)$, whose composite with the scalar inclusion is the identity. Its coordinate algebra therefore contains a one-dimensional copy of $\kappa(u)$, so its dimension is at least one. Consequently $U_0=\varnothing$. This uses precisely the fibre-algebra meaning of degree in [def-pc-finite-locally-free].

Existing tools: def-pc-finite-locally-free.

### Old step 3 → lem-a1-groupoid-source-pullback-translation

Write $P=R\times_{s,U,t}R$ and $Q=R\times_{s,U,s}R$, with second projections to $R$. Here $c(a,b)=a\circ b$. The maps $F:P\to Q$, $(a,b)\mapsto(c(a,b),b)$, and $G:Q\to P$, $(d,b)\mapsto(c(d,i(b)),b)$, are scheme morphisms by [def-pc-fibre-product]: the required equalities are $s(c(a,b))=s(b)$ for $F$, and for $G$ first $s(d)=s(b)=t(i(b))$, so that $c(d,i(b))$ is defined, and then $s(c(d,i(b)))=s(i(b))=t(b)$, so that $(c(d,i(b)),b)$ lies in $P$. The groupoid identities give $G F(a,b)=((a\circ b)\circ b^{-1},b)=(a,b)$ and $F G(d,b)=((d\circ b^{-1})\circ b,b)=(d,b)$. These are identities of morphisms, since they follow from the associativity, inverse and identity identities of the given structure maps. Thus $P\cong Q$ over $R$. This is the two lower cartesian squares of the source, with their comparison map and inverse written out.

Existing tools: def-pc-fibre-product.

### Old step 4 → lem-a1-groupoid-source-pullback-translation

The projection $P\to R$ is the base change of $s$ along $t$, whereas $Q\to R$ is the base change of $s$ along $s$. Clause (2) of [lem-weil-pbc-finite-flat-rank-iso] therefore gives their ranks at $z\in R$ as $d(t(z))$ and $d(s(z))$. The isomorphism over $R$ in step 3 identifies their fibres and hence their coordinate algebras, so these dimensions are equal. Thus $s^{-1}(U_r)=t^{-1}(U_r)$ as open subsets, hence as open subschemes. Set this common open subscheme equal to $R_r$. The restriction $s_r:R_r\to U_r$ already has degree $r$ by step 1.

Existing tools: lem-weil-pbc-finite-flat-rank-iso.

### Old step 5 → lem-a1-affine-split-morphism-positive-rank-pieces

By [lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs], $U$ is quasi-compact. Its open cover by the $U_r$ has a finite subcover. Since the $U_r$ are pairwise disjoint, every stratum outside that subcover is empty. The set $I=\{r\geq1:U_r\ne\varnothing\}$ is therefore finite, and $U=\coprod_{r\in I}U_r$. If $U=\varnothing$, this definition gives $I=\varnothing$.

Existing tools: lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs.

### Old step 6 → lem-a1-affine-split-morphism-positive-rank-pieces

For each $r\in I$, [lem-weil-pbc-spec-clopen-idempotent] applied to $U_r\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A$ gives an idempotent $a_r\in A$ with $U_r=D(a_r)$. Clause (0) of [lem-weil-pbc-affine-ideals-closed-subscheme] says that this principal open is an affine open subscheme, proving the required affineness of $U_r$. The subset $R_r=s^{-1}(U_r)$ is open and closed in $R=\operatorname{Spec}B$, since $s$ is continuous; applying the same two cited statements to $B$ shows that $R_r$ is affine as well.

Existing tools: lem-weil-pbc-spec-clopen-idempotent, lem-weil-pbc-affine-ideals-closed-subscheme.

### Old step 7 → lem-a1-groupoid-invariant-open-restriction-degree

The maps restrict to the indicated open subschemes: $s\circ e=\mathrm{id}$ puts $e(U_r)$ in $R_r$; $s\circ i=t$ and the equality of the two inverse images put $i(R_r)$ in $R_r$; and $s(c(a,b))=s(b)$, the source of the second factor $b$ (step 3), puts the image of $R_r\times_{s_r,U_r,t_r}R_r$ under $c$ in $R_r$. Factoring through these open subschemes is restriction of the continuous map and its structure-sheaf map, as in [def-weil-open-immersion]. All groupoid identities restrict because the open inclusions are injective on morphisms. The restricted inverse is involutive and satisfies $s_r\circ i_r=t_r$, so it is an isomorphism from $t_r$ to $s_r$ over $U_r$. Its fibre algebra isomorphisms preserve dimensions, and its isomorphism of morphisms preserves the finite, flat and locally finitely presented conditions defining [def-pc-finite-locally-free]. Hence $t_r$ too is finite locally free of degree $r$.

Existing tools: def-weil-open-immersion, def-pc-finite-locally-free.
