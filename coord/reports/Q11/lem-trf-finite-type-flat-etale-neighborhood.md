### Returned step 1 -> Assembly step 1 (routine affine, zero-support and induction setup)

Restrict to affine neighbourhoods of $x$ and $s$. If $x\notin\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal F_s)$, then $\mathcal F_x/\mathfrak m_s\mathcal F_x=0$. The stalk $\mathcal F_x$ is finite over the local ring $\mathcal O_{X,x}$, and $\mathfrak m_s\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is contained in its maximal ideal. Nakayama gives $\mathcal F_x=0$. Apply the surjective-on-stalk lemma to $0\to\mathcal F$: it is surjective on a neighbourhood, so $\mathcal F$ vanishes there. The identity neighbourhood of $S$ then works, since the zero module has an empty finite presentation and is flat. Otherwise put $n=\dim_x\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal F_s)$ and use strong induction on $n\ge0$. This is finite: the affine fibre is of finite type over $\kappa(s)$, hence has finite dimension, and its support has dimension at most that of the fibre.

### Returned step 7 -> lem-trf-generic-free-cokernel-dimension-drop

The module $Q$ is finite, and $(Q/\mathfrak mQ)_{\mathfrak p}=0$: reduce the chosen basis isomorphism at $\mathfrak p$ and use right exactness of tensor products. Its support is a proper closed subset of the integral $n$-dimensional closed fibre. Here closedness can be checked directly: for a finite generating list, vanishing at a prime means a product of denominators outside that prime kills every generator; hence support equals the zero locus of its annihilator. Every chain of irreducible closed subsets of this proper closed subset extends by appending the whole integral fibre, so its length is at most $n-1$. ### Returned step 9 -> lem-trf-small-support-cokernel-induction

Put $R=B_g$. The module $R$ is flat over $A$ because $D(g)\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is a restriction of the smooth morphism, and $Q_g$ is $A$-flat. To prove $N_g$ flat, let $U\hookrightarrow V$ be any injection of $A$-modules. The flat-quotient tensor lemma gives exact rows $0\to U\otimes_A R^r\to U\otimes_A N_g\to U\otimes_A Q_g\to0$ and the analogous row for $V$. The outer vertical maps are injective by flatness. An element in the kernel of the middle vertical map maps to zero on the right, hence comes from the left; injectivity on the left and exactness of the lower row make that lift zero. Thus the middle map is injective, which is precisely $A$-flatness of $N_g$.

### Returned step 10 -> lem-trf-small-support-cokernel-induction

For finite presentation of $N_g$, choose $R^a\to R^b\to Q_g\to0$. Lift the $b$ generators to $N_g$ and adjoin the $r$ generators of its submodule $R^r$. For each of the $a$ generating relations of $Q_g$, its lifted combination lies in $R^r$; subtract its unique expression in that basis. These $a$ relations generate the kernel of the resulting surjection $R^{b+r}\to N_g$: any kernel vector first projects to a relation of $Q_g$, so subtract a combination of the chosen lifted relations, leaving a relation in the injected free submodule, hence zero. This is a finite presentation of $N_g$. Its associated sheaf on $D(g)$ therefore has a finite presentation.
