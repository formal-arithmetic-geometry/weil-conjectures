The maximizing coefficient is nonzero, so division by it and the selected value representative is legal. Maximality puts every normalized term in the upper valuation ring. Equality of norms for a different coset would force their quotient to be a base-field norm, so those terms have strictly small norm and reduce to zero. Within the selected column the coefficients lie in the lower valuation ring and one equals one, giving a genuinely nonzero residue relation. This proof does not require the quotient value group to be ordered, or a discrete value group. Normalizing individual terms avoids an unstated theorem about norms of sums with distinct dominant terms.
