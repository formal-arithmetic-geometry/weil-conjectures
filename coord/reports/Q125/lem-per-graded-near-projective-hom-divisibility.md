## Source error

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the literal ideal-product notation in Stacks local-cohomology.tex, Tag 0GAA (lemma-vanishing-coh-almost-projective), parts (4)-(5), is false. The correct input to the source's argument is the image of the canonical map from an ideal tensor product, not the product submodule.

Take R=k[t], K=(t), n>=1, c=0, and M=R/(t^n). The blowup of this principal Cartier ideal is Spec R, Y_n=Spec(R/(t^n)), and the restricted line module O(1) is free of rank one. Here d=0 and the source's q_0=0. For r=n, the displayed surjection K^r M -> H^0(Y_n,p^*M(r)) would be a surjection 0 -> R/(t^n), which is false. The module is free of rank one over R/K^n, so even the strongest near-projective hypothesis holds. In contrast K^r tensor_R M is isomorphic to R/(t^n) and gives the required section map.

The source's actual proof takes sections of a finite free presentation. Its displayed source is (K^r/K^(n+r))^h, and mapping its coordinate vectors produces finite sums of coefficient tensors. Thus its proof gives image containment for K^r tensor_R N. That is precisely the supporting node's statement. In the main proof, tau and tau_j are tensors. Evaluation b tensor lambda -> (z -> b*lambda(z) modulo K^(n+r)) is balanced and independent of lifts, so this correction yields exactly the item's original Hom-divisibility conclusion. No item statement was weakened or corrected, and no citer needs a new statement. The new section-bound node's source field explicitly records the tensor correction for its future worker.

There is also a direction typo in the source's introductory sentence before lemma-annihilated: with O_X(1)=O_X(-Y), the canonical map is O_X(1)->O_X, and decreasing twists are induced by its powers. The nodes consistently use that direction. The double-twist lemma requires no injectivity after restricting to Y_n.

For the new section-bound node I read 0GA6's construction of d and q from Proj of the associated graded ring and 0GA7(1)-(2) on exceptional thickenings, followed by all cases of 0GAA. The integer d can be enlarged to max(1,actual fibre dimension), so h=d-1 is the loss when the actual dimension is >=2 and is zero in dimensions 0 and 1. The source's high-degree argument inducts on cohomology and uses a finite free presentation and its near-projective kernel. Its H0 consequence gives a preimage in sections of that free presentation, hence in K^v tensor N. It does not give a preimage in the product submodule K^vN, and that error was corrected before writing the node. The natural theta maps are canonical multiplication maps, and their compatibility is the formula x -> x for decreasing twists and x -> ax for multiplication by a. Their invariance requires degree-preserving unital graded isomorphisms, which the statement explicitly includes. K=R gives Y_n empty and all quotient modules zero; K=0 gives empty blowup and q>=1 unless R=0; nilpotent K gives empty blowup and eventual zero graded pieces. These are allowed, not excluded by a nonempty-scheme hypothesis.

For the dual-comparison node I read 0GAB and 0G96. Noetherianity is needed to ensure the dual is finite: from a finite presentation of M it is a submodule of a finite free module over the Noetherian ring A. Dualizing gf=a gives f^dual g^dual=a on N through the same finite free module. The comparison is an isomorphism on finite free modules, and its functoriality under these two maps makes a kill its kernel and cokernel for every a in J. This works on every affine open of any Z over Spec A, requires no flatness, and yields the claimed sheaf ideal annihilator. Evaluation on b tensor lambda is exactly b*lambda(m), as explicitly included in the statement. In particular the node does not assume M is locally free and does not take an unproved flat base-change theorem for granted. The cases J=0, M=0 and a=0 impose only the asserted zero scalar condition; J=A forces actual finite projectivity and makes the comparison an isomorphism.

For the reverse-map node I checked the omitted detail in 0GAC, then the existing algebraic scalar node, and verified the extra sheaf gluing. Locally trivialize L and write s as a scalar l. Given y in G, annihilation of the cokernel means that locally there is x with alpha(x)=l^c*y. Define the local answer to be l^c*x. A different x differs by a kernel section and gives the same answer because l^c kills the kernel. This independence also proves additivity, module linearity, and compatibility on restrictions; lifting only locally suffices because the answers glue uniquely. If a line basis is changed to u times itself, the scalar l becomes ul and the local answer becomes u^(2c) times the old answer. These are exactly the transformation factors of G tensor L^(2c), so they glue globally to beta. Taking y=alpha(z) and choosing x=l^c*z verifies the second composite identity; the first follows from alpha(l^c*x)=l^(2c)*y. This detail is more than a scalar calculation in one ring and deserves a separate node instead of hiding a globalization lemma in the item. It does not claim uniqueness among all morphisms satisfying the two identities. It works when s=0, when s is a zero divisor, and when c=0 (then alpha is an isomorphism). All three new nodes contain statements only, with their mathematical arguments kept here rather than in their proof fields.
