Step 2 uses that same exact module functor. Strict perfectness is preserved because finite sums and the equation exhibiting each summand are preserved, and cohomology approximation follows from step 1. This few-line argument is marked elementary and stays in the item's proof instead of creating a pullback node. Covers by Zariski opens remain covers after base change to any small etale object. Step 3 uses lem-weil-pbc-etale-morphism-open-map for the precise open-image assertion. The affine open is quasi-compact, so the open images of approximation objects have a finite subcover. Their different upper bounds have a maximum only after this finite selection. Step 1 detects vanishing above that bound. Thus there is no illicit global boundedness assumption or infinite maximum. Empty affine opens require no approximations and were explicitly excluded from this selection as vacuous.

Step 4 sets up the induction simultaneously for all schemes and complexes with a chosen bound. The measure is a nonnegative integer. The base N<m uses the zero strictly perfect complex and checks the required cohomology maps directly. E=0 therefore needs no largest nonzero degree. This is an elementary use of the definition.

Step 5 uses the new ringed-site top-cohomology node with index n, not blindly with m. Monotonicity from m to n is checked in the text: at n>m the existing approximation is already an isomorphism; at n=m it is surjective. The bound above n is the induction hypothesis on the object, not an additional assumption on the theorem. The new finite-type detection node then applies to the arbitrary sheaf H^n(E); the existing fpqc finite-type node would require quasi-coherence and cannot replace it. Finite type gives a finite set of generators after a Zariski cover.

Step 6 uses lem-sf-site-finite-projective-lift, whose statement explicitly lifts a finite-free source across any epi of module sheaves after a site cover. It applies to Z^n(A)→H^n(A) on the Zariski site; classical module sheafification and its local-surjectivity characterization are available on this site and its slices. A finite basis gives a finite common refinement. The lifted map lands in cycles, so its differential is zero and it really is a chain map. The derived long-exact-cohomology node explicitly states exactness for any abelian derived category. It shows C has zero cohomology in every degree at least n: at n one uses q surjective and H^{n+1}(P)=0, and above n all neighbours vanish. This fills the source's details-omitted sentence rather than treating it as a black box.

Step 7 uses part (1) and then part (2) of the new ringed-site triangle node. The first input P is strictly perfect in a single degree, hence is a-pseudo-coherent for every a via its identity; in particular the m+1 hypothesis is satisfied after etale pullback. C has bound n-1, so the induction measure decreases by one. The induction is applied on each of the further open neighborhoods already chosen, and the resulting approximations compose over covers. The second triangle implication uses P and C to conclude for E. The shifts here agree literally with 08FV. The generic ring-only cone node and the scheme all-m extension lemma do not supply these fixed-m ringed-site clauses.

Step 8 simply quantifies the already proved equivalence over m, using the definition of pseudo-coherence. It adds no compatibility demand between approximation choices for different m.
