### Returned step 1

Apply [lem-trf-fppf-sheaf-coproduct-partition] to the underlying fppf sheaves of the $F_i$, supplied by [def-trf-algebraic-space]. This gives the sheaf coproduct $F$ and injective coprojections $c_i:F_i\to F$. For every section $a\in F(T)$, write $T=\coprod_iT_i(a)$ for its clopen partition and $a_i\in F_i(T_i(a))$ for its component sections. The cartesian-square clause identifies $h_{T_i(a)}$ with $h_T\times_FF_i$, and these identifications persist under every scheme test. We will use these actual scheme partitions, rather than the generally false identity $F(T)=\coprod_iF_i(T)$.

### Returned step 2

Test $\Delta_F$ on a scheme $T$ with sections $a,b\in F(T)$. Set $T_{ij}=T_i(a)\cap T_j(b)$ with its open subscheme structure. For $i=j$, the component sections $a_i,b_i$ on $T_{ii}$ give a scheme $E_i=h_{T_{ii}}\times_{F_i\times F_i}F_i$, by the representable diagonal in [def-trf-algebraic-space]. Form the scheme $E=\coprod_iE_i$ with its evident map to $T$ [lem-weil-pbc-scheme-coproduct-family]. On a test $Z\to T$, equality $a|_Z=b|_Z$ forces their partitions to agree, by uniqueness in [lem-trf-fppf-sheaf-coproduct-partition]. Thus the opens $Z\times_TT_{ij}$ with $i\ne j$ are empty, and $Z_i=Z\times_TT_{ii}$ cover $Z$. On $Z_i$, injectivity of $c_i$ makes equality equivalent to equality of the two $F_i$ sections, hence to a unique map $Z_i\to E_i$. These maps glue uniquely to $Z\to E$ by [lem-weil-pbc-scheme-morphism-gluing]. Conversely a map $Z\to E$ gives this equality on its inverse images of the summands, hence globally by the sheaf condition [def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology]. The two assignments are inverse and commute with restriction. Therefore $E$ represents $h_T\times_{F\times F}F$, and $\Delta_F$ is representable.

### Returned step 3

Let $U=\coprod_iU_i$ be the scheme of the hypothesis. The coproduct lemma [lem-weil-pbc-scheme-coproduct-family] says its summands form a disjoint clopen open covering. The atlas maps $p_i:h_{U_i}\to F_i$ and the inclusions $c_i$ give sections $c_i(p_i)\in F(U_i)$. They agree on overlaps, which are empty; a sheaf has a unique section over the empty scheme by its empty-cover condition [def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology]. Glue them to $p\in F(U)$, equivalently a natural map $p:h_U\to F$ (on $Z\to U$, take the restriction of $p$). Its restriction to $U_i$ is $c_ip_i$. The hypothesis that $U$ belongs to the chosen big site ensures that this is an allowed scheme atlas candidate.

### Returned step 4

For a scheme $T$ and $a:h_T\to F$, use its partition $T_i=T_i(a)$ from Step 1. Because $p_i$ is representable surjective etale [def-trf-algebraic-space], $V_i=h_{T_i}\times_{F_i}h_{U_i}$ is a scheme and $V_i\to T_i$ is surjective etale. Put $V=\coprod_iV_i$, a scheme by [lem-weil-pbc-scheme-coproduct-family]. For any $Z\to T$, a section of $h_T\times_Fh_U$ is a map $u:Z\to U$ with $pu=a|_Z$. The inverse images $u^{-1}(U_i)$ are the partition attached to $pu$, since $p|_{U_i}=c_ip_i$. Uniqueness in [lem-trf-fppf-sheaf-coproduct-partition] identifies them with $Z\times_TT_i$. On each piece the datum is exactly a map to $V_i$, since $c_i$ is injective on sections. Conversely maps from these pieces to $V_i$ give a map to $U$ satisfying $pu=a|_Z$. The piecewise maps to $V_i$ glue uniquely to $Z\to V$ by [lem-weil-pbc-scheme-morphism-gluing], and maps to $V$ recover precisely these pieces because $V_i$ maps into $T_i$. These inverse natural constructions show $h_V\simeq h_T\times_Fh_U$. Thus $p$ is representable.

### Returned step 5

The projection $V\to T$ in Step 4 is surjective: any point $t\in T$ belongs to a unique $T_i$, and surjectivity of $V_i\to T_i$ gives a point above it. It is etale by the affine-chart characterization recorded in [def-weil-etale-morphism]: at any $v\in V_i$, choose affine etale charts for $V_i\to T_i$. Since $V_i\subset V$ and $T_i\subset T$ are open, these are the same affine charts, with the same ring homomorphism, for $V\to T$. Hence the etale-at-every-point definition holds for $V\to T$. This works for every $T\to F$, so $p:h_U\to F$ is representable surjective etale in the precise sense of [def-trf-algebraic-space]. Steps 1, 2 and 4 verify the sheaf, diagonal and atlas requirements, proving that $F$ is an algebraic space. If $I$ is empty, the partition lemma forces every test $T\to F$ to be empty, and the same construction has $U=V=\varnothing$; surjectivity and etaleness are vacuous. Empty individual summands cause no change.
