## Problems found and corrections

The unsplit item's statement correctly reproduces Milne Theorem 4.27 together with Remark 4.30. There is no statement dispute and no change to its hypotheses or conclusion. The important pitfall is inclusion versus equality for a direct summand over an arbitrary algebra: a block coefficient calculation first yields preservation by inclusion; it must also be applied to the inverse. Steps 4 and 6 explicitly include this inverse argument. In step 6 the scalar multiplying a generator of the preserved line is a unit because the restriction is bijective, not merely because it is the coefficient of a nonzero vector. Neither argument invokes reducedness, algebraic closure, or smoothness.

Milne's displayed expansion in the proof of Lemma 4.28 is rendered as `w wedge w` in the extracted PDF text, although the input and the immediately preceding/following formulas make clear that the expression is `w wedge v`. The split writes the correct expression explicitly. This is not a mathematical statement change.

## Review by Q146

I read Milne, Algebraic Groups, Chapter 4(a), printed pp.83–84, Proposition 4.7 on p.86, the regular representation on p.87, Theorem 4.27 and Lemma 4.28 on pp.94–95, and Remark 4.30 on p.96 in `literature/milne-algebraic-groups_iAG2017.pdf`. These establish the intended route: choose a finite stable space containing ideal generators, recover the ideal from off-diagonal coefficients using the counit, and use a top exterior power. Remark 4.30 expressly requires every k-algebra, not just geometric points. There are no new-node sources to audit. I also read the chapter/unit records, the three fixed/free route levels in SETUP, GOAL, the stage-1 brief, and delivery-errors B–E in full. The generic coalgebra input is stronger than the proposition used in Milne but carries out exactly the same regular-subrepresentation step, without changing the unit route.

For step 1 I opened `def-weil-closed-immersion`, which explicitly gives the affine quotient C/I, and `lem-pc-affine-finite-type-algebra`, which explicitly gives finite generation of the global coordinate algebra for an affine finite-type scheme over a field. Its affine and finite-type hypotheses are precisely those on G. I opened `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian` and checked its Noetherian base hypothesis: k satisfies it since its only ideals are zero and the unit ideal. `def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring` explicitly says every ideal is finitely generated, so it supplies ideal generators, not just algebra generators. The affine-coordinate lemma belongs to another unit and is declared as a generic tool in split_report; it does not replace a geometric step of this unit.

For step 2 I opened the full statements of `lem-sfb-affine-group-coordinate-hopf`, `lem-sfb-coalgebra-finite-subcoalgebra`, and `lem-sfb-rational-representation-comodule-dictionary`. The first gives exactly the coalgebra identities of C needed by the second. The second allows a finite subset, not just one element, and gives Delta(V) contained in V tensor V, hence also V tensor C. Including 1 ensures V is nonzero, even when I is zero. The dictionary explicitly covers arbitrary-dimensional vector spaces, gives the action on every algebra by evaluation of the second tensor factor, and identifies finite-dimensional natural representations with group-scheme homomorphisms. This is why no G(k)-density claim or characteristic-zero assumption is used.

For step 3 I re-read `def-sc-group-scheme-subgroup` and the Hopf statement: H's multiplication and identity are compatible with G's, so restricting both factors of Delta(e_j) to H gives zero, and restricting e_j to the identity gives zero. After setting W=I intersect V, the quotient images of a complement basis of W are independent in C/I. Applying their extended coordinate functionals to the displayed tensor equation proves q(a_ij)=0. This is the actual coefficient argument, not an invocation of a missing Hopf-ideal classification. The counit formula from the cited Hopf statement then expresses every W-basis vector in the ideal of off-diagonal coefficients. Since W contains the chosen generators, it generates I. Both ideal inclusions are written in the item. This argument uses no radical of I.

For step 4 the dictionary supplies the matrix entries over A, the affine-quotient clause of the closed-immersion definition supplies the factorization C to C/I, and the subgroup definition supplies g inverse in H(A). The equations first say r(g) carries W_A into W_A. The same equations for g inverse yield equality. Conversely equality implies all the off-diagonal entries vanish and hence x(I)=0. Thus the cited statements give exactly the conclusions taken from them; no definition of stabilizer is being used as a substitute for its equations.

For step 5 I read `def-weil-pbc-exterior-algebra` and `lem-weil-pbc-exterior-power-free-basis`. The quotient by squares permits each linear automorphism to descend, and its inverse descends too. The ordered wedge bases give both finite dimension and scalar extension compatibility by sending an explicit basis bijectively to a basis. Exterior matrix entries are obtained by the alternating expansion of pure wedges, so they are polynomial minors of the original matrix entries. Thus the construction is natural over all A, and the finite-dimensional clause of the cited dictionary gives an actual group-scheme homomorphism, not merely an abstract representation of G(k).

For step 6 the free-basis statement is applied over the commutative ring A, not just over k. It identifies the annihilator of wedge multiplication by w with W_A by the displayed coefficients. If the top-wedge line is preserved with equality, the induced map on the free rank-one summand is bijective, so its multiplier is a unit even over a nonreduced ring. The equation in the item then gives inclusion for alpha and alpha inverse, hence equality. This is the elementary basis calculation called elementary by Milne itself, spelled out for our chosen V and W. It creates no hidden non-elementary input and deserves no separate DAG node under the brief's elementary-node rule. Step 7 uses the explicitly stated functoriality of extension of scalars in `def-weil-module-base-change` and entrywise evaluation of the same coefficient functions to establish naturality, rather than asserting unexplained transport.

Small cases checked: H=G gives I=W=0, d=0, E=k and the full stabilizer; H=1 in G_a gives V=span(1,t), W=kt and the equation g=0; in characteristic p, H=alpha_p inside G_a gives V=span(1,t^p), W=kt^p and the exact equation g^p=0 on arbitrary A, including dual numbers. The last example would fail if one replaced scheme stabilizers by geometric-point stabilizers. The exterior test also works for W=V, rank-zero W and the zero test algebra. A nonzero generator of a line need not stay nonzero over the zero algebra, but its free-module coordinate calculation and automorphism restriction still make sense there. No use of a dimension count over A is hidden. Every step is below 2000 characters.
