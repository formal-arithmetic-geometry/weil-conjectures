A geometric torus is connected by the Laurent-domain argument; the open-factorization lemma then puts it in this identity subgroup as an actual scheme morphism. Restricting its original closed immersion to the open subgroup leaves a closed immersion. Maximality in the ambient group implies maximality there. Thus every hypothesis of the classical conjugacy node, including algebraic closedness and connectedness of the group, holds. I also read Milne Theorem 17.10 and its proof on printed pp.354–355 (PDF pages 366–367). Its output, and the node's explicit scheme-theoretic clause, provide a point of the transporter over every geometric point of S. We never assume G itself connected.

For step 6, the matrix-sheaf node states the correspondence for morphisms over **any scheme**, with arbitrary base change, composition and automorphisms; it is not restricted to reduced bases. Geometric maximality gives an isomorphism of the two torus fibres at every geometric point of Z. On a nonempty constant-matrix open, take a geometric point: the inverse fibre morphism corresponds to an integer matrix inverse. That same integer inverse works on the entire open by the composition law. This justifies equality on all nilpotent test algebras; geometric equality of arbitrary closed subschemes would not justify it. Rank zero gives the empty square identity matrix. If the ranks originally differed on a nonempty splitting member, step 5 would force them equal; we do not assume equality in advance. No new rigidity node is needed for this few-line specialization of the matrix-sheaf statement.
