## Proof in words

1. For every sheaf F, the given adjunction and the general Yoneda evaluation node identify Hom(Lh_V,F), F(uV), and Hom(h_uV,F). If r:h_uV→F, the adjunct of r c_V evaluates at id_V to r(id_uV). Hence under that bijection precomposition with c_V is the inverse bijection, not just an unspecified isomorphism of Hom sets.
2. Surjectivity of precomposition at F=Lh_V gives t with t c_V=id. Injectivity at F=h_uV gives c_V t=id. This proves isomorphy of the specified c_V. The specified adjunct is beta_V, whose identity value is u(id_V)=id_uV.
3. The adjuncts of the two naturality composites send b:W→V to u(ab) and u(a)u(b), respectively. These are equal by functoriality, so the original composites agree under the adjunction bijection.
4. For a finite-limit cone T→V_j, a compatible family of transformations A→h_Vj sends each section of A(W) to a compatible family of arrows W→V_j. The universal property gives a unique arrow W→T, naturally in W; thus the represented cone is a limit in sheaves. Apply this also in D, and use the existing node's finite-limit preservation for L and the hypothesis for u. The naturality equation identifies all legs, so uniqueness identifies the canonical cone comparisons themselves. For the empty diagram this is the unique isomorphism between terminal objects.
5. Specialize those comparisons to the terminal object and binary product. Naturality for multiplication, unit and inverse gives preservation of the corresponding operations under c_G. Functoriality and these product comparisons preserve the equations defining a group object. The inverse of c_G respects the same operations by cancellation. Naturality for any represented homomorphism then gives the required homomorphism-compatibility square.
