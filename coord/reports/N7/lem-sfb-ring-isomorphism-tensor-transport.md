## Complete item proof

1. Put $C=C_c$ and $B=C_{c^{-1}}$. By restriction of scalars, $r\in R(U)$ acts on $CM(U)$ as $c_U(r)$, while $s\in S(U)$ acts on $BF(U)$ as $c_U^{-1}(s)$. Consequently $BCM\cong M$ and $CBF\cong F$ by the identity on underlying abelian sheaves, naturally on all module morphisms. These identities obey the triangle identities. Both functors preserve addition of morphisms. They preserve limits and colimits by their universal properties: the natural bijections $\operatorname{Hom}_R(CM,F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_S(M,BF)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_R(F,CM)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_S(BF,M)$ identify cones and cocones and their unique factorizations. In particular both functors are exact and preserve all small direct sums. The site-size hypothesis ensures that the module categories are abelian.

Uses: def-pc-sheaf-restrict-scalars, def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category.

2. For every $U$, define $t_U:C(M(U)\otimes_{S(U)}N(U))\to CM(U)\otimes_{R(U)}CN(U)$ by $m\otimes n\mapsto m\otimes n$. It is well-defined: for $s\in S(U)$ write $s=c_U(r)$, so $(sm)\otimes n=(rm)\otimes n=m\otimes(rn)=m\otimes(sn)$ in the target. The same formula in the reverse direction is $R(U)$-balanced because $r$ acts through $c_U(r)$. Both maps are $R(U)$-linear and inverse on generators. They commute with restriction maps since $c$ and $c^{-1}$ are maps of ring sheaves. Restriction commutes with module sheafification: for any presheaf $P$ of $S$-modules and $R$-module sheaf $F$, maps $CP\to F$ correspond to maps $P\to BF$, hence uniquely to maps $P^\#\to BF$ and then to maps $C(P^\#)\to F$. Thus $C(P^\#)\cong(CP)^\#$ by the sheafification universal property. Sheafifying $t_U$ and its inverse gives the asserted natural tensor isomorphism $t_{M,N}$.

Uses: def-pc-sheaf-restrict-scalars, def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product, def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification.

3. Define $e:CS\to R$ by $e_U(s)=c_U^{-1}(s)$. It is $R$-linear since $c_U^{-1}(c_U(r)s)=r c_U^{-1}(s)$, and its inverse is $c$. The sheaf tensor constraints are induced by the sectionwise module constraints. In the associator diagram both composites send $(m\otimes n)\otimes z$ to $m\otimes(n\otimes z)$. In the symmetry diagram both send $m\otimes n$ to $n\otimes m$. In the left-unit diagram the path through $t$ and $e$ sends $s\otimes m$ to $c_U^{-1}(s)\cdot_Rm=sm$, which is the image under the original $S$-unitor; the right-unit check is $m\otimes s\mapsto sm$. Equalities on pure tensors imply equality on presheaf tensors by linearity, and on their sheafifications by uniqueness. This proves coherence, including the specified unit normalization.

Uses: lem-pc-sheaf-modules-tensor-closed, def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification.

4. Apply the exact-functor cohomology comparison to the exact additive functors $C,B$ of step 1. It gives $H^n(CK)\cong C(H^nK)$ and the analogous comparison for $B$, naturally in chain maps. Since $BC\cong\mathrm{id}$ and $CB\cong\mathrm{id}$, each functor preserves and reflects zero objects and isomorphisms. Thus each termwise functor preserves and reflects acyclicity and quasi-isomorphisms, with no boundedness condition.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-complex-cohomology, def-weil-abstract-derived-category.

5. Use preservation of direct sums from step 1 and $t$ from step 2 on every summand to define
$$t^n_{P,Q}:C\left(\bigoplus_{p+q=n}P^p\otimes_SQ^q\right)\xrightarrow{\sim}\bigoplus_{p+q=n}CP^p\otimes_RCQ^q.$$
On the $(p,q)$ summand it sends $x\otimes y$ to $x\otimes y$. The differential on both sides sends this tensor to $d_Px\otimes y+(-1)^p x\otimes d_Qy$; additivity of $C$ preserves the sign. Hence $t_{P,Q}$ is a natural chain isomorphism. The total associator is the sum-reindexing map $(x\otimes y)\otimes z\mapsto x\otimes(y\otimes z)$; the two bracketings have differential $d_P+(-1)^p d_Q+(-1)^{p+q}d_H$ on degree $(p,q,r)$. The total symmetry is $x\otimes y\mapsto(-1)^{pq}y\otimes x$: after differentiating its two terms have signs $(-1)^{pq}$ and $(-1)^{pq+q}$, the same as after applying symmetry to the differential. Thus these are chain maps and the scalar comparison commutes with them, with the unitors, and with their coherence diagrams by the summand checks of step 3. The identical construction works for $B$.

Uses: def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves, lem-pc-sheaf-modules-tensor-closed.

6. If $M$ is a flat $S$-module sheaf, let $0\to F_1\to F_2\to F_3\to0$ be any exact sequence of $R$-module sheaves. Apply the exact $B$, tensor with $M$, and apply the exact $C$. The result is exact; steps 1-2 identify its terms and maps with $F_i\otimes_R CM$. Thus $CM$ is flat. The same argument with $B$ proves the converse and proves preservation and reflection for both functors.

Uses: def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules.

7. Suppose $P$ is K-flat over $S$ and $F$ is any acyclic $R$-complex. Step 4 makes $BF$ acyclic, so $\operatorname{Tot}(BF\otimes_SP)$ is acyclic by K-flatness. Apply $C$ and steps 1 and 5 to identify its image with $\operatorname{Tot}(F\otimes_RCP)$, which is acyclic by step 4. Thus $CP$ is K-flat. Interchanging $C,B$ proves preservation in the reverse direction; the natural identities $BC\cong\mathrm{id}$ and $CB\cong\mathrm{id}$ then prove reflection. This quantifies over all acyclic complexes, not just those originally presented over the other ring.

Uses: def-pc-kflat-complex.

8. Fix either ring sheaf $T=R$ or $S$. If $P$ satisfies the acyclic-tensor definition, signed symmetry from step 5 also makes $\operatorname{Tot}(P\otimes_T A)$ acyclic for every acyclic $A$. For any quasi-isomorphism $f:X\to Y$, its cone is acyclic by the cone criterion. The tensor-cone isomorphism identifies $\operatorname{Cone}(1_P\otimes f)$ with $\operatorname{Tot}(P\otimes_T\operatorname{Cone}(f))$, which is acyclic. The cone criterion makes $1_P\otimes f$ a quasi-isomorphism, and signed symmetry gives the same for $f\otimes1_P$. Conversely, if both total tensor functors preserve quasi-isomorphisms, apply $-\otimes_TP$ to $0\to A$ for every acyclic $A$. Its image has zero source, so its being a quasi-isomorphism means that $\operatorname{Tot}(A\otimes_TP)$ is acyclic. This is exactly K-flatness in the acyclic-tensor definition, and proves its equivalence with the two-sided quasi-isomorphism convention.

Uses: def-pc-kflat-complex, def-sfb-preserves-kflat-quasi-iso, lem-pur-quasi-iso-cone-acyclic, lem-pc-tensor-mapping-cone-iso.

9. By step 4 the functor $Q_RC$ on complexes inverts all quasi-isomorphisms, so the localization universal property gives $D(C)$ with $D(C)Q_S\cong Q_RC$. Likewise it gives $D(B)$. The natural identities from step 1 descend, by full faithfulness of precomposition with localization, to $D(B)D(C)\cong\mathrm{id}$ and $D(C)D(B)\cong\mathrm{id}$. These specify the induced exact equivalence on unbounded derived categories. On homotopies termwise $C$ preserves $f-g=dh+hd$ by additivity, so these comparisons can equally be computed in the homotopy categories used below. Termwise $C$ commutes with shifts and sends the cone differential matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}d_Y&f\\0&-d_X\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ to the same matrix for $Cf$, using preservation of finite sums and negatives. Thus the descended equivalence preserves the distinguished cone triangles.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-localization-universal-property, def-weil-abstract-derived-category.

10. Choose flat-term K-flat resolutions $P\to K$ and $Q\to L$ over $S$, which exist for arbitrary unbounded complexes. By steps 4, 6 and 7 their $C$-images are flat-term K-flat resolutions of $CK,CL$. On such pairs use the canonical total-tensor models of derived tensor and define the comparison as
$$D(C)(Q_SP\otimes_S^{\mathbf L}Q_SQ)\cong Q_RC\operatorname{Tot}(P\otimes_SQ)\xrightarrow{Q_R(t_{P,Q})}Q_R\operatorname{Tot}(CP\otimes_RCQ)\cong D(C)Q_SP\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}D(C)Q_SQ.$$
The maps are natural in both homotopy-category variables by step 5 and the model isomorphisms. The resolving-subcategory descent theorem extends this natural isomorphism uniquely to all derived pairs. Its inverse extends the inverse of $t$. In particular it is independent of the chosen resolutions and is computed by the preceding total-complex map on corresponding resolutions.

Uses: lem-pc-kflat-resolution, lem-weil-pbc-site-derived-tensor-descent.

11. The regular module $T$ is flat because $-\otimes_TT\cong\mathrm{id}$, and $T[0]$ is K-flat because $\operatorname{Tot}(A\otimes_TT[0])\cong A$. Therefore the derived unit comparison is represented by $e:CS\to R$. For three flat-term K-flat complexes, their pairwise total tensors are again flat-term K-flat by the total-tensor model lemma. Hence the derived associator diagram is represented by the chain associator diagram of step 5; it commutes on every triple summand. The derived symmetry and unit diagrams are represented by the signed symmetry and scalar-action checks in steps 3 and 5. This proves the claimed coherent natural comparison.

Uses: def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules, def-pc-kflat-complex, lem-pc-sheaf-modules-tensor-closed, lem-pur-kflat-total-tensor-model, lem-weil-pbc-site-derived-tensor-descent.
