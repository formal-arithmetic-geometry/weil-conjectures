### Original step 1 → lem-a1-space-point-open-calculus

First record the point calculation. For maps $A\to B$ and $C\to B$ of algebraic spaces, equality of the images of $a\in|A|$ and $c\in|C|$ means that representatives agree over a common field $M$. The two maps from $\operatorname{Spec}M$ therefore give a point of $A\times_BC$ by the sheaf fibre-product universal property [lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers]. Conversely a point of that product gives such equal images. Consequently $\operatorname{im}(|A\times_BC|\to|C|)=|C|\times_{|B|}\operatorname{im}(|A|\to|B|)$, where the right side means inverse image as a subset. In particular a representable morphism is surjective on all scheme base changes if and only if it is surjective on point classes: one direction tests a field-valued point and chooses a point in the surjective scheme fibre; for the other, apply the calculation to each scheme $C\to B$.

Original uses: lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers, def-trf-algebraic-space. Source: literature/stacks-project/spaces-properties.tex, Tag 03H4 (lemma-points-cartesian), explicit common-field proof; Tag 03H5 (lemma-characterize-surjective), same scheme-test argument for representable maps.

### Original step 2 → lem-a1-space-point-open-calculus

Use [lem-a1-space-affine-etale-cover] for atlas-independent quotient topologies, point-surjective scheme atlases, continuity of every scheme-source map and openness of every etale scheme-source map. Every morphism $f:A\to B$ is continuous: for an atlas $p:U\to A$ and open $O\subseteq|B|$, the inverse image of $f^{-1}O$ in $U$ is $(fp)^{-1}O$, which is open, so the quotient topology applies. An open subspace $J\hookrightarrow A$ is injective on point classes, since equality after a common extension reflects through a monomorphism. For an atlas $U\to A$, its pullback $U_J\to U$ is an open subscheme by the definition of open subspace [def-weil-open-immersion]. The map $U_J\to J$ is a point-surjective etale atlas by base change [lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers]. Thus $|J|$ is an open subset of $|A|$: its inverse image is precisely $U_J$ by step 1. Its topology is the subspace topology, because a subset of $U_J$ is open there exactly when it is open in $U$, and both atlas maps define the quotient topologies.

### Original step 3 → lem-a1-space-invariant-open-quotient

Let $O\subseteq|F|$ be open. Choose a scheme atlas $p:U\to F$ and put $V=p^{-1}O\subseteq U$. Apply [lem-a1-etale-atlas-presentation]: $R=U\times_FU$ is an etale equivalence relation and $F=U/R$ as an fppf sheaf quotient. Since $ps=pt$, the open subschemes $s^{-1}V$ and $t^{-1}V$ of $R$ coincide. Their common value is $R_V=V\times_U R\times_U V$: membership in one inverse image forces membership in the other. The maps $R_V\to V$ are the base changes of $s,t$ along $V\hookrightarrow U$, hence etale by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-scheme-base-change]. On $T$-points this is the restriction of the equivalence relation to $V(T)$, so it is again an equivalence relation.

### Original step 4 → lem-a1-space-invariant-open-quotient

### Original step 5 → lem-a1-space-point-open-calculus

If $J_1,J_2\subseteq F$ are open subspaces and $|J_1|\subseteq|J_2|$, test any map $T\to J_1$ from a scheme. The open immersion $T\times_FJ_2\to T$ has every point of $T$ in its image by step 1. An open immersion with full underlying image is an isomorphism by [def-weil-open-immersion]. Hence $T\to F$ factors uniquely through $J_2$. Uniqueness makes these factorizations natural in $T$, proving $J_1\subseteq J_2$ as subfunctors. The converse follows by taking point classes. Thus step 4 gives the required inclusion-preserving bijection, including uniqueness of the open subspace with a given underlying open subset.

### Original step 9 → lem-a1-space-finite-affine-subatlas

The map $|E|\to|I|$ is continuous by step 2 and surjective by step 7. The identical finite-subcover argument proves that any representable surjective etale map $A\to Z$ from a quasi-compact scheme makes $|Z|$ quasi-compact, using continuity from step 2 and point-surjectivity from step 1.

### Original step 10 → lem-a1-space-finite-affine-subatlas

Conversely suppose $|Z|$ is quasi-compact. By [lem-a1-space-affine-etale-cover] choose an atlas $p:A\to Z$ with $A=\coprod_{a\in D}A_a$ and all $A_a$ affine. The components are open subschemes by [lem-weil-pbc-scheme-coproduct-family]. These images cover $|Z|$; select finitely many that still cover. Let $A'$ be the union of the selected components, an open subscheme of $A$. Each component is quasi-compact by [lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs], so $A'$ is quasi-compact: restrict any open cover to each of the finitely many components and unite their finite subcovers. The restriction $A'\to Z$ remains representable etale, since its scheme base changes are an open immersion followed by an etale scheme map, again using the composition node. It is point-surjective by construction, hence representably surjective by step 1. If $Z$ is empty the finite selection may be empty and $A'$ is the empty quasi-compact scheme, so the argument includes this case.
