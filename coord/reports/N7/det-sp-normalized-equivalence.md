### STATEMENT-DISPUTED: global and affine-local constructibility differ

The imported final equivalence is false for arbitrary T with the two definitions it explicitly chooses. `def-weil-derived-complete-integral-detailed` requires each finite reduction to have a finite stratification of all of T, through `def-weil-finite-torsion-constructibility`. In contrast, `sk-def-sp-normalized-towers` uses finite-level constructibility from `sk-def-sfb-constructible`, which is imposed locally on affine opens for arbitrary T. The old step 8 proves one inclusion only. Step 9 cannot reverse it by treating the two notations as the same definition.

Here is a counterexample with E = Q_ell. Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic different from ell and let T be the disjoint union, over d >= 1, of T_d = A^d_k. In T_d set Z_{d,j} = V(x_1,...,x_j), U_{d,j} = T_d minus Z_{d,j}, and let j_{d,j} be the open immersion, for 1 <= j <= d. At coefficient level n take the degree-zero sheaf

    C_n|_{T_d} = direct sum over j=1,...,d of (j_{d,j})_! (Z/ell^n)_{U_{d,j}}.

Use the quotient transitions. Extension by zero from these quasi-compact open immersions is exact and gives flat finite-coefficient modules; its tensor with a new scalar ring is the corresponding extension by zero over that ring. Thus C is normalized, with common cohomological and Tor intervals [0,0]. On each affine open of T only finitely many components occur, and the coordinate-flag stratifications show finite-level constructibility in the tower's affine-local sense.

For a fixed d, consider the specialization chain of generic points of T_d, Z_{d,1}, ..., Z_{d,d}. The ranks of C_1 there are d,d-1,...,0. A locally constant finite sheaf has equal rank at comparable points lying in the same stratum, since its locally constant rank function is constant along specialization. Consequently a global stratification of C_1 must have at least d+1 strata for every d. No finite global stratification exists. The ambient counit already proved in step 6 identifies the ell reduction of R lim C with C_1, so that limit cannot lie in the imported integral category. This is an explicit failure of the claimed inverse functor, not merely an ambiguity of terminology.

This is the geometric scope of the existing integral comparison tools, and lies within the topologically Noetherian constructibility comparison of Bhatt–Scholze Definition 6.8.10 / Proposition 6.8.11(3). The replacement proof explicitly bridges the two integral conventions, rather than silently changing either definition. This is a statement correction, not a change to any target or a claim that Noetherianity is necessary in every possible reformulation.
