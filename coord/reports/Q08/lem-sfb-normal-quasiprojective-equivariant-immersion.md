## Review by Q08

I checked the item against Brion's Introduction, Lemma 2.9 and Section 2.5 / Corollary 2.14 online. Lemma 2.9 says a power of any line bundle is linearizable for a connected affine group on a normal variety; the item assumes additionally a smooth group and an algebraically closed field. The equivariant-embedding assertion in the Introduction and the definition of G-quasi-projectivity agree with the item's scheme-theoretic meaning. I checked the local definition in Stacks `groupoids.tex`, Tag 03LF, `definition-equivariant-module`: it uses the inverse direction to my theta, with the same identity and cocycle. Taking the inverse gives the convention used by the already-existing cohomology-coaction node. No claim is made that the local Milne book proves the new power-linearization lemma.

For the new lemma I explicitly retained the nonintegral case rather than silently interpreting 'normal' as 'integral'. Here is the justification for that extension to the usual variety statement. A normal finite-type scheme over k has finitely many open-and-closed normal integral components: the exact existing statement `lem-sfb-normal-locally-finite-components` is Stacks 0357, `lemma-normal-locally-finite-nr-irreducibles`. Each component is G-stable. Indeed over the algebraically closed field each connected component of X is geometrically connected, G is connected and smooth, and G times that component is connected; its image under the action is in the component containing its identity slice. Because the target component is open, containment of the image gives the actual factorization of the action into that open subscheme. Apply the integral power-linearization theorem to the restriction of the specified L to each component, choose a positive common multiple of the finitely many exponents, tensor each component's linearization to that exponent, and glue over the disjoint open cover. Cocycle and identity are equalities on that cover. For the empty scheme use exponent 1 and the unique structure. This explains the extension in the new node's source place; it is not a hidden assumption that X is integral. Its remaining theorem, power linearization itself, is the deliberately exposed non-elementary prerequisite.

Step 1 uses exactly `def-trf-relatively-very-ample` for the pullback O(1) description, and `lem-trf-relative-ample-very-ample` for the implication from a relatively very ample first power to ampleness. I opened both statements. I read Stacks 01VM (`definition-very-ample`), 01VU (`lemma-finite-type-ample-very-ample`) and the affine form 01VT (`lemma-finite-type-over-affine-ample-very-ample`). The base is Spec k, hence quasi-compact; the morphism is finite type. The empty case is kept explicitly. In particular I did not cite the closed-only `lem-pc-very-ample-ample` for an immersion.
