## Arguments retained for the new statement-only nodes

For lem-per-blowup-graded-cohomological-bound, follow 0GA6. The exceptional divisor is Proj(gr_K R) by coefficient base change of the Rees algebra, with the matching twist; the finite degree-one presentation embeds it into a finite projective space over R/K. Thus its fibres have a finite maximum dimension, invariant under a graded unital ring isomorphism, even when the Noetherian base has unbounded Krull dimension. Every Y_n is a closed subscheme of the finite-type Noetherian blowup and is proper over Spec(R/K^n). Its fibres over primes have the same underlying spaces as the fibres of Y_1, because both the coefficient and exceptional ideals defining the thickening are nilpotent after restricting to these fibres. Apply the already existing generic lem-sfb-proper-quasicoherent-fibre-dimension-vanishing (the arbitrary-base form of coherent 02V7), then lem-pc-cohomology-sections-affine-base (01XK), to obtain zero coherent cohomology above this dimension. Set d=max(1,that bound). If Proj is empty, every Y_n is empty and d=1 works. This is a geometric input; it gives neither the ideal-power section identification nor the tensor-section bound of the item.

For lem-per-near-projective-higher-cohomology-loss, the proof of 0GAA (1)--(3) works under exactly the abstract hypotheses stated in the new node. Here are all parts of that induction. For any finite near-projective M choose A^t onto M with kernel T. The elementary lifted-scalar construction in the item's Step 6 makes T finite near-projective too. On Z, write B for the kernel of P(T) to O_Z^t and G for its image, obtaining 0 to B to P(T) to G to 0 and 0 to G to O_Z^t to P(M) to 0. Pulling back t_a i=a id_T kills B by I^c, so delta_c on B is zero; invertible twists preserve both sequences. All four sheaves are coherent. Above d their cohomology vanishes by hypothesis; the finite free twists are acyclic for twists at least q.

First, the second sequence gives H^d(P(M)(v))=0 for v>=q because H^{d+1}(G(v))=0. When d>=2, the first sequence gives H^d(G(v))=0 from H^d(P(T)(v))=0 and H^{d+1}(B(v))=0; the second sequence then gives H^{d-1}(P(M)(v))=0. These arguments apply to every finite near-projective module, so they also apply to all ensuing syzygies. For the descending induction let 1<=i<d-1 and ell=(d-1-i)c, with v>=q+ell. The boundary H^i(P(M)(v-ell)) to H^{i+1}(G(v-ell)) is injective because the middle finite free sheaf has zero H^i there. The boundary image at starting twist v lies in H^{i+1}(G(v)). After a first loss c, it lifts to H^{i+1}(P(T)(v-c)), since its obstruction in H^{i+2}(B(v-c)) is the delta_c image of the previous obstruction and hence zero. If i=d-2, this lifted group is zero by the already proved d-1 vanishing. If i<d-2, apply the induction hypothesis for degree i+1 to T, with remaining loss (d-2-i)c; the inequality v-c>=q+(d-2-i)c is exactly available. Boundary naturality and the target injection give the desired zero map. This also proves the case c=0: all positive-degree groups in question vanish. The argument uses only image(s)=I O_Z and never injectivity of s, flatness of g, nilpotence of I, or a completeness assumption.
