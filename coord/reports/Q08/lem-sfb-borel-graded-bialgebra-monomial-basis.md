## Review by Q08

I opened the item, the definition `def-weil-field`, Milne's PDF at I Lemma 12.2, printed p.55 (including the preceding counit-type identity (3)), the current blueprint unit/direct-image proof and the existing connected-finite-order node and proof. Milne's first proof sentence says that generators have only anticommutativity and individual nilpotence relations. It supplies the motivation but not a proof of that theorem. The present finite-dimensional formulation requires a homogeneous monomial basis, which is proved by the agent-written steps. The new prerequisite is self-contained: it introduces the field, graded algebra, signed tensor multiplication, coproduct, counit, augmentation ideal, quotient basis and the full signed derivation identity. Perfectness is not needed there. It is a genuine smaller operator-construction problem, not one clause of the item rephrased as another theorem.

For step 1, finite-dimensional direct sums have finite degree support: a nonzero vector from each nonzero summand is linearly independent. Positive products of more than the maximum degree vanish. Lifting a basis of I/I² generates by induction on homogeneous degree, because each factor of a product in I² has smaller positive degree. These are direct vector-space and grading arguments; the base case is H=k. Strong induction is explicitly on dimension, not on an informal recursive construction.

Step 2 uses exactly the new node's coordinate values and signed Leibniz identity, as quoted in citations.json. Its field, connectedness, finiteness and signed coproduct hypotheses are all those in the item, and the list was chosen as a quotient basis in step 1. An even generator in characteristic zero has a least nilpotence exponent N; differentiating its Nth power contradicts minimality because N is invertible. Odd squares vanish since 2 is invertible. Differentiating a maximal-length squarefree monomial extracts its coefficient. No unquoted exterior-algebra dimension theorem is being used.

Step 3 checks that the induction algebra B really satisfies the same hypotheses. In characteristic two all signs are one. In odd characteristic every odd element squares to zero; thus pth powers are additive and multiplicative even for the full supercommutative algebra, and their image is even. Perfectness makes that semilinear image a k-subalgebra. The proof expands the signed coproduct: the even-total-degree tensor summands commute, while an odd/odd tensor summand has square zero. This gives the stated restricted coproduct, not merely a subalgebra without a coalgebra. Tensor inclusion is verified by basis extension. The smallest nonzero positive degree is missing from B, proving a strict dimension decrease. All of these are explicit calculations, with no structure theorem being silently reapplied.

Step 4 applies the induction hypothesis only to that strictly smaller algebra. A bounded monomial basis makes the v_j classes a basis of B_+/B_+². Frobenius gives a surjective semilinear map on augmentation quotients. Choosing roots and completing with a homogeneous kernel basis is linear algebra after scalar twisting by the automorphism of the perfect field. This is not a new appeal to Borel's theorem, and the induction hypothesis is not a DAG dependency or a cycle. Step 5 writes the needed correction explicitly: take roots of the coefficients of a decomposable polynomial in v and substitute y for v. Its terms have length at least two, so the correction changes no quotient class. The degree and odd-parity cases are checked before subtraction.

Step 6 again uses precisely the new node's coordinate values and signed Leibniz rule, this time for the corrected minimal list. The lemma does not assert freeness over Frobenius; that is proved in this step. Derivations kill B, and reducing exponents gives spanning over B. For independence choose a coefficient of maximal total residue exponent and differentiate by its exponent vector. Every competing monomial has a deficient coordinate. The surviving factor is a sign times a product of factorials with arguments strictly less than p, hence a unit. No commutation of the derivations and no independence claim inside the prerequisite's proof is used. Step 7 combines the two bases by n=pb+a; nilpotence heights and homogeneous degrees are read directly from the preceding construction. All auxiliary algebraic claims are computed in the steps themselves. No step exceeds 2000 characters or hides a standard comparison lemma.

For a future worker on the new node, I also checked its consistency by the explicit invariant-vector-field construction (retained here as source material, not inserted into its statement or proof field). A homogeneous dual functional on I/I² extends to a counit derivation phi on A by zero on k. Put T=(phi tensor id) Delta. Expanding the signed multiplication gives T(ab)=T(a)b+(-1)^(d deg a)aT(b), and the counit identities give epsilon(T(x_j))=delta_ij. Order the generator degrees increasingly. The matrix T_i(x_j) has zero entries when d_j<d_i and identity blocks when d_j=d_i. Its strictly upper part is nilpotent, so a finite geometric-series inverse corrects the T_i to exact coordinate derivations. The coefficients have the required degree differences; multiplying a degree -d_j derivation by a homogeneous coefficient of degree d_j-d_i gives a degree -d_i derivation with the correct signed rule. This accounts for the signed case missing from the project's ordinary invariant-derivation statement. The new node leaves this proof to its own stage-1 worker.

I tested the new statement and the induction on A=k (empty list), an exterior algebra on an odd generator in characteristic different from two, k[x]/(x^p) with even x in characteristic p, and k[x]/(x^4) in characteristic two with primitive odd-degree x. In the last example x² is nonzero: B=k[x²]/((x²)²), the lifted height is 4, and the coordinate derivative exists because 4=0. This checks that the proof does not accidentally impose odd squares zero in characteristic two. For k[x]/(x^(p²)), the induction doubles the Frobenius height as p times p, rather than replacing it by p. Nonperfect fields are excluded precisely where coefficient roots and scalar-surjectivity are used; no such restriction is imposed on the derivative lemma.
