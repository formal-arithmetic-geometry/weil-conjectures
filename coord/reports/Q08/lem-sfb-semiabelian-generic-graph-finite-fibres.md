## Source and proof audit

Faltings states extension over a normal base. His proof starts with the complete-DVR case, then reduces to an excellent Noetherian base and invokes valuation rings for properness and one-point fibres of the graph projection. The imported item correctly asks only finite sets of fibre points and does not require reducedness. I retained this statement. The paper does not spell out the compatibility of the maps on special fibres obtained from different valuation rings. The delivery therefore marks its proof as agent assembly and isolates that compatibility, rather than silently assuming it. It also isolates the independent DVR case. Neither the general extension parent nor the properness sibling is used in the proof, so the parent-to-item dependency is not circular.

The intended reduction for the new DVR input is as follows (argument here, not in its statement). Use the complete-DVR case singled out by Faltings after passing from R to its maximal-ideal-adic completion. The completion is faithfully flat and is again a DVR (Stacks more-algebra.tex Tag 0AP1, lemma-completion-dvr, read). On the double overlap of that faithfully flat map, the two homomorphisms coincide: P is flat, so its generic restriction is schematically dense, and Q is separated. Homomorphisms descend for an fpqc cover; the group identities descend too. The same density/equalizer argument proves uniqueness. This explains the noncomplete formulation without asserting the general normal-base extension theorem as a premise. The complete trait case remains the substantive unsplit local theorem, exactly as the source treats it.

Here is the argument to be developed when the new specialization input is split; it explains why it is a distinct and reasonable local comparison. Pass to the strict local base at the chosen geometric s. Normality is preserved, and its local ring is a domain. For n prime to the residue characteristic, the n-torsion of a smooth commutative group is separated etale: multiplication by n has invertible differential. Every special n-torsion point of P therefore lifts to a section over this strictly henselian local base. Apply u to its generic value, obtaining a rational section of Q[n]. If this rational section has a special value along a trait, that value is independent of the trait. Indeed each proposed special value, by the henselian etale lifting property, gives a section of Q[n] over the strict local base. Its image is open (and closed since Q[n] is separated). A trait with that special value must have its generic value in this image as well. The two resulting sections thus have the same generic value, and are equal on the integral base by the closed diagonal. Consequently the special-fibre homomorphisms agree on every prime-to-characteristic torsion point of P. Such points are dense in a semiabelian variety: use density on the abelian quotient and on the split torus, and lift torsion of the quotient to torsion of the extension using divisibility of the torus. Equality of morphisms on this dense subset follows from reducedness of the smooth source and the closed diagonal of Q. Trait maps can be lifted to the strict local base after strict henselization of their local rings; residue embeddings into a common algebraically closed field specify the geometric points used in that construction. When one of the R_i is a field, s is eta, and the comparison is just generic equality and separatedness. No finiteness of relative n-torsion across a toric-rank jump is asserted. This comparison needs no graph and assumes, rather than constructs, both extensions.

These are genuine inputs left statement-only as the brief requires; the item itself carries the argument turning them into finite graph fibres. No new node is an elementary equalizer, product, or finite-set assertion. The closed-equalizer ideal argument and the final equality-of-coordinates argument remain elementary steps.
