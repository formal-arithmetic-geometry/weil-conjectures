Step 1 uses def-trf-algebraic-space, whose statement explicitly gives a representable surjective etale atlas and defines surjectivity by every scheme test. Thus its pullback by Spec F is a nonempty scheme, without any extra base-change theorem hidden in the step. lem-weil-pbc-morphism-from-spec-field supplies the canonical residue-field point. Composing it with the pullback projections gives the field map F to L and the commuting square. A unital homomorphism from a field is injective: a nonzero kernel element would have an inverse and force 1 to map to zero. I checked spaces.tex definition-algebraic-space (025Y) and the discussion preceding schemes.tex lemma-characterize-points (01J9).

Step 2 uses lem-weil-pbc-affine-opens-basis (01IT) to choose a single affine neighbourhood, lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-spec-points for its Spec identification, and lem-weil-pbc-morphism-factors-through-open-subscheme (01HI) to factor the one-point map. This is an open subscheme, where image containment does imply factorization; no analogous assertion about a closed subscheme is used. def-weil-affine-spectrum supplies the ring map and its contravariant functoriality. lem-weil-pbc-lft-affine-pairs (01T2) says precisely that every affine pair has a finite-type ring map. I read that statement and source equivalence (1)/(2), rather than assuming an arbitrary affine open lies in a chosen finite-type cover. Both field structures are tested on the same U_0.

Step 3 is the explicit elementary quotient argument. The kernel of A to a field is proper and prime; the quotient embeds in L. Each finite list of algebra generators maps to generators of the quotient, so the single affine chart Spec B verifies the locally finite type definition in def-weil-finite-type-morphism over each field. The field maps to B are injective because their composites to L equal h j_i. def-weil-field and def-weil-affine-spectrum are the only other inputs here. This argument requires no finite type theorem about closed immersions and no Noetherian hypothesis.

Step 4 uses the exact statements of lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-reduced-iff (01J2), lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-closed-subsets-primes (00ES, part (2)), and lem-pc-irreducible-space-connected (the sentence after 004V). I read each source: a domain has no nonzero nilpotents; its zero prime exhibits the entire spectrum as V(0), an irreducible closed set. The zero prime also witnesses nonemptiness. These establish reducedness and connectedness of Z, not of E.

Step 5 uses lem-sfb-connected-reduced-scalar-field-uniqueness. I opened its statement: it assumes exactly a nonempty connected reduced scheme with two locally finite type maps to field spectra and states equality of the two subrings of monic-polynomial roots. Steps 3 and 4 have checked all these hypotheses. def-weil-affine-spectrum identifies the global-section ring with B. Membership of rho_1(lambda) in C_1 is witnessed by T-rho_1(lambda). Equality with C_2 gives a monic relation over rho_2(k_2). Since B is nonzero a monic constant polynomial cannot vanish, so its degree is positive. The proof uses only the quoted conclusion of the node, not its as-yet-unwritten proof or its separate common-field clause.

Step 6 is polynomial evaluation and injectivity, written as an equation: applying B to L sends each coefficient to h j_2(c_r), and the root to h j_1(lambda). Injectivity of h reflects the equality to F. The resulting polynomial over j_2(k_2) has leading coefficient 1 and positive degree, hence is nonzero under def-weil-polynomial-ring and def-weil-field. This is why extending the point field in step 1 is harmless.
