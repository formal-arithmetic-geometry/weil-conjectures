Old step 4, retained verbatim:

In case (i) we already have the inclusion $R=T\hookrightarrow S=A$ and its $R$-linear retraction. In case (ii), $\rho(\Theta a)=\theta\rho(a)$ implies $T\cap\Theta A=\theta T$: if $t=\Theta a\in T$, apply $\rho$ to obtain $t=\theta\rho(a)$; the reverse inclusion is immediate. Thus $R=T/(\theta)$ embeds in $S=A/(\Theta)$, and $[a]\mapsto[\rho(a)]$ is a well-defined $R$-linear retraction $\bar\rho:S\to R$ fixing $1$.

Old step 6, retained verbatim:

The unital injection $R\hookrightarrow S$ makes $R$ a nonzero domain and embeds $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ into $\operatorname{Frac}(S)$. Let $x=a/b\in\operatorname{Frac}(R)$, $b\neq0$, be integral over $R$. Its same monic equation makes it integral over $S$, so $x\in S$ by the normality just proved. Apply the $R$-linear retraction of step 4 to $bx=a$: it gives $b\bar\rho(x)=a$, hence $x=\bar\rho(x)\in R$ inside $\operatorname{Frac}(S)$. (Use $\rho$ for $\bar\rho$ in case (i).) This proves that $R$ is integrally closed in its fraction field in both cases.

In its own notation contraction follows from rho(iota(theta)a)=theta rho(a); this proves quotient injectivity as well as the well-defined descended additive retraction. For the final assertion, the quotient injection supplies a nonzero domain and a fraction-field inclusion, so a fraction integral over the smaller quotient satisfies the same monic equation over the larger normal domain. Applying the descended linear retraction to bx=a then places it in the smaller quotient. There is no assertion that rho is a ring homomorphism or that arbitrary quotients of a normal ring are normal.
