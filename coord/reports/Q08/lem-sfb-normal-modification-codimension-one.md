### Original step 1 — lem-sfb-birational-generic-isomorphism-open

By [pqf-lem-proper-basic], $f$ is separated and of finite type. By [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian], $Y$ is Noetherian. By [lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-finitely-many-components], both schemes have finitely many irreducible components. By [pqf-lem-lft-fp-locnoeth], $f$ is locally of finite presentation. Thus [lem-sfb-birational-isomorphism-dense-open] supplies a dense open $V$ over which $f$ is an isomorphism. A dense open contains every generic point: for a component $C_i$, remove the finitely many other components; this leaves a nonempty open subset of $X$ inside $C_i$. Density forces $V$ to meet it. Every nonempty open of $C_i$ contains its generic point, so that point lies in $V$. These are the codimension-zero points, proving (1).

Original uses: pqf-lem-proper-basic, lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian, lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-finitely-many-components, pqf-lem-lft-fp-locnoeth, lem-sfb-birational-isomorphism-dense-open.

### Original step 2 — lem-sfb-birational-integral-normal-neighbourhood

We first prove (4). Fix $x,y$ as in that clause and put $R=\mathcal O_{X,x}$, a normal domain: normality at its maximal ideal in [def-weil-pbc-normal-ring] gives this because localizing a local ring at its maximal ideal is the ring itself. Apply [lem-sfb-noetherian-injective-germ-neighbourhood] and replace $X$ by the resulting affine neighbourhood $\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $x$, and $Y$ by its inverse image. The restriction remains proper by [lem-weil-pbc-proper-base-change]. Since $A\hookrightarrow R$, $A$ is a domain, hence $X$ is integral. Birationality gives exactly one generic point of the restricted $Y$: generic points of an open subscheme are precisely generic points of components that meet that open; their images are the generic points of this integral $X$. Thus $Y$ is irreducible, and remains reduced, so it is integral by [def-pc-integral-scheme].

## Small cases and limits

Generic-open lemma: the empty target has the empty dense open and no generic points; the identity of a reducible Noetherian scheme works and tests all components. Removing one generic point cannot leave a dense open when there are finitely many components, which is why the proof uses finite components.

Integral-neighbourhood lemma: on the identity, a normal stalk selects a component neighbourhood; a crossing point of two reduced components has a non-domain local ring and fails the normal-stalk hypothesis. Nilpotent thickening in the source fails reducedness. A component not meeting the chosen open disappears, while each that meets it retains its original generic point. No properness is required in this extracted statement.

Stalk-section lemma: the identity and an open immersion give their expected local inverses. The disjoint union of two copies of X mapping to X can have an invertible stalk at either copy and a section but is not an isomorphism; the node correctly asserts only a section, leaving the integral generic-section argument separate.

Generic-section lemma: for the identity it is immediate; the preceding disconnected example violates integrality. A closed point in an integral line does not contain its generic point, and projection from an affine line over a point has no singleton generic fibre. A nilpotent closed thickening shows why reduced/integral target of the closed section is essential in the ideal argument.

DVR-fibre lemma: the identity over Spec DVR has singleton special fibre; the empty source has empty finite fibre. A proper positive-dimensional family over a DVR has transcendental generic residue extension, so is excluded. The blowup exceptional-fibre counterexample in dimension two does not satisfy the dimension-one DVR stalk hypothesis. No global dimension-one assumption on X is made.

Algebra node: A=C, q=0 and away-localizations give the expected isomorphisms; a nonnormal singularity fails normality and a nontrivial finite field extension fails common fraction field. Integral domains exclude the zero ring. Every assembly step is below 2000 characters, so no long non-elementary step conceals another extraction.
