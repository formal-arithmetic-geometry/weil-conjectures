The new statement introduces the original and base-changed square, the overlap, both local objects, their isomorphism, the target and both maps. Step 1 uses the actual statement of lem-weil-pbc-strict-perfect-ringed-pullback-model: it computes derived pullback on bounded packaged complexes and supplies the pulled-back retractions. The slice theorem supplies the restriction comparison. Applying these locally and using the definition of perfectness is a short elementary deduction; I did not cite the scheme-only pullback theorem for an algebraic space.

Step 2 uses the explicit quasi-coherent-cohomology clause of lem-sfb-space-perfect-objects-compact; it does not infer this from compactness alone. The new 09RI input then gives the desired Hom map for a perfect target as a special case. The eventual-equality assertion is the defining equivalence relation of the directed colimit of sets and does not posit a faithful pullback at an individual stage.

Step 3 uses the base-change limit and affine-projection clauses of lem-sfb-space-affine-limits-schematic-stages, not its eventual-schematicity conclusion. It sets up exactly the predicate on qcqs etale objects required by lem-sfb-space-qcqs-distinguished-square-induction. The upper tail contains i_0 and is directed; it is cofinal by taking a common upper bound, so fixing a base stage is legitimate. This step is setup and a local geometry check, not an unproved descent assertion.

Step 4 reads each affine input at its actual generality. The scheme-limit node explicitly gives Spec of the colimit of coordinate rings. The space etale–Zariski equivalence has the canonical pullback comparison as part of its statement, and etale-detects-perfect gives exactly the translation of perfectness. The affine bounded-projective node supplies a complex representing the object, while affine-perfect-colimit-descent descends that whole complex with its differential. The affine-derived-pullback-tensor-tilde node supplies scalar extension in the derived category. Bounded finite-projective models compute this tensor without another flatness hypothesis. In particular I did not use the existing global scheme descent node with its extra Noetherian hypotheses.

Step 5 applies Hom continuity on the overlap, which is qcqs in the induction principle. It descends both the overlap isomorphism and its inverse, then both inverse equations after a common increase of index. This corrects the tempting but invalid inference that an arbitrary lift of a limit isomorphism is automatically already invertible. Only finitely many stages and equations occur. Both local models remain perfect under transition pullback by Step 1. The overlap predicate itself is available as an induction hypothesis, although Hom continuity alone suffices for this part.

Step 6 uses the canonical unbounded adjunction expressly named in lem-weil-pbc-ringed-projection-mate-exact. I read the slice-comparison theorem's restriction/direct-image identity and its counit-mate equation: these identify the restrictions of adjoint maps, rather than assuming an arbitrary base-change isomorphism for Rq_*. Etale objects over W_j define target slices, whose inverse-image slices are the etale base changes U,V,T on W. Consequently the equation beta Lc=alpha really transposes to a=b c on T_j. The new gluing theorem's moreover clause supplies an actual map into the fixed target Rq_*E. Its statement, unlike a gluing-of-objects-only lemma, is strong enough for this step even if that target is unbounded or not quasi-coherent.

Step 7 is the elementary local check: the two legs cover because the etale leg is an isomorphism on the reduced complement. Perfectness is local by definition. The adjoint of the glued map restricts to the chosen isomorphisms by the same slice-mate equation. The cone has zero cohomology on that etale cover, and a sheaf zero on a covering is zero. The induction node then applies with its affine hypothesis verified in Step 4 and its square implication verified in Steps 5–7. No additional condition on W_0 or its points is used.
