10. The affine-pushforward-base-change node is applied to the closed immersion Δ, hence an affine morphism. It gives its arbitrary base-change map and its compatibility with composition. 11. On a geometric fibre, dual base change is already proved. In a smooth open around x, the smooth-curve-section-Cartier node has the necessary separated smooth relative-dimension-one hypotheses and no base restriction. The divisor-line-bundle definition then identifies its inverse ideal with O(x). Multipliers agree off x, so this is the canonical identification rather than a line-bundle isomorphism chosen up to a scalar.
12. The normalization quotient node is for proper reduced connected nodal curves over an algebraically closed field, exactly the geometric fibre hypotheses. Its node statement, not just its proof, gives finiteness of the node set and the direct sum of residue-field quotients. Restricting away from other nodes gives the partial-normalization quotient k_x. Multiplication takes the point ideal into O because that ideal kills the quotient. The resulting map into Hom is injective because the point ideal has zero annihilator (the already proved multiplication injection): writing a multiplier q=c/s and multiplying by each ideal element gives cj=0, then c=0. This also avoids inferring a regular element downstairs merely from its existence on a faithfully flat chart. The quotient map is injective too, since any multiplier whose Hom action is a function must equal that function; both quotients have dimension one, so it is surjective. I read Stacks 0C1R and 0CBW for this input. This avoids assuming the local ring of a self-node is literally a product or treating full normalization at all nodes as partial normalization at x.
