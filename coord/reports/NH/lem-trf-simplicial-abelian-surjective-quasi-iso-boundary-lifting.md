### Old Step 1 → lem-trf-simplicial-normalized-kernel-acyclic

Let $K_n=\ker(f_n)\subseteq X_n$. Naturality of $f$ restricts every simplicial operator of $X$ to $K$, so $K$ is a simplicial abelian group. The alternating differentials restrict as well. Degreewise surjectivity gives the short exact sequence of cochain complexes $0\to s(K)\to s(X)\xrightarrow{s(f)}s(Y)\to0$, including the zero terms in positive degrees. Apply the cited long exact cohomology sequence. In $H^{r-1}(s(X))\to H^{r-1}(s(Y))\to H^r(s(K))\to H^r(s(X))\to H^r(s(Y))$, the first map is surjective and the last map injective by the quasi-isomorphism hypothesis. Exactness therefore makes the map out of $H^r(s(K))$ both injective and zero. Hence $H^r(s(K))=0$ for every integer $r$.

Cited statements: def-weil-pbc-simplicial-object, lem-weil-pbc-complex-ses-homology-long-exact.

### Old Step 2 → lem-trf-simplicial-normalized-kernel-acyclic

Use the positive-face normalization in the cited comparison: $N(K)_0=K_0$, $N(K)_m=\bigcap_{i=1}^m\ker(d_i)$ for $m>0$, with differential $d_0$. The alternating complex $C(K)$ in that comparison has exactly the terms and differentials of $s(K)$ after replacing chain degree $m$ by cochain degree $-m$. Thus $C(K)$ is acyclic by Step 1. The comparison supplies chain maps $i:N(K)\to C(K)$ and $p:C(K)\to N(K)$ with $pi$ homotopic to the identity. On a cycle $z$, an identity $pi-1=dh+hd$ gives $piz-z=d(hz)$, so $pi$ acts as the identity on homology. The class of $iz$ is zero in $C(K)$, hence $[z]=p_*i_*[z]=0$. This proves that $N(K)$ is acyclic, also in degree zero, whose outgoing differential is zero.

Cited statements: lem-cbc-normalized-alternating-homotopy-equivalence.

### Old Step 3 → parent Step 2

Fix a lifting square in dimension $n$. If $n=0$, choose $x\in X_0$ mapping to the vertex $y\in Y_0$, using surjectivity of $f_0$; the boundary is empty. For $n\geq1$, evaluate the lower map $b:\Delta[n]\to UY$ at $\mathrm{id}_{[n]}$ to get $y\in Y_n$, and evaluate the upper map $a$ at the codimension-one injections $\delta_i$ to get $x_i\in X_{n-1}$. Naturality gives $b(\alpha)=Y(\alpha)y$ for every operator $\alpha:[m]\to[n]$, and the lifting square gives $f(x_i)=d_i y$. For $n\geq2$, the face-face identity and naturality of $a$ give $d_i x_j=d_{j-1}x_i$ for $i<j$. For $n=1$ there are no such equations. It suffices to find $x\in X_n$ mapping to $y$ with faces $x_i$; Step 7 checks the whole boundary, including its degenerate simplices.

Cited statements: def-weil-pbc-simplicial-object, lem-weil-pbc-simplex-face-face-identity.

### Old Step 4 → parent Step 3

For $n\geq1$ choose $t\in X_n$ with $f(t)=y$ and put $a_i=x_i-d_i t$. Then $f(a_i)=d_i y-d_i f(t)=0$, so $a_i\in K_{n-1}$. When $n\geq2$, the face-face identity gives $d_i a_j=d_i x_j-d_i d_j t=d_{j-1}x_i-d_{j-1}d_i t=d_{j-1}a_i$. Thus $(a_i)$ is a compatible boundary in $K$. A simplex $v\in K_n$ with faces $a_i$ will give $x=t+v$ with $f(x)=y$ and $d_i x=x_i$.

Cited statements: def-weil-pbc-simplicial-object, lem-weil-pbc-simplex-face-face-identity.
