I opened every cited node and compared its statement with the use, with the following results.

1. lem-sfb-compact-hausdorff-surjection-quotient states exactly the open-subset test for a continuous surjection with compact source and Hausdorff target. The finite disjoint union is compact by the finite-subcover argument written in the step, while S is Hausdorff by the hypothesis. Agreement on the actual set-theoretic fibre products makes the assembled map constant on fibres; applying the cited open-subset test proves continuity. Uniqueness uses surjectivity. An empty family covers only the empty S, whose set of maps into any D is a singleton. This is an application of a genuine cited theorem, hence not marked elementary.

2. lem-sfb-set-continuous-functions-fpqc-sheaf explicitly states set-valued fpqc descent with arbitrary D, no separation assumption, and precomposition restrictions. def-sfb-continuous-set-presheaf identifies the restriction to the small site and def-weil-pbc-proetale-site explicitly says its covers are fpqc covers. Thus a small-site matching family is precisely data on which that descent theorem acts. No affine or qcqs hypothesis on U or X is inserted. The scheme fibre products used for matching are the same fibre products in the site, since the maps are over X. The step takes no construction from a cited node's proof.

3. lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-scheme-spectral assumes exactly qcqs U and concludes spectrality. lem-pe-spectral-components-profinite assumes spectrality and states compact, Hausdorff and totally disconnected for the connected-component quotient with the quotient topology. I checked their actual sources in properties.tex, Tag 094L, label lemma-quasi-compact-quasi-separated-spectral, and topology.tex, Tag 0906, label lemma-spectral-pi0. Neither requires Hausdorff U or Noetherian U. The second node is a generic tool outside this unit, declared as such in split_report.

4. This is elementary quotient topology, written out without hiding a separate lemma: a separation of the image pulls back to a separation of a component; total disconnectedness then makes the image a singleton. The quotient topology gives continuity of the factor and induced component map, and quotient surjectivity gives inverse identities. The displayed equalities explicitly check both naturalities, rather than merely claiming they transport. All maps on targets are between totally disconnected targets when both factorization equivalences are being compared. This step in fact works for every U; qcqs is needed only for step 3. Its text has no unstated Hausdorff assumption on D.

5. lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-etale states that open immersions are etale. Clause (1) of det-pe-morphisms-etale-comparison says etale implies weakly etale. The final sentence of lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability says weakly etale maps are stable under composition, exactly what is needed for W_i to remain an object over X. lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs supplies quasi-compactness of the affine V, and def-pc-scheme supplies the scheme setting. The finite-affine-refinement condition from def-weil-pbc-proetale-site is verified explicitly: take affine neighbourhoods inside V intersect W_i and use quasi-compactness of V. This avoids silently assuming any W_i is quasi-compact. The only extra elementary scheme fact here is the affine-open basis inherent in schemes. Both non-Mathlib geometric tools are declared in split_report.

6. The constant presheaf and restrictions are exactly those in def-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf and def-sfb-continuous-set-presheaf. Fibre singletons in a discrete space are open, so the open fibres supply constant lifts, with the cover admissibility already proved in step 5. Equality of constants is checked by evaluating at a point when U is nonempty. For empty U the empty cover, rather than an unjustified injectivity assertion on sections, witnesses local injectivity. For empty D, only empty U admits a continuous-map section and its empty cover witnesses local surjectivity without an impossible choice of an element of D. These are elementary pointwise and open-preimage checks, not a new lemma.
