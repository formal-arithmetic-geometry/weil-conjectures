## Review by NH

I read the master blueprint lem-weil-pbc-w-localization-points in full and compared its claims with Stacks proetale.tex Tag 0975 (lemma-make-w-local) and the construction preceding it. The blueprint indexes all finite constructible partitions and all denominators satisfying E subset D(f_E). This quantification matters: a fixed arbitrary denominator choice would not justify localizing at each s outside x. The existing construction node states a colimit over both partitions and denominators, and steps 2, 6 and 10 use precisely that diagram. The directed index is a set, not a large category: partitions belong to finite families of subsets of Spec A and denominators to finite families of elements of A.

For step 1, I opened def-weil-pbc-w-localization and checked the componentwise transition maps. Exactly one component idempotent avoids a prime, since they are orthogonal and sum to one. This argument, including its compatibility with refinement, is elementary; it does not assume any surjectivity of the finite-stage spectrum maps.

For step 2, I opened lem-weil-pbc-w-local-partition and used only its first sentence, compact Hausdorffness of the patch topology of any affine scheme. I do not apply its later assertions about an already w-local scheme to W before proving w-locality. The definition def-weil-pbc-w-local supplies finite Boolean constructibility, making every selected part patch closed. Common refinement gives the finite intersection property, and the D(f)/V(f) partition gives uniqueness. Contraction is prime and each f outside x becomes invertible at an allowed stage, so the claimed containment y subseteq x follows without identifying x with the contraction.

For step 3, I opened the localization universal property node and the prime-localization/residue-field definition. A permitted denominator on the part containing x is outside x, hence outside y; the localization maps to A_y and kappa(y) therefore exist. Kernel primality follows directly from multiplication in the field. Membership in the selected factor is determined by a numerator belonging to y because its denominator is a unit. The elementwise colimit node states that every element has a stage representative, exactly what uniqueness of the recovered prime uses. Thus no uncited theorem on inverse limits of spectra is being invoked.

For step 4, I read the full statement of lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-localization-at-prime. It applies to a small filtered diagram of commutative rings and the actual inverse-image primes; these hypotheses were established in steps 1–3. After killing the unselected factors, localization of A_f at the prime over y is A_y by the two universal maps, not merely an abstract isomorphism: they fix A, so transition maps are identities. I opened lem-weil-pbc-spec-map-stalk-localization and read Stacks Schemes Tag 01HV (lemma-spec-sheaves), part (6), for the local-ring identification. Its statement says the affine stalk is the corresponding prime localization, as needed.

For step 5, I read lem-weil-pbc-spec-clopen-idempotent and Stacks Algebra Tag 00EE (lemma-disjoint-decomposition). Its converse clause explicitly makes D(e_E) clopen. I use the concrete partition idempotents, with Boolean identities checked after common refinement; I do not need to assert that every idempotent in W descends to a partition. The inverse image of each constructible patch basic set is exactly D(e_E), giving continuity and separation of different fibres.

For step 6, the only non-elementary tools are the existing elementwise-colimit and localization-universal-property statements. Stage evaluation to A_x is permitted because every selected denominator is outside x. Surjectivity is witnessed by the D(s) stage, including zero coordinates on all other parts. If the evaluated coordinate a/f^n vanishes, the prime-localization definition gives s outside x with sa=0 in A; refining by D(s) and actually multiplying its denominators by s makes that coordinate zero. Multiplication by its refined part idempotent then proves membership in J_x. This corrects the imported proof's unsupported phrase about equality being detected after one further localization.

Step 7 uses the quotient description from step 6. I wrote out the elementary quotient-ideal correspondence and basic-open restriction, identifying the actual closed subset and its canonical affine scheme structure, rather than claiming a factorization from set containment. Inverting the idempotents is equivalent to imposing e=1, so the construction agrees with the localization description in the blueprint. For step 8, I checked connectedness directly: in a local ring every neighbourhood of the maximal prime contains a unit basic open and hence is the whole spectrum. The clopen separation of step 5 then makes a fibre a maximal connected subset. This is a few-line argument, not a new lemma restating the item's component clause.

For step 9, I checked specialization as prime inclusion directly from V(q). Clopen sets prevent specialization across first coordinates. The closed fibre's local spectrum has one maximal prime; thus diagonal pairs have no further specialization, and all other pairs specialize to them. The complement calculation uses f in x minus y, not the reverse difference: D(e_{V(f)}) intersect D(f) contains that pair and no diagonal pair. These are elementary deductions from the preceding steps, with no quasi-compactness or noetherianity hypothesis missing.

For step 10, the elementwise node provides the finite-stage tuple representing b. The prime at (x,x) tests its selected numerator and has an invertible denominator, so restriction of D(b) is exactly the finite union E intersect D(a_E). Those sets are patch open, and restriction of D(e_E) recovers every constructible E. Both inclusions of topologies are proved. The w-local definition then applies in the Zariski topology, with connected components from step 8 and a closed set of closed points from step 9.

For step 11, I opened def-weil-pbc-ind-etale-algebra and lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability. The construction node explicitly specifies an ind-Zariski algebra; the definition uses 'such a presentation' of an ind-etale algebra. Stability's statement says an ind-etale map is weakly etale, and the definition explicitly includes flatness. I opened lem-weil-pbc-flat-surjective-spectrum-faithfully-flat and read Stacks Algebra Tag 00HQ (lemma-ff-rings); its hypothesis is flatness and surjectivity on all prime spectra, both established here. No finite-presentation hypothesis is imposed on W.

Edge cases checked: for a field k there is one constructible part and every allowed denominator is a unit, so W=k and there is only (0,0). For A=k times k the partition separates the two primes and gives the expected two singleton components. For a local domain with nonmaximal prime y contained strictly in x, (x,y) and (y,y) are different points with the same structural image y, as the construction's idempotents require; only (x,x) is closed in that fibre. For the zero ring the empty partition gives the empty product (zero ring), both spectra are empty, the connected-component assertion is vacuous, and the flat-surjective criterion still gives faithful flatness. Nilpotents cause no problem: they lie in every prime and are never selected as denominators on a nonempty part; the annihilating-element criterion, rather than cancellation in a domain, was used in step 6. No new statements require further small-case testing.
