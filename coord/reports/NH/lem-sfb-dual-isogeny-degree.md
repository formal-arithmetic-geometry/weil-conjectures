For lem-sfb-finite-commutative-cartier-representability I read Milne Algebraic Groups §11c, pp.212–214, especially Lemma 11.11 and Theorem 11.12, including the proof using group-like elements. The existing finite-group-order-annihilator node supplies the annihilating integer when this prerequisite is later split; I did not create its duplicate. For lem-sfb-pointed-torsor-line-character-descent I read Stacks descent.tex, Tag 023T, proposition-fpqc-descent-quasi-coherent, in full statement and its effective/fully faithful descent argument, and lemma-finite-locally-free-descends. The further classification is an agent-derived prerequisite, not falsely attributed verbatim to Stacks. Its argument is: a normalized trivial line has a descent multiplier on X_T x G_T; universal global functions make this a unit on G_T, and the cocycle condition is exactly chi(gh)=chi(g)chi(h). Conversely that identity supplies descent data. Descent gives a line on Y_T; the point x_0 gives the rigidification at y_0. A normalized isomorphism is unique because any two differ by a unit pulled from T which restricts to 1 at x_0. This proves inverse correspondences, their tensor compatibility and base-change naturality. This prerequisite is substantial and independent of the item's Pic^0 argument. The identity torsor and the torsor G -> Spec k both give the expected classification; in the latter case the universal-global-functions hypothesis fails for nontrivial G, correctly excluding a false character/Picard assertion.

## Review by NH

I checked B–E against the node files and the sources, not just the mechanical output. Milne AV Theorem 9.1 on p.41 says exactly that the dual kernel is the Cartier dual over an arbitrary field. Its opening definition is the same Poincare pullback used in the item. The source's proof is a reference, so I wrote the argument and marked its origin as agent. The three new statements introduce all their objects, cite the defining notions, contain no proofs, and are each searched with the recorded commands. Their distinct subjects and hypotheses are explained above; none is a clause of the degree theorem renamed as a child.

Step 1 uses the existing kernel-subgroup statement to identify N as a group, abelian commutativity to make it commutative, and isogeny-degree to obtain finite flatness. The abelian-variety characterization explicitly gives geometric integrality; H0-proper-variety clause 2 then gives k. Flat-base-change-coherent explicitly allows a flat k-algebra, not only an extension field, so it applies to every coordinate ring R of a test scheme. I checked this hypothesis carefully: over a field every R is flat. Gluing over affine opens supplies exactly the universal global-functions condition of the descent prerequisite.

Step 2 uses the group-scheme definition for its two inverse formulas, not a claimed quotient theorem. Its only new structural use is pointed-torsor-line-character-descent, whose full statement explicitly classifies all rigidified pullback-trivial lines and gives the normalized multiplier. Finite flatness and surjectivity are already available; the formulas prove the required torsor isomorphism without assuming etaleness.

Step 3 uses precisely the multiplier clause of that prerequisite and the full faithfulness clause of lem-pc-fpqc-descent. It does not infer a downstairs line is trivial from an arbitrary upstairs trivialization. Rather, the translation preserves its particular character descent datum, so that the upstairs identity descends to an isomorphism t_b^*L=L. The finite-extension-alg-closed statement gives the rational lift of b even for an inseparable finite fibre. The homogeneous-line node requires an algebraically closed field and invariance under every rational translation: I apply it separately to each geometric fibre, where both hypotheses hold. Its conclusion is exactly algebraic triviality, which is what the Poincare datum requires on each geometric fibre, including families over nonreduced T.

Step 4 uses the Poincare datum's arbitrary-test universal property and its tensor law, and the new descent prerequisite's functorial group bijection. Cartier representability supplies precisely the same character functor on every T. The scheme identification follows by applying the two natural bijections to the identity maps, so no geometric-point identification or hidden separability inference enters. The Cartier prerequisite says the coordinate space is H*, enough for the final computation.

Step 5 uses the quasi-inverse prerequisite only for a positive n and a reverse group homomorphism. It constructs the reverse dual by the same Poincare universal property, uses pullback composition for contravariance, and invokes the algebraically-trivial-line-multiplicativity node in its stated arbitrary-parameter form. Its base is Spec k, locally Noetherian, and its group is a proper smooth commutative scheme, a special case of the node's algebraic space hypothesis. Iteration along the diagonal yields [n]^*M=M^n; rescaling at zero makes the isomorphism rigidified. This explains the [n]-duality identity rather than citing biduality or the much stronger polarization-calculus node; both would risk a cycle through this item. Step 6 uses multiplication-isogeny over an arbitrary field for A-dual and this strictly positive n, then the isogeny definition. Step 7 uses only the coordinate-space clause of Cartier representability and the degree definition, with the dual-basis calculation written out. No dimension theorem or claim about geometric kernel cardinalities is hidden there.

I checked the dependency paths from all the reused substantive lemmas: none leads back to the item. The existing biduality and polarization-calculus statements are deliberately not prerequisites. The off-unit inputs are generic tools and are declared in split_report.tool_uses for the maintainer's review. All proof steps are below 2000 characters; the longest is under 900. The two comparisons deserving separate lemmas are the general pointed-descent classification and finite-group Cartier representability, which are exposed as statement-only nodes rather than buried inline.

Small cases checked: zero-dimensional abelian varieties and identity isogenies give a kernel of length 1 and degree 1; [p] in characteristic p has a nonreduced kernel whose entire coordinate dimension is preserved; both mu_p and alpha_p satisfy Cartier representability without assuming etaleness. Arbitrary imperfect k and nonreduced test schemes are handled by the affine base-change calculation, the stated arbitrary-parameter descent lemma and the geometric-fibre argument. I corrected only the overly restrictive finite-locally-free phrasing of the general descent prerequisite to finite flat surjective, so the item does not use an unstated finite-presentation result. The item's statement needs no repair.
