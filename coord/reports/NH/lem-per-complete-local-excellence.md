The local tags/tags table confirms the source labels. This caught and corrected an initial draft typo 07PW for the G-ring ubiquity proposition: its actual tag is 07PX. Neither the statements nor mathematical route needed correction.

The first new theorem is exactly 07PI with J-0 expanded. Its proof in the source uses a finite regular coefficient subring, separable J-0 ascent, and the derivation argument for inseparable extensions. It is therefore a genuine non-elementary input, not something established by the definition of a domain or completeness. Its statement includes the domain and completeness hypotheses and asks only for a nonempty regular open, not regularity of the whole ring. The second new theorem is precisely implication (3) to (1) of 07PC with J-1/J-2 expanded. Its proof passes through finite domain models and separable/inseparable field reductions in the source; it is not an elementary finite-type/module-finite identification. Both new nodes have statement dependencies for every non-inline notion, no proof or proof dependencies, the required creation stamp and exact ancestor/via provenance, and honest negative searches.

Step 1 uses only lem-weil-pbc-complete-local-g-ring. Its statement says exactly that a Noetherian ring complete for its unique maximal ideal is a G-ring, so the required hypotheses are all in the item. I did not infer regularity of all formal fibres just from the definition of G-ring.

Step 2 uses def-weil-finitely-generated-module, lem-weil-pbc-finite-over-complete-local-product, and the new domain-regular-open theorem. The finite-algebra node explicitly gives Noetherianity for B and explicitly gives completeness and locality when the target is a domain. I use that domain clause on B/q after explaining both why the quotient is a nonzero domain and why the images of any finite generating family of B generate the quotient. There is no assertion that B is a domain or that a general quotient/localization of a complete ring is complete. The new theorem then supplies exactly the regular open in each domain quotient.

Step 3 uses lem-exc-regular-locus-open-from-prime-quotients. I read its entire statement: it assumes a Noetherian algebra and a nonempty regular open in every prime quotient, both provided in Step 2, and concludes openness of the full regular locus of that algebra. It does not assert completeness or J-2, and Step 3 takes neither from it.

Step 4 uses the new finite-algebra criterion. Step 3 quantified over every finite A-algebra, exactly its hypothesis, and A is Noetherian by the item. It concludes J-2 for A; no excellence or universal catenarity is taken from it.

Step 5 uses def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring to get finite generation of the maximal ideal, then lem-weil-pbc-cohen-structure-power-series-surjection and lem-weil-pbc-power-series-field-cohen-regular-complete. I opened their statements: the first supplies the surjection with field/Cohen coefficients without a domain or characteristic-zero assumption on A; the second explicitly says the source is Noetherian and regular local for both kinds of coefficients. The argument permits zero variables and does not require A to be regular.

Step 6 uses lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-localization and lem-trf-regular-ring-universally-catenary. The latter assumes regularity at every prime of a Noetherian ring, not just regularity at its maximal ideal, so the former is cited to verify precisely that missing hypothesis. The conclusion is universal catenarity, including finite bounds on chain lengths, and not merely catenarity of the source ring.

Step 7 uses def-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra and def-exc-excellent-ring. This is an elementary definitional argument: a polynomial expression over A has finitely many coefficients, each lifts across the surjection from S, so the same finite algebra generators work over S. The ring T and its prime chains have not changed, so its catenarity from the universal quantifier for S proves the universal quantifier for A. No uncited theorem on quotient descent, dimension formulas, or prime-chain transport is hidden here. Step 8 uses the excellence definition to assemble exactly the conditions already proved.

I tested the item on a field, on a complete DVR such as Z_p, and on a singular complete local ring such as k[[x,y]]/(xy). The singular example is excellent but is not regular, so the proof correctly avoids claiming A is regular. The new domain theorem holds for a field (all of Spec), while the domain assumption is essential: k[epsilon]/(epsilon^2) is a complete local ring with no regular point. The new finite-algebra criterion includes the zero finite algebra, whose regular locus is empty and open; no nonempty-open assumption is imposed on general finite algebras. Prime quotients in Step 2 are nonzero by definition of a prime. For the Cohen source, n=0 yields a field or a Cohen DVR, and the quotient-descent step still applies.
