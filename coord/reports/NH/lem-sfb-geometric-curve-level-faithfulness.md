## Review by NH

I reviewed the completed nodes myself against delivery-errors B–E, with the source texts and cited statements open. There is no second worker in this direct-delivery brief.

For the item I checked Pikaart–de Jong §§1.1 and 2.3.3 online and the local Milne passages listed above. The hypotheses are algebraically closed base, smooth projective connected curve, genus at least two, and an odd prime invertible on the base. No finite-field restriction was inserted. The automorphism is over the base field; this is essential for the roots-of-unity trivialization to be equivariant. No assumption that it fixes a chosen curve point is introduced.

For the new abelian rigidity lemma I read Milne I Proposition 14.4(b), including the preceding polarization convention in I §11. I state a group automorphism preserving the polarization, not an arbitrary automorphism of the underlying variety. I explicitly retain n invertible: testing only geometric n-torsion points cannot justify a statement in the characteristic-primary case. The inline definition of polarization uses an ample line bundle and the dual Picard variety, and preserves the equation a-dual composed with lambda composed with a = lambda. Its n >= 3 condition cannot be dropped: [-1] fixes all 2-torsion on a positive-dimensional polarized abelian variety but is nontrivial in characteristic zero. The zero-dimensional abelian variety satisfies the statement trivially. This theorem is independent of the curve case and needs no curve genus assumption.

For the new Jacobian faithfulness lemma I read Milne III §6 for the canonical theta polarization, and Theorem 12.1(b) for uniqueness of the curve isomorphism with a fixed sign. I use pullback on the Picard functor, which is inverse to the usual covariant Jacobian map for a curve automorphism, and therefore do not call the resulting assignment a group homomorphism. Pullback preserves the canonical polarization. Fixed-sign uniqueness includes the hyperelliptic case, so I have not discarded hyperelliptic curves or characteristic two. Genus at least two is essential: translations of a genus-one curve induce the identity on Picard^0; in genus zero the Jacobian is zero and cannot detect automorphisms. The statement introduces the Jacobian via its representing isomorphism, rather than choosing data from an unspecified existence statement. The canonical polarization is a mathematical theorem about this actual object, not hollow data.
