## What was wrong and what changed

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported blueprint's assertion that arbitrary extraction of fraction representatives terminates because the paths are identities or cocycles is false. Keep the accepted counterexample: let

    A = Z[f,e_0,e_1,...]/(f e_i : i >= 0).

In A[1/f], every e_i vanishes, and one may choose the fraction representatives

    f = (f^2 + e_0)/f,
    f^(n+2) + e_n = (f^(n+3) + e_(n+1))/f.

With f already adjoined, each newly selected numerator adjoins a new e_i. Modulo f these variables are algebraically independent, so this representative-extraction procedure never stabilizes. Two copies of Spec A glued by the identity along D(f) give a qcqs example. This only disproves that procedure: the coefficient subring generated by f already works in this example. The corrected statement does not claim that compatible finite models fail to exist.

### E5 / C4: compatible refinements and both direct citers

The index order is componentwise containment OF MODELS, not of arbitrary finite coefficient sets. For i <= j every chart transition is literal inclusion B_{P,i} -> B_{P,j}. Each localized transition sends b/f^m to b/f^m. Every comparison square commutes because its two composites send this fraction to the identical original fraction phi(b)/phi(f)^m. Thus a common bound is again a compatible record, by its type. There is no unsupported operation extending a map to an arbitrary larger subring.

For every marked affine inclusion P subset Q, its image at every index is the same marked union of basic opens D(f). Pulling back such a union along Spec(B_{Q,j}) -> Spec(B_{Q,i}) gives the same union at j. On each basic open, the small P and Q coordinate rings identify by the required localization isomorphisms, and both identifications use the original fractions. Thus the open squares are cartesian; equivalently

    B_{Q,j} tensor_{B_{Q,i}} B_{P,i} -> B_{P,j}

is the canonical isomorphism. Replacing B_{Q,j} by the ORIGINAL A_Q recovers the original affine open P. This is a chartwise statement about marked open restrictions. It does not assert that each entire original chart is the base change of an unrelated chart over a single common coefficient ring.
