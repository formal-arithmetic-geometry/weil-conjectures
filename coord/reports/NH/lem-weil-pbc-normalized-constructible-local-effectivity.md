The missing arguments behind those new statements were tested as follows. A bounded finite-module source over a finite ring has a bounded-above finite free resolution. For a bounded target, only a finite truncation deep enough below the target affects degree-zero Hom and the finitely many negative Hom groups: maps from the remaining sufficiently low tail vanish by the t-structure. Thus the degree-zero sheaf Hom is locally the ordinary finite D(B) Hom, and isomorphisms form a finite locally constant subsheaf. Local bounded constant-complex descriptions come from 094G. Connectedness makes the local isomorphism type constant, even without projectivity. The finite-ring condition, rather than finite Tor dimension, gives finite Hom sets.

For simultaneous trivializations, at a fixed n the images in the finite locally constant I_n stabilize fibrewise; on each connected stratum the stabilization index is the same, because a map of finite locally constant sheaves has constant image rank there. Stable images are nonempty finite locally constant sheaves, and their transition maps are surjective. Their finite-etale representations have a pro-etale inverse limit cover; compatible sections then exist on that cover. Refine it by weakly contractible affine coverings. On such objects exact sections identify sheafified derived degree-zero Hom with actual derived morphisms (the relevant derived Hom is bounded below). Consequently the compatible sheaf sections give actual isomorphisms and equations in the localized derived categories, rather than only equations after sheafification. This refinement is important because derived Hom presheaves are not generally sheaves.

For completed realization, finite-module completion is exact over a Noetherian ring and the topology induced on a submodule is equivalent to its adic topology. Passing to constant finite quotient sheaves and their inverse limits preserves those finite-module exact sequences on the pro-etale site. Completed constant finite free sheaves are flat, using the existing constant/completion scalar comparison. A bounded-above finite free resolution of a bounded finite module complex therefore computes its derived reductions after realization; exact realization identifies it with the realized bounded complex. For the relative statement use the constant ring C (which is complete), the flat map constant C to u^-1 completed A_T, and its finite quotient identity. Derived scalar reassociation gives reduction over that restricted ring. This is precisely why the relative clause was included.

Edge cases: zero complexes give singleton isomorphism sheaves and the zero local complex; I=0 reduces to bounded finite-ring local constancy; I=A forces every quotient, hence every level, to be zero. A finite singular ring with I=0 and a nonprojective finite module in degree zero satisfies the theorem and is represented by that module, not by a bounded projective resolution. For a<b, good truncation can have a nonprojective bottom term. Nothing uses ell invertibility, a field base, a discrete valuation coefficient ring, finite Tor amplitude, or projectivity of local terms. The bound is independent of strata, n, and covering member because good truncation uses the original a,b.
