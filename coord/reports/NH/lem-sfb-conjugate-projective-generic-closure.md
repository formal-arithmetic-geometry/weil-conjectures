I read ILO, Exposé X, Theorem 3.4, printed pp.160--161, especially Step 4. I also read Stacks coherent.tex, lemma-chow-Noetherian (0200) and remark-chow-Noetherian (0201), schemes.tex lemma-reduced-closed-subscheme (01J3), lemma-map-into-reduction (0356), lemma-fibre-product-after-map (01KR), lemma-section-immersion (01KT), and constructions.tex lemma-segre-embedding (01WD). There are no new nodes requiring additional sources. I checked the actual statement of each cited DAG node; citations.json copies the statements verbatim so the reviewer can compare every use.

Step 1 uses projective-implies-proper and proper-composition to verify the properness hypothesis for T/k. The existing integral Chow relatively ample statement actually supplies integral Y, a proper surjection, an immersion and a nonempty open isomorphism; it does not just supply an arbitrary reduced Chow scheme. Its additional ampleness clause is unused. Projective-space properness supplies separatedness of the target, proper-over-separated makes the immersion proper, and proper-immersion-closed supplies the closed embedding. Thus no unproved assertion that the arbitrary Chow output is integral is hidden here.

Step 2 uses the fibre-product and affine function-field/stalk statements to identify the given Spec L with the generic point of T. The nonempty generic fibre makes r dominant, and the singleton fibre must contain the generic point of integral T. The chosen open U contains that point, so the Chow map remains an isomorphism after this base change. The field embedding L into M then defines f with its stated structural map. This is an elementary application of the supplied open isomorphism, not a new birational-generic-fibre lemma.

Step 3 uses the fibre-product universal property for q after explicitly checking h_j v f d_(j inverse) = u eta_B. Projective-base-change applies to a field automorphism and produces exactly the induced k-structure on the same abstract Y: pullback along b_j inverse has second projection b_j composed with the old structure map. The projective-morphism definition in that cited node means a finite-space embedding plus quasi-compactness; Y already is of finite type and projective, so there is no convention change. This also preserves the dimension n of the chosen projective space.

Step 4 applies the existing projective-fibre-product closed-model node only to the equal-dimensional family of Y_j. Its nonempty-index hypothesis holds even when J is the trivial group. Its base X is the scheme S of this item; its base called S in that node is Spec k. S/k is separated. B is then added by the separate existing change-of-base closed-immersion statement, followed by closed-immersion base change and composition and the Segre statement. Thus I do not apply the equal-dimension theorem to B with an unjustified embedding into the same space.

Steps 5 and 6 use just the fibre-product universal property, with all structural maps and group laws written out. In particular the j component of g_P is the factor indexed by g inverse times j, and the resulting component of g_P q is f d_(j inverse times g). This agrees with q d_g, with no assumption that the group commutes. The convention d_g = Spec(sigma_g inverse) is essential to this calculation. Faithfulness is never used.

Step 7 uses the reduced-closed-subscheme statement for existence and reducedness; irreducibility and nonemptiness of the closure of one point give integrality by the integral-scheme definition. Closed-immersion composition and the projective definition give the finite-type projective structure. The reduced-source factorization node actually states factorization into an arbitrary closed subscheme whenever its support contains the source image. It applies both to Spec M and to C, so the action restriction is a scheme action, not merely an action on the underlying closed set. Uniqueness of these factorizations follows from the affine quotient description in the closed-immersion definition: an algebra map annihilating an ideal factors uniquely through its quotient. The group relations descend by cancellation, and the displayed p-equivariance follows from the B projection relation.

Step 8 uses projective properness, proper-over-separated and proper base change to make P_K to B_K = Spec M separated. Associativity of fibre products identifies this base change with P_K even though Spec M is generally not a k-rational point. The existing section theorem states that a section of a separated morphism is a closed immersion. This proves the exact residue field M, rather than merely a subfield or a purely inseparable extension. There is no separability hypothesis to verify or to add.

Step 9 uses the explicit radical-ideal description in the reduced-induced-closed-subscheme statement, the affine generic-fibre localization from the fibre-product construction, and the affine quotient description in the closed-immersion definition. The calculation of the kernel after localization is written out by testing the numerator, since every nonzero element of A maps to a unit of M. Surjectivity after localization is supplied by the closed immersion from step 8. Charts not meeting x cannot meet its closure. This is a routine affine computation from the cited inputs, not an invocation of an unstated general closure/base-change theorem. It excludes spurious generic components and nilpotents.

Step 10 uses the integral-affine function-field theorem, whose statement explicitly identifies the generic stalk with the fraction field on any nonempty affine open. The localization already calculated is the field M, so its fraction field is M as well. Equivariance comes from the equality of morphisms in step 6, not an independent arbitrary isomorphism. On functions the scheme action is sigma_g inverse. Step 11 uses only the fibre-product relation for the identity-index factor; no action on T or Y has been introduced.
