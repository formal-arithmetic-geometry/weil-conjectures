## Review by Q93

I read the source's Lemma 4.2 and Claim 4.3 on printed pp.4–5. The source assumes A Noetherian, X finite type over Spec A, and A/I J-2, exactly as the node does. It first proves constructibility on irreducible closed subsets of V(IB), using a regular sequence at a regular generic point, then uses generalization stability. I checked the source against the relevant Stacks texts: algebra.tex 00FN (lemma-Noetherian-permanence), 00NQ (lemma-regular-ring-CM), 061L (lemma-regular-sequence-in-neighbourhood), 00NU (lemma-regular-mod-x), 0AFS (lemma-localization-of-regular-local-is-regular), and more-algebra.tex 07P7 (definition-J) and 07P9 (lemma-intersection-regular-with-closed). For the last topological step I read topology.tex 053Z (lemma-characterize-constructible-Noetherian) and 0542 (lemma-characterize-closed-Noetherian); the existing spectrum specialization uses the stronger 00I0 without a Noetherian hypothesis. The exact statements of every cited DAG node were opened, not inferred from titles. citations.json contains their verbatim statements for each use.

Step 1 uses def-weil-finite-type-morphism's finite affine cover; lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian says both finite-type rings B and B/IB are Noetherian. lem-weil-pbc-spec-quotient-homeomorphism-zero-locus says precisely that Spec(B/IB) is V(IB) with its subspace topology. lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization explicitly provides the isomorphism from the localization to the ambient stalk, so no identification of the quotient's stalk with the ambient stalk is made.

Step 2 uses lem-weil-pbc-primes-of-localization for the prime over a smaller prime, lem-weil-pbc-localize-localization-at-prime for the actual local-ring isomorphism, and lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-localization for regularity. Their statements give all three facts, and the inclusion direction is p subset q, so a regular q implies a regular p.

Step 3 uses the quotient-spectrum homeomorphism and lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-closed-subsets-primes, whose statement identifies irreducible closed subsets with V(q) and singleton closure with containment. The nonregular generic point case is the contrapositive of step 2, not an assertion that a quotient has no regular points. The empty irreducible closed subset is not a case: irreducible subsets here are nonempty.

Step 4 uses the J-2 clause actually present in def-exc-excellent-ring; no G-ring, excellence, or completeness hypothesis is taken from that definition. B/q is finite type over A/I because generators descend and I maps to zero. The generic localization is the field of fractions: a nonzero fraction a/s has inverse s/a. Its maximal ideal is zero and its dimension is zero, proving regularity directly from def-weil-regular-scheme. The basic-open basis lemma and quotient-spectrum homeomorphism give b outside q. This does not require the regular locus of B itself to be open.

Step 5 uses the regular-local definition to choose d generators and the explicit localization definition to write fractions with numerators in q. Multiplying the generators individually by units leaves their generated ideal unchanged. lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-generators-regular-sequence then applies to exactly d generators of the maximal ideal. lem-exc-regular-sequence-principal-neighbourhood applies to the resulting elements of the Noetherian ring B, not to a sequence whose entries still live only in B_q. Its statement gives the required principal localization.

Step 6 cites the Noetherian definition (every ideal finitely generated) and lem-exc-localization-ideal-membership-clearing. That latter statement gives a multiplier outside q for each generator of q lying in the localized ideal K. Multiplying the finitely many multipliers proves the required ideal equality after localizing at any prime avoiding s; the argument is explicitly written rather than hidden behind 'clear denominators'.

Step 7 cites lem-exc-weakly-regular-sequence-localization, whose statement gives injectivity on all successive quotients and deliberately does not claim final nonzero quotient. The sequence lies in the eventual maximal ideal, which supplies that missing condition by def-weil-pbc-regular-sequence. The localization B_g to B_p is given by fractions since g avoids p. The quotient isomorphism is exactly the formula in lem-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime-of-quotient. lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian supplies Noetherianity, and lem-exc-regular-local-lift-regular-sequence-quotient applies with all entries in the maximal ideal. It is this last statement, not an invalid inference from an arbitrary regular quotient, that proves ambient regularity.

Step 8 uses lem-trf-noetherian-constructible-subset-criterion with the scheme Spec C and its single affine cover by a Noetherian ring. Steps 3 and 7 give exactly the two alternatives in its statement on every irreducible closed subset. Then lem-trf-spectrum-constructible-generalization-open is applied to Spec C, not to an arbitrary closed subspace without an identification. The quotient homeomorphism preserves inclusion of primes and identifies both topology and the subset in question. Thus all hypotheses of this Mathlib leaf are available.

Step 9 is elementary gluing and closed-subspace topology. I corrected its continuity citation during self-review: def-pc-scheme's short statement does not explicitly define the underlying continuous map, so the final version cites def-weil-locally-ringed-space-morphism, which does. The spectrum closed-set statement gives V(I) closed. Writing W as Z intersect F for F closed in X proves closedness in X. No quasi-compact image theorem or separatedness is used.

The proof never assumes X reduced or integral. Only B/q is a domain. Each non-elementary step is under 900 characters, so no long-step extraction is concealed.
