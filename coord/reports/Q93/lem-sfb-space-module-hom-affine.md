## Review by Q93

I read the current unit and chapter and the latest blueprint's constructibility/direct-image material, then the actual Stacks proof of Tag 08K6 and its basic case 08JY in `quot.tex`. I also read the Hom-sheaf and base-change arguments 08JV and 08JW. For every proof citation I opened the DAG node and checked its statement, not just its title or its proof. In particular, scheme descent alone is insufficient for an algebraic space, smallness is required for the limits, and the flatness conventions of the eventual node and this item require a comparison. The following is my check of the arguments and their hypotheses.

1. The fibre-product node supplies the displayed base changes, and the module-functor definition supplies ordinary tensor pullback. The written tensor formulas give the composition comparison and its naturality. `lem-sfb-space-module-pullback-quasicoherent` explicitly preserves quasi-coherence. The separate sentence “Tensor pullback preserves finite presentation” in `lem-sfb-space-fp-module-limit-category` is unconditional: this use does not need an inverse system. This proves the base-change identity of Hom without claiming that arbitrary non-flat base change preserves Hom groups on X itself.

2. The covering-base-change and fppf-to-fpqc nodes apply to the SCHEMES V_a over a scheme chart V. `lem-pc-fpqc-descent` is an equivalence and therefore fully faithful, so it glues maps with the specified compatibility. The Zariski/etale equivalence and the pushforward-restriction comparison give precisely the scheme modules and their pullbacks used there. `lem-sfb-space-qcoh-cartesian-chart-family` includes morphisms, not just objects; hence uniqueness on each chart glues the maps on all charts into a map of modules on X_T. This is the elementary assembly of the cited descent equivalences, not a citation of an unrelated Yoneda full-faithfulness statement.

3. The affine qcqs and fp-source nodes give the absolute qcqs hypotheses on X. `lem-sfb-space-module-fp-directed-approximation` explicitly gives a SMALL filtered category and a colimit in ALL module sheaves, exactly the form needed here. Tensor pullback has the adjunction specified in the module-functor node, so `lem-weil-pbc-left-adjoint-preserves-colimits` applies. The Hom-to-colimit formula is the universal property written out, including compatibility with T.

4. `lem-weil-pbc-spec-small-colimit-limit` applies to arbitrary small ring diagrams, not only a directed set; this matters because the module approximation supplied a filtered category. Yoneda converts the module diagram into affine-scheme transitions contravariantly, and then into a ring colimit in the original direction. The maps to B are compatible, giving the limit in the slice too. The argument claims only affineness for this inverse limit; it does not infer finite presentation of an infinite colimit algebra.

5. `lem-sfb-space-change-base-to-integers` identifies the etale sites and structure sheaves after forgetting to Spec Z, so it does not alter the modules. `lemma-nagata-noetherian-inverse-system-affine` supplies the directed system of affine bases with affine transitions. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian` applies to the finitely generated integer algebras; the needed Noetherian property of Z is justified by the least-positive-generator argument. `pqf-def-locnoeth` supplies the affine-ring criterion. Thus this is not a spreading-out invocation over a possibly non-quasi-separated algebraic-space base: it has already been reduced to an affine scheme base.

6. `lem-sfb-fp-space-qcqs-base-limit-descent` is applied to schemes B_i that are qcqs with affine transitions. It gives a finitely presented model of X. The fp permanence, affine locality/permanence and qcqs-source nodes then give the hypotheses for the module continuity node. The base-change limit clause of `lem-sfb-space-affine-limits-schematic-stages` identifies X with this inverse system's limit. The fp-module continuity node descends F and G separately; directedness gives one common stage. Both modules are finitely presented at this point: F by the reduction, G by the original hypothesis. No finite-presentation assertion about an arbitrary F was introduced.

7. I read the exact fixed-model hypotheses of `lem-sfb-space-module-eventually-flat` and `lem-sfb-space-module-eventually-proper-support` and the support equivalence in `lem-sfb-space-module-proper-carrier-support`. Here the bases are affine over Spec Z, X_i is finitely presented over them and G_i is a fixed finitely presented quasi-coherent model. The carrier equivalence applies because f is locally finite type and G is finite type. Eventual flatness is explicitly persistent at every later stage, so the later support stage can be chosen without losing it. For the stalk convention, `lem-sfb-etale-zariski-module-geometric-stalk` supplies the module tensor formula as well as the local-ring identification, the Zariski/etale equivalence applies to the chart module, and `lem-sc-strict-henselization-faithfully-flat` plus `lem-trf-relative-flatness-source-cover` preserve and reflect relative flatness after that source extension.
