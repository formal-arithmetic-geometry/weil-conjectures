## Review by Q93

I checked the split with Morrison's actual use, Milne's orbit argument and the Stacks statements open. For each citation I verified the statement, rather than assuming a result from the node's name or its proof. The following are the particular checks and corrections made while preparing it.

1. Step 1 uses `lem-kun-projective-implies-proper`, `lem-weil-pbc-ample-very-ample-immersion-affine-base`, `lemma-nagata-proper-to-separated-target`, and `lem-weil-pbc-immersion-closed-image-closed-immersion`. The ample definition is the affine-open-cover convention; the polarization node instead assumes a positive power comes from a closed projective embedding. I explicitly bridged that difference. The ample theorem gives only an immersion, so properness and separatedness of projective space are used to show its image closed before the closed-immersion criterion is applied. This avoids silently adding a very-ampleness hypothesis to the item.
2. Step 2 uses the affine-chart clause of `lem-sfb-projective-polarization-section-algebra` on projective matrix space with determinant of positive degree. The local-generator/scalar-quotient description identifies the matrix open with PGL on arbitrary base algebras, not only on k-points. Its affine charts are determinant localizations of polynomial domains and have nonempty intersections; this checks reducedness and irreducibility without assuming a characteristic restriction. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian` applies over a field, and `lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-scheme-closed-subsets-minimal` supplies the scheme-to-topology bridge (Stacks 01OZ, read locally), and `lem-sfb-noetherian-space-compact-subsets` then gives the finite subcovers needed below. I added this bridge during the final C1 check, so that scheme Noetherianity is not silently identified with the topology hypothesis of the compactness statement.
3. Step 3 is the two-line rational-point consequence of `lem-sc-quasi-compact-kolmogorov-closed-point` and `lem-weil-pbc-closed-point-residue-field-base`. I use the former on an affine open, and the latter on that open itself. Closedness in the whole original scheme is not a hypothesis of this application. This also supplies rational points in nonempty orbit-map fibres over k-rational points and in nonempty boundary strata.
4. Step 4 applies `lem-wf-affine-constructible-open` only to maps between affine finite-type schemes. The finite affine cover of the inverse image is justified in Step 2. Thus no general non-affine form is extracted from an affine statement. `lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme` constructs C with the same closed underlying set; its finite-type property follows by quotienting affine coordinate algebras. Irreducibility and invariance under rational translations are written out directly.
5. Step 5 again uses the affine constructible-open node, now on an affine open of C. Rational transitivity alone does not cover generic points of the orbit image. The proof therefore takes the preimage in G of the complement of the translated open and uses rational-point detection to show that closed subset empty. This corrects that possible point-versus-scheme gap and proves local closedness of the entire topological image.
6. Step 6 cites `def-weil-proper-morphism` only for what its statement defines: separatedness means closed diagonal. Properness of Z was already proved in Step 1. The fibre identification is checked over every k-algebra, with the explicit inverse translations, so it is scheme-theoretic. Group operations preserve the equalizer. This is an elementary step, not a new stabilizer lemma.
7. Step 7 uses the full statement of `lem-zl-irreducible-fibre-dimension-lower-bound`, especially its dense open equality locus, not merely its lower bound. Its source and target are G and Z, both finite type, and G is irreducible by Step 2. The equality locus contains a rational g. `lem-ag-rational-point-stalk-topological-local-dimension` identifies local topological fibre dimension with stalk dimension at that rational point. `lem-ag-group-identity-local-dimension` applies to the finite-type stabilizer even if it is nonreduced. `lem-sc-dimension-locally-algebraic` applies to the nonempty irreducible reduced closure C and its nonempty open O. `lem-kun-dimension-finite-type` rules out infinite-dimensional subtraction. These statements give every equality used in the calculation.
8. Step 8 uses `def-kun-topological-krull-dimension` for its actual chain definition. Appending the irreducible C_S to a chain in its proper closed boundary gives the strict drop; dimension monotonicity is justified by closure-and-intersection of chains. No separate boundary-dimension theorem is assumed. The boundary is invariant under rational translations, and the preimage under an orbit map is closed and contains dense G(k); this proves containment of the full orbit image. Semistability is used only through the given invariant-section definition.
9. Step 9 applies the argument already proved to the orbit of minimum natural-number dimension in C_S. The original orbit ensures nonemptiness; Step 3 supplies a rational point if its boundary is nonempty. Its closure stays in the closed subset C_S of S. Thus existence follows without asserting that dimension induction also proves uniqueness.
10. Step 10 uses `lem-sfb-projective-polarization-section-algebra` exactly for the affineness and finite-type property of Z_s after Step 1 checks its embedding hypothesis. The common-degree powers and choice of a scalar avoid zero values at both alleged closed-orbit representatives. I verified why this common chart also contains z and its whole orbit: otherwise the invariant zero locus would contain the original closure and hence y_1. Merely choosing a chart around one alleged closed orbit would not suffice.
11. Step 11 uses `lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme` for the two closed orbit subsets of this affine chart, then `lem-pc-crt-ideal-quotient-product` for the comaximal ideals. Nonempty intersection would have a rational point and rational lifts in both orbit maps, contradicting distinctness. A proper ideal sum would have a maximal ideal, so it cannot occur. This verifies the comaximality hypothesis before CRT is used.
12. Step 12 uses `lem-wf-affine-product-reduced`, whose statement explicitly includes the vanishing principle on products. Its two factors really are reduced affine finite-type schemes over algebraically closed k. The ambient E can be nonreduced. Pullbacks of functions in each I_i vanish at every pair of rational points, hence vanish identically; this proves ideal stability and equivariance of the quotient map. I do not mistake invariance of k-point sets for invariance under the full group scheme without that product argument.
13. Step 13 uses the power-lifting clause of `lem-sfb-pgl-geometric-reductivity` on the finite-type algebras E and E/I_1 times E/I_2, with the equivariant surjection established in Steps 11--12. The invariant element (0,1) is idempotent, so every positive power is itself. The obtained invariant function separates the two orbits in every characteristic. There is no assumption of linear reductivity or exact invariants.
14. Step 14 is the direct coaction evaluation at z, followed by taking a closed zero set containing its orbit and closure inside U. It gives simultaneously f(z)=0 and f(z)=1. This proves uniqueness without relying on a quotient theorem or a new node that merely repeats that clause of the item.
15. Step 15 audits the reducedness hypotheses in the preceding steps. Only C and the two closed-orbit test schemes are given reduced structures. The stabilizer itself is retained throughout the fibre-dimension calculation, and both ambient E and the PGL lifting theorem allow nilpotents. Thus the nonreduced clauses are proved, not removed.
