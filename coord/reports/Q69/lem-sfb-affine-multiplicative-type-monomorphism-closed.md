## Mathematical argument for the new auxiliary

Source read: literature/stacks-project/groupoids.tex, lines 2340–2370, Tag 0EKK, label lemma-complete-reducibility-Gm. That source states the Z-grading case, not the full arbitrary-M statement here. The following direct proof explains the generalization without any omitted geometric input.

The canonical mutually inverse maps between A tensor_R R[M] and finite M-indexed A-valued sums send a tensor b tensor (sum r_m e_m) to (r_m b)_m, and a family (a_m) to sum a_m tensor e_m. Bilinearity and finite supports prove both identities. Thus every rho(a) has a unique finite expression sum p_m(a) tensor e_m, with R-linear coefficient maps p_m. The counit gives a=sum p_m(a). Coassociativity, read in the twice-indexed finite A-valued sums, gives p_n(p_m(a))=0 for n different from m and p_m(p_m(a))=p_m(a). Consequently p_m(a) is in A_m. Conversely, for a in A_m its coefficient projections are zero except p_m(a)=a. Applying projections to a finite homogeneous sum proves uniqueness. Multiplicativity of rho gives rho(ab)=ab tensor e_(m+n) for a in A_m, b in A_n; the unit and structure scalars have degree zero because rho is an R-algebra map. These formulas prove every clause. They use no division by characters or orders, flatness of A, field assumption, or finite dimension. For R=0 all modules are zero; for M=0 all of A is degree zero; for M=Z/p over characteristic p the basis elements remain linearly independent despite nonreduced group schemes.
