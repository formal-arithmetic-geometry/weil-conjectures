## Review by Q69

I reviewed the statement against Milne, not just its dependency list. The orbit is the image of a scheme morphism with the reduced locally closed structure. I kept the scheme-theoretic stabilizer throughout; replacing it by its reduced subscheme, or replacing flatness of the orbit map by smoothness, would invalidate the arbitrary-characteristic proof. The seven steps retain Milne's constructibility/translation and generic-flatness/translation arguments. Every cited statement was opened and read. citations.json quotes the complete statements, including hypotheses, so those hypotheses remain visible in the review packet.

Step 1 uses finite type over a Noetherian base, finite type to a separated target, finite type algebra finite presentation, smooth-over-field regularity, and regular-connected integrality. Each cited statement says exactly this, with no characteristic restriction. The group is nonempty because of its identity. The Noetherian-open, quasi-compact-open and composition nodes justify finite type of all the reduced locally closed schemes later used. A field is Noetherian directly, since its ideals are zero and the unit ideal. The Jacobson node supplies density of closed points in every closed subset; applying this after open restriction supplies points in every nonempty locally closed subset. The closed-point residue-field node identifies them as k-rational. This prevents confusing Milne's closed-point convention with the full underlying scheme space.

Step 2 applies Chevalley to a quasi-compact locally finitely presented orbit map, with quasi-compact quasi-separated target: step 1 establishes these hypotheses. The affine constructible-open node is used ONLY on the affine open A, not on an arbitrary scheme. It gives a nonempty open in the closure of S intersect A. Translates cover the inverse image in G because that open contains every closed point of G. A nonempty fibre over the chosen rational point has a rational point by the same finite-type closed-point statements. Thus the proof covers all points of the orbit image, including nonclosed points.

Step 3 uses the reduced induced closed-structure node for existence and uniqueness, and the reduced-source factorization node only with the reduced source G. O is integral because it is reduced and the image of an irreducible source. The generic-flatness node requires an integral target and quasi-compact locally finite type map; these hold for G to O. Flatness on translates is checked on stalks through explicit inverse translations, so this step does not assert an unexplained flatness descent theorem. The covering argument is again the closed-point test already proved. Surjectivity is by the definition of the orbit image, so flat plus surjective is exactly faithful flatness.

Step 4 uses the perfect-field geometric-reducedness node with the reduced finite-type O. An algebraically closed field is perfect, as written out in the step. The dense-smooth-open node requires local finite presentation and geometric reducedness, both now checked. Isomorphisms preserve smoothness by its definition. Transitivity then makes the open smooth locus contain every closed point, and the Jacobson statement makes its closed complement empty. This proves smoothness of O without asserting that G to O is smooth.

Step 5 first checks finiteness before using a natural-number dimension in the equidimensional node. The identity stalk of G has finite Krull dimension; the identity-dimension node identifies global dimension with it. The irreducible-source fibre-dimension node then bounds the closure dimension, and the dense-open dimension comparison gives finite dimension of O. The fibre at x is identified with G_x on all test schemes. Its closed immersion is the pullback of the separated diagonal along the section. The proper/fp permanence node supplies base change of a closed immersion and finite type of that immersion. The flat composite fibre-dimension statement is applied to q and the structure map, at the actual pair e,x; its equality requires flatness of q, proved in step 3. The group identity-dimension statement allows ANY finite-type group scheme, so it applies to a nonreduced stabilizer. Its stalk/topological comparison statement applies at a rational identity point. The equidimensional-open statement is applied with O as its own unique irreducible component. Consequently all three local dimensions in 02JS give the desired global formula.

Step 6 uses smooth-flat and smooth-geometrically-reduced exactly as stated for the structural map of G. The flat geometric-reduced pullback node is applied to the reduced locally Noetherian T equal to Z or B, establishing reducedness of G times T. Density and rationality of its closed points then test containment of the action image in the CLOSED subset T. Only AFTER this containment and reducedness are established is the reduced-source factorization node applied. In particular the argument does not use the false general inference that a map with set image in an arbitrary closed subscheme factors through it. The invariant structures on Z and B are their reduced ones.

Step 7 uses the dense-open dimension node with the finite-type Z and its dense open O. Irreducibility of Z and properness of its closed boundary let one append Z to every irreducible closed chain in the boundary. This proves the strict dimension drop directly; no new dimension lemma is hidden. Subspace monotonicity is justified by taking closures of a chain and intersecting back. Invariance puts each boundary orbit in B, giving the strict inequality. The empty boundary is treated separately.

In the last example the stabilizer is alpha_p, the orbit map is Frobenius, flat but not smooth, and the dimension formula reads 1=0+1. Thus none of our steps smuggles in smoothness or reducedness of the stabilizer. No new statements needed edge-case testing because there are no new nodes.
