## Proof in words for the new generic tool

Let C,D,u,A,R_set be as in the new statement, write R=R_set, and choose a quasi-inverse L. Write F_a(c)=Hom_A(a,F(c)) for an A-valued sheaf F on C. These are set sheaves by the definition in 00VR.

For a set sheaf H on C define a small category I_H whose objects are pairs (c,s) with s in H(c), and whose arrows (c,s) to (c',s') are maps f:c' to c satisfying H(f)(s)=s'. There is a diagram I_H to A sending (c,s) to F(c) and f to F(f). It is small in V because C and each section set of H are small there; this is not a diagram indexed by the whole topos. Let T_F(H) be its limit. Its cone universal property identifies Hom_A(a,T_F(H)) naturally with Nat(H,F_a): a cone is exactly a family of maps a to F(c), one for each section s, compatible with restriction. This identification follows directly in both directions by evaluation on (c,s). No global representability theorem or adjoint functor theorem is assumed. A map H to H' restricts cones and induces T_F(H') to T_F(H), and maps F to F' induce maps of the limits; uniqueness proves all composition, identity and naturality laws. For H=h_c the identification and Yoneda give T_F(h_c) isomorphic to F(c), naturally in a,F,c.

For each d in D set G(d)=T_F(R(h_d)). Restricting a represented sheaf along u is a sheaf since u is continuous and D is subcanonical. A map d' to d induces R(h_d') to R(h_d), so the contravariance of T makes G a presheaf. There are natural bijections
Hom_A(a,G(d)) = Nat(R(h_d),F_a) = Nat(h_d,L(F_a)) = L(F_a)(d).
The middle one uses the equivalence R and the last one uses Yoneda on the subcanonical site D. As L(F_a) is a sheaf, all Hom tests of G are sheaves, so G is an A-valued sheaf. Each construction is natural in F, giving a functor S on A-valued sheaves.

The left adjoint of R carries h_c to h_{u(c)} by 04D3. Since R is an equivalence, its unit is invertible, and consequently R(h_{u(c)}) is naturally isomorphic to h_c. Thus (R_A S F)(c)=T_F(R(h_{u(c)})) is naturally isomorphic to T_F(h_c), hence to F(c).

For completeness, R_A is fully faithful. For a map alpha:R_A K to R_A K' take Hom_A(a,-), yielding a map R(K_a) to R(K'_a). Full faithfulness of R gives a unique map K_a to K'_a. Uniqueness makes it natural also in a. At each d, full faithfulness of the Yoneda embedding of A turns this natural family into a unique arrow K(d) to K'(d). Naturality in d and equality after restriction follow by the same uniqueness. This proves bijectivity on all Hom sets. Together with the displayed natural isomorphism R_A S F to F it proves R_A is fully faithful and essentially surjective, hence an equivalence of the actual restriction functor. This is one general categorical input independent of universal homeomorphisms, not a rephrasing of the item's geometric assertion.

The finite-limit hypotheses are available in the application; in the tool's proof they also ensure the usual geometric inverse-image construction. The proof works for categories A with all V-small limits and locally V-small Hom sets, so Grothendieck abelian is used only to supply these assumptions. No finite-product forgetful functor from A to sets is assumed.
