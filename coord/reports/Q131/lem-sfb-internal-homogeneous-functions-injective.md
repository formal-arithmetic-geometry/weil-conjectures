## Original arguments retained verbatim for the new workers

### Old step 1 → lem-sfb-internal-homogeneous-transpose-extension

Use the section-ring equivalence of [def-weil-pbc-internal-abelian-sheaf-module-equivalence] to regard internal $A$-modules as sheaves of modules over the section-ring sheaf $A$. It leaves sections, restriction maps and linear maps unchanged. The bilinear universal property identifies tensor products in these descriptions, and the right-adjoint universal property identifies their internal Homs. Equivalences preserve monomorphisms and the extension property defining injectivity: transport a lifting problem and its solution along the inverse equivalence. We may therefore work with these section-module sheaves. By [def-pur-sheaf-hom], a section of an internal Hom over $S$ is a map on the entire slice over $S$; evaluations below are required after every $T\to S$, not just at ordinary points.

### Old step 2 → lem-sfb-internal-homogeneous-transpose-extension

Put $X=G^n$, with $G^0=*$, a profinite object of the chosen small site, and $P=A[\underline X]$. The free-module universal property identifies $P$ with the sheafification of $V\mapsto\bigoplus_{t\in C(V,X)}A(V)$: a map out is exactly a natural assignment of an element to each generator $[t]$, and sheafification does not change maps to a sheaf. This is exactly $j_{X!}A_X$ in [lem-pc-sites-modules-j-shriek-flat]. That node applies to the sheaf of commutative rings $A$ on this site and gives that $P\otimes_A-$ preserves finite limits, hence monomorphisms. This uses flatness, not projectivity, of the free module.

### Old step 3 → lem-sfb-internal-homogeneous-transpose-extension

Let $i:N\hookrightarrow M$ be any monomorphism of internal $G$-equivariant $A$-modules, and $h:N\to H_n(J)$ an equivariant linear map. The forgetful functor $U$ sends $i$ to a monomorphism. Indeed $K(S)=\ker(i_S)$ is a subsheaf of $UN$, because compatible kernel sections glue uniquely in $UN$. Equivariance gives $i_S(gx)=g i_S(x)=0$, so the action of [def-sfb-condensed-action-module] restricts to $K$, naturally after every base change. Its inclusion $k:K\to N$ is equivariant and $ik=0$; monicity of $i$ implies $k=0$. Thus every $i_S$ has zero kernel, so is injective. A sectionwise injective sheaf map is monic, by testing equality of maps on sections.

### Old step 4 → lem-sfb-internal-homogeneous-transpose-extension

For each profinite test object and $\mathbf t=(t_1,\ldots,t_n)$, evaluation of $h(x)$ at $(1,\mathbf t)$ defines $a_{\mathbf t}(x)=h(x)(1,\mathbf t)$. It is $A$-linear in $x$ and natural in the test object and $\mathbf t$. Extend in the free generators of $P$ and apply the tensor universal property, equivalently the natural tensor--Hom adjunction of [lem-pc-sheaf-modules-tensor-closed], to get $\alpha:P\otimes_A UN\to UJ$ with $\alpha([\mathbf t]\otimes x)=a_{\mathbf t}(x)$. The evaluation is the slice evaluation of [def-pur-sheaf-hom], so this is a morphism of sheaves, including all profinite base changes. For $n=0$, the tuple is empty and the evaluation is at the single group coordinate $1$.

### Old step 5 → lem-sfb-internal-homogeneous-transpose-extension

By Steps 2 and 3, $P\otimes_A Ui:P\otimes_A UN\hookrightarrow P\otimes_A UM$ is monic. The assumed injectivity of $UJ$, in precisely the extension sense of [def-sp-injective-resolutions], supplies a sheaf-module morphism $\beta:P\otimes_A UM\to UJ$ satisfying $\beta\circ(P\otimes_A Ui)=\alpha$. No sectionwise splitting or injectivity of the individual modules $J(S)$ is asserted: this is one extension in the category of internal modules.

### Old step 6 → lem-sfb-internal-homogeneous-lift-reconstruction

Define $b:M\to H_n(J)$ on generalized arguments by
$$b(m)(g_0,\ldots,g_n)=g_0\cdot_J\beta\bigl([g_0^{-1}g_1,\ldots,g_0^{-1}g_n]\otimes(g_0^{-1}\cdot_M m)\bigr).$$
Here $m\in M(S)$ is first restricted to $T$ for every $T\to S$, and $g_i\in C(T,G)$. Inversion, multiplication and both internal actions commute with restriction; so does $\beta$. The formula is therefore natural on each slice. For fixed tuple it is $A(T)$-linear in $m$, since the two actions are $A(T)$-linear. The free-module universal property in the tuple, followed by the tensor--Hom adjunction of [lem-pc-sheaf-modules-tensor-closed], gives an internal linear map $b$; the slice description of [def-pur-sheaf-hom] ensures these formulas specify its actual internal-Hom sections. Empty tests and the empty $n$-tuple are interpreted by the same formula.

### Old step 7 → lem-sfb-internal-homogeneous-lift-reconstruction

Check equivariance on every profinite test object. Write $\mathbf r=(g_0^{-1}g_1,\ldots,g_0^{-1}g_n)$. For $k\in\underline G$, the definition gives
$$b(km)(\mathbf g)=g_0\cdot_J\beta([\mathbf r]\otimes(g_0^{-1}k\cdot_M m)).$$
On the other hand the prescribed homogeneous action gives
$$(k\cdot b(m))(\mathbf g)=k\cdot_J\bigl((k^{-1}g_0)\cdot_J\beta([\mathbf r]\otimes((k^{-1}g_0)^{-1}\cdot_M m))\bigr).$$
Since $k(k^{-1}g_0)=g_0$ and $(k^{-1}g_0)^{-1}=g_0^{-1}k$, these expressions agree. These equalities use only the action laws of [def-sfb-condensed-action-module] and hold after all base changes, so $b$ is equivariant.

### Old step 8 → lem-sfb-internal-homogeneous-lift-reconstruction

Finally let $x\in N$ on any test object. Equivariance of $i$ and the extension identity give
$$b(ix)(\mathbf g)=g_0\cdot_J\alpha([\mathbf r]\otimes(g_0^{-1}\cdot_N x))=g_0\cdot_J h(g_0^{-1}\cdot_N x)(1,\mathbf r).$$
Equivariance of $h$ and the stated homogeneous action identify the last evaluation with $g_0\cdot_J(g_0^{-1}\cdot_J h(x)(g_0,g_0r_1,\ldots,g_0r_n))=h(x)(\mathbf g)$. Equality on all generalized tuples, followed by linear extension in the free generators, gives $bi=h$ as internal maps. Every lifting problem has thus been solved, which is exactly injectivity by [def-sp-injective-resolutions].
