## New auxiliary: retained mathematical argument

For lem-sfb-strong-grading-torsor-algebra, here is the full independent argument for the future splitter. It is deliberately in this report, not in the unsplit node's statement or proof fields.

Write B=A_0. For each m choose a finite expression 1=sum_j u_j v_j with u_j of degree m and v_j of degree -m. The products p_j=u_j v_j belong to B and generate its unit ideal. On B[1/p_j], u_j is a homogeneous unit of A[1/p_j] with inverse v_j/p_j. Every degree-m element a is u_j times the degree-zero element av_j/p_j. Multiplication by u_j thus identifies B[1/p_j] with A_m[1/p_j], including injectivity by multiplication with u_j inverse. In particular A_m is locally free of rank one on a finite principal cover, hence finite projective and flat over B. The direct sum A of these flat modules is flat; its degree-zero summand is B itself, so tensoring with A detects a nonzero module, proving faithful flatness.

The canonical comparison beta sends a tensor b_m to ab_m tensor e_m. An explicit inverse on a tensor e_m is sum_j a v_j tensor u_j, with u_j and v_j as above. Beta composed with this expression is a tensor e_m. Conversely, on a tensor b_m, the other composite is sum_j a b_m v_j tensor u_j = a tensor b_m sum_j v_j u_j = a tensor b_m, since b_m v_j is in B. These identities prove that beta is bijective; beta is an algebra homomorphism from the grading multiplication law, so its inverse is an algebra map too. Independence of the unit expression follows from being its inverse, rather than being an extra assertion to assume.

To prove finite presentation, choose finite generators m_1,...,m_r of M. For each generator choose its finite homogeneous-unit expression. Refine the finitely many principal covers just described to one finite principal cover of Spec B on which there is a homogeneous unit u_i of degree m_i for every i. On one such localization, let Z^r map onto M and let l_1,...,l_s generate its kernel, a subgroup of a finitely generated free abelian group. For l in that kernel the unit u^l has degree zero. Set c_j=u^{l_j} in B^times. Then

    A = B[x_1^{+-1},...,x_r^{+-1}] / (x^{l_1}-c_1,...,x^{l_s}-c_s)

on this localization, with x_i mapping to u_i. To check the presentation, in each degree m choose an exponent vector lifting m. All homogeneous Laurent monomials of that degree differ from this chosen monomial by an element of the kernel lattice. The finitely many displayed relations express such a difference as multiplication by the corresponding unit in B, because any lattice relation is an integer combination of the l_j and the variables and c_j are units. Thus the degree-m component is exactly B times the chosen monomial. Its map to A_m is an isomorphism, as multiplication by the corresponding homogeneous unit identifies A_m with B. Summing over degrees proves both surjectivity and absence of further relations. This is a finite Laurent presentation; invertible variables can be presented by pairs x_i,y_i with x_i y_i=1. Finite presentation is local on a finite principal target cover, hence B to A is finitely presented globally. This argument does not assume A finitely presented over the original R.

Taking spectra translates the ring comparison to the stated action/kernel-pair map, and the faithfully flat finitely presented ring map gives an affine faithfully flat finitely presented scheme map. For R to R', every inclusion A_m into A is split by the degree projection, so it stays injective after tensoring, without flatness of R'. Tensor commutes with direct sums, hence the resulting grading has degree-zero piece B tensor_R R'. The unit expressions persist. The tensor comparison is the base change of beta by the formula on homogeneous pure tensors; this proves the full compatibility, not just a degreewise analogy.

The zero-ring case is harmless: A=0 implies B=0, both spectra are empty, the tensor comparison is 0 to 0, and the finite presentation is the identity of 0. The above principal-cover argument is applied only when B is nonzero; otherwise this direct check applies. For M=0, B=A and the comparison is the identity. For finite torsion M, the lattice relations give u^n=c, with c a unit; their derivative need not be invertible, and no smoothness assertion about the torsor map is made.
