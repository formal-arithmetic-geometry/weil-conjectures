### Old step 2 → lem-sfb-uniform-projective-fibre-vanishing

### Old step 3 → lem-sfb-uniform-projective-fibre-vanishing

For $d\geq m$, the regularity consequences give $H^j(\mathbf P^n_k,\mathcal I_k(d))=0$ for every $j>0$. The projective-twist calculation gives $H^j(\mathbf P^n_k,\mathcal O(d))=0$ for $j>0$ and $H^0(\mathcal O(d))=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]_d$. Apply the cohomology long exact sequence to the twisted ideal sequence and use closed-immersion coherent cohomology. It gives a surjection $k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]_d\to H^0(Z_k,\mathcal O_{Z_k}(d))$ and vanishing $H^j(Z_k,\mathcal O_{Z_k}(d))=0$ for $j>0$. The Euler polynomial equality therefore gives $\dim_k H^0(Z_k,\mathcal O_{Z_k}(d))=P(d)$. For $n=0$, the separate $\mathbf P^0$ clause in the twist calculation and the regularity theorem apply without overlapping degree-zero/top-degree formulas.

### Old step 4 → lem-sfb-projective-ideal-sections-from-fibres

Fix $d\geq m$. The sheaf $\mathcal O_Z(d)$ is invertible, hence finitely presented and flat over $T$ (locally it is $\mathcal O_Z$, and $q$ is flat). Apply the open-locus theorem to $q$, this sheaf, degree $0$ and rank $P(d)$. Step 3 says every geometric point factors through that open locus: over a field its derived image has only degree-zero cohomology, of dimension $P(d)$. Every point of $T$ is the image of a geometric point; hence this open is all of $T$. Thus $Rq_*\mathcal O_Z(d)$ is a finite locally free module $E_d$ of rank $P(d)$ in degree zero, and $E_d=q_*\mathcal O_Z(d)$. The perfect-direct-image base-change theorem supplies the canonical derived base-change isomorphism for every $T'\to T$. Since a locally free sheaf has no higher derived pullback, its degree-zero map is exactly $g^*E_d\to q'_*\mathcal O_{Z'}(d)$, which is therefore an isomorphism. This argument uses no reducedness hypothesis.

### Old step 5 → lem-sfb-projective-ideal-sections-from-fibres

The projective-twist calculation on each affine base open identifies $p_*\mathcal O(d)$ with $V_d$, freely generated by degree-$d$ monomials; these identifications commute with coefficient extension. Let $\rho_d:V_d\to E_d$ be restriction. By step 4 its geometric fibres are the surjections of step 3. The cokernel of $\rho_d$ is locally finitely generated, being a quotient of the finite locally free $E_d$. At each local ring of $T$ its residue quotient is zero (a field extension detects zero vector spaces), so Nakayama makes the cokernel zero. The kernel $K_d$ is finite locally free: locally trivialize $E_d$, lift its finitely many basis elements through $\rho_d$, and obtain a splitting of $V_d\to E_d$. Thus $K_d$ is a direct summand of a finite free module. By left exactness of sections, $K_d=p_*\mathcal I(d)$. This proves the required exact sequence and its ranks.

### Old step 7 → lem-sfb-relative-regular-ideal-generation

At $d=m$, pull the evaluation map $p^*K_m\otimes\mathcal O(-m)\to\mathcal I$ to a geometric fibre. Steps 1 and 6 identify it with the ordinary global-section evaluation for $\mathcal I_k(m)$, tensored with $\mathcal O(-m)$. Regularity makes that map surjective. Let $C$ be the cokernel of the relative evaluation; it is of finite type since $\mathcal I$ is of finite type. For $x\in\mathbf P^n_T$ over $t$, the geometric-fibre vanishing implies $C_x/\mathfrak m_t C_x=0$ after (and hence before) the faithfully flat residue-field extension. The ideal $\mathfrak m_t\mathcal O_{\mathbf P^n_T,x}$ lies in the maximal ideal of this local ring. Nakayama applied to the finite module $C_x$ gives $C_x=0$. Therefore the evaluation map is surjective everywhere.

### Old step 8 → lem-sfb-relative-regular-ideal-generation

On each geometric fibre, the regularity theorem gives surjective linear multiplication from degree $b$ to $b+1$ for every $b\geq m$. Induction on $e=d-m$ shows that each section in degree $d$ is a sum of products of $e$ linear forms and sections in degree $m$. Each product is a degree-$e$ polynomial, so the composite factors through $V_{d-m,k}\otimes K_{m,k}\to K_{d,k}$ and this map is surjective. For $d=m$ the map is scalar multiplication by $1\in V_0$ and is the identity. Steps 5--6 identify these maps with the geometric fibres of the required relative multiplication map. Its cokernel is finitely generated locally on $T$, since $K_d$ is finite locally free. Applying local Nakayama as in step 5 proves surjectivity on $T$.
