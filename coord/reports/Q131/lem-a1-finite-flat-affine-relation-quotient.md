### lem-a1-affine-relation-comparison-preparation

**Returned step 1.** An equivalence relation on all $S$-valued test schemes supplies identity, inverse and composition uniquely: send $u$ to $(u,u)$, reverse $(u,v)$, and send a composable pair $(u,v),(v,w)$ to $(u,w)$. These operations are natural; full faithfulness of Yoneda turns them into morphisms, and equality on all test points proves the groupoid laws. They also give a groupoid on arbitrary test schemes by considering each possible structure map to $S$. Write $\sigma=s^\sharp$, $\tau=t^\sharp$. Since they agree on $C$, the canonical map $q:U\to M=\operatorname{Spec}C$ equalizes $s,t$. The affine fibre-product formula gives $U\times_M U=\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_C A)$, and the comparison is induced by $\phi(a\otimes a_2)=\sigma(a)\tau(a_2)$. If $A=0$, the maps $A\to B$ force $B=C=0$; all schemes concerned are empty and the assertions follow. Henceforth $A\ne0$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra, lem-galois-yoneda-fully-faithful.

Source: literature/stacks-project/groupoids.tex; Tag 03BM, first paragraph, and the equivalence-relation groupoid operations.

**Returned step 2.** The comparison $k:R\to U\times_MU$ is a monomorphism: equality after its two projections implies equality after $j$, and the hypothesis on $H$-points gives equality of the original maps. It is finite: a finite list generating $B$ over $A$ via $\sigma$ also generates it over $A\otimes_C A$ via $\phi$, because this larger scalar action includes $\sigma(A)$. The finite-monomorphism lemma makes $k$ a closed immersion. On these affine schemes this means exactly that $\phi$ is surjective.

Uses: lem-a1-finite-monomorphism-closed-immersion, def-weil-finite-integral-morphism, def-weil-closed-immersion.

Source: literature/stacks-project/groupoids.tex; Tag 03BM, first paragraph; Morphisms Tag 03BB.

**Returned step 3.** Apply the invariant-ring integrality and orbit lemmas to the groupoid just constructed. Thus $A$ is integral over $C$, $q$ is surjective, and the fibres of $|U|\to|M|$ are the $R$-orbits. These statements apply also to every affine groupoid obtained below after changing the invariant base.

Uses: lem-a1-affine-groupoid-integral-invariants, lem-a1-affine-groupoid-orbit-fibres.

### lem-a1-affine-relation-local-basis-reduction

**Returned step 5.** For each prime $\mathfrak p\subset C$, apply the flat local extension lemma to $C_{\mathfrak p}$ and the infinite field extension $\kappa(\mathfrak p)(z)$. It gives a flat local map $C_{\mathfrak p}\to D$ with infinite residue field. This local map is faithfully flat. Localization is flat, so apply the preceding equalizer argument first to $C\to C_{\mathfrak p}$ and then to $C_{\mathfrak p}\to D$. We will prove $A_D$ finite free over $D$ and $\phi_D$ an isomorphism for each such choice.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-flat-local-extension-given-residue-field, lem-weil-pbc-flat-local-hom-faithfully-flat, lem-weil-pbc-localization-flat.

Source: literature/stacks-project/groupoids.tex; Tag 03BM, second and fourth paragraphs; Algebra Tag 03C3.

**Returned step 7.** Work now over the local invariant ring $(D,\mathfrak m)$ and abbreviate $A_D,B_D$ by $A,B$. By integrality, every maximal ideal of $A$ contracts to $\mathfrak m$, and every prime of $A$ above $\mathfrak m$ is maximal. There is such a point $u_0$ by surjectivity of the orbit lemma. Every other maximal point lies in its $R$-orbit and is therefore in $t(s^{-1}(u_0))$. The fibre $s^{-1}(u_0)$ is finite, so $A$ is semilocal. Also $\mathfrak m A$ lies in every maximal ideal of $A$, hence in its Jacobson radical.

Uses: lem-a1-affine-groupoid-integral-invariants, lem-a1-affine-groupoid-orbit-fibres, lem-weil-pbc-integral-maximal-contracts-maximal, lem-weil-pbc-integral-prime-over-maximal-maximal.

Source: literature/stacks-project/groupoids.tex; Tag 03BM, final paragraph, first half.

**Returned step 8.** The degree function of $s$ is locally constant. Its values at $s(r)$ and $t(r)$ are equal: composition with the arrow $r$ and its inverse identifies the source fibres after extension to $\kappa(r)$, and fibre degree is unchanged by that extension. All maximal points of $A$ are in one orbit by the preceding step, hence have the same degree $r$. Every prime is contained in a maximal ideal, and a locally constant function takes equal values at two points in a specialization chain; therefore this degree is $r$ everywhere. The identity section makes $s$ surjective, so $r\geq1$. The module $B$ via $\sigma$ is consequently finite locally free of constant rank $r$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-finite-locally-free-degree-locally-constant, lem-weil-pbc-fibre-degree-base-change.

### lem-a1-affine-relation-flat-local-descent

**Returned step 10.** Return to the original $C,A,B$. For each $\mathfrak p$, faithful flatness of $C_{\mathfrak p}\to D$ detects exactness, so $\phi_{\mathfrak p}$ is an isomorphism, since $\phi_D$ is. The flatness descent lemma likewise gives that $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is flat over $C_{\mathfrak p}$. A module vanishing at every prime is zero: a nonzero element has proper annihilator, contained in a maximal ideal where its localization is still nonzero. Apply this test to the kernel and cokernel of $\phi$ to conclude that $\phi$ is an isomorphism. For any injection $L\to L_2$, localization exactness makes $L_{\mathfrak p}\to L_2_{\mathfrak p}$ injective, and flatness of $A_{\mathfrak p}$ gives injectivity after tensoring. The same zero test on its kernel shows $L\otimes_C A\to L_2\otimes_C A$ injective. Thus $A$ is flat over $C$.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-ring-map, lem-weil-pbc-flatness-descends-faithfully-flat, lem-weil-pbc-localization-module-exact.

Source: literature/stacks-project/groupoids.tex; Tag 03BM, second paragraph, descent of the comparison and flatness.

**Returned step 11.** Since $\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}C$ is surjective and flat, $C\to A$ is faithfully flat. The finite principal-open free cover of $B$ over $A$ from step 6 shows that $B$ is a finitely presented $A$-module. Under $\phi$, this is $A\otimes_C A$ with the $A$-action on its first factor. Faithfully flat descent of finite presentation, applied to the $C$-module $A$, makes $A$ finitely presented over $C$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-flat-surjective-spectrum-faithfully-flat, lem-sc-affine-standard-cover-finite-presentation-flat, lem-trf-finite-presentation-module-descent-flat-local.

Source: literature/stacks-project/groupoids.tex; Tag 03BM, second paragraph, finite-presentation descent using Algebra Tag 03C4.

**Returned step 12.** By steps 10 and 11, $A$ is a flat and finitely presented $C$-module, so the finitely-presented flat projectivity lemma makes $A$ a finite projective $C$-module. A finite projective module has a finite principal-open free cover: there are $g_1,\ldots,g_m\in C$ generating the unit ideal with each $A_{g_i}$ finite free over $C_{g_i}$. By the affine fibre-product formula the preimage of $D(g_i)$ in $U$ is $\operatorname{Spec}A_{g_i}$, and the finite-free algebra spectrum lemma shows that $\operatorname{Spec}A_{g_i}\to\operatorname{Spec}C_{g_i}$ is finite, flat and locally of finite presentation. Flatness at a point is a condition on local rings, and finite presentation at a point asks for affine neighbourhoods of the point and of its image; every point of $U$ lies over some open $D(g_i)$, so $q$ is flat and locally of finite presentation. For finiteness, let $V=\operatorname{Spec}R\subseteq M$ be an affine open. The affine fibre-product formula gives $q^{-1}(V)=\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_CR)$, and the images of finitely many $C$-module generators of $A$ generate $A\otimes_CR$ as an $R$-module. Hence $q$ is finite locally free. Surjectivity was already proved in step 3, and the affine fibre-product formula together with $\phi$ gives $R\cong U\times_MU$ with exactly the original projections.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-finitely-presented-flat-module-projective, lem-weil-pbc-finite-projective-standard-free-cover, lem-weil-pbc-finite-free-algebra-spectrum, lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra, def-pc-finite-locally-free, def-weil-finite-integral-morphism, def-weil-flat-morphism, def-sbc-locally-finite-presentation.

Source: literature/stacks-project/groupoids.tex; Tag 03BM, second paragraph, last sentence ('Hence A is finite locally free over C, see Algebra, Lemma 00NX'), and comparison conclusion.


### lem-a1-fppf-kernel-pair-quotient-descent

**Returned step 13.** The finite locally free surjection $q$ is an fppf cover. It is also a singleton fpqc cover, since its inverse image of each affine open is affine, flat and surjective. The maps $U\to S$ agree on $R=U\times_MU$, so fpqc descent of scheme morphisms gives a unique map $M\to S$ making $q$ an $S$-morphism. Every fppf covering is an fpqc covering: its maps are open by flat-lfp openness, and the images of affine opens inside the inverse image of an affine open $V$ form an open cover of $V$; quasi-compactness selects finitely many of them, as required in the fpqc definition. Thus representable fpqc descent makes $h_M$ an fppf sheaf. On the fppf site over $S$, every $H\to M$ lifts after the fppf covering $H\times_MU\to H$, by base-change stability of fppf coverings. If two elements of $U(H)$ have the same image in $M(H)$, their pair is an $H$-point of $U\times_MU=R$, so their quotient classes are equal. The quotient presheaf therefore maps injectively and locally surjectively to $h_M$. The local-bijection sheafification criterion identifies its associated sheaf with $h_M$.

Uses: def-pc-fppf-site, def-weil-pbc-fpqc-covering, lem-weil-pbc-fpqc-representable-descent, lem-cbc-set-presheaf-sheafification-local-bijection, lem-weil-pbc-flat-lfp-open, lem-trf-fppf-covering-base-change.

Source: literature/stacks-project/groupoids.tex; Tag 03BM, first paragraph via Tag 03C5 (groupoids-lemma-criterion-quotient-representable); descent of the S-structure.
