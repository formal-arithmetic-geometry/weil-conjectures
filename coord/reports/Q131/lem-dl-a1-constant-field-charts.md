Step 1 uses finite-type-over-Noetherian only for Noetherianity, the zero-dimensional closed-subscheme result only after the explicit specialization-chain argument, the closed-point-field lemma for finiteness of kappa(s), and the separable-extension theorem only after proving separability with T^(#L)-T. The hypotheses are finite type over a field, dimension one and density. A dense open contains every generic point; the dimension argument excludes a positive-dimensional boundary. The boundary may be empty, in which case there is no s to choose and this construction is vacuous.

Step 2 uses the affine fibre-product theorem with the actual maps from k0 to A and L. The displayed evaluation homomorphism is surjective because it is the identity on L, so the lift really has residue field L. Smooth relative dimension is preserved by this field base change. Smooth-normal and finite-type-Noetherian provide all hypotheses of the normal-sections finite-product statement. I apply that statement to an affine scheme, so its sections recover the scheme itself. The chosen idempotent gives an affine open-and-closed domain component; the rational point selects a nonzero factor. This argument works when W0 is disconnected or has nontrivial constants and does not assume W0 was geometrically connected.

Step 3 uses precisely open immersions and compositions being etale and etale base-change stability, rather than inferring them from a definition node. The smooth relative-dimension permanence result applies also to the open restriction. The connected-rational-point theorem is applied over L with the actual L-rational lift from step 2; its statement supplies connectedness after all field extensions. Etale openness proves density by taking the image of each nonempty open, and the zero-dimensional complement theorem then proves boundary finiteness. No surjectivity of the chosen chart onto W0 is assumed.

Step 4 uses the same smooth/normal/Noetherian statements after algebraic closure. The finite-components theorem and the normal-section product description show that the geometric irreducible components are disjoint; finitely many then are open and closed. Connectedness leaves one. The finite boundary remains finite after tensoring its coordinate algebra, so it cannot exhaust a dimension-one curve. Consequently its nonempty open complement is irreducible and connected. This verifies the algebraic-closure criterion actually stated in def-weil-geometric-hypotheses. Density alone would not have justified this without the normal geometric curve argument.

Step 5 uses the Weil-pair and local-Frobenius definitions for the ordered m-fold iterate and the lisse-sheaf definition for local trivializations of finite systems. The pullback-composition lemma supplies the canonical integral comparison, rather than silently treating iterated pullbacks as definitionally equal. Pullback preserves the finite local trivializations and their quotient transition maps.

Step 6 uses the weakly-etale localization statement on geometric open immersions, which are objects of the small pro-etale sites. Its constant-ring identification is exactly the one needed here. The completed rings agree because limits of sheaves are evaluated objectwise. Thus the ring map is an identification and the module pullback is restriction. The arbitrary-ring-sheaf exact restriction theorem, not just the constant-ring version, applies and provides exactness on the completed module category.

Step 7 uses the general ringed derived slice localization statement, whose hypotheses allow these pro-etale sites and arbitrary coefficient ring sheaves. It is localized at the object V. Step 8 uses completed-coefficient pushforward comparison for the open j and the derived-complete integral representative K.
