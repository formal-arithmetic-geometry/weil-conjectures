## Original seven arguments retained verbatim

This appendix preserves the exact old proof text for comparison and for child workers. It is not a proof attached to an unsplit node.

### Original step 1

Fix $r\geq1$ and apply [lem-weil-point-automorphism-restriction-natural] to the scheme automorphism $g=h^r$ and $\Lambda=A_n$. It gives $\sigma_{r,M_n}$ on every unbounded complex, its $A_n$-linearity with the action transported through $c_n^{-1}$, and naturality for chain maps. It also identifies the insertion with the identity under $(F_rM_n)(s\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}s)\cong M_n(s\xrightarrow{h^r}s)$, so this is the actual restriction along $h^r$, not an arbitrarily chosen identification of fibers. The reindexing and coefficient isomorphisms used here are those of [lem-weil-proetale-isomorphism-inverse-image], applied to $h^r$.

### Original step 2

For the comparison $j_{r,M_n}$ of [def-weil-inverse-image-pullback-comparison](a), define the inverse on local tensor generators by $x\otimes a\mapsto c_n^{-1}(a)x$. It is balanced: $(b x)\otimes a$ and $x\otimes c_n(b)a$ both map to $b c_n^{-1}(a)x$. It commutes with restrictions and hence sheafifies, using the sheafified tensor construction [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product]. Its composite with $j$ sends $x$ to $x$; the other composite sends $x\otimes a$ to $c_n^{-1}(a)x\otimes1=x\otimes a$. Thus $j$ is an isomorphism. A module map $f$ commutes with $j$ because $(F_rf(x))\otimes1=(F_rf\otimes1)(x\otimes1)$; the same equality for $f=d^i$ proves compatibility with differentials. This checks the comparison on arbitrary representatives, without any flatness condition on $M_n$.

### Original step 3

Check the actual quotient-linear transition $v_n:M_{n+1}\to\operatorname{Res}_{\rho_n}M_n$. In the reindexing model, its underlying abelian-sheaf map commutes with restriction along $h^r$, giving $v_n|_{(s,h^r)}\operatorname{res}_{h^r}=\operatorname{res}_{h^r}v_n|_{(s,\mathrm{id})}$. Since insertion is the identity in that model, this is $\Gamma(F_rv_n)\sigma_{r,M_{n+1}}=\sigma_{r,M_n}\Gamma(v_n)$ on the underlying sections. The actions agree because [lem-weil-proetale-isomorphism-inverse-image](3) gives $\rho_n c_{n+1}=c_nF_r(\rho_n)$. In the tensor model the transition sends $x\otimes a$ to $F_r(v_n)(x)\otimes\rho_n(a)$, so its composite with $j_{n+1}$ sends $x$ to $F_r(v_n)(x)\otimes\rho_n(1)=F_r(v_n)(x)\otimes1$, precisely $j_nF_r(v_n)(x)$. Balancedness follows from the displayed coefficient square. For completed scalars, [def-weil-inverse-image-pullback-comparison] gives $\pi_nc_{\widehat R}=c_nF_r(\pi_n)$; hence the same square is linear after restriction through $\pi_n$. These are equalities of strict transitions, without replacing them by derived tensor equivalences.

### Original step 4

Set $(u_{r,M})_n=\sigma_{r,M_n}$ in the inverse-image model, or $\Gamma(j_{r,M_n})\sigma_{r,M_n}$ in its identified tensor model. Step 3 makes this a map of whole $R$-module towers, and Step 1 makes it a chain map and $R$-linear through $R\to A_n$. Here $\Gamma_\bullet$ retains the original transitions exactly as in [def-trf-coefficient-tower-operations]. Strict tower maps are families commuting with the transitions, so the prescribed components determine $u_{r,M}$ uniquely. For any chain map of systems $f$, Step 1 applied to each $f_n$ gives $\Gamma_\bullet(G_rf)u_{r,M}=u_{r,M'}\Gamma_\bullet(f)$; equality here is equality of the families defining strict tower maps, before localization.

### Original step 5

The source system category is abelian with componentwise exactness by [lem-coef-semilinear-derived-sections]. The functor $G_r$ is exact: each $F_r$ is an exact inverse-image equivalence by [lem-weil-proetale-isomorphism-inverse-image](2), and the coefficient identifications do not change the underlying kernels or cokernels. Restriction of scalars preserves those kernels and cokernels, and sections are exact by [lem-weil-point-proetale-sections-exact]. Therefore both $\Gamma_\bullet$ and $\Gamma_\bullet G_r$ are exact functors from systems to $R$-module towers (whose kernels and cokernels are also levelwise). Apply [lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-derived-factorization] to these two functors: their termwise complex functors preserve quasi-isomorphisms and give the stated functors between the ordinary unbounded derived categories.

### Original step 6

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-localization-universal-property](2) with source the category of complexes of strict systems, $W$ its quasi-isomorphisms, and target $D(\operatorname{Tow}(\operatorname{Mod}R))$. Step 5 ensures that the two functors obtained by target localization invert $W$, and Step 4 supplies their natural transformation. This produces $I_{r,C}$ through the exact-functor factorizations, uniquely with the stated representative maps. Its naturality holds for every morphism of the derived SYSTEM category. Explicitly, if $w:X\to Y$ is a quasi-isomorphism, $B(w)u_X=u_YA(w)$ implies $B(w)^{-1}u_Y=u_XA(w)^{-1}$, where $A=\Gamma_\bullet$, $B=\Gamma_\bullet G_r$ and all maps have been localized. Thus inverses of quasi-isomorphisms, as well as the original chain maps and their composites, satisfy naturality. This uses localization of the entire system category, not a claim that derived evaluations detect equality.

### Original step 7

Evaluate the representative map of Step 4 at level $n$ and apply $\Gamma(j_{r,M_n})$. It is exactly restriction along $h^r$, colimit insertion, sheafification, and tensor insertion $x\mapsto x\otimes1$. This is the finite-coefficient recipe of [def-weil-frobenius-point-transport], with $h^r$ in place of $g_s$, and comparison (a) of [def-weil-inverse-image-pullback-comparison]. The comparison identifies ordinary termwise module pullback with the derived pullback at this finite level. Consequently when $C_n$ lies in $D^b_c(s,A_n)$ the evaluated derived map is exactly $\iota^{(r)}_{C_n}$. Steps 1-6 worked on all unbounded complexes with the given quotient-linear transitions: no step used cartesianness, constructibility of other levels, boundedness, finite Tor-amplitude, or derived completeness.
