## Statement dispute and source error

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: injectivity of Cl(B) -> Cl(B_p), which is what the imported hypothesis says, does not control Pic of every punctured B_q away from the generizations of p. A divisor on a neighbourhood of q can be locally principal away from q without extending to a divisor on Spec B which is principal at p. The English proof of EGA 21.13.14 calls the relevant conditions local before making this reduction. Interpreted solely as a condition on global cycles (as the imported statement does), that locality assertion is not justified. I request checking the original French and the intended local quantifiers; I do not silently assume the missing implication.

Here is an explicit counterexample over k=C. Put

    B = k[x,y,z,t]/(xy-z(z-t^2+1)),
    p = (x,y,z,t-1),    q = (x,y,z,t+1).

B is a Noetherian normal domain of dimension three. The polynomial is linear in y and primitive over k[x,z,t], hence irreducible; the hypersurface is Cohen–Macaulay and its singular locus, computed from the four partial derivatives, consists exactly of p and q. Thus it is regular in codimension one and normal. The primes p and q are distinct maximal ideals, so q is not contained in p, and dim B_q=3.

The global class group is Z, generated by P=(x,z). Indeed B[1/x]=k[x,x^{-1},z,t] is factorial. The two prime divisors above x are P and Q=(x,z-t^2+1), both with coefficient one in div(x), since the other factor and y are units at the relevant generic point. Localizing divisors at x makes Cl(B) the group generated by P,Q modulo principal divisors supported on those two primes. Every rational function with that support is a unit of B[1/x], hence is c*x^n, so the only relation is P+Q=0. This also describes the principal-divisor kernel directly, without confusing the Weil class group with the group of invertible fractional ideals.

At either singular point the completed local ring is the ordinary double-point ring k[[x,y,z,w]]/(xy-zw). At p write t=1+u and w=z-2u-u^2; at q write t=-1+u and w=z+2u-u^2. In each case (z,u)->(z,w) is an invertible formal coordinate change, because the u coefficient is a nonzero scalar. The completed node has class group Z generated by (x,z). One standard explicit computation blows up its vertex: the exceptional quadric is P^1 x P^1, a ruling represents (x,z), and the exceptional divisor restricts as O(-1,-1). Thus the ruling class has infinite order modulo the diagonal class. Equivalently, the punctured node's Picard group is generated by O(1,0) modulo O(1,1). Only the infinite order of the ruling is needed here.

Consequently the composite Cl(B)=Z -> Cl(B_p) -> Cl(completion(B_p))=Z sends generator to generator, and has zero kernel. This verifies exactly the old hypothesis: if D agrees with div(a) at all height-one primes below p, its class maps to zero in Cl(B_p); hence its global class is zero and D=div(b) for some b. At q, however, the rank-one reflexive module of P has nonzero class, as witnessed by the same completion calculation. Since q is an isolated singularity, its restriction to Spec B_q minus the closed point is invertible. That line is nontrivial: if it were trivial, the local height-one divisor would be principal, contradicting its nonzero class after completion. Thus the old Picard conclusion fails. The global-sections conclusion remains true by normal S2 and the dimension bound.

The counterexample is used to audit the statement, not as a new DAG premise. Its algebra is given here so that the reviewer can check the disputed implication independently. I did not introduce a counterexample node, an unsupported false locality lemma, or a node restating the whole item.

## Corrected hypothesis and proof route

The added hypothesis quantifies, for every q, over divisors of B_q, and requires global principality there whenever that divisor is principal at every common generization t of p and q. This is the explicit local version of the cycle-extension condition which EGA's locality reduction needs. It is a genuine additional hypothesis, plainly recorded in the statement. It does not assume the line-bundle conclusion. It also leaves the original q=B maximal/local case useful when the stronger input is known.

For an arbitrary line L on the punctured B_q spectrum, associate its rational-section Weil divisor d. All height-one points survive puncturing because dim B_q>=2. Every common generization t of p and q is a strict generization of q, hence lies in the puncture; the local equation of L there makes d principal at t. The corrected hypothesis gives one rational b representing d at every height-one prime. Injectivity of Pic into Cl for a normal scheme then trivializes L. This is the divisor/line-bundle route of EGA 21.13.14, with the local quantifier supplied explicitly. No locality assertion about the old hypothesis remains in the proof.

The rational-section dictionary is one genuine smaller theorem, not a clause of the item: it applies on every nonempty open of any normal Noetherian affine domain, with no p, no puncture and no divisor extension hypothesis. Stacks 02SG gives local finiteness, hence finiteness on a Noetherian open; 0BE6 constructs the associated Weil class and its local equations; 0BE8 identifies its zero class with a trivial line. A nonzero rational section exists by choosing a basis at the generic stalk and a local representative. All this is statement-only in the new node; its future worker can split those inputs further.
