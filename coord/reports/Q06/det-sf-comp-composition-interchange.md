## Proof of lem-sfb-derived-open-square-restriction

The scheme hypotheses make both ambient schemes qcqs, and the pullback of the quasi-compact open i is a quasi-compact open j. [sk-prop-sfb-cartesian-coefficients] identifies each untwisted module inverse image with exact sheaf inverse image, transporting its action along the inverse coefficient-ring comparison. [det-sf-open-closed-sequence-ring-sheaf](1),(4) supplies the exact open functors, their adjunctions, invertible open units, and r* i! ≅ j! p* on sheaves. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-derived-factorization] to these exact functors. The same termwise base-change maps descend, with their inverses, to the unbounded derived categories and commute with the termwise shift identifications.

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-termwise-adjunction-homotopy](a) to the two open adjunctions, then [lem-sfb-adjunction-localization] to their quasi-isomorphism localizations. Its unit/counit formulas identify the resulting units with the localized degreewise units. They remain isomorphisms. Every degreewise unit and counit commutes with shifts: in degree q the shifted component is the same component at q+n, and additivity respects the differential sign. Thus the localized Hom bijections have this compatibility too.

[sk-def-sfb-module-derived-pushpull] supplies p* ⊣ Rp* and r* ⊣ Rr*, since the exact pullbacks need no resolutions. Compose these and the open adjunctions with the derived open base-change isomorphism, obtaining precisely the displayed Hom chain in the statement. Apply that natural chain and its inverse to identity arrows. Naturality makes the two resulting maps inverse, giving rho, naturally in B. All entries of the chain use the specified derived adjunctions and termwise comparison; their shift-compatible units and counits give rho's shift compatibility. No proper-base-change theorem or boundedness assumption enters.

## Proof of lem-sfb-proper-open-boundary-zero

By [det-proper-image-localizing-open], K=j!A lies in Dctf(W,Lambda). In particular it is bounded constructible by [sk-def-sfb-constructible]. Form W_Z=W×_(Ybar)Z, with c':W_Z→W and r_Z:W_Z→Z. All these schemes and maps remain in the standing setting: closed subschemes and fibre products of separated finite-type k-schemes have that property.

Proper base change identifies c*Rr*K with R(r_Z)* c'^*K. For finite coefficients apply [det-thm-sfb-proper-base-change-finite](2) to K in Dctf. For integral coefficients apply [det-thm-sfb-proper-base-change-integral](3), which needs only bounded constructibility. For E and the chapter's finite-subextension Qbar coefficient use [det-thm-sfb-proper-base-change-rational](3), again on bounded constructibles. Z_l and Q_l are the cases E=Q_l. Thus no unbounded or localizing proper-base-change statement is being assumed.

The open U=W×_(Ybar)Y has empty inverse image in W_Z because Y and Z are complementary. Apply [det-sf-open-closed-sequence-ring-sheaf](4) to j and c': c'^*j! is extension from that empty open, hence zero. A module sheaf on the empty scheme has a single section over its only object, by the empty covering axiom, so it is zero; this also holds termwise and in the derived category. Consequently R(r_Z)* c'^*K is zero, proving the assertion.
