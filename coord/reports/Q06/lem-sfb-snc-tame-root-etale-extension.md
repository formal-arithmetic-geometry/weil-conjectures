I checked the item step by step against the statements actually cited, rather than their proof internals:

1. The monic quotient node gives the basis and finiteness used to define the finite-root algebras. Power substitutions respect x_i^a=t_i, and composition is literal exponent multiplication. The empty scheme, r=0 and empty cover are discharged before any nonempty/generic-point argument.

The SNC definition gives the regular intersection conditions on the restricted boundary, and Noetherian finite-components gives the finite shrinking operation. After shrinking, the active zero loci really are distinct reduced integral components, so the narrower existing finite-root regularity theorem applies. I corrected an initial appeal to cotangent-space independence to cite the exact dimension bound instead. The original reducible-label hypothesis was not silently discarded.
3. The polynomial regularity statement and the hypersurface derivation criterion apply with derivative aT^(a-1). Both a and T are units for a root of an inactive unit. The finite-algebra Noetherian theorem and regular-implies-normal theorem have their required hypotheses. The normal-component theorem gives clopen integral components. Injectivity of multiplication by f follows at every B-stalk from the parameter-domain statement and then in C_a coordinatewise in the monic basis; it supplies the density used later.
4. The etale base-change statement applies to the actual pulled-back cover. Module generators remain generators under tensoring, so finiteness needs no extra descent theorem. On each clopen component X of X_a, X is normal integral Noetherian and the puncture is an open; the finite-etale-open integral-closure theorem says exactly that the relevant integral closure is a finite module.
