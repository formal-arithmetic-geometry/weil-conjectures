## Review by Q06

I read Milne *Algebraic Groups* (2017), printed pp.509-511 (PDF pp.521-523), including (152), (153), Theorem 23.72, its omitted-details proof, and the subsequent discussion of groups over Z. I also read the cited field/Borel/flag nodes against their statements and Milne 21.68, 21.84 and 21.86. The proof uses the existing field construction as an input, while the integral argument is written out. For the new relative Gauss lemma I read the two-block Schur factorization and determinant identities at Mathlib `LinearAlgebra/Matrix/SchurComplement.lean:50` and `:370`, and the existing field-only chart and its supporting Mathlib leaf. This new general lemma has no root-theoretic assumption. For the new DVR criterion I read the Prasad-Yu theorem online: affineness and flatness, generic smooth connectedness, reductivity of the reduced geometric special identity component and equality with generic dimension are exactly the relevant hypotheses. The conclusion is connectedness; no characteristic restriction is needed for part (c). I did not accidentally import the additional hypotheses used for their stronger smoothness assertion. Their Section 5 reduction by local extensions of DVRs gives the geometric version stated here.

Step 1 uses the statements of `lem-weil-pbc-pid-torsion-free-module-flat` and `lem-trf-flat-tensor-preserves-injection`. I checked both: the former applies to Z and to both torsion-free modules B and Q[H_Q], and the latter gives each of the two tensor injections. Vanishing of the comultiplication of I is then an actual coordinate-ring test, not just a test on rational points. Inverse and counit descend in torsion-free rings. The maps from the torus and affine line factor through the closure by their torsion-free source rings, giving n_w as an integral point before it is used for translation.

Step 2 uses the subgroup-freeness theorem for the weight lattice summands and the weight-lattice theorem for P. I checked that the latter explicitly states P is free of the required rank, including rank zero, and recorded the specialization M=Z Delta so its initial free-lattice hypothesis is supplied. The separating functional is obtained inside a nonempty rational open cone; its finitely many forbidden weight hyperplanes do not fill that cone. The divided-power and weight-shift calculations are elementary coefficient equalities over Q which hold over Z by torsion-freeness; they do not divide by a scalar in characteristic p.

Step 3 uses exactly the statement of `lem-sfb-chevalley-unipotent-polynomial-coordinates`, not its proof: the ordered product is a closed immersion over every algebraically closed field in every characteristic. I checked the order and representation hypotheses against the item. Positive cocharacter degrees make each target homogeneous piece finite free. The finite quotient C_d is zero over Q and modulo every prime; a nonzero finite abelian group has a quotient of prime order. Thus the integral coordinate map is surjective. This argument applies separately to p_+ and p_- by replacing q with -q; it does not infer integral surjectivity merely from fieldwise surjectivity of an ungraded map.

Step 4 uses the new relative Gauss lemma over A=Z. The field-only predecessor was deliberately not cited for this application. Closedness of the torus follows from both e^lambda and e^-lambda and the exact hypothesis Z Omega=P. Surjections tensor to surjections, giving a closed subobject of the Gauss source; hence the product map is an immersion into the specified principal open. The source ring is explicitly polynomial/Laurent polynomial and torsion-free.

Step 5 uses `lem-sfb-chevalley-root-exponential-group` for the field generated group's semisimplicity and torus, `lem-sfb-chevalley-exponential-big-cell` for its open identity chart, `lem-sfb-reductive-borel-pair-structure` for semisimple implies reductive, and `lem-sfb-bruhat-longest-cell-charts` for geometric integrality and hence density of that chart. I checked all of those conclusions in the statements. Kernels commute with the field extension, so the generic chart's image ideal is a subgroup ideal after extending to Qbar. Matrix identities descend faithfully to Q. Each generator is an axis of that product chart, and conversely the chart is a product of generators; this proves equality with the *defined* smallest H_Q as schemes. I corrected an initially implicit smoothness-descent assertion by citing `lem-trf-smooth-fpqc-base-descent`; Spec Qbar -> Spec Q is flat, quasi-compact and surjective, so that statement applies.

Step 6 uses `lemma-nagata-immersion-open-in-scheme-image`, whose statement gives a quasi-compact open immersion into the schematic image. The kernel computed in step 5 contracts to the integral kernel because the chart ring is torsion-free. Thus its image is the item's C. Translation by an actual integral point has the explicit inverse translation, so every prescribed theta_w is open, without first knowing it covers.

Step 7 uses the two existing field root-group/chart nodes, and I added `lem-sc-lft-over-field-jacobson-space` and `lem-weil-pbc-closed-point-residue-field-base` rather than leaving the closed-point argument tacit. Their statements give density of closed points in any closed subset and residue field F for those points. Translates of the identity neighbourhood therefore cover H_F and make its closed inclusion in C_F open as well. Smoothness and connectedness of H_F identify the reduced identity component. I also added `lem-ag-group-identity-local-dimension`, whose statement applies without a smoothness or reducedness hypothesis and gives global dimension from the identity local ring. The polynomial/Laurent chart computes that local dimension for both H_F and the generic fibre, so the equal-dimension hypothesis is proved, not assumed. This general identity-dimension theorem is declared as a tool use; it does not replace any unit route.

Step 8 uses the new DVR criterion with A=Z_(p), the fraction field Q, and each prime p. Flatness and finite type come from step 1 via the written tensor identification over Z_(p), generic smoothness and geometric connectedness from step 5, and reductivity plus the exact dimension equality from step 7. The semisimple-implies-reductive node is cited here too. Connectedness makes the nonempty open-and-closed identity subgroup equal to the entire special fibre *as a scheme*, since its open immersion now has full image. For larger algebraically closed fields I checked geometric integrality in the Bruhat statement before scalar extension. This addresses nonreduced and bad-prime fibres rather than hiding them in a base-change assertion about closure.

Step 9 uses the regular-cocharacter Borel statement, uniqueness of normalized one-dimensional root subgroups, the field chart's nonzero tangent vectors, ordered Borel root products, and the Borel semidirect product. The signs are compatible: roots positive for q give B=P(q); negative coordinates give the opposite unipotent. Both root parametrizations and the positive product are scheme maps, so U_-B is a right B-invariant open, including over nonreduced test algebras.

Step 10 uses the rank-one node's explicit n_delta conjugation formula, the Weyl reflection realization theorem, and the root-base induction lemma to check that the n_w chosen by the item cover the actual normalizer quotient. For the induction, signed simple-root orbits are stable under negation and simple reflections and contain all simple roots; conjugation then generates every root reflection. I checked that `lem-sfb-flag-torus-fixed-geometry` gives the fixed points for *any* choices of Weyl representatives, so no preferred-word assumption was smuggled in.

Step 11 uses the Bruhat node's actual Zariski trivializations of the right B-torsor and the flag node's projective embedding, fixed points and separating cocharacter. On each trivialization, the inverse image of U_-B under the section is open, and right B-invariance makes these opens agree. No unquoted theorem about openness of an arbitrary quotient is needed. The explicit projective weight formula extends the orbit to zero; its limit lies in the closed invariant complement and in Q^T, where all points have already been covered. I again cite the two closed-point results when passing from nonemptiness of that closed complement to an F-point. This is an elementary projective-coordinate limit using the cited flag geometry, not a hidden Bruhat-cell containment taken from another node's proof.

Step 12 is the elementary passage from geometric fibre coverage to pointwise coverage of the underlying scheme. I checked the empty-root case directly: spanning forces V=P=0 even if the trivial representation has arbitrary dimension, every generator is the identity, and the kernel closure is Spec Z. The unique chart is the identity, including W=0 and GL(0)=1.

For the relative Gauss statement I tested s=0, s=1, two scalar blocks, nonreduced algebras, and the zero ring.
