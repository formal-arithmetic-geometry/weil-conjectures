Step 1 uses the new cover node for the actual finite surjective scheme cover, lem-sfb-space-fp-qcqs-source for its qcqs source, and the i=0 clause of lem-sfb-space-higher-direct-image-quasi-coherent for quasi-coherence of both pushed-forward modules. The latter applies because finite presentation includes quasi-compactness and quasi-separatedness here. It does not assert exactness and I do not attribute exactness to it. Instead I check on an affine etale chart V: W=Y×_Z V is affine by the definition of finite, and the cokernel computation in lem-sfb-space-qcoh-affine-chart-calculus gives surjectivity on sections over W. The chart-limit definition def-trf-space-etale-module-functors computes the direct image on V by these sections: charts of W are the same compatible chart diagram, and the sheaf gluing axiom identifies the compatible families. Global sections are compatible chart families too, so they identify with the original sections on Y. This supplies power lifting on Y for arbitrary algebras there, with no unsupported lifting across an etale base change and no field hypothesis.

Step 2 uses the exact statement of lem-trf-scheme-zariski-etale-quasi-coherent-equivalence: its inverse is restriction and is the identity on open-subscheme sections, while its forward tensor pullback transports multiplication and unit. Surjectivity is checked by the affine-chart cokernel statement already cited. I use lem-sc-closed-subset-qc-ideal twice, for the complement of the affine neighbourhood and for the closed singleton. Their sum is the unit ideal stalkwise; hence I→O/J is surjective. Quasi-coherence and the quotient are given by the chart-calculus node. The full quasi-coherent symmetric-algebra node gives the algebras and maps; the degreewise-surjectivity node supplies their surjection. Thus the hypothesis actually applies to the degree-one global section t induced by 1 modulo J. A closed point is needed here and is explicitly assumed; there is no assertion that an arbitrary point is closed.

Step 3 uses the ring-level grading node only after restricting to affine opens, where the cited symmetric-algebra sections theorem identifies the sheaf with the ordinary symmetric algebra. Degree projections commute with restrictions because these preserve the degree-one generators; this writes out the gluing of s_N rather than assuming a sheaf grading that the ring-level node does not state. The universal property gives evaluation and its compatibility with reduction modulo J. Positive N is crucial for f∈I^N⊆I. The two consequences are f(q)=1 and vanishing on the complement, so q∈Y_f⊆U. The final affineness is exactly clause (b) of lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-section-vanishing-prime, applied to the affine U and f restricted to U.

Step 4 uses lem-sc-quasi-compact-kolmogorov-closed-point for a point closed in Y in a nonempty closed complement; its statement has precisely this closed-subset version, so no Jacobson or Noetherian premise is smuggled in. Quasi-compactness was established in step 1. I give the elementary affine-chart argument that a scheme is Kolmogorov. A finite subcover is the definition of quasi-compactness, not a new node. The empty scheme is handled separately; no closed point is claimed to exist on it.

Step 5 uses the symmetric-algebra node and finite-free polynomial identification to construct the quasi-coherent polynomial algebra sheaves. On Y_fi the element fi is a unit by lem-weil-pbc-basic-open-function, so T_i↦fi x is sheaf-surjective there. The cited direct-sum cohomology statement at degree 0, on the qcqs scheme Y, identifies global sections with polynomials; it is not used for an algebraic space or for a non-qcqs scheme. The coefficient calculation is written explicitly: degree L>0 monomials in the fi lie in their ideal, and their indicated linear combination is 1. This is an elementary polynomial calculation, not another adequate-affineness theorem hidden as a step.

Step 6 uses exactly the unit-ideal affine-principal-open criterion for schemes and the existing arbitrary-base finite-surjective-affine-space-target node. The latter is the full non-Noetherian space form of 07VS and states the conclusion we take. The finite surjectivity hypotheses are those from step 1. It is not the restricted Noetherian affineness node or the scheme-only Chevalley lemma.

I checked B–E against these actual files and statements: one genuinely missing general lemma is new; elementary stalk sums, gluing of degree projections, finite subcover and coefficient comparisons stay in the proof.
