Step 1 uses the actual statement of def-sfb-algebraic-moduli-stack to choose U with U to S smooth. Hence R to U is smooth, and R to W is smooth surjective even though W to X is not surjective. The statement of lem-sfb-space-smooth-source-point-locality applies over an arbitrary scheme B and imposes no surjectivity condition on its smooth q: first use q:R to U, h:U to S, and then q:R to W, h:W to S. Step 3 uses the statement, not the proof, of lem-sfb-algebraic-stack-fibre-products: it gives algebraicity and the 2-fibre-product universal property for arbitrary algebraic stacks over S. Schemes S and S' have their discrete representable stacks; their identity atlases and representable diagonals are the defining scheme instances. Applied to the product, this retains object descent, and the isomorphism sheaf between two objects over T/S' is unchanged because the specified maps to S' are equal and S' has discrete fibres. This verifies the diagonal over the new base without a hidden global change-of-base assertion. Def-pc-fibre-product gives the actual scheme U times_S S'. Def-sfb-stable-pointed-moduli-groupoids defines the objects and arrows of the 2-fibre product; eliminating the object identified by its isomorphism with the image of U gives its displayed equivalence with U times_X X'. No representability by a scheme of U times_X W is assumed. The representable-smooth calculus then supplies smoothness, representability and surjectivity of the changed atlas. Over T/S' its tests are literally the same tests, so no extra hypothesis on S' to S is needed.
