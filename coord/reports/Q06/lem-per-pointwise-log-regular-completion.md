## Review by Q06

I checked the proof against the pointwise hypotheses rather than borrowing the global hypotheses of a neighbouring node. For each citation I opened the node and read its **statement**, and `citations.json` copies the actual sentence/formula used; I did not cite a definition as an existence or comparison theorem.

**Step 1.** `pqf-def-locnoeth` supplies a Noetherian affine neighbourhood. The statement of `lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization`, clause (c), gives the ordinary localization, and `lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian` explicitly states Noetherianity of that localization. `lem-sbc-geometric-strict-henselization-local-presentation` supplies the algebraic strict henselization of an affine localized local ring, so `lem-sc-strict-henselization-noetherian` has the Noetherian local base it requires. `lem-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-local-structure` actually asserts the local-ring structure. Restriction to the chosen affine is justified by refining every pointed neighbourhood to its inverse image, retaining the marked point. The geometric residue map certifies nonzeroness. These statements do not require excellence.

**Step 2.** The fs definition gives an etale-local finite saturated chart Q. `lem-trf-chart-face-localization-logification` has exactly such a chart as its hypothesis and explicitly gives the face F, localized chart pushout and the **canonical** characteristic identification. I keep that identification throughout; I do not assume a sharp chart merely from knowing what “chart” means.

**Step 3.** `lem-trf-fs-face-sharpening` states not only sharpness but finite freeness of the quotient group completion. This checks the hypothesis of `lem-trf-integral-monoid-free-sharpening-splitting`, which explicitly does not require a torsion-free unit group. Its monoid section and product decomposition are exactly the conditional hypotheses of the face-localized logification statement. That statement then supplies the stalk-chart isomorphism and the precise characteristic map. No log regularity is used in these chart steps.

**Step 4.** `lem-trf-fine-log-stalk-chart-spreading` says affine pointed etale neighbourhood and **specified germ**; step 3 checks its finite integral monoid and stalk-isomorphism hypotheses. Its statement is sufficient for the prescribed chart, with no unsupported strengthening of its proof. The scheme-map clause of `lem-per-integral-chart-logification-thickening` holds for **every** scheme map and every log structure on its source; it is not limited to the later thickening clause. Applying it to Spec A -> W and then to the identity gives the two identical sets of maps on P. This is the universal property of the pulled-back log structure and proves the pullback-chart clause, with compatibility on P stated explicitly. The construction applies because P is finitely generated integral; it needs neither global regularity nor any hypothesis on other points.

**Step 5.** The step writes the ideal equality out using the stalk product P + units, so it does not silently change the source's ideal of all log nonunits into an unrelated ideal. `def-weil-regular-scheme` defines a regular local ring by precisely dim(R0) generators of its maximal ideal; `def-weil-pbc-regular-system-of-parameters` names that list, including an empty one. Thus the parameter lifts exist by the quotient map. I read all clauses of `lem-trf-completed-chart-coefficient-surjection`: it applies to arbitrary nonzero Noetherian local A with a sharp fs monoid map, nonzero monomials nonunits, and A/I regular. It gives the coefficient ring, completion local/Noetherian properties, dimension equality, monomial formula, **surjectivity**, and phi(J) equal to the target maximal ideal. None of these are taken merely from the definition of completion or a coefficient ring. The dimension hypothesis in this item gives dim S = n + rank(Pgp). The residue-field isomorphism gives S nonzero. No additional hypothesis that A is already a domain is needed for this application.

**Step 6.** `lem-weil-pbc-power-series-field-cohen-regular-complete` gives every base-ring hypothesis required by `lem-trf-formal-monoid-ring-dimension`. The latter is stated for a complete regular Noetherian local base D and the completion of D[P], rather than the polynomial presentation used in this item. `lem-trf-polynomial-power-series-monoid-completion` supplies precisely the missing comparison, compatible at every finite quotient and fixing the monomials. Hence the dimension and domain assertions apply to B_k(P,n). I did not identify these differently presented completions without citing that comparison.

**Step 7.** This is an elementary prime-chain argument, not an unproved assertion that a same-dimensional surjection must be an isomorphism. It explicitly uses that the **source is a domain**, the dimension is a finite integer, and a nonzero kernel lies in every pulled-back prime. The definition of dimension supplies a maximal-length chain: a finite integer supremum of integer lengths is attained. Surjectivity makes its inverse-image chain strict; the source's prime (0) is an extra strict term. The argument proves the kernel zero. It also works when the dimension is zero. No non-elementary dimension-comparison theorem is hidden in this step.

**Step 8.** `lem-trf-cohen-monoid-surjection-principal-kernel` is a statement about a general coefficient-monoid surjection; it does not assume a log scheme, global log regularity, or that the target is a domain. Its target hypotheses are nonzero Noetherian local, dimension n + r, and phi(J) = m_S, all already supplied in step 5. It explicitly gives a preimage j of phi(p), the congruence theta-p in J, the kernel (theta), and the monomial-compatible quotient isomorphism. Thus every mixed-characteristic clause used here is in its statement, and the use does not depend on the proof of `lem-trf-log-regular-completed-chart` or on generization.

**Step 9.** The direction of the final isomorphism is the inverse of phi (or its quotient), so the monomial formula is checked on the specified image, not asserted as an unspecified transport. This assembles the two cases and retains the chart obtained before the completion calculation. No new theorem is used.

I checked small cases P = 0, n = 0, and both zero.
