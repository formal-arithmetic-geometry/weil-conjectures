## Findings and corrections

The statement is valid as written. Neither global surjectivity nor finite presentation of p is needed: one applies faithful flatness only to the local homomorphism at a chosen source point. The original source's 00TT is not by itself the converse from geometric regularity to smoothness. Its assertion is a differential criterion with regularity as a consequence. The appropriate converse is Varieties Tag 038X (lemma-geometrically-regular-smooth), with 038U for the local-ring interpretation and 01V9 for flat fibrewise smoothness. The imported source field is preserved; the refined tags are in the new comparison statement and split report.

The existing ancestry through the resolution branch and the immediate parent's Temkin Proposition 3.3.5 route are retained. No chapter/unit route or realized fields are changed: this item is not a key unit node.

I reused lem-weil-pbc-regular-permanence (07NT) and lem-weil-pbc-flat-local-hom-faithfully-flat (00HR/00HQ); creating new cancellation or faithful-flat nodes would duplicate them. The two new statements are comparisons of notions, not new versions of the item: neither has a precomposition morphism, composite-regular hypothesis, or image conclusion.

## Review by Q50

I read Temkin Lemma 2.1.4(ii) in the online PDF, including its proof using faithful flatness of the local map, rather than assuming p was a cover. I checked the local Stacks texts more-algebra.tex (07C0, regular-local; 07NT, regular-permanence), more-morphisms.tex (07R8, regular), morphisms.tex (01V9, smooth-at-point), and varieties.tex (038U, geometrically-regular-at-point; 038X, geometrically-regular-smooth). The new scheme/stalk comparison is the combination of the affine regularity equivalence in 07R8 and ring localization equivalence in 07C0. Its locally Noetherian hypothesis ensures affine coordinate rings and local rings are Noetherian; it does not assume the morphism is of finite type. The second comparison uses the closed fibre of a regular local ring map, which is geometrically regular; local finite presentation supplies a finite-type fibre, 038U and 038X give smoothness at that fibre point, and 01V9 gives a smooth source neighborhood. This is the intended later proof of that statement, recorded here only; its node has no proof or proof dependencies.

For Step 1 I opened and quoted the entire statements of the new scheme/stalk comparison, def-weil-locally-ringed-space-morphism, and def-weil-pbc-g-ring. The comparison supplies both regular stalk maps, the locally ringed-space definition supplies localness and the composition rule, and the regular ring map definition supplies flatness. The notation A, B, C uses the correct order O_S,s -> O_T,t -> O_Z,z. Both applications have locally Noetherian source and target by the item hypotheses. The second comparison is not used here.

For Step 2 I opened lem-weil-pbc-flat-local-hom-faithfully-flat and lem-weil-pbc-regular-permanence. The former explicitly concludes surjectivity on spectra, not only a faithful-flat predicate; the latter requires a regular composite and a flat spectrally surjective second map, all already supplied. It concludes regularity of A -> B. I used only their statements, not constructions buried in their proofs. These exact clauses are quoted in citations.json.

For Step 3 I opened the new lfp regular-stalk comparison and matched f=g, x=t. T and S are locally Noetherian, g has the required local finite presentation, and its full stalk map is regular by Step 2. Thus it gives an actual open source neighborhood where g is smooth. It is not being applied merely to a ring that happens to be regular or to a smooth composite.

For Step 4 I opened def-sbc-smooth-morphisms. Its statement explicitly identifies the chosen definition with the affine-local smooth-ring-map definition. For each point of the union, an affine smooth chart inside its chosen open neighborhood is also an affine smooth chart inside the union. This verifies smoothness on the union directly from that affine-local definition; no theorem about g being globally flat is being smuggled in. The step is elementary and spells out its argument.

Small cases checked: if Z is empty, the image condition is vacuous and the union argument still works (the empty open is allowed). For p an open immersion into a proper smooth open of T, g may remain singular outside that open; the proof only concludes smoothness around the image. For p=id, the result reduces to locally finitely presented regular morphisms being smooth. A regular non-finitely-presented p is permitted throughout. Over an imperfect field an inseparable field extension can have a regular underlying ring without being smooth; the new comparison assumes a regular ring map, including geometric regularity, so this is not a counterexample. Constant maps to a singular point from a regular point generally fail flatness and cannot enter Step 2. No nonempty local scheme stalk is the zero ring. No dimension, characteristic, excellence, separatedness, quasi-compactness, or surjectivity assumption has been added.
