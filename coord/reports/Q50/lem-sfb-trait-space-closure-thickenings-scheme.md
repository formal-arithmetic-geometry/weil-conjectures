## Sources and remaining generic arguments

For `lem-sfb-ferrand-pushout-space-agreement`, I read `spaces-pushouts.tex` Tag 0EDP, label `lemma-pushout-along-closed-immersion-and-integral`, its statement and proof, and the three hypotheses in `more-morphisms.tex`, `situation-pushout-along-closed-immersion-and-integral`. This is precisely the category-change assertion needed, independent of traits. Its proof uses the equivalence for etale schemes over the Ferrand pushout (0ECK and 0ECL), not merely the universal property for scheme targets. The new statement therefore includes the integral and affine-neighbourhood hypotheses; it is not an assertion that arbitrary scheme colimits are preserved.

For `lem-sfb-closed-space-union-pushout`, I read `spaces-pushouts.tex` Tag 0GFQ, label `lemma-pushout-fpqc-local`, including the overlap requirement, and `morphisms.tex` Tag 0C4J, label `lemma-scheme-theoretic-union`, statement and affine-local proof. The future argument is: cover the space by etale affine charts; flat etale pullback preserves the zero intersection of ideals. On a chart the ring is the fibre product of its two quotient rings over their common quotient. The scheme closed-union pushout, and 0EDP applied to the two closed immersions, give the pushout for algebraic-space targets. The same holds on overlaps after an affine open cover. Morphisms to an algebraic space then descend from the etale atlas, or equivalently apply the local-colimit criterion with both its chart and overlap hypotheses. This generic lemma does not claim arbitrary-base-change preservation: nonflat pullback need not preserve the zero ideal intersection. It makes no schematicity assertion and does not involve a DVR.

These are all possible nonempty base opens, so the map to S is quasi-compact. The morphism-properties definition says precisely that this plus the given local finite-type chart test means finite type. The closed-immersion definition includes its affine quotient-ring description, giving quasi-compactness of the diagonal. The DVR definition says all ideals are principal, so S is Noetherian.
