Step 1 uses the normality statement of `lem-sc-smooth-normal`, which is over a field without an algebraic-closure restriction, and the finite-field compactification theorem, which explicitly supplies projectivity, smoothness, geometric connectedness and finite boundary. I checked normality and pure dimension one of the input. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian` and `lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-finite-components` provide the finiteness used when disjoint components are shown open. Local domains forbid intersecting components, so connectedness then makes the curve integral. After algebraic closure the same argument applies to the smooth connected base change. These are the hypotheses needed for the later curve and function-field arguments.

Step 2 uses exactly the statement of `lem-sc-proper-affine-finite-general`: proper plus affine implies finite. The written linear-algebra argument proves that a finite algebra over a field has no nonmaximal prime, so dimension one rules out an empty boundary. The residue field is finite because the boundary scheme is finite. Its separability is verified directly with the polynomial T^(q^ds)-T, whose derivative is -1, instead of silently assuming the point rational.

Step 3 uses `lem-sc-point-cartier` for the reduced point divisor, the general integral-scheme fraction-field portion of `def-sc-function-field`, and the full statement of `sk-thm-picard-rr`. The local dual of the ideal (t) is t^(-1)A, so the inverse tensor powers are the claimed rational-function line bundles. The cited RR statement explicitly says degree(ns) = n ds and h0 = degree + 1 - genus when degree > 2g-2. Both n=m and n=m-1 satisfy that strict inequality. This is precisely what the previous narrower citations did not provide over k0.

Step 4 uses every relevant clause of `lem-weil-pbc-sections-morphism-to-proj-polynomial-ring`: the two sections generate the line bundle, the construction is over the ground ring, the inverse images of the standard charts are the generating loci, and coordinate ratios pull back to ratios of sections. I checked the unit t^m f at s and the generator 1 away from s; therefore there is no base point. This also establishes the actual pullback of the affine chart, not merely containment of images.

Step 5 uses properness of projective space from `lemma-nagata-projective-space-base-change-proper`, the proper-source/separated-target theorem `lem-weil-pbc-proper-over-separated`, and `lem-sc-dim0-finite` for the proper closed fibres. I expanded the generic-fibre case separately: properness sends closed points to closed points, leaving at most the source generic point over the target generic point. All fibres are consequently finite. The target is locally Noetherian by its two PID charts, so the locally Noetherian hypothesis of `thm-pqf-finite-iff-proper-finite-fibres` is met. No finiteness is inferred just from the existence of a nonconstant function without this argument.

Step 6 uses exactly the DVR and basis assertions of `lem-a1-uniformizer-differential-basis`. At this point the curve is smooth and the chosen closed point has finite separable residue field. With z = 1/f = t^m v the displayed derivative follows by Leibniz; the coefficient m v + t a is a unit modulo the maximal ideal, since p does not divide m. Thus its product with t^(m-1) and the basis dt is nonzero. This is an elementary calculation from the cited statement. It proves nonzero differential at the stalk, sufficient for a nonzero sheaf morphism; no differential/function-field kernel theorem is being used without a citation.

Step 7 uses precisely the equivalence of nonzero differential and a nonempty etale restriction in `lem-a1-nonzero-differential-generically-etale`. The target is a smooth integral curve: its charts are polynomial affine lines, and lifting their one coordinate verifies the formal lifting definition in `def-sbc-smooth-morphisms`; finite presentation is explicit. The source already has those hypotheses from Step 1. Hence the criterion is applicable rather than assuming etaleness of the finite map.

Step 8 uses part (a) of `lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence`, whose statement actually asserts finite base change. Step 4 identified the inverse image open, so the square is Cartesian. The intersection of two nonempty opens of an integral curve is nonempty. Restriction retains smoothness and etaleness by their affine-local presentations as explained with the cited definitions. The geometric base change of the chosen open is nonempty because it contains C0 after base change, and is an open of a geometrically integral completion; hence it is connected. The remaining complement is a subset of the already finite boundary, so the inclusion has all the stated properties.
