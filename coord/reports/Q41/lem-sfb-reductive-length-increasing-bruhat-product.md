I read the actual PDF pages of Milne listed above, not just the item's reference. Proposition 21.75 first removes all roots other than the chosen simple root and, when v inverse alpha is positive, conjugates the remaining negative root group through the product representative. Those are exactly steps 5 and 6. I checked the sign carefully: (s v) inverse sends minus alpha to v inverse alpha, not to its negative. The proof does not use the negative-root branch of Milne's calculation or need to repair that branch's omitted final w.

Step 1 cites reductive-borel-pair-structure for the reduced root datum, base and coroot reflection formula, and weyl-reflection-realization for finite W and its faithful action on the rational root span. Both statements were opened and read; neither assumes semisimplicity. The finite sum defining the invariant form is written out, and positivity, invariance and perpendicular eigenspaces are checked. This is the explicit averaging of C.19–C.20, not an extra unquoted existence theorem. The root span, rather than the entire character space, is used, so central torus directions cause no basis error. Extending a rational basis to the reals preserves independence and the root coefficients.

Step 2 is the coordinate proof that a simple reflection permutes the other positive roots. It uses only the already established base and reflection formula, so has no new citation. A positive root supported only on alpha would be alpha by reducedness; otherwise a different positive coefficient survives reflection. Applying the reflection twice proves surjectivity. This verifies, rather than assumes, the sign hypothesis required in step 3.

Step 3 cites root-sign-word-deletion. I read its full statement: it permits any sign-partitioned root set with each simple reflection preserving the other positive roots and returns the precise deletion identity w s_b. Steps 1 and 2 verify its real inner-product and sign hypotheses. A shortest word for v inverse has length ell(v) by reversing words; the deletion identity gives a shorter word for v inverse s. Faithfulness converts equality of linear actions into equality in W. Reversing that word contradicts ell(s v) = ell(v) + 1. The empty-word case is handled by the deletion conclusion k >= 1, so there is no negative index exception hidden in the proof.

Step 4 cites reductive-root-subgroup-parametrizations for uniqueness of the smooth connected T-normalized root subgroup with the indicated tangent weight, and weyl-reflection-realization for the character action convention. Conjugation preserves the subgroup properties, and its weight is computed as beta(m inverse t m). This gives the root conjugation identity from precisely the cited uniqueness statement, rather than invoking an unstated normalizer lemma. Representative independence is the elementary quotient-group fact that representatives differ by T(F), which is a subgroup of B(F). No chosen representatives are assumed multiplicative, and no special rank-one representative or simply connected cover is required.

Step 5 cites borel-root-product-coordinates for arbitrary-order multiplication and inclusion of every positive root subgroup in U, and reductive-borel-pair-structure for B = U semidirect T. Taking F-points of these isomorphisms provides the stated factorization of every b, without a rational-point surjectivity inference from a mere scheme epimorphism. I checked that q is only a product of root-group points, never assumed a subgroup. Conjugating q stays in B because s permutes its root labels.
