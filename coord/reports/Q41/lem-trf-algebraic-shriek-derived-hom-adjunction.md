### Returned step 1 → lem-trf-coinduction-total-hom-model

Choose a K-injective $P$-complex $I$ representing $Y$ and a complex $E$ of $A$-modules representing $X$, using [def-pur-derived-internal-hom] on the one-object site with only the identity covering. Cited nodes: def-pur-derived-internal-hom.

### Returned step 2 → lem-trf-coinduction-total-hom-model

For any $A$-complex $E$ and $P$-complex $I$, use the product total Hom differential of [def-pur-hom-complex]. Define $P$-linear maps in every degree $r$ by
$$T(f)(x)=f(x)(1),\qquad S(g)(x)(a)=g(ax).$$
The second formula is $A$-linear in $x$, since $S(g)(bx)(a)=g(abx)=(bS(g)(x))(a)$, and is $P$-linear in $a$. The inverse identities are $T S(g)(x)=g(x)$ and $S T(f)(x)(a)=f(ax)(1)=(af(x))(1)=f(x)(a)$. For a homogeneous $f$ of degree $r$,
$$T(df)(x)=d_I(f(x)(1))-(-1)^r f(d_Ex)(1)=d(Tf)(x).$$
Thus $T$ is an isomorphism of complexes
$$U\operatorname{Hom}_A^\bullet(E,J)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_P^\bullet(UE,I),$$
with inverse $S$. The formula also applies to cochains of degree $-1$, hence carries chain homotopies to chain homotopies. Every product factor is treated separately, so unboundedness causes no interchange of sums and products.

Cited nodes: def-pur-hom-complex.

### Returned step 3 → lem-trf-coinduction-total-hom-model

The complex $J$ in Step 1 is K-injective over $A$. Indeed, for an acyclic $A$-complex $N$ and a chain map $v:N\to J$, the restricted complex $UN$ is acyclic because restriction leaves the kernel/image quotient in every degree unchanged. By [def-pur-kinjective-complex], the chain map $T(v):UN\to I$ has a null homotopy $s$, so $T(v)=d(s)$. Step 2 gives $v=S(d(s))=d(S(s))$. This is an $A$-linear null homotopy, exactly the defining condition of the cited node. This three-line transposition argument uses exact restriction, not preservation of K-injectivity by restriction in the opposite direction.

Cited nodes: def-pur-kinjective-complex.

### Returned step 5 → lem-trf-coinduction-shift-scalar-model

Now choose $I$ representing $P[0]$ and write $V=\operatorname{Hom}_P^\bullet(A[0],I)$, so $Q_AV=D$. For a complex $M$, take $(M[n])^i=M^{i+n}$ and $d_{M[n]}=(-1)^n d_M$, as in [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]. Since $A[0]$ is in degree zero, $\operatorname{Hom}_P(A,I[n])=V[n]$ with the same differential and action. Thus this is the model of $C(P[n])=D[n]$. For complexes $L,M$, the simultaneous-shift comparison
$$\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(L[n],M[n])\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet(L,M)$$
sends a degree-$r$ cochain $f$ to the degree-$r$ cochain with component $(-1)^{nr}f^{i-n}$ on $L^i$. The shifted Hom differential is $(-1)^n$ times the unshifted one, and $(-1)^{n(r+1)}(-1)^n=(-1)^{nr}$, so this is a chain isomorphism. Its degree-zero part is the ordinary shift of chain maps. The shift preserves K-injectivity: a map from an acyclic complex into $I[n]$ corresponds to a map from its inverse shift into $I$, and a null homotopy shifts with the same signs; apply [def-pur-kinjective-complex]. These comparisons commute with $T$, since evaluation at $1$ commutes with the factor $(-1)^{nr}$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-abstract-derived-category, def-pur-kinjective-complex.

### Returned step 6 → lem-trf-coinduction-shift-scalar-model

The scalar-action map is represented by
$$m:A[0]\longrightarrow U\operatorname{Hom}_A^\bullet(V[n],V[n]),\qquad m(a)(h)=a h.$$
It is a chain map: multiplication by $a$ commutes with the differential, so $d(m(a))=0$ in the Hom differential of [def-pur-hom-complex]. Step 2, followed by Step 5's simultaneous-shift comparison, sends it to
$$\delta:A[0]\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_P^\bullet(UV,I),\qquad \delta(a)(h)=(ah)(1)=h(a).$$
There is no shift sign, since $m(a)$ has cochain degree zero. This equality is an equality of actual chain maps. The target computes $R\operatorname{Hom}_P(R\operatorname{Hom}_P(A,P),P)$ by [def-pur-derived-internal-hom], since $I$ is K-injective and $UV$ represents $R\operatorname{Hom}_P(A,P)$.

Cited nodes: def-pur-hom-complex, def-pur-derived-internal-hom.

### Returned step 7 → lem-trf-degree-zero-derived-bidual-model

We verify that $Q\delta$ is the canonical bidual map, rather than merely a map with the correct target. By [lem-sfb-ringed-site-duality-maps] it is enough to identify the transpose of coefficient evaluation after the Koszul exchange. Choose K-flat flat-term resolutions $r:F\to A[0]$ and $t:T\to UV$ by [lem-pc-kflat-resolution]. The tensor of these resolutions computes the derived tensor by [lem-weil-pbc-site-derived-tensor-descent]. Put $W=\operatorname{Hom}_P^\bullet(F,I)$. By [lem-pur-rhom-well-defined], $r^*:UV\to W$ is a quasi-isomorphism. By [lem-pur-hom-kflat-into-kinjective], $W$ is K-injective. The model $W$ represents $R\operatorname{Hom}_P(A,P)$ by [def-pur-derived-internal-hom]. The chain tensor-Hom isomorphism of [lem-sc-derived-hom-tensor-total] is currying $f\mapsto(x\otimes y\mapsto f(x)(y))$; substituting the Hom differential verifies this sign-free formula. The evaluation pairing on $T\otimes F$ is therefore
$$z\otimes x\longmapsto (r^*t(z))(x)=t(z)(r(x))\in I.$$
It is the evaluation counit: it is the uncurrying of the model comparison $r^*t:T\to W$, hence of the identity of $R\operatorname{Hom}_P(A,P)$. The K-injective-target interpretation is justified by [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-hom-derived]. This uses no projectivity of $A$ and no flatness of $V$.

### Returned step 8 → lem-trf-degree-zero-derived-bidual-model

Precompose $\delta$ with $r:F\to A[0]$, and precompose its Hom variable with $t:T\to UV$. By the currying formula of Step 7, its transpose on $F\otimes T$ is
$$x\otimes z\longmapsto t(z)(r(x)).$$
On the other hand, exchange the factors in Step 7's evaluation pairing using the Koszul symmetry in [lem-sfb-ringed-site-duality-maps]. This gives $(-1)^{|x||z|}t(z)(r(x))$. These are the same map: if $|x|\ne0$ then $r(x)=0$, while if $|x|=0$ the sign is $1$. The targets $\operatorname{Hom}_P(F,I)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_P(T,I)$ are K-injective by [lem-pur-hom-kflat-into-kinjective]. The tensor-Hom transpose is a bijection on the K-flat/K-injective models by [lem-sc-derived-hom-tensor-total] and [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-hom-derived]; the tensor models and comparison maps are those of [lem-weil-pbc-site-derived-tensor-descent]. Thus $Q\delta$ is exactly the transpose of braided coefficient evaluation, which is the canonical bidual map in [lem-sfb-ringed-site-duality-maps]. Combined with Step 6, this proves the scalar-action compatibility for every integer $n$. The zero complexes and the zero ring satisfy the same formulas, and no step used boundedness, Noetherianity or perfectness.
