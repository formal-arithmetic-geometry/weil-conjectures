The affine V's cover the inverse images of the U's, and hence cover T.

Step 5 is the elementary kernel-coordinate argument after step 4. All coordinates in zero imply the kernel element is zero. A bijective linear map has a linear inverse by uniqueness of preimages. The tilde-Gamma functor and free-tilde theorem then take that isomorphism to the desired free sheaf. The locally-free definition is exactly an open cover by those trivializations.

Step 6 uses the locally-free definition to refine by trivializing affine opens, and free-tilde to obtain the section module C^r. The identity presentation has zero kernel, so its one-by-one relation minors vanish. Presentation independence transfers that ideal to the specified generating family; step 4 identifies it with the image ideal. Zero subsheaves are detected on an open cover by the sheaf axiom. This is a few-line application of the cited statements and explains the elementary judgment. I used a trivializing refinement, not the false assertion that a finite locally free module is free on every affine open.

Edge tests: r=0 forces E=0; r>0 with E=0 over a nonempty scheme has unit relation ideal and is not free of rank r. The empty scheme satisfies the equivalence vacuously. For r=1, E associated to A/I with I infinitely generated, the relation ideal is I, and after A to B the module is B/IB, so the criterion is IB=0 without finite presentation.
