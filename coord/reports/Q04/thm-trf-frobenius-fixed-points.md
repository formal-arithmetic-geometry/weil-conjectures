For example, take $X_0=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbf F_q[e]/e^2)$ with $m\geq1$. Frobenius sends $e$ to zero, and its fixed scheme is indeed $\operatorname{Spec}k$. 1. The displayed global map $\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_X$ denoted $\Phi^{m\#}-1$ is not available on arbitrary opens: a scheme morphism has $\Phi^{-1}\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_X$, and the base-changed Frobenius need not act identically on the geometric space. Even on a stable affine chart, the additive image is generally not an ideal. For $q=2$ on $k[t]$, $t^2-t$ is such a difference but its multiple $t^3-t^2$ is not: any nonzero polynomial difference $f(t^2)-f(t)$ has even leading degree. Step 1 uses the ideal GENERATED by differences and proves the quotient's equalizer universal property instead.
2. A general equalizer is not a closed subscheme without separatedness of the target. The conclusion here can be obtained locally from the affine quotient computation, but is not assumed globally. The cover consists of scalar extensions of affine opens of the model; these are Frobenius-stable because the model Frobenius fixes prime ideals. General arbitrary opens of $X$ are not claimed stable.
3. The source writes evaluation on $k[t]/\mathfrak a_k$ at tuples which may not satisfy the relations; that map need not exist. Step 4 evaluates first on $k[t]/(t_i^Q-t_i)$ and then quotients its product by the relation ideal. Only surviving factors evaluate on the quotient defining $X$.
4. The finiteness of the rational-point index is proved from the finite model affine cover, rather than inferred from an unqualified disjoint union. Empty schemes and empty products are included.
5. A smooth-coordinate chart need not carry Frobenius by $z_i\mapsto z_i^Q$ if its coordinates do not descend to the model. The differential is computed on generators of the model affine algebra, using dual numbers. No Frobenius stability of an arbitrarily chosen smooth affine neighbourhood is asserted: Step 8 uses $W\subset V\cap(\Phi^m)^{-1}V$ and ambient $W\times_kV$.
6. The source's tangent-vector assertion does not justify higher Tor vanishing. The new helper isolates this general regular-local algebra fact, with its exact hypotheses, from the Frobenius argument.
