## Review by Q04

I read Dwyer--Kan Proposition 2.7 and Sections 7.1, 7.4-7.6 against every clause of the unchanged item. Its weak equivalences and fibrations are exactly their mappingwise classes. The source's final free-product assertion has no cofibrancy assumptions, and steps 5-7 impose none on A, A', C or the four objects in the item.

For step 1 I opened lem-coef-sorted-simplicial-algebra-model and checked that its statement supplies not only existence but local fibrations and weak equivalences, the evaluation/free adjunction, the boundary generating maps and functorial small-object factorizations. The constructed O-times-O theory has only composition and identity operations and their equations, so its algebras are precisely the intended categories, including arbitrary loops and possibly empty off-diagonal mapping sets. This is the fixed-object example actually described in Bergner Example 3.5. The degreewise coproduct assertion is justified by its universal property rather than attributed to an existence theorem. The set O is small, so the sorts, formal finite terms and generating families are small in a suitable larger universe.

For step 2 I opened lem-coef-coherent-nerve-comparison and read its definitions of Bergner fibrations, weak equivalences and cofibrations. The extra equivalence-lifting condition, together with essential surjectivity, supplies surjectivity on actual objects for a trivial fibration. Conversely a surjection on objects with local trivial Kan fibrations gives all those properties. Pulling back the mapping objects along the specified object functions a and b produces categories on the original O even when these functions identify objects; no full-subcategory or injectivity assumption is smuggled in. The fixed-object lifting property used here is explicitly in the many-sorted theorem. This proves the cofibration comparison instead of quoting a general enriched comparison theorem.

For step 3 the same sorted-algebra statement supplies the boundary-cell factorization and trivial right factor. The added degree-k graph generators are tagged surjections [k] to [m], not all simplices of Delta-m. Non-surjections already lie in the boundary. Degeneracies preserve surjectivity; faces need not preserve the free generators and the definition does not require that. Pushouts and transfinite unions add these generators without relations beyond those in the old category, as the written finite-word description verifies. Lifting against the right factor supplies an actual section s with p s = 1 and s i = j, so the domain is fixed. This is an argument for the clause, not a new node restating it.

For step 4 I checked the two different initial objects explicitly. The Bergner initial object is the empty category, whereas the fixed-object initial object is I_O. Surjectivity on objects allows a lift from I_O against every global trivial fibration; the axiom of choice is sufficient for arbitrary small O. A lift against a fixed-object map automatically has the fixed object function. Thus global and fixed-object cofibrancy agree, and the degreewise-free retract follows from step 3. This avoids assuming that the two initial objects agree.

For step 5 I opened lem-coef-realization-finite-limits and verified that its statement says realization preserves all small colimits and all finite limits in k-spaces. This is exactly what is used for the finite products and indexed disjoint unions in the edge-word expression. An arbitrary-factors reduced-word decomposition into simplicial subsets of nonidentities would fail under faces, and is not used.
