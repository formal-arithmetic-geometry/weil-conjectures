This is a definition, so there are no numbered proof steps and no proof-step citations; `proof`, `proof_deps` and `citations.json` are all empty. I checked the statement of every dependency rather than relying on its title:

- `def-wf-fundamental-representation` specifies the actual lisse Weil pair, its geometric stalk, group and normalized Frobenius extension. This supplies the input and fixes the action convention; it assumes connected geometric base and invertible ell, which are inherited explicitly by using its setting.
- `def-pod-lisse-l-adic-sheaves` defines finite/integral systems, rationalization, finite coefficient extension and the common-stage algebraically closed coefficient category. This is the category in which the sheaf determinant is taken. Exterior powers are applied to local coefficient modules and their maps, and then use these same rationalization and scalar-extension conventions; no Frobenius-stable integral lattice is imposed. A rational Weil isomorphism can be represented after clearing a denominator; taking its exterior power and inverting that denominator defines the same rational map.
- `def-weil-pbc-exterior-algebra` explicitly gives the tensor quotient and degree-r component. `sk-d-linear` explicitly defines its induced representation action. The wedge formula in the split spells out that action, and composition of the maps on generators shows it is a representation.
- `lem-weil-pbc-exterior-power-free-basis` explicitly supplies the increasing-wedge basis of rank binomial(r,n). Applied to a basis of V and n=r, it supplies the unique basis wedge and hence dimension one. Its hypotheses are satisfied because V is finite dimensional over a field. This is a reused general fact, not a new node restating the item.
- `def-weil-endomorphism-determinant` explicitly defines the basis-independent matrix determinant. Expanding the transformed top wedge in a basis gives the permutation sum for that determinant, so the scalar action is precisely det(rho(g)); no identification from the proof of the cited node is used. Changing the basis rescales the line basis but leaves this scalar unchanged.
- `def-wf-lisse-adic-automorphism-pullback` explicitly defines reindexing on the actual etale site, at every coefficient stage, with unchanged coefficients. Reindexing a local coefficient module commutes with taking its tensor quotient, so the displayed Weil structure on the exterior sheaf has exactly the asserted direction. The fibre operation takes its local wedge basis to the wedge of the stalk basis, so the fundamental representation is the displayed exterior representation.

For the sheaf construction, on a trivialization a rank-r locally free coefficient module has its single top basis wedge. Its transition matrices act on that wedge by their determinants. The exterior-power functor respects restriction, overlap cocycles, coefficient reduction and scalar extension because all maps send a wedge to the wedge of its images. Thus this produces a lisse line with the stated Weil isomorphism in the same coefficient category. This is the definition of the sheaf operation, not an invocation of the curve-specific sheaf–representation equivalence on a more general scheme.

Small cases checked: r=0 gives the empty wedge 1 and trivial action, not the zero representation; r=1 recovers V and its original action; a diagonal matrix acts by the product of its diagonal entries; a transposition acts by -1; and a unipotent matrix of determinant 1 acts trivially on the top wedge. A nontrivial rank-one Frobenius scalar stays that scalar, so there is no accidental inverse or forced unit eigenvalue. Polynomial determinant entries preserve geometric continuity without a finite-image hypothesis.
