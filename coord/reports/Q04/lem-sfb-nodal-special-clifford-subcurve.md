## Proof details retained for the new statements

For the nodal duality/Riemann--Roch auxiliary I read `curves.tex`, Tags 0BS2 (`lemma-duality-dim-1`), 0BS3 (`lemma-duality-dim-1-CM`), 0BS4 (`remark-rework-duality-locally-free`) and 0BS6 (`lemma-rr`). Proper one-dimensional duality represents the dual of H1 by Hom into omega; a node is a hypersurface, hence Gorenstein, so omega is invertible. The nodal specialization is also the field case of the existing relative-dualizing nodes and Tag 0E6R. With M invertible, Hom(M,omega) is H0(omega tensor inverse M), by local generators, so the first displayed equality is exactly Serre duality. For the structure sheaf, H0(O)=k on a proper connected reduced curve over an algebraically closed field (existing `lem-sc-global-functions`), hence duality gives h0(omega)=g. Higher coherent cohomology vanishes above dimension one, and Riemann--Roch yields h0(M)-h1(M)=degree(M)+1-g. For the degree convention, the finite disjoint component normalization is an isomorphism at every generic point. Its structure-sheaf cokernel is supported at the finitely many nodes, with one k per node. Tensoring by M does not change any of those lengths, because its stalks are rank one. Subtract the structure-sheaf and M Euler identities: the correction terms cancel, leaving the sum of the degrees on smooth component normalizations. This is also precisely the more general generic-isomorphism version of Tag 0AYU, `varieties.tex`, `lemma-degree-birational-pullback`, whose statement and proof were read. Existence/normalization is not being inferred from the definition of normalization.

For the subcurve auxiliary I read Tag 0E33, `curves.tex`, `lemma-closed-immersion-dim-1-CM`, especially part (2), and Tag 0E34, `lemma-closed-subscheme-reduced-gorenstein`. The former states the needed ordinary sheaf-Hom identification, rather than just a definition of duality or a fully faithful embedding. For a local quotient A/I, Hom_A(A/I,omega) maps injectively to omega by f maps to f(1), and its image is exactly elements killed by I. Tensor-Hom adjunction with a locally free rank-one M, checked in a generator, gives the global map image description. At a generic point in Y the quotient is the identity, so the inclusion is an isomorphism there; at a generic point outside Y its source is zero. A reduced union of branches at a node has completed ring k[[u,v]]/(uv), k[[u]], or k[[v]] according to whether both or one branch remains. Self-nodes retain both branches of that component. Thus the subcurve is again nodal, including the possibility of a newly smooth boundary point. Projectivity follows by composing closed immersions. This is a reusable closed-immersion duality assertion, not the existence or Clifford clause of the item.

For Hopf I supply the complete intended argument here, since the new node must remain statement-only. Write r=dim V, s=dim W, t=dim P. The bilinear map induces a surjection V tensor W -> P. If its kernel K is zero, t=rs >= r+s-1 since (r-1)(s-1)>=0. Otherwise consider the Segre variety Sigma=P(V) times P(W) in P(V tensor W), of dimension r+s-2. The projective linear centre P(K) has dimension rs-t-1, whereas the ambient dimension is rs-1. If t<=r+s-2, the projective intersection dimension theorem makes Sigma intersect P(K), because the sum of their dimensions is at least the ambient dimension. A closed point of the intersection is k-rational since k is algebraically closed; it corresponds to a nonzero pure tensor v tensor w in K. This says b(v,w)=0 with v,w nonzero, contradicting the hypothesis. Consequently t>=r+s-1. For the eventual split of this node, the Segre embedding and projective intersection theorem are substantive inputs to cite separately; no such argument is hidden in the item's proof. This is an agent auxiliary, not a claim that Morrison states Hopf's lemma. The valuation-product node in the DAG cannot replace it: a reducible curve has a product of function fields, and generic restriction of all sections to one component need not be injective.
