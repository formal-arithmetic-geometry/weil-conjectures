The complete extracted argument is the following, identical to the item's steps:

1. Apply clause (1) of [lem-weil-proetale-weakly-etale-localization] to the given object $u:V\to S$ (the auxiliary ordinary ring in that lemma may be $\mathbf Z$). Its equivalence sends $W\to V$ to $(W\to S,W\to V)$ and identifies covering families. Thus the localized site is precisely $V_{\mathrm{proét}}$. Clause (1) of [lem-sfb-proetale-object-inverse-image-restriction], applied to arbitrary abelian and ring sheaves, identifies the actual inverse image with sectionwise restriction, including its maps: $(u^{-1}G)(W\to V)\cong G(W\to S)$. Only its coefficient-independent clause (1) is used, not its constant-ring module clause.

2. The coefficient map of [sk-def-sfb-coefficient-morphisms] is an isomorphism of ring sheaves for every condensed ring, without an admissibility condition. Through Step 1 write it $\alpha:R=\Gamma_S|_V\xrightarrow{\sim}B=\Gamma_V$. For an arrow $h:W'\to W$ over $V$, naturality says $B(h)\alpha(W)=\alpha(W')R(h)$, and its being a ring morphism gives $\alpha(W)(ab)=\alpha(W)(a)\alpha(W)(b)$ and $\alpha(W)(1)=1$. Its inverse satisfies the corresponding equations. These are exactly the required compatibility with restrictions and scalar multiplication.

3. Define $T:\operatorname{Mod}(R)\to\operatorname{Mod}(B)$ on a sectionwise module sheaf $M$ by retaining its abelian sheaf and setting $b\cdot_T m=\alpha(W)^{-1}(b)m$ over $W$. The ring identities give the module axioms. The restriction equation in Step 2 gives $(b\cdot_T m)|_{W'}=b|_{W'}\cdot_T m|_{W'}$, so this is a sheaf of modules in [def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category]. Retain the same underlying maps on morphisms: $R$-linearity gives $B$-linearity by this formula. Transport back uses $a\cdot n=\alpha(W)(a)n$; the two composites recover the original actions and morphisms because $\alpha^{-1}\alpha=1=\alpha\alpha^{-1}$. Thus $T$ is an equivalence and is additive, as addition of morphisms is unchanged.

4. Put $M=F|_V$. The pullback specified in [sk-def-sfb-module-pushforward-pullback] is $B\otimes_R M$. By [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product] it is the sheafification of the sectionwise tensor product. On that presheaf define $\psi_W(b\otimes m)=\alpha(W)^{-1}(b)m\in T(M)(W)$. Balancing follows from $\alpha^{-1}(b\alpha(a))m=\alpha^{-1}(b)(am)$, and $B(W)$-linearity follows from the transported action. These maps commute with restrictions by Step 2. The universal property in [def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification] therefore extends $\psi$ uniquely to a $B$-linear sheaf map $B\otimes_R M\to T(M)$. Its inverse sends $m$ to the image of $1\otimes m$; this is $B$-linear since $1\otimes\alpha^{-1}(b)m=b\otimes m$. One composite fixes $m$; the other fixes every $b\otimes m$ since $1\otimes\alpha^{-1}(b)m=b\otimes m$, and hence is the identity after sheafification by uniqueness. For a module map $f$, $f(\alpha^{-1}(b)m)=\alpha^{-1}(b)f(m)$ proves naturality. This proves $u^*\cong T\circ(-|_V)$, with underlying sections $F(W\to S)$ and precisely the asserted coefficient action.

5. Restriction $(-)|_V:\operatorname{Mod}(\Gamma_S)\to\operatorname{Mod}(R)$ is exact by [lem-pur-site-restriction-module-adjunction-exact], with the localized topology of Step 1; the coefficient ring is a sheaf of commutative rings, as required. The equivalence $T$ preserves all limits and colimits by [lem-galois-equivalence-preserves-limits], in particular the finite ones. Their composite preserves finite limits and finite colimits (apply the two preservation properties successively to each finite cone and cocone). The natural isomorphism of Step 4 transfers both properties to $u^*$ by [lem-sfb-finite-exactness-nat-iso]. Additivity can also be read directly from Step 4: restriction and $T$ preserve addition of maps and conjugating by the natural module isomorphism preserves sums. This proves the exact additive assertion of (1).

6. The module sheaf categories are abelian by [def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category], taking sites small relative to the module universe as in that definition and the site's size convention. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-derived-factorization] to the additive exact functor $u^*$ of Step 5. It carries quasi-isomorphisms to quasi-isomorphisms and yields $D(u^*)Q_S(K)\cong Q_V(C(u^*)K)$ for every unbounded cochain complex $K$. The componentwise isomorphism of Step 4 is an isomorphism of complexes: its naturality applied to each $d^n$ gives $\psi_{K^{n+1}}u^*(d^n)=T(d^n|_V)\psi_{K^n}$. Thus the termwise restricted complex with transported actions represents this derived image. If $Q_S(K)\cong Q$, applying $D(u^*)$ identifies the same complex with $D(u^*)Q$.

7. For each integer $q$, apply [lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-complex-cohomology] to $u^*$. Inverting its $c^q_K$ gives $u^*H^q(K)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^q(C(u^*)K)$. That lemma specifies the identifications of kernels and images, including cycle inclusions and cohomology projections, so this is the asserted comparison on representatives. Through Step 4 these are exactly the restriction-and-transport identifications. Both sides, viewed as functors on complexes, invert quasi-isomorphisms: cohomology does so by [def-weil-abstract-derived-category], functors carry isomorphisms to isomorphisms, and $C(u^*)$ preserves quasi-isomorphisms by Step 6. Part (2) of [lem-weil-pbc-localization-universal-property] therefore descends this natural isomorphism to $u^*H^q(Q)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^q(D(u^*)Q)$, natural in derived objects and morphisms. All arguments apply in every degree independently, without bounds on the complex; the only scalar extension was along the ring isomorphism $\alpha$, so no flatness or finiteness condition on the coefficients or on $u$ has entered.
