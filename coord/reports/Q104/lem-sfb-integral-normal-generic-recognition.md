## Audit findings

The seeded statement is correct and is retained verbatim. In particular, an integral map with unchanged generic residue fields is not called birational over a nonreduced base in the Stacks local-ring definition. The reduction footnote in 035Q (3) is essential: we apply its criterion to the induced map on reductions. Normality of Z makes it reduced, and the generic local rings of reduced schemes are fields by the existing minimal-prime localization lemma.

The existing lem-per-componentwise-normalization-properties (3) assumes that Z is locally Noetherian and cannot be used here. Likewise, finite-type-over-a-field normal-locus results do not apply to the possibly non-Noetherian Z. The proof therefore explicitly verifies finiteness of the components of inverse opens and of arbitrary quasi-compact opens in Z. It never infers local Noetherianity from integrality. Its inherited blueprint.from.node is null; blueprint_refs.py ancestor returned None, and the existing concrete alteration-proof place and via chain were retained. For step 1 I opened pqf-def-locnoeth and lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-finitely-many-components. The former explicitly states inheritance by opens; the latter assumes exactly a quasi-compact locally Noetherian scheme and concludes the finite-component assertion. Thus it applies to every quasi-compact open U, not merely affine ones.

Step 2 is an elementary topological argument written out, not an uncited normality lemma. In a scheme every irreducible closed component has a generic point. Intersecting a component with an open containing that point is dense in that component; taking closures proves the correspondence of components of an open and ambient components meeting it, in both directions. Restricting the stipulated generic-point bijection gives the finiteness of a-inverse(U). For a quasi-compact W in Z, finitely many inverse images of affine opens of T cover W, and the set of component-generic points of W is a subset of their finite union. Neither connected-component decomposition nor Noetherianity of Z is used here.

For step 3 I opened the reduced-source factorization theorem, the scheme-reduction definition and the minimal-prime reduced-localization field theorem. The first explicitly gives factorization of any reduced source through reduction, not just an image containment. Its reduced-source hypothesis follows because nilpotent germs vanish in domain stalks. The reduction definition identifies the same points with quotient structure sheaf; prime quotients and their fraction fields show that residue fields do not change. The last cited theorem explicitly gives the vanishing maximal ideal and field structure at minimal primes for any reduced ring, without Noetherianity. Applied on affine charts, it identifies both generic reduced stalks with their residue fields, so the hypothesized maps are precisely the local-ring isomorphisms required by 035Q (3). I corrected a draft source-tag typo from 00EC to the actual 00EU before delivery.

For step 4 I reread the statement of the new integral-birational-factor node: it gives exactly e:T-normalization to Z-normalization and the equality a nu_Z e = nu_T. Steps 1 and 2 establish its two finiteness hypotheses, step 3 its reductions condition, and integrality is an original hypothesis. It assumes neither normality nor Noetherianity of either scheme. I used its statement, not an intermediate construction hidden in its future proof.

For step 5 I reread the new normal-locally-finite node: the original normal-domain stalk hypothesis and step 2 supply all its assumptions on Z. It states that the actual nu_Z is an isomorphism, so composition with e and inversion prove the desired displayed equation over T. No unsupported assertion that a normalization is finite is used.

For step 6 I reread the new component-dominant lift statement, including uniqueness among all morphisms over T. Continuity and the image of the generic point verify its dominance condition on each component. Normality is given; the target's finite-component condition is step 1. This proves uniqueness of the isomorphism, rather than merely existence of some comparison. The proof needs no extra normality theorem for T-normalization.
