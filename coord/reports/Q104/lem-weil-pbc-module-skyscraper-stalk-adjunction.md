## Complete retained argument from the returned proof

*Step 1 (the skyscraper is a sheaf of modules).* The restriction maps in the statement respect the action of $\mathcal O_X$: if both opens contain $x$, $(rs)_x=r_xs_x$ by [def-weil-pbc-module-stalk], and if the smaller open does not contain $x$, both sides restrict to $0$. For an open cover $U=\bigcup_jU_j$ and compatible sections of $i_{x,*}A$, if $x\notin U$ every section is $0$ and the unique gluing is $0$ (also for the empty cover of $U=\emptyset$). If $x\in U$, choose $j_0$ with $x\in U_{j_0}$. Any two members of the cover containing $x$ have an intersection containing $x$, where their restrictions are the identity of $A$, so their sections equal the one on $U_{j_0}$; members missing $x$ have the unique zero section. This common element of $A=(i_{x,*}A)(U)$ is the unique gluing. The sectionwise group laws and the germ action thus give a sheaf of $\mathcal O_X$-modules in the sense of [def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category]. For an $\mathcal O_{X,x}$-linear $h:A\to A'$, the maps $h$ or $0$ commute with these restrictions and actions; identities, composition, and addition of maps are checked on each open, giving the asserted additive functor.

*Step 2 (the map on stalks).* If $s\in\mathcal G(U)$ and $t\in\mathcal G(V)$ represent the same germ at $x$, [def-weil-pbc-module-stalk] gives an open $W$ with $x\in W\subseteq U\cap V$ and $s|_W=t|_W$. For a sheaf morphism $\varphi:\mathcal G\to i_{x,*}A$, restriction from either $U$ or $V$ to $W$ acts as the identity on $A$; hence $\varphi_U(s)=\varphi_W(s|_W)=\varphi_W(t|_W)=\varphi_V(t)$. Thus $\Phi(\varphi)(s_x)=\varphi_U(s)$ is well defined and additive. Given $r_x\in\mathcal O_{X,x}$ represented by $r\in\mathcal O_X(V)$, restrict $r$ and $s$ to $W\ni x$ contained in $U\cap V$. The germ action and $\mathcal O_X(W)$-linearity of $\varphi_W$ give $\Phi(\varphi)(r_xs_x)=\varphi_W((r|_W)(s|_W))=r_x\varphi_W(s|_W)=r_x\Phi(\varphi)(s_x)$. Therefore $\Phi(\varphi)$ is $\mathcal O_{X,x}$-linear.

*Step 3 (constructing the inverse).* Given an $\mathcal O_{X,x}$-linear $\psi:\mathcal G_x\to A$, define $\Psi(\psi)_U(s)=\psi(s_x)$ if $x\in U$, and the unique zero map into $(i_{x,*}A)(U)=0$ otherwise. For $x\in U$, $\Psi(\psi)_U(rs)=\psi((rs)_x)=\psi(r_xs_x)=r_x\psi(s_x)$, so this component is $\mathcal O_X(U)$-linear; the other components are linear since their targets are zero. For $V\subseteq U$, if $x\in V$ then $x\in U$ and $(s|_V)_x=s_x$, so the maps commute with restriction; if $x\notin V$, both composites land in zero. These are all cases. Thus $\Psi(\psi)$ is a morphism of sheaves of $\mathcal O_X$-modules. For a germ $s_x$, $\Phi(\Psi(\psi))(s_x)=\psi(s_x)$. Conversely, if $x\in U$, then $\Psi(\Phi(\varphi))_U(s)=\Phi(\varphi)(s_x)=\varphi_U(s)$, while for $x\notin U$ both component maps have zero target. Hence $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are inverse bijections.

*Step 4 (naturality).* For $g:\mathcal G'\to\mathcal G$ and $s'_x$ represented by $s'\in\mathcal G'(U)$ with $x\in U$, [def-weil-pbc-module-stalk] gives $g_x(s'_x)=(g_U(s'))_x$, so $\Phi(\varphi\circ g)(s'_x)=\varphi_U(g_U(s'))=\Phi(\varphi)(g_x(s'_x))$. For $h:A\to A'$ linear, the component $(i_{x,*}h)_U$ is $h$ on any $U\ni x$, so $\Phi(i_{x,*}h\circ\varphi)(s_x)=h(\varphi_U(s))=h(\Phi(\varphi)(s_x))$. Germs exhaust the stalk, proving both naturality equations and hence the adjunction.

For the new extension lemma's slightly more general compatible-family form, the same component argument uses e_U in place of psi(s_x). Precomposition with g gives e_U(g_U(s)), and postcomposition with h gives h(e_U(s)); module-linearity and restriction compatibility of these families follow by the same two printed hypotheses. The equalities of extended morphisms are verified on a neighbourhood by those formulas, and on every other open by the zero target.
