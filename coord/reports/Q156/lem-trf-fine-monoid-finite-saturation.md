## Allocation of the complete returned argument

For **lem-trf-root-saturation-torsion-cone**, start with the finitely many p_i. They generate G as a group. The quotient L=G/T is finitely generated; if k times a quotient class vanishes, then kg is torsion and a further positive multiple kills g, so that class was zero. Apply the existing Mathlib leaf `lem-trf-fg-torsionfree-group-free`, whose statement gives a finite Z-basis under precisely these two hypotheses. Lift this basis to G to obtain a section of the quotient. Its complementary projection G -> T is surjective, so images of the p_i generate T. If t_1,...,t_k generate T and have positive orders m_i, reducing integer coefficients modulo m_i gives at most the product of the m_i elements. Thus T is finite (the empty product is 1). A positive common annihilator m can be this product; it is also legitimate to use #T, as the returned proof does. For the cone criterion, write a quotient class as sum q_i pi(p_i), take a positive common denominator n and put p=sum(n q_i)p_i in P. Injectivity of L -> L tensor Q gives ng-p in T; hence mn g=mp in P. Conversely ng=sum n_i p_i yields pi(g)=sum(n_i/n)pi(p_i) in the cone. This allocates the basis section, finiteness of T, rational injection, denominator clearing, and both directions of the old criterion to this task, without assuming G torsion-free.

For **lem-trf-finite-kernel-cone-translates**, use `lem-trf-gordan-rational-cone-finite` with the stated finite-rank free group L and v_i=pi(p_i). Its statement explicitly gives a finite B in C intersect L such that C intersect L is the union of b+pi(P); it is stronger than merely finite generation of that cone monoid. By surjectivity choose a lift f_b in G for each b. Let F={f_b+k : b in B, k in K}; both indexing sets are finite, and every member maps into C. If g maps into C, Gordan gives pi(g)=b+pi(p) with p in P, so k=g-f_b-p lies in K and g=(f_b+k)+p. Conversely adding p in P to a member of F remains over C because C is closed under addition and contains pi(P). This allocates the old Gordan application, finite lift set, all torsion fibres, and both inclusions of the translate equality to the second task. It applies also to lists that do not generate G and to cones containing lines, so is a separate reusable lifting/Gordan argument rather than the parent under another name.

The old closure calculation ab(g+h) in P and its saturation calculation stay in new Step 3. Finite generation follows from the same F together with the p_i. The fact that P already generates G gives the group-completion property, now with the extension and its well-definedness written out. Both localization inclusions of old Step 3 stay in new Step 4, and every monomial calculation of old Step 4 stays in new Step 5. The original full text is reproduced at the end for an exact argument-by-argument comparison.

## Returned proof retained verbatim

Old Step 1: Choose generators $p_1,\ldots,p_r$ of $P$; their classes generate $G$, so $G$ is a finitely generated abelian group. Let $T$ be its torsion subgroup and $L=G/T$ with projection $\pi:G\to L$, $\overline g=\pi(g)$. The group $L$ is finitely generated, and torsion-free: if $k\overline g=0$ with $k>0$ then $kg\in T$, so $g\in T$. By [lem-trf-fg-torsionfree-group-free], $L$ has a finite basis $e_1,\ldots,e_d$. Choosing $\tilde e_j\in G$ with $\pi(\tilde e_j)=e_j$, the map $L\to G$, $e_j\mapsto\tilde e_j$, is a section of $\pi$, so $G=T\oplus\tilde L$ and $T$ is a quotient of $G$, hence finitely generated; a finitely generated torsion abelian group with generators $t_1,\ldots,t_k$ of orders $m_1,\ldots,m_k$ has at most $m_1\cdots m_k$ elements, so $T$ is finite, of order $m$ say. Apply [lem-trf-gordan-rational-cone-finite] to $L$ and $v_i=\overline p_i$; its monoid generated by the $v_i$ is $\overline P=\pi(P)$, and it gives the rational cone $C$ and a finite set $\overline F\subseteq C\cap L$ with $C\cap L=\bigcup_{\overline f\in\overline F}(\overline f+\overline P)$. Claim: $g\in P^{\mathrm{sat}}$ if and only if $\overline g\in C$. If $\overline g=\sum_i q_iv_i$ with $q_i\in\mathbf Q_{\ge0}$, let $n>0$ be a common denominator of the $q_i$; then $n\overline g=\sum_i(nq_i)v_i=\overline p$ with $p=\sum_i(nq_i)p_i\in P$, so $ng-p\in T$ and $m(ng-p)=0$, i.e. $(mn)g=mp\in P$. Conversely, if $ng=\sum_i n_ip_i\in P$ with $n>0$, then $\overline g=\sum_i(n_i/n)v_i\in C$ in $L\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\mathbf Q$, $L$ being free so that $L\to L\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\mathbf Q$ is injective. Thus $P^{\mathrm{sat}}=\pi^{-1}(C\cap L)$. For each $\overline f\in\overline F$ choose $f\in G$ with $\pi(f)=\overline f$ and put $F=\{f+t:\overline f\in\overline F,\ t\in T\}$, a finite set contained in $\pi^{-1}(C\cap L)=P^{\mathrm{sat}}$. If $g\in P^{\mathrm{sat}}$, then $\overline g=\overline f+\overline p$ for some $\overline f\in\overline F$ and $p\in P$, so $t=g-f-p\in T$ and $g=(f+t)+p$ with $f+t\in F$. Conversely $F+P\subseteq P^{\mathrm{sat}}$ because $P^{\mathrm{sat}}$ is closed under addition (if $ag,bh\in P$ then $ab(g+h)\in P$). Hence $P^{\mathrm{sat}}=\bigcup_{f\in F}(f+P)$.

Old Step 2: The inclusion $P\subseteq P^{\mathrm{sat}}\subseteq G$ and the finite translate set in Step 1 show that $P^{\mathrm{sat}}$ is finitely generated and that its group completion is $G$, because $P$ already generates $G$. It is integral because it is a submonoid of the group $G$. It is saturated by definition: if $ng\in P^{\mathrm{sat}}$ for $n>0$, then some $mng\in P$, hence $g\in P^{\mathrm{sat}}$.

Old Step 3: For a submonoid $S\subseteq P$, if $x\in(P-S)^{\mathrm{sat}}$, choose $n>0$ and $p\in P,s\in S$ with $nx=p-s$. Then $n(x+s)=p+(n-1)s\in P$, so $x+s\in P^{\mathrm{sat}}$ and $x\in P^{\mathrm{sat}}-S$. Conversely, if $x=a-s$ with $a\in P^{\mathrm{sat}}$ and $s\in S$, choose $n>0$ with $na\in P$; then $nx=na-ns\in P-S$. Hence $(P-S)^{\mathrm{sat}}=P^{\mathrm{sat}}-S$ inside $G$.

Old Step 4: Every element of the monoid algebra $R[P^{\mathrm{sat}}]$ is a finite $R$-linear combination of basis monomials $z^g$. By Step 1, write $g=f+p$ with $f\in F$ and $p\in P$, so $z^g=z^f z^p$. Therefore the finitely many monomials $z^f$ for $f\in F$ generate $R[P^{\mathrm{sat}}]$ as an $R[P]$-module.
