## Statement and source errors

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported unequal-thickness diagrams are not literally ghost blowup sequences, and contracting empty stages does not literally compare their different thicknesses. Here is an explicit counterexample to that inference. Let X be the disjoint union of the nodal curve Spec(k[x,y]/(xy)) and Spec(k[t]), with distinguished element (x+y,t). It is a non-zero-divisor, and the nonregular locus of X lies in its zero locus. Every strong resolution of the node has at least one nonempty stage. All its centers are empty on the smooth component. But on that component an arrow of the first staggered restriction is Spec(k[t]/t^(n(n-1))) to Spec(k[t]/t^(n^2)). Its center is empty and it is not an isomorphism: the class t^(n(n-1)) is nonzero in the target coordinate ring and zero in the source. Thus it fails the definition of a ghost blowup even though the ordinary arrow on the smooth component is an isomorphism. The open-and-closed inclusion Y=Spec(k[t]) to X is regular. F(Y) has length zero, whereas deleting the empty centered arrow in the unadjusted staggered diagram leaves a base of the wrong thickness.

I repaired the statement by comparing every level at a COMMON precision t at most n(n-P), P the maximum of the two original tower lengths. Empty arrows at this common precision are actual isomorphisms. As n grows these precisions are cofinal. All centers eventually retain their entire scheme structure, and the intended completed ordered-tower conclusion is retained. This is a correction faithful to the completion argument of section 3.4.1, not an assertion that the problematic literal finite diagrams are equal. I also distinguish independence of induced finite comparisons from uniqueness among all automorphisms of an arbitrary nilpotent thickening. The completed centered-tower comparison is unique.

The factorization paragraph in the printed proof of Proposition 3.3.5 reverses X and Y relative to h:Y to X. The item uses Y to Y_b to X_a, with the source stage made large and the target stage fixed. This fixes arrow types and lets r depend on X only. The old statement itself already had the right direction for h.

## Review by Q156

For the item I read Temkin's actual definitions of principal pairs, pair morphisms and conductors in sections 3.2.3--3.2.5, the recovery and span results 3.3.1--3.3.2, the chart calculation in 3.3.3, the staggered restrictions and ghost definitions 3.3.5--3.3.6, both smooth and regular comparison proofs 3.3.4--3.3.5, and the completion application 3.4.1. I checked the imported quantifiers: a single r for X, n depending on both pairs and the fixed rule, arbitrary regular thick maps, no field-linearity requirement. Finite characteristic-zero fields in this sentence means fields of characteristic zero, not finite fields. The fixed characteristic-zero hypothesis is essential.

For lem-per-elkik-smooth-span-lifting I read Corollary 3.3.2 with the hypotheses of Proposition 3.3.1, rather than citing the span without its n>r and n+r lift. The uniform smooth-target extension uses precisely Remark 3.2.2: conductor does not increase after smooth extension, and an r which is larger than the actual conductor still works by restriction. Source and target pairs are finite type, pi is a non-zero-divisor, the map preserves the ideal, and the etale neighborhood is identical on every thick fibre. A single affine neighborhood was not assumed for a general neighborhood. If the ideal is the unit ideal, all fibres are empty and there is no assertion that an empty formal neighborhood reconstructs the whole algebraization.

For lem-per-elkik-rational-smooth-models I read the full proof of Proposition 3.3.5 and Lemma 2.1.4. I checked the rational target stage is fixed first and the source stage is enlarged afterward. The new statement does not assert a lift of the smooth fibre map to an ordinary map of algebraizations. The smooth locus neighborhood is allowed to be open, with finite affine covers, so no unwarranted affine-intersection hypothesis appears. Its existence has no resolution rule F in its statement: it is a geometric approximation fact, not a renamed clause of this item.

For lem-per-supported-blowup-precision-loss I read the fraction computation on the formal blowup charts in Lemma 3.3.3, including the comparison of the g and phi(g) charts. On an overlap, the difference of fractions has the form rho^(m-e) times (rho^e/u(g)) times a regular fraction, because the center ideal contains the e-th power. When comparing two maps, their center generators differ by rho^m; m>e makes the corresponding chart overlap the whole reduced chart at the lower precision. This proves equality modulo rho^(m-e), not modulo rho^m. The complete, Noetherian and non-zero-divisor hypotheses and the exact extended-ideal equality are stated. Empty centers have e=0; iterating loses the SUM of finitely many exponents. It neither claims arbitrary automorphisms of truncated blowups are trivial nor claims bounded loss for infinitely many stages.

Step 1 cites only the actual smooth-span statement and uses F-functoriality as an explicit hypothesis of the item. The whole closed fibre is in the etale neighborhood, so a supported nonempty center cannot disappear there. Step 2 cites the actual rational-model/factorization statement and the uniform smooth-span bound, which jointly justify the asymmetric quantifiers; the regular projections supply exactly the functoriality needed. Step 3 uses the two-map congruence statement, not merely a blowup definition: containment of a power in each center makes congruent extended ideals equal, and the calculation then applies inductively. The bounds are drawn from fixed initial model towers and remain valid under later smooth pullback, so the bound cannot secretly depend on the map being compared. Step 4 is only the explicit inequalities and the common-precision assembly. Step 5 cites regular base change and completion-mod-power exactly for their statements (finite quotient is finite type; the original ideal is principal and hence finitely generated), plus the precision-loss estimate for independence as n grows. Step 6 uses the same estimate for arbitrarily high precision and the inverse-limit structure sheaf defined in the statement, giving actual inverse formal maps. Step 7 invokes the rational-model finite-union clause and uses the given synchronized F tower; it does not resynchronize independently resolved components.
