## Previous proof, retained verbatim for the extracted tasks

### Previous step 1

For each coefficient field $F$ under consideration, apply [lem-weil-pbc-continuous-coefficients-immersion-restriction] to $X=s$. Its noetherian hypothesis holds: the spectrum of a field has only one point and its coordinate ring is noetherian. Its continuous-function sheaf description identifies $R_F$ canonically with $V\mapsto C(|V|,\mathcal O_F)$, compatibly with the finite quotient projections. In particular $\Gamma(s,R_F)=C(\{*\},\mathcal O_F)=\mathcal O_F$, by evaluation at the unique point. These identifications respect coefficient inclusions, since their maps to each quotient send a constant value to its residue and the inverse-limit projections determine a map uniquely. Apply [lem-weil-finite-free-completed-scalar-comparison] to $Y=s$: $R_L$ is a finite free $R_E$-module sheaf, of rank $d=[L:E]$. Choose a sheaf-module basis $b_1,\ldots,b_d$, meaning an isomorphism $R_E^{\oplus d}\to R_L$. Taking sections identifies it with an isomorphism $\mathcal O_E^{\oplus d}\to\mathcal O_L$: finite direct sums of sheaves of modules are finite products, and products of sheaves are sectionwise. Thus these same global sections $b_i$ are an $\mathcal O_E$-basis of $\mathcal O_L$. This derives precisely the compatible basis needed below; it does not identify the completed coefficient sheaf with the discrete constant sheaf.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-continuous-coefficients-immersion-restriction, lem-weil-finite-free-completed-scalar-comparison.

### Previous step 2

For any sheaf $F$ of $R_E$-modules define the balanced map $\theta_F:\Gamma(s,F)\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}\mathcal O_L\to\Gamma(s,F\otimes_{R_E}R_L)$ by $n\otimes a\mapsto n\otimes a$, where the tensor on the right is the image of the sectionwise tensor in the sheaf tensor product [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product]. Under the basis of step 1, $F\otimes_{R_E}R_L\cong F^{\oplus d}$: the inverse coordinate maps send $f\otimes\sum_i r_i b_i$ to $(r_i f)_i$. Taking sections gives $\Gamma(s,F)^{\oplus d}$, while the source of $\theta_F$ has the same description, and $\theta_F$ is the identity on each coordinate. Its inverse sends $(n_i)_i$ to $\sum_i n_i\otimes b_i$. In particular it is an isomorphism. For $a,c\in\mathcal O_L$, the equality $\theta_F(n\otimes ac)=c\theta_F(n\otimes a)$ shows $\mathcal O_L$-linearity. Every sheaf map commutes with this formula, so it is natural. Apply degreewise to any complex; differentials commute with $\theta$ by the same formula. The basis proves both tensor functors exact, since they are finite direct sums, so they preserve quasi-isomorphisms. Exactness of $\Gamma(s,-)$ from [lem-weil-point-proetale-sections-exact] supplies quasi-isomorphism preservation on sections. These are inverse chain maps, not an assertion that arbitrary sheaf tensor products are computed sectionwise.

Cited nodes: def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product, lem-weil-point-proetale-sections-exact.

### Previous step 3

Choose a K-flat resolution $P\to K$ with flat terms by [lem-pc-kflat-resolution]. Then $P\otimes_{R_E}R_L$ computes $K\otimes^L_{R_E}R_L$ by [lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings]. The degreewise map of step 2, applied to $P$, therefore gives $\theta_{L/E,K}$ on derived categories: $\Gamma(s,P)\to\Gamma(s,K)$ is a quasi-isomorphism by [lem-weil-point-proetale-sections-exact], and its tensor with $\mathcal O_L$ is a quasi-isomorphism by step 1's basis. The integral input $K$ is derived complete by [def-weil-derived-complete-integral-detailed]. Hence [lem-weil-finite-free-completed-scalar-comparison] gives the specified natural isomorphism $\chi_{L/E,K}$, and $\Gamma(s,\chi_{L/E,K})$ is an isomorphism. Define $\varphi$ as the inverse of their composite, as in the statement. Naturality for chain maps follows from step 2 and naturality of $\chi$. For an arbitrary derived morphism use a roof $K\xleftarrow{q}H\xrightarrow{f}J$ with $q$ a quasi-isomorphism. The termwise functors $A(C)=\Gamma(s,C)\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}\mathcal O_L$ and $B(C)=\Gamma(s,C\otimes_{R_E}R_L)$ both preserve every quasi-isomorphism by step 2, not only those between K-flat complexes. For a chain map $g:C\to C'$ the formula on tensors gives $B(g)\theta_C=\theta_{C'}A(g)$. Applying this to $q,f$ and inverting $A(q),B(q)$ gives $B(f)B(q)^{-1}\theta_K=\theta_J A(f)A(q)^{-1}$, the required roof naturality. For a K-flat resolution $P\to C$, step 2 also makes $P\otimes_{R_E}R_L\to C\otimes_{R_E}R_L$ a quasi-isomorphism, so [lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings] identifies this termwise functor canonically with derived change of rings. Thus the roof calculation proves naturality and independence of representatives for the specified $\theta_{L/E,K}$; composing with the natural $\chi$ proves the assertion for $\varphi$.

### Previous step 5

\Gamma(\alpha)\,\theta_{M/L,A}(\theta_{L/E,K}\otimes1)=\theta_{M/E,K}\,a^{\mathrm{mod}},\qquad A=K\otimes^L_R S,

$$
\theta_{M/L,T_{L/E}K}(\Gamma(C)\otimes1)=\Gamma(F(C))\theta_{M/L,A}.
$$
Writing $\psi_{L/E,K}=\Gamma(\chi_{L/E,K})\theta_{L/E,K}$, substitution now gives the explicit cancellations
$$
\begin{aligned}
\Gamma(a^{\mathrm{int}})\,\psi_{M/L,T_{L/E}K}(\psi_{L/E,K}\otimes1)
&=\Gamma(H)\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(F(C^{-1}))\Gamma(D^{-1})\Gamma(D)\,
\theta_{M/L,T_{L/E}K}(\Gamma(C)\otimes1)(\theta_{L/E,K}\otimes1)\\
&=\Gamma(H)\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(F(C^{-1}))\Gamma(F(C))\,
\theta_{M/L,A}(\theta_{L/E,K}\otimes1)\\
&=\Gamma(H)\Gamma(\alpha)\theta_{M/L,A}(\theta_{L/E,K}\otimes1)\\
&=\psi_{M/E,K}\,a^{\mathrm{mod}}.
\end{aligned}
$$
The common domain is $(\Gamma(s,K)\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}\mathcal O_L)\otimes_{\mathcal O_L}\mathcal O_M$. Multiplying by the inverse maps $\varphi=\psi^{-1}$ gives the statement's composition equation. For $L=E$, $\theta(n\otimes r)$ followed by multiplication is $rn$, so under the completeness-unit identification of step 4 the comparison is the identity. For four coefficient fields, each ordinary iterated associator sends $(((n\otimes a)\otimes b)\otimes c)$ to $n\otimes abc$; substitution of the displayed conjugation cancels each intermediate $\chi^{-1}\chi$, proving the iterated coherence. The comparisons $C,D,H$ are natural isomorphisms by [lem-weil-finite-free-completed-scalar-comparison].

### Previous step 6

Put $N=\Gamma(s,K)$. Tensoring the integral comparison with $L$ gives $\Gamma(s,T_{L/E}K)\otimes_{\mathcal O_L}L\cong(N\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}\mathcal O_L)\otimes_{\mathcal O_L}L\cong N\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}L$, where the second map sends $(n\otimes a)\otimes b$ to $n\otimes ab$ and its inverse sends $n\otimes b$ to $(n\otimes1)\otimes b$. The DVR assertion [lem-weil-l-adic-extension-integers-dvr] makes $E$ the fraction field of $\mathcal O_E$. For a nonzero $r\in\mathcal O_E$, multiplication by $r$ on $N\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}L$ is invertible, its inverse multiplying the $L$-coordinate by $r^{-1}$. Thus there are inverse maps $(N\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}E)\otimes_E L\leftrightarrows N\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}L$ given by $(n\otimes x)\otimes b\mapsto n\otimes xb$ and $n\otimes b\mapsto(n\otimes1)\otimes b$; moving a denominator across the tensor relation proves the other composite is the identity. These maps commute with differentials. Localization is exact: a fraction maps to zero precisely when some nonzero denominator annihilates its numerator, so localizing preserves kernels as well as images; hence all termwise localizations preserve quasi-isomorphisms. Extension from $E$ to $L$ is exact because every finite-dimensional field extension has a basis (successively choose a vector outside the span until the finite dimension is exhausted), so its underlying functor is a finite direct sum. The same reasoning applies to $L\subset M$. Consequently these are isomorphisms in $D(L)$, natural in $K$. On a tower all localization and associativity maps send a pure tensor with coefficient factors $a,b,c$ to their product $abc$; applying step 5's integral equation and these balanced maps proves the rational identity and composition equations, including fractions since the inverted coefficients act by invertible maps.
