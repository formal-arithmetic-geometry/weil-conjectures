I checked the split against delivery-errors B--E with the cited node statements and ILO pp.103--106 open. There are no new nodes to conceal a clause of the item, and the five proof steps together contain 2392 characters; no individual step exceeds 2000 characters. Every step has its uses explicitly listed and proof_deps is their union. citations.json quotes verbatim the relevant public node statements, not their proofs. All sources are locally available, and no Stacks tag is offered as though it stated this specifically monoidal result.

Step 1 states the inductive datum and its base, using the definition's centered tower convention. This is elementary: at length zero the prescribed base and its realization supply all the data. The induction is on the given finite length, not on a regularity assertion that is proved later.

Step 2 applies exactly clause (i) of `lem-trf-toric-monoid-comparison`: a bijection of coherent monoid ideals with coherent torus-equivariant ring ideals, including the zero/empty case and nonzero/nonempty assertion. Its hypotheses hold since the datum through stage i includes fineness, torsion freeness, a finite affine cover and the equivariant identification preserving the original torus. The public statement of `lem-trf-monoscheme-finite-chart-components` explicitly provides clopen integral realization components and their identification with the connected components. Hence an empty monoid ideal on a component would make the zero scheme contain that nonempty open component, contradicting nowhere density. This uses neither an unproved claim of affine intersections nor any change of coefficient field.

Step 3 applies exactly clause (ii) of the comparison: it states both the canonical equivariant normalized-blowup identification and fineness, torsion freeness and a finite affine cover for the new monoscheme. The nonempty-on-each-component hypothesis was proved in Step 2. Its unchanged original dense torus ensures that the invariant-center hypothesis remains the correct one for the following induction step. This is existence by the actual specified saturated blowup, not an unspecified object chosen from an existence assertion.

Step 4 is the elementary uniqueness induction using the same comparison's injective ideal correspondence and canonical chart comparison. Two candidate lifts start at the prescribed M; after agreement through i their ring center is the same J_i, so their monoid ideals coincide and their specified saturated blowups and canonical chart identifications coincide. It does not claim that every arbitrary scheme automorphism is monoidal, nor that a scheme blowup morphism determines its ideal center. Ordered ideals are retained; no contraction occurs.

Step 5 uses the final sentence of clause (iii), which explicitly says that a regular Q-realization has stalks N^r plus Z^t. Thus no extra regular-implies-smooth theorem is silently inserted here: that implication is already part of the public cited comparison statement. The definition identifies precisely this stalk condition as smoothness.

I tested length zero (including the regularity conclusion), empty monoscheme (all component conditions vacuous and the ideals unique), multiple components (nowhere density excludes a zero ideal separately on each clopen component), and the unit-ideal center. In the last case an unsaturated initial monoscheme may acquire a genuine saturation, just as its scheme may acquire a genuine normalization; Step 3 retains that stage. A zero center on a nonempty component is correctly excluded by nowhere density. These checks required no correction to the item statement. I also verified that the comparison and component nodes do not depend on this item, so their reuse introduces no reverse dependency cycle.
