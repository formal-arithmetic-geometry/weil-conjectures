### Original step 1 → lem-sfb-common-normal-compatible-groups

Enumerate the finite family. Construct finite extensions $L_r/e$ containing the first $r$ fields: start with $L_0=e$; put $L_r$ and $F_{r+1}$ in a common field and replace that field by their compositum. The compositum degree bound gives $[L_{r+1}:L_r]\leq[F_{r+1}:e]$, so $L_{r+1}/e$ is finite (multiply spanning sets along the tower). Apply the finite normal-extension lemma to the final $L_r/e$, obtaining a finite normal $K/e$ and the required embeddings $F_i\hookrightarrow K$. Identify each $F_i$ with its image.

Original cited statements: lem-weil-common-extension-fields, lem-weil-pbc-compositum-degree-bound, lem-weil-pbc-finite-extension-in-finite-normal-extension.

### Original step 2 → lem-sfb-common-normal-compatible-groups

Put $A=\operatorname{Aut}_e(K)$. It is finite: choose a finite $e$-basis of $K$; an automorphism is determined by the images of these basis elements, each of which must be a root of its nonzero minimal polynomial, so there are finitely many choices. Put $G=A\times\prod_{j\in I}J_j$, with its surjection $\pi:G\to A$. Define $\Gamma_i=\{(a,(j_t)_t):a|_{F_i}=j_i\}$. Compatibility is preserved under multiplication and inversion, hence this is a subgroup. Its map to $J_i$ is surjective: given $j\in J_i$, lift the $e$-automorphism of $F_i$ by which $j$ acts (the action need not be faithful) to $a\in A$ by the intermediate-isomorphism lifting lemma, and take $(a,(j_t)_t)$ with $j_i=j$ and all other $j_t=1$. No normality of $F_i/e$ is used. The action of $G$ on $K$ has image $A$, so $K^G=K^A$; the normal fixed-field lemma makes this extension of $e$ purely inseparable.

### Original step 3 → elementary diagonal-action assembly (new parent step 2)

Fix $i$, and abbreviate $\Gamma=\Gamma_i$, $V=V_i$, $F=F_i$, $B=B_i$. Form $W=V\times_F K$. ### Original step 4 → lem-sfb-semilinear-coset-scheme

Choose representatives $s(c)$ of the left cosets $c\in G/\Gamma$, with $s(\Gamma)=1$, and form the coproduct $Y=\coprod_c W_c$ in schemes. On the copy represented by $g=s(c)$ give the scalar structure $p_g=\operatorname{Spec}(\pi(g)^{-1})p$. This represents the contracted product: replace an arbitrary pair $(g\gamma,w)$ by $(g,\gamma_Ww)$. The two scalar structures agree, since $p_g\gamma_W=\operatorname{Spec}(\pi(g)^{-1})\operatorname{Spec}(\pi(\gamma)^{-1})p=\operatorname{Spec}((\pi(g)\pi(\gamma))^{-1})p=p_{g\gamma}$. Each pair has a unique representative in a chosen copy, and changing representatives gives these scheme isomorphisms, which compose by the action law. Consequently this finite coproduct realizes the relation $(g\gamma,w)\sim(g,\gamma w)$ as a scheme, without any quotient-existence hypothesis on $V$.

### Original step 5 → lem-sfb-semilinear-coset-scheme

### Original step 7 → lem-sfb-normal-scalar-extension-geometric-orbits

Let $b:\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to B$ be geometric, and suppose the underlying points of $V_b$ form a nonempty single $J_i$-orbit. These points are all $\Omega$-rational. Indeed, $V_b$ is locally of finite type over $\Omega$: on affine charts the algebra $R\otimes_S\Omega$ is generated over $\Omega$ by finite generators of $R/e$, since $V$ is finite type over the finite extension $F/e$. A nonempty finite-type affine chart has a maximal ideal, whose residue field is finite over $\Omega$ by Zariski\textquotesingle s lemma and hence equals $\Omega$. Its $J_i$-translates exhaust $V_b$ and stay rational because the action on the fibre is over $\Omega$. Above each rational $x\in V_b$, the projection $W_b\to V_b$ has fibre $\operatorname{Spec}(\Omega\otimes_{F,x}K)$. This algebra is nonzero and finite-dimensional over $\Omega$ (a basis of $K/F$ remains a basis after tensoring). Its prime quotients are finite-dimensional domains and hence fields; being finite extensions of the algebraically closed $\Omega$, they equal $\Omega$. Its points therefore correspond exactly to embeddings $\alpha:K\hookrightarrow\Omega$ extending the scalar map $F\to\Omega$ of $x$. A maximal ideal proves this set is nonempty, including when the tensor algebra has nilpotents.

### Original step 8 → lem-sfb-normal-scalar-extension-geometric-orbits

The points of $W_b$ form one $\Gamma$-orbit. Given two such points, first choose $j\in J_i$ carrying the first image in $V_b$ to the second and lift $j$ to $\gamma\in\Gamma$. After applying $\gamma$ the two points lie over the same rational $x$. The extension $K/F$ is normal by normality over intermediate fields. Its embeddings in $\Omega$ extending this $F$-structure differ by an element of $\operatorname{Aut}_F(K)$, by the normal-embeddings lemma. Every $a\in\operatorname{Aut}_F(K)$ gives $(a,(1)_t)\in\Gamma$, acting over $V$; on these embeddings its action is $\alpha\mapsto\alpha\circ a^{-1}$. As $a$ ranges through the group, this is transitive on the embeddings. This gives the second move between the two points without assuming separability of $K/F$.

### Original step 10 → lem-sfb-finite-scalar-extension-generic-points; final clopen-copy assembly in new parent step 5

Finally consider a generic point $y$ of a component of $W$. On an affine chart $\operatorname{Spec}R$ of $V$, the projection is $\operatorname{Spec}(R\otimes_F K)\to\operatorname{Spec}R$. This ring extension is finite free. It is flat because tensoring an $R$-module with it is a finite direct sum of that module, and hence preserves exact sequences. If the prime $\mathfrak q$ of $y$ contracts to $\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak p_0\subseteq\mathfrak p$, flat going-down gives $\mathfrak q_0\subseteq\mathfrak q$ above $\mathfrak p_0$. Minimality of $\mathfrak q$ forces equality, hence $\mathfrak p$ is minimal and $x=\operatorname{pr}_1(y)$ is generic in $V$. The algebra $\kappa(x)\otimes_F K$ is finite-dimensional; its quotient at the prime of $y$ is a finite-dimensional domain, hence a field, and equals $\kappa(y)$. Thus $\kappa(y)/\kappa(x)$ is finite. By the hypothesis on $v_i$, $v_i(x)$ is generic in $B_i$ and $\kappa(x)/\kappa(v_i(x))$ is finite; multiplying finite spanning sets proves the same for $\kappa(y)/\kappa(v_i(x))$. The clopen copies in $Y_i$ have exactly the generic points of their copies of $W$; scalar twists do not change the underlying $e$-scheme or the map to $B_i$. This proves the generic-point clause.
