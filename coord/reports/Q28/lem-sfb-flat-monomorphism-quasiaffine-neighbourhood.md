## Review by Q28

I read Raynaud's primary scan, Propositions 1.1, 1.2(i), 2.3(i), and the proof and two preceding lemmas on printed pp.14–15. The item assumes a quasi-compact flat mono, a quasi-affine source (hence quasi-compact), and a quasi-separated target. These are exactly the source hypotheses. No finite-presentation or Noetherian assumption is introduced. Quasi-compactness is included in Raynaud's quasi-affine convention. For the extension node, 2.3(i) applies to the whole quasi-compact source identified with its induced ringed subspace by 1.2(i); generalization stability comes from 1.1. Its statement does not require that image to be open. The principal-neighbourhood criterion is the preceding lemma on p.14: every ambient neighbourhood contains all E and is quasi-separated, and E is quasi-compact. I also read Stacks properties.tex, lemma-quasi-compact-quasi-separated-spectral (094L), and topology.tex, lemma-constructible-stable-specialization-closed (0903), especially part (1).

Step 1 uses the extension node's stability clause and the existing principal-comparison-cover statement. I opened the latter: it says its V_g on the ambient quasi-affine scheme lie in the specified arbitrary open and cover that open. For f inverse A inside X this gives x in X_a contained in f inverse A, without needing f inverse A quasi-compact. E is quasi-compact by continuity from quasi-compact X. The affine neighbourhood A uses the defining affine basis of a scheme.

Step 2 uses the new extension statement with Z=X and Y=T. Every hypothesis is available: f is quasi-compact flat mono, X quasi-compact and T quasi-separated. It gives a quasi-compact open V containing all E and g pulling back to a. The equation f inverse V_g = X_a is the stalk formula and reflection of units by local ring homomorphisms, not a claimed isomorphism or open image of f. This proves E intersection V_g subset A.

Step 3 uses the Boolean-algebra clause of def-weil-constructible-subset and the precise statements of the two existing spectral nodes, all opened and read. The qcqs-scheme node applies to V because it is quasi-compact and open in quasi-separated T. The closure-specialization node applies to F as the intersection of a one-element family of constructibles in that spectral V. It yields z in F generalizing any e in E intersection closure F, contradicting generalization stability and F disjoint E. Thus V minus closure F contains all E; the argument is written in the step instead of hidden in a new 05LW node. The closure-specialization node is declared as a generic topology tool use; no same-unit node gives this closure description. The qcqs spectrality node is already a Mathlib leaf.

For that same step I checked the constructibility hypotheses carefully. V_g is quasi-compact by taking principal affines on a finite affine cover of V; V intersection A is quasi-compact by quasi-separatedness and that finite cover. Intersecting finite affine covers proves retrocompactness, so their difference F is constructible in V. No intersection of affine opens is claimed affine. Choose finitely many affine opens inside V minus closure F covering E; their union W is quasi-compact. Its basic open is quasi-compact on a finite affine cover and is contained in the actual affine A, giving quasi-affineness directly by definition. All factorizations here are through open subschemes; no factorization through a non-open subscheme is inferred from point containment.

Step 4 uses exactly implication (ii) to (i) of the new principal-neighbourhood criterion. Every W is quasi-separated as an open of T, contains all E rather than just the current point, and has the function g restricted to W with its quasi-affine basic open containing that point. Thus each quantified hypothesis holds. This criterion is a genuine gluing prerequisite: its future proof takes a finite subcover, intersects the ambient neighbourhoods, and uses the preceding quasi-affine basic-open covering criterion. That future argument is recorded here, not inside its statement-only node.

Small cases checked: empty source and empty E (choose the empty affine scheme), identity maps of quasi-affine schemes, and localization maps Spec(S inverse A) to Spec A, including generic points which are not open. In the localization example function extension is a fraction represented on a principal neighbourhood; it does not turn the flat mono into an open immersion. The principal criterion handles one-member covers and empty E, while retaining quasi-compactness so finite intersection is sufficient.
