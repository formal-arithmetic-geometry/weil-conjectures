Steps 1–2 use finite-type Noetherianity, localization Noetherianity, Noetherian completion, the Jacobson-radical fact and completion modulo its ideal. Finiteness of B over the Noetherian R supplies finite type; x belongs to every maximal ideal because B is x-adically complete. The exact-completion and finite-module tensor statements apply to finite ideals and quotients of the Noetherian local ring A. The quotient A/m^j has nilpotent x, so its x-adic completion stabilizes. The resulting isomorphisms commute with quotient maps because all come from A to C. This identifies the maximal-adic completions of A and C, rather than confusing either with the x-adic completion C itself.

Step 3 uses precisely quasi-excellent localization, the local complete-pair G-ring lifting theorem, semilocal G-ring implies J-2, regularity of the G-ring completion, faithful flatness of local completion, and normality descent. The lifting theorem is applied to C, which is local, Noetherian and complete for xC, with G-ring quotient C/xC. It is not applied to the generally incomplete B_n. Normality of the common maximal completion is an explicit hypothesis of this item, so the normality-descent node applies. Local normality gives a domain. Semilocal J-2 applies because a local ring has one maximal ideal and proves the singular locus is closed for D=C.

Step 4 uses quasi-excellent localization, G-ring completion regularity and the formal-fibre quotient isomorphism. Its nonzero prime t makes R/t quasi-excellent by hypothesis, which is exactly what is needed to identify all nongeneric formal fibres as geometrically regular. Step 5 uses the Jacobson-radical theorem, Theorem 4.1's existing G-ring closed-fibre criterion, the maximal-ideal G-ring criterion and local-completion flatness. The Noetherianity of the generic fibre is checked as a localization of the Noetherian completion. The definition of geometric regularity then gives a finite purely inseparable extension detecting its failure. Degree one is allowed, so this also covers characteristic zero without a separate assumption.

Step 6 applies the existing finite-extension completion product node to R to B_L, which is finite by hypothesis. Its conclusion explicitly includes the algebra structures induced by the local maps, so it can be tensored with L. I wrote the denominator argument showing that localizing B_L at R minus zero gives L. Base change of localization and tensor cancellation are the exact existing nodes used for the scalar identifications. Tensoring the finite product with L commutes with its finite direct-sum underlying modules; the coordinate idempotents show that prime local rings and Noetherianity are componentwise. This is a routine calculation from that product isomorphism, not a hidden new formal-fibre theorem.

Step 7 uses regular ascent for a flat local map with regular closed fibre and existence of a minimal prime below a prescribed prime. The regular map C to its maximal completion supplies the required regular fibres. Thus a nonregular factor in step 6 yields an actual singular prime of C over zero. A minimal prime of its closed singular locus lying underneath it still contracts to zero. Step 8 uses finite height of Noetherian primes and Serre normality criterion, with the R_1 definition, to exclude heights zero and one. Least-element existence is ordinary well-ordering of a nonempty set of natural heights. It does not assume the global ring R has finite Krull dimension.

Step 9 now runs for every finite L/K, not just L=K. The dimension-formula node requires an inclusion of domains, a Noetherian universally catenary base and a finite-type algebra. These all hold for R inside B_L. Finiteness makes its fraction and residue extensions algebraic, giving equal heights. Completion flatness plus locality yields faithful flatness; surjectivity on spectra makes qC proper. Flat going down proves that a prime Q minimal over qC contracts exactly to q. The closed fibre of B_q to C_Q has dimension zero because Q is minimal, and the flat height formula has no finite-type hypothesis. Thus height Q equals height q and is at most h.

Step 10 uses exactly localization Noetherianity, completion Noetherianity, flatness of the induced completion map, locality of maximal completion and flat local implies faithfully flat. Its source A is B_q. The G-ring definition supplies regularity of C_Q to its maximal completion T. No excellence of B_q is used at this stage, so there is no circularity.

Step 11 uses finiteness of minimal primes over the singular ideal. A strict inclusion below Q gives a strict height inequality because these heights are finite. Minimality of h then forces each surviving singular component to meet B nontrivially; the component equal to Q contracts to the nonzero q. The explicit product of chosen nonzero elements is nonzero because B is a domain. For no surviving components the empty product is 1. Step 12 checks each prime of T avoiding that product: a singular source localization would contain one of those minimal components, contradicting avoidance of b.
