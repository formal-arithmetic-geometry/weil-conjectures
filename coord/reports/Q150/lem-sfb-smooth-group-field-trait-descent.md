## Proof material retained for the new nodes

These are future proof plans, not proofs inserted in unsplit node records.

For field ample-scheme datum effectivity, BLR p.160 first spreads the scheme and the finite-presentation datum to a finite-type subalgebra of the extension field, then uses a closed point to reduce to a finite extension. A common finite normal extension separates the radicial and Galois parts. A radicial cover makes all opens invariant, permitting affine descent and gluing; for the finite Galois part an orbit is a finite set, and ampleness puts it in an affine open. Intersect its finitely many translates, then descend the invariant affine opens and their overlaps. The line itself was not assumed invariant. General categorical full faithfulness or descent of a morphism property alone would not do this construction.

For the generic-effective dense-affine node, read BLR pp.161-162 together with p.160 case (a). Begin with a dense affine open of the descended generic model, and remove the closure of its complement from the trait scheme. The remaining scheme has affine generic fibre. Its affinization and the quasi-finite locus are compatible with the datum; Lemma 6 ensures that this locus has a point on a nonempty special fibre. The normal version of Zariski's Main Theorem makes that locus quasi-affine. It descends by quasi-affine effectivity. Remove the special-fibre components already met and repeat; there are finitely many. Glue the effective invariant pieces to obtain a fibrewise dense effective open. Its descended scheme is smooth separated finite type by fpqc locality. Apply the fibre-dense affine shrinking of BLR 6.4 Lemma 4 (already represented by lem-sfb-dedekind-smooth-fibre-dense-affine) to this descended scheme, and pull back. This last shrinking, rather than the union of quasi-affine pieces itself, is the invariant affine in the new statement. The generic open embedding is retained. No group action enters this lemma.

For the boundary node, read the actual paragraph beginning with an effective divisor on the descended generic model in BLR p.160. That model is smooth separated finite type by generic property descent, hence regular; the complement of its dense affine open is the support of an effective divisor. Its pullback and schematic closure give D'. Regularity of the smooth DVR scheme makes this horizontal effective Weil divisor Cartier. Fibrewise density excludes vertical boundary components, giving exactly the requested support. For each of the two flat C-to-C-tensor_B-C projections, flatness preserves the kernel ideal defining schematic closure: locally this is the kernel of A to (A[1/t])/I, preserved by flat tensor product. The two generic pulled-back ideals agree by the specified generic descent. Their closures therefore agree, even when C tensor_B C is non-Noetherian. This gives an actual identification of ideals, dual lines and cocycles, not invariance of a Picard class. The empty boundary uses the unit ideal.

For polarized effectivity, read BLR 6.1 Theorem 7 and its section-algebra proof, p.138. Descend the graded algebra of sections of positive powers with its multiplicative datum. Ampleness identifies the original scheme with its canonical open in the Proj of that algebra. The datum preserves that open, which descends to the Proj over B; the line descends on that scheme by module descent. Its ampleness is fpqc local. The cover is affine faithfully flat, with no finite-presentation assumption. This is more general in the base and scheme than the item, but requires the additional, essential compatible polarization; projectivity without a polarization does not replace it.

For the neutral-open node, read BLR p.154 immediately after Proposition 2 and its EGA IV3 15.6.5 reference. The union of fibre identity components is open for a smooth group with its identity section. Its geometric-fibre description gives base-change compatibility and closure under multiplication and inverse. On the Noetherian finite-type total space the open is quasi-compact, hence finite type. The geometric fibres are smooth connected groups, so their geometric connected components are precisely translates of the identity component. Existing field-component nodes cover a single field, not this relative openness statement.
