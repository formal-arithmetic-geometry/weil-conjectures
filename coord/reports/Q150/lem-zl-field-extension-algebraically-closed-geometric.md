### Task A: lem-zl-finite-separable-tensor-primitive-presentation

By [lem-weil-pbc-finite-separable-extension-primitive-element], choose $\alpha\in E$ with $E=k(\alpha)$. Let $P\in k[T]$ be its minimal polynomial. It is monic and irreducible by [def-weil-pbc-separable-algebraic-extension]. By [lem-weil-pbc-minimal-polynomial-quotient-field-iso], $d=\deg P\ge1$ and evaluation at $\alpha$ gives $k[T]/(P)\cong E$. Its hypotheses hold because $E/k$ is separable algebraic, hence $\alpha$ is algebraic.

Put $P_K\in K[T]$ for the polynomial with the same coefficients viewed in $K$. Under the presentation in Step 2, there is an isomorphism $K\otimes_kE\cong K[T]/(P_K)$: send $a\otimes g(\alpha)$ to the class of $a g(T)$. This is well-defined since the kernel of evaluation $k[T]\to E$ is $(P)$. Conversely send a coefficient $a\in K$ to $a\otimes1$ and $T$ to $1\otimes\alpha$; the relation $P_K=0$ holds. The tensor universal property [def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product] extends the first map from these balanced bilinear formulas. The two maps preserve multiplication and are inverse on coefficients, on $T$, and on the pure tensors that generate the tensor product. Thus they are inverse ring homomorphisms.

### Task B: lem-zl-relative-algebraic-closure-polynomial-quotient-field

Suppose $P_K=UV$ with both factors nonconstant. Replace $U$ by its leading-coefficient inverse times $U$ and $V$ by that leading coefficient times $V$. Both become monic since $P_K$ is monic. Each divides $P_K$, so [lem-zl-monic-divisor-algebraic-coefficients] makes every coefficient of both factors algebraic over $k$. The hypothesis on $K/k$ puts all these coefficients in $k$. Therefore $U$ and $V$ are coefficientwise images of monic nonconstant polynomials $U_0,V_0\in k[T]$. Injectivity of $k\to K$ gives $P=U_0V_0$, contradicting the irreducibility from Step 2. Hence $P_K$ is irreducible. This uses neither separability nor finite generation of $K/k$.

The ring $K[T]$ is a principal ideal domain by [lem-pur-polynomial-field-pid]. For $f\notin(P_K)$, write $(P_K,f)=(h)$. Since $h$ divides the irreducible $P_K$, either $h$ is a unit or $h$ is associated to $P_K$. The latter would put $f$ in $(P_K)$, so $h$ is a unit. Thus $1=AP_K+Bf$ for some $A,B\in K[T]$, and the class of $B$ is inverse to the class of $f$ in $K[T]/(P_K)$. This quotient is nonzero: $P_K$ has the positive degree $d$, so cannot divide $1$. It is therefore a field. By [def-weil-affine-spectrum] its spectrum has exactly one point, so is nonempty and irreducible. Step 3 now gives the required irreducibility for the arbitrary finite separable extension $E/k$; the criterion of Step 1 proves geometric irreducibility.
