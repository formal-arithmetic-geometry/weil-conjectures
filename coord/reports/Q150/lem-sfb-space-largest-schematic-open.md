Step 1 uses the statement of `lem-a1-space-etale-open-image`, specifically its inclusion-preserving bijection between open subspaces and topological opens. This both bounds the collection by a set (a subset of the power set of |X|) and supplies an open subspace for the union O. The topology's union operation is elementary; the correspondence is cited, not treated as a definition. No dense-open theorem, finite subcover or scheme representability of the union is assumed.

Step 2 uses `lem-a1-space-open-factor-scheme-tests`. Its statement really supplies the unique factorization through an open subspace when the point image is contained there, and the isomorphism Y x_J T = Y x_X T for every scheme T mapping to J. This avoids the invalid general claim that any morphism factors through any subspace containing its point image. The subspace here is open, as that node requires. `def-a1-space-open-subspace` supplies representability by an open subscheme on every scheme test. The inclusion Xi in X is sectionwise injective; its factorization is therefore also sectionwise injective. Thus the Xi are precisely the kind of open subfunctors required by 01JJ.

Step 3 uses `lem-a1-space-point-open-calculus`, whose statement gives the exact equality between the point image of a fibre product and the inverse image of the source image. Applied to Xi -> J and T -> J it identifies the underlying Ti with a^{-1}(|Xi|). Their union is all of T because |J| is the union constructed in step 1. The scheme Ti is already provided by step 2; no representability of an arbitrary space-to-scheme map is being inferred. Its projection to Xi gives the local section, which verifies the actual functor-cover definition `def-pc-cover`, not merely a field-point covering. Both node statements were read, including the fact that the definition permits arbitrary open covering families.

Step 4 uses `def-a1-space-open-subspace` for J being an algebraic space and `def-trf-algebraic-space` for its fppf sheaf condition. The existing Mathlib leaf `lem-pc-zariski-coverings-are-fppf` states the precise covering comparison. I checked the sheaf equalizer formulation in `def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology` and the unique-gluing formulation in `def-pc-zariski-sheaf-property`: they give the same matching-section statement on an open covering. This is an elementary restriction of the sheaf axiom to covering families already proved to be fppf, not an uncited topological comparison theorem.

It is explicitly for functors on schemes over S, and gives representability by an S-scheme from a Zariski sheaf, a set of representable open subfunctors and the functor-cover condition.
