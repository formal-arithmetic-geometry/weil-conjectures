## Review by Q99

I read the item, the release and boundary decision, the exact two cited node statements, the Stacks source and the current blueprint, with the node JSON open. The source places checked were Stacks algebra.tex Tag 00IB (lemma-valuation-ring-x-or-x-inverse), Definition 04GF (definition-henselian), Lemma 04GG (lemma-characterize-henselian), and more-algebra.tex Definition 09XE with its following local-ring remark. The cited local-root node states the comparison with finite-algebra idempotent lifting; it is not merely a definition of henselian rings. ILO XX 4.4, printed p.374, supplies the setting only, as reported above. No SGA source is used.

Step 1 uses exactly the either-an-element-or-its-inverse condition in def-trf-valuation-ring. I verified its elementary proof of locality: two nonunits have a common nonunit factor using the ratio comparison, multiples of nonunits remain nonunits, and every proper ideal consists of nonunits. Thus there is a unique maximal ideal for U as well as V; this is not imported from an uncited theorem.

Step 2 again uses that same valuation condition, now on x and each coefficient. The inclusion of maximal ideals is an inclusion of subsets of K, not an assumption that V to U is local (usually it is not). For a coefficient outside V its inverse lies in V and is a unit in U. A finite product of these inverses absorbs all coefficients simultaneously, without requiring the value group to be Archimedean. I checked the zero coefficient and empty-list cases explicitly.

Step 3 uses the statement of lem-weil-pbc-henselian-local-root-lifting in its reverse direction to specify what must be proved for U. It requires a local ring, which Step 1 supplies, and only monic input polynomials. A simple residue root ensures that the chosen derivative d is a unit of U, so dividing by d is legal and c_0 remains in the maximal ideal. If F(a)=0 there is nothing to divide by c_0 later; that branch is handled first. A constant monic polynomial has no residue root, so n is at least one in the remaining case.

Step 4 uses no additional theorem. I checked each coefficient: b_0 is in the maximal ideal of U because s is a unit there; Step 2 then puts it in the maximal ideal of V. Each b_i is in V because i is at least two. Expanding the explicit finite sum proves both Q's monicity and its displayed reduction. This is an elementary polynomial calculation, not a hidden comparison lemma. No claim that g is monic is made.

Step 5 uses exactly the forward direction of the same cited local-root equivalence, with R=V and monic polynomial Q. I checked the derivative of Y^(n-1)(Y+1) at -1: it is (-1)^(n-1), including characteristic two and n=1. The lifted y is a unit in V since its residue is -1, hence is a unit in U. Expanding Q(y) gives (y^n/b_0)g(b_0/y); the two denominators are nonzero. Since b_0 lies in the maximal ideal of U, dividing by the U-unit y leaves z in that ideal. These are computations from the already introduced objects, not facts taken from the proof of a dependency.

Step 6 uses the reverse direction of the cited local-root equivalence again. The explicit equality F(a+sz)=ds g(z) proves that this is a root, and sz is in the maximal ideal of U. This residue verification is essential: merely lifting a root in the maximal ideal of V would not have sufficed. The criterion then gives exactly henselianity as the item's definition states it. The only off-unit theorem is the generic local-ring criterion, declared in split_report.tool_uses for independent review; it replaces no affine-curve route step.
