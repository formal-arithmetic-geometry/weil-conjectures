## Source error

Two auxiliary claims in Morrison's printed argument cannot be used literally.

1. At the end of Theorem 5's geometric argument the proposed bound e_i <= (d/(N-1)-delta)i for positive delta fails at i=N-1. A one-dimensional nonzero subspace of H0(C,L) generates a line isomorphic to O_C, so its codegree is d. The proposed inequality would give d <= d-delta(N-1). The repair imposes strict inequalities only at 0<i<N-1 and keeps e_0=0, e_{N-1}=d.
2. Lemma 6 omits a factor of two. Take N=4 and weights (1/2,1/3,1/6,0). All points (i,w_i) are on a line. The sum in that lemma is 3/2 for every subsequence, whereas its printed upper bound is (N-1)/N=3/4. The correct bound for the sum (twice the area) is 2(N-1)/N. The proof's final displayed inequality also has the wrong direction for an upper-bound argument. The new polygon statement uses the corrected bound and strict interior codegrees; it does not repeat the false printed lemma.

These are errors in supporting source claims, not a counterexample to the item's high-degree stability assertion. The conclusion and hypotheses of the item are unchanged.

Step 2 takes the endpoint clauses and degree range from that same statement. The full space generates L because the assumed evaluation gives an embedding; a one-dimensional space generates a copy of O_C, not a line of positive moving degree. Subtracting degrees gives e_0=0, e_{N-1}=d, and 0<=e_i<=d. This is a few-line elementary deduction from the cited statement, and it repairs the source's endpoint mistake.

Step 3 again uses exactly the bound q<=max(a+1-g,a/2+1), with q=N-i. Since N=d+1-g, its two alternatives give e_i<=i or e_i<=d-2(N-1)+2i. I checked d>=2g+1 and g>=2 imply N>=4 and 1<alpha=d/(N-1)<2. In the second alternative subtracting alpha*i yields (2-alpha)(i-(N-1))<0 for interior i. Thus neither special nor nonspecial subseries is omitted, and no false strict inequality is asserted at an endpoint. This arithmetic is elementary from the cited inequality.

Step 4 uses exactly the new polygon statement, whose inputs are integer codegrees, the strict interior bounds, endpoint equalities, N>=3 and d>=1. All were proved in the preceding steps. Its conclusion supplies the same eta(N,d) for every integer tuple and every normalized real weight vector, so the uniformity over flags and curves is part of the cited statement. It is not inferred by exchanging quantifiers in a pointwise result.

I checked the polygon child's truth beyond the printed source. For a fixed weight vector take the lower convex envelope h of (i,w_i). It is decreasing, has the original endpoints, and sum h_i<=1. With a=d/(N-1), the cost along its vertex subsequence is at most 2a times the area under h. This area is sum h_i-w_0/2. Convexity gives sum h_i<=N*w_0/2, hence the cost is at most 2d/N. Equality throughout forces sum h_i=1 and h to be the straight endpoint chord, so all original weights are that chord. In this equality case insert any interior index into the endpoint subsequence: its cost drops by (a*i-e_i)*w_0>0. Thus the minimum is strictly less than 2d/N for every weight vector. The minimum of finitely many linear functions is continuous; the normalized weight simplex is compact; and only finitely many integer codegree tuples are allowed. Taking half the minimum positive gap over these compact sets gives eta(N,d)>0. This is a genuine finite optimization lemma, longer than an elementary arithmetic step, so I left it as its own statement-only node. It also explains why N>=3 is essential: for N=2 there is no interior index and e=(0,d), w=(1,0) gives equality 2d/N=d.

Step 5 uses the whole implication in the uniform Gieseker child: the curve, line and embedding hypotheses and a bound for every normalized flag yield a stable SL Hilbert point for every sufficiently large m, with a bound depending only on g,d,N,epsilon, in every characteristic. The item supplies a complete series, whereas the criterion even permits an embedding subseries. Step 4 supplies epsilon=eta(N,d), so the required uniform hypothesis, rather than stability for each individual one-parameter subgroup, is in force. The criterion also specifies the Hilbert polynomial dm+1-g and the determinant linearization. The arithmetic strengthening is explicitly attributed as an agent expansion rather than a verbatim assertion about C from Morrison's exposition.

Step 6 uses the existing lem-sfb-hilbert-plucker-pgl-linearization, whose full statement I opened. It states the field-independent threshold for the Hilbert embedding and equality of SL and PGL stable loci on the indicated tensor power, even when mu_N is nonreduced. The step uses precisely those clauses. I did not infer PGL linearization of Lambda_m itself or assume the characteristic is prime to N. Taking the maximum of the two thresholds preserves dependence only on g,d.

For small cases I checked g=2,d=5,N=4: alpha=5/3 and the two interior codegrees have integer upper bounds e_1<=1,e_2<=3, with e_3=5, so the polygon statement applies. The one-dimensional subseries test exposes the source's false endpoint gap. The explicit N=4 linear weight vector above exposes its factor-two error. For the geometric child U spanned by one nonzero section has A_U=O_C and satisfies 1<=max(1-g,1); on a hyperelliptic genus-two curve its degree-two pencil realizes Clifford equality, so the maximum inequality must be non-strict. Characteristic two and characteristic dividing N are not excluded anywhere. The generated-image construction and Clifford inequality are characteristic independent, and the uniformity issue is retained as an actual mathematical obligation in the unsplit criterion.
