## Audit and repairs

The item agrees with ILO VIII Theorem 1.1, the algorithms in 4.1.1--4.2.13, the rigidified construction in 5.3.8--5.3.11, and the synchronization/descent argument in 5.4--5.5. The first two stages are saturated log blowups; the third uses permissible ordinary blowups. The fourth source need only be log regular, not regular; the draft incorrectly called it regular after Kummer pullback, and that assertion was removed. The item's hypotheses and conclusions are unchanged; only its projective-bundle definition citation was made explicit.

The interrupted draft had several C4 gaps. The excellent quasi-projective resolution node cannot be applied to a general quasi-excellent X or its quotient. The general log-regular resolution theorem is now used instead. The existing relative fixed-locus log-chart lemma assumes a log-smooth morphism to a log regular base; that hypothesis is absent here. The absolute fixed-family transversality statement is now exposed directly from VIII 4.2.3. The existing strict-resolution node only gives a proper boundary-containing resolution under excellence; it gives neither this equivariant algorithm nor its exact boundary. It is no longer used. The final refinement cannot use the excellent-affine-base normalized-blowup node because B is merely Noetherian: the proof now uses an ample twist on the immersion's ambient open, restriction, conversion to H-projectivity, and Segre.

The draft also conflated the common G-equivariant minimal-family tower with a blowup along one subgroup's commutator fixed scheme. Step 4 now explains the factorization through that blowup after its ideal becomes invertible. The complete-local comparison is applied with a finite set S detecting every center, and then S together with each test point, so equality before contraction is proved rather than assumed. This is the synchronization argument of VIII 5.5.3. All proof steps are below 2000 characters, including the explicit boundary chart calculation.

## Review by Q91

I read the item's source in the locally extracted ILO text, printed pages 85--86, 107--112, 117--130, and the fixed-locus calculation in VI pages 76--79. The four-stage assertion is not taken as a black box from another restatement of the item: the item itself constructs the successive towers, proves that the common third-stage tower decreases inertia, builds the fourth stage on rigidified affine charts, and proves synchronization before descending the centers. The unit sk-p-dl-a1-perverse is not this node's key statement. This node is a geometric tool inside the ancestor lem-per-artin-gabber-affine-curve proof Step 3, through the regular-pair-purity and XVI 3.7 alteration route; no unit realization fields are changed and no SGA source is cited.

For the new resolution tool I read VIII 3.3.15, 3.4.5--3.4.9 and 5.3.11: saturated blowups preserve log regularity, underlie normalized blowups, and have finite saturation even without excellence. It applies componentwise, which matters for the affine cover and quotient. For the weak-family tool I read 4.2.6--4.2.11, checking the distinction between a weakly snc family and a single common coordinate arrangement. For fixed-family transversality I read VI 4.2 and VIII 4.2.3: invariants on the tangent space, regularity of fixed schemes and their intersections, and G-strictness of the boundary are all needed. The commutator tool is VI Proposition 4.8, not merely the setup in 4.7; its proof rules out an H-stable line in the normal representation because [H,H] has no invariants there. The extra factorization is exactly VIII 2.2.4 and 4.2.13.

For the affine rigidification tool I read 5.3.2, 5.3.5 and 5.3.7: quasi-excellence opens the snc locus and the very tame locus; abelian inertia and invertibility permit eigenparameter averaging after adjoining roots of unity. For the Kummer tool I read VI 1.8, 2.1--2.3, 3.1--3.2, VIII 5.3.8 and IX 2.1; its boundary deletion requires etaleness off the original boundary, not just off the enlarged one. For the Cartier tool I read the middle paragraph of 5.3.8: an inertia-eigenvector boundary equation b has invariant power b^N, so the image is locally a Cartier support, and its pullback on the regular resolution is a divisor supported on the enlarged normal crossings divisor. For tower comparison I read Proposition 5.4.2 and Corollary 5.4.4, including their center/order and contraction clauses. For complete-local independence I read the whole 5.5.2 proof, including the Cohen-ring lift and its finite-disjoint-union paragraph. For descent I read VIII 2.2.9--2.2.11, 3.4.8 and 5.5.3--5.5.5, and Stacks 035S (morphisms.tex, lemma-nagata-normalization), 02NS (divisors.tex, lemma-blowing-up-projective) and 0C4P (morphisms.tex, lemma-composition-projective). The Noetherian base is qcqs, as composition requires. Quasi-excellence ensures finite normalizations for general normalized towers; the specialized log towers have finiteness already by saturation.

Step 1 uses exactly the resolution theorem's regular integral source, normalized tower, strict functoriality, and regular-locus identity. The existing divisorial-structure and free-characteristic statements supply density, normality, the canonical log structure and normal crossings. Step 2 uses the existing coordinate-branch-drop lemma's actual displayed chart equations and its etale-base-change clause, not the proof of the strict-resolution node. The weak-family and permissible-boundary statements supply preservation. The depth induction and G-strictness argument are written in the step, including the last Cartier blowup, whose strict transform is empty even though its scheme map is an identity.

Step 3 uses the new fixed-family statement for exactly its regular/intersection/transversality hypotheses and the weak-family statement for finite permissible towers and invertible total-transform ideals. Step 4 uses the commutator lemma only after the preceding common tower has made its fixed ideal invertible. Proper subgroup containment gives uniform termination because no point can pass through more than |G| strictly decreasing nonabelian inertia orders. No center supported on an isolated non-invariant subgroup fixed scheme is silently claimed to be G-equivariant.

Step 5 uses the cover's surjectivity, affineness, strictness, inertness and very tame enlarged boundary. The existing very-tame log-quotient statement really supplies existence, finiteness, invariant log structure, log regularity and Kummer etaleness, whereas the weaker quotient-log-regular node alone did not supply all these. Step 6 checks these hypotheses before applying quotient resolution. The Cartier tool supplies the original boundary's divisorial support after resolution. The Kummer tool gives fs base-change stability and the deletion criterion, and its trivial-inertia clause gives etaleness on the free open. Flat-under-regular gives regularity of that quotient open; hence the resolution is the identity there. The source after pullback is claimed only log regular. The rigidified-comparison statement fixes the underlying ordered normalized tower used later.

Step 7 uses the actual statements for strict-henselization Noetherianity and regularity, completion regularity, and both faithful-flatness maps. The complete-local lemma applies to the finite disjoint unions, not just individual local rings; the finite detecting set S is essential to synchronization. The descent node says equality of synchronized center ideals can be checked faithfully flat, so this step does not use the unproved global independence theorem as a hidden input. Step 8 uses the rigidified comparison for both projections and the triple overlap, and descends the ordered tower by the descent statement. Strict inertness identifies both inertia groups and local log strata, so very tameness is retained.

Step 9 takes projectivity, finite type, integrality and unchanged dense opens from the normalized-tower statement. Boundary equality is tracked at each stage, not inferred from mere support containment. Step 10 uses the existing Proj-open ample-twist statement, closed-immersion ample restriction, projective-bundle-to-H-projective statement, and the actual Segre closed immersion. I read Stacks 087S for the conversion. There is no appeal to an excellent ring on B. Each citation in these steps has been opened and its full relevant statement is quoted verbatim in citations.json.

Small-case checks: for G=1 and an empty boundary all towers can have length zero. For one smooth boundary component the last boundary blowup has identity scheme map and empty strict transform; this does not create an extra boundary component. The weak-family statement allows an empty family and excludes X itself as a center. Two crossing axes in A^2 test the minimal-intersection strict-transform and ideal-factorization clauses; three lines through the origin show why a weakly snc family must not be assumed simultaneously coordinate. The dihedral plane action of VI 4.6 tests the commutator inertia drop; abelian inertia is a termination case. A scalar tame action on A^2 shows the image of an invariant boundary on the quotient may be only Q-Cartier, so replacing the Cartier tool by a false Cartier assertion would fail. An auxiliary boundary with unramified cover tests deletion, while a ramified auxiliary component is excluded by the etaleness assumption. Disconnected complete local input tests synchronization, and unit-center contractions are explicitly retained until that comparison. No new node is a full modification theorem or a renamed clause such as “the fourth stage glues”: the global construction and globalization argument remain in the item.
