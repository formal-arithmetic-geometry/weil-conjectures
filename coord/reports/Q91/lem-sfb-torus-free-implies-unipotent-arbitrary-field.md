## Findings and corrections

The imported statement agrees with Milne Proposition 17.81 and Theorem 14.5 and needs no change. The original item had no proof. Milne's concise proof says to apply induction to the semisimple centralizer, without spelling out its connected identity component. The new centralizer input states the smaller group as the smooth connected closed subgroup C°; it retains the nonzero semisimple tangent vector, so is nonunipotent. This avoids silently assuming the entire centralizer connected.

The equal-dimensional isogeny step must not itself invoke dimension induction. Step 1 transfers the absence of tori to G' by the lifting clause of Proposition 17.80; steps 2 and 3 then treat its two alternatives directly. Induction is used only on K of strictly smaller dimension. The base case is included: dimension-zero groups cannot have a nonempty negative-dimensional subgroup, and the nilpotent case does not use induction.

The finite-field case does not follow from density of rational points. It uses Proposition 17.99, in the finite-field section 17.k, to obtain a torus that is geometrically maximal. Triviality over k then implies there are no geometric tori, enabling the existing algebraically closed theorem. A final application of the existing representation comparison and geometric-to-unitriangular embedding returns the conclusion to k. We do not assert that an arbitrary geometric torus descends or apply the arbitrary-field maximal-torus base-extension theorem, which is the parent and would be circular.

I corrected two source-place details while reading: the height-one quotient input is Corollary 11.38 (not a theorem), printed p.223; the represented quotient inputs 5.25–5.29 are on printed p.105 (not p.110).
