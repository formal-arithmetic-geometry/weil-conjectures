## Proof route and sources

I read the fixed sec-trace-formula chapter and sk-i-curve-zero unit. This node remains the uniqueness input to the Temkin construction used in the nearby-cycle semistable-alteration branch of lem-trf-local-term-locality. It changes no unit step or route. The current item's ancestry is retained, with the item prepended to the new nodes' via chains; blueprint search found no node stating any of these four facts.

The proof follows the chain in the proof of Theorem 1.2 on printed page 35: approximate the whole diagram, obtain eta-normality from Proposition 3.5.2, compare over valuation bases, and use Proposition 5.2 to obtain global domination. Apply domination in both directions and prove the composites equal the identities. I wrote out the marking-ideal and open-gluing arguments so that the conclusion is an isomorphism of pairs, including the stated generic identification. I did not replace the item by a single new global minimality theorem.

For the diagram node I read §3.1, Remark 3.1.1(ii), Lemma 5.1(ii), the first paragraph of the proof of Theorem 1.2, and the normal approximation discussion at the start of Proposition 3.5.2. The node concerns an arbitrary finite list of semistable modifications and a specified stable subset. It asserts existence of models and preservation of their conditions, not comparison morphisms. Over a normal affine domain, one can normalize an integral finite-type affine model inside its own function field: normality of the given domain makes its map factor through that normalization. The finite-type normalization is finite, so the new model is still affine of finite type over Z. The diagram can then be pulled back to it. Smoothness of the generic curve and etaleness of the generic marking survive this dominant refinement. A generic isomorphism between these finite-presentation diagrams spreads over a dense open of the model base; I included that open in the statement because it is needed to check schematic density after a nonflat valuation base change. Reduced total spaces are also stated: flatness over the integral base embeds affine sections into the smooth or etale generic fibre, which has no nilpotents.

For the eta-normality node I read Proposition 3.5.2 on printed pages 20–21 and the meaning of eta-normal before §3.1. The total space is only partially normal relative to its generic fibre: every finite eta-modification is trivial. No normality of nodal fibres is claimed. The statement allows arbitrary qcqs normal integral bases, as the source does after its noetherian reduction. Semistable curves have reduced geometric fibres, and etale markings have reduced geometric fibres, so both are legitimate instances. A nodal special fibre is a useful check: it may fail to be normal, while the relative eta-normality assertion still makes sense.

For the valuation node I read the standing hypotheses at the beginning of §4, Corollary 4.4.3(i), and the final paragraph of Proposition 4.5.1 on printed page 33. The source first obtains a common semistable dominating model and then compares contractions, giving domination of the stable modification by a semistable one. The newly stated version permits arbitrary K. To derive it from the displayed separably closed case, extend the valuation to a separable closure L of K and let W be the resulting valuation ring. The valuation ring W is faithfully flat over V (torsion-free over the valuation domain and local), and its height is still finite under the algebraic extension. Over W use Proposition 4.5.1. The two pullbacks of the comparison to W tensor_V W agree: they agree generically with the prescribed rational comparison, their target is separated over C, and the source is V-flat, so the generic localization is schematically dense. Faithfully flat descent of scheme morphisms then supplies the morphism over V, with the same marking compatibility. These are tasks for splitting this new lemma, not assumptions smuggled into the item's steps. No global uniqueness theorem or item descendant is used to justify that descent.

For the domination node I read Proposition 5.2 and its proof on printed pages 34–35. This is a theorem about arbitrary reduced flat finite-type schemes, not stable curves. It tests factorization after base change to centred valuation rings of the base function field. I specialized its base to finite type over Z, so that those valuation rings have finite height by the Abhyankar-bound argument in §2.1.2, exactly as used in the next paragraph of the source. The K-valued criterion does not assume a separably closed function field. Its conclusion is one factorization; invertibility and the marked-pair conclusion remain in the item's proof. Its eta-normal source assumption is essential to the Stein-factorization descent in the source proof, and is checked separately in the item.
