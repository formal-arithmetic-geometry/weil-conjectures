## Preserved argument from the returned head

The following is the exact proof text recovered before editing, with the assignment of work stated above; it is retained as guidance for the two statement-only children, not as proof steps in their JSON files.

Step 1: Fix $z\in Z$. Choose an affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}R\subseteq B$ containing $h(z)$. By the locally-of-finite-type hypothesis [def-weil-finite-type-morphism], $h^{-1}(V)$ has an affine open cover by schemes $\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $A$ a finitely generated $R$-algebra; let $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be a member of this cover containing $z$, so $h(U)\subseteq V$ and $R\to A$ is finitely generated. For every prime $\mathfrak p\subset R$, let $s\in V\subseteq B$ be the corresponding point; since $V$ is open, $\kappa(s)=\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ and $\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)\to B$ factors through $V$, so the fibre [def-pc-scheme-fibre] $U_s=U\times_B\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)=U\times_V\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(\mathfrak p)=\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p))$ over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. It is an open subscheme of $Z_s$ (the base change of the open immersion $U\hookrightarrow Z$, i.e. the preimage of $U$ under the projection $Z_s\to Z$). By the hypothesis and the existing field-fibre decomposition criterion [lem-trf-unramified-field-fibre-decomposition], $Z_s\to\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)$ is unramified; its restriction to the open $U_s$ is unramified by [lem-weil-pbc-unramified-base-change-open], clause (2) with $V=\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(s)$. Thus for every prime $\mathfrak p\subset R$ the fibre morphism $\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p))\to\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is unramified.

Uses: def-weil-finite-type-morphism, def-pc-scheme-fibre, lem-trf-unramified-field-fibre-decomposition, lem-weil-pbc-unramified-base-change-open

Step 2: For each $\mathfrak p$, apply the affine criterion [lem-weil-pbc-unramified-ring-map-iff-unramified-affine] to the unramified fibre $\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p))\to\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ from step 1. It gives $\Omega_{(A\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p))/\kappa(\mathfrak p)}=0$. Kähler differentials commute with this base change by [lem-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials-base-change], so after identifying the fibre module with $(A\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p))\otimes_A\Omega_{A/R}$, we obtain $\Omega_{A/R}\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak q)=0$ for every prime $\mathfrak q\subset A$ over $\mathfrak p$. Because $A$ is finitely generated over $R$, the module $\Omega_{A/R}$ is generated by the differentials of a finite set of algebra generators (using the definition [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials]), hence is finite. Localize at $\mathfrak q$ and apply Nakayama [lem-weil-pbc-nakayama-local-generators] to the finite module $(\Omega_{A/R})_{\mathfrak q}$: its quotient by the maximal ideal is zero, so the localization is zero. Since this holds at every prime, $\Omega_{A/R}=0$. The affine criterion therefore gives that $\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}R$, and hence $h|_U$, is unramified.

Step 3: The affine opens $U$ chosen in step 1 cover $Z$, and every restriction $h|_U$ is unramified by step 2. The affine-cover characterization in the definition of an unramified morphism [def-weil-unramified-morphism] (locally finite type together with finite-type affine charts having vanishing relative differentials, equivalently an open-immersion diagonal) therefore gives that $h$ itself is unramified.

Uses: def-weil-unramified-morphism

The closest pairs deserve explicit comparison: the parent is the final geometric criterion from a separable-spectrum decomposition, whereas neither child concludes unramifiedness of the original h. The field-fibre decomposition is an equivalence for a scheme over one field, not a compatible relative affine-cover result. The open-fibre-product and affine-fibre-product nodes give only one geometric comparison apiece, not the finite-generation cover, its unramified restrictions and their differential vanishing. The affine unramified criterion converts an already chosen affine morphism into a finite-generation/differential condition, not all residue-field fibres into global differential vanishing. Differential base change alone supplies an isomorphism, not finiteness and Nakayama; local Nakayama assumes a local ring and says nothing about the R-algebra fibres or global detection. The local maximal-ideal/residue criteria require stalk conditions; they do not give either new interface. Nodes about cotangent dimensions at rational points, smooth morphisms or nodal curves have extra geometric conditions and different conclusions. Raw tag matches in simplicial homotopy nodes use the same alphanumeric text in an unrelated context and cannot duplicate commutative algebra. All these existing tools are reused as guidance, rather than recreated as single-result nodes.
