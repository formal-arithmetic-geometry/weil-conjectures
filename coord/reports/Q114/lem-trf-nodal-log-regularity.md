### Original step 1

Apply [lem-trf-nodal-etale-chart] at each geometric point. Its hypotheses are exactly flatness, local finite presentation, the stated nodal geometric fibres, smoothness over $Y\setminus T$, and the etale Cartier marking in the smooth locus; projectivity is not required. It suffices to verify the divisorial log-regular conditions on these etale neighbourhoods, since [def-trf-log-regular-pair] imposes its chart and strict-local conditions at geometric points.

Cited nodes: lem-trf-nodal-etale-chart, def-trf-log-regular-pair.

### Original step 2

On a smooth unmarked chart take the associated log structure of the pulled-back base chart $P$. The comparison morphism for $P\to P$ is the given smooth morphism and the group map is the identity. On a marked chart take the associated log structure of $P\oplus\mathbf N\to\mathcal O_X$, $(p,n)\mapsto\alpha(p)z^n$. This monoid is fs: finite generation and cancellation are componentwise, and saturation follows componentwise in $P^{\rm gp}\oplus\mathbf Z$. The group map is injective with cokernel $\mathbf Z$; the comparison is the etale coordinate map to $\mathbf A^1_Y$ supplied by [lem-trf-nodal-etale-chart]. The associated structures and their morphisms to the base are those of [def-trf-fs-log-blowup]. Their triviality locus is exactly $X\setminus B$: a base nonunit stays a nonunit under a local ring map, and the additional generator is a unit precisely off $z=0$. Apply [lem-trf-log-smooth-log-regular] to these fs charts. It proves log regularity and canonically identifies the constructed structure with the divisorial structure of this complement.

Cited nodes: lem-trf-nodal-etale-chart, def-trf-fs-log-blowup, lem-trf-log-smooth-log-regular.

### Original step 3

On a nodal chart write $h=\varepsilon\alpha(a)$ as in step 1 and put $R=\mathbf Z\oplus P$, charted on the base by $(n,p)\mapsto\varepsilon^n\alpha(p)$. Adding the group generator mapping to a unit changes no associated base log structure, by the chart pushout in [def-trf-fs-log-blowup]. By [lem-trf-nodal-divisorial-chart], $Q=\mathbf N^2\amalg_{\mathbf N}R$ is fs, $R^{\rm gp}\to Q^{\rm gp}$ is injective with cokernel $\mathbf Z$, and its monoid-algebra base change is exactly $uv=\varepsilon\alpha(a)$. Define the log structure on the etale nodal neighbourhood by this $Q$-chart: the two generators map to $u,v$, while $R$ maps through the base. In particular both branch generators occur, even though $D$ is absent. The comparison to the monoid-algebra base change is exactly the etale nodal map from [lem-trf-nodal-etale-chart].

Cited nodes: lem-trf-nodal-etale-chart, def-trf-fs-log-blowup, lem-trf-nodal-divisorial-chart.

### Original step 4

For the chart constructed in step 3, apply [lem-trf-log-smooth-log-regular]: the base is log regular, both log structures are fs, the group kernel is zero, its cokernel is the torsion-free group $\mathbf Z$, and the comparison is etale. Thus the constructed source is log regular. Its triviality locus is exactly the inverse image of $Y\setminus T$: over that open all base monomials and $h$ are units, and $uv=h$ forces both $u$ and $v$ to be units. Conversely, over $T$ some base chart element maps to a nonunit in the base strict local ring and therefore in the source strict local ring, so the associated log structure is nontrivial by [def-trf-fs-log-blowup]. Since $D$ is absent here, this locus is precisely $X\setminus B$. The same preservation lemma now identifies the $Q$-log structure canonically with the divisorial structure of this complement, and gives its dense open complement.

Cited nodes: lem-trf-log-smooth-log-regular, def-trf-fs-log-blowup.

### Original step 5

The local divisorial structures in steps 2 and 4 are restrictions of the single sheaf $\mathcal O_X\cap j_*\mathcal O_{X\setminus B}^{\times}$: on an etale test scheme each consists of regular functions invertible on the same open complement. Hence their fs charts and their log-regular strict-local conditions give those of [def-trf-log-regular-pair] at every geometric point of $X$. The complement is dense globally: a nonempty open of $X$ has nonempty inverse image in some chart, and that inverse image meets the dense triviality locus supplied by [lem-trf-log-smooth-log-regular]; its image lies in the original open outside $B$. As $B$ is closed, it is nowhere dense. The pair $(X,B)$ is therefore log regular as claimed.

Cited nodes: def-trf-log-regular-pair, lem-trf-log-smooth-log-regular.
