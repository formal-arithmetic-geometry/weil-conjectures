### Original step 1

Work with the small site representatives in the coefficient universe. By [lem-weil-proetale-weakly-etale-localization](1), the sites over the open $U$ are the slice sites at $j$ and $i$. By [lem-sfb-internal-module-operations-transport], internal module objects, their pullbacks, direct images and specified adjunctions identify with sectionwise modules over the reconstructed coefficient ring sheaves, including the units and counits. This exact equivalence preserves K-injectivity: it identifies the Hom groups from acyclic complexes, which define K-injectivity. We may therefore calculate with ringed-site modules and carry back the resulting morphisms. By [sk-prop-sfb-cartesian-coefficients](1), each coefficient comparison is an isomorphism and every module pullback in the diagram is exact and additive. Thus its derived functor is the termwise functor. The Cartesian identification gives $D'\times_{Z'}U'=h^{-1}(U)\times_UU'=U'$, with $h'|_{U'}=\mathrm{id}_{U'}$. All the following constructions apply to the primed and unprimed triples.

Uses: lem-weil-proetale-weakly-etale-localization, lem-sfb-internal-module-operations-transport, sk-prop-sfb-cartesian-coefficients.
Source: literature/stacks-project/sites-modules.tex — lemma-localize-morphism-ringed-sites; module-object transport and Cartesian coefficients as in the cited blueprint nodes

### Original step 2

At the sheaf level write $c_h:j^*h_*\xrightarrow{\sim}i^*$ for the sectionwise identification. Indeed, for every pro-etale $T\to U$, $T\times_ZD=T$ by the specified identification $h^{-1}(U)=U$, so both sides evaluate to $M(T\to U\xrightarrow{i}D)$ by [def-sfb-finite-lower-star] and [def-pur-localized-ringed-site]. Their restriction maps coincide. Their module actions coincide as well: [def-sfb-module-lower-star] uses the structural ring map, whose restriction along the identity $U\to U$ is the identity. Let $a_h:i^*h^*\xrightarrow{\sim}j^*$ be the pullback-composition comparison for $h i=j$ from [def-sfb-module-upper-star-comp], followed by the specified equality. On the localized sites this is the usual identity-pullback identification, with tensor unit $1\otimes m\mapsto m$: its right-adjoint comparison is the sectionwise composition identification of [def-sfb-module-lower-star-comp], so the defining conjugacy gives this normalization. Define $c_{h'}$ and $a_{h'}$ in exactly these ways for $h'i'=j'$.

Uses: def-sfb-finite-lower-star, def-pur-localized-ringed-site, def-sfb-module-lower-star, def-sfb-module-upper-star-comp, def-sfb-module-lower-star-comp.

### Original step 3

These underived comparisons respect adjuncts. In fact, for a section $s\in M(T\to U\xrightarrow{j}Z)$ the unit of the canonical module adjunction sends $s$ to the universal pullback section $1\otimes s$. On the localized identity morphism, $a_{h,M}$ sends that section back to $s$. Consequently $c_{h,h^*M}\,j^*(\eta_{h,M})=a_{h,M}^{-1}$ on every site object, hence as module-sheaf maps. This uses the concrete tensor/adjunction bijection in [def-sfb-module-upper-lower-adjunction] and its preservation by [lem-sfb-internal-module-operations-transport]. For any $t:h^*M\to N$, write $t^\sharp=h_*t\circ\eta_{h,M}$. Naturality of $c_h$ gives
$$c_{h,N}\,j^*(t^\sharp)=i^*(t)\,c_{h,h^*M}\,j^*(\eta_{h,M})=i^*(t)\,a_{h,M}^{-1}.$$
This is precisely the adjunct compatibility with the identity adjunction on $U$. The same section calculation with $T\to U'$ proves the primed compatibility.

Uses: def-sfb-module-upper-lower-adjunction, lem-sfb-internal-module-operations-transport.
Source: literature/stacks-project/sites-modules.tex — lemma-localize-morphism-ringed-sites; calculation of the localized module-adjunction unit

### Original step 4

Choose a K-injective resolution $K\to I$ in the sectionwise module category using [prop-pur-kinjective-resolutions-exist], and use the exact equivalence of step 1 for internal modules. By [sk-def-sfb-module-derived-pushpull], $h_*I$ represents $Rh_*K$. The complex $i^*I$ is K-injective on the slice by [lem-pur-kinjective-restrict-localized]; $K\to I$ restricts to a quasi-isomorphism because restriction is exact. The degreewise section identity $c_{h,I}:j^*h_*I\cong i^*I$ therefore yields
$$r_{h,K}:j^*Rh_*K\xrightarrow{\sim}i^*K.$$
This is exactly the K-injective normalization in the statement. On complexes the maps $c_{h,I}$ are natural, and the canonical resolution comparisons are natural by the right-derived construction [def-sfb-right-derived-functor], so $r_h$ is a natural isomorphism. Repeating this argument for $h'$ gives $r_{h'}$; there is no assumption that $w^*I$ is K-injective.

Uses: prop-pur-kinjective-resolutions-exist, sk-def-sfb-module-derived-pushpull, lem-pur-kinjective-restrict-localized, def-sfb-right-derived-functor.
Source: literature/stacks-project/sites-cohomology.tex — Tag 0D6G, lemma-restrict-direct-image-open, proof; Tag 08FI, lemma-restrict-K-injective-to-open

### Original step 5

Check the right-derived-functor hypothesis needed to transport adjuncts. The underived functor $j^*h_*$ is naturally isomorphic via $c_h$ to the exact functor $i^*$. Its termwise derived factorization is consequently its total right derived functor by [lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-derived-functor](1). The natural isomorphism $r_h$ of step 4 identifies that factorization with $j^*Rh_*$. It also identifies their comparison units: if $P\to I$ is a resolution, naturality of $c_h$ says
$$c_{h,I}\circ j^*h_*(P\to I)=i^*(P\to I)\circ c_{h,P}.$$
After localization, the right-hand resolution map is invertible and is exactly the one used to identify $i^*I$ with $i^*P$. Thus $r_{h,QP}\,j^*(\gamma_{h,P})=Q(c_{h,P})$, with $\gamma$ the right-derived comparison unit of [def-sfb-right-derived-functor]. Hence $j^*Rh_*$, with its localized comparison unit, is a right derived functor of $j^*h_*$. The identical argument proves this assertion for $h'$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-derived-functor, def-sfb-right-derived-functor.
Source: literature/stacks-project/sites-cohomology.tex — lemma-restrict-direct-image-open, proof; exact-functor derived universal property applied to the sectionwise identification

### Original step 6

Apply [lem-kun-derived-adjoint-square-comparison] with
$$(L,R,V,W)=(h^*,h_*,i^*,j^*),\qquad L_0=R_0=\mathrm{id}_{\Mod_{\Lambda_U}},\quad (a,c)=(a_h,c_h).$$
The identity adjunction has identity unit and counit [def-weil-pbc-composite-adjunction]. All four functors required to be exact are exact by step 1; the underived adjunct condition was verified in step 3, the total derived adjunction exists by [sk-def-sfb-module-derived-pushpull], and the extra right-derived hypothesis and normalization $b=r_h$ were verified in step 5. Its two formulas consequently give, now on the derived categories,
$$r_{h,h^*A}\circ j^*(\eta_{h,A})=a_{h,A}^{-1},\qquad i^*(\varepsilon_{h,K})=r_{h,K}\circ a_{h,Rh_*K}.$$
Apply the same theorem to $(h'^*,h'_*,i'^*,j'^*)$ to obtain these formulas with primes. Here and below $a_h$ also denotes its termwise derived comparison, as required by the theorem; no other derived adjunction or arbitrary isomorphism of functors is substituted.

### Original step 7

Put $s:j'^*v^*\xrightarrow{\sim}v_U^*j^*$ and $t:i'^*w^*\xrightarrow{\sim}v_U^*i^*$. The comparison $\theta$ and these two comparisons all use [def-sfb-module-upper-star-comp]. The composite Hom bijection is the composite of the two Hom bijections [def-weil-pbc-composite-adjunction]. Their associativity follows directly from that definition and the comparison normalization: the conjugate of either reassociation is the same sectionwise composition map of pushforwards in [def-sfb-module-lower-star-comp], and conjugation is injective and respects composition [def-weil-pbc-adjunction-conjugate]. Inserting the identity localized map $h|_U=h'|_{U'}=\mathrm{id}$, the two reassociations of pullback along the equality $hwi'=vj'=jv_U$ therefore give
$$t_{h^*A}\circ i'^*(\theta_A)\circ a_{h',v^*A}^{-1}
 =v_U^*(a_{h,A}^{-1})\circ s_A.$$
To check the normalization, before deriving, both right-adjoint composites evaluate a sheaf on the very same iterated fibre product; the associativity map sends a tuple of projections to that tuple. Their composites to the final fibre product agree by its universal property, so the section maps agree, not merely up to an unspecified automorphism. This proves the displayed comparison identity on sheaves, and all pullbacks are exact, so the same equality holds termwise on complexes and on their derived categories.

Uses: def-sfb-module-upper-star-comp, def-sfb-module-lower-star-comp, def-weil-pbc-adjunction-conjugate, def-weil-pbc-composite-adjunction.
Source: literature/stacks-project/sites-cohomology.tex — remark-base-change, its canonical pullback-composition identification; elementary reassociation with the specified conjugate normalization

### Original step 8

Set $A=v^*Rh_*K$. Expand the mate by [def-weil-pbc-adjunction-mate] as the specified unit/square/counit composite. Naturality of $r_{h'}$, first at $w^*\varepsilon_{h,K}$ and then at $\theta_{Rh_*K}$, followed by the primed unit equation of step 6, gives
$$r_{h',w^*K}\circ j'^*B_{h,K}
 =i'^*(w^*\varepsilon_{h,K})\circ i'^*(\theta_{Rh_*K})\circ a_{h',A}^{-1}.$$
Now apply $t_K$ and use naturality of $t$, the comparison identity of step 7, and the unprimed counit equation of step 6, in that order:
$$\begin{aligned}
t_K r_{h',w^*K}j'^*B_{h,K}
 &=v_U^*(i^*\varepsilon_{h,K})\,v_U^*(a_{h,Rh_*K}^{-1})\,s_{Rh_*K}\\
 &=v_U^*(r_{h,K})\,s_{Rh_*K}.
\end{aligned}$$
The right side is exactly the statement's source identification $j'^*v^*Rh_*K\cong v_U^*i^*K$; $t_Kr_{h',w^*K}$ is exactly its target identification. Conjugating the displayed equality by the inverse of the source identification proves that $j'^*B_{h,K}$ is the identity of $v_U^*i^*K$. Every comparison and every equation used is natural in $K$, so this is equality of natural derived morphisms for arbitrary unbounded $K$.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-adjunction-mate.
Source: literature/stacks-project/sites-cohomology.tex — remark-base-change, explicit restriction of the unit/square/counit construction to the identity vertical maps
