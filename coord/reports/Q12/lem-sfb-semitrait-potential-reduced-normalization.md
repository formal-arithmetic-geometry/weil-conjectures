I read Anantharaman Appendix II, Theorem 1 and Corollary 2 on printed p.73. Theorem 1 has a Dedekind base, flat locally finite-type source and geometrically normal generic fibre, with normalization after the algebraic closure finite over the source and flat, separable and locally finitely presented over the new base. In the affine case the finite source is quasi-compact, so this is the finitely presented B-algebra of the new node. Here separable means geometrically reduced fibres, not separability of the finite extension chosen later. The affine integral closure inside the generic normal algebra is the normalization: flatness embeds the chart into its generic fibre and normality of the generic fibre makes integral elements of its total fraction ring lie in that generic algebra. This is why the new node uses actual integral closure data and includes the canonical generic identification. No excellence, completeness, separability of an eventual finite extension, or finite B/R hypothesis appears in Theorem 1 or our new statement. Corollary 2 explicitly descends along finite subextensions. The item retains exactly this generality and adds only the finite-family assembly and the stated properties of canonical normalization.
