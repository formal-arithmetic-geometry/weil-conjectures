### Old step 2: lem-trf-geometric-node-residue-rationalization

The prescribed nonsmooth point $x$ belongs to $Z$. The morphism $Z\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is locally of finite type: on a finite-type affine chart of $C$, its ring is the quotient of the chart ring by the first Fitting ideal. Apply [lem-trf-unramified-over-field-local-criterion] to $Z$. It gives that $x$ is closed in $Z$, $\mathcal O_{Z,x}=\kappa_Z(x)$, and $F=\kappa_Z(x)$ is finite separable over $k$. ### Old step 6: lem-trf-quadratic-completion-separable-splitting

Construct a field $K/F$ of degree at most two. If $a=0$, then $b\ne0$, and take $K=F$. If $a\ne0$, put $p(T)=aT^2+bT+c$. If $p$ has a root $r\in F$, again take $K=F$. Otherwise $p$ is irreducible (a reducible quadratic has a linear factor), and take the field $K=F[T]/(p)$, with $r$ the class of $T$. The coefficient map embeds $F$, and division by $p$ gives the basis $1,r$, so $[K:F]=2$. This extension is separable: when $2\ne0$, a common root of $p$ and $p'=2aT+b$ would force $\Delta=0$ by $(2aT+b)^2-4ap=\Delta$; in characteristic two, $p'=b\ne0$. Every element is $s+tr$; for $t\ne0$ it satisfies the quadratic $t^2p((T-s)/t)$, whose discriminant is $t^2\Delta\ne0$, and for $t=0$ it lies in $F$. Its minimal polynomial divides that separable polynomial, so it is separable. This proves separability from its definition [def-weil-pbc-separable-algebraic-extension], including characteristic two.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-separable-algebraic-extension. Source: literature/stacks-project/curves.tex, Tag 0CBT (remark-quadratic-extension), explicit quadratic algebra, and the two cases in Tag 0CBY.

### Old step 7: lem-trf-quadratic-completion-separable-splitting

The quadratic splits into two independent linear forms over $K$. If $a=0$, set $U=v$, $V=bu+cv$; then $q=UV$ and the inverse substitution is $v=U$, $u=(V-cU)/b$. If $a\ne0$, set $r_2=-b/a-r$. From $p(r)=0$ obtain $q=a(u-rv)(u-r_2v)$ and $\Delta=a^2(r-r_2)^2$, so $r\ne r_2$. Set $U=a(u-rv)$ and $V=u-r_2v$. Their inverse is $v=(U/a-V)/(r_2-r)$, $u=V+r_2v$. Substitution and its displayed inverse define inverse continuous $K$-algebra maps on the two-variable power series rings: in every fixed total degree only finitely many terms contribute, so these identities hold coefficientwise. They carry $q$ to $UV$ and hence identify $K[[u,v]]/(q)$ with $K[[U,V]]/(UV)$. This is the concrete split-form calculation behind Tag 0CBU; it requires no extraction of a square root or division by two.
