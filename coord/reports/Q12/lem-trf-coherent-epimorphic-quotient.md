### Original step 1 → lem-trf-topos-finite-cover-calculus

Work in a small-site presentation of $\mathcal E$ supplied by [def-trf-coherent-topos]. Epimorphisms are stable under pullback: a local lift of a section, provided by [lem-weil-pbc-site-sheaf-sets-epi-locally-surjective], combines with its given section in the other factor by sectionwise pullbacks [lem-weil-pbc-site-sheaf-sets-limits-sectionwise]. The coproduct of any family of sheaves is the sheafification of their presheaf coproduct: the sheafification adjunction [def-weil-pbc-sheafification] identifies maps out of it with families of maps from the summands. Every section of this coproduct locally comes from a summand by [lem-weil-pbc-site-sheafification-sections-local]. Consequently a jointly epimorphic family is exactly a family in which every target section locally lifts into one member; this condition is stable under pullback. Indeed local surjectivity of its coproduct map gives a local coproduct section, and a further covering gives a section of one summand; the converse is immediate from the same criterion.

### Original step 2 → lem-trf-topos-finite-cover-calculus

An epimorphic image of a quasi-compact object is quasi-compact. For an epimorphism $e:X\to Y$ with $X$ quasi-compact, pull a jointly epimorphic family over $Y$ back to $X$ by step 1 and select finitely many members. If two maps from $Y$ agree on these selected members, they agree after $e$ by their pulled-back epimorphy, and hence agree by cancellation with $e$. Thus the selected family is epic over $Y$. Also a finite coproduct of quasi-compact objects is quasi-compact: pull any jointly epic family back to each summand, select finitely many indices for each, and take the finite union; right cancellation on every summand proves joint epimorphy. The empty coproduct is quasi-compact as well: any two maps from the initial object agree, so the empty subfamily already is epic.
