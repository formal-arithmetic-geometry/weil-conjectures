## Findings and route

I read both papers online and used the local Stacks source for the precise power-splitting and G-ring algebraization formulation. Because the papers are not archived under literature/, proof_origin.by is agent, with sources and WANTED notices preserved.

The proof first presents B as a quotient of the t-adic completion of k[t,x]. Rig-regularity makes B[1/t] regular. The continuous Jacobian criterion gives a power-splitting on J/J². Stacks 0GAJ condition (3) and 0GAT algebraize over the excellent ring k[t], rather than merely producing a finite type algebra over k[[t]]. Shrinking to one principal neighborhood of V(t) removes extraneous components, t-torsion and singularities away from V(t), while preserving the completion. The existing supported tower correspondence supplies the final clause. The four new statements are independent ingredients with narrower or general algebraic hypotheses, not copies of the item or its concluding clauses.

## Review by Q12

I checked delivery-errors B--E against each node and the original sources. All four new nodes contain statements only, with every ring, ideal, presentation, topology and map introduced and with empty proof/proof_deps. Each has its own actual LeanFinder, LeanSearch, Loogle and source-grep records. The two semantic phrasings asked different versions of the same mathematical question; I read the returned hits. I also read Mathlib RingTheory/Smooth/Basic.lean around formallySmooth_iff_split_injection and Smooth/AdicCompletion.lean around exists_adicCompletionEvalOneₐ_comp_eq: those statements assume algebraic formal smoothness and lift maps, rather than proving continuous generic smoothness or algebraization. Weierstrass and ordinary reducedness hits likewise do not state any new node. Thus there are four unsplit nodes and zero Mathlib leaves, with no invented searches.

For the coefficient-presentation node I read Temkin's earlier Propositions 3.2.1 and 3.2.3. The coefficient field is lifted from B/rad(pi), not assumed given in B; the nilpotent thickening of its finite type quotient and the complete presentation are included in the theorem. For the Jacobian node I read the proof of Proposition 3.3.1 and Proposition 3.3.2, and local restricted.tex Tag 0GAJ. Characteristic zero is what allows regularity of the affinoid generic fibre to imply smoothness. The resulting splitting is h delta = t^c id on J/J², not delta h = t^c id on B^r; confusing them would assert generic etaleness. For the G-ring node I read restricted.tex proposition-approximate (0GAT), definition-rig-smooth-homomorphism (0GAI) and lemma-equivalent-with-artin-smooth (0GAJ). Its quantified power-splitting is condition (3) of 0GAJ; its completeness and finite presentation hypotheses are explicit. For the shrinking node I read Corollary 3.1.5, the following reducedness paragraph, and Theorem 3.4.1's shrinking paragraph. The additional torsion condition is detected using the finite module ker(t) and removed on a neighborhood of the closed fibre. A principal neighborhood exists because the complement of a suitable neighborhood is closed and disjoint from V(t), so its defining ideal contains an element 1+tc. All three good properties can be imposed together by multiplying the chosen elements. Localization at 1+tc changes none of the positive t-power quotients.

Step 1 uses exactly the coefficient-presentation conclusion. Its Noetherian completeness and characteristic-zero finite type reduced-fibre hypotheses are the item's. Step 2 first checks generic regularity directly from pi^N in the singular ideal, then cites exactly the continuous Jacobian statement and the definition of regularity; I wrote out the extension from t^c to every a in (t)^c. Step 3 uses the actual statements of fields-excellent and finite-type-localization-excellent, and unpacks the actual definition of excellence to obtain a G-ring. The algebraization node then supplies the R-algebra completion isomorphism, preserving t. Step 4 again uses finite-type excellence, then the shrinking node with reducedness and non-zero-divisor verified on the supplied completion; a mere open neighborhood would not suffice for the affine output, so the node explicitly supplies a principal one. Step 5 uses the full statement of the existing supported-formal-blowup-algebraization, which I opened, including its preservation of ordered center ideals. It assumes a Noetherian quasi-excellent Q-scheme: finite type over k gives exactly that by the cited excellence result. I wrote out pi in rad(L) implies pi^e in L at each stage so set-theoretic support is not confused with an unproved scheme-theoretic factorization. Every citation has its own verbatim quote in citations.json. Cross-unit excellence results are declared as tools in split_report.tool_uses; they verify hypotheses without replacing the unit route.

I tested the new statements on B=0, the regular example B=k[[t]]{x}, t-torsion, and relative dimension zero. The zero ring has a unital map from k and a presentation with unit kernel, so no false field-embedding claim is made. The regular relative-line example has a non-torsion differential cokernel and therefore demonstrates why a rig-etale node cannot replace the rig-smooth node. Generic t-torsion disappears after localization and the Jacobian node still only promises a power splitting. The shrinking node additionally assumes injectivity of t on the completion and need not claim t was already regular on C. A nilpotent non-zero-divisor can occur only in the zero ring, already covered. Completion maps are not assumed globally faithful; the good-neighborhood node explicitly removes defects away from the closed fibre. The finite tower includes empty centers, isomorphism blowups and length zero, with no deletions or reordering.
