## Old argument allocation (verbatim preservation)

### Returned step 1 → lem-sfb-cofactor-local-descent

Choose finite standard free covers for $P,Q$ and refine them by pairwise products; these products generate the unit ideal since the product of two expressions for $1$ is again an expression for $1$. Exterior powers localize by the cited localization theorem, and tensor products localize by the tensor theorem. Put $J=\bigwedge^cP$. It is finite projective: apply $\bigwedge^c$ to splitting maps $P\to A^n\to P$ whose composite is the identity; the exterior maps, obtained by applying the maps to every wedge factor, also have composite the identity, and $\bigwedge^c A^n$ is finite free by the exterior basis theorem. Duals of such summands localize as well: if $J$ is the image of an idempotent matrix $e$ on a finite free module, $J^\vee$ is the image of the transpose idempotent on the dual free module; the same matrices after localization identify $(J^\vee)_f$ with $(J_f)^\vee$. Thus the displayed $D(E)$ localizes to $(\bigwedge^cP_f)^\vee\otimes\bigwedge^{c+1}Q_f$. This also specifies the identifications on overlaps, since all the maps send a wedge or a functional to its localized value.

### Returned step 2 → lem-sfb-cofactor-local-descent

On a free chart write $p=p_1\wedge\cdots\wedge p_c$ and define $\phi(v)=p^\vee\otimes(u(p_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge u(p_c)\wedge v)$. This is linear in $v$. Replacing $v$ by $v+u(x)$ changes the value by zero: expand $x$ in the $p_i$ and every extra summand repeats a wedge factor. If another basis is $\widehat p_j=\sum_i p_i g_{ij}$, multilinear expansion gives $\widehat p=\det(g)p$ and $u(\widehat p_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge u(\widehat p_c)=\det(g)u(p_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge u(p_c)$; their dual generators differ by $\det(g)^{-1}$. The factors cancel. The determinant is a unit because expanding the inverse change of basis gives its inverse. No division by a nonunit is used. For $c=0$ these are empty wedges and the same formula is the identity $Q\to\bigwedge^1Q$.

### Returned step 3 → lem-sfb-cofactor-local-descent

For each $v\in Q$, the values $\phi_f(v/1)$ on the chosen finite standard cover agree on overlaps by step 2 and the localization identifications of step 1. Apply the cover exactness theorem to the module $D(E)$: they are the localizations of a unique element $\phi(v)\in D(E)$. Uniqueness implies additivity and $A$-linearity, and implies that the localization of $\phi$ is the chart map, first on $v/1$ and then on every fraction. Since every chart map kills $u(P)$, injectivity in that same exactness theorem gives $\phi u=0$. Hence $\phi$ factors uniquely through $Q/u(P)$, giving $w_E$ with the asserted local formula and independence of the lift. A common product refinement and the uniqueness clause give independence of the cover. Apply the identical construction to $E'$. The argument includes the zero ring, where all modules and maps are zero.

### Returned step 4 → lem-sfb-surjective-cofactor-compatibility

First consider a degreewise surjective quasi-isomorphism $f:E\to E'$. By the surjective determinant calculus, $N^{-1}=\ker f^{-1}$ and $N^0=\ker f^0$ are finite projective and $d_N:N^{-1}\to N^0$ is an isomorphism. Projectivity of the quotient terms splits the two term sequences. Choose a common finite standard cover on which $N^{-1},P',Q'$ are free. On each nonzero chart, let $k_1,\ldots,k_r$ be a basis of $N^{-1}$, put $l_i=d_Nk_i$, choose bases $p'_1,\ldots,p'_{c'}$ and $q'_1,\ldots,q'_{c'+1}$, and lift them to $\widetilde p_j,\widetilde q_j$. Then $(k_i,\widetilde p_j)$ and $(l_i,\widetilde q_j)$ are kernel-first bases of $P,Q$; in particular $c=r+c'$. The calculus trivializes $D(N)$ by $(k_1\wedge\cdots\wedge k_r)^\vee\otimes(l_1\wedge\cdots\wedge l_r)$. Its $T_f$ is precisely the comparison stipulated in the item. Ordinary tensor symmetry identifies its target-first notation with our dual-first notation, with no graded sign.

### Returned step 5 → lem-sfb-surjective-cofactor-compatibility

Use the bases of step 4 and abbreviate wedges by $k,l,\widetilde p,\widetilde q,p',q'$. For a lift $v\in Q_f$ of a cokernel element write $v=\widetilde{f^0v}+z$, $z\in N^0_f$, using the chosen linear splitting. Likewise $u(\widetilde p_j)=\widetilde{u'(p'_j)}+z_j$ with $z_j\in N^0_f$. In $u(k_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge u(k_r)\wedge u(\widetilde p_1)\wedge\cdots\wedge u(\widetilde p_{c'})\wedge v$, the initial factors are exactly $l_1,\ldots,l_r$. Every term containing a $z$ or $z_j$ is zero by expanding it in this basis of $N^0_f$. Thus only $l\wedge\widetilde{u'(p'_1)}\wedge\cdots\wedge\widetilde{u'(p'_{c'})}\wedge\widetilde{f^0v}$ remains. The kernel-first rule for $T_f$ sends $(k\wedge\widetilde p)^\vee\otimes(l\wedge\widetilde q)$ to $(p')^\vee\otimes q'$. Comparing coefficients in these rank-one generators therefore gives $T_fw_E(\bar v)=w_{E'}(\overline{f^0v})$ on every chart. This calculation keeps the kernel block first throughout. Empty blocks ($r=0$ or $c'=0$) satisfy the same equation. Apply injectivity of the cover exactness theorem in $D(E')$ to the difference, for every global lift $v\in Q$. All cokernel elements have such lifts. The imposed normalization identifies $D(f)$ with $T_f$, proving $D(f)w_E=w_{E'}H^0(f)$ globally.

### Returned step 6 → assembly step 2 (unchanged)

The K-projective Hom bijection represents $a$ by a chain map $f:E\to E'$. ### Returned step 7 → existing lem-trf-good-two-term-surjective-diagrams, applied in assembly step 3

Choose a finite free module $F$ and a surjection $\pi:F\to P'$: a projection from a finite free module containing $P'$ as a summand supplies one. Form $M=[P\oplus F\xrightarrow{u\oplus1}Q\oplus F]$, with projection $b:M\to E$. Define $t:M\to E'$ by $t^{-1}(p,x)=f^{-1}(p)+\pi(x)$ and $t^0(q,x)=f^0(q)+u'\pi(x)$. The chain-map equation is $t^0(u(p),x)=u't^{-1}(p,x)$. The homotopy $h^0(q,x)=\pi(x)$ gives $t^{-1}-(fb)^{-1}=h^0d_M$ and $t^0-(fb)^0=u'h^0$, so $t$ and $fb$ have the same derived image and cohomology maps. Projection $b$ is a degreewise surjective quasi-isomorphism: its kernel is $[F\xrightarrow{1}F]$, and explicitly $\ker d_M=\ker u\oplus0$ while $(Q\oplus F)/\operatorname{im}(u\oplus1)\cong Q/\operatorname{im}u$. Consequently $t$ is a quasi-isomorphism too. Its degree $-1$ map is surjective by $\pi$. Given $q'\in Q'$, surjectivity of $H^0(f)$ gives $q'=f^0(q)+u'(p')$; lifting $p'$ through $\pi$ proves surjectivity of $t^0$. The terms of $M$ are finite projective of ranks $c+n,c+n+1$, where $F=A^n$.

### Returned step 8 → assembly step 4 (same cancellation)

Apply the surjective case of steps 4--5 to $b$ and $t$. It gives $D(b)w_M=w_EH^0(b)$ and $D(t)w_M=w_{E'}H^0(t)$.
