### lem-zl-partition-module-evaluation-localization

Old step 2: Apply [lem-zl-partition-refinement-sheafification] to presheaves of underlying sets; this is legitimate because a module sheafification [def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification] is the sheafification of the underlying presheaf of abelian groups, whose underlying sheaf of sets is the sheafification of the underlying presheaf of sets [lem-pc-sites-sheafification-underlying-sets]. For any small diagram of $\mathcal O$-module sheaves, its colimit is the sheafification of the sectionwise module colimit by [lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-colimits-forgetful]. That presheaf sends finite disjoint unions to products: on $U=U_1\sqcup U_2$, the ring is $\mathcal O(U_1)\times\mathcal O(U_2)$ and each module is the pair of its two components; module colimits are formed separately in those components. Its value on the empty object is zero. Thus sheafification does not change this colimit at $T$, proving preservation of all small colimits by $\Gamma(T,-)$. Kernels are sectionwise by the same node. Cokernels are colimits, so evaluation preserves kernels and cokernels, hence is exact. All bijections here preserve the induced $A$-actions.

Old step 3: Exact evaluation preserves cohomology of every complex and hence all quasi-isomorphisms, with no boundedness condition. It therefore descends termwise to $D(\mathcal O)\to D(A)$. Every complex $K$ admits a K-injective resolution $K\to I$ by [prop-pur-kinjective-resolutions-exist]. By [def-sfb-right-derived-functor], $R\Gamma(T,K)$ is represented by $\Gamma(T,I)$. Exactness makes $\Gamma(T,K)\to\Gamma(T,I)$ a quasi-isomorphism, proving that the descended functor is $G=R\Gamma(T,-)$ with its canonical comparison. In particular $G(\mathcal O[0])=A[0]$.

### lem-zl-partition-section-tensor-flat

Old step 4: For module sheaves $M,N$, their tensor is the sheafification of $P(U)=M(U)\otimes_{\mathcal O(U)}N(U)$ by [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product], taken as module sheafification [def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification]; its underlying sheaf of sets is the set sheafification of the underlying presheaf of sets [lem-pc-sites-sheafification-underlying-sets]. This presheaf sends finite disjoint unions to products: for rings $R_1,R_2$ and component modules, the isomorphism is $(M_1\times M_2)\otimes_{R_1\times R_2}(N_1\times N_2)\cong(M_1\otimes_{R_1}N_1)\times(M_2\otimes_{R_2}N_2)$; mixed tensors vanish since the two orthogonal idempotents multiply to zero. It sends the empty object to zero. Thus [lem-zl-partition-refinement-sheafification] identifies the usual section pairing $M(T)\otimes_A N(T)\to(M\otimes_{\mathcal O}N)(T)$ as an isomorphism. The unit is $A=\mathcal O(T)$. Naturality and the associativity, symmetry and unit diagrams hold on pure tensors: both paths send local generators to the same tensor or scalar product.

Old step 5: If $F$ is a flat module sheaf, then $F(T)$ is a flat $A$-module. Indeed, consider a finite relation $\sum_i a_ix_i=0$ in $F(T)$ and the kernel sheaf $H$ of the row map $\mathcal O^n\xrightarrow{(a_i)}\mathcal O$. By the definition of flatness [def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules], $H\otimes F\to F^n\to F$ is exact. Exact evaluation from step 2 and the tensor identification from step 4 show that $(x_i)$ is the image of an element of $H(T)\otimes_A F(T)$. Write that element as a finite sum $\sum_j h_j\otimes y_j$. If $h_j=(b_{ij})$, then $x_i=\sum_j b_{ij}y_j$ and $\sum_i a_i b_{ij}=0$. Hence every relation in $F(T)$ is trivial in the precise sense of [lem-weil-pbc-flat-equational-criterion], and that criterion gives flatness. The case $n=0$ is included.

### lem-zl-evaluation-adapted-flat-telescope

Old step 6: For any complex $K$, take the compatible bounded-above flat complexes $E_n$ and quasi-isomorphisms $E_n\to\tau_{\le n}K$ supplied by [lem-pc-flat-truncation-tower]. Put $B=\bigoplus_n E_n$ and $P=\operatorname{Cone}(1-s:B\to B)$, where $s$ shifts each summand along $E_n\to E_{n+1}$. By [lem-pc-kflat-closure], $P$ is K-flat and has flat terms. The compatible maps $E_n\to K$ define $P\to K$ (use their sum on the unshifted summand of the cone and zero on the shifted summand). By [lem-pc-complex-hocolim-colimit], $P\to\varinjlim_nE_n$ is a quasi-isomorphism. The second clause of [lem-pc-kflat-hocolim-truncations] makes $\varinjlim_n E_n\to\varinjlim_n\tau_{\le n}K$ a quasi-isomorphism. The last colimit equals $K$ degree by degree, since each fixed degree eventually stabilizes. Thus $P\to K$ is a K-flat resolution with flat terms. This uses only the tower's stated compatibility, not a choice of a functorial tower.

Old step 7: Evaluation commutes with the sums, shifts and cone in step 6, by step 2 and the explicit cone differential. Hence $\Gamma(T,P)$ is the telescope of the $\Gamma(T,E_n)$. Each $\Gamma(T,E_n)$ is bounded above with flat $A$-module terms by step 5. Apply [lem-pc-kflat-closure] on the one-object site with ring $A$: these complexes, their sum, and the cone of $1-s$ are K-flat. Consequently the particular resolution $P\to K$ of step 6 has both $P$ K-flat on the site and $\Gamma(T,P)$ K-flat over $A$. Exact evaluation also makes $\Gamma(T,P)\to\Gamma(T,K)$ a quasi-isomorphism. No assertion that evaluation preserves all K-flat complexes is needed.

### lem-zl-derived-section-pairing-coherence

Old step 8: Choose resolutions $P\to K$ and $Q\to L$ as in steps 6-7. By [lem-weil-pbc-site-derived-tensor-descent], their total tensor represents $K\otimes_{\mathcal O}^L L$; the same node on the one-object site says that $\Gamma(T,P)\otimes_A\Gamma(T,Q)$ represents $G(K)\otimes_A^L G(L)$. In degree $n$, step 2 and step 4 give the canonical isomorphism $\bigoplus_{p+q=n}P^p(T)\otimes_AQ^q(T)\cong(\bigoplus_{p+q=n}P^p\otimes_{\mathcal O}Q^q)(T)$. It commutes with the differential $d_P\otimes1+(-1)^p1\otimes d_Q$ by naturality of section pairings, so is an isomorphism of total complexes [def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves]. It represents the canonical comparison in the statement: on K-flat resolutions the derived comparison is the usual tensor pairing of sections, preceded, in general, by the derived-to-ordinary module tensor map; here that preceding map is an isomorphism since both evaluated resolutions are K-flat. Thus the canonical comparison is invertible for every unbounded $K,L$.

Old step 9: The section pairings used in step 8 are natural in both complex variables. To interpret them without any choice of resolution, for arbitrary flat-term K-flat $P,Q$ use K-flat module resolutions of $P(T),Q(T)$, map their total tensor to $P(T)\otimes_AQ(T)$, and then apply the section pairing into $(P\otimes_{\mathcal O}Q)(T)$. Resolving-class localization [lem-weil-pbc-site-derived-tensor-descent], also on the one-object site, identifies changes of these resolutions and gives the canonical natural comparison. The computations in step 8 compute its value using the adapted resolutions. For coherence use the constraints of [lem-sf-ringed-derived-closed-symmetric-monoidal]: on total-complex representatives associativity merely reindexes $(p,q,r)$, symmetry sends $x\otimes y$ to $(-1)^{pq}y\otimes x$, and the unit multiplies by a scalar. Section pairing preserves these formulas term by term, so the associativity, symmetry and unit diagrams commute before localization and therefore after localization. The unit comparison is the identity $A[0]\to A[0]$ of step 3. The invertible tensor comparisons and this unit make $G$ strong symmetric monoidal.
