## Review by Q27

For Step 1 I checked the new node against 01SM(1) and (3): the hypothesis is precisely that the inverse image of every affine open is a quasi-compact open in an affine scheme, and the conclusion supplies a quasi-coherent algebra and a quasi-compact open immersion over the same base. This is a distinct characterization lemma, not a clause or restatement of base-change stability. Its statement introduces all objects and cites the existing definitions of schemes, quasi-coherence, quasi-affine morphisms, quasi-compact morphisms and open immersions, together with the existing relative-Spec construction. It has no proof or proof dependencies. I corrected the draft's mistaken source tag and the missing chapter/unit fields.

For Step 2 I opened `def-pc-fibre-product`, `lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-base-change` and `lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence`. The first really states existence, uniqueness and the universal property used to identify `X ×_S S'` with `X ×_Y (Y ×_S S')`; the step writes out the identical pairs of maps so that the pullback identification is not hidden. The second says that the fibre-product projection of an open immersion is an open immersion, and nothing about quasi-compactness. Clause (3) of the third states separately that arbitrary base change preserves a quasi-compact morphism. Thus the source of this latter base change is the given quasi-compact immersion `i`, and its target is the base-changed relative spectrum; no quasi-compactness, quasi-separatedness or finite-presentation hypothesis is imposed on `g` or its base.

For Step 3 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-relative-spec-base-change` and read its full statement, not just its title. Clause (1) gives quasi-coherence of the pullback algebra; clause (2) gives an isomorphism **over the new base**, directed from `Spec_{S'}(g^* A)` to the fibre product. Accordingly the proof composes `i'` with its inverse. It does not take a nonexistent inverse direction or merely an equality of underlying spaces. The algebra multiplication and unit needed by that statement are part of the algebra chosen in Step 1; no extra geometric hypothesis is needed. Composition with a target isomorphism preserves the open-subscheme identification and quasi-compactness directly from their definitions, as the step explains. This is the sole non-Mathlib off-unit proof tool; it is declared in `split_report.tool_uses`, since the assigned unit contains no relative-Spec base-change theorem.

For Step 4 I checked the reverse implication of the same characterization: the constructed quasi-compact open immersion is over `S'`, and the composite structure map is the original fibre-product projection because every identification in Steps 2–3 preserves that projection. This proves exactly the assigned statement, without a finite-presentation assumption. Each citation has a literal quotation in `citations.json`, and `proof_deps` is exactly their union. No proof step exceeds 2000 characters (lengths 309, 655, 641, 305).

I tested the characterization and proof on the empty scheme and empty base, the identity base change, an affine morphism (where the chosen open immersion may be an identity), and the identity on a non-quasi-compact scheme. In the last case the identity is quasi-affine as a morphism with algebra `O_S`, although its source need not be quasi-affine as an absolute scheme; the new statement correctly keeps the relative meaning. This also guards against misusing the absolute Mathlib results. The open immersion in the characterization is required to be quasi-compact as a **morphism**, not to have an absolutely quasi-compact source.
