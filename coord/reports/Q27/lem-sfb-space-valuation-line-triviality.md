## Review by Q27

I checked the item directly against the entire valuation-ring portion of 0DNJ. Its proof starts after the preceding Picard-class lifting reduction because the item already supplies an actual line module with an actual generic trivialization. Thus no gerbe lifting, faithful-flat descent of Picard classes, or existence of a Picard algebraic space is used. The source makes no Noetherian or rank restriction on the valuation ring. I retained this generality.

In step 1 I read the full statement of lem-sfb-proper-flat-space-derived-base-change. It gives perfect Zariski direct images and the canonical arbitrary base-change arrows, with the etale comparison required here. Locally a line module and its inverse are free rank one; flatness over the base follows from the given flat morphism on those charts. They meet precisely its finite-presentation, quasi-coherence and relative-flatness hypotheses. This avoids trying to use a scheme-only proper-image theorem for an algebraic space.

For step 2 I read the new opposite-sections statement against 0DMZ and the existing constants statement against 0DMY(7). Both statements introduce their field and space and spell out properness/integrality and what a line module is. The 0DMZ assertion is a separate field lemma used in the source, not the valuation statement in disguise. I applied it over the algebraic closure of the closed residue field, so ordinary-fibre integrality descent is not silently used. The geometric fibres are proper integral by the hypotheses. The constants lemma gives one-dimensional H^0 after the generic trivialization, and again after closed-fibre triviality. I opened lem-sc-perfect-jump-loci: its statement really gives upper semicontinuity and arbitrary-base-change invariance, for a pseudo-coherent (in particular perfect) complex on a SCHEME; the scheme here is Spec R, not X. Its closed superlevel set containing the generic point contains the closed point. Field-extension invariance lets the argument stay on the geometric closed fibre without needing a descent theorem for a trivialization.

In step 3 I opened lem-pc-perfect-affine-module-preimage: it gives a pseudo-coherent ring complex with finite Tor amplitude, hence bounded below on taking the tensor module R. The affine equivalence in lem-pc-affine-qcoh-derived-equivalence gives the termwise associated-sheaf comparison. I read the complete statement of lem-sfb-bounded-pseudocoherent-residue-model, which gives the exact ranks and a principal localization for bounded-below pseudo-coherent objects; its hypotheses hold because the derived closed fibre is the cohomology of a sheaf in degree zero and has no negative cohomology. Its localization parameter outside the maximal ideal is a unit; no shrinking of the local base is being concealed. I checked the explicit scalar-extension clause of lem-pc-flat-complex-derived-pullback for the bounded finite-free model. No assertion of that node is used beyond termwise pullback of a bounded-above flat complex.

In step 4 the new arithmetic is elementary: a map out of the one-dimensional generic degree-zero term with a one-dimensional kernel is zero, and every matrix entry vanishing in the fraction field vanishes in the domain R. This produces an actual cycle 1, rather than merely an abstract isomorphism between the endpoints of a base-change map. I added the citation lem-sfb-space-affine-base-higher-image-sections after checking its degree-zero canonical isomorphism with actual sections; this closes the identification of the class with a section of L. The canonical base-change arrow is computed by the cycle mapping to 1 tensor 1, so the special-fibre restriction is the required nonzero class, not just a dimension count. Geometric extension and the constants statement say that this very section generates on the geometric special fibre.

Step 5 uses the properness hypothesis as written: the local zero sets of a section of a line module glue, since their functions differ by units, and their image is closed by universal closedness. A nonempty closed subset of Spec of a local ring contains the maximal ideal, so absence of zeros on the closed fibre forces absence everywhere. Local inverse functions then glue to the inverse module map. This is a short elementary use of the definitions, not a hidden relative Cartier-divisor or Picard separatedness theorem.

I tested R a field (the generic and closed points coincide), a DVR, and a higher-rank non-Noetherian valuation domain: the proof uses only the local-domain injection into K and the given perfect/base-change theorem, so the same computation works. Empty fibres are excluded by integral meaning nonempty. For the new constants statement, Spec k and projective space satisfy it; Spec k' for a nontrivial finite separable extension is not geometrically integral, and k[epsilon]/epsilon^2 is not reduced, so neither is a counterexample. For the opposite-sections criterion, a nontrivial degree-zero line on a proper smooth curve cannot have a nonzero section in both directions, while the trivial line satisfies it. Dropping properness permits nonconstant regular functions with zeros, and dropping integrality invalidates the generic-product argument in the source; neither hypothesis was dropped.
