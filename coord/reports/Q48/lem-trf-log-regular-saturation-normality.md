Read ILO *Travaux de Gabber*, Expose VI 1.6(i)--(ii), printed p. 70: the equal-characteristic model is `k[[P]][[t]]`, and the Cohen model has a relation congruent to p modulo the monoid and variable ideal. The source fields identify these as agent formulations motivated by these passages, rather than misattributing proofs to the book or to unavailable Kato. The following are plans for future workers, kept OUT of the unsplit nodes' statements and proof fields.

For the orthant lemma: saturation and sharpness force the finitely generated group completion G to have no torsion (if ng=0, saturation puts g and -g in P; sharpness forces g=0). Choose a lattice basis for G. The rational cone generated by a finite generating set for P is pointed: a line would give a nonzero rational lattice vector with it and its negative in the cone, hence, after clearing denominators and using saturation, a nonzero unit of P. Also P equals its cone intersected with G: a lattice point in the rational cone has a rational nonnegative expression, so a positive multiple lies in P, and saturation applies. Express this pointed rational cone by finitely many integral nonnegative linear inequalities. Their joint map lambda takes G into Z^r, has trivial kernel because the cone has no line, and detects exactly P. This is genuine polyhedral algebra still to split; no assertion about normality is moved into it.

For the completion lemma: choose nonzero generators q_i of Q, and let D be at least 1 and at least every total degree |q_i|. A product of a generator monomial or an element of m has ambient (m,X)-order at least its number of factors. Conversely, for q expressed with ell generator factors, |q| <= D ell. Thus a monomial with a coefficient in m^a and ambient weight a+|q| >= DN has J-order at least N. These inclusions make the J-adic and induced ambient filtrations cofinal on L[Q]. Finitely generated m ensures powers of m are finitely generated, so coefficientwise membership in m^a for a series is membership in m^a A; this justifies the ideal-filtration comparison. Completeness of L then identifies the completion with all series supported on Q, by truncating degrees and taking coefficient limits. For q in Q and e in N^r, q+e lies in M iff e lies in M; therefore, coefficient by coefficient in the finite convolution sum, deleting exponents outside Q commutes with multiplication by a Q-supported series. This proves module-linearity. The projection is usually not multiplicative: for M={(a,a)} in Z^2, it deletes X and Y but keeps XY.
