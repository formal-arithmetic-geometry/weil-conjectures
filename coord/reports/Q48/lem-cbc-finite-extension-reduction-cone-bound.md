## Original proof, verbatim preservation

1. Write $\underline A$ for the constant sheaf of rings on $T_{\Proet}$. Since $A$ is a DVR and $\varpi$ is a uniformizer, multiplication by $\varpi^n$ is injective and the ordinary sequence $0\to A\xrightarrow{\varpi^n}A\to A/I^n\to0$ is exact. The quotient is finite by the finite-quotient clause of [def-weil-l-adic-coefficient-extension]. Applying the exact constant-sheaf section property [lem-weil-pbc-constant-module-section-properties] gives the corresponding exact sequence of constant module sheaves. The completed-coefficient reduction lemma [lem-weil-pbc-completed-constant-sheaf-reduction] identifies the locally constant quotient sheaf with $(A/I^n)_T$, says that $A_T$ is flat over $\underline A$, and identifies $A_T\otimes_{\underline A}(A/I^n)_T$ with $(A/I^n)_T$. Tensoring the constant exact sequence by this flat $\underline A$-module therefore gives an exact sequence of $A_T$-modules $0\to A_T\xrightarrow{\varpi^n}A_T\to(A/I^n)_T\to0$.

2. Put $R=A_T$ and $S=(A/I^n)_T$. Let $F=[R\xrightarrow{\varpi^n}R]$ in degrees $-1,0$, with augmentation $e:F\to S[0]$. Step 1 says exactly that $e$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Choose a K-flat representative $P$ of $M$ using [def-weil-pbc-adic-coefficient-datum]. The cone of $e$ is acyclic by [lem-pur-quasi-iso-cone-acyclic]. By the definition of K-flatness [def-pc-kflat-complex], $\operatorname{Tot}(P\otimes_R C(e))$ is acyclic: the signed swap $x\otimes y\mapsto(-1)^{ij}y\otimes x$ for degrees $i,j$ converts the order in the definition, and commutes with the total differential of [def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves]. The tensor-cone isomorphism [lem-pc-tensor-mapping-cone-iso] identifies this complex with the cone of $\operatorname{Tot}(P\otimes_R e)$; applying the same cone criterion shows that $\operatorname{Tot}(P\otimes_R F)\to P\otimes_R S$ is a quasi-isomorphism. By [lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings], the target with its $S$-action computes $M_n$, and its underlying $R$-complex computes $\operatorname{Res}_nM_n$ [def-pc-sheaf-restrict-scalars]. This proves the comparison in the required completed coefficient category, without assuming that tensoring an arbitrary complex preserves quasi-isomorphisms.

3. The complex $F$ is the mapping cone of $\varpi^n:R[0]\to R[0]$. Apply [lem-pc-tensor-mapping-cone-iso] to this map: under the unit identifications $P\otimes_R R=P$, its tensor cone is $C(\varpi^n\operatorname{id}_P)$, compatibly with the inclusion of $P$. The concrete derived-category structure of [def-weil-abstract-derived-category] therefore gives the distinguished triangle $M\xrightarrow{\varpi^n\operatorname{id}}M\to\operatorname{Res}_nM_n\to M[1]$ after the comparison of Step 2. Naturality is also at the complex level: a chain map $h:P\to P'$ commutes with multiplication by $\varpi^n$, and the maps on $P\otimes_R F$ and $P\otimes_R S$ are $h\otimes1$; the augmentation square commutes on each tensor $x\otimes y$. The tensor-cone isomorphism is natural by its cited statement. If $h$ is a quasi-isomorphism between K-flat representatives, then $h\otimes_R S$ is a quasi-isomorphism by clause (1) of [lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings]; the augmentation square and Step 2 imply the same for $h\otimes_R F$. These squares therefore descend through localization, and clause (2) gives independence of resolutions. They thus give the asserted natural triangle for derived morphisms.

4. Apply the long exact cohomology sequence [lem-sf-derived-homology-long-exact] to the triangle of Step 3, using the cohomology-sheaf functors of [def-weil-derived-cohomology-sheaves]. Restriction of scalars leaves the underlying sheaf and its kernels and quotients unchanged, so $\mathcal H^q(\operatorname{Res}_nM_n)$ is the restricted underlying sheaf of $\mathcal H^q(M_n)$. If $q<a-1$, both $\mathcal H^q(M)$ and $\mathcal H^{q+1}(M)$ vanish by the assumed interval, and exactness forces $\mathcal H^q(M_n)=0$. If $q>b$, the same argument uses vanishing in degrees $q$ and $q+1$. Hence every positive $n$ has $\mathcal H^q(M_n)=0$ for $q\notin[a-1,b]$, with one interval independent of $n$.
