## Review by Q48

I read BLR 5.1 Theorem 5 in the primary scan for the item's intended no-shrinking argument and BLR 2.2 Corollary 10 for the new lemma. The latter explicitly compares the pullback of the target's relative differential module with the source module under smoothness of both structure maps. Its base is not a field and it imposes no separatedness or relative-rank restriction. The statement here uses global smoothness, hence supplies its pointwise smoothness hypotheses. I read Stacks Tag 02GU locally as the supporting square-Jacobian criterion; I have not mislabeled the weaker criterion involving vanishing absolute relative differentials without flatness as the new lemma. The new lemma itself remains unsplit.

For step 1 I opened the DVR definition (which explicitly includes principal ideals), the DVR dimension-one node, the definition of locally Noetherian schemes, finite-type-over-Noetherian, smooth-flat, and flat-lfp-open. They supply exactly Noetherian X and an open structure map. The assertion that the generic fibre is dense is proved using nonempty open images in the two-point spectrum, not taken from a definition of flatness. R being Noetherian follows because its ideals have one generator. Dimension one, locality and the domain property rule out a third prime. The empty-X case is vacuous and the open-image argument also rules out a nonempty smooth X with empty generic fibre.

For step 2 I read the flat height formula in the node and the full local Stacks Tag 00ON. It has precisely the needed Noetherian and flat hypotheses; we apply it to R and the local ring at a special-fibre point. No regularity or equidimensionality is tacitly imposed. Dimension zero of the fibre local ring means the affine fibre prime is minimal, by prime localization; thus the dense-fibre-open statement I read really does place the point in U. That node allows every irreducible-component generic point for a flat lfp morphism. Both flatness and lfp were established in step 1, and the given U is dense in the special fibre. The generic-fibre branch of the first assertion is explicit.

For step 3 I read the relative differential definition, module pullback definition, smooth-differentials-finite-locally-free node, and finite-locally-free definition. They allow ranks to vary. Local bases pull back by tensoring, and the universal derivation supplies the comparison map. Its restriction to U is an isomorphism because f there is the identity after identifying U with its open image; the differential construction is compatible with open restriction. I corrected the tempting constant-relative-dimension shortcut by choosing a neighbourhood where both ranks are constant and using its intersection with X_F to prove the two ranks equal. This needs no unrecorded base-change theorem for differential sheaves.

For step 4 I read the principal-support node and the full local source Tag 0BJG. Its conclusion is dimension of the ambient local ring exactly one, not merely dimension of the zero locus. Its hypotheses are locally Noetherian, locally principal quasi-coherent ideal and positive ambient stalk dimension; each is explicit on the affine neighbourhood W. The principal ideal sheaf is obtained by localizing the determinant ideal. The contradiction uses step 2, so even a determinant with componentwise zeros cannot escape the argument. The only algebra in the step is the adjugate identity. At a dimension-zero or dimension-one point the assumed bad determinant already contradicts step 2; at a rank-zero point its determinant is 1. No regular-local-UFD or unquoted divisor theorem is hidden here.

For step 5 I checked both smooth maps and the T-morphism condition against the new node, then read etale-local-on-source, etale-implies-smooth, and smooth-flat. They give exactly the assembled etale map and then the flatness and lfp required at the end. The proof does not assume these properties of f merely from those of X/T and Z/T.

For step 6 I read etale-base-change-finite-limits (its first clause gives etaleness of the projection), closed-immersion-base-change, separated-base-change, and the section-clopen lemma. The Cartesian diagonal square is written out through the equality of the two X-coordinates; this uses X/T separated, not an unproved f-separated hypothesis. Thus f is separated by definition before separated base change is invoked. The map Delta_f really is a section of p_1, which is both etale and separated, so the section lemma gives its actual open-and-closed subscheme identification, not just a topological claim.

For step 7 I read the etale-open-map node and flat-lfp-monomorphism-open. Openness of p_1 plus density from step 1 proves density of P_F without any appeal to an uncited birational-density assertion. The generic diagonal is an isomorphism since f_F is an isomorphism. Its clopen image must then fill P, and the supplied open-immersion identification shows that the entire diagonal is an isomorphism of schemes. The proof spells out how this makes f a monomorphism for every test scheme. With step 5, all three hypotheses of the final flat-monomorphism criterion are present. This establishes the immersion including disconnected schemes, empty fibres, and differing ranks on different components.
