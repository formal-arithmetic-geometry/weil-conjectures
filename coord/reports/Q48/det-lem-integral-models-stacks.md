## Proof of lem-sfb-constructible-local-tor-etale-origin

1. Apply `lem-sfb-proetale-tor-local-global` to the constant ring A and M. Quasi-compactness is assumed. Its second condition is exactly the slice-wise local Tor hypothesis, testing arbitrary modules on each slice. It is equivalent to a global interval [a,b] for all pro-étale test modules.

2. Test that interval with the unit A_X. The derived tensor unit identity (`sk-def-sfb-derived-tensor-hom`) gives H^q(M)=0 off [a,b]; hence M is bounded. The constructibility conventions agree: a finitely presented module is finitely generated by its finite presentation, and the converse over A is `lem-sc-noetherian-finite-module-finite-presentation`. The affine-local convention is the one used by `sk-thm-pe-constructible-comparison`.

3. That comparison, using qcqs X and discrete Noetherian A, gives a bounded constructible étale C and an isomorphism nu_X^*C ≅ M. Apply `det-pe-finite-tor-comparison-noetherian` to this C and [a,b]. Its reflection implication gives the same Tor interval for C. Isomorphism invariance here is elementary: tensor the isomorphism with each test module and apply cohomology.

4. Conversely, given C of condition (2), `sk-thm-pe-constructible-comparison` makes nu_X^*C bounded constructible, and `det-pe-finite-tor-comparison-noetherian` preserves its finite Tor interval. Transport along the given isomorphism to M. The first-to-second implication of `lem-sfb-proetale-tor-local-global` then gives local finite Tor dimension with all slice test modules. This proves condition (1).

This factors the residue argument into the already-named globalization, constructible descent and interval comparison theorems, with only the tensor-unit test and isomorphism invariance elementary. The proof neither asserts that arbitrary sheaves over a field are projective nor adds completeness to M.
