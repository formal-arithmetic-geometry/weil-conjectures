## Intended arguments for the statement-only inputs

Generation (Milne C.11 first assertion): for a positive root b not simple, write b = sum n_a a. Since (b,b)>0, some (b,a)>0. The coefficient m = 2(b,a)/(a,a) is a positive integer. Reflection changes only the a-coordinate. As b is not proportional to a, some other coordinate stays positive, so s_a b is positive and its integer height is ht(b)-m < ht(b). Induct over positive height until a simple root is reached. Orthogonality gives s_(s_a b) = s_a s_b s_a, so reverse this descent to express s_b as a product of simple reflections. Negative roots have the same reflections as their negatives. This argument also works in rank zero by empty generation. It is a real root-reduction lemma with its own base and integrality hypotheses; it is not merely a relabelled chamber claim.

Deletion: track b through a word from right to left. It starts positive and ends negative, so some factor is the first positive-to-negative transition. Immediately before that factor s_(a_j), the root must equal a_j, because s_(a_j) preserves all other positive roots. Put v = s_(a_(j+1)) ... s_(a_k); then v b = a_j and v s_b v^-1 = s_(a_j) by the reflection formula and orthogonality. Writing w = p s_(a_j) v gives w s_b = p s_(a_j) s_(a_j) v = p v. This is exactly the specified deletion, with the stated rightmost-first convention. The empty word cannot satisfy its premise. No Coxeter presentation is used.
