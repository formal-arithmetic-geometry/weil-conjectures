STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the delivered finite-etale clause of lem-sfb-flat-trait-group-finite-component-opens was false. I accept every part of the maintainer's counterexample. Take R = C[[pi]], K = C((pi)), L = K(t) with t^2 = pi, and the etale K-group with lattice Z^2 and the ramified Galois involution swapping coordinates. Extend only its zero point over S. A quasi-compact open subgroup has a finite generic subgroup of Z^2, hence only zero, so H=S. The generic orbit of (1,0) is Spec L; it does not split over an unramified generic extension. For the trivial H-action, the diagonal of that degree-two orbit is not surjective onto its square. The original test of generic-only components did not test ramification, and was insufficient.

The corrected clause permits B_a to be the normalization of S in a finite possibly ramified extension of K. It explicitly supplies an integral, surjective, flat, quasi-compact cover with finite underlying fibres and a Noetherian normal semilocal domain of dimension one. It neither asserts finite etaleness nor assumes the normalization is finite as an R-module. The item statement, its fpqc sheaf hypothesis, and the other three child statements are unchanged. This is a genuine mathematical repair, not a rebase of the returned delivery.

Here is the justification of the corrected structural input, separating the source result from my extension. In the primary Numdam scan I reread 4.2.4 (printed pp.59-60), its paragraph on the subgroup G_(0) and its action (pp.60-61), the finite-etale paragraph immediately following it (p.61), the independent field case in 4.1, and the normalization construction in 4.2.1. The local archive remains WANTED. The source gives H and the finite-presentation H-invariant opens meeting the special fibre, with the almost homogeneous decomposition on a finite-etale semi-trait cover. These opens cover all special points. I do not infer that their union covers all generic points.

For the remaining generic points use the source's field-case result first: F_K is a locally finite-type group scheme over K. The open subgroup H_K is of finite type and contains the geometric identity component. Over a separable closure, its left cosets are open and closed, each of finite type: translating by a geometric point identifies a coset, after a faithfully flat field extension if necessary, with H. They are unions of finitely many geometric connected components. For any one coset, its finite-presentation open-and-closed subscheme descends to a finite separable extension: its finitely many geometric components, and thus the finite union defining the coset, are fixed by an open subgroup of the absolute Galois group. Its Galois orbit is finite. The union of that finite orbit descends to an H_K-invariant finite-type open U in F_K. Doing this for all coset orbits covers the generic fibre. Each such U is also an open subfunctor of F over S. It is finite presentation over S because it is finite type and separated over the field K, and Spec K = D(pi) is a finitely presented principal open of S; its pullback to T is therefore finite presentation. These are additional pieces, not asserted special-component pieces.

A finite separable extension L/K fixing every member of the finite orbit makes U_L a finite union of open H_L-cosets V. The geometric identity component is already contained in H, so no further connected components have to be separated. Each V has the required homogeneous action, without assuming a rational point on V: for two test sections x,y in the same coset, y x^{-1} lies in H, as can be checked after a faithfully flat field extension that trivializes that coset. Membership in the open subgroup is fpqc local, so the difference lies in H on the original test scheme as well. The map (h,x) -> (hx,x) is consequently an isomorphism for such a coset, with inverse (y,x) -> (y x^{-1},x). This argument works for noncommutative groups and survives arbitrary base change. No claim that V has a K-point, and no smoothness assumption on H, is used.

For a special-fibre piece take a connected component E of the source's finite-etale surjective semi-trait cover. Every such component maps surjectively to the trait (its image is open and closed); it is normal and integral. Its fraction field M is finite separable over K, and E is the integral closure of R in M. Thus the source decomposition restricts to B=E, which is already a normalization of the stipulated form. For a generic-only piece take instead the normalization B of R in its splitting field L. Pullback from Spec L gives the homogeneous generic-only opens of U_B. The two kinds of pieces may have different B_a; the lemma never requires one cover for the entire infinite family.

For either kind, Krull-Akizuki gives that the integral closure R_L is a Noetherian normal domain of dimension one. It has finitely many maximal ideals above the maximal ideal of R and a single generic point, so Spec R_L has finite underlying fibres. Integrality and lying over give an affine, surjective, quasi-compact morphism; R_L is torsion-free over the DVR R, hence flat, so this is fpqc. These arguments require neither excellence nor unramifiedness. In the explicit counterexample R_L=C[[t]] gives the ramified cover, and L tensor_K L = L x L separates Spec L into two generic singleton pieces. With H=S each singleton has diagonal an isomorphism. This directly repairs the failed test.
