I checked B–E with the files and sources open. The item's hypotheses are stronger than those used in 0AGB (regular and Noetherian, nowhere dense Z), and integral X with proper Z makes the support nowhere dense. The dimension-two hypothesis is used only for the final residual-support conclusion. Neither excellence, separatedness, a field base, catenarity nor an ample sheaf is introduced. The new node agrees with 0AGA/0AG9: regular local rings are UFDs by 0AG0, an integral prime divisor has height-one prime stalk ideals, and their generators are prime. Its local-ring identification A_(f) is precisely the one used in the middle paragraph of 0AGB. The generic DVR has maximal ideal generated by f. The new node has its own objects and notions, no proof, no proof dependencies, full source tags, actual searches and blueprint ancestry. It is not an elementary lemma: Cartier principality for a prime divisor uses the substantive regular-local UFD theorem and the stalk-to-neighbourhood argument.

Step 1 cites the actual ideal-sheaf affine description, integral-scheme definition, Noetherian minimal-closed-subset theorem, finite-component theorem, scheme sobriety, and reduced-induced-closed-subscheme theorem. I opened every statement. In particular, the reduced-induced result explicitly provides a quasi-coherent radical ideal and a reduced closed subscheme; integral means reduced and irreducible in the cited definition. No generic point of a closed subset is assumed without the sobriety citation. Noetherianity of a closed subspace is the immediate restriction of its descending chain condition. The exclusion of the generic point is proved using injectivity into the fraction field, not assumed for an arbitrary nonreduced ambient scheme.

Step 2 uses exactly the new node's Cartier and generic-DVR assertions. The DVR definition actually states that every ideal is principal, and the cited existing DVR arithmetic statement actually writes each nonzero element as a unit times a power of the prescribed nonzero generator of the maximal ideal. These statements give a_i without a missing ideal-classification lemma: first choose a generator, then factor it. Nonzeroness uses the integral affine domain and properness of Z; properness of the stalk ideal follows from xi_i in Z. No zero ideal is passed to the element-factorization theorem.

Step 3 is the explicit elementary product calculation. The effective-Cartier definition supplies local nonzerodivisor generators; the product is a nonzerodivisor because a composite of injective maps is injective. Quasi-coherence is checked on principal affine restrictions of the finite product. An independent theorem on sums of Cartier divisors is unnecessary; the cited 01WT/01WU source calculation is written in the step.

Step 4 uses the new node's actual prime-generator and localization clauses, not a mere definition of Cartier ideal. I checked the contraction calculation: s h=f_i^a b with s outside (f_i) implies f_i^a divides h by induction using primeness and cancellation in a domain. The product division is another finite induction because distinct prime-divisor stalk ideals are distinct primes. These are short algebraic inductions, not a hidden codimension-one-part theorem. In particular, no localization-unit argument is applied to a denominator inside the prime. This is the middle argument of 0AGB written out.

Step 5 proves the factorization instead of citing a restatement of it. The intrinsic condition aL contained in I defines a sheaf. On an affine Cartier chart, division by f identifies J with the inverse image of I in A under A isomorphic to fA. The finite generators h_j=f g_j give the finite generators g_j of J. The displayed denominator criterion checks localization, giving quasi-coherence by the cited definition. Unit changes of the local equation do not change this ideal. This avoids assuming without explanation that an arbitrary sheaf quotient is an ideal or that local choices glue.

Step 6 uses the generalization-chain statement, which actually identifies local dimension with chain length. A codimension-one point of Z must be a component generic point, since a strict intervening point would give a length-two chain. At each such generic point Step 2 and the unit other factors give I=L; cancellation gives J=O. Outside Z the equality I=LJ and I=O force J=O. I made explicit that codimension zero means the single generic point in integral X. This supplies every claimed support containment.

Step 7 uses the exact closed-support assertion of the finite-type quasi-coherent module theorem, applied to the explicitly locally cyclic quotient. The same chain formula bounds local dimensions by two and proves that codimension-two points have no strict specializations. I then use the actual finite-component and generic-point statements to deduce finiteness. This avoids the false shortcut that an arbitrary collection of closed points in a Noetherian scheme must be finite: closedness of T is established first. No source is used only by name; citations.json quotes each cited statement verbatim.

Small cases: for Z empty, r=0 and I=J=L=O; for an effective Cartier divisor, J=O and T is empty; for a punctual subscheme r=0 and J=I. In Spec k[x,y], the ideal x(x,y) gives L=(x), J=(x,y), and the origin as the single residual point. Powers (x^a) verify the multiplicity choice. For a reducible Cartier support such as (x^2 y^3), the distinct prime equations and product cancellation give precisely the two exponents and no residual.
