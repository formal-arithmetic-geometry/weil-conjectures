### lem-sfb-reduction-exact-inverse-image — original step 10

Finally let $u^{-1}$ be an exact inverse-image functor as in the statement. Since it is a left adjoint preserving finite limits, it preserves the constant rings: a constant object is the coproduct of copies of the final object, and it preserves this coproduct and the final object. Exactness preserves $\ker n$, $\operatorname{coker}n$ and both short exact sequences, including the injectivity/epimorphy hypotheses. The ringed-topoi tensor-pullback theorem says that $u^{-1}P$ is again flat-term K-flat, so the exactness of $u^{-1}$ makes it a resolution of $u^{-1}A$. Its scalar-reduction complex is
$$u^{-1}(P/nP)=(u^{-1}P)/n(u^{-1}P).$$
Indeed exactness preserves the cokernel and the tensor-pullback comparison identifies the two scalar-extension models. The termwise units $P\to P/nP$ are preserved by this equality. The exact-functor factorization gives the termwise triangulated action on derived categories. Explicitly, for a short exact sequence $0\to X\xrightarrow{a}Y\xrightarrow{b}T\to0$, let $q:C(a)\to T$ be $b$ on the $Y$ summand and zero on $X[1]$, and let $p:C(a)\to X[1]$ be the projection; its canonical boundary is $-p\circ q^{-1}$ (Stacks Tag 0152, label lemma-derived-canonical-delta-functor). Additivity identifies $u^{-1}C(a)$ with $C(u^{-1}a)$, with exactly the same differential matrix, and sends $q,p$ to the corresponding maps $q',p'$. Exactness makes $u^{-1}q$ a quasi-isomorphism, so applying $u^{-1}$ to the boundary gives $-p'\circ(q')^{-1}$, the boundary of the pulled-back sequence. It also commutes with the truncation zigzags because it preserves kernels and images. The equation $(-i)H^{-1}(t)=H^{-1}(\delta_B)$ is consequently preserved, so uniqueness in step 6 preserves the normalized $\alpha_B$ as well. Thus $\alpha_F$, $\alpha_B$, $d_{S,n}$ and the displayed adjunct formula all commute with $u^{-1}$ under the induced constant-ring identifications.
