## Algebraic arguments for the new statements

For lem-per-tame-inertia-surjective-group-trace, I read Stacks more-algebra.tex, Tag 0BRH (lemma-base-change-invariants), 0BRI (lemma-one-orbit), and 0BRJ (lemma-one-orbit-geometric), including their proofs. These tags supply flat localization of invariants and transitivity of the primes over a fixed invariant prime; the claimed trace criterion is an agent consequence, not their literal statement. The source field-trace input was also read in fields.tex, lemma-separable-trace-pairing: separable finite extensions have nonzero, hence surjective, trace.

Here is a full argument for this auxiliary statement. Put A=R^G and T(r)=sum_g g(r); T(R) is an ideal of A. The orbit polynomial product_g (Z-g(r)) proves integrality of R over A. Fix a maximal ideal m of A, localize at A\m and write R' for the resulting ring. Flat localization identifies (R')^G=A_m by 0BRH and commutes with the group sum. Integrality and 0BRI say that the maximal ideals of R' are the finitely many primes above m and form a single G-orbit. Choose one q and let D be its stabilizer. Its residue field L=R'/q has the D-action, with kernel I equal to the inertia group at the corresponding prime of R. The finite image D/I acts faithfully on L; Artin's fixed-field theorem makes L/L^D a finite Galois extension with this group. The field trace to L^D is surjective, so choose c in L with sum over D/I of its conjugates equal to 1/|I|. The scalar |I| is invertible by the hypothesis. The Chinese remainder theorem gives r in R' having residue c at q and zero at all other maximal ideals. Reducing T(r) at q leaves exactly sum_{d in D} d(c)=1. Since T(r) is invariant, its residue is 1 at every maximal ideal of R'. Thus T(r) is a unit of R', and its inverse is invariant, so is a unit of A_m. Consequently the localized trace ideal is all of A_m. This holds for every maximal m; if T(R) were proper it would be contained in a maximal ideal, a contradiction. Hence T(R)=A and some a has trace one. The zero ring is included: there are no primes and its unique element has trace 1=0. No finite generation, Noetherianity, separability of k(q)/k(m), or invertibility of |G| was used. Only L/L^D, which is always separable, was used for the trace.

For lem-per-trace-one-noetherian-invariant-finiteness, I read ILO VI 3.7(b), printed p.75, the sentence asserting the finite strict-local invariant quotient; this motivates the input but does not state its trace-one generalization. Its source field explicitly says the proof is agent-supplied. Here is a complete algebraic argument that avoids any finiteness assumption on a base.

Let S=R*G be the skew group ring: its elements are finite sums r_g[g], with (r[g])(s[h])=r g(s)[gh]. Because G is finite, S is finite free as a right R-module; R is Noetherian, so ascending chains of right ideals of S terminate. Choose a with T(a)=1 and set e=sum_g g(a)[g]. Then [h]e=e, the coefficient sum of e is 1, and e^2=e. The usual left S-action on R is (sum r_g[g])r=sum r_g g(r). The maps R -> S, r -> r e, and S -> R, sum r_g[g] -> sum r_g, give an S-linear splitting of the latter map. In particular Se=R e and r -> r e is injective. For s in S write e s e=r e. Applying [h] and using [h]e=e shows h(r)e=r e; injectivity shows r is invariant. Conversely b e=e b e for b in A. Thus eSe=A e, as rings, and Se=R e identifies with R as a right A-module.

For completeness, Se is a Noetherian right eSe-module: if N is any right eSe-submodule of Se, let NS be the right ideal it generates in S. Then (NS)e=N. Indeed n=ne implies n s e=n(e s e) is in N, and n=n e gives the reverse inclusion. A strict ascending chain of such submodules therefore gives a strict ascending chain of right ideals NS of S, impossible. Thus Se is finitely generated over eSe, proving that R is finite over A. To see A is Noetherian directly, use the A-linear retraction rho(r)=T(ar), for which rho(1)=1. Given an ideal J of A, Noetherianity of R supplies finitely many j_i in J generating JR: choose the finite subset of J needed for a generating set of JR. If b is in J, write b=sum_i j_i r_i and apply rho to obtain b=sum_i j_i rho(r_i). Hence J is generated in A by the j_i. This proves A Noetherian without invoking Eakin's theorem. All rings and actions here are exactly those of the auxiliary node. No faithfulness or reducedness was assumed.
