## New lemma: source and mathematical check

I read Varshavsky §§5.1.1–5.1.5 and §§5.2.1–5.2.2 (printed pp.39–42). Section 5.1.5 supplies the filtered Hom construction and its H^0 and grade-zero projection interpretation. The new node is an agent lemma about its naturality, not a claim that Varshavsky states this exact-adjunction theorem verbatim. I also read Bhatt–Scholze §5.1, especially Corollary 5.1.6 and Remark 5.1.8: the unit is invertible on bounded-below objects, and no unbounded-unit assertion is used.

Here is why the new statement is true and suitable for a separate split. For an exact adjunction L ⊣ R, R preserves injectives: Hom(-,RI)=Hom(L(-),I) is exact. A termwise filtered-injective object splits as a finite sum of pure injectives; applying additive R preserves this split filtration and gives gr(RI)=R(gr I). Resolve LQ by a bounded-below filtered-injective J in the same finite filtration interval. The derived adjunction unit Q→R^F L^F Q, represented by Q→RJ, is a graded quasi-isomorphism: on each grade it is exactly the assumed ordinary unit. Thus RJ is a filtered-injective resolving model of Q. The adjunction identifies the shift-filtered Hom complexes Hom_A^•(P,RJ) and Hom_B^•(LP,J), in every filtration step, not merely their H^0 groups. Indeed maps sending F^pP into F^{p+r}RJ correspond exactly to maps sending L(F^pP)=F^p(LP) into F^{p+r}J, by naturality of the unit/counit for filtration inclusions. The chain identification respects the Hom differential. Resolution comparison then gives the canonical isomorphism in DF^+(Ab). Restriction to filtration subcomplexes and quotienting by the next step commute with this identification, so it gives the ordinary graded Hom comparisons and both asserted projections. This is the non-elementary comparison argument extracted from the last sentence of the old step 12; no proof is placed in the new node's proof field.

The hypotheses matter. Enough injectives is required on both sides. L is exact, not merely additive. The ordinary derived unit is required for every bounded-below object, not just degree-zero sheaves. Cochain bounds and filtration bounds are separate, and the latter are uniform. For the application, L=ν_X^*, R=ν_{X,*}; sk-prop-sf-comparison supplies exactly that derived unit. The ringed-site Grothendieck and enough-injectives nodes supply the categorical hypotheses. No constructibility hypothesis is needed in this generic lemma.

Small cases checked: L=identity gives the identity comparison; P=0 or Q=0 gives the zero Hom complex; a single pure filtration degree reduces to ordinary derived adjunction with unit; two pure objects in degrees p,q have Hom in filtration degree q−p, agreeing with the sign in the statement; shifted complexes preserve this calculation. A nonexact left adjoint is expressly excluded, so tensoring over a nonflat ring map is not an accidental example. The infinitely descending or cochain-dependent finite-filtration cases are excluded.
