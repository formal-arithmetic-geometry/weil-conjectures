Its intended proof first chooses a nonzero primitive residue, applies the Chinese remainder theorem to lift it at the selected maximal ideal and zero at the others, then perturbs by a sufficiently small scalar times a primitive field generator. Only finitely many scalars fail primitiveness: distinct embeddings impose at most one forbidden scalar per pair. This is a reusable approximation lemma, not the converse or a clause of the item.

For the henselian valuation node I read local algebra.tex Tags 04GH (lemma-finite-over-henselian) and 04GK (lemma-henselian-cat-finite-etale), including the finite-type quasi-finite local-factor argument. Etale gives quasi-finiteness at the closed point, so its localization is a finite local direct factor and remains etale. Normality plus the henselian valuation extension theorem identifies it with the unique integral-closure valuation ring. Finite etale gives a finite free module; reduction identifies its rank with the residue degree. For a residue generator y, the residue powers 1,y,...,y^(f-1) form a basis; their lifts form a basis by the local finite-free determinant criterion. Expressing y^f in this basis gives the monic minimal polynomial with that reduction, and separability makes its derivative a unit. Thus the last clause is a polynomial-basis assertion, rather than an unjustified claim that every element generates.
