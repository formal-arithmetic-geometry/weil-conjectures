## Review by Q81

I read BLR §4.4 proof of Theorem 1 on printed pp.109–110 in the primary scan, particularly the paragraph where the diagonal is locally defined by d equations and the divisorial boundary is intersected with it. I also read Stacks divisors.tex Tag 067R, lemma-section-smooth-regular-immersion, including its local nature without separatedness; algebra.tex Tag 0FCC, lemma-depth-localization (lines 18023–18026 and its proof); Tag 00KQ's dimension/ideal-of-definition proposition and the existing 00KW height-quotient node; and the statements of every cited node. The new node states exactly 0FCC, including arbitrary finite M and the case M_p=0 of infinite depth. It makes no unproved scheme identification part of its statement.

Step 1 uses the finite irreducible-component theorem twice, for E inside T and F inside W, and the scheme sobriety theorem for a generic point of a component of E. These statements apply to both schemes because they are Noetherian. The generic point of a maximal irreducible closed subset has no strict generalization within E: a strict generalization would have a larger irreducible closure. This fact, rather than a closed-point choice, is essential for the primary ideal in step 3. The component C of F containing x has the stated height-one generic point by the item's hypothesis, and every neighbourhood of x contains that generic point.

Step 2 reads the actual statement of lem-sfb-smooth-section-regular-immersion: it gives an affine quotient chart and a finite regular sequence, allowing an empty list and no global separation. The affine-open-stalk-localization node identifies the local rings at both x and xi with the appropriate prime localizations of the same B. The weakly-regular-sequence-localization-prime node applies because all the section equations lie in P and have the injective multiplication property. The equality R=A/I is written by mutually inverse fraction formulas; it is not an assertion that an arbitrary set-theoretic containment factors a scheme map. The section here is already the supplied quotient immersion. The iterated localization A_p=B_p0 is by fractions, so dim A_p=1 is precisely the item's codimension hypothesis.

Step 3 cites only the affine spectrum and prime-localization definitions and writes the prime correspondences out. A prime of A over p+I is exactly a generalization of x on C and on the quotient section. Its section preimage is therefore a generalization of z in E, so it must be z. This proves radical(p+I)=m_A. The quotient is nonzero because p+I⊆m_A; its sole prime is maximal, hence its dimension is zero. I checked that no claim of scheme-theoretic factorization is deduced merely from point containment. This step is elementary spectrum/quotient/localization algebra.

Step 4 uses the precise height-quotient-bound node with the Noetherian local ring A/p, its maximal prime, and r images of the section equations; the resulting quotient prime has height zero by step 3. It follows that dim(A/p)≤r even without catenarity. The support-depth node applies to the nonzero finite A_p-module A_p and gives depth(A_p)≤dim(A_p)=1. The only new statement, 0FCC, then applies to the finite A-module A and its prime p, yielding depth(A)≤r+1. These are three different statements; none is substituted for a definition of depth. No zero localization case occurs here because A_p is a local ring at an actual point.

Step 5 checks each intermediate A_i is a nonzero Noetherian local quotient and each next equation lies in its maximal ideal and is a nonzerodivisor. The depth-drop theorem states the depth of the quotient as an A_i-module; the separately cited quotient-depth theorem identifies that with its own local-ring depth. Iterating gives depth(A)=r+depth(R). Thus depth(R)≤1. The normal-local-depth-two node has exactly the hypotheses of R=O_T,z when dim R≥2 and rules this out. This is where normality of the base is used; I do not substitute a theorem requiring regularity or factoriality. Normality and integrality of W are stronger than the local height/depth argument needs, and remain in the unchanged statement.

Step 6 is contraposition and tracks the chosen z. For F empty there is nothing to prove. For r=0 the iteration is an identity and the same bound proves the conclusion. Over a field any nonempty inverse image already has a dimension-zero point. A section contained in a component of F also causes no problem: p+I still has the stated primary property at the chosen generic point, and the algebra forces base codimension zero or one. Zero finite modules and zero localized modules satisfy the new 0FCC inequality with infinite depth. For M=R over a field it reads 0≤0; over a DVR, the generic and closed primes give the expected 1≤1 bounds. I checked nonseparated q is harmless because only a neighbourhood where the section is closed is used, and no relative dimension is chosen globally.
