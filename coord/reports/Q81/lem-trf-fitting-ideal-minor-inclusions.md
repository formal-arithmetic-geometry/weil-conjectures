## Former proof retained verbatim

The following is the baseline proof used for this repair, so its arguments and citations remain available to the maintainer and subsequent workers.

Former step 1:

For an ideal generated by a set of determinants, it suffices to put each generating determinant in the proposed target ideal: that ideal is closed under sums and multiplication by ring elements. When $r=0$, all three conclusions hold since their target is $J_0(A)=R$. Hence assume $r\ge1$. Whenever a source ideal has no minors of the indicated size it is zero, so its asserted inclusion is automatic. This treats empty row sets, empty column sets, and all oversized-minor cases without choosing nonexistent indices.

Former step 2:

For (1), take an $(r+1)\times(r+1)$ submatrix $Q$ of $A$ with its rows and columns in increasing order. Fix any column $j$ of $Q$. By [lem-weil-pbc-matrix-adjugate-identity], the $(j,j)$ entry of $\operatorname{adj}(Q)\,Q=\det(Q)\cdot1$ reads $\det Q=\sum_i\operatorname{adj}(Q)_{ji}Q_{ij}=\sum_i(-1)^{i+j}Q_{ij}\det Q^{(i,j)}$, where $Q^{(i,j)}$ deletes row $i$ and column $j$ of $Q$ (the expansion of $\det Q$ along its column $j$). Every cofactor is the determinant of an $r\times r$ submatrix of $A$, so belongs to $J_r(A)$. The coefficients are signed entries of $Q$, hence elements of $R$. Closure of the ideal under finite sums and scalar multiplication gives $\det Q\in J_r(A)$. Applying Step 1 to all generators yields $J_{r+1}(A)\subseteq J_r(A)$.

Former step 3:

For (2), fix $r\ge1$ and induct on $d$, with the claim quantified over all $p,n,A,B$ as in the statement. At $d=0$ one has $B=A$ and the inclusion is equality. For the induction step, let $B$ have $n+d+1$ columns and let $D$ be its first $n+d$ columns. The induction hypothesis gives $J_{r+d}(D)\subseteq J_r(A)$. Take a square submatrix $Q$ of $B$ of size $r+d+1$. If it omits the last column of $B$, its determinant lies in $J_{r+d+1}(D)\subseteq J_{r+d}(D)$ by Step 2 applied to $D$ with index $r+d$. If it contains that last column, let $j$ be its position in $Q$ and expand $\det Q$ along column $j$ by the $(j,j)$ entry of the identity $\operatorname{adj}(Q)\,Q=\det(Q)\cdot1$ of [lem-weil-pbc-matrix-adjugate-identity], exactly as in Step 2. Deleting it and any selected row leaves an $(r+d)\times(r+d)$ submatrix of $D$, so every cofactor belongs to $J_{r+d}(D)$. Thus in both cases $\det Q\in J_{r+d}(D)\subseteq J_r(A)$. Taking generators proves $J_{r+d+1}(B)\subseteq J_r(A)$ and completes the induction. If there is no such $Q$, the inclusion follows from Step 1.

Former step 4:

For (3), choose row and column sets $I\subseteq\{1,\ldots,p\}$ and $J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\}$ with $|I|=|J|=r$, both ordered increasingly. Let $A_I$ be the $r\times n$ matrix formed by those rows of $A$, and $C_J$ the $n\times r$ matrix formed by those columns of $C$. Entrywise, the chosen submatrix of $AC$ is $A_I C_J$, because both entries are $\sum_{h=1}^n A_{i,h}C_{h,j}$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-cauchy-binet] with its $m=r$, its $n=n$, and matrices $A_I,C_J$. It gives $$\det((AC)_{I,J})=\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,n\},\ |S|=r}\det(A_{I,S})\det(C_{S,J}).$$ Every first determinant is an $r$-minor of $A$ and hence belongs to $J_r(A)$; every second determinant is an element of $R$. Therefore the displayed sum belongs to $J_r(A)$. Taking the generated ideal proves $J_r(AC)\subseteq J_r(A)$. If $r>\min(p,n)$, both source and target have no such minors and are zero, as already treated in Step 1.
