For the first new input I checked the semilocal (not merely local) wording of the G-ring LLP in Kurano–Shimomoto Appendix A: it applies to D_gamma, which is Noetherian and x-complete and has finitely many maximal ideals identified through D_gamma/x = (A/x)_gamma. Its quotient is a G-ring by localization. The regular transition assertion can be checked at a maximal ideal indexed by m in gamma: the maximal completions of (D_delta)_(m D_delta), (D_gamma)_(m D_gamma) and A_m are canonically the same maximal completion. D_delta is a G-ring, so its localized map to that completion is regular. The map from the localized D_gamma to its maximal completion is faithfully flat; regularity descends, and the maximal-local criterion gives the whole transition. This uses only semilocal G-ring LLP, not global quasi-excellence lifting. The statement includes no normality, universal catenarity or characteristic assumption. I tested x=0 (all D_gamma are ordinary semilocalizations of a G-ring), x nilpotent, gamma=delta, and a singleton gamma. Empty gamma is deliberately not claimed, and is not required by the item.

For the second new input I checked a full proof before stating it. Put H=E tensor_R S. A nonzero common denominator d for the finite birational algebra E makes E[1/d]=R[1/d]. Flatness gives S embedded in H and makes d a non-zero-divisor on both; hence H is finite and birational over S. Regularity is preserved by the finite base change to E, and Stacks 0BFK (more-algebra.tex, lemma-normal-goes-up) makes H Noetherian normal. Thus H is a finite product of normal domains. Each minimal prime P of S has its corresponding component of H, since after inverting d the map is an isomorphism and d misses all minimal primes. The image of x lies in every maximal ideal of H by integrality over S. In that component choose a prime minimal over x; x is nonzero there because E -> H is flat. The principal ideal theorem gives height one. Its contraction Q in E contains the nonzero x and has height at most one by flat going-down; hence Q is minimal over xE. Contract the selected prime to q in S. This q contains the specified P and f(x), and its contraction to R equals Q intersect R, exactly the statement. I tested S=0 (vacuous), S=R with R normal, a finite product of copies of a complete DVR, and splitting of a domain after regular base change. The nonzero-x and Jacobson hypotheses prevent a component without a boundary; neither is dropped. This lemma does not mention Gamma, singular primes or stabilization, so it is genuinely an intermediate fact, not the item renamed.
