## Complete retained argument for subsequent workers

### Original step 1

First verify that $\underline D$ is a sheaf on the profinite site. Continuous maps out of a finite disjoint union are exactly tuples of continuous maps; the empty union gives a singleton. For a continuous surjection $q:S\to T$ of profinite spaces, use the compact-to-Hausdorff quotient theorem. A continuous map $b:S\to D$ equal on $S\times_T S$ defines a unique function $c:T\to D$ with $cq=b$. For every open $O\subset D$, $q^{-1}(c^{-1}O)=b^{-1}O$ is open, hence $c^{-1}O$ is open by the quotient property. Thus the equalizer condition holds, and the profinite coherent-sheaf criterion applies. Independently, the fpqc continuous-map sheaf theorem, restricted to the small pro-etale site (whose covers are fpqc), gives the sheaf $F_{D,X}$.

Citations: lem-sfb-compact-hausdorff-surjection-quotient, lem-sfb-profinite-coherent-sheaf-criterion, lem-sfb-set-continuous-functions-fpqc-sheaf, def-weil-pbc-proetale-site.
Source: literature/bhatt-scholze-pro-etale-topology_arXiv-1309.1198.pdf, Lemmas 4.3.2(1) and 4.2.12, quotient-map descent; Example 3.1 of Hemo--Richarz--Scholbach.

### Original step 2

For a qcqs object $U$, its space is spectral and therefore its component quotient $q_U:|U|\to\pi_0(U)$ is profinite, by the two cited statements. A continuous $s:|U|\to D$ is constant on each connected component: its image there is connected, and a totally disconnected space has no connected subset with two points. Consequently $s=\bar s q_U$ uniquely. The quotient topology makes $\bar s$ continuous, since $q_U^{-1}(\bar s^{-1}O)=s^{-1}O$ for each open $O$. Conversely composition with $q_U$ is continuous. These inverse operations give the asserted component formula; they commute with precomposition in $U$ and postcomposition in $D$.

Citations: lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-scheme-spectral, lem-pe-spectral-components-profinite.
Source: literature/bhatt-scholze-pro-etale-topology_arXiv-1309.1198.pdf, Lemma 4.2.12, second assertion; proof, last paragraph.

### Original step 3

Write $X[S]$ for the relative test object of the cited functor theorem. Its canonical identification with $X\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z}a(S)$ follows from its finite-quotient presentation: pulling back $\coprod_{S_i}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbf Z$ gives $\coprod_{S_i}X$, because the summands are a disjoint open covering. These identifications commute with the transition maps. The pullback of the limit has the same universal property as the limit of these pullbacks: a map into either is precisely a compatible family of maps to the stages over $X$. Thus the identification is canonical and natural in $S$ and $X$. The inverse of the spectrum homeomorphism in the third citation, for $R=\mathbf Z$, followed by projection to $S$ gives a continuous map $|a(S)|\to S$, hence $r_{X,S}:|X[S]|\to S$. On finite clopen partitions this sends a prime to the unique summand containing it; precomposition of locally constant functions carries that label to its image. Hence $r_{X,T}|X[v]|=v r_{X,S}$ for $v:S\to T$, and $r_{X,S}$ commutes with base change in $X$.

Citations: lem-weil-pbc-profinite-test-object-functor, lem-weil-pbc-scheme-coproduct-family, lem-weil-pbc-locally-constant-prime-spectrum.
Source: literature/hemo-richarz-scholbach-constructible-sheaves_arXiv-2305.18131.pdf, Example 3.1, relative profinite test objects and the projection to their profinite index; compare Bhatt--Scholze Lemma 4.2.13 proof.

### Original step 4

Let $a_X(S)=X[S]$ and let $P_F=\operatorname{Lan}_{a_X^{op}}F$. The site inverse-image theorem applies to $a_X$, by its finite-limit and covering properties. Its sheafified Kan extension is the actual $p_X^*$ in the statement: for every sheaf $G$ on $X_{\mathrm{proet}}$, the composite adjunction for $\pi_X^{-1}a^*$ gives $\operatorname{Hom}(p_X^*F,G)=\operatorname{Hom}(F,(S\mapsto G(X\times a(S))))$, whereas the Kan/sheafification adjunction gives $\operatorname{Hom}(P_F^\#,G)=\operatorname{Hom}(F,(S\mapsto G(X[S])))$. Step 3 identifies the right sides, with their units. Applying these two natural bijections to identity maps yields inverse maps $p_X^*F\leftrightarrows P_F^\#$; their composites are identities because their adjoints are identities. This fixes the comparison uniquely with its unit normalization.

Source: literature/hemo-richarz-scholbach-constructible-sheaves_arXiv-2305.18131.pdf, Example 3.1, interpretation of pullback from the point; explicit adjunction calculation for the fixed inverse image.

### Original step 5

The maps $b\mapsto b\circ r_{X,S}$ define a natural transformation $\underline D(S)\to F_{D,X}(X[S])$ by the naturality equation of step 3. Kan extension and sheafification therefore give a morphism $\sigma_{D,X}:p_X^*\underline D\to F_{D,X}$, normalized by sending each test function $b$ to $b\circ r_{X,S}$. This is a genuine morphism of set sheaves, using step 1, rather than a comparison only of section sets. The adjunction bijection shows that these equations on all $S,b$ determine $\sigma_{D,X}$ uniquely.

### Original step 6

Let $U$ be a weakly contractible affine object and $T=\pi_0(|U|)$; $U$ is qcqs, so $T$ is profinite and every continuous map $|U|\to S$ to a profinite $S$ is $gq_U$ for a unique $g\in C(T,S)$ (step 2 with $D=S$). Claim: $\phi\mapsto r_{X,S}|\phi|$ is a bijection from $\operatorname{Hom}_X(U,X[S])$ onto $C(|U|,S)$. For finite $S$, $X[S]=\coprod_SX$ with disjoint open summands covering it and $r_{X,S}$ the summand label (step 3); an $X$-map $\phi:U\to\coprod_SX$ gives the open disjoint covering $U_s=\phi^{-1}(\iota_s X)$, on which $\phi=\iota_s\circ(U_s\to X)$, and conversely a continuous $g:|U|\to S$ gives open disjoint $U_s=g^{-1}(s)$ and the glued map $\iota_s\circ(U_s\to X)$ on them. For $S=\varprojlim S_i$ over its finite quotients, $X$-maps to the limit $X[S]$ and continuous maps to $S$ are compatible families over the $S_i$, matched by the naturality $r_{X,S_i}|X[p_i]|=p_ir_{X,S}$ of step 3. Let $\phi_0:U\to X[T]$ be the map with $r_{X,T}|\phi_0|=q_U$. If $r_{X,S}|\phi|=gq_U$, naturality gives $r_{X,S}|X[g]\phi_0|=gq_U$, so $\phi=X[g]\phi_0$ by the claim. Hence every element $[(S,\phi),b]$ of the Kan colimit $P_{\underline D}(U)=\operatorname{colim}_{(U\to X[S])}C(S,D)$ equals $[(T,\phi_0),bg]$. By step 5, the presheaf adjunct of $\sigma_{D,X}$ sends $[(S,\phi),b]$ to $\phi^*(br_{X,S})=br_{X,S}|\phi|$, hence $[(T,\phi_0),c]$ to $cq_U$. This map $P_{\underline D}(U)\to C(|U|,D)$ is surjective by step 2 and injective since $c\mapsto cq_U$ is injective ($q_U$ surjective). It is $\sigma_{D,X}(U)$ composed with $P_{\underline D}(U)\to P_{\underline D}^\#(U)\cong(p_X^*\underline D)(U)$ (step 4), which is bijective by the sheafification-sections theorem; so $\sigma_{D,X}(U)$ is bijective. Every site object has a covering by such affines. Thus $\sigma_{D,X}$ is locally surjective and locally injective, and the set-sheaf local-bijection theorem makes it an isomorphism.

### Original step 7

For a continuous map $h:D\to D^{\prime}$, both ways around the naturality square for $\sigma$ send a test function $b:S\to D$ to $hbr_{X,S}$, so uniqueness in step 5 gives equality. Finite products of the function sheaves are computed by $(b_i)\mapsto(s\mapsto(b_i(s))_i)$, on both the profinite and the scheme sites. The site inverse-image theorem gives finite-product preservation for $p_X^*$. The projections of the proposed product comparison are the preceding naturality squares, so they determine the comparison uniquely. For the one-point space there is exactly one section on every object; this also proves the empty-product assertion.

### Original step 8

For $f:Y\to X$, define $\rho_f:F_{D,X}\to f_*F_{D,Y}$ by $s\mapsto s\circ|\operatorname{pr}_X|$ on $V\times_X Y$. Let $c_f:f^{-1}F_{D,X}\to F_{D,Y}$ be its adjunct. Under $\sigma_{D,X}$ and $\sigma_{D,Y}$ this is the canonical comparison $f^{-1}p_X^*\underline D\to p_Y^*\underline D$ of the point-pullback theorem: transpose both maps through the composite adjunction and evaluate on $b:S\to D$. Each sends $b$ to $br_{Y,S}$, because $r_{X,S}|\operatorname{pr}_X|=r_{Y,S}$ under $Y\times_X X[S]=Y[S]$ (step 3). Injectivity of the adjunction hom bijection proves equality. Consequently $c_f$ is an isomorphism. For the identity, $s\circ\mathrm{id}=s$. For $Z\xrightarrow{g}Y\xrightarrow{f}X$, the two adjuncts act by $s\mapsto s\circ\operatorname{pr}_{VX}\circ\operatorname{pr}_{VY}$ and $s\mapsto s\circ\operatorname{pr}_{VX}$ on the canonically identified iterated pullback, hence agree. Equivalently use the identity/composition coherence already asserted for the point-pullback comparison.

### Original step 9

If $D$ is discrete, map the constant presheaf with value $D$ into $F_{D,X}$ by constant functions. Any $s:|U|\to D$ is locally in its image: the open fibres $s^{-1}\{d\}$ cover $U$, and on each fibre $s$ is the constant $d$. For two constants $d,e$ with identical functions on $U$, either $U$ is nonempty and evaluation at a point gives $d=e$, or $U$ is empty and the empty covering makes them locally equal. Thus the presheaf map is locally bijective, including $D=\varnothing$. The cited set-valued sheafification criterion and the definition of constant sheaf identify its sheafification with $F_{D,X}$. This proves the discrete clause without a finiteness assumption on $D$.
