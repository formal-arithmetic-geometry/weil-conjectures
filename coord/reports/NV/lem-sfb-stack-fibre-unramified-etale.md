In step 1 I checked the statement of the existing stack calculus lemma, not merely its title or source: it says all five listed properties are stable under arbitrary base change and composition, and explicitly says local finite presentation implies local finite type. The stack-groupoid definition gives 2-fibre products and representability. Representability of U -> X follows by expressing U x_X T as the diagonal base change along U x_S T -> X x_S X. In step 2 I checked each clause of the new locus statement: it gives the largest unramified open W, allows the arbitrary representable field base change a, and identifies its inverse image on the possibly non-surjective chart V with the union of unramified source neighbourhoods. Its citation quote is the verbatim statement giving these clauses.

In step 3 the same locus theorem says q restricted to W is unramified. Inverse-image membership gives u in W. I opened and read def-weil-flat-morphism and def-sbc-locally-finite-presentation: restriction to an open preserves the stalks and the affine-neighbourhood finite-presentation data in those definitions. The item's chart convention applies this definition to representable stack morphisms. Applying the definition on all scheme charts proves q|_W etale. I also read spaces-morphisms.tex Tag 06LU, whose assertion agrees with this conjunction; a separate node for it would be elementary under the DAG's chosen definition and is deliberately not created. The elementary final step does not need a new assertion that openness of the etale locus is automatic: the necessary open has already been supplied by the cited locus theorem.

I tested the statements on empty sources and fibres (then the item has no v), non-surjective charts, the identity field morphism, and a positive-characteristic purely inseparable finite field extension (unramifiedness fails, so no false etaleness conclusion is forced).
