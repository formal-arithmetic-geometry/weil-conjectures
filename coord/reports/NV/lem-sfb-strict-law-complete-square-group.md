## Review by NV

I read the worker rules and both copies of delivery-errors B–E in full. I checked the item against BLR 5.3 Lemma 8, printed pp.127–128: its smooth separated finite-type hypotheses, section, and dense open square are all present. The normal Noetherian strictly henselian base is the section's standing hypothesis, not a discarded restriction. The strict law requires both translation charts and density for both projections; these conditions are used exactly when choosing a generic auxiliary variable. No irreducibility or connectedness is assumed; intersections of finitely many dense opens in a nonempty fibre remain nonempty, component by component.

For the translation node I read 5.2 Lemma 4 and the preceding definition of the birational-transformation group functor, printed pp.118–119. Its statement includes the injectivity, naturality for arbitrary S-schemes T (including nonreduced ones), and compatibility on any representative domain. This is precisely what steps 1–4 use; no representability of that group functor is claimed. Multiplication in that functor is composition, with the order tau_x tau_y giving the left translation by xy.

For the graph node I read 5.3 Lemmas 2 and 3 and the following interpretation, printed pp.120–121. All three q_ij are open immersions, their images are dense for both projections, and division is obtained from q_13. The maximal-domain assertions prevent the C4 error of using a mere definition of a rational map as a regularity theorem. Step 1 invokes these properties; step 2 uses birationality of a -> ba and a -> a^{-1}c after the relevant residue-field extension; step 3 uses a -> b^{-1}a and a -> a^{-1}c. The conclusion about all graph points records the relation needed after the maximal domains become the entire square.

For the regularity node I read BLR 2.5's definition and graph description of dom and Proposition 5, printed pp.55–58. I deliberately used flat pullback of the source, which is Proposition 5, rather than silently replacing it with base change of the base (Proposition 6). Its statement says both that all representatives glue uniquely and that dom(phi p)=p^{-1}dom(phi). Thus it supplies the existence and uniqueness used in steps 2, 3 and 5. The hypotheses are verified: Z^2, Z^3 and the open E or E' are smooth finite type over the Noetherian S; an open in a Noetherian finite-type scheme is again finite type. The two projections are open restrictions of a smooth product projection and have a nonempty fibre at every point by the explicitly checked dense conditions. The node explicitly includes this open-product case, so no unrecorded flatness-preservation theorem is being taken from a definition node.

In step 2 the outer multiplication is defined because both inner outputs lie in U, not merely because some unspecified representative exists. The cover E is formed from this exact condition. Its formula has translation (tau_b tau_a)(tau_a^{-1} tau_c)=tau_b tau_c. Equality of the two rational representatives follows from the injective translation map, with the common domain fibrewise dense; no equality of only geometric points is substituted for equality of morphisms. The regularity lemma then gives mu. In step 3, mu is now everywhere defined, so the two division outputs need not lie in U. Its formula has translation (tau_b^{-1} tau_a)(tau_a^{-1} tau_c)=tau_b^{-1} tau_c. This supplies delta and both full graph domains. I wrote this second argument explicitly instead of citing the source's phrase indicating a similar argument.

Step 4 is elementary algebra inside the already cited faithful translation groups: e=delta(z0,z0) and iota(x)=delta(x,e) are actual scheme morphisms, since delta is everywhere defined. For every test scheme T their translations are respectively 1 and tau_x^{-1}. Injectivity proves the five group diagrams, including associativity and both sides of the unit and inverse laws. Evaluating universal points makes these equalities of scheme morphisms, so no separate representability theorem or Yoneda isomorphism is assumed. Step 5 uses the regularity lemma's explicit uniqueness clause for the multiplication; uniqueness of identity and inverse is written out as two elementary equations. These are the reasons steps 4–5 are elementary, rather than a label concealing a geometric lemma.

I tested the statements on the trivial group, a disconnected finite constant group, and a group scheme itself with U=Z. In each case the graph projections and the two cover arguments work, and the constructions return the usual unit and inverse independently of z0. Empty test schemes are harmless. The nonempty-fibre hypothesis is indispensable to selecting auxiliary points. Affine-line multiplication with zero fails strictness, as noted above, and therefore is not a counterexample. Flat pullback is used only in the regularity lemma, with no unsupported claim about arbitrary nonflat preservation of maximal domains.
