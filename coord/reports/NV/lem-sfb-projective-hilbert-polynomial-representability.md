I read the item against Grothendieck's primary Expose 221 PDF: the polynomial decomposition preceding Theorem 3.2, the Grassmannian injection before Lemma 3.3, the rank-stratum construction of Lemmas 3.4--3.6, and the DVR extension in Lemma 3.7. The source of the rank-stratum node is Lemma 3.4 itself, not the Hilbert representability theorem it helps prove.

For step 1 I checked the statements of the closed-immersion and flat-morphism base-change nodes, and clause (3) of qcqs-morphism-permanence: the last really includes finite presentation, whereas the similarly named ring finite-presentation node would not state the scheme-level assertion. The new polynomial locality statement supplies residue-field invariance and the decomposition on arbitrary tests. The decomposition is by locally constant polynomial, not by assuming each test connected.

For step 2 the uniform-section statement gives exact locally free quotients, arbitrary base change, ideal evaluation generation, and multiplication surjectivity in every degree at least m. The Grassmannian node applies to the globally free module of all ambient degree-m monomials. This choice is essential: the sections of E itself need not be locally free because E/S is not assumed flat. Invalid ranks are handled by the empty representing scheme.

For step 3 I opened lem-per-projective-closed-subscheme-homogeneous-ideal and used exactly its presentation of a closed subscheme over an arbitrary affine ring by a homogeneous ideal. I do not claim it gives a global homogeneous presentation over nonaffine S. All ideals are formed over affine base opens; the rank-stratum node is applied over the Noetherian Grassmannian to a finite graded module over its polynomial algebra. Its conclusion is a finite-type immersion with an arbitrary-test-scheme factorization property. No unrestricted infinite intersection of open subsets is asserted to be open by hand.

For step 4 the graded-tail node supplies flatness, finite presentation and fibre polynomial for Proj, even with an unsaturated homogeneous ideal. The equations include the ideal of E. In the opposite direction the multiplication clause of the uniform-section node identifies every degree at least m, so the map really factors through the rank stratum. For step 5 I checked the potentially delicate inverse over nilpotent bases: the natural restriction V_m -> H^0(O_Z(m)) is surjective by the same bound, factors through Q_m -> B_m, and both intermediate bundles have the same prescribed rank. Surjective equal-rank matrices are invertible. Equality of the resulting ideal sheaves follows from evaluation generation, so this is equality of closed subschemes, including their nilpotents. The naturality uses the stated arbitrary-base-change clauses, not a fibrewise bijection alone.

For step 6 I opened the statements of lem-pc-representable-unique and lem-weil-pbc-relative-glueing-affine-opens. The former supplies the unique overlap maps; their uniqueness gives precisely the identity and cocycle conditions required by the latter. Intersections of affine opens are treated by affine refinements, not assumed affine. The constructed Y is of finite type and separated over the Noetherian base because it is locally a locally closed subscheme of a finite-type separated Grassmannian. This verifies the hypotheses needed by the DVR criterion later.

For step 7 I compared the contraction argument with Lemma 3.7. On an affine chart C of E_R, C/J injects into the prescribed generic quotient, hence is torsion-free. I opened lem-trf-valuation-torsionfree-flat; its statement applies to every module over a valuation ring, so it applies even when that quotient is not finite as an R-module. Finite presentation here comes from the Noetherian scheme E_R. Localized contractions glue. The new polynomial-locality lemma on connected Spec R ensures the closed fibre has exactly P. Conversely flatness makes multiplication by a nonzero R-scalar injective, forcing the contracted ideal and proving uniqueness. R/S need not be excellent or finite type.

For step 8 I read lem-sfb-noetherian-dvr-properness: its statement allows arbitrary DVRs and arbitrary generic maps, exactly as verified. I also read proper-to-separated, proper-immersion-closed, closed-immersion-comp, and projective-implies-proper. First Y is proper over S, then its map to the separated Grassmannian is proper, then that immersion is closed. Only then do I compose the closed immersion with Plücker. The result is H-projectivity into finite projective space, rather than only projective-bundle projectivity.

For step 9 the polynomial-locality lemma permits every locally constant polynomial assignment on a disconnected or non-quasi-compact test scheme. The maps on its clopen pieces glue to the disjoint union. The base-change assertion is obtained by identifying the actual closed subschemes of E_T on every S'-test T, which gives the same classifying maps and universal family. The representable-unique node supplies the canonical comparison. It would be incorrect to apply the original theorem over S' by assuming S' Noetherian; the proof does not do that.

I tested n=0, the empty base, E empty, the empty subscheme (P=0), P outside possible ranks, Grassmannian ranks 0 and N, disconnected test schemes with different polynomials, nilpotent tests, and the nonflat ambient E over S. The rank and Proj nodes allow degree-zero equations, which is necessary when E is empty on some base components. The DVR argument uses contractions rather than reduced closures, preserving nilpotent structure. Every new node is statement-only and self-contained; no source tag supplies an unstated definition. All proof steps are below 2000 characters; no long-step exception is needed. citations.json contains one verbatim quoted statement for each step/node pair. I checked that each quoted statement supplies exactly the interface described above.
