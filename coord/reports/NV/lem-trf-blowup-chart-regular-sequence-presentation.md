Steps 1-2 are the complete proposed argument for lem-trf-polynomial-regular-sequence-syzygies; Step 3 is the complete proposed argument for lem-trf-koszul-relations-quotient-torsionfree (replace a by f_1 and y_i by t_i for its general statement); Step 4 is the complete proposed argument for lem-trf-blowup-chart-presentation-power-torsion; Step 5 is the final assembly. ### Original step 1

Put $S=R[y_2,\ldots,y_s]$, $f_1=a$, $f_i=a_i$ for $i\ge2$, $q_i=ay_i-a_i$, $J=(q_2,\ldots,q_s)$ and $Q=S/J$. For each prefix ideal $K=(f_1,\ldots,f_{j-1})$ in $R$, a polynomial lies in $KS$ exactly when all its coefficients lie in $K$: one implication follows by multiplying coefficients; for the converse, express each of the finitely many coefficients as a sum of multiples of the generators of $K$ and group these sums into polynomials. Consequently $S/KS=(R/K)[y_2,\ldots,y_s]$. If $f_jP\in KS$, injectivity of multiplication by $f_j$ on $R/K$, supplied by [def-weil-pbc-regular-sequence], forces every coefficient of $P$ into $K$, hence $P\in KS$. Thus the images of $f_1,\ldots,f_s$ in $S$ satisfy successive injectivity. The final quotient is nonzero because $(R/I)[y_2,\ldots,y_s]$ contains the nonzero constant $1$, as $R/I\ne0$ by regularity; hence this sequence is regular in $S$. This uses no permutation of a regular sequence and no Noetherian hypothesis.

### Original step 2

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-regular-sequence-koszul-regular] to the regular sequence in the commutative ring $S$ established in Step 1, using its final clause that every regular sequence in a ring is Koszul-regular. It gives vanishing of $H_1(K_\bullet(S,f_1,\ldots,f_s))$. In particular every vector $v\in S^s$ with $\sum f_iv_i=0$ is a boundary: by [def-weil-pbc-koszul-complex] there are $b_{ij}\in S$, $1\le i<j\le s$, such that $v=\sum_{i<j} b_{ij}(f_i e_j-f_j e_i)$. Here the asserted formula uses both the displayed basis of $K_2$ and its displayed differential, not merely the name of the complex.

### Original step 3

We prove multiplication by $a$ on $Q$ is injective by writing out the degree-one Koszul calculation of Step 2. Suppose $ah=\sum_{i=2}^s c_iq_i$ in $S$. Then $v=(h-\sum_{i=2}^s c_i y_i,c_2,\ldots,c_s)$ satisfies $\sum f_i v_i=0$. Define the $S$-linear map $\lambda:S^s\to S$ by $\lambda(e_1)=1$ and $\lambda(e_i)=y_i$ for $i\ge2$, so $\lambda(v)=h$. The boundary expression in Step 2 gives $h=\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}\lambda(f_i e_j-f_j e_i)$. For $i=1$ its summand factor is $ay_j-a_j=q_j\in J$; for $2\le i<j$ it is $a_i y_j-a_j y_i=y_iq_j-y_jq_i\in J$. Hence $h\in J$, proving the claimed injectivity. Iterating this implication proves that $Q$ has no nonzero element killed by any power of $a$. The sign in this calculation is exactly $d_2(e_i\wedge e_j)=f_i e_j-f_j e_i$ of [def-weil-pbc-koszul-complex].

### Original step 4

Let $B=R[I/a]\subseteq R_a$ be the subalgebra of [def-nagata-blowup-chart-ring]. Evaluation $y_i\mapsto a_i/a$ kills every $q_i$, so gives $\phi:Q\to B$. It is onto: if $x=ra+\sum_{i=2}^s r_i a_i\in I$, then $x/a=r+\sum r_i(a_i/a)$, so the image contains every subalgebra generator. To calculate the kernel directly, write $P=\sum_\nu r_\nu y^\nu\in S$ and choose $D\ge|\nu|$ for every monomial in its finite support. Repeated multiplication of the relations $ay_i\equiv a_i\pmod J$ gives $a^DP\equiv c\pmod J$, where $c=\sum_\nu r_\nu a^{D-|\nu|}\prod_{i=2}^s a_i^{\nu_i}\in R$. If $\phi([P])=0$, its image in $R_a$ is $c/a^D=0$. By the defining equality of localization fractions, some $N\ge0$ has $a^Nc=0$ in $R$. Therefore $a^{N+D}P\in J$. Conversely an $a$-power-torsion class maps to zero because $a$ is invertible in $R_a$. Thus $\ker\phi$ is exactly the $a$-power torsion, with the denominator calculation explicit.

### Original step 5

Step 3 makes the kernel computed in Step 4 zero, and Step 4 proves surjectivity. Hence $\phi$ is an $R$-algebra isomorphism, with $[y_i]\mapsto a_i/a$ as asserted. It is canonical since an $R$-algebra map out of $Q$ is determined by the images of the displayed polynomial generators. For $s=1$ all sums and pairs are empty; the same proof says $J=0$, $Q=R$, and the chart $R[(a)/a]$ is the image of $R$ in $R_a$, identified with $R$ because $a$ is a nonzerodivisor. Thus the one-element case is included.
