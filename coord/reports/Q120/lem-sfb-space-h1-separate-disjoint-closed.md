## Review by Q120

I checked the split against delivery-errors B–E with the source and the statements of all twelve dependencies open. There are no new nodes, so no statement-only leaves, duplicate declarations, definition choices or new-node source tags remain to audit. All proof steps are below 2000 characters. The non-elementary facts are existing cited statements; the few-line consequences stay in the item.

Step 1 deals explicitly with empty subsets: a=1 when Z0 is empty and a=0 when Z1 is empty. This includes the empty scheme and the zero global ring. No nonempty-point or nonzero-ring hypothesis is introduced.

Step 2 uses precisely the existence of the reduced closed subspace in `lem-sfb-space-reduced-closed-support`, and the quasi-coherence and sheaf surjectivity clauses of `lem-sfb-space-closed-immersion-quasi-coherent-modules`. Both apply to arbitrary spaces over a scheme; no Noetherian assumption is needed. The local free-presentation definition in `def-trf-space-quasi-coherent-module` gives the structure module with zero relations and one generator. This is an elementary instance of the definition, not an extra lemma.

Step 3 uses the complement-vanishing clause of `lem-sfb-space-closed-pushforward-geometric-stalks` and the finite-limit/colimit and epimorphism-detection clauses of `lem-sfb-space-etale-conservative-geometric-points`. A geometric point cannot factor through both disjoint subspaces. If only one target stalk may be nonzero, the map to the pair is the corresponding surjective quotient map; if neither is nonzero, it is automatically surjective. Thus neither a stalk formula for a non-closed morphism nor a product-of-surjections argument with an infinite product is being smuggled in. The direct sum is finite and agrees sectionwise with the product.

Step 4 cites exactly the direct-sum and kernel closure assertions of `lem-sfb-space-qcoh-kernels-colimits`. It applies to the actual map between the quasi-coherent modules O and N from step 2. `def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category` states that kernels are computed sectionwise, which identifies K with the kernel on underlying additive sheaves. The surjectivity in step 3 was tested on the underlying sheaves, so the same sequence is short exact there. This is why the hypothesis H1(X,K)=0 is legitimate; no module-derived/additive-derived comparison is assumed.

Step 5 uses the general abelian-category statement of `lem-weil-pbc-derived-functor-long-exact`. Its source hypothesis is verified by `lem-weil-pbc-site-sheaves-enough-injectives`; its functor is additive and left exact because compatible families of sections have componentwise addition and kernels. In particular X need not itself be an object of its small scheme etale site: global sections are the limit of the chart diagram, or maps from the terminal sheaf. `lem-weil-pbc-left-exact-injective-resolution-degree-zero` supplies the canonical R0 comparison, so the exact sequence really concerns the global restriction map. H1(K)=0 makes every element of Gamma(N) lift. I did not cite the scheme-only lifting node `lem-sc-lift-sections-h1-vanishing` or the ringed-topological-space node `lem-sc-module-section-boundary-exact` for this step.

Step 6 uses only the chart-limit definition of direct image in `def-trf-space-etale-module-functors` to define its compatible zero and unit sections. The chosen lift is a lift of this actual pair, rather than an unspecified quotient element. The projections of the chart-limit definition identify its two images with restrictions to the two subspaces.

Step 7 checks the pointwise conclusion. The base-change support statement of `lem-sfb-space-reduced-closed-support` ensures that a representative field point lying in Zj has nonempty inverse image in Zj. By the scheme-test quotient description in `def-weil-closed-immersion`, a nonempty closed subscheme of Spec of a field must be all of it (the only ideals are zero and the unit ideal). Thus the representative factors through Zj and its function value is the asserted zero or one. Invariance under changing representatives follows from compatibility with field extensions and their injectivity. This is an elementary two-ideal calculation, not a new factorization lemma hidden inside the split.

Edge checks: the two empty-subset cases; X empty; Spec k; a disconnected finite union of spectra of fields; and Spec k[t] with Z0=V(t), Z1=V(t-1), where t realizes the conclusion. On the nonreduced affine example Spec k[e]/(e^2), nonempty disjoint closed subsets cannot both occur at the unique point, so the argument correctly reduces to the constant cases. For more general nonreduced spaces the kernel need not be finite type; only quasi-coherence is used. The argument does not claim that a map from a nonreduced source factors through a reduced closed subspace by set containment; the source of that final calculation is a field.
