## Review by Q120

I checked the eight-step proof against the actual Stacks source and opened all eleven distinct proof-dependency statements, as well as the eight statement-definition dependencies. In particular I checked the following applications, rather than relying on their titles or proofs.

1. The finite-type Noetherian theorem really states Noetherianity of any finite-type algebra over a Noetherian ring, which applies to the polynomial algebra. The localization definition gives the stated local ring and its maximal ideal. I wrote the contraction/extension argument to obtain Noetherianity after localization; no unstated Noetherian-localization theorem is being used.
2. The complete-local-excellence theorem assumes exactly commutative, Noetherian, local and maximal-ideal completeness. The excellence definition explicitly includes universal catenarity and explicitly defines it by finite-type algebras. These give catenarity of P. I then checked the prime intervals under localization: every intermediate prime between primes contained in n' is itself contained in n'. The formula for extension in fractions gives the inverse correspondence. This proves preservation of both bounded chain lengths and saturated-chain lengths directly, without assuming that localization preserves equidimensionality in general.
3. The minimal-prime-existence node actually gives a minimal prime below **any** prime (take I=0), so it applies to Q intersect C. The polynomial definition gives coefficientwise reduction and the lexicographic leading-monomial product verifies that the quotient by pP is a domain. The extended prime stays proper because pP is contained in n'. Its containment in q and q's minimality give equality. I checked that this proof does not assume that contraction alone preserves minimality for an arbitrary map.
4. I checked the quotient and polynomial algebras are finite type before applying the same Noetherian theorem. The quotient maximal ideal M is maximal because its quotient is P/n', and its contraction is exactly n/p. The component-ring isomorphism is given on f/s, with denominator lifts and kernel checked; there is no set-image factorization being substituted for a scheme/ring identification.
5. The flatness definition asks that tensoring every injective module map stay injective. The polynomial definition gives finite monomial expansions. The displayed maps between N tensor T and finite coefficient families are inverse by bilinearity and commute with any module map, so injectivity is coefficientwise. This is the elementary argument itself, not a hidden invocation of free-implies-flat or a tensor/base-change theorem.
6. I checked mT is contained in M and M0 is maximal. Reducing fractions identifies the local closed fibre with k[x] localized at M0, including nonsplit closed points. The cited polynomial-maximal-localization statement is exactly dim(k[x]_M0)=r, with k any field and r>=0; it supplies all the dimension content needed in this step.
7. The cited flat-height formula explicitly includes the equivalent local equality dim(T_M)=dim(R_m)+dim(T_M/mT_M), and assumes both rings Noetherian. Steps 4 and 5 provide both Noetherianities and flatness; Step 4 gives the right contracted prime. R is already local, so localizing it at its maximal ideal changes nothing. Thus the source's equality applies with no catenarity or finite-residue-extension hypothesis missing.
8. The irreducible-component node states precisely V(p) for minimal primes, for both C and D. The topological/ring dimension node identifies the two meanings of dimension; quotient-prime correspondence identifies Spec(C/p) with V(p). The minimal-prime-existence node makes every prime chain lie in some component. Together these justify that the common component dimension of an equidimensional Spec C is dim C. Substituting Step 7 gives dim C+r for every component of D, which proves all remaining clauses.

I tested r=0 (D=C), C a field (arbitrary closed points of affine space, not just the origin), C=k[e]/(e^2) (nilpotents do not invalidate the prime argument), and the equidimensional reducible ring k[[u,v]]/(uv). Each component gives dim C+r. The requirement that n' lie over the closed point matters: for C=k[[t]], localizing C[x] at the prime (x) instead gives dimension one, not dim C+1=2. This tests the exact hypothesis used when identifying the special fibre and the contraction of M. An unequal-component local ring does not satisfy the equidimensional hypothesis; the proof correctly gives component dimensions dim(C/p)+r rather than erasing their difference.
