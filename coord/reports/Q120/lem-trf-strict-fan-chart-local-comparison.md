### Original step 1 -> lem-trf-fine-fan-point-localization

First spell out the affine localizations from [def-trf-fine-fan-blowup]. In an affine neighborhood $\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathrm{mon}}A$ of a point $z$ represented by a prime $\mathfrak p$, choose finite generators $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ for $A$ and let $s$ be the sum of those outside $\mathfrak p$ (the empty sum is $0$). Since the complement of a prime ideal is closed under addition, $A\setminus\mathfrak p$ is generated by exactly these generators. Therefore $D(s)=\{\mathfrak q:\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak p\}$ is the set of generalizations of $z$, and $A[s^{-1}]=A_{\mathfrak p}$. It is a basic open affine monoid spectrum. Every neighborhood of $z$ contains $D(s)$, so this is the smallest open neighborhood, even in the original fan. A sheaf on this smallest open has its sections equal to its stalk at $z$. Its sharp monoid of sections is consequently $\overline{A_{\mathfrak p}}=\mathcal P_{F,z}$; this is fine, since the finite generators of $A$ and the inverses of the finitely many generators outside $\mathfrak p$ generate $A_{\mathfrak p}$, and their images generate its sharpening. Prime ideals are invariant under adding units, so quotienting by units bijects the primes and their basic opens; localization then identifies the sharp structure sheaves. Thus $F_z=S(\mathcal P_{F,z})$. Apply this to $z=c(f(y))$ in $F$ and $w=d(y)$ in $G$. Write $P=\mathcal P_{F,z}$ and $Q=\mathcal P_{G,w}$. Both are fine sharp monoids.

Cited statements: def-trf-fine-fan-blowup. Source: literature/illusie-laszlo-orgogozo-travaux-de-gabber_arXiv-1207.3648.pdf, VIII 3.1.2--3.1.4, printed p.98, and 3.1.10(ii), printed p.99; explicit finite-generator verification of the affine-localization remark.

### Original step 2 -> lem-trf-strict-log-characteristic-pullback

By the definition of fan chart in [lem-trf-log-fan-chart-blowup], the chart stalk maps are isomorphisms $a:P\xrightarrow{\sim}\overline M_{f(y)}$ and $b:Q\xrightarrow{\sim}\overline N_y$. Strictness gives an isomorphism $t:\overline M_{f(y)}\xrightarrow{\sim}\overline N_y$: indeed the pulled-back log structure is the associated-log pushout of the inverse-image log structure with $\mathcal O_Y^\times$; at $y$, an element of $M_{f(y)}$ maps to a unit precisely when its structure image is a unit in $\mathcal O_{X,f(y)}$, because the local ring map to $\mathcal O_{Y,y}$ reflects units. Quotienting the pushout by $\mathcal O_{Y,y}^\times$ therefore gives exactly $\overline M_{f(y)}$. This is the characteristic quotient in [def-trf-fs-log-blowup]. Set $\alpha=b^{-1}\circ t\circ a:P\xrightarrow{\sim}Q$.

### Original step 3 -> parent assembly step 3

Use [lem-trf-affine-fan-hom-universal] for the fine sharp monoids $P,Q$ of Step 1. The monoid homomorphism $\alpha:P\to Q=\Gamma(G_w,\mathcal P_G)$ induces a fan morphism $\lambda:G_w=S(Q)\to S(P)=F_z$. Its inverse is induced by $\alpha^{-1}$. Naturality of the bijection identifies both composites with the identity, since their pullbacks on canonical monoid sections are $\alpha^{-1}\alpha$ and $\alpha\alpha^{-1}$. Hence $\lambda$ is an isomorphism, with induced section homomorphism exactly $\alpha$.

### Original step 4 -> lem-trf-affine-fan-germ-rigidity

Let $U_0=(c\circ f)^{-1}(F_z)\cap d^{-1}(G_w)$. These inverse images are open by continuity, and contain $y$ by Step 1. Restrict the chart morphisms, using the definition in [lem-trf-log-fan-chart-blowup], to obtain homomorphisms $\psi:P\to\Gamma(U_0,\overline N)$ and $\phi:Q\to\Gamma(U_0,\overline N)$. The first is the section map for $c\circ f$ and the second that for $d$. By construction in Step 2, the germs at $y$ of $\psi$ and $\phi\circ\alpha$ coincide. Choose a finite generating set $p_1,\ldots,p_m$ of $P$ (fineness is as in [def-trf-fine-fan-blowup]). Equality of the two germs on $p_i$ means, by the definition of a sheaf stalk, that the sections $\psi(p_i)$ and $\phi(\alpha(p_i))$ agree on an open neighborhood $U_i\subseteq U_0$ of $y$. On $U=\bigcap_{i=1}^m U_i$ they agree on every generator and hence on every element of $P$, by the homomorphism laws. If $m=0$, take $U=U_0$. Thus $\psi|_U=\phi|_U\circ\alpha$.

### Original step 5 -> lem-trf-affine-fan-germ-rigidity

Both $(c\circ f)|_U$ and $\lambda\circ d|_U$ are morphisms of sharp monoidal spaces from $(U,\overline N|_U)$ into $F_z=S(P)$. Under the bijection in [lem-trf-affine-fan-hom-universal] they correspond to the equal section homomorphisms $\psi|_U$ and $\phi|_U\circ\alpha$ of Step 4, respectively. Injectivity of that bijection proves $(c\circ f)|_U=\lambda\circ d|_U$ as morphisms of monoidal spaces, including their underlying continuous maps and sheaf maps. The containment conditions hold since $U\subseteq U_0$, and the section map of $\lambda$ is the stipulated composite $b^{-1}ta$ by Step 3.
