Bhatt--Scholze 6.8.2 proves the finite-extension unit-ball limit identity and rationalization by continuous functions. I use the same mechanism but explicitly replace compact/profinite unit-ball reconstruction by the following general metric-group argument, recorded here for the new unsplit node:

For a compatible family of cosets, choose any representative x_n in G of its nth coordinate. For m >= n, compatibility gives x_m-x_n in U_n. The shrinking hypothesis and translation invariance show (x_n) is Cauchy. Completeness gives a limit x. Each open subgroup U_n is also closed: its complement is a union of its other open cosets. The tail x_m lies in x_n+U_n, so its limit x does as well. Thus x has all the prescribed cosets. If x,y have identical cosets, x-y lies in every U_n; shrinking forces d(x-y,0)=0, whence x=y. The canonical map is continuous because each coordinate quotient is continuous (all cosets are open). For inverse continuity, a ball of radius epsilon about x contains x+U_N for some N, by shrinking. The inverse image in the limit of the condition that coordinate N equals q_N(x) is open and maps into that ball. The canonical bijection is therefore a homeomorphism and a group isomorphism. Continuous maps into a product are precisely the maps with continuous coordinates; imposing the compatibility equations gives maps into its subspace H. Composing with the homeomorphism proves the continuous-function assertion, with the stated projections and precomposition compatibility. This is a genuine completeness theorem with its own hypotheses and proof, not one of the item's open-restriction clauses.

For the item, O is closed in B and hence complete. The closed norm balls ell^n O are also open by the ultrametric inequality and shrink to zero. This verifies every hypothesis of the new group lemma, even for nondiscrete valuations or infinite residue fields. Multiplication and addition of reconstructed elements agree because all quotient projections agree and reconstruction is injective.

For rationalization, on every affine test object the increasing open sets where the norm is < ell^m have a finite subcover. Their union is the entire source, so a single m bounds each continuous function; ell^m times that function is O-valued. This avoids a local-compactness assumption on B.
