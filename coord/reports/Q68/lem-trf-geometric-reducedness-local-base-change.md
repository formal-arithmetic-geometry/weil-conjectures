### Argument for lem-trf-field-localization-tensor-comparison

Old step 1: Assume (1) and take any extension $H/L$. Write $V=R\setminus\mathfrak p$ and $U=S\setminus\mathfrak q$. Every $v\otimes1$ with $v\in V$ belongs to $U$. By [lem-weil-pbc-localization-base-change] and [lem-weil-pbc-tensor-cancel-base-change], first $A\otimes_kH$ is the localization of $R\otimes_kH$ at the images of $V$, and $B\otimes_LH$ is its further localization at the images of $U$. The cancellation isomorphism sends $h\otimes(r\otimes l)$ to $r\otimes hl$, with inverse $r\otimes h\mapsto h\otimes(r\otimes1)$; swapping factors gives the displayed order. Condition (1), applied to $H/k$, makes $A\otimes_kH$ reduced. A localization of a reduced ring is reduced: if $(x/t)^n=0$, some denominator $u$ satisfies $ux^n=0$; then $(ux)^n=0$, so $ux=0$ and $x/t=0$. Thus $B\otimes_LH$ is reduced, proving (2).

Cited statements: `lem-weil-pbc-localization-base-change`, `lem-weil-pbc-tensor-cancel-base-change`.

Old step 4: Condition (2) applied to $F/L$ makes $B\otimes_LF$ reduced. By [lem-weil-pbc-tensor-cancel-base-change], $B\otimes_LF\cong B\otimes_S(S\otimes_LF)$, $b\otimes f\mapsto b\otimes(1\otimes f)$. By [lem-weil-pbc-localization-base-change] and the isomorphism $S\otimes_LF\cong T$ of step 3, this is the localization of $T$ at the image of $S\setminus\mathfrak q$. All those elements lie outside $\mathfrak Q$, so $D=T_{\mathfrak Q}$ is a further localization of $B\otimes_LF$. Explicitly a fraction $t/u$ in the first localization maps to the same fraction in $T_{\mathfrak Q}$; localizing further at all images of $t\notin\mathfrak Q$ gives every fraction in $D$ and the same equality relation. Thus $D$ is reduced by the nilpotent-fraction calculation of step 1.

### Argument for lem-trf-inseparable-prime-localization-identification

Old step 2: Assume (2), fix a finite purely inseparable extension $E/k$, and set $C=A\otimes_kE$. The map $A\to C$ is faithfully flat by [lem-weil-pbc-field-extension-base-change-faithfully-flat], hence injective by [lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-universally-injective], applied with the module $N=A$ and $A\otimes_AC\cong C$. In characteristic $p>0$, the $A$-algebra $C$ is generated by $1\otimes e$ for $e\in E$. By [def-weil-pbc-finite-purely-inseparable-extension], each such generator has a $p^n$-th power in $A$; increase $n$ if necessary to have $n>0$. Also $p^n(1\otimes e)=0$. The injective map has nilpotent kernel, so [lem-pur-power-generated-spectrum-homeomorphism] applies and makes contraction $\operatorname{Spec}C\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ a homeomorphism. In characteristic zero $E=k$, so the same conclusion holds by the identity. A homeomorphism preserves the specialization order (closures of points); maximal prime ideals are its maximal elements under inclusion. Since $A$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak pA$, $C$ is local and its unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$ is the unique prime contracting to $\mathfrak m$. This argument uses no Noetherian hypothesis.

Cited statements: `lem-weil-pbc-field-extension-base-change-faithfully-flat`, `lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-universally-injective`, `def-weil-pbc-finite-purely-inseparable-extension`, `lem-pur-power-generated-spectrum-homeomorphism`, `def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime`.

Old step 5: Let $R_E=R\otimes_kE$ and let $\mathfrak P$ be the inverse image of $\mathfrak Q$ under $R_E\to T$. Its contraction to $R$ is $\mathfrak p$. By [lem-weil-pbc-tensor-cancel-base-change], $C=A\otimes_kE\cong A\otimes_R R_E$, $a\otimes e\mapsto a\otimes(1\otimes e)$; by [lem-weil-pbc-localization-base-change], $C$ is the localization of $R_E$ at the images of $V=R\setminus\mathfrak p$. Since these images avoid $\mathfrak P$, it gives a prime $\mathfrak P_C$ of $C$, consisting of fractions whose numerator belongs to $\mathfrak P$. It contracts to $\mathfrak m$ in $A$: a fraction $r/v\in A$ maps into it exactly when $r\in\mathfrak p$. Step 2 now gives $\mathfrak P_C=\mathfrak n$. Hence every element of $R_E\setminus\mathfrak P$ is a unit in $C$. The fraction map $(R_E)_{\mathfrak P}\to C$, $t/u\mapsto(t/1)(u/1)^{-1}$, is inverse to the localization map $C\to(R_E)_{\mathfrak P}$: both composites fix $R_E$ and all inverted denominators. Thus $(R_E)_{\mathfrak P}\cong C$, compatibly with the maps to $D$.

Cited statements: `lem-weil-pbc-localization-base-change`, `lem-weil-pbc-tensor-cancel-base-change`, `def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime`.
