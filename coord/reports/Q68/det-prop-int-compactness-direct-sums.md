## New lemma: source and intended argument

I read `literature/stacks-project/derived.tex`, section-derived-limit (08TB), definition-derived-limit (08TC), lemma-products, and remark-map-into-derived-limit-truncations, together with the blueprint item's proof (2) and its left-completeness/Milnor inputs. The source defines the Roos-triangle notion and explains the truncation tower. It does not state the new class-relative conclusion verbatim; the node explicitly identifies it as an agent lemma extracted from this argument. In particular it is not mislabelled as a quoted Stacks theorem.

Here is the argument to guide the eventual split of that statement-only node. For K with cohomology in S and no cohomology above b, put K_n=tau_{>=-n}K. Finite induction on its nonzero cohomology interval, using the standard truncation triangles and the bound on T(M[0]), gives TK_n in degrees <= b+c. Left completeness and preservation of products and distinguished triangles give a Roos triangle for TK as the derived limit of TK_n. Exact products in B make cohomology of its two products the products of cohomology. The long exact sequence first gives the crude bound <= b+c+1. To remove that possible extra degree, the triangle

`H^{-n-1}(K)[n+1] -> K_{n+1} -> K_n ->`

has, after T, first term bounded above by c-n-1. Thus for any fixed r the transition maps H^r(TK_{n+1}) -> H^r(TK_n) are isomorphisms for all sufficiently large n. At r=b+c, the map 1-s on the product of this eventually constant-up-to-isomorphism tower is a split epimorphism: set a starting tail coordinate to zero, solve successively using the inverse tail transition maps, and solve the finitely many earlier coordinates backwards. Each coordinate is a finite sum of morphisms applied to projections, so this defines a morphism in any abelian category, not an argument requiring elementwise lifting. Its cokernel is zero. The Roos long exact sequence consequently also kills H^{b+c+1}(TK). All higher cohomology was already zero. This is why the new statement needs neither a lower amplitude bound on T nor a bound on T on every object of A.

I tested the statement on the identity functor (c=0), the zero functor, shifts T=[r] (c=-r), complexes concentrated in one degree, and a complex with nonzero cohomology in arbitrarily negative degrees but none above b. The signs give H^j(TK)=H^{j+r}(K), hence the asserted cutoff b-r. An S without zero has no K satisfying the written all-i hypothesis, so introduces no false exception. S need not be closed under extensions: the finite induction uses triangles, not closure of S under extensions. The potentially nonzero Milnor degree is handled by the explicit eventual-stabilization argument above.
