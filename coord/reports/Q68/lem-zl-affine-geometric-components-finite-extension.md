Step 2 uses exactly the finiteness and openness asserted by `lem-sfb-noetherian-connected-components-open` and the quasi-compactness of arbitrary subsets asserted by `lem-sfb-noetherian-space-compact-subsets`. The proof explicitly explains the elementary component-partition argument from `def-weil-connected-scheme`: unions of connected sets through one point are connected, so maximal components cover and are disjoint. Closedness then follows from the other open components. Thus the source's connected-components partition is not an uncited existence assumption. The source allows the empty space.

Step 3 invokes `lem-weil-pbc-descend-qc-open-fg-subalgebra`, whose statement really supplies a finitely generated subalgebra and an open with the exact inverse-image equality. Its base algebra is the algebraic closure, and step 2 supplies quasi-compactness. This tool does not require Galoisness, reducedness, or quasi-separatedness. I therefore do not need a hidden identification of algebraic and separable closures. At this step I assert neither closedness nor connectedness downstairs.

Step 4 takes the finite union of the finite algebra generating sets. `def-weil-algebraic-closure` supplies algebraicity of each generator; `lem-weil-pbc-algebraic-generators-finite-extension` states precisely finiteness of the generated subfield. The subfield contains each whole subalgebra, because it contains that subalgebra's generators. The finite-dimensional basis gives the cardinality by a coordinate bijection, and `lem-weil-unique-subfield-of-closure` supplies uniqueness of the subfield with that cardinality. This last node is a generic finite-field tool, not the unrelated coefficient-field directed-set definition. The degree is positive since the subfield contains the nonzero base field. With no components the empty generating set gives the base field and degree one.

Step 5 uses `lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-base-change` to produce opens downstairs and the iterated-base-change assertion actually written in `def-pc-base-change` to identify their geometric base changes. The composite inclusions of coefficient rings give the same canonical morphism; hence the inverse-image equality is an equality of these opens with their restricted structure sheaves, not only a resemblance of topological subsets.

Step 6 uses `lem-pc-field-extension-surjective` for spectra of fields and `lem-weil-pbc-spec-surjective-base-change` for its affine base change, with the affine tensor model supplied by `lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra`. This is why the chosen projection really is surjective. The rest is the explicitly written elementary set argument: reflect empty intersections and the empty uncovered locus along that surjection, then identify every complement with the union of the other opens. Nonemptiness of each piece follows from its nonempty inverse image. I did not use faithful flat descent of any additional property implicitly.
