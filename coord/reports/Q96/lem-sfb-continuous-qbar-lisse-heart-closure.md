I checked delivery-errors.md B-E in full against the actual statements and sources. For the item I read Hemo–Richarz–Scholbach Definition 3.3 and the immediately following stable-subcategory statement (printed p. 7), Theorem 4.13 (p. 12), and Definition 6.1, Theorem 6.2(2) and its proof (pp. 16-17). The paper's derived lisse convention is dualizability, so I did not silently identify that convention with the module convention: step 3 uses the explicit heart description of the general theorem, and step 1 proves its agreement with locally finite free modules over a field. The source's finite-colimit discussion does not imply any finite-subextension condition on the norm-topology ring used here.

Step 1 uses precisely the field clause of `lem-sfb-scalar-finite-free-resolutions`, which gives a finite basis for any finitely presented field module, together with the scalar-local-model and lisse definitions. Tensoring its basis isomorphism gives the displayed finite free model; the reverse direction chooses the scalar module Omega^r. This is a short elementary deduction, not a new lemma. The rank-zero case is explicitly included by that cited field clause and by the lisse definition.

Step 2 checks each hypothesis required by `lem-zl-t-admissible-dualizable-truncations`: ideals in Omega are zero or the whole field; its finitely presented modules are already finite free by the preceding cited basis theorem; and every C(P,Omega) is a flat scalar module by `lem-weil-pbc-module-over-field-flat`, whose statement is for every field module, not only finite modules. The finite-components node is explicitly for Noetherian topological spaces, hence needs no ring-theoretic local Noetherianity. This is direct verification of the general theorem's hypotheses. I did not infer finite-image or finite-coefficient-field factorization from compactness. The empty profinite set gives the zero module and is covered by field-flatness.

Step 3 uses the statement of `def-weil-pbc-internal-abelian-sheaf-module-equivalence`, which actually asserts the category equivalence and identity on underlying sheaves. I checked its site-size and monoidal sheafification hypotheses and made the common universe choice explicit. Equivalences preserve the defining universal properties of kernels and cokernels. The general truncation node asserts both restriction of the standard t-structure and the exact finite constant local description of the heart; its closing sentence supplies all cohomology sheaves of dualizable objects. Thus step 3 takes no claim from an uncited proof or a mere definition of lisseness.

Step 4 reads the first paragraph of `lem-zl-condensed-dualizable-local-perfect-models`, which explicitly contains stability under shifts and distinguished cones, not just its local-perfect comparison. Completing the given morphism to a cone is allowed by the derived triangulation. Clause 3 of `lem-weil-heart-short-exact-triangles` gives the kernel and cokernel formulas with degrees -1 and 0; I checked the signs against the cochain convention and against the actual cited statement. Applying this to the ambient standard t-structure identifies ambient kernels, rather than just kernels in an unverified full subcategory. The two cohomology sheaves are lisse by step 3.

Step 5 cites the actual short-exact-complex triangle node, whose statement applies in every abelian category and in particular to degree-zero module complexes. Rotation gives M[-1] -> L -> E -> M; no prior lisseness hypothesis on E is used. The stability node therefore gives dualizability of E[0], and its degree-zero concentration puts it in the heart identified in step 3. This avoids the circular assertion that a short exact sequence is already an extension inside the restricted heart.

Step 6 supplies the complete elementary subspace argument for the final locally closed clause. Closure in the parent followed by intersection with the subspace recovers a subspace-closed set, so the descending-chain argument works for arbitrary subspaces. A locally closed scheme immersion has precisely this subspace topology. No claim of preservation of Noetherian local rings is required.
