Step 1 takes the invariant log stalk, units, and structural maps from `lem-per-finite-quotient-strict-local-invariants`. Its Noetherian-source hypothesis holds after restricting to the finite inverse image of an affine open in Noetherian Y: that source is quasi-compact and locally Noetherian. Strict-local local structure supplies strictly henselian local rings; the separate Noetherian strict-henselization lemma supplies Noetherianity. The residue-field assertion is proved using the geometric-point embedding, not attributed to the local-structure node.

Step 2 uses fine-log chart spreading only for the given fs source M. The etale open-map node makes the neighbourhood surjective after pullback to the local spectrum. The finite-strict-henselian-algebra section lemma is applied with base A and finite algebra A itself. This produces the source chart on Spec A. No fineness of the invariant log structure N is assumed.

Step 3 gives explicit finite generators for P by coefficient remainders modulo n, proves saturation in its group completion, and writes the two positive elements expressing any element of L as a difference in P. Thus neither finite generation nor Pgp=L is silently assumed. The closed-stalk chart calculation uses the fs chart definition, the invariant-stalk identification, and the elementary factorization of z^n-1; n is invertible. It is deliberately not used as a neighbourhood chart theorem.

Steps 4–6 are written algebraic calculations. Averaging gives A=B+I. Powers of each chart generator are invariant nonunits, so I to the power r(n-1)+1 lies in m_B A. The induction expands every coefficient as b+i, giving A=D+I^k and then A=D+m_B A. No completeness, domain, reducedness, or intersection theorem is used. The monoid-ring map is the chart-square definition. Step 7 applies exactly the local Nakayama submodule statement: A is finite over local B by hypothesis and D is a B-submodule; D need not separately be assumed finitely generated. Step 8 uses the affine closed-immersion characterization and the pullback chart to prove strictness, rather than inferring strictness from the closed immersion.

Step 9 uses chart spreading for M, finite-piece refinement for the source, and finite-base strict localization to get the fibre decomposition. The finitely many idempotent permutation equalities descend by the finite-data colimit statement; this is the reason the chosen piece is H-stable. Equality of the diagonal identities is represented by equality of geometric germs. Root coefficients come from B because they are H-fixed; the strict-local roots lemma gives all n distinct roots, and their pairwise differences and n are made units on the base neighbourhood. Restriction to one of the G/H translates identifies invariant sections directly, test scheme by test scheme. This proves the invariant-sheaf identifications used for b without assuming that N is fine. The invariant sheaf is a log structure: a section whose image is a base unit is a unit upstairs, and its invariant inverse is a base unit by the same invariant-functions identification.

Step 10 uses precisely the finite-base strict-localization statement to identify the chosen one-point piece with Spec A over Spec B. Finiteness supplies module generators; surjectivity already proved in Step 7 supplies their finite monomial expressions. Coefficients and zero differences are represented on neighbourhoods using the stalk colimit. Finite-piece refinement, rather than arbitrary shrinking of a finite source, makes those expressions global on another finite piece. Since the old chosen piece has just one marked geometric-fibre point, after refinement the chosen clopen component is preserved by H; any other components with no point over the marked base can be removed by a further base neighbourhood. The idempotent permutation equalities of Step 9 are retained. Step 11 checks that scalar extension and the idempotent quotient preserve the generating expressions; this proves surjectivity on the actual affine neighbourhoods.

Step 12 uses the actual algebra generation from Step 11 to factor the action through the finite abelian character image E. This factorization applies also to the chart log structure, which is generated by chart sections and units. At an arbitrary geometric t, an element fixes t exactly when it fixes every monomial value: those values and base functions generate all functions. Nonzero values are exactly the face F of unit monomials. The distinct-root condition then identifies its stabilizer with the common kernel of the face characters. The strict-local quotient node, now for this finite quotient and its actual stabilizer, supplies N_s=M_t^K and the equality of invariant units. All its quotient hypotheses are inherited from the G/H decomposition of Step 9.

Step 13 applies the new leaf with T=A and exponent e dividing n. The roots lemma gives cyclic mu_n(A) of order n, so the primitive e-th root and cyclic e-torsion are present. Double annihilation identifies K-perpendicular with the subgroup generated by face characters. Writing an element of that subgroup as a difference of two face elements, and adding n times one of them, explicitly produces p in P with p-q in F. Step 14 uses this p to rewrite any invariant log section as a base unit times a(p). Cancellation here takes place in the integral fs log monoid; it does not cancel a possibly zero structural value in the ring. Reduction is valid because a geometric stabilizer fixes the residue field. The new coefficient is fixed, so the strict-local invariant-units statement applies.

Step 15 proves L intersect Fgp=(P intersect F)gp with an explicit positive expression. The associated-log relations then prove injectivity, using the injection of base units into source units. The cited geometric-stalk isomorphism statement is for sheaves of sets, so applies to the underlying monoid sheaves; I did not use the abelian-sheaf conservativity node. Together with surjectivity at all geometric points this proves b is an actual neighbourhood chart of N. In particular a closed-stalk isomorphism alone has not been used to infer neighbourhood fineness.

I tested the new duality statement on E trivial (exponent 1), on D trivial or the full character group, on the cyclic group of order two, and on a line in the character group of (Z/2)^2. The annihilator formulas and the double-annihilator conclusion agree in each case. For the item, Q=0 gives I=0 and A=B; H trivial gives n=1, P=Q and the identity comparison. With k algebraically closed of characteristic not 2, A=k[x,y]/(x^2,y^2,xy), H of order two acting by x to -x and fixing y, and Q=N mapped to x, one has B=k[y]/(y^2), P=2N. The comparison B[t]/(t^2) surjects onto A with kernel (yt). This checks that closed immersion, rather than isomorphism, is correct without log regularity and that zero divisors are allowed. At generizations with nonempty face F, the character-face calculation explicitly accounts for smaller stabilizers.
