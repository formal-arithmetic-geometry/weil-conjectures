## Argument reserved for the new unsplit algebra node

For each coset c choose t_c. Every m in M is uniquely l+t_c for l in L. Expanding coefficients in finite sums gives inverse R[L]-linear maps from the direct sum of copies of R[L], indexed by M/L, to R[M]: (f_c) maps to sum f_c e_(t_c), and a basis term e_m maps to e_(m-t_c) in coordinate c=[m]. This proves the stated basis and the injection of R[L]. It does not require finite generation or nonzero R.

When M/L is finitely generated, use the existing finitely-generated abelian-group structure theorem to choose the displayed decomposition, omitting trivial cyclic factors. Choose lifts a_i,b_j. Modulo the relations Y_j^d_j=e_(d_j b_j), whose right side is a unit of R[L], every Laurent monomial has a unique candidate normal form with arbitrary integer exponents in the X_i and exponents 0,...,d_j-1 in Y_j. Reduce positive and negative Y exponents by Euclidean division; the unit on the right makes this valid for negative exponents too. These normal forms represent distinct cosets of L in M. Their images are therefore an R[L]-basis by the first assertion. This proves both generation and independence, so the displayed map is an algebra isomorphism. Adjoin inverse variables and the finitely many equations ZW=1 to obtain an ordinary finite polynomial presentation. The same coefficient formulas after any R-to-R' map prove the asserted base change, including R'=0. This is a genuine supporting algebra normal-form theorem, strictly more general in L and M than the item's finite character groups; it does not assert any group-scheme Hom, kernel, image or quotient clause.
