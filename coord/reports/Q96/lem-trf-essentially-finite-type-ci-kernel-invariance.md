Returned step 1: Unpack [def-weil-pbc-essentially-finite-type-algebra] for $P$ and $Q$: choose finite type $k$-algebras $B,C$, multiplicative subsets $W\subset B$, $Z\subset C$, and identifications $P=W^{-1}B$, $Q=Z^{-1}C$. By [def-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra], choose algebra generators $b_1,\ldots,b_r$ of $B$ and $c_1,\ldots,c_s$ of $C$. Write $p_i=b_i/1\in P$ and $q_j=c_j/1\in Q$. Thus every element of $P$ is $b/w$, with $b,w$ polynomials in the $b_i$ and $w\in W$; every element of $Q$ has the corresponding form $c/z$. No finite generation of either localization as a $k$-algebra is being assumed.

Returned step 2: Surjectivity of $u$ and $v$ supplies elements $\widetilde q_j\in P$ and $\widetilde p_i\in Q$ with $u(\widetilde q_j)=v(q_j)$ and $v(\widetilde p_i)=u(p_i)$. Set $D=k[x_1,\ldots,x_r,y_1,\ldots,y_s]$. The evaluation property in [def-weil-pbc-mv-polynomial-ring] gives maps $f:D\to P$, $g:D\to Q$ by $f(x_i)=p_i$, $f(y_j)=\widetilde q_j$, $g(x_i)=\widetilde p_i$, $g(y_j)=q_j$. They have the same composite $h=u\circ f=v\circ g:D\to A$, since these composites agree on the variables and on $k$. Put $\mathfrak n=h^{-1}(\mathfrak m_A)$. This is a prime ideal: if $h(ab)\in\mathfrak m_A$, the field $A/\mathfrak m_A$ gives $h(a)\in\mathfrak m_A$ or $h(b)\in\mathfrak m_A$, and $h(1)\notin\mathfrak m_A$.

Returned step 3: Let $T=D_{\mathfrak n}$, a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak nT$ by [def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime]. Since $u,v$ are local, $u^{-1}(\mathfrak m_A)=\mathfrak m_P$ and $v^{-1}(\mathfrak m_A)=\mathfrak m_Q$: elements in the source maximal ideal map into the target maximal ideal, and elements outside it are units and stay units. Hence $f^{-1}(\mathfrak m_P)=\mathfrak n=g^{-1}(\mathfrak m_Q)$. The localization universal property [lem-weil-pbc-ring-localization-universal-property] extends $f,g,h$ to $\alpha:T\to P$, $\beta:T\to Q$, $\gamma:T\to A$, by $d/t\mapsto f(d)f(t)^{-1}$ and the analogous formulas. The maps are local, since numerators in $\mathfrak n$ map into the respective maximal ideals. Uniqueness gives $u\alpha=\gamma=v\beta$.

Returned step 4: The extensions $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are surjective, directly from the fraction descriptions in step 1. Indeed, for $b/w\in P$ choose polynomials $a(x),t(x)$ representing $b,w\in B$. Then $f(t)=w/1$ is a unit of $P$, so $h(t)=u(w/1)$ is a unit of $A$, whence $t\notin\mathfrak n$. The element $a(x)/t(x)\in T$ maps to $b/w$. For $c/z\in Q$, represent $c,z$ by polynomials $a(y),t(y)$ in the $c_j$; the same argument uses $g(t)=z/1$ and gives a preimage under $\beta$. Thus $\gamma=u\alpha=v\beta$ is surjective too.

Returned step 5: Apply [lem-weil-pbc-polynomial-ring-field-regular-ring] to the polynomial ring $D$ and its prime $\mathfrak n$: $T$ is regular local. Consequently $T\xrightarrow{\alpha}P\xrightarrow{u}A$ is a chain of surjective local homomorphisms with both source rings regular local. The statement of [lem-trf-regular-local-surjection-kernel-comparison] applies without completeness assumptions and says that $\ker\gamma=\ker(u\alpha)$ is generated by a finite regular sequence in $\mathfrak m_T$ if and only if $\ker u$ is generated by a finite regular sequence in $\mathfrak m_P$.

Returned step 6: Apply [lem-trf-regular-local-surjection-kernel-comparison] to $T\xrightarrow{\beta}Q\xrightarrow{v}A$, whose maps are surjective local and whose first two rings are regular local. It identifies the same property of $\ker\gamma=\ker(v\beta)$ with the asserted property of $\ker v$. Combining the two equivalences proves the desired equivalence for $\ker u$ and $\ker v$. The comparison lemma explicitly permits empty sequences, so this argument also includes zero kernels. All rings used are the original local rings or the polynomial localization $T$; none has been completed.
