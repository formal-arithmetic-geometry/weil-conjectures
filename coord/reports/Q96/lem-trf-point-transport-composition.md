### Old step 2

For a sheaf $F$ of $A_s$-modules define $R_rF$ by $(R_rF)(U\xrightarrow{u}s)=F(U\xrightarrow{h^ru}s)$, with the induced restrictions and the constant-coefficient action. Apply [lem-sfb-proetale-object-inverse-image-restriction] to $h^r:s\to s$. It gives a natural $A_s$-linear isomorphism $e_{r,F}:H_rF\xrightarrow{\sim}R_rF$. Its normalization is explicit: at $U$, the initial index for inverse image is $(U,h^ru)$ with map $(u,\operatorname{id}_U):U\to s\times_{s,h^r}U$. Its coprojection identifies $F(U,h^ru)$ with the presheaf inverse image, and the map to its sheafification is an isomorphism. The tensor extension along the coefficient isomorphism has inverse $x\otimes a\mapsto c_{h^r}^{-1}(a)x$; in particular $e_{r,F}$ takes the insertion of $x$, followed by $x\mapsto x\otimes1$, to $x$. This also describes $e_r$ on restrictions and on sheaf morphisms.

### Old step 3

Let $j_{r,F}:F(s,\operatorname{id})\to (H_rF)(s,\operatorname{id})$ be the finite-level recipe in the statement, before localization. By [def-weil-frobenius-point-transport] and the finite clause (a) of [def-weil-inverse-image-pullback-comparison], this is restriction along $h^r$, insertion at $(s,h^r)$, and tensoring with $1$. Step 2 therefore gives
$$e_{r,F}(s)j_{r,F}=\operatorname{res}_{h^r}^F:F(s,\operatorname{id})\longrightarrow F(s,h^r).$$
This equality identifies precisely the specified insertion, rather than choosing an unspecified isomorphism of fibers. Restriction preserves the constant section $a\in A$ by [def-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf], so this is $A$-linear. For a sheaf morphism $v:F\to G$, the defining commutation of $v$ with restrictions says $v(s,h^r)\operatorname{res}_{h^r}^F=\operatorname{res}_{h^r}^Gv(s,\operatorname{id})$. Naturality of $e_r$ then gives $(H_rv)(s)j_{r,F}=j_{r,G}v(s)$.

### Old step 4

Let $T_{m,F}:H_1H_mF\xrightarrow{\sim}H_{m+1}F$ be the ordinary module-pullback composition comparison. Under $e_r$ its normalization is
$$e_{m+1,F}T_{m,F}=R_1(e_{m,F})e_{1,H_mF},$$
where $R_1R_mF=R_{m+1}F$ by $h^mh=h^{m+1}$. To check this, use the inverse-image colimits of [def-weil-proetale-inverse-image]. A section $x\in F(U,h^{m+1}u)$ at the direct initial index is sent by the inverse composition comparison to its nested initial insertions: first at $(U,hu)$ for $h$, then at $(U,h^mhu)$ for $h^m$. Both sides of the displayed equality return its label $x$. The initial coprojections are isomorphisms by [lem-sfb-proetale-object-inverse-image-restriction], so this checks the entire inverse-image comparison, not just selected sections. For module pullback the corresponding calculation is $(x\otimes1)\otimes1\mapsto x\otimes1$; the tensor relations and sheafification defining [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product] give the same equality. The coefficient identifications agree: each is the map preserving constant sections, as specified by [prop-weil-pbc-proetale-constant-ring-map]; equivalently the two adjoint maps from the constant sheaf agree on its generating constant presheaf. Thus these are the canonical composition comparisons with the coefficient maps in the statement.

### Old step 5

Apply Step 4 at the final object, followed by Step 3 twice. Naturality of $e_{m,F}$ with respect to the site arrow $h:(s,h)\to(s,\operatorname{id})$ gives
\begin{align*}
e_{m+1,F}(s)T_{m,F}(s)j_{1,H_mF}j_{m,F}
&=(R_1e_{m,F})(s)\operatorname{res}_h^{H_mF}j_{m,F}\\
&=\operatorname{res}_h^{R_mF}e_{m,F}(s)j_{m,F}\\
&=\operatorname{res}_h^{R_mF}\operatorname{res}_{h^m}^F
=\operatorname{res}_{h^{m+1}}^F
=e_{m+1,F}(s)j_{m+1,F}.
\end{align*}
The penultimate equality is the presheaf composition law: after $R_m$, the arrow $h$ is $h:(s,h^{m+1})\to(s,h^m)$, whose composite with $h^m:(s,h^m)\to(s,\operatorname{id})$ is $h^{m+1}$. Cancel $e_{m+1,F}(s)$ to obtain the required composition identity for sheaves. The restriction maps and the coprojection and tensor-unit maps commute with every sheaf morphism (Step 3), so applying this degree by degree to a complex gives an identity of chain maps, including their differentials.

Cited statements: def-weil-proetale-inverse-image.

Source: literature/stacks-project/sites.tex, Tag 06UM (section-pullback): restriction formula and the presheaf composition law.

### Old step 7

It remains to check naturality for every derived morphism, rather than only chain maps. Step 3, applied termwise, proves $V(Q_ru)\iota_N^{(r)}=\iota_{N'}^{(r)}V(u)$ for each chain map. If $w:K\to L$ is a quasi-isomorphism, both $V(w)$ and $V(Q_rw)$ are invertible by Step 1. Multiplying that equality on the left by $V(Q_rw)^{-1}$ and on the right by $V(w)^{-1}$ gives
