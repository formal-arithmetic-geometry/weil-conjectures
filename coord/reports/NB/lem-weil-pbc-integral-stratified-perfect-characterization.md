## Review by NB

I read the item against Bhatt–Scholze Proposition 6.6.11 and Proposition 6.8.11(3), with Definition 6.5.1 open. The paper uses the completed coefficient sheaf here, unlike Stacks' constant-coefficient D_cons notation. I therefore opened lem-weil-pbc-constant-completion-equivalence and lem-weil-pbc-completed-constant-sheaf-reduction and used their explicit flatness, reduction and completion comparisons. I did not identify the ambient constant and completed derived categories. The valuation ring is a complete DVR with finite residue field by the coefficient conventions; the new lifting lemma is stated for an adically complete Noetherian datum and locally perfect residue complexes, exactly the hypothesis used in Stacks 09C6 after restriction of scalars. Passing from its connected formulation to a Noetherian scheme is justified by its finitely many open connected components, as in Bhatt–Scholze Lemma 6.6.10; it is not asserted for an arbitrary disconnected non-Noetherian scheme.

For step 1 I opened the immersion-limit-preservation statement: it includes exactness and all small limits and explicitly requires a ring isomorphism for scalar extension. Taking the inverse limit of finite constant coefficients gives that isomorphism here. The weakly-etale localization statement gives the covering slices. Exact countable products, the derived product core and the strict-tower Roos triangle supply the comparison of completion units under these restrictions. The stratum-detection statement applies because T is Noetherian, and it detects the cone, not just fibres of a guessed constant model.

For step 2 the quoted constant-module section statement gives surjectivity on each object of a constant finite-projective quotient tower. The explicit recursive solution of (1-s)x=y shows there is no hidden appeal to a Mittag–Leffler theorem about arbitrary sheaf sections. Finite-projective summands commute with the finite operations and the inverse limit, so the actual completed realization is recovered by its reduction tower. The pro-etale site definition explicitly has a finite-affine-pieces covering condition; this is why finitely many local perfect models suffice for global bounds on each quasi-compact stratum. The detection node supplies global vanishing. No finiteness of cohomology groups of a covering scheme is used.

For step 3 I read both uniform-tower definitions and the finite-ring derived-local-constancy statement. The latter gives bounded finite-module constant models, not perfectness over arbitrary finite rings. Only at level one do I use the residue FIELD: splitting vector-space cycles and boundaries makes those models perfect. The coefficient DVR node states that its maximal-ideal quotient is a field. The exact finite-level comparison and finite-ring derived-origin statements give the corresponding cohomology properties of the witness. I added lem-weil-pbc-comparison-pullback after the first dry run to cite explicitly the identification of the witness on a stratum; its statement gives precisely that natural isomorphism. The new lifting statement therefore has all its hypotheses on each Noetherian stratum. This verifies the cited local inference, whereas 09C3 alone would not.

For step 4 I read the normalized-local-effectivity statement in full: it asks for normalization, intrinsic finite-ring constructibility and an interval independent of n, and concludes the common tower models needed here. These hypotheses are supplied by steps 1–2 and the coefficient datum, which explicitly constructs the strict normalized tower. I then opened finite-ring derived origin and thm-weil-pbc-site-comparison: origin only gives each L_n, while full faithfulness is only stated on D+. The coefficient-tensor-pullback statement and the sheaf unit in finite-level comparison show that the derived-reduced source also has bounded cohomology, before invoking full faithfulness. The beta maps, their inverses and their cocycle are lifted uniquely from the displayed normalization composites. This repairs the possible gap between levelwise origins and compatible origins.

For step 5 the completed-constant reduction statement explicitly gives flatness over constant A. The Noetherian submodule and finite-module finite-presentation statements apply to the finite-projective terms and kernels in P. They give finitely presented cohomology modules, not a coherence assertion about an unrelated structure sheaf.

For step 6 I checked the DVR torsion-free-free statement on the kernel of A^r onto N: the kernel is a submodule of a torsion-free finite free module and is finitely generated by the cited Noetherian lemma. Thus it is finite free. Its two-term resolution is a real construction, and flat completed scalar extension preserves the exact sequence. This establishes degree-zero membership using the already proved reverse implication, without inventing a node for that clause.

For step 7 I opened the standard t-structure definition, the derived truncation triangle statement and uniform-complete-triangulated statement. The last is specifically about the same completed category and arbitrary cones; it is not the finite-ring Dbc lemma. The first two give the Postnikov triangles, and the induction starts at zero and ends at K over its actual finite interval. I did not use the proof of any cited node or commute finite reductions with truncations.

For step 8 I read both detailed definitions, the intrinsic-completion definition and the completion-reflector statement. The detailed completeness condition allows a Roos triangle with its fixed projections but does not specify a unique lift to a chosen derived limit. The proof only obtains an object isomorphism with the quotient completion object; the reflector says that object is complete, and completeness is isomorphism invariant. The reverse implication separately verifies the common interval and intrinsic finite-level constructibility required by local effectivity, then repeats the witness-lifting argument. The comparison is restricted to where the detailed notation has its own coefficient hypotheses and the identical ring and reduction functors.

For step 9 the standard t-structure gives the unchanged cohomology sheaves of standard truncations. Conditions (iii) and the finite induction give their membership, while uniform-complete-triangulated gives shifts and arbitrary cones. The example N=A/varpi shows why this is different from normalized levelwise truncation: its derived reductions may have cohomology in degrees -1 and 0, and truncating those reductions need not give the reductions of the standard truncation of N.

I tested the new lemma on the zero object, a geometric point, a finite disjoint union, a perfect constant complex with torsion cohomology, and a residue-lisse complete complex on a connected stratum. At a point it gives the expected perfect A-model. I also checked the excluded profinite non-Noetherian case from Bhatt–Scholze Example 6.6.12: it does not meet the Noetherian hypothesis. For the item I checked the empty scheme, zero complex, and C=A/varpi in degree zero; the bounds and finite Postnikov construction include them. The torsion example uses a length-one A-resolution, not a false claim of flat A-cohomology. Searches were run through msearch.py, and LeanSearch's HTTP 500 is recorded as unavailable.
