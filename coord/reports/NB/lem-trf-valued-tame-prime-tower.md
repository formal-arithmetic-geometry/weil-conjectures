For the abstract prime-tower node, Temkin explicitly separates the subgroup chain in the pro-p group from the purely inseparable tower. I checked why the general formulation is true: split L/F into its maximal separable subextension S/F and a purely inseparable extension L/S. A finite normal closure of S/F has p-power order by the hypothesis; a chain from Gal(N/S) to Gal(N/F) with successive index p gives the separable part of the field tower by reversing fixed fields. Such chains exist in finite p-groups. In characteristic p, adjoining the successive pth roots needed for a finite purely inseparable extension finishes the tower. In characteristic zero L=S. This is more than an elementary step, so it is its own statement-only node. The characteristic restriction is essential and explicitly included; pro-p Galois theory alone in an unrelated positive characteristic would not control inseparability.

For the tower-descent node I read the finite-tame-descent inference on Temkin p. 49 and made its finite-data assertion precise. To verify it, choose bases over M for the F_i, and over F_i for F_{i+1}, and express their multiplications, inclusions, the elements of a finite A-basis of B, and all generators in BM using finitely many coefficients from M and finitely many elements of B. Enlarge A_0 inside M to contain these coefficients. The resulting finite-dimensional subalgebras inside BM are domains, hence fields. Relations give the required upper dimension bounds; linear independence after embedding into F_i gives the lower bounds. Include the expressions in both directions at the final stage to ensure the endpoint is exactly BA_0, not an unspecified finite overfield. Algebraicity of M/A makes this coefficient field finite. No separability or linear-disjointness assumption is needed. This finite-data comparison is substantive, so it was isolated rather than hidden in a long proof step.
