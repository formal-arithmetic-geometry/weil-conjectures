### Explicit counterexample to the original clause

Set ell = 3, F = Q_3, A = Z_3, and Omega = the algebraic closure of Q_3 with its norm topology. Let k be an algebraic closure of F_5. Let S be the one-point compactification of the positive integers, and let R = C(S,k_discrete), the ring of eventually constant sequences of elements of k. Put V = Spec R. Its underlying Stone space is S; in particular V is affine and qcqs. Denote by e_n the idempotent supported at n.

Choose an elliptic curve C over k and a connected finite etale isogeny q:C' -> C with deck group of order 2. Such an isogeny exists by quotienting an elliptic curve by a subgroup generated by a nonzero 2-torsion point. Its nonidentity deck map is translation by that point. Let L be the sign summand of q_* A on C; the idempotent defining it is (1-tau)/2. It is a locally free rank-one A local system, trivialized by q.

For each n choose a reduced closed subscheme D_n of C consisting of n distinct k-points. Inside C_V define the closed subscheme U by the quasi-coherent ideal generated by all e_n I(D_n). The fibre at n is D_n; the fibre at infinity is C. Equivalently U is the inverse limit of the schemes U_m that have fibres D_n for n <= m and are C over the remaining clopen tail. Every U_m is the finite disjoint union of those D_n and C times that tail. The transition maps are closed immersions. The map h:U -> V is proper, as a closed subscheme of the projective V-scheme C_V, and U is qcqs. It is not necessary or legitimate to infer finite presentation: indeed the unbounded number of components in the finite fibres prevents it here. Put J = L|_U. This is lisse of rank one for exactly the pro-etale finite-constant-model definition in the item, bounded in degree zero and of Tor amplitude [0,0]. It therefore belongs to D_ctf(U,A).

Here are the cohomology and section calculations, including the reasons the derived source does not create extra degree-zero classes.

1. For every r, R Gamma(C,L/3^r) = 0. Translation on an elliptic curve acts as the identity on its finite 3-primary cohomology. One way to check this without an SGA reference is Milne, *Abelian Varieties*, I, Theorem 12.1 and the addition/cup-product calculation immediately following it: H^1 is primitive under addition, and all cohomology is its exterior algebra. Pulling addition back along x -> (x,a) fixes H^1 and consequently every degree. Freeness of integral cohomology gives the same conclusion modulo 3^r. Thus the sign projector on the cohomology of C' is zero. Since q is finite etale and 2 is invertible, the cohomology of its sign summand is exactly that projector's image. This proves the asserted vanishing. The isogeny's existence is also supplied by Milne I, Section 7 (etale prime-to-characteristic torsion and quotients).
2. Finite-coefficient proper base change (Stacks Tag 095T, `etale-cohomology-theorem-proper-base-change`) applies to h without a finite-presentation hypothesis. Its finite-fibre calculation and step 1 give
   R h_*(J/3^r) = G_r[0], where G_r is the direct sum over the open points n of extension by zero of (A/3^r)^n. These are classical etale sheaves, pulled back to the pro-etale site, so their geometric stalks do suffice for this calculation. In particular G_r(V) is the algebraic direct sum of the finite-fibre groups. All transition maps G_(r+1) -> G_r are surjective.
3. J is locally free over the complete continuous ring A_U, hence J = R lim_r J/3^r. Direct image, being a right adjoint, preserves this derived limit. The pro-etale topos is replete, and a surjective tower of sheaves is acyclic for inverse limit: Bhatt–Scholze Propositions 4.2.8, 3.2.3, 3.1.9 and 3.1.10. Therefore R h_*J = M[0], with M = lim_r G_r. In particular
   M(V) = { (v_n), v_n in A^n : max_j |v_(n,j)| -> 0 }.
   This is the module of vectors tending uniformly to zero along the tail. It is not the direct sum. No commutation of inverse image with this inverse limit is being asserted.
4. Let U' = U times_C C'. Its profinite component space P' is the one-point compactification of the disjoint union of the 2n lifted points over n. This follows as well from the explicit U_m: any finite clopen partition descends to some finite stage, whose remaining C' tail is connected. The deck involution pairs the points over each D_n and fixes the limit component. Bhatt–Scholze Lemma 4.2.12 identifies sections of the actual continuous coefficient sheaf on this qcqs scheme with continuous functions on P'. The sign projector then identifies
   Gamma(U,J_Omega) = { (w_n), w_n in Omega^n : max_j |w_(n,j)| -> 0 }.
   The value on the limit component must be zero, because 2 is invertible and the involution acts trivially there. The same description gives M(V) for A. The rank-one local freeness of J identifies J_Omega with ordinary, as well as derived, scalar extension.
5. Since R h_*J is concentrated in degree zero, H^0 of its derived scalar extension is the ordinary sheaf tensor B_V tensor_(A_V) M, where B_V is the actual continuous Omega coefficient. Thus H^0 of the specified comparison is the multiplication map from that sheaf tensor to h_*J_Omega. Its local elementary tensor b tensor v specializes over the open point n to the vector b(n)v_n. A sum of N such tensors has all its n coordinates in the span of N elements of Omega over F. This bound remains valid after every base change of the base pro-etale site: evaluate at any geometric point above n. A section of the sheaf tensor is locally a finite sum of elementary tensors. A pro-etale cover of the qcqs V admits finitely many qcqs refinements that still cover V. Refining further for those tensor expressions and taking their maximum length therefore gives one finite bound N for all n. This argument concerns the sheaf tensor, not merely the tensor of global sections.
6. For each n choose n F-linearly independent integral elements alpha_(n,1),...,alpha_(n,n) in a finite extension of F of degree at least n. Set w_(n,j) = 3^n alpha_(n,j), and give the paired points on U' the opposite values. These functions tend uniformly to zero and hence, by step 4, define an actual section of h_*J_Omega on V. Their n coordinates have F-span of dimension n. For n > N this contradicts the bound in step 5. The section has no local preimage under H^0 of the comparison. Thus the original rho is not an isomorphism, already in degree zero.

This counterexample satisfies every incoming hypothesis: properness, qcqs, finite integral constant local models, finite Tor amplitude, and even ell invertible on the schemes. It uses precisely the all-continuous norm topology. For a fixed finite coefficient field E/F the span is bounded by [E:F], so it gives no obstruction to the corrected statement. For filtered-union coefficients the displayed function is not a section; that convention does not repair the original norm-topological assertion.
