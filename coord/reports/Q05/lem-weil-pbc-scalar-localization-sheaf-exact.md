## Original argument retained verbatim

### Original step 1

Let $P$ be the presheaf of $R$-algebras $P(V)=R(V)[1/s|_V]$, with the localization restriction maps of [def-weil-pbc-ringed-section-localization]. For every $V$, apply [lem-weil-pbc-localization-flat] to the multiplicative subset $\{1,s|_V,(s|_V)^2,\ldots\}$ of the commutative ring $R(V)$. Thus $P(V)$ is flat over $R(V)$. This argument also permits $0$ in the multiplicative subset: if $s|_V$ is nilpotent then $P(V)=0$, which is flat.

### Original step 2

We verify the presheaf finite-limit hypothesis needed below, rather than assuming exactness on sheaf sections. In the category of presheaves of $R$-modules [def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification], kernels are formed by $V\mapsto\ker(f_V)$ and finite products by sectionwise products: the restriction maps preserve the kernel equations, and the universal maps are obtained componentwise. For a map $f_V:A(V)\to B(V)$, apply [lem-weil-pbc-flat-tensor-exact] with the flat module $P(V)$ to $0\to\ker(f_V)\to A(V)\xrightarrow{f_V}B(V)$. It identifies $\ker(f_V)\otimes_{R(V)}P(V)$ with the kernel of $f_V\otimes1$, including its kernel inclusion. Tensoring also preserves finite products: for a finite family the mutually inverse maps are $(a_i)_i\otimes p\mapsto(a_i\otimes p)_i$ and the sum of the maps induced by the coordinate inclusions; for the empty family both sides are zero. These maps commute with restrictions because their formulas use only restrictions, addition and tensoring. By [def-pur-presheaf-tensor-product], the functor $-\otimes_{p,R}P$ therefore preserves finite products and kernels. Every finite limit is the kernel of the difference of the two incidence maps between the finite products over objects and arrows of its diagram, so it preserves finite limits.

### Original step 3

Apply [lem-pc-sites-modules-sheafification-flat] with $\mathcal O=R$ and $\mathcal F=P$, using step 2. As $R$ is already a sheaf, $R^\#=R$, and by construction $P^\#=R_s$ as an $R$-module. Thus $R_s$ is a flat $R$-module sheaf. By [def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules], $M\mapsto M\otimes_R R_s$ is exact as an endofunctor of $\operatorname{Mod}(R)$. The symmetry and additivity in [lem-pc-sheaf-modules-tensor-closed] give that $T(M)=R_s\otimes_R M$, with $T(f)=1\otimes f$, is an additive exact endofunctor of $\operatorname{Mod}(R)$.

### Original step 4

Let $V=\operatorname{Res}_{\varphi}:\operatorname{Mod}(R_s)\to\operatorname{Mod}(R)$ be restriction of scalars [def-pc-sheaf-restrict-scalars]. There is a natural identification $VE\cong T$. Indeed, both underlying abelian sheaves are the abelian sheafification of the very same presheaf $W\mapsto R_s(W)\otimes_{R(W)}M(W)$, by [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product, def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification]. In both, a local $a\in R(W)$ acts by $b\otimes m\mapsto\varphi(a)b\otimes m=b\otimes am$; the equality is the balancing relation. Thus the identity on this abelian sheafification is $R$-linear. Both functors send $f$ to the sheafification of $b\otimes m\mapsto b\otimes f(m)$, which proves naturality. The $R_s$-action retained in $E$ is multiplication on the first tensor factor.

### Original step 5

For either ring sheaf $A=R$ or $A=R_s$, [lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-colimits-forgetful] gives a faithful exact functor $U_A$ to abelian sheaves, with kernels computed sectionwise and cokernels the sheafified sectionwise cokernels. Consequently exactness can be checked after $U_A$: the kernel, image and cokernel have the same underlying abelian sheaves, and an underlying zero sheaf has only zero sections and is the zero module sheaf. Now $U_{R_s}E=U_RVE\cong U_RT$. Since $T$ and $U_R$ are exact, the image under $U_{R_s}$ of any short exact sequence after applying $E$ is short exact. The preceding test proves exactness in $\operatorname{Mod}(R_s)$. Also $U_{R_s}$ is faithful and leaves addition of morphisms unchanged; additivity of $T$ gives $U_{R_s}(E(f+g))=U_{R_s}(E(f)+E(g))$, and similarly for zero maps. Faithfulness gives additivity of $E$. No surjectivity assertion on sections of a sheaf epimorphism has been used.

### Original step 6

To obtain the finite-limit and finite-colimit formulation, work in the two abelian module categories [def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category]. For any arrow $a:M\to N$, the two short exact sequences $0\to\ker a\to M\to\operatorname{im}a\to0$ and $0\to\operatorname{im}a\to N\to\operatorname{coker}a\to0$ show, after applying step 5, that $E(\ker a)\to E(M)$ is a kernel of $E(a)$ and $E(N)\to E(\operatorname{coker}a)$ is its cokernel. Additivity preserves the biproduct equations $p_i i_j=\delta_{ij}$ and $\sum_i i_i p_i=1$, including the zero object. Finite diagram limits and colimits are respectively kernels and cokernels of the differences of the two incidence maps between these finite biproducts. Hence $E$ preserves both. Finally let $\alpha:F\xrightarrow{\sim}E$ be any natural isomorphism of functors. For $f:M\to N$, $F(f)=\alpha_N^{-1}E(f)\alpha_M$, so the same conjugation proves additivity. The components of $\alpha$ identify the image under $F$ of every short exact sequence with its exact image under $E$, proving exactness and thus both finite preservation assertions for $F$ as well.
