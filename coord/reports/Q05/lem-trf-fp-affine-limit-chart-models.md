1. **Qcqs limit.** I opened every cited statement. `lem-weil-pbc-limit-affine-transition` explicitly asserts affine projections. `lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-finite-affine-open-cover` supplies a finite affine cover of the given qc stage. `lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs` supplies compactness of its affine inverse images. The forward and converse clauses of `lem-weil-pbc-quasi-separated-affine-cover-criterion` supply respectively compactness of stage overlaps and quasi-separatedness from the resulting cover upstairs. Crucially, inverse images of those overlaps need not be affine: `lem-weil-pbc-affine-morphism-permanence` supplies quasi-compactness of the projection and `lem-weil-pbc-quasi-compact-morphism-preimage-qc-open` supplies compactness of their inverse images. Thus the proof does not assume separatedness or that affine overlaps are affine.
2. **Finite refinements.** The finite-cover nodes apply to any quasi-compact scheme/open, not only a Noetherian one. `pqf-lem-qs-intersection-qc` states the intersection assertion for arbitrary quasi-compact opens on a quasi-separated scheme. With the result of Step 1 and compactness of affine opens this gives pair, refinement-overlap and iterated triple intersections. The triple charts are chosen inside the intersections of one member from each pair cover, so they are genuinely subordinate to all three. The covering argument is the pointwise finite choice of the three pair members, written in the step. Empty intersections use empty covers.
3. **Labelled descent.** `lem-trf-affine-open-limit-descent` states that each affine open, with no cover restriction, is exactly the inverse image of a stage affine. The system is small and cofiltered via the opposite directed set, and the stages are quasi-separated, as required. A finite common upper bound includes the prescribed initial index. The affine-morphism definition says precisely why pulling those stage affines further back preserves affineness. No claim that the original cover automatically covers at that stage is made.
4. **Incidences and covers.** `pqf-lem-limit-qc-open-descends` clause (2), not merely its existence clause, asserts eventual equality of two descended quasi-compact opens with equal limit inverse images. Each inclusion is encoded as equality with an intersection, and each cover as equality of a finite union with its target. I checked quasi-compactness of both expressions using the affine compactness and qs-intersection statements, the given compactness of the whole stage, and the elementary finite-union argument. Equality is applied finitely often at a common stage, and every equality persists by inverse image. This also proves cover equations with an empty covering family and removes possible extra stage components invisible in the limit.
5. **Canonical ring colimits.** The restriction clause of `lem-weil-pbc-limit-affine-transition` gives the actual restricted scheme limit, and its affine clause identifies it with the spectrum of the colimit of the actual section rings. This yields the canonical comparison, rather than an unrelated abstract ring isomorphism. Every stage chart is affine by the affine-morphism definition. The argument works on any later tail, so moving the final index does not lose clause (2).
6. **Algebraic local objects.** `lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-finite-type-affine` explicitly includes finite presentation implies quasi-coherence and finite presentation of sections on every affine open. Thus there is no hidden assumption that F was already quasi-coherent or had a global presentation on S. `lem-pc-fp-module-map-descent-affine` explicitly supplies the object model over a directed ring colimit. Its application is to the small nonempty tail above h, so its model index is automatically at least h. Only its object clause is used.
7. **Sheaf realization.** `lem-pc-fp-affine-sections-realization` is stated for arbitrary affine X,Y, a quasi-coherent module on Y, a finite-presentation module over the global ring of X, and a supplied scalar-extension isomorphism. Those are exactly the Step 6 data with X the stage chart and Y its limit chart. Its conclusion provides the finite-presentation quasi-coherent sheaf and the needed pullback isomorphism. It assumes neither a Noetherian stage nor any fixed ambient affine-base family.
8. **One index and sections.** `lem-pc-tensor-pullback-finite-presentation` has no flatness hypothesis; it therefore applies to the arbitrary restricted transitions used to move each local model to one upper bound. The quasi-coherence clause already checked in Step 6 applies to these finite-presentation sheaves. `def-pc-module-pullback` supplies the named canonical composition identifications. `lem-weil-pbc-module-pullback-affine-sections` explicitly gives the canonical isomorphism on affine sections, with formula sending a pure tensor to the pullback section multiplied by its scalar. Composing that map with the section map of e_d gives exactly the sections assertion, after the elementary symmetry of tensor factors. The statement does not demand that the independently chosen models glue, and none of these steps asserts that.
