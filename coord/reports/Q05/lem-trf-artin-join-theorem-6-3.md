For the internal-normalization refinement I compared the two normalized centers in the proof of Theorem 6.3. The argument I intend for this auxiliary is as follows. In L=Frac(J), let B be the integral closure of P. The integral closure S_i of H_i in L is a local henselian domain, since H_i is henselian. Since J is normal and contains H_i, S_i is contained in J. With r_i the contraction of the maximal ideal of S_i to B, the usual normalization/localization description of the chosen henselization inside its algebraic field gives S_i=B_{r_i}: the chosen H_i lies in this localization, which is normal, and its elements are integral over H_i. This identification, including compatibility with the original embeddings, is a substantive part of the new normalization lemma, not an uncited elementary step in the item. If r_1 and r_2 were comparable, the reverse inclusion of B-localizations would make the generated join equal to one S_i: both S_i lie in J and their union of generators contains both H_i. That would make J integral over that H_i and hence the map local, contrary to the node's hypothesis. Thus r_1 and r_2 are incomparable. Choose an integral element in each difference and put both in a finite K-subextension K_0 of L. Their contractions to the normalization P_0 remain incomparable. The corresponding P_0-local rings embed into the appropriate S_i; their henselizations embed there as well, since S_i is henselian, using the compatible branches. The enlarged henselizations contain H_i and are contained in J, so together generate exactly J. This explains why K_0 is internal and why the stronger equality in the new statement is justified. It uses no real-closed descent assertion. The statement does not hide this argument as a proof sketch: it remains statement-only, and records that its source formulation is by an agent.
