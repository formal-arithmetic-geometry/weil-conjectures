## Review by Q05

I read BLR §4.4/1 and its proof at printed pp.109–110 in the primary scan. Its hypothesis “S-rational” is exactly fibrewise dense domain, as §2.5 explicitly defines. Its codimension condition is ambient local-ring dimension at most one; it is not just relative fibre codimension. These are the two separate hypotheses retained in the item. The group is only assumed smooth and separated, with no connectedness or commutativity. Uniqueness follows from the existing relative separatedness lemma and reducedness, not from absolute separatedness of H. I read the complete statements of all the cited nodes while preparing citations.json, rather than relying on their titles or proofs.

Step 1 uses `lem-weil-pbc-smooth-restrict-open-subschemes` for smooth restriction, `lem-sfb-smooth-normal-ascent` for normality and `lem-trf-smooth-reduced-dense-pullback` for reducedness and local Noetherianity. The latter explicitly allows non-surjective and non-separated smooth maps. The base is reduced because its normal stalks are domains. Affineness plus local Noetherianity gives a Noetherian source open. The density argument here is elementary: all generic points have dimension-zero stalks and are in U, so every nonempty open meets U. Open restrictions preserve the stalks.

Step 2 takes only clause (2) of `lem-pc-proper-base-change`, which actually states smooth base-change stability. The same smooth-density node applies to each projection to T, since T is reduced locally Noetherian. Intersection of two dense opens is dense. Multiplication and inverse are part of the given group data; the diagonal equality is the inverse identity. Base-changing the closed diagonal to S_0 preserves the quotient ideal on affine charts, giving the relative separatedness needed by the new lemma.

Step 3 cites the entire new section lemma. Its base T is normal Noetherian by step 1; its smooth map is the first projection from step 2, and its section is the diagonal. The target is separated over B=S_0, not necessarily over T or absolutely separated. The agreeing morphism is b=e composed with the structural map. The preimage of D is exactly U_T, so its codimension-at-most-one hypothesis is the original one. This check is why the new lemma is stated with an independent B and a relatively separated B-target. The source of the new node is the codimension/affine-target portion of BLR's proof, generalized only by replacing the diagonal by a smooth section. Its statement contains no proof sketch.

For the future splitter of the new lemma, the argument is the one in BLR: take the maximal domain of the map, restrict its target to an affine neighbourhood of b(z), use BLR Lemma 2 to see that its missing locus is purely divisorial, and compare its intersection with the section. Locally a section of a smooth relative-dimension-d map is cut out by d equations and has the smooth local dimension formula. A divisor meeting it would give codimension at most one on the section, contradicting the assumed absence in codimension at most one. Thus the map is defined near the section. Those affine-chart existence, pure-codimension and dimension-comparison facts belong to the new node's eventual split; they are not silently assumed by an item step. This is a geometric regularity lemma with no group structure, rather than a restatement of the item.

Step 4 uses the same base-change and smooth-restriction statements for W→T, then `lem-pc-smooth-flat` for both flatness and local finite presentation and `lem-weil-pbc-flat-lfp-open` for openness. I checked the latter's statement, which explicitly includes all base changes. Surjectivity is written out using the diagonal point and `lem-sc-scheme-over-field-universally-open`: the field-scheme projection has open nonempty image meeting U_s. This avoids an uncited claim that topological density is always preserved under arbitrary base change. Finally the exact finite-affine formulation of `def-weil-pbc-fpqc-covering` is proved from open images and quasi-compactness of each affine target, without any global finiteness assumption on W.

Step 5 constructs h=v·u(second coordinate). On f^{-1}(U_T), cancellation is in the indicated order; it does not commute factors. For C=W×_T W, smooth base change and the smooth-density node give reducedness and a dense inverse image of U_T by successive maps C→W→T. The two h maps then agree over a genuinely dense open of the reduced C; `lem-sfb-relative-separated-dominant-ext` supplies equality with precisely this hypothesis and a relatively separated target. It is not being used to construct an extension.

Step 6 uses `lem-weil-pbc-fpqc-representable-descent`, whose statement is exactly the equalizer/descent of compatible morphisms. Step 5 supplies the compatibility, and step 4 supplies an fpqc cover. Applying its uniqueness clause also with target S checks that the descended morphism is over S, rather than merely a scheme morphism. Restricting the cover to U_T has the same flat/open finite-affine argument and detects its agreement with u.

Step 7 uses the relative separatedness lemma again on reduced overlaps with their dense U restrictions, and the exact existing scheme-morphism gluing statement. These permit an arbitrary index set, so infinitely many affine source opens cause no loss. Uniqueness uses dense U in reduced Z.
