For step 1 I opened `lem-trf-monoid-prime-minimal-neighborhood`: it explicitly gives the finite-generator sum, the intersection of all basic opens containing the prime, minimality, and the localization equality. The cited monoscheme definition gives sections on each basic open. I checked the extra passage from an affine neighborhood to the whole monoscheme by intersecting any other neighborhood with that affine. The identification with the stalk is justified by restricting representatives to the smallest neighborhood; it does not assert that arbitrary scheme stalk spectra are open.

Step 2 uses the continuous map and sheaf morphism of the monoscheme definition. Taking the inverse image of the smallest target neighborhood proves the required factorization. Both section and stalk maps send the same representative to its image germ; this is an elementary restriction argument, with no hidden affine-representability theorem. Step 3 cites the exact monoscheme clause of `lem-trf-fine-monoid-local-smooth-charts`: its statement supplies an isomorphism of the smallest source neighborhood and identifies the arrow as the first-factor inclusion. I checked the hypotheses are precisely fineness and smoothness in force. I have not used its extra fan or localization clauses.

Step 4 uses only the definition of a monoid algebra as finite sums. The map and inverse on the bases are written explicitly, multiplication agrees by exponent addition, and restriction to the coefficient monoid is literally the inclusion. This works with torsion in P, because monoid-algebra basis vectors remain indexed by elements of P, irrespective of nilpotents or zero divisors in k[P]. It is an elementary calculation, not a new comparison lemma.

For step 5 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-principal-localization-open-immersion`, `def-sbc-affine-space`, `sbc-lem-affine-space-smooth` and `lem-pc-open-immersion-smooth-composition`. I read Stacks Tag 01I3, `schemes.tex`, lemma-standard-open-affine and its proof, Tag 00T7, `algebra.tex`, lemma-standard-smooth (apply with zero equations), and Tags 01VA/01VC, `morphisms.tex`, lemma-composition-smooth/lemma-open-immersion-smooth. The elementary identity B[1/(product y_j)] = A[x,y_j^{+-1}] is proved with explicit inverse formulas. Thus no assertion that a monoid algebra over a field is regular, reduced, or a domain is used. The only off-unit non-Mathlib statement, affine-space smoothness, is declared in split_report.tool_uses as a generic scheme tool.

Step 6 verifies covering on the realized space, rather than assuming every scheme point comes from a monoid point. Each original affine monoid chart is the smallest neighborhood of its maximal monoid prime: the nonunits form that prime, proper primes contain no units, and every prime therefore generalizes it. Minimality in the whole space follows from step 1. Consequently every original realized affine chart is among the realized smallest neighborhoods, and those original charts cover by the gluing definition. This uses the actual statements of the fine-monoid and toric-realization definitions, rather than an unstated surjectivity property of a map of spectra.

I checked the edge cases P={0}, P a finite abelian group (including characteristic dividing its order), the generic prime, the maximal monoid prime, the empty generating set, E minus the prime empty, r=0, t=0, and an empty source monoscheme. In the empty sum case a=0 and D(0) is the whole appropriate monoid chart; at a maximal prime all omitted generators are units. With t=0 the empty product is 1 and B_1=B, so the same smoothness argument applies. There is no nonempty-fibre assumption or characteristic restriction.
