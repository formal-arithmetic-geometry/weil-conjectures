## Statement corrected

The original definition used the ordinary left Kan extension along the fibre at [1] as its edge object and defined Q_C^S by locality for those extensions. For terminal C, that extension is J(phi)=phi times the represented 1-simplex. Thus J(empty) is the empty presheaf, and J(point) is the represented 1-simplex. Locality for their map forces the total edge space Z_1 to be contractible. The Segal presheaf for the algebroid over the terminal coefficient category with empty object space has Z_1 empty; the one with two discrete objects has Z_1 a four-point space. Neither is local. Indeed the degeneracy and face maps make Z_0 contractible whenever Z_1 is contractible, and the Segal condition then makes every level contractible. The old Q_C^S is therefore terminal. In contrast P^S(C) is the terminal coefficient category, whose algebroids form Spc with unrestricted object spaces. This is the return's E2/E7 counterexample, not a missing hypothesis of the item.

The corrected definition first constructs E_C(phi) under two fixed endpoint representables. Its underlying presheaf is the pushout J_C(phi) amalgamated with the two endpoints along the copower of those endpoints by colim phi. This is an actual construction with no unspecified data. For a representable phi=y(c), colim phi is contractible, so E_C(phi) is the usual labelled edge. For phi empty, it is the two endpoints, rather than the empty presheaf. For terminal C, E_C(phi) is (phi times Delta^1) amalgamated with its two endpoints along phi times the boundary. Consequently E(empty) → E(point) is boundary → Delta^1.

The revised model node states the precise free-edge test: maps from E_C(phi) to Z form a space over Z_0 squared with fibre Map(phi,H_Z(x,y)). This follows directly by mapping the defining pushout into Z: the resulting pullback is

    Map(phi,Z_1) ×_{Map(colim phi,Z_0²)} Z_0².

Taking its fibre at (x,y) gives Map(phi,H_Z(x,y)). The endpoints are specified before taking this fibre. The source and target of every E_C(s) have the same two endpoints, so locality of the total map is equivalent to locality of every fibre over Z_0². Thus Q_C^S consists exactly of Segal presheaves with S-local mapping presheaves H_Z(x,y). The unlocalized algebroid comparison identifies those fibres with the enriched Hom objects, proving that this is the correct restriction for Algd(P^S(C)). Monoidal coefficient reflection gives its reflector, fixes the object space, and localizes the Hom objects. No condition is imposed on Z_0 itself.

For terminal C and S={empty → point}, locality now means Z_1 → Z_0² is an equivalence. For every space X, the Segal presheaf Z_n=X^(n+1) has this property. It is exactly the unique terminal-coefficient algebroid with object space X. This includes X empty, point and two discrete points. The corrected category is Spc, as required. This repairs E2/E7; step 4 now uses this corrected comparison and explicitly describes its object spaces and mapping fibres, repairing E3.

## Source error

The online Haugseng text's Proposition 8.5 describes locality of the whole restriction at [1]. That condition fails the counterexample above. In Observation 8.6, an ordinary fibre left Kan extension is identified with a labelled simplex having fixed vertices; these differ for an empty label, since a left adjoint carries the empty presheaf to the empty presheaf. Observation 8.7's ensuing localization therefore cannot be applied literally with that definition. We correct the intended construction to colimits of represented edges taken under their fixed vertices; its pushout formula is in the definition node. We correct Proposition 8.5 to Hom-fibre locality, as its algebroid interpretation requires. The model theorem records the mapping-space formula explicitly.

For the coefficient-local clause of `lem-coef-segal-external-local-equivalences`, the section-8 simplex argument must accordingly use chains formed from these fixed-endpoint edges. Their endpoints persist when a label is empty. The square of two free edges is decomposed into its two ordered triangles along their diagonal; the diagonal label is the external product of the labels, and the other labels are their products with the appropriate tensor units. Locality on a labelled chain is tested one edge at a time by the Segal condition, with the chain's vertices fixed. The compatible coefficient class S odot T contains each required one-variable label change and its tensor translates. The resulting map of these two-triangle diagrams is therefore local after coefficient reflection; their pushout and then colimits of labelled simplices give the external-product local-equivalence statement. This is a reconstruction of the intended Lemma 8.11/Corollaries 8.13–8.14 argument with the corrected generators, not reliance on the false total-edge equivalence. That foundational statement remains unsplit for its later worker; it asserts preservation of a local-equivalence class, not the bilinear colimit theorem being proved here.

The adjoint-direction typo in the proof of Corollary 8.15 is also corrected: L is left adjoint to the fully faithful inclusion i. Accordingly a local-category colimit is L applied to the ambient colimit of the included diagram. The seven-step proof uses precisely this orientation.
