## Review by Q05

I checked the unchanged statement against the precise algebra paragraph of 04MK, not against the broader geometric proposition alone. The source only uses commutativity for abelian unit groups and the two embedded fields for inverses; none of its geometric assumptions is used after finite generation of the ambient quotient has been supplied. The statement therefore legitimately makes no reducedness or domain assumption. Its finite extension conclusion is a vector-space conclusion, not a claim of equality of the two subfields.

Step 1 cites the sentence in `def-weil-field` saying nonzero elements have multiplicative inverses. The inverse of a common nonzero element in the two fields must coincide in A by uniqueness of an inverse. This proves that the intersection really is a field with the same 1, without treating A as a field. Closure under the ring operations and compatibility of the embeddings are elementary consequences of the stated subring hypotheses.

Step 2 has no external citation: it constructs the quotient homomorphism, checks that multiplication by an intersection unit leaves its ambient class unchanged, and computes the kernel explicitly. If a representative in L has its class trivial modulo K units, it lies in both fields and is nonzero, hence is an intersection unit. The resulting injection holds for arbitrary A, including zero divisors; no cancellation by a nonunit is used.

Step 3 cites the complete finite-basis assertion of `lem-weil-subgroup-finite-free-abelian-group-free`. I checked that its J is the finite index set of the chosen generators of Q, and its N is exactly the inverse image T of P under the explicitly defined surjection from Z^r. This T is a subgroup of a finite free integer group, precisely the cited hypotheses. Images of a finite basis generate P because every p lifts to T, and the injection identifies B with P. This is a short assembly using an existing theorem; it is not an uncited appeal to the structure theorem of finitely generated abelian groups. The step is marked non-elementary because it invokes the finite-free theorem, while the maps and image calculation are written out.

Step 4 cites `def-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra`, whose statement explicitly equates finite algebra generation with a surjective evaluation from a finite polynomial ring. Each nonzero field element is a scalar times an integer-power monomial in the chosen quotient representatives. Adding the inverses as another finite list of polynomial generators converts all integer exponents to nonnegative exponents. The zero element is also in the image. This establishes *algebra* finite generation, which is stronger than and not confused with field finite generation. Laurent rings are not introduced as an extra unprovided construction. Only the surjectivity definition is used, so the step is elementary relative to the cited definition.

Step 5 cites all of `lem-weil-pbc-zariski-lemma`: it requires a field base, a field target and a finite type algebra map. Those are respectively step 1, the given L, and the exact polynomial surjection of step 4. Its conclusion is verbatim the desired finite-dimensionality. I did not cite an integral-closure definition as if it implied finite degree, and did not use the proof of a cited node for an extra conclusion absent from its statement.

I tested the degenerate generator cases r=0 and s=0: the inverse-image subgroup argument permits the empty basis, the polynomial ring in zero variables is the base field, and the conclusion is L=K intersection L, of degree one. K=L is included. Finite fields in a common finite field supply finite quotients and finite intersection degrees. For A=F_p[epsilon]/(epsilon^2) with K=L=F_p and for A=F_p x F_p with diagonal K=L=F_p, the hypotheses and the proof still hold, testing nilpotents and disconnected zero divisors. A transcendental field L=F(t) cannot be treated as finite type merely from field generation; step 4's unit hypothesis is indispensable and precisely what provides inverse generators. There is no characteristic or separability restriction.
