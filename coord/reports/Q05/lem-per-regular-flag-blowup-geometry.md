- Step 3 uses `prop-nagata-affine-blowup-chart-cover` for the cover and `lem-trf-blowup-chart-regular-sequence-presentation` for its quotient ring. Its regular-sequence hypothesis follows from Step 1. `def-nagata-blowup-chart-ring` states the subalgebra of R localized at the chart generator; this makes that generator a nonzerodivisor. `lem-pur-regular-sequence-graded-polynomial` identifies the graded algebra by the canonical monomials, not merely its graded ranks. `def-nagata-effective-cartier-ideal` supplies only the Cartier definition, which the computed equation satisfies, and `def-weil-pbc-proj-twisting-sheaf` specifies the degree-one homogeneous-fraction twist used to check the transition functions.

The rank is n > 0, so its n+1-rank notation applies with parameter n-1. The local basis proves regularity of i prime and j prime i prime explicitly.

This proves surjectivity and the kernel bases; it does not infer the kernel comparison from ranks alone. The map between kernels comes from the canonical first conormal map, so the chart computations certify the canonical global isomorphism.
- Step 6 reapplies the regular-sequence chart theorem to J and to its quotient modulo f. The g sequence is regular in R/(f), and permutation is used only in the local ring as in Step 1. The chart definition embeds each quotient chart in the correct localization, proving that a remains a nonzerodivisor after t=0.
