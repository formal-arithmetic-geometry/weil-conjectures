### Returned step 5: lem-sfb-twisted-module-presheaf-equivalence

### Returned step 7: lem-sfb-equivariant-module-sheafification-reflector

By [def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification] its underlying abelian sheaf is the abelian sheafification; [lem-pc-sites-sheafification-underlying-sets] identifies its underlying set sheaf and unit with set sheafification. Set sheafification preserves finite products [lem-weil-pbc-sheafification-sets-exact]. Sheafify the natural set map $K\times P\to P$. Since $K$ is already a sheaf, this produces a map $K\times A\to A$ compatible with the unit $P\to A$. Its unit and associativity identities are the sheafifications of the original identities on $P$ and $K\times K\times P$. Its additivity and scalar-linearity identities are obtained in exactly the same way from the two equal maps on $K\times P\times P$ and $K\times R\times P$, respectively. Product preservation identifies their sheafifications with the corresponding products involving $A$, $K$ and $R$, since $K,R$ are sheaves. Thus all the required laws hold, and the extended action is unique because maps from the sheafification of $K\times P$ to the sheaf $A$ are determined by their composites with its unit.

### Returned step 8: lem-sfb-equivariant-module-sheafification-reflector

For an equivariant map $M\to N$, its sectionwise kernel is stable under $K(U)$ since the map is equivariant. It is the underlying kernel sheaf on $C$ and has the kernel universal property among equivariant maps by restricting their images. The sectionwise cokernel $P(U)=N(U)/\operatorname{im}M(U)$ inherits the linear $K(U)$-action and restriction maps. Its module sheafification on $C$, with the action of step 7, is the equivariant cokernel: maps killing $M$ factor uniquely first through the quotient presheaf and then, equivariantly by step 8, through its sheafification. The formulas agree with the ordinary module kernels and cokernels [def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category], so forgetting creates both. Finally the equivalence changes no section modules: a module sheaf on $C_K$ has zero restriction exactly when every section module is zero, which is exactly the zero module sheaf on $C_K$. This also covers the trivial group, the zero ring, and objects admitting empty covers.
