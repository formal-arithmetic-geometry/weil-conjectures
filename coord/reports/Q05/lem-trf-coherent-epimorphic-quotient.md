## Argument for the new statement-only auxiliary

This proof plan belongs in the report; the new node itself is unsplit. Let G be the finite-limit-closed coherent generating family. The terminal object is coherent by definition, so any product of two quasi-compact objects is quasi-compact by quasi-separatedness of the terminal object. A quasi-compact subobject T of a coherent object U is coherent: for quasi-compact B,C mapping to T, their pullback over T equals their pullback over U because T → U is monic.

To show that P × Q is quasi-separated for coherent P,Q, test maps from quasi-compact B,C, and cover them by finitely many coherent generators G_i,H_j. It suffices, by local pullback stability and finite epimorphic-image quasi-compactness, to prove G_i ×_{P×Q} H_j quasi-compact. Put U=G_i×H_j, a coherent generator product, and E=G_i×_P H_j. Quasi-separatedness of P makes E quasi-compact. Its map to U is monic, so E is coherent by the preceding subobject observation. Also E×E is quasi-compact (terminal-object argument), and its mono into the coherent generator product U×U makes E×E coherent. The two Q-components give maps f,g:E→Q. Since Q is quasi-separated, E×_{Q,f,g}E is quasi-compact. Their equalizer is

Eq(f,g) = (E×_{Q,f,g}E) ×_{E×E} E,

where the last E maps by its diagonal. The base E×E is coherent, so its quasi-separatedness makes this pullback quasi-compact. It is exactly G_i×_{P×Q}H_j. This proves the binary-product assertion. Induction proves every nonempty finite product; the empty product is the coherent terminal object. This argument uses generators and equality loci and is more than a two-line definitional step, while making no use of the epimorphic-quotient criterion being split.
