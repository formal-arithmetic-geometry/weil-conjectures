## STATEMENT-DISPUTED: missing qcqs hypothesis in (a)

[Stacks Tag 0F14](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0F14), `etale-cohomology-lemma-kunneth-one-proper`, explicitly assumes that Y is quasi-compact and quasi-separated. The imported node assumed only that Y was a scheme over k. That stronger assertion is false.

Let k be algebraically closed, X = Spec k, E = the constant sheaf A = direct sum over j in the positive integers of Z/2, concentrated in degree zero. Let Y be the disjoint union over i in the positive integers of Spec k, and let K = Z_Y in degree zero. These satisfy every imported hypothesis of (a). In degree zero the Kunneth map is

`A ⊗_Z (product_i Z) → product_i A`.

Its image consists of sequences whose coordinates all lie in the span of a common finite set of the basis vectors of A: every tensor is a finite sum of pure tensors, and each first factor has finite support. The sequence with i-th coordinate equal to the i-th basis vector is outside the image. There are no higher sections on the components, and product of abelian groups is exact; alternatively only H^0 is needed. H^0 of derived tensor of modules in degree zero is their ordinary tensor. Hence the Kunneth map is not an isomorphism. The purported inverse of the constant projection map in the imported theta does not exist in this example.

I restored exactly the source's qcqs hypothesis in (a), and retained all the original hypotheses in (b), including invertibility of n. Both existing users, `lem-kun-0f14a` and `lem-kun-0f14b`, already have respectively qcqs Y and finite-type Y, so neither use loses its applicable case. No target node is changed.

The fourth original arrow started at RΓ(Y, underline A ⊗ K) but cited the canonical constant projection isomorphism, which goes from B ⊗ A to RΓ(Y, K ⊗ underline A). The corrected statement inserts RΓ of the tensor symmetry before the inverse of this map. The final symmetry B ⊗ A → A ⊗ B is still present. The first arrow is explicitly the canonical normalized unbounded Leray comparison; this agrees with the bounded or finite-cd presentation of that same comparison and avoids invoking a lower-bound assertion for a derived tensor over arbitrary rings.
