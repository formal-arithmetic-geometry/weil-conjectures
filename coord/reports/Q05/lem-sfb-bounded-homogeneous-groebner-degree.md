## Arguments retained for the statement-only children

Controlled sequences: form the tree of finite tuples satisfying the two stated conditions. At depth i the next tuple lies in the finite degree ball of radius h(i). If prefix lengths were unbounded, the root would have arbitrarily long extensions; among its finitely many children one must also have arbitrarily long extensions. Repeating this choice constructs an infinite sequence all of whose prefixes are bad, contradicting the sequence clause of the existing Dickson leaf. Thus some finite length bound exists. This argument covers nonmonotone control and r=0 (the only tuple is empty, so a bad list has length at most one). It is the mathematical content left for the new node, not silently treated as elementary inside the item.

Criterion: for a nonzero ideal element choose a finitely supported monomial-multiple representation minimizing its largest leading exponent delta. If the polynomial has smaller leading exponent, the coefficients of all summands at delta sum to zero. Fix one of these summands; subtract its normalized version from each of the others. Their differences are monomial multiples of the corresponding pair polynomials, multiplied up from their coordinatewise maximum to delta. Each supplied strict representation then replaces those differences by terms below delta. The unselected terms already lie below delta. This contradicts minimality. Thus delta is the polynomial's leading exponent and contains a generator leading exponent. Every representation and substitution uses finitely many generators even if B is infinite. This is the strict-standard-representation version of the lecture's cancellation proof; no termination bound is concealed in this criterion.
