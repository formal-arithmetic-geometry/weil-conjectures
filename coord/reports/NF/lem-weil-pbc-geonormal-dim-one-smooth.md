## Review by NF

I read varieties.tex at 0GK5 (lemma-dim-1-projective-completion-after-insep), at 038T (definition-geometrically-regular), and at 038X (lemma-geometrically-regular-smooth); properties.tex at 0BX2 (lemma-normal-dimension-1-regular) and 033M/033N (normal Noetherian/locally Noetherian component decomposition); algebra.tex at 00P3 (lemma-dimension-preserved-field-extension) and 00PD (lemma-characterize-dvr). The item matches the 0GK5 auxiliary reasoning, with its additional reducedness and irreducibility clauses justified explicitly. The sole new criterion matches 038T's finitely generated extension quantifier and the pointwise equivalence of 038X applied at every point. It requires locally finite type, not merely an arbitrary algebra, and does not assume the field perfect. Both implications are supplied by the quoted source; the new node contains no proof sketch.

For step 1 I opened the statements of def-weil-finite-type-morphism, lem-sbc-finite-affine-tensor-base-change, def-weil-open-immersion, def-pc-fibre-product, and lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian. The tensor lemma has arbitrary commutative base algebras and a finite coproduct; taking one summand gives the chart formula. The inverse-image open assertion is written out using the open factorization and pullback universal property. Expanding pure tensors gives generators, so the finite cover proves finite type without assuming separatedness. The finite-type Noetherianity lemma then applies over Spec L: a field has only the two ideals, its spectrum has one point, and is Noetherian. This is a genuine generic tool use of the off-unit tensor lemma, recorded in split_report.tool_uses; it does not replace a proper-base-change unit step.

For step 2 I read def-kun-topological-krull-dimension, lem-weil-pbc-dimension-preserved-field-extension, and lem-sc-scheme-stalk-dimension-generalization-chains. The first dimension lemma really is for every field extension and every finite type algebra, not only curves over algebraically closed fields. The stalk lemma identifies dimension with the chains beginning at the closure of the point, which bounds it by the whole affine chart dimension. Taking closures of a chain in an open subset preserves strictness since restriction recovers the original chain. The affine charts are finite type by their choice in step 1, and their spectra have dimension at most one before scalar extension. Empty charts introduce no stalk and may be ignored. No equality of the dimension of an arbitrary infinite-type scalar extension was used.

For step 3 I read def-weil-pbc-normal-ring, the literal statement of lem-sc-normal-dim1-dvr, def-sc-discrete-valuation-ring, and def-weil-regular-scheme. Normality is given at every stalk of every scalar extension, rather than deduced from normality only over k. Noetherianity comes from step 1, and the dimension bound from step 2. The DVR theorem is applied only in dimension exactly one. Its definition says every ideal is principal, so the maximal ideal is generated by one element, which is exactly regularity in dimension one. In dimension zero the domain's zero prime and the maximal prime must coincide; the zero maximal ideal has an empty generating family. This repairs the possible gap of silently treating dimension zero as dimension one. Locally Noetherianity is included when passing from regular stalks to a regular scheme.

For step 4 I read the new criterion's exact statement. Its needed locally-finite-type hypothesis follows from the item's finite-type hypothesis, and the stronger assertion for all L proved in step 3 includes the finitely generated extensions in its quantifier. Thus it states precisely what is used, and no imperfect-field regular-implies-smooth inference is made.

For step 5 I opened def-pc-reduced-scheme and def-pc-geometrically-irreducible. They define reducedness through stalks and geometric reducedness through all field extensions respectively. The domain-to-reduced argument is explicitly elementary and independent of smoothness. In particular this does not require an uncited general theorem that smooth schemes are geometrically reduced.

For step 6 I opened lem-sc-regular-connected-integral and lem-pc-integral-scheme-irreducible. The former applies to a regular scheme with connected nonempty underlying space and concludes integral; the latter explicitly concludes irreducible from integral. The regular scheme was established for arbitrary L, so these are applicable under exactly the final clause's antecedent. I did not use a scheme declared normal merely by a definition that also requires irreducibility. The sources 033M/033N explain why connectedness is essential: normal components form a disjoint union.

It is not elementary, a duplicate, a clause of the item, or a Mathlib match.
