### Retained old Step 1

For a simplicial abelian group $Z$, let $C(Z)_n=Z_n$ with alternating-face differential, and let $FZ$ have $(FZ)_n=\mathbb Z[Z_n]$, with each simplicial operator sending $[z]$ to the basis vector indexed by the corresponding operator on $z$. The evaluation maps $\epsilon_Z:FZ\to Z$, $[z]\mapsto z$, commute with every simplicial operator; since $f$ is additive, $f\epsilon_X=\epsilon_Y Ff$. Use the cited normalization convention $N(Z)_0=Z_0$, $N(Z)_n=\bigcap_{i=1}^n\ker d_i$ for $n>0$, with differential $d_0$ in positive degrees. For each $Z=X,Y,FX,FY$, the cited natural chain homotopy equivalence $N(Z)\to C(Z)$ induces a natural homology isomorphism: if two chain maps differ by $dh+hd$, their values on a cycle differ by the boundary $dh(z)$, so homotopy inverse maps induce inverse homology maps. Thus all comparison squares for $f,Ff,\epsilon_X,\epsilon_Y$ commute on homology. In this convention an $n$-cycle for $n>0$ has all its faces zero, while every element in degree zero is a cycle. No homology isomorphism between $FZ$ and $Z$ is asserted.

### Retained old Step 2

Apply the cited free-chain homotopy invariance with coefficient ring $\mathbb Z$ to each elementary homotopy in the finite chains joining $gf$ to $\mathrm{id}_{UX}$ and $fg$ to $\mathrm{id}_{UY}$. Each homotopy, in either direction, gives equality of the two induced maps on free-chain homology. Here $Fg:FY\to FX$ is defined on generators by $[y]\mapsto[g(y)]$; it is additive regardless of whether $g$ is additive, and free abelianization preserves composition and identity on generators. Hence $H_n(C(Ff))$ is bijective. The natural comparison of Step 1 makes $H_n(N(Ff))$ bijective as well.

### Retained old Step 3

Fix $n\geq0$. To show surjectivity of $H_n(N(f))$, represent a target class $\eta$ by a normalized cycle $y\in N(Y)_n$. Put $\widetilde y=[y]-[0_n]\in(FY)_n$. If $n>0$, every face is $d_i\widetilde y=[d_i y]-[0_{n-1}]=0$, so $\widetilde y$ is a normalized cycle; if $n=0$, no face condition is required. Moreover $\epsilon_Y(\widetilde y)=y$. ### Retained old Step 4

For injectivity, represent a kernel class $\xi\in H_n(N(X))$ by a normalized cycle $x$. Since its image is zero, there is $y\in N(Y)_{n+1}$ with $d_0y=f_n(x)$; all $d_i y$ for $1\leq i\leq n+1$ vanish. As in Step 3, $\widetilde x=[x]-[0_n]$ is a normalized cycle lifting $x$. Its higher faces vanish because $d_i\widetilde y=[0_n]-[0_n]=0$ for $i\geq1$, so it lies in $N(FY)_{n+1}$, and its differential is $d_0\widetilde y=[f_n(x)]-[0_n]=(Ff)_n(\widetilde x)$, where $f_n(0_n)=0_n$ uses additivity of $f$. ### Retained old Step 5
