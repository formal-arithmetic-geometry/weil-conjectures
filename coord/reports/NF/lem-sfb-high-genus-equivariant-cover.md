For each new statement I checked its hypotheses. The pencil lemma assumes a smooth geometrically integral generic curve and an etale finite marking, exactly what is needed to avoid finitely many separable branch points; positive dimension is required only to keep the constants unchanged. The Fermat lemma requires ell invertible, ell at least five, a normal base and the specified root of unity; these hold after the initial constant extension, and its equation gives genus six already for ell=5. The tame lemma requires branch support with separable residue fields and exponent prime to characteristic; arbitrary ramification or inseparable marked points are not allowed. The finite-model lemma assumes both finite extensions and the smooth geometrically integral normalized generic curve over the proposed constants. It claims neither flatness nor a cartesian normalization square. Its functoriality gives the actions only after compatible field actions have actually been constructed. The genus lemma assumes an algebraically closed ground field and smooth proper curves; the item normalizes reduced geometric components before using it.
