The existing Ext-bounds node applies to a nonzero finite M=A with support dimension d, under the same normalized dualizing definition. It gives precisely vanishing outside [-d,0]. The new node gives the missing vanishing within that interval above -d and nonvanishing at -d, since depth A=d. There is no inference of a depth formula from a degree-bounds-only statement.

Step 2 uses only def-weil-abstract-derived-category's actual localization-at-quasi-isomorphisms statement. I wrote both maps of the explicit good truncation, checked their cohomology in degree -d and every other degree, and inverted them. Finiteness of W is an explicit hypothesis of the item; nonzero W was established in Step 1. The shift convention places W[d] in degree -d, not +d. No assertion about truncations is borrowed from a definition which fails to state it.

Step 3 uses the derived-Hom and Hom-complex definitions and the support-annihilator theorem. For any annihilator a, scalar multiplication on Hom(W[d],J) is literally zero termwise because af(w)=f(aw). Thus it is zero on H^0. The scalar-action map A[0] to RHom(K,K) is an A-linear isomorphism by hypothesis; its H^0 carries the zero scalar action back to A, forcing a=a·1=0. Applying the support-annihilator theorem to this finite W proves every localization nonzero. This argument is written out rather than using a generic full-support theorem or silently assuming the ring is a domain/equidimensional.

Step 4 again uses the two Hom definitions and the derived-category shift convention. I checked the sign: Hom(W[d],J)^n=Hom(W,J^{n-d}), and the map in degree n multiplies by (-1)^(dn), matching the (-1)^d shifted differential. Therefore C[-d] is RHom(K,K)=A[0], so C=A[d], with sole nonzero cohomology in degree -d. The new depth-detector is now applied to W, whose nonzero and finite hypotheses have already been established. Its minimum is d, proving depth W=d without assuming that W is free or A is Gorenstein.

For the new node I read the full 0A7U proof: induction on support dimension, the finite-length/Matlis base case, division by a module nonzerodivisor and Nakayama, and removal of maximal-ideal torsion in depth zero. This justifies that the detector is a substantive general prerequisite, not an elementary fact disguised as a new node. Its statement excludes M=0, whose depth is infinite by the project's convention; no conclusion incorrectly demands nonzero Ext of zero.

Small cases: over a field d=0 the normalized complex is the field in degree zero, so W is nonzero of depth zero and has full support. For a non-Gorenstein Artinian local ring the normalized complex is the residue field's injective hull; the proof only claims finite faithful W of depth zero, not W=A or a rank-one free module. For a regular local ring of dimension d, K=A[d] and W=A satisfy all signs and minima. For A=k[[x,y]] and M=A/(x) the new detector has first nonzero degree -1; for M=k it is degree zero, confirming it is not merely the M=A case. The zero ring and zero M are explicitly excluded where nonvanishing is used. For d=0 the truncation and sign calculations still work without any positive-dimension assumption.
