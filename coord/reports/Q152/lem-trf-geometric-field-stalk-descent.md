## Extracted argument: geometric prime quotients (original steps 3-4)

Fix any prime $\mathfrak q$ of $S$ contracting to $\mathfrak p$. It contains every $a\otimes\lambda$ with $a\in\mathfrak p$, since $a\otimes\lambda=(a\otimes1)(1\otimes\lambda)$. The bilinear map $(\bar t,\lambda)\mapsto(t\otimes\lambda)\bmod\mathfrak q$ is therefore well defined and induces a surjection $F\otimes_k\Omega\twoheadrightarrow S/\mathfrak q$. If $e_1,\ldots,e_d$ is a $k$-basis of $F$, the elements $e_i\otimes1$ span this tensor product over $\Omega$: expand each first factor of a pure tensor in that basis. Hence $S/\mathfrak q$ is finite dimensional over $\Omega$. It is a nonzero domain, so [lem-weil-pbc-finite-dimensional-domain-is-field] makes it a field. Thus $\mathfrak q$ is maximal.

Write $D=S/\mathfrak q$, now a finite-dimensional field extension of $\Omega$. For $x\in D$, linear dependence of $1,x,\ldots,x^{\dim_\Omega D}$ gives a nonzero polynomial $P\in\Omega[X]$ with $P(x)=0$. By [lem-weil-alg-closed-linear-factorization], $P=c\prod_i(X-a_i)$ with $c\ne0$. Evaluating in the field $D$ gives $0=c\prod_i(x-a_i)$, so some $x=a_i$ (the constant-polynomial case cannot vanish). Thus the structural map $\Omega\to D$ is surjective and, as a field homomorphism, injective. The residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ equals $D$ canonically: localizing the field $D$ at the images of $S\setminus\mathfrak q$ only inverts nonzero elements. This proves the asserted structural $\Omega$-algebra identification for every such prime.

## Extracted argument: flat local field descent (original steps 5-6)

Choose a prime in the hypothesis and put $A=T_{\mathfrak p}$, $B=S_{\mathfrak q}$. Since $t\notin\mathfrak p$ implies $t\otimes1\notin\mathfrak q$, the map $T\to B$ factors through a local map $f:A\to B$: a fraction with numerator in $\mathfrak p$ maps to the maximal ideal of $B$. The map $T\to S$ is flat by its faithful flatness in step 2. The localization $S\to B$ is flat by [lem-weil-pbc-localization-flat], hence $T\to B$ is flat by [lem-weil-pbc-flat-ring-map-comp]. Now [lem-weil-pbc-flat-over-localization] applied to $T\to A$ shows $f$ is flat. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-flat-local-hom-faithfully-flat] to $f$, and then [lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-ideal-contraction] with zero ideal: $f$ is injective.

The hypothesis identifies $B$ with the field $\Omega$ as an $\Omega$-algebra, so its maximal ideal is zero. Locality of $f$ and its injectivity give $\mathfrak pA=0$. The residue homomorphism $A\to F$ sends $t/s$ to $\bar t/\bar s$ and is surjective (every $\bar t$ is the image of $t/1$); its kernel is $\mathfrak pA$, since $\bar s\ne0$. Consequently this residue map is an isomorphism $A\cong F$.
