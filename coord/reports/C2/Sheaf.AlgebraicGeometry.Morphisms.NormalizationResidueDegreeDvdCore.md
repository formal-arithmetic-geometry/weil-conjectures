## Where the brief, the DAG or earlier work was wrong

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the statement is false because `G` is completely unrelated to
the cover. In fact neither a group action nor a Galois condition is assumed, and
there is no factorization condition relating `a`, `p`, and the complementary open.

Counterexample, checking every hypothesis:

- Let `N = Spec R[t]` and `P = V = Spec C[t]`, with `R` and `C` the real and complex
  fields. Let `p` be the identity of `P`. This is finite and etale.
- Let `a : P -> N` be induced by the coefficient inclusion. It is finite (the
  polynomial algebra is free of rank two over `R[t]`), hence quasi-compact and
  quasi-separated. Every element of `C[t]` is integral over `R[t]`, so their
  relative integral closure is all of `C[t]`. Consequently `a.normalization` is
  `P` over `N`, and `a.fromNormalization` is finite.
- Let `D = Spec R` and let `j` be the zero section defined by `(t)`. The scheme
  `D` is regular and integral, and `(t)` is an invertible ideal generated by a
  nonzerodivisor, so `j` is an effective Cartier centre. Let `O = D(t)`; it is
  exactly the complement of the range of `j`, as `hcomp` requires.
- The unique point `eta` of `D` is generic. Take the normalization point `x`
  corresponding to the prime `(t)` of `C[t]`. Its image is `j eta`. The canonical
  residue field map is `R -> C`, with finrank two.
- Choose `G` to be the trivial group (lifted to the required universe). It has
  cardinality one. The conclusion is therefore `2 | 1`, which is false.

This is a mathematical counterexample checked against the definitions, not a
Lean-certified refutation. In particular, Mathlib's relative normalization is
the integral closure *inside the source's sections*, not a different choice of
an algebraic closure; see `normalizationDiagram` in
`Mathlib.AlgebraicGeometry.Normalization`.

Read [Stacks Tag 09EB](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09EB): its hypotheses
include a finite Galois extension of the fraction field of a DVR. Its degree
formula concerns the degree of that extension, not the order of an unrelated
finite group. Thus step 2 of this node's proof plan cannot follow from its
hypotheses. There are no recorded proof dependencies supplying the missing fact.
Stating it as a new lemma would introduce another false statement.
