### lem-sfb-matrix-unit-corner-embedding

For v with finite basis expansion sum a_j b_j, theta_v=sum a_j E_(j,j0), so theta_v is in the given subalgebra. Checking coordinates gives E_ij E_kl=delta_(j,k) E_il, e squared=e, theta_(b_j)=E_(j,j0), and T theta_v=theta_(Tv). Since e is K-linear on H, its image eH is a K-subspace, and e acts as identity there. The function (v,h) -> theta_v h is bilinear: theta is K-linear and K acts through central scalar operators. The algebraic tensor universal property gives Phi, and T theta_v=theta_(Tv) proves D-linearity.

A tensor is a finite sum of pure tensors by the definition of the algebraic tensor product. Expanding each first factor in the algebraic basis and collecting terms writes it as z=sum_(j in F) b_j tensor h_j. If Phi(z)=sum E_(j,j0)h_j=0, applying E_(j0,k) gives eh_k=h_k=0 for every k in F. Thus z=0; no tensor-basis independence theorem is being assumed. D-linearity makes N=im(Phi) a D-submodule. For h in H, eh=theta_(b_j0)(eh) is in N, since e(eh)=eh. Therefore e acts as zero on H/N. The identity E_jj=E_(j,j0)eE_(j0,j) shows every E_jj acts as zero on the quotient. All modules here have scalar restriction from the algebra, so the necessary scalar compatibility is available. No countability or topology enters this proof.

### lem-sfb-bounded-coordinate-split-projections

For a finite expansion v=sum t_j b_j, the lower norm bound gives |lambda_j(v)| <= norm(v)/c; hence each coordinate is Lipschitz and continuous. The upper bound on the singleton expansion gives norm(b_i)<=C. These two inequalities imply norm(E_ij(v)) <= (C/c)norm(v). The identity norm(lambda_j0(w)v)=|lambda_j0(w)|norm(v) similarly gives the specified theta bound. Matrix-unit multiplication follows by evaluating every basis vector.

Deleting coefficients outside any subset A does not increase their finite maximum. Combining the upper bound for the resulting finite expansion with the lower bound for v gives norm(p_A(v)) <= (C/c)norm(v). Evaluating basis vectors proves p_A p_B=p_(A intersection B). If the intersection F is finite, that map is the finite sum of E_kk for k in F, and E_kk=E_(k,j0)eE_(j0,k).

For infinite A contained in N, choose successively its least not-yet-chosen element. At each finite stage an element remains, because A is infinite. The list is strictly increasing. Any m in A occurs among the first m+1 choices: if it had not, all those choices, being smaller than m, would be m+1 distinct natural numbers below m. This proves surjectivity as well as injectivity of the increasing enumeration. Define u_A and r_A on the basis by the stated formulas and extend using finite expansions. Reindexing preserves the maximum of coefficients and deletion cannot increase it, so both maps have bound C/c. On each basis vector the identities r_A u_A=1, u_A r_A=p_A and p_A u_A=u_A follow, hence hold on V. The argument never takes an infinite sum or a completion, and it does not require an ultrametric inequality on the given vector norm.

## Original returned proof, retained verbatim

1. Write $\lambda_j$ for the coordinate of $b_j$. The lower bound gives $|\lambda_j(v)|\leq c^{-1}\|v\|$, so each coordinate is continuous. For every $i,j\in J$ the operator $E_{ij}(v)=\lambda_j(v)b_i$ satisfies $\|E_{ij}(v)\|\leq (C/c)\|v\|$, hence belongs to $D$. Checking basis vectors gives $E_{ij}E_{kl}=\delta_{jk}E_{il}$ and $e=E_{00}$. Also $\theta_v(w)=\lambda_0(w)v$ has $\|\theta_v(w)\|\leq c^{-1}\|v\|\|w\|$ and is continuous. These estimates use finite algebraic expansions only.

2. Since $e^2=e$, its image $eH$ is an $E$-subspace and $eh=h$ for $h\in eH$. The function $(v,h)\mapsto\theta_vh$ is $E$-bilinear, because $v\mapsto\theta_v$ is $E$-linear and the $E$-action on $H$ comes from the central scalar operators in $D$. It therefore induces the specified linear map $\Phi:V\otimes_E eH\to H$. For $T\in D$, evaluation on $w$ gives $T\theta_v(w)=\lambda_0(w)T(v)=\theta_{T(v)}(w)$. Thus $\Phi(Tv\otimes h)=T\Phi(v\otimes h)$, proving $D$-linearity.

3. To prove injectivity, expand the first factors of any tensor using the algebraic basis and collect terms: $z=\sum_{j\in F}b_j\otimes h_j$ for a finite $F\subseteq J$ and $h_j\in eH$. If $\Phi(z)=\sum_{j\in F}E_{j0}h_j=0$, apply $E_{0k}$ for each $k\in F$. The matrix-unit identities give $0=eh_k=h_k$. Hence $z=0$. No independence theorem for tensor products is required, only bilinearity and the finite expansions.

4. Put $N=\operatorname{im}\Phi=\{\sum_{r=1}^n\theta_{v_r}h_r:n\geq0,\ v_r\in V,\ h_r\in eH\}$. The preceding $D$-linearity makes $N$ a $D$-submodule, so $Q=H/N$ is a unital $D$-module. It is countably spanned over $E$: project the given countable spanning family of $H$ to $Q$. For every $h\in H$, $eh=\theta_{b_0}(eh)$ lies in $N$, so $eQ=0$. Consequently $E_{jj}=E_{j0}eE_{0j}$ acts as zero on $Q$. If $J$ is finite, $1_D=\sum_{j\in J}E_{jj}$, whence $Q=0$. It remains to prove $Q=0$ when $J=\mathbf N$.

5. Assume now $J=\mathbf N$. Apply [lem-sfb-uncountable-almost-disjoint-family] to obtain an uncountable index set $I$ and infinite subsets $A_i\subseteq\mathbf N$ with finite pairwise intersections. For each $i$, let $a_i:\mathbf N\to A_i$ enumerate $A_i$ in increasing order. This enumeration exists by successively taking the least element not yet listed: infinitely many elements ensure each next choice, and an element $m\in A_i$ must occur among the first $m+1$ choices. Thus $a_i$ is a bijection.

6. For $i\in I$, define $u_i(b_n)=b_{a_i(n)}$ and define $r_i(b_{a_i(n)})=b_n$, with $r_i(b_m)=0$ for $m\notin A_i$. Extend by finite linear combinations. Reindexing preserves the maximum of the nonzero coefficients; deleting coordinates cannot increase it. The two norm bounds therefore give $\|u_i(v)\|\leq(C/c)\|v\|$ and $\|r_i(v)\|\leq(C/c)\|v\|$. Both maps belong to $D$. On basis vectors $r_iu_i=1_D$ and $u_ir_i=p_i$, where $p_i$ keeps precisely the coordinates in $A_i$. In particular $p_i^2=p_i$ and $p_iu_i=u_i$.

7. For distinct $i,j$, put $F=A_i\cap A_j$, a finite set by step 5. Computing on each basis vector gives $p_ip_j=\sum_{k\in F}E_{kk}=\sum_{k\in F}E_{k0}eE_{0k}$. Since $e$ annihilates $Q$, this product annihilates $Q$. This uses only finite coordinate projections, and needs no identification of all continuous finite-rank operators with a generated ideal.

8. If $Q\ne0$, fix $0\ne q\in Q$ and put $x_i=u_iq$. Then $r_ix_i=q$, so $x_i\ne0$, and $p_ix_i=x_i$. For $j\ne i$, $p_ix_j=p_ip_jx_j=0$. In a finite relation $\sum_{j\in F}t_jx_j=0$, apply $p_i$ to get $t_ix_i=0$ for every $i\in F$, hence $t_i=0$ since $E$ is a field. Thus $(x_i)_{i\in I}$ is an $E$-linearly independent family.

9. Let $W\subseteq Q$ be the image of a countable spanning set of $H$, so $W$ is countable and spans $Q$. Apply [lem-sfb-independent-family-spanning-cardinality] with $K=E$, $M=Q$ and the independent family of step 8. It supplies an injection $I\to W$. Composing with an injection $W\to\mathbf N$ contradicts the uncountability of $I$. Therefore $Q=0$ also for infinite $J$.

10. In either case $Q=H/N=0$, hence $N=H$ and $\Phi$ is surjective. Together with step 3 this makes $\Phi$ a bijective $E$-linear and $D$-linear map. Its inverse is $D$-linear as well: if $y=\Phi(z)$, then $\Phi(Tz)=Ty$, so uniqueness of the preimage gives $\Phi^{-1}(Ty)=Tz$. This is precisely the claimed isomorphism with the given action on the first tensor factor. If $H=0$, both tensor product and target are zero and the same argument applies; finite-dimensional $V$, including $d=1$, was handled in step 4.
