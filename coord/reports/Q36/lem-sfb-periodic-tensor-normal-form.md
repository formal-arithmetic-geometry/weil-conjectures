## Review by Q36

I checked every use against the actual statement of the cited node, not against a proof hidden in it. In step 1, `def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category` supplies restriction-compatible linear morphisms and `def-pc-cochain-complex-modules` supplies the square-zero and chain-map conditions. Multiplication is linear because the sheaf of rings is commutative; t-squared-zero proves the interior composite and the endpoint composites contain a zero map.

In step 2, `def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product` defines the sheaf tensor as sheafification, and `def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification` states the unique linear extension property needed to extend both explicitly inverse presheaf maps. I wrote the two inverse identities using the balancing relation and checked that restriction preserves the unit. I do not assert that arbitrary sections of a tensor sheaf are pure tensors, or that an arbitrary presheaf tensor is already a sheaf.

In step 3, the exact direct-sum formula is in `def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves`; the zero-tensor calculation uses `def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product` as in step 2. Projection onto (n,0) and its injection are inverse by checking every coproduct injection. This justifies the collapse even though the displayed indexing set is infinite; there is only one potentially nonzero summand. No product, exactness of infinite products, or exchange of cohomology with sums is used.

In step 4, those same tensor and sheafification statements justify checking the scalar map on presheaf pure tensors and then extending it uniquely. The cochain-complex definition says exactly what must be verified for the graded maps and their inverses. The total sign multiplies only the already-zero differential from M[0]. I checked n<0, n=0 and n>0 separately, including the differential from -1 to 0. The only scalar identity needed is t(am)=a(tm)=0.

In step 5, the module-map definition gives f(am)=af(m), the total tensor definition gives the component 1 tensor f, and the module sheafification statement extends this equality. The square and its inverse are explicit, so naturality is not inferred from an unspecified transport. In step 6, `def-weil-abstract-derived-category` explicitly gives the functorial kernel/image definition of cohomology. Both differentials of Z_M vanish, so the kernel is the term and the image zero. The tensor definition specializes to ordinary modules on the one-point site.

For step 7 I read the whole statement of `lem-sf-ringed-kflat-single-tensor-comparison`. It applies to essentially small sites in the coefficient universe, hence the small site here, and its comparison is specifically the K-flat resolution comparison with the degree-zero second factor, without extra termwise flatness or boundedness. `def-pc-kflat-complex` says that the supplied epsilon is a quasi-isomorphism from a K-flat complex; the localization definition in `def-weil-abstract-derived-category` makes its image invertible. Tensoring that inverse has source E[0] tensor-derived M[0] and target Q(P) tensor-derived M[0], so the composition has the correct direction. The comparison's termwise complex has precisely the terms and differential computed in steps 3–4. This is the only non-elementary theorem used.
