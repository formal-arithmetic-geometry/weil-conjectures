## Review by Q36

I read HTT Proposition 5.4.1.7 for the item, then its actual supporting proof in Proposition 5.4.1.2. Its minimal-model induction is the route of this split. For the first new node I read the homotopy definition preceding Definition 2.3.3.1 and Proposition 2.3.3.8, including the retraction and the relative homotopy. The statement explicitly includes dimension zero, the equivalence condition on the vertex tracks, and the empty quasicategory. It assumes no local smallness and makes no unsupported claim about the smaller universe.

For the second new node I read Lemma 5.4.1.1 and the fixed-boundary argument within the proof of Proposition 5.4.1.2. The comparison there controls relative simplex homotopy classes by relative cube-extension classes in the endpoint mapping space. For dimension one those are the mapping-space components; for higher dimensions a chosen extension lets homotopy groups of that mapping space control the relative extensions. A small model of this one mapping space makes the resulting set small. I isolated just that assertion, with n at least one so every vertex belongs to the fixed boundary. Thus the vertex tracks of its relative homotopies are constant and are equivalences automatically. I deliberately did not put a proof sketch into the new statement or replace the entire item by the general essential-smallness criterion.

For step 1 I checked the full-subcategory definition in def-cbc-enhanced-diagrams-cones. An empty object condition forces every simplicial degree empty. There are no maps from nonempty inner horns, so this really is a quasicategory without an artificial nonemptiness hypothesis. The identity functor gives the requested equivalence.

For step 2 I read the same definition's inner-horn and right-pinched mapping-space formulas. All vertices of an inner simplex occur in its inner horn, so fullness preserves the ambient filler. A right-pinched mapping simplex has only the two fixed endpoint vertices, so the two mapping spaces agree degreewise. This is an argument from the definitions, not an appeal to an unstated full-faithfulness theorem. The hypothesis transferred is exactly equivalence to a small space; no stronger degreewise smallness is assumed.

For step 3 I checked the statement of lem-coef-infinity-minimal-retract after writing it. It supplies all the data used: M is a quasicategory, minimal, has strict ri = identity, and the inclusion is an equivalence. D is U'-small since it is a simplicial subset of the ambient C. This step invokes a non-elementary theorem and is marked accordingly.

For step 4 the same new statement's dimension-zero minimality is the precise cited fact. If a minimal vertex is equivalent to some X_j in D, applying the actual retraction gives an equivalence in M. Applying a simplicial map to the inverse edge and the two inverse triangles proves preservation here directly. Dimension-zero minimality then gives equality. This proves the surjectivity J to M_0 explicitly, including repeated representatives. It does not silently replace a merely essentially surjective functor by a surjection of object sets.

For step 5 I checked both new statements. The boundary lemma applies in D to i b because step 2 supplies its only size hypothesis, and n is positive. Its small quotient concerns all extensions in D, not just those whose vertices happen to be chosen representatives. To inject the actual M fillers into that quotient I use the strict retraction: every link of a chain of ambient relative homotopies becomes a relative homotopy in M, where minimality identifies its endpoints. This repairs the potential gap of using intrinsic minimality as if it automatically ruled out ambient homotopies. Boundary maps are determined by finitely many face simplices. The induction has its base in step 4 and its step written out; taking small subsets, finite products and small indexed unions is elementary set theory. An unfillable boundary has an empty extension set, so no extension is chosen without an existence hypothesis.

For step 6 I read def-coef-presentable-infinity-category's exact definition of essentially small and the minimal-retract statement's inverse data. Degreewise bijections to small sets allow transport of all operators; cancellation proves the simplicial identities, and transport of horn fillers proves the quasicategory property. Countably many degrees and operators remain small in the fixed universe. The inverse e^{-1}r and its two identities are explicitly identified. This step proves the actual equivalence required by the item's definition, not just a bound on object classes.

For edge cases I tested empty J and empty D; a repeated family of one object; a discrete quasicategory (the argument reduces to its image set of vertices); a one-object quasicategory with nontrivial higher mapping homotopies (these survive and enter the boundary lemma rather than being discarded); and a boundary admitting no filler.
