Former Step 1:

First prove the inner-fibration part for any inner fibration $r:\mathcal E\to\mathcal F$, without equivalence lifting. Identify a degreewise injective $A\to B$ with its image simplicial subset: the degreewise bijections to its image commute with operators. Unfold [def-cbc-enhanced-diagrams-cones]. The internal-hom adjunction is explicit: a map $V\to\operatorname{Fun}(H,X)$ corresponds to a map $H\times V\to X$; in degrees, its value at $(h,v)\in H_n\times V_n$ is obtained by evaluating the diagram represented by $v$ at $(h,\operatorname{id}_{[n]})$. Conversely a map $a:H\times V\to X$ sends $v\in V_n$ to the map with $m$-simplex value $(h,\alpha:[m]\to[n])\mapsto a_m(h,V(\alpha)v)$. These rules commute with operators and are inverse. Thus a lifting problem for the corner map against an inner horn $j:\Lambda_k^n\hookrightarrow\Delta^n$ transposes to one for $r$ against
$$j\mathbin{\square}i:(\Lambda_k^n\times B)\amalg_{\Lambda_k^n\times A}(\Delta^n\times A)\longrightarrow\Delta^n\times B.$$
The two specified maps agree on the intersection and glue by the colimit property of [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit]. Every inner horn is inner anodyne under the lifting-class definition in [lem-coef-inner-anodyne-pushout-product], directly because every inner fibration lifts that horn. That node makes the displayed map inner anodyne, so it lifts against $r$. Transposition proves the corner is an inner fibration. Taking $r:X\to\Delta^0$ and $i:\varnothing\hookrightarrow H$ proves $\operatorname{Fun}(H,X)$ is a quasicategory for every quasicategory $X$. Taking arbitrary $i$ and $r:\mathcal D\to\Delta^0$ proves restriction to $A$ is an inner fibration. Consequently the target $T=\operatorname{Fun}(A,\mathcal C)\times_{\operatorname{Fun}(A,\mathcal D)}\operatorname{Fun}(B,\mathcal D)$ is a quasicategory: fill an inner horn in its first factor, then use the restriction inner fibration in its second factor to lift over that chosen filler. Its source is a quasicategory by the preceding special case.

Former Step 4:

$$U_{\mathcal D}(p(x),p(y))\longrightarrow\operatorname{Fun}(A,\mathcal C)\times_{\operatorname{Fun}(A,\mathcal D)}\operatorname{Fun}(B,\mathcal D)$$
sends a simplex $v$ to $(f,v)$, using the constant simplex at the diagram $f$. Its ordinary simplicial pullback along $\theta$ is exactly $U_{\mathcal C}(x,y)$: in each degree the equations are precisely that both endpoint simplices of the arrow diagram are constant at $x,y$. A lifting square for this pullback against an inner horn maps to one for $\theta$; its filler paired with the already specified base simplex is a filler of the original square. Thus the induced map $s:U_{\mathcal C}(x,y)\to U_{\mathcal D}(p(x),p(y))$ is an inner fibration. Prove independently that $U_X(a,b)$ is Kan for every quasicategory $X$. Its inner horns fill by the same Step 1 argument applied to $X\to\Delta^0$ and the boundary inclusion $A\subset B$. For $n\geq2$, a left outer horn in $U_X(a,b)$ transposes to a map $B\times\Lambda_0^n\to X$; adjoin the constant maps at $a,b$ on $A\times\Delta^n$. They agree on $A\times\Lambda_0^n$, so [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit] glues them. For each vertex of $B=\Delta^1$, the edge in the parameter direction $[0,1]$ is an identity, since both vertices belong to $A$. Apply [lem-cbc-invertible-prism-extension] to extend over $B\times\Delta^n$, keeping the two endpoint strips constant. Transposition gives the required filler in $U_X(a,b)$. For the right outer horn use the same argument with opposites: define $X^{\mathrm{op}}_m=X_m$ with the operator of $\alpha:[k]\to[m]$ that of $\alpha^{\mathrm{rev}}(j)=m-\alpha(k-j)$. This operation sends $\Lambda_i^n$ to $\Lambda_{n-i}^n$, preserves inner horn filling, and commutes with products and degreewise pullbacks. Reversing the shape interval gives the degreewise identification $U_X(a,b)^{\mathrm{op}}=U_{X^{\mathrm{op}}}(b,a)$. Thus a right outer horn becomes a left outer horn, the preceding construction fills it, and applying the involution again gives the desired original filler. In dimension one either horn specifies only a vertex, filled by its degenerate identity edge. Hence all horns fill, with no assertion that the space is nonempty. Apply this result to $X=\mathcal C,\mathcal D$. This establishes that the source and target of $s$ are Kan independently of the pinch comparison.
