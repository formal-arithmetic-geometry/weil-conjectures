## New nodes

`lem-sfb-local-split-torus-extension-points`: I read Stacks Tag 03P8 (theorem-picard-group) in `etale-cohomology.tex`, including its fppf clause, and Tags 0499/049A in `groupoids.tex`. Pulling an exact extension back along a section produces a torsor for the split torus, the coordinate character line modules are invertible and trivial over a local base, and choosing frames produces a section. This works for arbitrary nonzero local rings, including fields and valuation rings, and for rank zero. The old field-only frame theorem and the old etale Hilbert-90 theorem do not state this mixed-characteristic fppf lifting result. Searches found local Picard-group triviality at Mathlib/RingTheory/PicardGroup.lean:538, source read, but no scheme-torsor classification bridge. This is why the node is unsplit rather than a Mathlib leaf. The associated torsor construction is the real lemma, not an elementary application of the current DAG's etale-only statement.

`lem-sfb-strict-local-group-component-translates`: I read Stacks Tag 056U in `varieties.tex` and Tag 0H74 in `more-algebra.tex`, and BLR's strict-local construction on printed pp.292–293. The intended proof chooses a rational point in each smooth special-fibre component over the separably closed residue field, lifts it in a smooth affine neighbourhood using henselianity, and uses translation of the identity component. Over a separably closed field the smooth components are geometrically connected, so the translated open captures every geometric point of that component. The generic fibre is already the identity component. An arbitrary local section factors through one such open since its closed point does. This geometric component identification is retained as a genuine input, not hidden in the item's step. No finite set of components or quasi-compactness of M is used. The trivial group and infinitely many special-fibre components (as in the split-torus Néron lft-model) satisfy the statement; the lemma assumes no Néron property and hence is strictly smaller than the coset item. The existing affine-translate-cover lemma requires an algebraically closed residue field, finite type and affine generic fibre, so is not a substitute.

`lem-sfb-essentially-smooth-dvr-index-one`: I read BLR 3.6 Definition 1 and its following characterization, and Stacks Tags 00T4/00TT in `algebra.tex`. For a local DVR A over D, A/(pi) is zero-dimensional; smoothness makes this local fibre geometrically regular, hence a field, so (pi) is the maximal ideal. Its being the function field of the smooth fibre component gives a separably generated residue extension. The DVR hypothesis forces the point to be generic in that fibre. This is a general geometric input, with no group, quotient or torsor. Test A=D and A=D[t]_(pi): both pass, the latter has a transcendental residue field. A ramified map pi=u t^e with e>1 fails the smooth hypothesis since its special fibre is not reduced. The search hits for formally unramified local rings cannot be applied to D[t]_(pi); they were rejected after reading their types. No excellence or completion regularity is invoked.
