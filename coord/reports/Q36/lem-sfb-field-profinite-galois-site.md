## Review by Q36

I read Bhatt–Scholze pp. 27–28, Definition 4.1.1, Examples 4.1.4 and 4.1.9–4.1.10, and the adjacent affine-basis discussion on p. 29. Example 4.1.10 explicitly gives the qcqs equivalence with profinite continuous Galois sets and the finite jointly surjective covering criterion. It uses a separable closure, so I kept that hypothesis rather than silently assuming an algebraic closure or a perfect field. Example 4.1.9 supplies the same finite-coproduct test objects as the existing test-object node. The unit is the inherited sk-thm-sf-ctf-stability; its generic field-site input does not replace its six-functor arguments.

For the new affineness node I read Stacks properties.tex, Tag 0CKV, lemma-dimension-zero, including the implication from quasi-separatedness and its last paragraph. Its conclusion applies to quasi-compact quasi-separated zero-dimensional schemes without a Noetherian assumption. Existing zero-dimensional affineness nodes need either Noetherianity or finite discreteness, so none can be applied here. I checked the empty scheme, a field spectrum, a finite product of fields, and an infinite profinite spectrum. Removing quasi-separatedness would permit the doubled-point type of nonseparated zero-dimensional gluing, so I did not remove it.

For the new equivariant quotient node I read Example 4.1.10's pro-object assertion and Stacks topology.tex Tag 08ZY, lemma-profinite, especially its directed clopen-partition proof. The additional group detail is substantial: compactness and continuity give an open stabilizer of any clopen subset; compactness of G makes its orbit finite; the Boolean partition generated by the finitely many translates refines the original partition and is invariant. Its finite permutation action is continuous. Invariant partitions are cofinal among finite clopen partitions, so the same compatible-family argument gives the equivariant homeomorphism. The converse finite-action limit is a closed subspace of a product of finite compact Hausdorff spaces, with coordinatewise action; each action coordinate is continuous. This explanatory argument is here, not inserted as a proof in the unsplit node. I checked trivial G, singleton P, empty P, a free action on G, and inverse systems with an empty stage. No freeness, discreteness of P, or finite orbit hypothesis was smuggled into the statement.
