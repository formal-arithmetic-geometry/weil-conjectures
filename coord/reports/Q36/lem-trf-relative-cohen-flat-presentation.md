### Original step 1

Put $A_0=\widehat A$, $B_0=\widehat B$, and $f_0=\widehat f$. The completion results give Noetherian local rings with maximal ideals $\mathfrak m_0=\mathfrak m A_0$ and $\mathfrak n_0=\mathfrak n B_0$, residue fields canonically $K,L$, and completeness for these maximal ideals. The induced map is local: its reduction is the induced field inclusion $K\hookrightarrow L$, so an element with nonzero residue has nonzero image residue. By flatness under completion, $f_0$ is flat. The canonical maps preserve the $\mathbf Q$-algebra structures.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-adic-completion-noetherian, lem-weil-pbc-local-completion-local-ring, lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete, lem-weil-pbc-flat-completion.

Source: literature/stacks-project/more-algebra.tex, Tag 0C4G (lemma-flat-completion); algebra.tex Tags 0316, 05GG, 05GI.

### Original step 2

Let $C=B/\mathfrak mB$ with maximal ideal the image of $\mathfrak n$. This is a nonzero Noetherian local ring. Completion of the surjection $B\to C$ gives $B_0/(\mathfrak mB)B_0\simeq\widehat C$. Since $\mathfrak m_0=\mathfrak m A_0$ and $f_0$ commutes with the canonical maps, $(\mathfrak mB)B_0=\mathfrak m_0B_0$. Write $C_0=B_0/\mathfrak m_0B_0$. Its identification with $\widehat C$ preserves residue fields and quotient maps. Completion preserves regularity and dimension, so $C_0$ is complete regular local of dimension $s$, with residue field $L$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-quotient-local-structure, lem-weil-pbc-surjective-image-noetherian, lem-weil-pbc-local-completion-quotient, lem-weil-pbc-local-completion-local-ring, lem-weil-pbc-completion-regular-local, lem-weil-pbc-completion-dimension, lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete.

Source: literature/stacks-project/more-algebra.tex, Tags 07NY (lemma-completion-regular), 07NV (lemma-completion-dimension); algebra.tex Tags 00MA and 05GG.

### Original step 3

Choose a residue section $a:K\to A_0$. Give $B_0$ its $K$-algebra structure $f_0a$. The residue map $B_0\to L$ is then a $K$-algebra map for the given inclusion $K\hookrightarrow L$. The arbitrary characteristic-zero extension $L/K$ is formally smooth. Apply the complete-target lifting lemma to $R=K$, $S=L$, the ideal $0\subset L$, target $B_0$, and $I=J=\mathfrak n_0$, lifting the identity map $L\to B_0/\mathfrak n_0=L$. Its hypotheses hold with $r=t=1$: the zero ideal maps into every ideal, $J\subset I$, and $J=\bigcap_{h\ge1}(J+I^h)$ because every summand is $J$. We obtain a $K$-algebra map $b:L\to B_0$ reducing to the identity. Consequently $b|_K=f_0a$, with no finiteness assumption on $L/K$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-coefficient-residue-section, lem-weil-pbc-char-zero-field-extension-formally-smooth, lem-weil-pbc-fs-adic-lift-into-complete.

Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex, Tags 032A (theorem-cohen-structure-theorem), first proof paragraph, and 0322 (proposition-characterize-separable-field-extensions); more-algebra.tex Tag 07NJ (lemma-lift-continuous).

### Original step 4

Choose generators $x_1,\ldots,x_e$ of $\mathfrak m_0$, possible since $A_0$ is Noetherian. Put $R=K[[X_1,\ldots,X_e]]$. Evaluate coefficients through $a$ and $X_i$ at $x_i$ using the power-series universal property to obtain $q:R\to A_0$. The source is complete for $\mathfrak r=(X_1,\ldots,X_e)$, $q(\mathfrak r)A_0=\mathfrak m_0$, and $R\to A_0/\mathfrak m_0=K$ is surjective by constants. The complete-source surjectivity lemma applies, as $A_0$ is $\mathfrak m_0$-adically Hausdorff, and proves $q$ surjective. Let $I=\ker q$. Reduction of $q$ is the constant-coefficient map, so $I\subseteq\mathfrak r$. The quotient isomorphism $R/I\simeq A_0$ is $K$-linear for $a$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-power-series-universal-property-complete, lem-weil-pbc-power-series-field-cohen-regular-complete, lem-weil-pbc-complete-source-surjective-mod-ideal.

Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex, Tag 032A, final proof paragraph; Tag 032C (remark-Noetherian-complete-local-ring-universally-catenary); Tag 0315 (lemma-completion-generalities).

### Original step 5

The section $b$ induces a coefficient map $\bar b:L\to C_0$ whose reduction is the identity of $L$. By regularity and dimension $s$, choose $\bar t_1,\ldots,\bar t_s$ generating the maximal ideal of $C_0$. Apply the specified-coefficient coordinate theorem to obtain $\rho:L[[T_1,\ldots,T_s]]\xrightarrow{\sim}C_0$ with coefficients $\bar b$ and variables $\bar t_i$. Lift the $\bar t_i$ to $t_i\in\mathfrak n_0$; these lifts exist because the maximal ideal of the quotient is the image of $\mathfrak n_0$.

Uses: lem-trf-regular-complete-coefficient-coordinates, lem-weil-pbc-quotient-local-structure.

Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex, Tag 0C0S (lemma-regular-complete-containing-coefficient-field), clause (2) and proof.

### Original step 6

Put $S=L[[X_1,\ldots,X_e,T_1,\ldots,T_s]]$ and define $v:S\to B_0$ by coefficients $b$, $X_i\mapsto f_0(x_i)$, $T_i\mapsto t_i$, using the universal property. The images of the variables generate $\mathfrak n_0$: the images of the $t_i$ generate its quotient modulo $\mathfrak m_0B_0$, and the $f_0(x_i)$ generate $\mathfrak m_0B_0$. Thus the complete-source surjectivity lemma applies to the variable ideal of $S$, since constants surject onto $L$ and $B_0$ is $\mathfrak n_0$-adically Hausdorff. It gives surjectivity of $v$. The ring $S$ is Noetherian complete local, with maximal ideal generated by all its variables.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-power-series-universal-property-complete, lem-weil-pbc-power-series-field-cohen-regular-complete, lem-weil-pbc-complete-source-surjective-mod-ideal.

### Original step 7

Let $j:R\to S$ extend coefficients $K\hookrightarrow L$ and retain the $X$ variables. The maps $vj$ and $f_0q$ agree on constants by $b|_K=f_0a$ and on $X_i$ by construction; uniqueness in the power-series universal property gives $vj=f_0q$. The map $j$ is local since series of zero constant term go into the maximal ideal. Setting $X=0$ gives $p:S\twoheadrightarrow L[[T]]$ with kernel $(X)S$: a series vanishing at $X=0$ is $\sum_{i=1}^eX_iF_i$, grouping each monomial by its first positive $X$ exponent. Reduction of $v$ to $C_0$ is $\rho p$, by the coefficient and variable formulas and the same universal-property uniqueness. Since $\rho$ is injective, $\ker v\subseteq\ker p=(X)S=\mathfrak rS$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-power-series-universal-property-complete.

Source: literature/stacks-project/more-algebra.tex, Tag 07NQ, power-series diagram and agreement on the special fibre; elementary coefficientwise verification.

### Original step 8

Apply flat closed-fibre kernel rigidity with presentation $q:R\twoheadrightarrow A_0$, flat map $f_0:A_0\to B_0$, local map $j:R\to S$ and surjection $v:S\twoheadrightarrow B_0$. Here $R,A_0,S$ are local, $S$ is Noetherian, $vj=f_0q$, and $\ker v\subseteq\mathfrak rS$ by the preceding step. It yields $\ker v=(\ker q)S=IS$. The quotient isomorphism $S/IS\simeq B_0$ is $L$-linear for $b$, and the one for $R/I\simeq A_0$ is $K$-linear for $a$. The equation $vj=f_0q$ identifies the induced map with coefficient extension and inclusion of the $X$ variables. These local isomorphisms identify maximal ideals and all their powers, hence are isomorphisms of complete local algebras. Modulo $\mathfrak m_0B_0$, the images of $T_i$ are the chosen $\bar t_i$, which generate the maximal ideal and number $s=\dim C_0$. This proves every asserted compatibility.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-flat-closed-fibre-kernel-rigidity.
