The local-prime correspondence explicitly preserves minimality in both directions; I have not inferred global disjointness merely from uniqueness at the identity. It is applied at every point. Step 2 uses only the locally finite component-family theorem and the 047M assertion about the unique neutral irreducible component. Removing the finitely many other closed components proves openness point by point. The earlier disjointness then identifies irreducible and connected components. The reduction construction and universal-homeomorphism node state exactly what is needed to pass geometric irreducibility from the reduced closed component to its full open-scheme structure, including after every field extension. Step 3 uses the product theorem for geometrically irreducible schemes and unique factorization through an open subscheme. Its hypotheses hold by step 2. The multiplication and inverse images are connected and contain the identity, so they cannot leave the connected component. The group laws follow after composing with the inclusion, by uniqueness of the open factorizations. No set-theoretic factorization through a general closed subscheme is used here.

Step 4 first applies the irreducible-group quasi-compactness input to the subgroup just constructed. Local finite type follows by restricting finite-type charts to principal affine neighbourhoods, where adjoining one inverse keeps the algebra finitely generated. Taking a finite subcover proves finite type by the cited definition. We never assume the original G is quasi-compact.

Step 5 is elementary from the cited definitions of geometric irreducibility, field base change and connectedness: the pullback of a clopen decomposition remains clopen, and the connected identity component cannot cross it. The argument is for every field extension K/k, not only algebraically closed extensions. Equality is equality of open subschemes and consequently of their restricted group structures, rather than merely equality of point sets.

The definition of integral applies to its nonempty irreducible reduced space; nonemptiness follows from the identity. Algebraic closedness implies perfectness in all characteristics.
