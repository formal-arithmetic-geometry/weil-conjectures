The prerequisite's source is Proposition 20.24 with M=R; R is a nonzero finitely generated graded R-module because J is proper and 1 generates R. The source uses nil(R/J); a power of a scalar kills R/J exactly when that power kills the class of 1, equivalently belongs to J, so the radical/power formulation is exact. For later splitting, one can prove the criterion by passing to R/J: homogeneous zero divisors are nilpotent by hypothesis. Given a nonzero element a killed by b, discard the zero low components of a. If b is not nilpotent, remove its initial nilpotent components c (their finite sum is nilpotent by the binomial expansion). Then (b-c)^N a=0 for N killing c, since ba=0. The lowest nonzero component of this product is b_v^N a_t, where b_v is the first component of b that is not nilpotent and a_t is the first nonzero component of a. The homogeneous condition would force b_v^N, hence b_v, to be nilpotent, a contradiction. This argument is background for the unsplit prerequisite, not inserted into its statement or concealed in an assembly step.
