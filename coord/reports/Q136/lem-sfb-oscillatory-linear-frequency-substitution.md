## Argument reserved for the new node's later split

This explanation is in the report, not embedded in the new statement-only node. For eta=t omega with t>=1 and |omega|=1, surjectivity gives |DH(z)^T omega|>0. Compactness of C times the unit sphere gives a uniform positive lower bound, persisting near C. Put v_omega=gradient(H·omega)/|gradient(H·omega)|² there. All derivatives through any fixed finite order are uniformly bounded on C. The identity v_omega·gradient(exp(i t H·omega))=i t exp(i t H·omega) permits integration by parts with u, giving the adjoint operator -div(v_omega u)/(i t). Iterating N times gives t^(-N) times an integral whose amplitude is a finite sum of derivatives of u through N with uniformly bounded coefficients, supported in C. Bound its integral by the volume of a fixed bounded neighborhood times p_N(u). For |eta|<=1 use the direct volume bound, enlarging the constant to replace |eta|^(-N) by (1+|eta|)^(-N). The needed compactly supported localization and integration-by-parts bookkeeping belong to this genuinely reusable estimate, not to an elementary one-line step.
