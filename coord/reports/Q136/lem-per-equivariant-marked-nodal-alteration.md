## Review by Q136

I checked the item against all six clauses of IX 1.2 and the affine-base quotient paragraph of IX 1.4, with the source open. I also read the preparatory portion of the proof of X Theorem 3.4 (pp.160--161) and the definitions of multipointed semistable curves in §3.2.2 (p.158). The new stable-modification input retains the separable alteration of Step 3, the projective modification, and actual stability used in Step 5. Its base-change clause uses the fibrewise condition on contracted rational components: extension of the geometric ground field neither introduces a new component after choosing an algebraic closure nor changes the number of distinct special points. Flatness and a geometrically integral generic fibre imply integrality of its total curve over any normal integral dominant base. Affine-locally the flat coordinate algebra injects into its generic localization, which is a domain, and the integral generic fibre is dense. Excellence and Noetherianity follow from finite type over the excellent Noetherian base. This explanation is in the review, not hidden in the unsplit node's statement as a proof sketch.

The Galois-envelope source is X 3.4 Step 4 and the definition in §3.3.1. I checked that it starts with a separable extension; an inseparable alteration would not be dominated by a separable Galois one. One construction is Chow preparation of the given alteration, normalization in a finite Galois closure, and closure of its generic diagonal inside the finite product of conjugate projective models. The normalization is finite because the base and finite-type models are excellent. The product has a G-action, the generic diagonal is invariant, and its normalization is the required normal projective dominating model. Shrinking the base makes the finite generically Galois map an etale torsor. This is a genuine base-model theorem, not a renamed semistable alteration.

For the quotient-separatedness node I read IX 1.4's finite admissible quotient construction and checked the distinction from the two free-action Stacks statements. The separation assertion is a standard property of the finite invariant-affine construction, stated as its own unsplit input because the existing quotient node does not say it. It can be checked using the finite quotient map: the inverse image of the quotient diagonal is set-theoretically the finite union of action graphs, closed because the original scheme is separated. Finite surjectivity makes this closedness descend; the scheme diagonal is an immersion locally on affine charts, hence is a closed immersion when its image is closed. No division by |G| or freeness is involved. The node is source-contextualized in IX 1.4; it is not advertised as an explicitly numbered lemma there.
