### Returned Step 4

Choose a scheme-theoretically dense compactification $X\xrightarrow{j}\bar X\xrightarrow{p}s$ by [det-nagata-theorem-dense]; $s$ is affine and hence quasi-compact and quasi-separated. The scheme $\bar X$ is finite type over $k$ (since $p$ is proper), and is Noetherian by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian]. We justify the dimension bound rather than assuming it for an arbitrary compactification. Scheme-theoretic density implies topological density: a nonempty open disjoint from $X$ would have its nonzero structure sheaf inject into zero. By [lem-sc-noetherian-closed-subset-finite-components], $\bar X$ has finitely many irreducible components $Z_i$. For each $i$, maximality of the finitely many distinct components makes $\bar X\setminus\bigcup_{h\ne i}Z_h$ a nonempty open contained in $Z_i$. Density of the open $X$ makes its intersection with this open nonempty. Hence every $Z_i\cap X$ is nonempty open in $Z_i$. Give $Z_i$ its reduced induced closed subscheme structure using [lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme]. It is an irreducible finite-type $k$-scheme (on affine charts its ring is a quotient of a finite-type $k$-algebra), and [lem-sc-dimension-locally-algebraic] gives $\dim Z_i=\dim(Z_i\cap X)\leq\dim X$. The inequalities and $\dim\bar X=\max_i\dim Z_i$ follow directly from chains of irreducible closed subsets [def-kun-topological-krull-dimension]: each such chain lies in a component, and intersection of a chain in a closed subset with its ambient space preserves it. Thus $\dim\bar X\leq d$.
