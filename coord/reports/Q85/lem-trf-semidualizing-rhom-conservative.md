### lem-trf-complete-local-coherent-duality

I read `literature/stacks-project/dualizing.tex`, `lemma-ubiquity-dualizing` (Tag 0BFR), especially case (2) and its proof, and `lemma-dualizing` (Tag 0A7C), including its proof and the preceding definition and finite-Ext lemma. This is ordinary commutative algebra; no six-functor coefficient category is substituted for D(R).

The source's existence argument is: Cohen structure presents a complete Noetherian local ring R as a quotient of a regular local ring S; a regular local ring has a dualizing complex; duality for a finite ring map passes it to R, using RHom_S(R,-). Thus choose a dualizing complex Omega for R. By its finite injective dimension it has a bounded injective representative; by the defining coherent-cohomology condition it belongs to D^b_fg(R). For K with finite cohomology in each degree, the preceding finite-Ext lemma shows RHom_R(K,Omega) still has finite cohomology. Its proof uses truncation of K: in each fixed degree, finite injective dimension permits replacement by a sufficiently low bounded-above truncation, for which the coherent-Hom result applies. There is no assumption that K is bounded on both sides. The canonical tensor-Hom evaluation comparison from the proof of 0A7C gives

K = RHom_R(Omega,Omega) tensor^L_R K -> RHom_R(RHom_R(K,Omega),Omega),

and is an isomorphism. The equality at the left uses the dualizing homothety. This is the evaluation-adjoint biduality, not an unspecified abstract quasi-inverse. Finite injective amplitude reverses the one-sided cohomological bounds; applying the same argument twice yields the stated anti-equivalence, exchanges D+ and D-, and preserves Db. These are precisely the conclusions of 0A7C. The new node packages existence plus this generic property and contains no assertion about an arbitrary semidualizing C.

Small cases checked: for a field, Omega=R gives ordinary vector-space duality degree by degree, even for complexes unbounded in both directions with finite-dimensional cohomology in every degree. For a complete regular local ring, the usual shifted ring dualizing object fits. For a singular complete local ring Omega need not be perfect, and the statement imposes no such requirement. Completeness and Noetherianity are retained; existence for every arbitrary Noetherian ring is not asserted. A local ring here has a unique proper maximal ideal, so the zero ring is not accidentally included in this local lemma.

### lem-trf-local-derived-tensor-nonvanishing

I read `literature/stacks-project/more-algebra.tex`, `lemma-tensor-pseudo-coherent` (Tag 0DJE), the following Noetherian pseudo-coherence criterion, and the local-module Nakayama input in `algebra.tex`, `lemma-NAK` (Tag 00DV). The source field cites 0DJE for closure and explicitly identifies nonvanishing as an agent corollary, rather than misattributing that corollary to 0DJE.

Here is the complete intended elementary algebra behind that corollary. Over a Noetherian ring the bounded-above finite-cohomology criterion gives bounded-above finite free models P and Q for U and V. If their upper term bounds are a and b, the total tensor has upper bound a+b. In degree n only n-b <= i <= a contributes to P^i tensor Q^(n-i), a finite sum of finite free modules. Its cohomology is finite because cycles are submodules of finite modules over a Noetherian ring, and cohomology is a quotient of cycles. This proves the closure clause without a bounded-below or finite Tor-dimension assumption.

For nonzero U and V, choose a and b to be their highest nonzero cohomological degrees. The standard construction of the bounded-above free model can start in exactly those degrees: choose finite generators of the highest cohomology, lift them to cycles to define the map from the first finite free term, then successively choose finite generators for the next cohomology of its cone and continue to the left. No term above a (respectively b) is needed. At the top total degree a+b there is a single term P^a tensor Q^b and no outgoing differential. The incoming image is the sum of the images of d_P^(a-1) tensor Q^b and P^a tensor d_Q^(b-1). Its cokernel is therefore

H^(a+b)(U tensor^L V) = coker(d_P^(a-1)) tensor coker(d_Q^(b-1)) = H^a(U) tensor H^b(V).

This uses the explicit presentation of a tensor product of two quotient modules, not an unjustified degeneration of an unbounded spectral sequence. Put M=H^a(U), N=H^b(V), and k=R/m. These are nonzero finite modules, so Nakayama gives M/mM and N/mN nonzero finite-dimensional k-vector spaces. Their tensor over k is nonzero (extend a nonzero vector in each to a basis). The canonical quotient of M tensor_R N by m identifies with (M/mM) tensor_k (N/mN), so M tensor_R N is nonzero. Hence the displayed top cohomology, and thus U tensor^L V, is nonzero. If either input is zero the derived tensor is zero by its resolution construction. This proves the iff.

Small cases checked: shifts of R, modules concentrated in degree zero, and zero inputs agree with the assertion. Over a singular local ring, k tensor^L k can have infinitely many negative cohomology groups; the statement allows that. Localness is necessary: over R=k times k the two nonzero summands k times 0 and 0 times k tensor to zero. Thus no global version lacking localness has been introduced.
