Step 1 uses spec-domain-stalks-function-field for integrality, the generic point and the stalk identifications; open-immersion for the restricted structure sheaf; integral-scheme for its reduced/irreducible characterization; and invertible-module-generic-stalk for the one-dimensional generic fibre, injectivity and the characterization of global sections by stalk membership. The integrality hypothesis holds for every nonempty open in this integral spectrum: reducedness restricts, and irreducibility restricts to nonempty opens. Restriction does not change stalks or the generic field. These are exactly the objects the generic-stalk statement introduces.

Step 2 uses invertible-module for a Zariski frame, the basic-open basis for a nonempty D(f) inside its domain, and generic-stalk for a nonzero generic frame and the unique rational coefficients. In a domain D(f) nonempty forces f nonzero, and s is chosen to be that frame's generic germ; no extension or existence theorem for a rational section is hidden here.

Step 3 takes both valuation additivity and the equivalence v_r(u)=0 iff u is a local unit from normal-domain-principal-divisor-kernel. I opened its statement: it explicitly provides DVRs, normalized valuations, both membership criteria, finite support and kernel for arbitrary-dimensional normal Noetherian domains, not just Dedekind domains. The item assumes precisely those hypotheses on A. Two stalk generators differ by a local unit, giving the written independence calculation. On D(f) the chosen coefficient is 1.

Step 4 uses finite-minimal-primes-over-ideal, whose statement applies to every ideal of a Noetherian ring. A supporting height-one prime contains f and cannot contain a smaller prime containing f, because f is nonzero and (0) is prime. Thus its finiteness conclusion applies directly. This proves finite support without assuming that U is affine or using an uncited quasi-compactness theorem.

Step 5 uses invertible-module and generic-stalk for a frame on a neighbourhood of t and its coefficient in the same generic field. Opens of Spec A contain every generalization of t: if r is contained in t, then t is in the closure of {r}. Consequently the same frame reaches every indexed r, and the equality of coefficients follows from Step 3. This also verifies that all primes in the local-equation quantifier actually lie in U.

Step 6 uses localization-noetherian and localization-normal-domain to verify all the ring hypotheses for R=A_t, including the same fraction field K. I opened primes-of-localization and localize-localization-at-prime: clause (1) of the first explicitly supplies the inclusion-preserving prime bijection below t, and clause (1) of the second explicitly identifies the repeated local ring with A_r by r/u mapped to (r/1)/(u/1). The bijection preserves zero and strict prime chains, so height-one primes correspond exactly. The identified DVRs have the same subrings of K and the same normalized valuations. The local equation in Step 5 and the assumed principal equation give zero valuation of c=a_t/a at every one of them. The unit-kernel statement therefore applies to R, and says precisely that c is a unit in R. I did not cite a definition for this substantive normality argument.

Step 7 uses part (3) of invertible-module-generic-stalk to turn the element with all the stalk memberships into a global regular section, and part (2) to identify its germs with the generators obtained locally. The remaining inverse-map construction is written explicitly using local frames and inverse germs; this is elementary sheaf gluing, not a further theorem about divisors. The converse uses the one-dimensional fibre in part (1) to write the originally chosen s as a rational multiple of a global frame. It proves the principal equation for the SAME family constructed earlier, rather than choosing a new family after trivializing L.

I tested the statement and the steps for a field (H(A) empty, U the unique point), a DVR (U either the generic point or the whole spectrum), a normal domain of dimension at least two with nontrivial divisor class group, a non-affine punctured spectrum, and a nontrivial line bundle on an open. The argument does not infer that every Weil divisor is Cartier or every divisor class is trivial. The chosen nonzero section is essential throughout.
