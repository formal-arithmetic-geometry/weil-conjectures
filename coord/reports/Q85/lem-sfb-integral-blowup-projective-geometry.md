### Returned step 1

Take a nonempty affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ in $T$ and a finite generating set $I=I_V=\Gamma(V,\mathcal I)=(f_0,\ldots,f_r)$. The global blowup [def-nagata-global-blowup] is glued from the local models $\mathsf B_V(\mathcal I)=\operatorname{Bl}_{I_V}(\operatorname{Spec}A)$ of [def-nagata-global-blowup-local-model] along the datum [def-nagata-global-blowup-glue-data], whose transition maps identify $\mathsf B_W(\mathcal I)$ with the inverse image of $W$ in $\mathsf B_V(\mathcal I)$; by the relative gluing statement [lem-weil-pbc-relative-glueing-affine-opens] the restriction $b^{-1}(V)\to V$ is therefore the affine blowup $\operatorname{Bl}_I(\operatorname{Spec}A)=\operatorname{Proj}(\mathcal R_A(I))$ of [def-nagata-affine-blowup]. The chart-cover statement [prop-nagata-affine-blowup-chart-cover], together with the definition [def-nagata-blowup-chart-ring], covers this restriction by $\operatorname{Spec}(A[I/f_i])$. Since $T$ is integral and $\mathcal I\neq0$, the ideal $I$ is nonzero: $\mathcal I$ is nonzero at some stalk, the stalks of $\mathcal O_T$ embed into the function field, so $\mathcal I$ is nonzero at the generic point, which lies in $V$. Whenever $f_i\ne0$, the ring $A[I/f_i]\subseteq A_{f_i}$ is a domain: its elements are fractions $x/f_i^m$ with $x\in I^m$, and a zero product implies $f_i^Nxy=0$ in the domain $A$, hence $x=0$ or $y=0$ (Stacks Tag 052R). If $f_i=0$ then $A_{f_i}=0$ and the chart is empty, so zero generators may be omitted; at least one generator is nonzero.

### Returned step 2

Let $U=T\setminus V(\mathcal I)$. Since $T$ is integral and $\mathcal I$ is nonzero, the generic point is in $U$, so $U$ is a nonempty dense open integral subscheme. By [thm-nagata-global-blowup-complement], $b^{-1}(U)\to U$ is an isomorphism. In each chart from Step 1, a nonzero chart generator defines the nonempty dense basic open $D(f_i)$, and these opens are exactly the corresponding pieces of $b^{-1}(U)$; hence $b^{-1}(U)$ is dense in every chart and therefore in $B$. The chart rings are domains, so $B$ is reduced; the dense irreducible open $b^{-1}(U)$ makes $B$ irreducible. Thus $B$ is integral by [def-pc-integral-scheme].

### Returned step 3

Let $\mathcal R=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}\mathcal I^n$ be the sheaf Rees algebra of [def-nagata-sheaf-rees-algebra], a quasi-coherent graded $\mathcal O_T$-algebra with $\mathcal R_0=\mathcal O_T$. By [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-symmetric-algebra](a) the degree-one inclusion $\mathcal I\to\mathcal R$ extends to an algebra map $\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathcal O_T}(\mathcal I)\to\mathcal R$; on an affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ it is, by (b), the map $\operatorname{Sym}_A(I_V)\to\bigoplus_n I_V^n$ sending $x_1\cdots x_n\in\operatorname{Sym}^n$ to the product $x_1\cdots x_n\in I_V^n$, where $\Gamma(V,\mathcal I^n)=I_V^n$ because $\mathcal I^n$ is the quasi-coherent image of $\mathcal I^{\otimes n}$. With the symmetric-degree grading [lem-weil-pbc-symmetric-algebra-graded] it is therefore graded and surjective, since every element of $I_V^n$ is a finite sum of products of $n$ elements of $I_V$. The relative Proj $\operatorname{Proj}_T(\mathcal R)$ [def-nagata-relative-proj] restricts over each affine $V$ to $\operatorname{Proj}(\Gamma(V,\mathcal R))=\operatorname{Proj}(\mathcal R_A(I_V))=\operatorname{Bl}_{I_V}(V)$ by clause 1 of [prop-nagata-relative-proj-functoriality] and [def-nagata-affine-blowup], compatibly with restriction to smaller affines; these are the local models of [def-nagata-global-blowup-local-model] with the same transition maps (both are induced by restriction of $\mathcal I$, i.e. by coefficientwise localization of the Rees algebras). By the uniqueness in the relative gluing statement [lem-weil-pbc-relative-glueing-affine-opens], $B\cong\operatorname{Proj}_T(\mathcal R)$ over $T$. Applying clause 3 (surjective maps) of [prop-nagata-relative-proj-functoriality] gives a closed immersion $B\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Proj}_T(\operatorname{Sym}\mathcal I)=\mathbf P_T(\mathcal I)$ over $T$. Since $\mathcal I$ is quasi-coherent of finite type, this is exactly the presentation required by [def-trf-projective-bundle-morphism] with $\mathcal E=\mathcal I$, so $b$ is projective in the projective-bundle sense.
