## Review by Q85

I read the statement of every node listed in a proof step's `uses`, and `citations.json` quotes its complete statement verbatim. I checked the following actual mathematical uses against those statements.

Step 1 uses `lem-sfb-space-change-base-to-integers`, whose clauses explicitly preserve fibre products, small etale sites and structure sheaves. Thus reducing the base to Spec Z preserves the data used by the proof, not just the underlying point sets. This is essential because the item's separatedness is absolute, while S is arbitrary.

Step 2 uses `lem-trf-space-separated-affine-chart-overlap` on the separated map Y -> Spec Z with affine target. At each induction stage its two inputs really are affine etale charts; its statement explicitly supplies affineness and etale projections for their overlap. The distinct-tuple geometry node supplies the clopen equality locus in the twofold product. The definitions of open and closed immersions then give separatedness of v by the explicit two-open sheaf-surjectivity argument. I did not cite a separation-composition statement for cancellation, which would not be what that statement says.

Step 3 uses `lem-sfb-space-alternating-sign-cohomology-spectral` with the now-separated, representable, surjective etale v. Its statement supplies the free action, fppf orbit sheaf, etale quotient and structural maps, locally trivial sign line, first-quadrant E1 page, stabilization and finite abutment filtration. In particular none of these assertions is extracted from its proof or from a definition node.

Step 4 uses `lem-sfb-etale-atlas-distinct-tuple-geometry` only for clopenness of the equality loci, with the one-based tuple count r=p+1 made explicit. The other two citations are exactly the open- and closed-immersion definitions. The complement inclusion is a closed immersion by the written structure-sheaf argument, and the affine spectrum-of-a-quotient clause of the closed-immersion definition gives affineness. This is a few-line consequence, so it is an elementary step rather than a new geometry node. No assertion that an arbitrary open of an affine is affine is made.

Step 5 uses the finite-disjoint-union clause of `lem-weil-pbc-affine-morphism-permanence`, and `lem-weil-pbc-finite-free-algebra-spectrum` on the finite product of the coordinate ring, with its displayed finite standard basis. The action projection is conjugate to the trivial projection; hence both projections satisfy the finite-locally-free hypotheses of `lem-a1-finite-flat-affine-relation-quotient`. Freeness from the spectral-sequence node makes the action groupoid a relation on all scheme tests. The quotient lemma states that the fppf orbit sheaf is an affine spectrum, which is precisely the conclusion needed about Q_p; I did not use the weaker finite-group-quotient node, whose statement only asserts a scheme quotient without asserting its affineness. Empty W_p and the trivial group at p=0 satisfy these hypotheses.

Step 6 uses the actual restriction clause in `def-trf-algebraic-space` and the local-presentation criterion of `def-trf-space-quasi-coherent-module`. Its third citation explicitly trivializes the sign line on an etale cover. Refining the presentation covers by that cover identifies the tensor with the original module locally and literally reuses its presentation; hence this is elementary from the stated definition. It does not require a theorem about tensoring exact sequences with a flat module or exactness of sign invariants. This is the proof of 072A written out for the coefficient actually used in E1.

Step 7 uses the essential-surjectivity clause of `lem-trf-scheme-zariski-etale-quasi-coherent-equivalence` to express this etale quasi-coherent module as an associated Zariski module. Only then does `lem-sc-etale-affine-qcoherent` apply to every positive degree on the affine Q_p. Thus coefficient categories and underlying abelian cohomology agree, and no module-derived versus abelian-derived comparison is assumed silently.

Step 8 uses the geometric-fibre tuple statement of the existing geometry node. The etale-fibre decomposition statement says that over an algebraically closed field every component is Spec of a finite separable extension, hence is just one copy of that field by algebraic closedness. This identifies points with geometric entries, so the hypothesis on at-most-d points really controls the tuples. The canonical residue-field morphism and existence of an algebraic closure are explicitly supplied by `lem-weil-pbc-morphism-from-spec-field` and `def-weil-algebraic-closure`, respectively, to justify emptiness from absence of geometric points. Surjectivity of W_p -> Q_p is supplied by the spectral-sequence node. The empty covering forces zero sections. The numerical pigeonhole argument and this empty-sheaf evaluation are elementary; no bound on nongemetric fibres is substituted for the given hypothesis.

Step 9 uses the finite convergence filtration from the spectral-sequence node, not merely a definition of spectral sequence. I wrote how zero E1 entries remain zero and how the finite filtration with zero subquotients gives zero abutment. The filtration has q+1 successive quotients, so there is no unbounded-convergence or infinite-intersection assumption.
