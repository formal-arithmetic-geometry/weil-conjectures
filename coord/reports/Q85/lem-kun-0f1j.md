### Original step 4

Induct on a positive divisor $m$ of $n$ annihilating $\mathcal F$. For $m=1$ the sheaf is zero. If $m$ is not prime, choose a prime $\ell\mid m$ and use $0\to\mathcal F[\ell]\to\mathcal F\to\mathcal F/\mathcal F[\ell]\to0$. The first term is killed by $\ell$, and the last by $m/\ell$: for a local section $x$, $\ell((m/\ell)x)=mx=0$, so $(m/\ell)x\in\mathcal F[\ell]$. Both annihilators are smaller than $m$. The associated triangle and Step 1 reduce to prime torsion. When $\ell\mathcal F=0$, put $k_\ell=\mathbf F_\ell$ and $\overline{\mathcal E}=\mathcal E/\ell\mathcal E$. For any sheaf $N$ killed by $\ell$, the mutually inverse maps $x\otimes y\mapsto\bar x\otimes y$ and $\bar x\otimes y\mapsto x\otimes y$ give $\mathcal E\otimes_R N\cong\overline{\mathcal E}\otimes_{k_\ell}N$: independence of the lift follows from $\ell y=0$, and the balanced relations give both composites equal to the identity. This is an isomorphism of sheaves by sheafifying these maps [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product]. In particular $\overline{\mathcal E}$ is flat, since its tensor functor on $k_\ell$-sheaves is the restriction of the exact tensor functor of $\mathcal E$. Derived direct images with either coefficient ring agree on underlying abelian complexes by [lem-weil-pbc-exchange-forget-coefficients](2). More explicitly, for either $u=g$ or $u=h$ and any $k_\ell$-module sheaf $H$, choose a bounded-below injective $k_\ell$-resolution $H\to J^\bullet$. Each term is acyclic for the $R$-module direct image: the coefficient-forgetting comparison first with $k_\ell$ and then with $R$ identifies its higher direct images with the same abelian higher direct images, which vanish. Hence $u_*J^\bullet$ computes the derived direct image with both coefficients by [src-right-derived-functor-plus-acyclic-unit]. Its hypotheses hold by [lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-enough-injectives] and [lem-weil-pbc-module-direct-image-left-exact]. This gives the natural $R$-linear identification of $Ru_*$ with restriction of the $k_\ell$-coefficient $Ru_*$, not merely an abstract isomorphism of underlying abelian objects. For the left side use $u=g$, $H=\mathcal F$ and apply the balanced tensor identity termwise to $(f')^{-1}g_*J^\bullet$. For the right side first use that identity on sheaves to identify $h^{-1}p^{-1}\mathcal E\otimes_R e^{-1}\mathcal F$ with $H=h^{-1}p^{-1}\overline{\mathcal E}\otimes_{k_\ell}e^{-1}\mathcal F$, and only then take a $k_\ell$-injective resolution of this $H$ for $u=h$. Thus no assertion that tensoring an $h_*$-acyclic resolution preserves $h_*$-acyclicity is needed. The flat factors compute the derived tensors, and exact tensor commutes with cohomology [lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-complex-cohomology]. Inverse image is exact [prop-weil-pbc-inverse-image-modules-exact], so the same balanced maps identify the pulled-back factors. The comparison itself is unchanged: its adjoint sends the pulled-back tensor $\bar x\otimes s$ to $\bar x\otimes\varepsilon_g(s)$ with either coefficient ring, by the stated counit formula. We have reduced to sheaves of $k_\ell$-vector spaces. Here $\ell$ is invertible in $K$, because it divides $n$.

### Original step 5

We first supply the arbitrary-$K$-scheme version of the untensored base change needed below; the existing [lem-kun-0f1i] states only its finite-type case. Let $Z$ be any $K$-scheme, retain $g:T\to S'$ qcqs, and let $\mathcal G$ be a $k_\ell$-module sheaf on $T$, with $\ell$ invertible in $K$. We first work with its underlying abelian sheaf. The assertion for $Z$ is local on $Z$: for an open $U\subseteq Z$ its pullback $U\times_K S'$ is open, and [lem-weil-pbc-derived-open-restriction], applied with the finite ring $k_\ell$, identifies restriction of the module right direct image with that of the restricted square. Forgetting coefficients via [lem-weil-pbc-exchange-forget-coefficients](1)–(3) gives the same restriction identification and canonical comparison for the underlying abelian sheaf. The left side simply restricts by inverse image. The comparison restricts to the same counit-defined comparison. We may take $Z=\operatorname{Spec}A$. The finitely generated $K$-subalgebras $A_i\subseteq A$ form a small directed poset (the algebra generated by the union bounds any two); $A=\varinjlim_i A_i$, since every element and every finite ring equation occurs in such an algebra. Thus $Z=\varprojlim_i\operatorname{Spec}A_i$ by [lem-weil-pbc-spec-small-colimit-limit]. The squares with upper left $\operatorname{Spec}A_i\times_KS'$, upper right $\operatorname{Spec}A_i\times_KT$, and lower row $T\xrightarrow g S'$ have this square as limit by [lem-kun-fibre-product-limits]. All transitions are affine (between affine $K$-schemes and then by base change [lem-weil-pbc-affine-morphism-permanence]); the lower transitions are identities. The sheaf system on $T$ is constant with value $\mathcal G$ and hence has colimit $\mathcal G$. For every $q\ge0$, [lem-kun-0f1i] gives the stage degree-$q$ comparison isomorphisms, and [lem-kun-0ezt] gives their limit comparison isomorphism. In the application here $\mathcal G$ is the underlying sheaf of $\mathcal F$, so its required torsion condition is [lem-kun-zn-sheaf-invertible-orders] with $n=\ell$. The module comparison follows from [lem-weil-pbc-exchange-forget-coefficients]. Therefore the untensored comparison for $\mathcal F$ is an isomorphism for every $Z$ over $K$, without any finite-type assumption on $Z,S'$ or $T$.

### Original step 8

For a constructible $M$ on affine $X$, [lem-weil-pbc-constructible-module-finite-pushforward-embedding] gives a monomorphism $M\hookrightarrow P=\bigoplus_i\pi_{i,*}\underline{V_i}$ with $\pi_i:X_i\to X$ finite and locally of finite presentation and $V_i$ finite $k_\ell$-modules. Choosing a finite basis of each $V_i$ identifies $\underline{V_i}$ with a finite direct sum of $\underline{k_\ell}$, so it suffices to handle $M=\pi_*\underline{k_\ell}$. Form $X'_i=X_i\times_X X'$ and $Y_i=X'_i\times_{X'}Y$, with projections $\pi':X'_i\to X'$, $\rho:Y_i\to Y$, $h_i:Y_i\to X'_i$ and $e_i=e\rho:Y_i\to T$. These are the diagram in the last paragraph of 0F1J. Base changes $\pi'$ and $\rho$ of $\pi$ are finite by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence](a). They are therefore proper by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-proper], and their direct images are exact by [lemma-weil-pbc-finite-direct-image-exact] (also for $k_\ell$-modules by [lem-weil-pbc-exchange-forget-coefficients]). Finite exchange [lemma-weil-pbc-finite-exchange], the projection formula [lem-kun-0f0g] for these finite proper morphisms and the torsion ring $k_\ell$, and the derived composition isomorphism $R(u\circ v)_*\cong Ru_*\circ Rv_*$ [def-weil-pbc-derived-direct-image-composition] give canonical identifications
$$A(\pi_*\underline{k_\ell},\mathcal F)\cong\pi'_*((\pi')^{-1}(f')^{-1}Rg_*\mathcal F),$$
$$B(\pi_*\underline{k_\ell},\mathcal F)\cong\pi'_*Rh_{i,*}e_i^{-1}\mathcal F.$$
Indeed, finite exchange first replaces $p^{-1}\pi_*\underline{k_\ell}$ by $\pi'_*\underline{k_\ell}$ and $h^{-1}p^{-1}\pi_*\underline{k_\ell}$ by $\rho_*\underline{k_\ell}$. The two projection formulas replace tensor with these sheaves by $\pi'_*((\pi')^{-1}-)$ and $\rho_*\rho^{-1}(-)$; then $h\rho=\pi'h_i$ gives the second identity by composition. Abelian derived composition and finite-exactness transfer to module coefficients through [lem-weil-pbc-exchange-forget-coefficients]. Under these identities the comparison is $\pi'_*$ of the untensored exchange for $X_i$ and $g$, which is invertible by Step 5. To check the map rather than just its endpoints, use the commuting projection/exchange square [lem-kun-proj-formula-natural] for the finite maps and paste adjunction mates [lem-weil-pbc-mate-pasting](2): the composite counit is $\varepsilon_{h_i}\circ h_i^{-1}\varepsilon_{\pi'}$, and the resulting adjunct is exactly $e_i^{-1}g^{-1}Rg_*\mathcal F\xrightarrow{e_i^{-1}\varepsilon_g}e_i^{-1}\mathcal F$, with the tensor unit on $X_i$. This is the defining adjunct of the untensored exchange. Thus $a^q_P$ is an isomorphism for all $q$, by finite additivity. No claim that $X_i$ is finite type over $K$ is needed, and no claim that $P$ or its quotient is constructible is used.
