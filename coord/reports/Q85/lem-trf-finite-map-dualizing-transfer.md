## Preserved original proof material and work briefs

### Original Step 1

For ordinary modules, restriction $U_0:\operatorname{Mod}(B)\to\operatorname{Mod}(A)$ has right adjoint $C_0(I)=\operatorname{Hom}_A(B,I)$ with $(b\varphi)(b')=\varphi(b'b)$. Explicitly the adjunction sends $f:N\to C_0(I)$ to $g:n\mapsto f(n)(1)$, with inverse $g\mapsto(n\mapsto[b\mapsto g(bn)])$. These are linear maps: $f(bn)(1)=(bf(n))(1)=f(n)(b)$, and $g(b'bn)$ verifies $B$-linearity of the inverse; both composites are identities and the formulas commute with pre- and postcomposition. Restriction preserves monomorphisms, since an injective underlying module map remains injective. Thus [lem-exc-rg-right-adjoint-preserves-injectives] applied to $U_0\dashv C_0$ proves that each injective $A$-module $I$ gives an injective $B$-module $C_0(I)$.

### Original Step 2

Choose a bounded complex $I^\bullet$ of injective $A$-modules representing $L$. It is K-injective by [lem-weil-pbc-bounded-below-injectives-kinjective]. Hence [def-pur-derived-internal-hom] computes $R\operatorname{Hom}_A(B[0],L)$ by $J^\bullet=\operatorname{Hom}_A^\bullet(B[0],I^\bullet)$. By [lem-trf-derived-coinduction-adjunction], this Hom complex with precomposition action represents $C_B(L)\in D(B)$ and has the required underlying $A$-object. Its degree $q$ term is $\operatorname{Hom}_A(B,I^q)$, injective over $B$ by Step 1, and it vanishes whenever $I^q$ vanishes. Consequently $\Omega_B=C_B(L)$ has a bounded injective $B$-representative $J^\bullet$.
