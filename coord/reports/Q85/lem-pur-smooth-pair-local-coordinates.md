Regular-sequence convention: `IsWeaklyRegular` is precisely injectivity of each multiplication
map on the quotient by the preceding equations. The assertion is imposed on every affine
open, so it is the sheaf condition, and not merely a claim about global sections of a
possibly nonaffine chart. At every point of `V` the final quotient is nonzero (its stalk is
the nonzero local ring of the closed subscheme), so the germ sequence is `IsRegular` in the
usual proper-ideal sense. One must not require `IsRegular` on *every* affine open: an affine
open disjoint from `V`, including the empty open, has zero final quotient. This does not
weaken the node's parenthetical successive-nonzerodivisor requirement. The point `y` and
`i y ∈ U` ensure that `V` is nonempty and that the equations are proper near `y`.

The square also implies that `v` is over `S`: the coordinate inclusion is constructed over
`S`, the square commutes, and `h` is over `S`. There is no replacement of a scheme-theoretic
inverse image by a set-theoretic zero locus, nor any arbitrary-map hypothesis.
