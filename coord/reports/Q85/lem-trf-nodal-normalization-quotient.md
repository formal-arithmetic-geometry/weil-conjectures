## Original arguments retained for the child workers

### Original step 1

Properness includes finite type by [def-weil-proper-morphism]. Apply [lem-trf-normalization-singular-skyscraper] to $X=B$. Reducedness, algebraic closedness, and purity of dimension one are precisely its remaining hypotheses. Its componentwise normalization is the map $\nu$ in the statement. It gives finiteness of $N$, a coherent exact sequence $0\to\mathcal O_B\to\nu_*\mathcal O_{\widetilde B}\to Q\to0$, and $Q\cong\bigoplus_{x\in N}\mathscr S_x(M_x)$. For each $x$, put $A=\mathcal O_{B,x}$, $C=(\nu_*\mathcal O_{\widetilde B})_x$ and $R=\widehat A$. The same statement identifies $M_x=C/A$ with $\widehat C/R$, where $\widehat C$ is the integral closure of $R$ in its total fraction ring.

### Original step 2

Fix $x\in N$ and choose the stated local-ring isomorphism $R\cong k[[u,v]]/(uv)$. Evaluation on the axes embeds this ring in $P=k[[u]]\times k[[v]]$: its kernel before quotienting consists exactly of the series all of whose terms are divisible by $uv$. Its image consists of the pairs with equal constant term, since such a pair $(f,g)$ is represented by $f(u)+g(v)-f(0)$. Put $s=(u,v)\in R$. It is a non-zero-divisor. Every non-zero-divisor of $R$ has two nonzero components: if one component is zero, multiplication by a nonzero series on that axis annihilates it. Thus localization embeds $Q(R)$ in $k((u))\times k((v))$. Conversely, for any pair of Laurent series $h$, choose $n$ so large that both components of $s^nh$ have zero constant term and no negative powers. Then $s^nh\in R$, so $h=(s^nh)/s^n\in Q(R)$. This proves the claimed total fraction ring directly.

### Original step 3

Let $e=(1,0)\in Q(R)$; explicitly $e=(u,0)/s$. The relation $e^2=e$ makes it integral over $R$, and every $(f,g)\in P$ is an $R$-linear combination of $1,e$: take $a=(g(0),g)\in R$ and $b=(f-g(0),f(0)-g(0))\in R$, so $(f,g)=a+be$. Hence $P=R[e]$ is integral over $R$. Each factor $k[[u]]$ and $k[[v]]$ is integrally closed by [lem-weil-pbc-power-series-normal-domain], applied to the Noetherian normal domain $k$ (a field has only two ideals and is its own fraction field). If $(h_1,h_2)\in Q(R)$ satisfies a monic equation over $R$, project that equation to each factor. The factorwise normality forces $h_1\in k[[u]]$ and $h_2\in k[[v]]$. Therefore $P$ is exactly the integral closure of $R$, so it is $\widehat C$ from Step 1.

### Original step 4

The map $P\to k$, $(f,g)\mapsto f(0)-g(0)$, is surjective and has kernel $R$ by Step 2. Multiplication by an element of $R$ acts on this difference by its common constant term. Thus $P/R$ is free of rank one over the residue field of $R$, and hence of $A$. That residue field is $k$ via the structural map by Step 1. Even if the chosen completed-ring isomorphism twists the scalar field, choosing a nonzero quotient vector gives an $A$-linear identification with this structural residue field. The $A$-linear comparison $M_x\cong\widehat C/R$ of Step 1 therefore identifies $M_x$ with $k$, killed by the maximal ideal of $A$. Consequently $\mathscr S_x(M_x)$ is $i_{x,*}k$, where $i_x:\operatorname{Spec}k\hookrightarrow B$ is the closed point inclusion. Substituting these identifications in Step 1 gives $Q\cong\bigoplus_{x\in N}i_{x,*}k$. The identifications can depend on the chosen ordering of the branches; the statement asserts an isomorphism, with no canonical branch ordering. Both a self-node and an intersection of distinct components give the same single quotient copy of $k$.

### Original step 5

The quotient $Q$ is coherent by Step 1 and supported on the finite set of closed points $N$ by Step 4. Apply [lem-pc-chi-zero-dim-support] with the given proper scheme $B$: it gives $H^j(B,Q)=0$ for $j>0$ and $\chi(B,Q)=\dim_k\Gamma(B,Q)$. By the description of the skyscraper sections in Step 1 and the finite direct sum of Step 4, $\Gamma(B,Q)=\bigoplus_{x\in N}k$. Its standard coordinate vectors form a basis of size $|N|=e$, so $\chi(B,Q)=e$. When $N$ is empty the quotient is zero and the same calculation gives zero.

The child-normalization worker should cite the existing lem-weil-pbc-power-series-normal-domain for factorwise normality, as original step 3 did; it is not an additional newly created leaf. Locality of S follows because a pair with nonzero common constant has inverse pair with equal reciprocal constant; the remaining pairs form the kernel of the surjection c to the field k. These observations make the extra residue-field vocabulary in the new statement explicit.
