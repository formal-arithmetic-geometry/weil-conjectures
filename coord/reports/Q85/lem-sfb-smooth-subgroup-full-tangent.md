Each citation below was checked in the cited JSON statement itself; `citations.json` copies each whole statement verbatim, with its step number.

Step 1 uses `def-weil-closed-immersion` for precisely the affine quotient presentation, and `lem-pc-affine-finite-type-algebra` for finite generation of C. The same generators generate C/I, so no quotient-finiteness lemma is hidden. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian` applies because the base field is Noetherian (only ideals 0 and k); `lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization` and `lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian` give Noetherian identity local rings. A rational identity is a k-point, so its residue field is k. No finite-type hypothesis on B is added: it is obtained from the affine quotient.

Step 2 uses `lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-cotangent-dimension` with exactly the Noetherian local rings established in step 1. It supplies finite cotangent dimension, the inequality, and the equality/regularity equivalence. `lem-sc-rational-tangent-cotangent-duality` applies to the rational identity points; its dual spaces have the same dimension by the explicitly finite-dimensional statement `lem-sfb-linear-dual-dimension-rank`. `lem-ag-group-identity-local-dimension` states equality of local and global group dimensions over any field and without smoothness. This is why the dimension comparison is valid even for the possibly nonsmooth A. I use only its statement, not its proof or its restricted Lie-algebra definition dependencies.

Step 3 converts the given affine smooth morphism to the smooth ring map using `lem-weil-pbc-smooth-ring-map-iff-smooth-affine`, then applies `lem-weil-pbc-smooth-over-field-regular-local` at the identity prime. Both statements explicitly allow an arbitrary field. Step 2 then yields d_B=t_B. B is not assumed connected.

Step 4 is elementary. Lifts of a basis through the surjective tangent map are linearly independent, proving t_A<=t_B without an uncited rank theorem. A closed embedding sends strict chains of irreducible closed subsets to strict chains, proving d_B<=d_A directly from `def-kun-topological-krull-dimension` and `def-weil-closed-immersion`. The four-term sandwich then proves equality and the already cited local regularity criterion applies. This is the actual argument of 10.15, not a new node carrying its conclusion.

Step 5 applies `lem-sfb-regular-separable-affine-smooth-neighbourhood` to U=A and the identity prime. Its finite-type and regularity hypotheses were proved, and its residue field is k/k, hence finite separable even when k is imperfect. This supplies an actual smooth open neighbourhood. `lem-sfb-group-smoothness-identity-criterion` then applies over Spec k, a locally Noetherian base, to the finite-type flat group A. `lem-weil-pbc-module-over-field-flat` applies to every stalk as a k-vector space and `def-weil-flat-morphism` is precisely the stalkwise definition, so this flatness does not use an uncited preservation theorem. The general criterion's first clause has no Dedekind or Neron hypotheses: those belong only to its final special case. I checked that distinction in its statement. This proves smoothness of A and discharges the arbitrary-field issue without extending scalars.

The statement of `lem-sc-smooth-algebra-reduced-field` gives reducedness of C. The existing `lem-wf-smooth-components-disjoint` applies to affine finite-type schemes over any field with regular closed-point local rings; it says that the irreducible components are the connected components. The actual connectedness hypothesis on A now gives a single irreducible component. I read its current blueprint proof, which uses regular local domains and minimal primes, and did not infer irreducibility from smoothness alone.

Step 7 applies `lem-wf-affine-dimension-drop` only now that A is an irreducible reduced affine variety of finite type, exactly its hypotheses. A and B are nonempty due to their identities. Equality d_A=d_B contradicts a proper closed image. The dimension of B equals the dimension of its image by the homeomorphism in the closed-immersion definition.
