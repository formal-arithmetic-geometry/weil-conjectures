## Review by Q47

I read the retained text extraction of the supplied ILO volume with the source places open: IX Theorem 1.1 and 1.2, §§1.3--1.4 and §§2.1--2.4, printed pp. 131--134; VII §4 and Theorem 4.3, pp. 82--83; VIII Theorem 1.1, p. 85, and §§4.1.2--4.1.8, pp. 117--118; VI §§1.8--1.9, §2 and §3.2. These justify the four separate new statements and the existing marked nodal input. In particular VIII 1.1 includes quasi-excellence; excellence of C in the model supplies it. I kept this hypothesis and separatedness rather than using an unrestricted quotient theorem.

For the reduction node, IX 1.3 first reduces to finite dimension and inducts, with dimensions at most one handled by normalization. It passes through completion and partial algebraization (III 6.2 and V 3.1.3), then compactification and making the original closed set a divisor. IX 1.4 applies the equivariant nodal model, passes to an ell-Sylow subgroup, and uses induction on the lower-dimensional base. The final starred situation is precisely a regular affine base with strict boundary and a finite normalization of a connected etale ell-group torsor. D is a Cartier marking etale in the relative smooth locus; it is finite because it is closed in the projective curve. The quotient is integral excellent and its action admissible, as IX 1.4 explicitly checks. Consequently the conditional reduction is narrower than general uniformization and does not presume its logarithmic conclusion. The original S need only be quasi-excellent, with no global dimension bound; those stronger hypotheses occur only in the intermediate model. The empty scheme permits an empty covering family; the low-dimensional induction is included in the reduction, not in a nonexistent nodal model of negative dimension.

For the normalization input, IX 2.1 identifies finite covers tame along the strict boundary with Kummer etale covers and identifies their schemes with normalization in the cover. VI 1.8 and 2.3 give log regularity. Invertible group order excludes wild inertia. I checked the empty boundary: this gives an ordinary finite etale cover of the regular scheme, not a ramified singular cover. For the trivial group it recovers the original regular pair. The statement explicitly defines normalization in the extension field, since `def-sc-normalization` alone describes normalization in the original function field. It does not claim that the normalized scheme must itself be regular.

For the quotient input, VIII 1.1 modifies the excellent separated log regular source with a tame generically free action to make the action very tame, allowing the boundary to enlarge by the nonfree locus. VI 3.2 then gives a log regular quotient. IX 2.4 spells out the quotient modification and boundary containment. The node permits enlarging the boundary, so it does not require the original action to be free off B. Its admissibility and existing finite quotient assumptions ensure a scheme quotient. For the trivial group the identity with the given log regular pair suffices; for an empty initial boundary with nontrivial stabilizers the enlarged boundary is essential and is allowed. I did not infer log regularity of the original quotient from mere tameness.

For the resolution input, VII 4.3 applies to a Noetherian log regular pair without a ground-field restriction and gives a projective birational regular modification with a normal crossings boundary. VIII 4.1.6--4.1.8 makes that boundary strict through permissible blowups. IX 2.4 explicitly uses this final form. Properness is weaker than the source's projectivity, and the selected integral component dominating integral M remains integral; no smoothness over an unavailable field is asserted. For B empty, log regularity means ordinary regularity and the identity and empty boundary are allowed. Strictification is necessary for self-intersecting global normal-crossings components; I kept it in this genuine input rather than treating strictness as automatic.

Every condition is in the model; `lem-kun-projective-implies-proper` supplies the properness conversion. That nodal statement returns both log regularity and nowhere density of B.
