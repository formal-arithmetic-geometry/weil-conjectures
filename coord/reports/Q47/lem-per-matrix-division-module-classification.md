Step 1 cites def-weil-matrix for matrices and def-weil-pbc-simple-module for the exact nonzero/no-proper-submodule condition. The matrix with jth column w_i v_j^{-1} sends v to w because v_j^{-1}v_j = 1 in this order. This proves column simplicity without an unrecorded simplicity theorem.

Step 2 cites def-weil-matrix for e and lem-per-division-ring-finite-basis for a finite D-basis. Its finite-generation hypothesis is proved, not assumed: projecting a finite K-basis of N by e gives a finite K-spanning family of eN, hence a D-spanning family using the structural K map. On eN, a I_d and E_11(a) have equal action. The cited basis statement says precisely that a finite spanning family yields a basis with unique coordinates. There is no use of commutativity of D or an unjustified transfer of finite dimension between fields.

Step 3 cites lem-per-matrix-corner-reconstruction. Its statement gives N isomorphic to (eN)^d with the column action actually used here, including both maps. It does not merely say that the categories are equivalent. The remaining map is explicitly the arrangement of the coefficient array a_it into n columns; both its inverse and B-linearity follow from unique coordinates and the displayed coefficient formula. No theorem is extracted from a cited proof rather than its statement.

Step 4 cites the finite-basis node's unique-size clause and the matrix definition. A B-module isomorphism commutes with e and a I_d, so its restriction is a D-module isomorphism of the corners. The corners of S^n and S^m are explicitly D^n and D^m. A transported standard basis is a basis because a bijective linear map preserves unique linear expressions; comparison with the standard basis is exactly the cited unique-size clause. This proves uniqueness without assuming an uncited formula for dimensions of products.

Step 5 cites the simple-module definition and the matrix definition. Simplicity makes Bx = T. A finite K-basis c_t of D gives the explicit K-spanning family E_ij(c_t)x of T, so applying the finite-module result is legitimate even though the statement quantifies over all simple modules. Nonzero T excludes n = 0; a single summand is visibly a nonzero proper submodule for n > 1. No semisimplicity, Jordan–Hölder or Artin–Wedderburn result is hidden here.

Step 6 uses the matrix definition and computes every endomorphism explicitly. E_11 forces f(u_1) = u_1 a. The expansion using E_i1(v_i) forces f(v) = v a in the correct order. Composition gives R_a after R_b equal to R_ba, exactly the opposite multiplication, and the central image of K identifies the scalar maps. Addition, unit, injectivity, surjectivity and K-linearity of the algebra map are all accounted for. This calculation avoids an uncited claim about preservation of Hom under Morita equivalence.
