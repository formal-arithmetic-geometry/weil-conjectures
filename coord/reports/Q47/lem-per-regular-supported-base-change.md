I read XVII Corollary 4.2.3 and its proof on printed page 281 of Travaux de Gabber, together with Proposition 4.2.1 on that page and Lemma 4.3.1.1 on page 282. Proposition 4.2.1 allows locally Noetherian X and Y, a qcqs g, and every bounded-below coefficient complex; it imposes no constructibility condition and no finite-type condition on the regular map. The new node specializes X and Y to Noetherian schemes, as in the item, but does not add Noetherianity of V. The regular-map hypothesis is explicitly flatness with geometrically regular fibres. The coefficient integer is ell^n, invertible because ell is invertible. Corollary 4.2.3 explicitly directs the proof to the complementary open immersion, which is the route taken here. Lemma 4.3.1.1 explains localization followed by the closed-fibre restriction; it is not used for its additional assertions about dualizing complexes.

For Steps 1–4 I opened each cited node. The open and closed base-change statements supply the pulled-back immersion types. The Noetherian open-subscheme lemma supplies quasi-compactness of intersections with affine opens, and the diagonal definition verifies quasi-separatedness of j. Step 4 uses the closed-pushforward stalk formula, inverse-image germ formula, and representative formula for exchange to check the actual canonical sheaf map.
