## Review by Q47

I read Milne, *Algebraic Groups*, section 9.b, printed pp.166--171, especially Theorem 9.11 (pp.168--169), Theorem 9.18 (p.170), and section 9.19 (p.171), and chapter 14, printed pp.279--283, especially Proposition 14.1 and its proof (pp.279--280), the definition in 14.b, Proposition 14.3 and Corollary 14.12. These verify the original statement: faithful-matrix unipotence implies unipotence in every rational representation, after which the common fixed vector exists. The conclusion requested is only on K-points, so no scheme-valued invariant functor needs to be identified. I did not assume connectedness, characteristic zero, finiteness of U(K), or faithfulness of the requested representation.

For the new pointwise node, Milne 9.19 says that an element unipotent in one faithful representation is unipotent in every representation. Its preceding Theorem 9.18 constructs intrinsic commuting semisimple and unipotent parts of each group point, compatible with every rational representation. The faithful representation identifies the semisimple part with the identity when its matrix is unipotent, by uniqueness in 9.11, and injectivity then identifies the group point with its unipotent part. Algebraically closed K is a valid specialization of Milne's perfect-field hypothesis in all characteristics. The new statement retains the strong functorial faithfulness of the item, so it does not accidentally replace scheme faithfulness with only geometric injectivity. It requires no smoothness because Theorem 9.18 covers arbitrary affine algebraic groups. This is a genuine pointwise prerequisite, with no common vector in its conclusion.

For the new matrix-group node, Proposition 14.1 explicitly allows an abstract group acting on a nonzero finite-dimensional vector space over any field; a subgroup H of GL_d(K) acting on K^d is precisely a specialization. I checked the proof through its double-centralizer argument: after choosing a simple submodule over an algebraic closure, the span of g-id is a two-sided ideal of the full endomorphism ring and every such difference has trace zero. The trace map is nonzero because a matrix unit has trace one, even when the characteristic divides the dimension; no division by d or by a group order is valid or used. The existence of a common fixed vector is therefore not a finite-group averaging assertion. This argument is source material for the future split and is not embedded in the unsplit node's statement.

Step 1 uses only `lem-sfb-rational-representation-unipotent-point`. I opened the new statement and checked it supplies exactly the nilpotence of rho(u)-I needed. G=U satisfies affine finite type, K is algebraically closed, m and d are positive, i has the same every-R injectivity hypothesis, and the original item gives the required nilpotence separately for each u. The exponent is allowed to vary. This step does not take the fixed vector from that node.

Step 2 uses `def-sc-group-scheme` and `def-sc-general-linear-group`. I opened both statements: the former explicitly says the functor of points takes values in groups and that homomorphisms preserve multiplication; the latter explicitly identifies GL(R) with invertible matrices with their ordinary product, identity and inverse. The written formulas rho(e)=I, rho(uw)=rho(u)rho(w), and rho(u^{-1})=rho(u)^{-1} prove that the image is a subgroup. Applying the pointwise result to a chosen preimage of h proves the unipotence hypothesis for H. This is elementary image-subgroup algebra, not an uncited theorem about algebraic images or closed subgroup schemes.
