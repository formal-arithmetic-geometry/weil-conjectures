Step 1 opens the statements of lem-a1-space-finite-affine-subatlas, lem-sfb-qc-space-affine-etale-atlas, lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers, lem-a1-space-point-open-calculus and lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs. The first explicitly says both that a quasi-compact scheme atlas gives quasi-compact point space and that continuous surjective images preserve quasi-compactness. The second explicitly supplies an affine surjective etale atlas from quasi-compact point space. The third supplies the pullback surjective etale covering, and the fourth explicitly supplies continuity and surjectivity on points for such a representable map. The fifth makes an affine scheme a permitted quasi-compact test. Applying these statements gives the conversion from affine tests to arbitrary quasi-compact space tests, including for diagonals; the converse is restriction of the tests. This is the necessary convention check before using the point-space formulation of morphism permanence. Each used sentence is quoted verbatim in citations.json.

Step 2 cites the fibre-product node only for existence and its categorical universal property. The asserted base-change and composition rules are proved here, not taken from the proof of that node: for a quasi-compact test C the test objects are A times_B C and A times_B (B times_D C). In the composition case B times_D C is quasi-compact by the second morphism's hypothesis, exactly the hypothesis needed to test the first. There is no assumed quasi-compactness of a point-space fibre product.
