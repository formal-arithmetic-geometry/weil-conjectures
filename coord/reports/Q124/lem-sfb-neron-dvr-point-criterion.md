## Review by Q124

I read the statement and proof of BLR §10.1 Proposition 2 on PDF p.296, including its definition of essentially smooth as an actual local ring of a smooth scheme. I read BLR §4.4 Theorem 1 and its diagonal argument on PDF pp.115–116: the map must be S-rational (fibrewise dense domain), not merely defined on a topologically dense generic open. The new node explicitly includes that hypothesis and the codimension-at-most-one condition. It does not silently strengthen the source to arbitrary dense opens. The theorem itself does not impose finite type on the entire target, and applies locally on the source. I also read Stacks algebra.tex Tags 00PD (lemma-characterize-dvr), 07NF (lemma-regular-goes-up), 0567 (lemma-regular-normal), 00ON (lemma-dimension-base-fibre-equals-total), and schemes.tex Tag 0HA1 (lemma-local-ring-fibre) against the nodes used here.

Step 1 uses the DVR definition and dimension-one statement to check all prime localizations of R; the regular-ring definition therefore applies. Regular smooth ascent says exactly that a smooth algebra over a regular ring is regular, and regular-normal says precisely that its stalks are normal domains. Smooth reduced/dense pullback supplies reducedness, local Noetherianity and density for arbitrary smooth test schemes. I did not use the misleadingly broad title of lem-sc-smooth-regular: its statement only concerns curves over fields and is not applicable here.

Step 2 uses the closed-subset-minimal statement to get the Noetherian topological condition on a Noetherian affine chart, then the normal component statement, which actually supplies disjoint open normal integral components. Taking an affine open inside the component needs no global finiteness theorem. The affine-stalk node states the full localization fraction/zero criteria, so it supports both the fraction-field equality here and the calculations in steps 4 and 6.

Step 3 uses the unit-times-uniformizer-power node on the actual A, with its nonzero maximal generator and Noetherian domain hypotheses checked by the DVR definition. The image of the base uniformizer is a nonzero nonunit by injectivity and locality; its positive power makes inverting it invert every nonzero element. The finite-presentation definition provides finitely many generators AND finitely many relations for the chosen smooth affine chart. Step 4 writes the denominator and relation calculations, using s outside the prime so that the neighbourhood still contains y. This is a finite localization calculation, marked elementary, followed directly by hypothesis (i); no general approximation lemma is hidden there.

Step 5 applies normal-dimension-one-DVR only to actual one-dimensional stalks, whose normality and Noetherianity were proved. Smooth-flat supplies flatness, and the injection R→A is justified by tensoring multiplication by each nonzero r, not assumed from an arbitrary structural map. Thus the test rings meet all hypotheses of (ii), including being actual local rings of smooth schemes and being local injective extensions.

Step 6 spells out the other spreading calculation rather than assuming that a morphism from Spec A spreads over all of X. The local spectrum factors through an affine chart at the closed image; finite presentation lets one clear the generator denominators and the finitely many relation numerators. The chosen neighbourhood is integral by step 2. Equality on its generic fibre follows from the exact statement of relative-separated-dominant-ext (reduced source, dense-image test, separated target), because its generic point has field Frac A. I checked that the target G is separated by the explicit quotient/base-change diagonal calculation. This is not a use of the field-only group extension theorem.

Step 7 cites relative-separated-dominant-ext again on overlaps with their dense generic fibres, and scheme-morphism-gluing for the actual compatible maps on an arbitrary open cover. Its zero-dimensional local-domain check rules out missing ambient codimension-zero special points. Step 8 uses the flat local dimension formula with Noetherian rings and flat stalk map, the exact fibre-stalk quotient identification, and the already established dim R=1. A generic point of a fibre component has zero-dimensional fibre stalk by prime localization. Thus it lies in the previously extended dimension-one locus. This verifies the fibrewise density required by the new theorem, rather than inferring it from density of X_K in X.

Step 9 applies the new theorem with all its hypotheses now checked: normal Noetherian S, smooth X and N, separated N, fibrewise dense U, and all ambient points of dimension ≤1 in U. Relative-separated-dominant-ext separately gives injectivity of restriction using the reduced X and dense X_K. The new node supplies neither the forward implication nor the DVR-to-rational-domain construction, so this is not a relabelled clause of the item.

Small cases checked: empty X and empty special fibres; relative dimension zero; disconnected test schemes (integral charts, never a false assertion that the entire X is integral); non-quasi-compact X and N (arbitrary gluing, finite data only inside individual affine charts); arbitrary residue characteristic and imperfect residue field. A constant identity map is included, and the nonproper multiplicative group illustrates why an S-dense codimension-one domain is necessary in the new theorem. No properness, completeness, excellence or perfectness is used. Every non-elementary step cites statements that supply its inputs, and citations.json copies those actual statements verbatim.
