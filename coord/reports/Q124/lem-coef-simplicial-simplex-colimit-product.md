## Review by Q124

I read Hovey's definition of the simplex category and Lemma 3.1.3 on printed p.75, the realization-product argument on p.77, and the k-space exponential law in Proposition 2.4.22(5) on p.58 online. The item's category is all k-spaces, without a weak-Hausdorff restriction; Hovey distinguishes this category from his compactly generated weak-Hausdorff category. The statement assumes colimit preservation of R and the exponential law, and its product conclusion is binary. Those hypotheses suffice; no local compactness, fibrancy, finiteness, connectedness, or extra separation condition is used. I left the statement verbatim and checked every step against the node statements actually cited, not their proofs.

1. Step 1 cites `def-cbc-enhanced-diagrams-cones` for the exact formula for standard simplices and `def-weil-pbc-simplicial-set` for functorial operators and natural transformations. The formula evaluating a map at the identity is proved, with inverse `alpha ↦ X(alpha)(x)`. This also proves that all simplex-category arrows are monotone maps. Its explicit object set and finite hom-sets show that all colimits used later have small indices. This step is elementary evaluation and the functor identity.

2. Step 2 cites those same definitions and `def-weil-pbc-category-colimit`, whose statement gives exactly existence and uniqueness of a map from a cocone. The map from X is constructed degreewise from each cocone leg, and the cocone equation is evaluated to prove simplicial naturality. The equation on every simplex then proves both factorization and uniqueness. This is the direct proof Hovey abbreviates, with no uncited general Yoneda or density theorem. Degenerate simplices are included, since all monotone maps are used; the argument does not silently restrict to nondegenerate simplices.

3. Step 3 cites the full statement of `lem-weil-pbc-presheaf-colimits-sectionwise`, with the presheaf category on Delta and value category Sets, and `lem-weil-pbc-left-adjoint-preserves-colimits`, with left adjoint `T × -` and right adjoint the set of functions from T. Ordinary function currying supplies the adjunction explicitly. The first cited node does state that the resulting sectionwise object and legs are the colimit cocone, not merely that some colimits exist. The standard-simplex definition node also states that products are formed degreewise. Thus the set comparison is precisely the comparison in every simplicial degree. Naturality of that comparison forces its inverse to commute with operators. I marked the step non-elementary because it applies the two cited colimit theorems, rather than hiding them in the word 'degreewise'. These existing Mathlib leaves are generic tools, so no off-route theorem is being substituted for a unit argument.

4. Step 4 cites `lem-coef-kspace-exponential-law`, whose statement explicitly defines k-ification and the mapping-space notation and states the natural currying bijection, and the same left-adjoint-colimit theorem. The latter applies to arbitrary categories and any existing small colimit. Its hypotheses are met by the assumed exponential law and the small-colimit assumption on K. Swapping factors uses continuous inverse coordinate permutations and their k-ifications. This establishes preservation separately in each variable in K; it does not assume ordinary products of arbitrary topological spaces preserve quotient topologies. This step too is marked non-elementary, with the theorem cited.

5. Step 5 uses the coordinatewise product convention from `def-cbc-enhanced-diagrams-cones` and writes the two projection equations proving naturality of the canonical comparison. It invokes no theorem of geometric realization, and defines the very map whose invertibility is required. The equality is checked after both product projections, so no unexplained identification of comparison morphisms remains.

6. Step 6 uses previously established colimit cocones from steps 2–4 and the hypotheses on R and simplex pairs. Its only new citation is `def-weil-pbc-category-colimit`, specifically the universal map and its uniqueness. The objectwise inverse equation along every transition is written explicitly, which verifies compatibility of the inverse cocone. Both inverse identities are then checked on all colimit legs. Marking this step elementary means these few universal-property equations from the already established cocones; it does not mean Hovey's whole reduction is assumed elementary or cited as an opaque lemma.

7. Step 7 repeats those explicit universal-property equations over Simp(X), now using the isomorphisms just proved in step 6. It again cites the ordinary-colimit definition for the factorization and both uniqueness arguments. The functor R is used only on the small simplex diagrams and their products. The resulting inverse is a morphism in K, hence continuous, so this proves a homeomorphism, rather than just a set bijection. Step 5 already proves naturality of the canonical maps, and composing that equation with inverses gives naturality of the inverse. No separate unproved 'transport' assertion is used.
