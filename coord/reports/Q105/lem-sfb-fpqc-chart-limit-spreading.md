## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported statement omitted quasi-separatedness of the locally representing scheme P. This is false even for the identity fpqc covering and an already representable F. The correction requires P to be quasi-separated (equivalently P/A quasi-separated because A is affine). It retains every conclusion and does not assert any finite-stage representation of F. Raynaud's reduction 2 makes his X' separated before reduction 3; our correction retains only the weaker finiteness hypothesis his later limit test needs. This repairs an overgeneralization of the cited source, not Raynaud's theorem.

Explicit counterexample to the old statement: let k be a field, R=k[x_1,x_2,...], S=A=Spec R and let U=union D(x_n). Glue two copies of S along U, obtaining P, and take F=h_P. The map P→S is locally an identity and therefore locally of finite presentation. Set T_i=Spec R/(x_1,...,x_i), with transition closed immersions, and T=Spec k. Then U_T is empty and Y=P_T is two disjoint k-points. Take V=Y. This is affine and finitely presented over T, so it satisfies even the quasi-affine clause of the old statement.

If a requested chart V_i existed, representability of F would identify it with an open subscheme of P_{T_i}, whose image contains both points over the origin m_i=(x_{i+1},x_{i+2},...). Since V_i is finitely presented over affine T_i, it is quasi-separated. Choose affine basic neighborhoods D(f) and D(g) of the two origins contained in V_i and their respective branches, with f(0),g(0) nonzero. Their intersection in V_i is D(fg)∩U_{T_i}. This open is not quasi-compact: the cover by D(fg x_n), n>i, has no finite subcover. Given finitely many chosen variables, set them, and all variables occurring in f and g, to zero and a fresh variable to one. The resulting k-point belongs to D(fg)∩U_{T_i} and avoids that finite subcover. Thus two affine opens of V_i have a non-quasi-compact intersection, contradicting quasi-separatedness. This also shows why eventual openness cannot be invoked without checking the target hypothesis.

Proof to be retained for the future worker of the new continuity node: let A_2=A×_S A and P_2=P×_{A,pr_1}A_2. On every Z_i, the fpqc sheaf condition identifies F(Z_i) with the equalizer of F(Z_i×_S A) ⇉ F(Z_i×_S A_2). The same equalizer holds on Z. Both base-changed source systems are qcqs with affine transitions (A→S and A_2→S are affine); their limits are the corresponding base changes of Z. For injectivity, two stage sections equal on Z have equal maps into P on the limit over A; eventual equality makes those maps equal at a later stage and the covering equalizer makes the sections equal.
