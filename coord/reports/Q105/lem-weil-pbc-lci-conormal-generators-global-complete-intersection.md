### Localized conormal span modulo the square

Read Stacks Algebra Tag 00SC, `lemma-lci`, lines 35944–35946, before its invocation of `lemma-NAK`. For arbitrary specified f_j in I, let z be an element of I. Because [f_j]/1 span the conormal localization, write [z]/1 as a sum (beta_j/t)[f_j]/1 with one common t outside q. The fraction equality criterion gives v outside q annihilating t[z] minus the sum beta_j[f_j] in I/I². Lift v,t,beta_j to P; the first two lifts are outside Q. Thus

$$e=\widetilde v\widetilde t z-\sum_j\widetilde v\widetilde\beta_j f_j\in I^2.$$

In R=P_Q the product of the first two lifts is a unit. Solve this equality for z/1: its first summand lies in JR and its error lies in I²R. Consequently IR is contained in JR+I²R, since the z/1 generate IR. Conversely J and I² are contained in I. Finally I²R=(IR)²: a generator (xy)/1 equals (x/1)(y/1); conversely expand products of finite linear combinations of images of I and distribute coefficients. This proves the stated ideal equality. No Nakayama, finite generation, regularity, or quotient-localization isomorphism is being assumed. The quotient S-action on I/I² is precisely multiplication by lifts, so the error formula has the correct scalar action. The empty family is handled by the identical calculation with empty sum.

This task is not the existing generator-bound lemma: the latter's statement gives only a minimum number of generators, not an equality modulo I² for a supplied family; it also assumes I finite. The new task can be proved without that hypothesis and is a separate localization/quotient comparison argument.

### Nakayama and finite spreading for a fixed subideal

Read the first and third proof paragraphs of Stacks Tag 00SC (`lemma-lci`, lines 35944–35946 and 35967–35972), and its cited Nakayama statement Tag 00DV (`lemma-NAK`). Write M=IR, N=JR and m=QR. A finite generating set b_1,...,b_m of I maps to a finite generating set of M. The containment I in Q gives M in the maximal ideal of the local ring R, hence M² is contained in mM. The assumed equality M=N+M² gives M=N+mM: one inclusion follows from M² contained in mM; the reverse follows from N and mM contained in M. Apply the submodule form of `lem-weil-pbc-nakayama-local-generators` to obtain M=N.

For each b_i, membership b_i/1 in JR is a finite sum of elements of J with localized coefficients. Choose a common denominator s_i outside Q and a numerator y_i in J so that b_i/1=y_i/s_i. The localization equality criterion supplies t_i outside Q with t_i(s_i b_i-y_i)=0. Thus t_i s_i b_i belongs to J. Set u to the product of the t_i s_i, with empty product 1. Primality ensures u is outside Q; for every i, u b_i belongs to J. Multiplication and addition then show ux belongs to J for all x in I. Localizing at u gives IP_u=JP_u because u is a unit there. No generators of J are chosen or replaced. This is why the existing local-generators-clearing statement, whose conclusion chooses an unspecified new list F_i, cannot supply this fixed-subideal assertion by specialization. No global Noetherian assumption is needed.

### Original step 1

By [lem-weil-pbc-polynomial-quotient-dimension-at-point-height-difference], $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)$ and $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q')$ are integers with $0\le\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)\le\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q')\le n$ and $d=\dim_{\mathfrak q}(\operatorname{Spec}S)$ [def-kun-topological-dimension-at-point] equals $n-\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q')+\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)$. Hence $d$ is an integer, $d\le n$ (as $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)\le\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q')$) and $d\ge n-\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q')\ge0$. This is the first assertion. Put $c=n-d$, an integer with $0\le c\le n$, and assume from now on that $(I/I^2)_{\mathfrak q}$ is generated over $S_{\mathfrak q}$ by $c$ elements.
