## Allocation of all five old arguments

The first new task carries the substantial part of old Steps 1--2: simultaneous power-annihilator rigidity. Its statement is stronger than the needed ring case but omits both of the parent's localization/sheaf conclusions. Its intended proof, recorded here and not inside the unsplit node, is the full original induction: assume the common kernel of multiplication by f_i on M is zero. Induct simultaneously for all elements m and positive exponent tuples on E=sum_i(e_i-1). For E=0 use the hypothesis. For E>0 pick j with e_j>1. The element b=f_j m is killed by exponents e_i for i unequal j, and by e_j-1 for j, by commutativity of scalars. Their excess sum is E-1. Induction gives f_j m=0. Apply induction again to m with its j-th exponent replaced by one; the excess sum has strictly decreased. This gives m=0. For n=0 the hypothesis already forces M=0. The parent retains the fraction test, the immediate converse implication and the difference argument from old Step 1, and uses this lemma for old Step 2.

The second task carries the geometric argument of old Step 3 and the common-denominator/localization argument of old Steps 4--5. For X=Spec A, Stacks Tag 01HV (lemma-spec-sheaves) identifies sections and all restrictions on principal opens with localizations; the existing affine-open-section-vanishing-prime clauses (a),(b) give the same facts. D(g) intersect D(f_i) is the principal open of f_i/1 in Spec A_g: the prime-ideal test is exactly membership of f_i. Its ring is (A_g)_(f_i/1), canonically also (A_fi)_g. Both identifications send a to a/1 and invert g and f_i, so follow from the same universal property of fractions. A section vanishes on D(g) intersect U precisely when it vanishes on every D(g) intersect D(f_i), by restriction and uniqueness for that finite open cover. Write it a/g^r. Vanishing means that for every i some power g^ki annihilates the image of a in A_fi. Taking k=max_i ki gives g^k a in K. Hence a/g^r=(g^k a)/g^(r+k) belongs to the displayed set of fractions with numerator in K. Conversely a numerator in K restricts to zero on every D(f_i), hence every intersection, and sheaf uniqueness gives zero on their union. For n=0, K=A and the restriction target is the zero section ring on the empty open, so the formula still holds (take k=0).

This kernel formula contains the local-injectivity mechanism of old Step 4 in a form independent of the induction. The original alternative calculation is retained: assuming tuple multiplication injective on A, if every f_i a/g^r is zero in A_g, find ki with g^ki f_i a=0 and take their finite maximum k, zero for an empty family. The tuple annihilates g^k a, so g^k a=0 and a/g^r=0. The old application of the power induction on A_g then gives localization-tuple injectivity there. The new kernel calculation proves the same local conclusion directly, for arbitrary K, and records the actual comparison as a reusable statement. Thus no denominator argument or sheaf-section identification has been discarded or hidden in the short parent text.

The parent retains old Step 5's basis-to-all-opens assembly explicitly: restrict a section in the kernel to every principal basis member contained in its open of definition, apply the new kernel formula with K=0, and apply the sheaf uniqueness axiom. The reverse sheaf implication is evaluation at X, using the new formula with g=1. These are short direct uses of the cited statements.
