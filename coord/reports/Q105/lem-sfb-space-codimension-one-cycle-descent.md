1. Step 1 opens and uses the statements of `lem-sfb-quasiseparated-space-sober`, `lem-sfb-space-reduced-closed-support` and `lem-sfb-space-local-dimension-chart-invariance`. They respectively say that irreducible closed supports have unique generic points, that each closed support has a unique reduced closed structure, and that the ambient local dimension is independent of the chart. Thus the basis is exactly the set of codimension-one points. Quasi-separatedness follows from the item's stronger separatedness assumption; no dimension bound is needed.

2. Step 2 uses the actual two assertions of `lem-sfb-quasiseparated-space-etale-specializations`: generizations lift to a specified scheme-chart point and a specialization in one fibre must be trivial. Lifting the base generic point proves one direction, and forbidding strict generizations in a fibre proves the other. Sobriety supplies generic points; the chartwise-etale permanence statement supplies the composite chart. This is an explicit short argument, not use of the proof of the dimension node. It applies to the possibly reducible inverse image; the base T is integral and separated.

3. Step 3 checks the etale permanence, scheme reducedness, affine atlas, closed-support base-change, finite-components and chart-dimension statements. Each gives exactly what is taken from it. The scheme atlas W is Noetherian because it is an affine chart of the Noetherian space Y; W_T is closed in W, so the existing closed-subset theorem applies without any regularity assumption on T. Finitely many closures of images of its components cover P, so no extra theorem asserting finiteness of space components is hidden. Reducedness is checked on charts. The dimension calculation is in the ambient spaces Y and Z, not the dimension of T or a dimension formula involving components.

4. Step 4 is the elementary finite-sum calculation after step 3. It writes the coefficient formula explicitly, checks finite support and computes both composites at an arbitrary codimension-one point. There is no multiplicity or residue-field degree inserted: the pullback is the sum of reduced components, each occurring once.

5. Step 5 reads `lem-a1-etale-atlas-presentation` for the scheme fibre product and the surjective etale projections. Quasi-compactness of those projections is the scheme-test definition of quasi-compactness applied to the given q; regularity and local Noetherianity come from R's composite chart of X. Since U is Noetherian and the projections are quasi-compact, R is quasi-compact and locally Noetherian. Surjectivity plus the stated dimension invariance gives codimension-one lifts, which explicitly recover every coefficient. Thus injectivity is not inferred just from existence of a presentation.

6. Step 6 opens `lem-a1-space-fibre-product-point`: it states a point above the specified pair u,u', not merely a point somewhere in R. Dimension invariance makes this point codimension one. The equalizer then forces equality of the two specified coefficients. The finite image of the support is the support bound for the descended cycle, and the inverse is written out. There is no closed-subspace factorization inferred solely from containment of images.

7. Step 7 checks `def-weil-regular-scheme` for the regular local stalks, `lem-weil-pbc-regular-ring-normal` for normal domains at every prime of a regular affine, and `lem-sc-normal-dim1-dvr` for precisely a dimension-one Noetherian normal local domain. The codimension comparison uses the stated chart theorem, with identity chart on the target.

8. Step 8 uses `lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization` to identify the actual stalk map, and `lem-weil-pbc-etale-prime-generates-maximal` for p S_q = q S_q. Localizing R at p adds only units, so the generated maximal ideal is unchanged. The image of a uniformizer therefore generates the nonzero maximal ideal of the already established DVR B, exactly as `def-sc-discrete-valuation-ring` defines a uniformizer. The etale permanence node covers the open restrictions. No claim that this stalk map is itself a finitely presented ring map is used.

9. Step 9 uses `lem-weil-pbc-dvr-unit-times-uniformizer-power`, whose statement really gives u times a nonnegative power for every nonzero element, separately for numerator and denominator. It proves injectivity before passing to fraction fields and writes the integer-exponent valuation formula, including negative exponents. `lem-sfb-space-meromorphic-generic-chart-model` explicitly gives total fractions on affine charts and compatibility with further etale restrictions, which identifies the meromorphic germ with this calculation. It is used only for that clause, not for a divisor classification that it does not state.

10. Step 10 checks `lem-trf-normal-topologically-noetherian-components` for finite clopen integral normal components, after regular-normality. On each integral affine, the meromorphic chart model gives an actual fraction. A height-one prime containing its nonzero numerator or denominator is minimal over their product: any strictly smaller prime containing that nonzero product would contradict height one. The cited Noetherian closed-subset theorem gives finitely many such primes. This does not invoke the principal ideal theorem, nor assume an affine intersection is affine. The finite affine cover and then the coefficient formula prove precisely finite support and equality of cycles. Open subschemes are Noetherian here, so the local-open clause is covered as well.
