## STATEMENT-DISPUTED: inherited domain omitted

The imported statement says only that f is smooth. But the orientation it names, `thm-pur-diagonal-dualizing-object`, requires a separated finite-type morphism between finite-type schemes over the fixed algebraic closure of a finite field; the exceptional inverse image it uses has that same domain. This is a typing problem, not a reason to abandon the item. For an explicit example, glue two copies of the affine line along the punctured affine line and map the doubled-origin line to Spec k. This is smooth of pure dimension one, hence satisfies the imported words, but is not separated: its diagonal is not closed, since the two distinct origins specialize from the common punctured diagonal. The closed diagonal DNC construction named in the conclusion does not apply. Also, an arbitrary base scheme in the imported words need not be in the finite-type k-domain at all.

The corrected statement explicitly restores precisely the finite-coefficient domain inherited from the unit and named definitions: k, ell, E=Q_ell, uniformizer ell, n>=1, Lambda=Z/ell^n, finite-type X,Y and separated finite-type f. The conclusion (equality of the actual two orientation maps) is unchanged. It adds no absolute separatedness assumption on Y. It names the canonical factors of the orientation so that “diagonal orientation” means the construction used here rather than an arbitrary choice of isomorphism. The unit itself says “since f is separated” despite omitting separatedness in its opening line; that omission is the same inherited domain error. Our proof stays with its diagonal/DNC/vector-bundle route.

The imported proof also says “because e is separated and étale” without establishing separatedness. Étale alone would not imply separatedness (the doubled-origin map to the affine line is a counterexample). After restoring the relative-separatedness domain of f, Step 2 derives separatedness of e by the Cartesian diagonal square, so no extra chart assumption is introduced.
