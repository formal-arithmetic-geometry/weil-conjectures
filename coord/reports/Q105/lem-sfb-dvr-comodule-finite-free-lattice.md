I checked the new core theorem itself, not just its use. A proof for its later worker is as follows (kept out of its statement-only JSON): let q:M to M/W and f=(q tensor 1)rho. Tensor right exactness identifies W tensor C with the kernel of q tensor 1, and flatness identifies ker(f tensor 1) with ker(f) tensor C. Coassociativity implies (f tensor 1)rho(m)=(1 tensor Delta)f(m), so rho(ker f) lands in ker(f) tensor C. The counit gives ker f contained in W. Restriction is unique because tensoring the inclusion with C is injective; composing twice with that inclusion transports coassociativity, and the counit follows by composing with the original inclusion. Finally any stable U contained in W has its coaction in W tensor C, so lies in the core. This uses genuine flat kernel preservation, hence deserves a separately queued theorem; it is not marked elementary or hidden in the item's assembly. Neither a noetherian assumption nor freeness of C is needed for this core lemma.

Small cases: W=0 has zero core because rho is split by the counit; W=M has core M. For the trivial coalgebra C=R with its usual coaction on M, the core of W is W, even when W is not finite. C=0 forces M=0 by the counit identity. For the item, T empty yields W=V=0 with empty basis, and M=0 works; arbitrary repeated or zero tensor coefficients cause no issue.
