## Review by Q86

I read the item source, Stacks Tag 0BND (`theorem-fundamental-group`), in the local TeX file. It gives precisely an equivalence with finite continuous fundamental-group sets, and identifies pointed base change with restriction along the continuous fundamental-group homomorphism. The seven steps are an agent-written application of that theorem; I do not attribute the extra inverse-limit or action formulas verbatim to Stacks. There are no new-node sources to review.

For step 1 I opened `thm-galois-fundamental` and `def-galois-fibre-functor`. The latter specifies finite continuous actions and their topology; the former states the equivalence, hence full faithfulness. The kernel of the permutation action is a finite intersection of open stabilizers and is normal, so a permitted N really exists. The coset formula is well-defined exactly because N kills E. Transitivity of Pi/N determines the whole map from its value at N; no unrecorded Yoneda or point-rigidity assertion is used.

For step 2 I used the same theorem's explicitly stated compatibility with finite limits and colimits. I wrote both directions of the finite fibre bijection and checked equivariance; the inverse uses N acting trivially on E. I also opened `def-pc-fibre-product` and `def-galois-finite-coproduct`: their statements give the universal properties used to identify the graph and summand maps. Pullback is justified by the theorem's base-change clause for connected Y_N and connected T. Under restriction of group actions, finite products and coproducts retain their underlying sets, so this comparison does preserve the actual splitting; I did not simply assert that some transported isomorphism is the required one.

For step 3 I applied the theorem afresh on T, which is connected by the item's explicit hypothesis and has the geometric point t. No Noetherian assumption is present in that theorem, and none is introduced. Sections of the split finite cover become maps from the terminal singleton to a trivial finite action. This proves evaluation uniqueness for every X-map T→Y, without assuming that an arbitrary such map already descends to a finite stage. It avoids introducing a separate connected-equalizer lemma or a stronger limit-Hom theorem.

For step 4 the theorem's faithfulness identifies the two finite-stage maps by the written coset formula. For two possible N's their intersection is still open normal and kills E, so the fixed limit cone compares them. Cover naturality then follows by the just-proved evaluation uniqueness. I checked that its maps are finite-etale X-maps as in the item, and that no surjectivity on the group homomorphism is assumed.

For step 5 the cone compatibility is the finite-cover naturality of step 4; the limit universal property is part of the supplied datum T, not an existence theorem with a hidden smallness hypothesis. The only finite-stage input is the equivariant formula from the cited fundamental-group theorem. I wrote the composition calculation: p_N d_h d_g = f_{hN} d_g = f_{ghN}. This proves the requested right-action convention, not its opposite. Taking inverses proves that the constructed endomorphisms are automorphisms. The calculations also show uniqueness from all limit projections.

For step 6 the same theorem supplies natural fibre bijections under the given pointed morphism and its continuous homomorphism. I checked every coordinate of the cone and the marked point. Uniqueness is proved after pairing with the structural map to X' by the cited fibre-product universal property, then applying the primed evaluation uniqueness. This distinction matters: an arbitrary map over f is not initially a map to a finite cover over X' until it is paired into the pullback. The identical argument proves the formula for every Y, not merely the Y_N.

For step 7 the cited theorem states exactly the fibre-equivariance used, and hence tau(hr)=f_*(h)s. The four displayed composites prove equivariance before taking Y=Y_N and s=N to use limit joint uniqueness. Identities and composition are proved using the pointed uniqueness already established, with the appropriate composite base morphism.

I checked small cases explicitly. If E is empty, the action kernel is all of Pi, the finite-set bijection in step 2 is empty, and step 3 has no sections; all s-indexed formulas are vacuous. If Pi is trivial, the only stage is X and the limit is X, so the result becomes the constant finite-cover decomposition. For a nonabelian finite quotient (for example S_3), the formula aN↦as gives d_h d_g=d_{gh}, confirming the order rather than relying on commutativity. The pointed base-change argument works for homomorphisms with trivial image or nonsurjective image, since restriction of a finite action needs neither injectivity nor surjectivity.
