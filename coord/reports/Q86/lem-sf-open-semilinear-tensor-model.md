### Original step 1

Use [lem-weil-proetale-weakly-etale-localization] to identify the site of U with the slice at U of the site of X. Put O=\widehat R_X and O_U=j^*O. For module sheaves G on U and H on X, let P_G be the extension presheaf [def-weil-extension-by-zero-presheaf]. On V, define a pairing P_G(V) times H(V) into j_!(G\otimes j^*H)(V) by ([x]_f,a)\mapsto[x\otimes a|_{(V,f)}]_f and extend additively over the summands f:V\to U. Balancing holds since O_U(V,f)=O(V); the formula respects restrictions because both x and a restrict along the same arrow. The bilinear sheafification extension theorem [lem-pc-sites-module-sheafification-bilinear-extension-existence] and the sheaf tensor as sheafification of the presheaf tensor [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product], with the universal property of module sheafification [def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification], therefore give a map \psi_{G,H}:j_!G\otimes H\to j_!(G\otimes j^*H). It is natural in G and H: both composites for a pair of module maps send [x]_f\otimes a to the extension summand of the tensor of their images. Uniqueness of bilinear extension [lem-pc-sites-module-sheafification-bilinear-extension-uniqueness] makes these equal as sheaf maps.

### Original step 2

Construct \kappa:j_!(G\otimes j^*H)\to j_!G\otimes H by the module adjunction [lem-pur-site-restriction-module-adjunction-exact]: transpose G\otimes j^*H\xrightarrow{\eta_G\otimes1}j^*j_!G\otimes j^*H\cong j^*(j_!G\otimes H), using the pure-tensor restriction comparison [lem-sfb-localized-site-tensor-restriction]. Explicitly, a map \alpha:G\to j^*L is extended by summing \alpha_{(V,f)} on the summands of P_G(V) and sheafifying [def-weil-extension-by-zero-presheaf]. Conversely a map j_!G\to L restricts to each such summand; the two operations are inverse by the universal property of module sheafification [def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification]. For L=j_!G and its identity this gives \eta_G(x)=[x]_f on (V,f); hence \kappa sends [x\otimes a]_f to [x]_f\otimes a. Thus \kappa\psi fixes every [x]_f\otimes a. Such elements determine a bilinear pairing after sheafification by [lem-pc-sites-module-sheafification-bilinear-extension-uniqueness], so \kappa\psi=1. For \psi\kappa, take the adjoint and precompose with the unit on G\otimes j^*H. On x\otimes a the resulting map is [x\otimes a]_f, which is precisely that unit. Pure tensors determine a tensor map [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product], and the adjunction is bijective; consequently \psi\kappa=1. This proves invertibility with the specified generator normalization rather than choosing an unspecified isomorphism.

Taking the sum of the \psi_{Q^a,F^b} defines an isomorphism in total degree d. Naturality gives \psi_{a+1,b}(j_!d_Q\otimes1)=j_!(d_Q\otimes1)\psi_{a,b} and \psi_{a,b+1}(1\otimes d_F)=j_!(1\otimes j^*d_F)\psi_{a,b}. Add the second equality with coefficient (-1)^a. The total differential of [def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves] is exactly that sum, so these equalities on each coproduct insertion prove the chain equation. Replacing the differentials by chain-map components proves naturality. Each source summand has only two differential components, so this argument works for unbounded complexes.
