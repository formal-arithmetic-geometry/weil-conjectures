### Former step 1

On $\mathcal T$, a presheaf is determined by its value at $*$: its only restriction is the identity. A matching family on the maximal sieve consists of one element $x$ at the identity arrow, and its unique amalgamation is $x$. Thus every presheaf is a sheaf. By [def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category], a $\underline A$-module sheaf is precisely an $A$-module at $*$, and a sheaf morphism is precisely an $A$-linear map there. Define $I_A(M)(*)=M$, with identity restriction, and define $I_A(u)$ by its sole component $u$. Then $E_AI_A=1$ and the map $I_AE_A\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$ whose component is the identity is a natural isomorphism. Both inverse identities are identities at the only object. This constructs the asserted equivalence.

Cited nodes: def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category.

### Former step 2

Kernels in [def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category] are sectionwise, and cokernels are sheafified sectionwise cokernels. Step 1 makes that sheafification the identity up to its canonical isomorphism. Thus $E_A$ and $I_A$ preserve kernels, cokernels and hence images and exact sequences: exactness here is exactly ordinary module exactness. For any family $(M_i)$, the one-object sheaf with value $\bigoplus_iM_i$, with the usual inclusions, is a coproduct: a sheaf map out of it is exactly a linear map from that direct sum, hence exactly a family of linear maps from the $M_i$. Evaluation therefore also identifies arbitrary direct sums, including the empty direct sum.

Cited nodes: def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category.

### Former step 3

By [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product], $\mathcal F\otimes_{\underline A}\mathcal G$ is the sheafification of the presheaf with sole value $\mathcal F(*)\otimes_A\mathcal G(*)$. It is already a sheaf by step 1. The comparison with ordinary tensor sends the section represented by $x\otimes y$ to $x\otimes y$. For maps $u,v$, both tensor maps send this generator to $u(x)\otimes v(y)$; generation and uniqueness in [def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product] make the square commute. The inverse comparison has the same formula. These formulas also identify the unit $\underline A$ with $A$ and all iterated tensor comparisons: the two possible composites act identically on pure tensors.

Cited nodes: def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product, def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product.

### Former step 4

The equivalence in step 1 preserves zero maps, since its action on morphisms is evaluation. Apply [lem-weil-equivalence-map-homological-complex] to obtain inverse termwise equivalences on integer-indexed cochain complexes. Their differentials are exactly $E_A(d^n)$ and $I_A(d^n)$. By [def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves], a total tensor has degree $n$ equal to $\bigoplus_{p+q=n}\mathcal F^p\otimes\mathcal G^q$ and differential $d_{\mathcal F}\otimes1+(-1)^p1\otimes d_{\mathcal G}$. Steps 2 and 3 identify each summand and both differential terms with the corresponding module maps. Consequently the degreewise tensor comparison is a natural chain isomorphism of the direct-sum total tensors, even for unbounded complexes.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-equivalence-map-homological-complex, def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves.

### Former step 5

By the formula $H^n(K)=\ker(d^n)/\operatorname{im}(d^{n-1})$ in [def-weil-abstract-derived-category], step 2 gives $E_AH^n(K)\cong H^n(E_AK)$, naturally in chain maps, and the inverse functor gives the reverse identification. Thus acyclicity and quasi-isomorphisms correspond in both directions. Every acyclic module complex is $E_AI_AF$ for an acyclic sheaf complex $I_AF$. Using the total tensor comparison of step 4, the assertion that $\operatorname{Tot}(F\otimes K)$ is acyclic for every acyclic $F$ is consequently identical on the two sides. This is exactly K-flatness in [def-pc-kflat-complex] and [def-pc-module-kflat-complex]; the same observation identifies K-flat resolutions.

Cited nodes: def-weil-abstract-derived-category, def-pc-kflat-complex, def-pc-module-kflat-complex.

### Former step 6

In the homotopy category, cochain maps $u,v:K\to L$ are identified when there are maps $h^n:K^n\to L^{n-1}$ satisfying $u^n-v^n=d_L^{n-1}h^n+h^{n+1}d_K^n$. Evaluation and its inverse preserve this equality termwise, including its sums and compositions. They therefore give inverse functors $e_A:K(\underline A)\rightleftarrows K(A):i_A$ with the same termwise unit and counit. This proves the homotopy-category equivalence. The shift and the cone matrices in [def-weil-abstract-derived-category] are also preserved termwise, because both functors preserve sums, negatives and the differentials.

Cited nodes: def-weil-abstract-derived-category.

### Former step 7

Write $Q_s:K(\underline A)\to D(\underline A)$ and $Q_m:K(A)\to D(A)$ for localization. By step 5, $Q_me_A$ and $Q_si_A$ invert quasi-isomorphisms. Parts (1) and (2) of [lem-weil-pbc-localization-universal-property] give $d_A:D(\underline A)\to D(A)$ and $j_A:D(A)\to D(\underline A)$ with $d_AQ_s\cong Q_me_A$ and $j_AQ_m\cong Q_si_A$. They also lift the unit and counit of $e_A\dashv i_A$ to $1\cong j_Ad_A$ and $d_Aj_A\cong1$. Their inverse and triangle equations hold after precomposition with $Q_s$ or $Q_m$, since there they are the equations in step 6; part (3) makes them hold on the derived categories. Hence $d_A,j_A$ are the desired inverse derived equivalences, with the stated localization comparisons.

Cited nodes: def-weil-abstract-derived-category, lem-weil-pbc-localization-universal-property.

### Former step 8

Fix $f:A\to A\prime$. Denote termwise sheaf change of rings by $T_s$ and module extension of scalars by $T_m$. Steps 1 and 3 identify the value of $T_s\mathcal F$ with $\mathcal F(*)\otimes_A A\prime$. The $A\prime$-action sends $x\otimes b$ to $x\otimes ab$, and the map induced by $u$ sends $x\otimes b$ to $u(x)\otimes b$. These are precisely the object, scalar-action and morphism formulas of [def-weil-module-base-change]. Thus the identity on these tensors gives $e_{A\prime}T_s\cong T_me_A$ on homotopy categories; differential compatibility is the same termwise formula. Here $T_s$ is the sheaf change of rings of [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product].

Cited nodes: def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product, def-weil-module-base-change.

### Former step 9

Apply [lem-pur-left-derived-from-kflat-class] on $\mathcal T$ with $\mathcal A=\operatorname{Mod}(\underline A)$, the functor $F_s=Q_s'T_s:K(\underline A)\to D(\underline{A'})$ and the class $\mathcal P$ of K-flat sheaf complexes [def-pc-kflat-complex]; primes denote the target ring, and likewise $F_m=Q_m'T_m$. ### Former step 10

### Former step 11

To check universality, let $H:D(A)\to D(A\prime)$ be any functor. The inverse equivalences in step 7 and their units/counits give bijections $\operatorname{Nat}(H,L_m)\cong\operatorname{Nat}(j_{A\prime}Hd_A,L_s)$ and $\operatorname{Nat}(j_{A\prime}Hd_AQ_s,F_s)\cong\operatorname{Nat}(HQ_m,F_m)$: apply the inverse functors to components and insert the unit/counit isomorphisms, reversing with the forward functors. These operations are inverse by the two triangle identities; for the second bijection also substitute $d_AQ_s\cong Q_me_A$ and $d_{A\prime}F_s\cong F_me_A$, then precompose with $i_A$. By the formula of step 10, the resulting composite sends $\alpha:H\to L_m$ precisely to $\gamma\circ(\alpha Q_m)$. This proves the required bijection for every $H$, hence $(L_m,\gamma)$ is a universal total left derived pair for ordinary scalar extension.

Cited nodes: lem-pur-left-derived-from-kflat-class.

### Former step 12

Let $q:P\to M$ be any ordinary K-flat resolution. Step 5 makes $i_Aq$ a K-flat sheaf resolution. By [lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings] and the K-flat counit clause of [lem-pur-left-derived-from-kflat-class], $\gamma_P$ is invertible, and so is $L_m(Q_mq)$. The resolution computation is therefore $L_m(Q_mM)\xrightarrow{L_m(Q_mq)^{-1}}L_m(Q_mP)\xrightarrow{\gamma_P}Q_m\prime(T_mP)$. This is exactly evaluation of the sheaf resolution computation. Naturality gives $\gamma_M=F_m(q)\circ\gamma_P\circ L_m(Q_mq)^{-1}$, so its derived-to-ordinary comparison is the same localized tensor-augmentation map. Comparisons between two resolutions are the composites of these isomorphisms, hence their cocycle identities are preserved too. All constructions above use the full unbounded categories and make no flatness assumption.

Cited nodes: lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings, lem-pur-left-derived-from-kflat-class.
