### Residue algebra task: old Step 1

By [def-weil-pbc-essentially-finite-type-algebra], choose a finite type $A$-algebra $C$, a multiplicative subset $W\subseteq C$, and identify $B=W^{-1}C$ as $A$-algebras. Put $k=A/\mathfrak m_A$, $D=C/\mathfrak m_A C$, and $R=B/\mathfrak m_A B$. The quotient $k$ is a field [def-weil-field]: if $a\notin\mathfrak m_A$, maximality gives $ab+t=1$ with $t\in\mathfrak m_A$, so the class of $b$ inverts that of $a$. Every ideal of $k$ is either zero or contains a nonzero invertible element and hence equals $k$; it is therefore generated by the empty set or by $1$. Thus $k$ is Noetherian by [def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring]. Choose algebra generators $c_1,\ldots,c_n$ of $C$ over $A$ as in [def-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra]. Their images generate $D$ over $k$, since reducing a polynomial expression in the $c_i$ reduces each coefficient modulo $\mathfrak m_A$.

The choice of C and W remains in the parent's Step 1; the residue-field, Noetherianity-of-k, and reduction-of-generators arguments belong to the new residue-algebra task. Its statement includes the precise quotient scalar map and polynomial coefficient reduction, so it does not hide these identifications behind abstract finite type.

### Quotient-localization task: old Step 2

Let $\overline W$ be the image of $W$ in $D$. The map $C\to B\to R$ kills $\mathfrak m_A C$, so induces $\phi:D\to R$. Verify the three localization conditions of [lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian] directly using the fractions of [def-weil-pbc-essentially-finite-type-algebra]. (a) For $w\in W$, $\phi(\bar w)$ has inverse the class of $1/w$. (b) Every element of $R$ is the class of some $c/w$, hence equals $\phi(\bar c)\phi(\bar w)^{-1}$. (c) If $\phi(\bar c)=0$, write $c/1=\sum_{i=1}^r a_i d_i/w_i$ in $B$, with $a_i\in\mathfrak m_A$, $d_i\in C$, $w_i\in W$. Put $w=\prod_iw_i$ and $e=\sum_i a_i d_i\prod_{j\ne i}w_j\in\mathfrak m_A C$. Equality of fractions $c/1=e/w$ gives $u(wc-e)=0$ for some $u\in W$. Consequently $(uw)c=ue\in\mathfrak m_A C$, so $\overline{uw}\,\bar c=0$ in $D$ with $\overline{uw}\in\overline W$. This verifies (c) without any finiteness assumption on $\mathfrak m_A$.

The new statement includes all three localization conditions and the full product-of-denominators certificate. In particular it retains the extra annihilator u from equality of fractions. It does not assume injection C to B, does not cancel a denominator in C and does not assume the base ideal is finite.

### Arguments retained in the parent

Old Step 3: The field $k$ is Noetherian and $D$ is a finite type $k$-algebra by Step 1. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian] with base $k$ and algebra $D$ to conclude that $D$ is Noetherian. Step 2 gives a localization map $D\to R$ at $\overline W$, including its kernel condition. Applying [lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian] now shows that $R=B/\mathfrak m_A B$ is Noetherian. No Noetherianity of $A$ or $B$ is used.

Old Step 4: Let $N$ be a finite $B$-module and choose generators $n_1,\ldots,n_s$ using [def-weil-finitely-generated-module]. Multiplication induces an $R$-module structure on $Q=N/\mathfrak m_A N$: every element of $\mathfrak m_A B$ is a finite sum $\sum_i a_i b_i$ with $a_i\in\mathfrak m_A$, and $(\sum_i a_i b_i)n=\sum_i a_i(b_i n)$ lies in $\mathfrak m_A N$, so the action is independent of the representative in $B$. Changing the representative in $N$ also leaves the action unchanged, since $\mathfrak m_A N$ is a $B$-submodule. Reducing $n=\sum_j b_j n_j$ gives $\bar n=\sum_j\bar b_j\bar n_j$; hence the $\bar n_j$ generate $Q$ over $R$. This includes $s=0$ and $N=0$.

Old Step 5: By Step 3, $R$ is Noetherian, and by Step 4, $Q$ is a finite $R$-module. Thus [lem-sc-noetherian-finite-module-finite-presentation], applied to $R$ and $Q$, shows that $N/\mathfrak m_A N$ is finitely presented over $B/\mathfrak m_A B$, which proves the second assertion for every finite $N$.
