### Original step 1 — lem-pur-point-tate-root-coordinates

Choose compatible primitive roots $\zeta_r\in\mu_{\ell^r}(k)$, $\zeta_{r+1}^{\ell}=\zeta_r$: the cyclicity and orders in [lem-pur-frobenius-roots-of-unity-power] give a primitive $\zeta_1$, and an $\ell$-th root of $\zeta_r$ exists in the algebraically closed field $k$ and has order $\ell^{r+1}$. These choices trivialize the sheaves [def-pur-roots-of-unity-sheaf] on $s$, not just their groups of global sections. Indeed, for a section $u$ on any $U\to s$, the factorization $X^{\ell^r}-1=\prod_{j=0}^{\ell^r-1}(X-\zeta_r^j)$ has pairwise unit differences. The Lagrange expressions $e_j=\prod_{i\ne j}(u-\zeta_r^i)/(\zeta_r^j-\zeta_r^i)$ satisfy $e_je_i=0$ for $i\ne j$, $e_j^2=e_j$, $\sum e_j=1$, and $(u-\zeta_r^j)e_j=0$; these identities follow by reducing polynomial identities modulo $X^{\ell^r}-1$. The clopen pieces $D(e_j)$ therefore cover $U$ and on them $u=\zeta_r^j$. The unique label $j$ gives the inverse of $(\mathbf Z/\ell^r)_s\to\mu_{\ell^r,s}$, $j\mapsto\zeta_r^j$, as sheaves. The transitions are reduction of labels since $(\zeta_{r+1}^j)^\ell=\zeta_r^j$. Taking the sectionwise inverse limit [def-pur-sheaf-inverse-limit] gives $\Zl(1)_s\cong\widehat{\Zl}_s$, with generator $e=(\zeta_r)_r$.
