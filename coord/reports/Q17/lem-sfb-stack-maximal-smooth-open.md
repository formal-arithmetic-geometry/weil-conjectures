## Review by Q17

I read the item against 0DZR existence and clause (3). Its target is a scheme, a valid special case of the source's algebraic-stack target. The finite-presentation assumption belongs only to the base-change clause; the change of base is flat but need not be surjective or locally finitely presented. Both new nodes have independently quantified algebraic spaces and inline definitions of open subspaces, chartwise properties, and (in the pointwise lemma) field-valued points and smoothness at a point. Their sources and statements agree: the first is 0DZI existence and clause (2), with no flatness on h; the second is the pointwise form of 06F2. For the reverse implication of the latter, take a smooth neighbourhood N of z for hq; q(N) is open since q is smooth, and N to q(N) is smooth surjective. Smooth-source descent gives smoothness of h on q(N). Forward implication is smooth composition after restricting to the given smooth neighbourhood of q(z). This proof explanation is kept here, not smuggled into a statement-only node.

Step 1 cites the invariant-open descent statement for the presentation R, chartwise calculus for base change of the atlas, and the new arbitrary-map space locus for W. I opened all three statements: the first explicitly introduces P times_X P and its projections, the second covers representable smooth maps and their base changes, and the third gives existence without a finite-presentation assumption. The atlas is representable by algebraic spaces because the stack has representable diagonal. No smoothness of f was assumed.

Step 2 cites the pointwise lemma for each smooth projection and the invariant-open descent theorem for the resulting substack. The two pullbacks of W coincide because h s and h t are the same map to the scheme S, not because the two projections agree. Membership in W is exactly smoothness at the point: a smooth neighbourhood is contained in W by maximality, and W itself is a smooth neighbourhood. The descent statement gives precisely the invariant-open correspondence and the equality of the atlas pullback with W. There is no appeal to a construction hidden in its proof.

Step 3 uses chartwise calculus to restrict the atlas and preserve surjectivity, and the inclusion-preserving descent correspondence to establish largestness. The item itself defines smoothness to be tested after smooth source atlases, so the smooth composite W to S establishes smoothness of U to S. The same test on any other smooth open V makes its atlas pullback an open where h is smooth, hence contained in W. This proves largestness, rather than moving it into a new substack lemma.

Step 4 uses chartwise calculus for the pulled-back smooth surjective atlas, the new space locus's flat-base-change clause for h, and injectivity of the invariant-open correspondence for equality on the new stack. Local finite presentation of h is exactly the chart test in the item for local finite presentation of f. I checked algebraicity of the new stack using descent of triples, the pullback of the diagonal, and the pulled-back atlas; no representability of f is asserted. The open equality follows from comparing both pullbacks along the SAME new atlas. The routine associativity here is an equivalence of the componentwise triples, not an unproved geometric comparison theorem.

I checked small cases. Empty stacks, empty atlases and empty base changes give the empty open; the identity change of base returns W. A smooth f gives the whole stack. A nonflat example Spec k to Spec k[epsilon]/(epsilon squared) has empty smooth locus; this illustrates why the flat-f input cannot replace 0DZI and is covered by the general node. A non-finitely-presented map still has an open obtained from its smooth neighbourhoods; finite presentation is not needed for existence. For the pointwise lemma, an open immersion q and a smooth projection give the expected equivalence; an empty q makes the pointwise assertion vacuous and causes no claim outside its image. The proof only descends over q(N), so surjectivity of q is never added. Flat base change is not replaced by arbitrary base change: the nonflat quotient Spec k to Spec k[epsilon]/(epsilon squared) can make the displayed nonflat closed immersion smooth after base change, so that stronger assertion would be false.
