The definition allows finite covers by finitely generated algebras over the single affine base Spec K. The open-product lemma gives exactly the intersections of inverse images of the two chosen affine opens; all structure maps land in the whole base. The affine-product lemma identifies each such open with the spectrum of its tensor algebra, including the two projection maps. The finite number of pairs gives a finite cover. The written polynomial/pure-tensor expansion proves finite generation directly, so no unquoted theorem about stability of finite type is being used. This supplies the hypothesis needed later for P.

For step 2 I read clause (a) of `lem-kun-closed-point-field`, which says closed iff the residue field is finite over K, and the entire `def-weil-residue-field`, which explicitly gives the residue-field maps induced by scheme morphisms. The first application is to P, whose finite type was proved in step 1; the last applications are to X and Y, which are finite type by hypothesis. The maps are over K because both projections are K-morphisms. Their injectivity is justified by the kernel-ideal argument, and finite dimension of their images is the elementary subspace argument. No finite-generation hypothesis on those subfields is needed.

For step 3 I again checked that clause (a) of `lem-kun-closed-point-field` applies to the closed x and y in the given finite-type schemes. Both extensions are finite dimensional over the same K. The explicit expansion in the two finite bases shows that the pure tensor basis products span A; this needs no separability or product decomposition. It remains valid for inseparable extensions, where A may have nilpotents.

For step 4 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-point-residue-field` and checked that its statement, rather than its proof, says that the kernel is prime, the residue field is the fraction field of the tensor quotient, and the induced identification with kappa(z) respects both factor field maps. This is exactly what this step takes. I also read `def-weil-affine-spectrum`, which says Spec K has one point with residue field K. Thus the general theorem's tensor over kappa(s) is the tensor over K used here. Compatibility with K follows from compatibility with the factor maps. This repairs the original proof's missing non-elementary citation; citing just the existing points-bijection node would not have sufficed.

Step 5 has no black-box citation: quotient images of a finite spanning set span the quotient; primeness makes it a nonzero domain; multiplication by a nonzero element is an injective linear endomorphism and therefore surjective in finite dimension, producing its inverse. This writes out why the residue field is finite, without using the structure theorem for Artinian rings. Step 6 uses exactly clause (a) of `lem-kun-closed-point-field` for P with step 1's hypothesis and step 5's conclusion. Every node cited in all six steps has its full statement copied verbatim into `citations.json`.
