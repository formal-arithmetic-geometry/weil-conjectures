## Preserved argument for immersion normalization

Normalize the immersion factors first. For an open $w$ equal to $s$ or $v$, the compactification $(w,\mathrm{id})$ in [def-amb-etale-compact-support] computes $Rw_!=Dw_!$: direct image by the identity is the identity, including on a K-injective representative. For $i$, which is proper by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-proper], use $(\mathrm{id},i)$ to compute $Ri_!=Ri_*$. The closed immersion is finite by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-finite], so [lemma-weil-pbc-finite-direct-image-exact] gives exactness and $Ri_*H=i_*H$ for bounded-below $H$. Although that node is stated for abelian sheaves, it applies to the underlying abelian sheaves of a short exact sequence of $A$-module sheaves: direct image is the same section functor with its $A$-action, and kernels and cokernels have the underlying abelian kernels and cokernels. Thus $i_*$ is exact also with these coefficients. Exactness gives $\mathcal H^q(i_*H)=i_*\mathcal H^q(H)$ and, by [lem-weil-pbc-extension-by-zero-exact], $\mathcal H^q(s_!H)=s_!\mathcal H^q(H)$, so both inputs remain bounded below. The natural comparisons of [lem-amb-etale-compact-comparisons] transport these computations to the chosen compactifications; their domains are bounded-below inputs, which is all that is used here.

## Preserved argument for the chart compositor

Write $\alpha_{b,a}:Rb_!Ra_!\xrightarrow{\sim}R(ba)_!$ for the natural compositor of [lem-amb-etale-compact-comparisons]. Apply it to $(s,f)$, $(i,p)$ and $(pi,v)$. All these morphisms and composites have exactly the compactifiability, separated finite-type, Noetherian and finite-ring hypotheses required by that node. Starting with the bounded-below $H$, its values and the intermediate compact-support values are bounded below, since that node works on $D^+$. After the normalizations in step 1, the equality $fs=vpi$ gives the isomorphism $\gamma_H:Rf_!s_!H\xrightarrow{\sim}v_!Rp_!i_*H$, namely $\gamma_H=(\alpha_{v,pi,H}\circ v_!(\alpha_{p,i,H}))^{-1}\circ\alpha_{f,s,H}$ with transport along the displayed equality understood. Precomposition by $\gamma_H^{-1}$ gives $\operatorname{Hom}(Rf_!s_!H,K)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(v_!Rp_!i_*H,K)$. Its inverse is precomposition by $\gamma_H$. This uses composition only on bounded-below inputs; precomposition makes sense for every target $K$.
