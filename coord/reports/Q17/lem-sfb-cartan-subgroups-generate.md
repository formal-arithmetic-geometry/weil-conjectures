## Review by Q17

I read Milne 17.91 (p.381), 17.83 and 17.82 (p.379), 17.76–17.80 (pp.377–378), 17.90 (p.381), and the generated-subgroup construction of 2.46–2.53 (pp.54–56). I compared the unchanged item hypothesis by hypothesis: infinite field, smooth geometrically connected affine finite-type G; no reductivity or perfectness; closed subgroup containment on all algebras rather than merely on rational points. Milne's algebraic-subgroup convention here is scheme-theoretic. The maximal tori in the assertion are defined over k, not split tori.

For the envelope helper I read 2.51 and 2.53: the first gives smoothness for arbitrary families; geometric connectedness follows after base change by connected finite words through the identity. For finitely many members, their product and reversed product give a connected pointed inversion-stable source, so the existing single-source construction applies. For arbitrary families choose a finite-family envelope of largest dimension; adjoining any member cannot increase dimension and hence gives the same smooth connected group. This explains the family version without consulting the external reference mentioned in Milne. Its statement does not include this proof sketch.

For the tangent-span helper I read 17.76–17.79. The statement includes the exact tangent spaces, smoothness, geometric connectedness and strict dimension drop needed in step 4, not only the existence of one noncentral element. Over an imperfect field a Jordan semisimple part need not be rational. In characteristic p, for a rational Y in the nonempty open where ad(Y) has a nonzero eigenvalue, a sufficiently large restricted p-power X=Y^[p^N] is rational semisimple, noncentral, and commutes with Y. Thus the rational points of that open lie in the union of the centralizer subspaces; polynomial density over an infinite field implies they span. In characteristic zero the field is perfect and the usual Jordan part suffices. This is the missing rationality detail in the source's terse proof.

For the maximal-tori helper I read the proof of 17.90. It concerns semisimple tangent elements (not merely semisimple group points). Centralizers are smooth; tori in C_G(X) lie in its identity component. The proof places X in the Lie algebra of its Cartan centralizer, then in Lie(T), by its unipotent quotient. A larger ambient torus therefore centralizes X and would lie in the same identity component, contrary to maximality. X=0 reduces to the tautological case K=G. No hypothesis of a perfect base is added.

For the radicial helper I read the torus-image assertion of 17.20, field extension 17.82, torus-centralizer smoothness 17.72, and faithful-flat image 3.34. This is explicitly an agent formulation, not a quotation of an absent Milne theorem. Over an algebraic closure, a finite radicial surjection is bijective on geometric points. If f(g) centralizes T', then for every t in T the images of gtg^-1 and t agree, so radicial injectivity makes them equal on geometric points. The resulting morphisms from the reduced torus agree scheme-theoretically by density of geometric points. Thus g centralizes T. Smoothness of both torus centralizers and the faithful-flat image theorem upgrade this onto-point statement to the faithfully flat comparison of schemes; finiteness and radiciality are inherited from f. Maximality is checked over the algebraic closure using images of maximal tori under quotient maps, then descended by 17.82. The conclusion asserts faithful flatness, not surjectivity on rational points over k.
