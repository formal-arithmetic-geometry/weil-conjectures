I checked the following statements and hypotheses, step by step.

- Step 1 uses the Noetherian-localization and normal-domain-localization nodes exactly for the two properties of D at its primes. The dimension-one normal-local criterion says precisely that these local rings are regular; I apply it only at nonzero primes, having explicitly computed dimension one. At the zero prime the field case is elementary. The regular-ring definition then gives D regular. This supplies the base regularity rather than silently assuming it from the word Dedekind.
- Step 2 uses affine quasi-compactness and the locally Noetherian definition for S, and finite-type-over-Noetherian for X. The separated-over-affine statement gives absolute separatedness. Affine-opens-basis and smooth restriction supply the charts for the smooth-ring equivalence; regularity ascends along precisely those smooth ring maps by 07NF. Regular-ring-normal gives the normal local rings. The normal topologically Noetherian component theorem explicitly gives a finite disjoint open-and-closed decomposition into integral schemes, so the later prime-divisor theorem is applied on an integral component, not on possibly disconnected X.
- Step 3 uses smooth-flat and the definition of a flat morphism to get the flat local stalk map. I corrected the initial draft here: I apply the Noetherian flat dimension formula directly to D_t -> O_(X,x), avoiding an unstated comparison between flat scheme charts and flat ring maps. Both stalk rings are Noetherian by Steps 1--2. The fibre-stalk-quotient node gives exactly the quotient occurring in that formula. The generalization-chain statement gives dimension zero at fibre generic points and at total-space generic points. This proves the precise dimensions needed by 09NN, without assuming a fixed relative dimension.
- Step 4 uses finite irreducible components of a closed subset, and the scheme-fibre definition explicitly identifies the topology of the fibre with the inverse-image subspace. Sobriety gives generic points. Noetherianity gives finitely many components on every quasi-compact open. All hypotheses of the existing 09NN node are therefore met: separatedness, that finiteness condition, and Noetherian local rings of dimension at most one. Its conclusion is one affine open containing the entire finite set, not merely individual affine neighbourhoods.
- Step 5 uses finite boundary components and sobriety, then the dense-affine 0BCV node for local dimension exactly one. U_s is dense because it contains all total-space generic points. A vertical boundary component cannot lie over the selected s: the established dimension formula would make its generic point a fibre generic point, already in U_s. - Step 6 uses the basic-open basis of Spec D and the affine principal-open/localization statement. Removing vertical supports, taking principal restrictions, and retaining the horizontal generic points are set-theoretic steps written explicitly; no new existence lemma is concealed here.
- Step 7 is the finite-support construction. Every horizontal closure equals the original closed horizontal boundary component before restriction: the restriction contains its generic point and is dense in that irreducible component. Hence its generic local dimension stays one. The finite union H contains each local boundary by construction. This step introduces the actual object used later instead of taking unspecified data from an existence theorem.
- Step 8 uses only the already-established dimensions and the cited generalization-chain formula. If a horizontal H_a contained a closed-fibre component, its generic point z would admit the strict chain z, xi_a, zeta of length two, contradicting its dimension one. Over eta, the fibre generic points have total dimension zero, likewise excluding membership in H_a. Because H is finite, containing a fibre component would force one H_a to contain it. This proves density in every residue-field fibre even when fibres have different dimensions, and does not assume geometric irreducibility.

The regular-prime-divisor statement requires an integral regular locally Noetherian ambient scheme and dimension one at the generic point: these hold on the open and closed component C from Step 2 and by Step 7. I use only its effective-Cartier-ideal clause, extending by the unit ideal to the other components. Its DVR and uniformizer clauses are unnecessary.

The common-principal-neighbourhood node and the affine localization statement justify restricting the finitely many local generators to one affine neighbourhood. The non-zero-divisor localization calculation and product calculation are written out. Thus the product ideal meets the definition of an effective Cartier ideal; an unsupported 'sum of divisors' assertion is not used.
