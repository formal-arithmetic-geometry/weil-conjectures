Step 2 completes a structural induction: addition takes a union of finite row supports, composition a finite union of finite supports. Step 3 proves equality transport by testing each monomial, giving equality of each individual matrix entry, and then using the finite rows. This is necessary because intertwining only on sigma_*H would not suffice for arbitrary S inputs. Value equalities follow from intertwining; inverse equations are checked entrywise in both orders. Step 4 interprets full-series partial tests as vanishing of every selected scalar coefficient. Their union D may be infinite. Image containment gives the zero rows on D; fixing the kernel gives identity columns at every allowed monomial. Those are exactly the entries needed to fix an arbitrary series supported outside D after transport. The argument makes no flat-kernel base-change assertion. In particular the tests vanish as full complementary-variable series, as required by the node.

Step 5 is an explicit formal-family calculation, citing only the power-series definition. The finite-variable degree-n divisors express a tail as an element of I^n, so the coefficient topology is the I-adic topology. The inverse of 1-bh is the displayed geometric series, well-defined since h has zero constant coefficient. The maximal-ideal argument then proves I is contained in the Jacobson radical. This does not wrongly claim that an arbitrary proper ideal of an arbitrary ring lies in that radical.

Step 6 cites lem-weil-pbc-power-series-noetherian for exactly the Noetherianity of B=S[[T]], then lem-weil-pbc-krull-intersection-jacobson for exactly the vanishing of the intersection of I^n(B/J). Its module is cyclic over B and hence finite. The identity I^n(B/J)=(I^n+J)/J is just the definition of the quotient action. Thus intersecting J+I^n gives J, which is the required closedness consequence; it is not falsely ascribed to the statement of the Krull node. This step is marked non-elementary because it invokes those two theorems.

Step 7 uses the coefficient-family definition again to transport each finite ideal-membership witness for Q(T^nu). S-linearity then handles polynomial inputs with coefficients that need not lift to R. The truncations converge, and finite rows show that their images converge; the preceding closedness applies to the fixed ideal generated by the transported f_j. No uniform degree bound on all rows is assumed, and no infinite sum of ideal-membership witnesses is asserted to converge. This is a short assembly of earlier steps.
