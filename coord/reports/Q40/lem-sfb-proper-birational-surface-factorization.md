## Review by Q40

I read the item against the full statement and proof of Tag 0C5R in `resolve.tex` (label `lemma-proper-birational-regular-surfaces`, lines 4891 onward). I also read Tag 0BFP in `varieties.tex` (label `lemma-modification-normal-iso-over-codimension-1`, all four clauses), point-blowup Tags 0AGQ/0AGR, rational-map elimination Tag 0C5H, the conormal diagram in `morphisms.tex` Tag 067L (`lemma-two-immersions`), and the unramified criterion Tag 02GF and quasi-finite implication 02V5. These sources justify the two unsplit subclaims as follows; the actual proof steps of those nodes remain empty, as required for one-level splitting.

For the count statement, the regular target is normal, its codimension-one local rings are DVRs, and 0BFP puts all codimension-zero/one points in the isomorphism open. A closed complement in a Noetherian surface containing only codimension-two points is a finite set of closed points. Every contracted curve lies in one of those finitely many fibres; a fibre is a proper closed subset of the integral two-dimensional source, hence has dimension at most one and finitely many irreducible components. Its one-dimensional irreducible subsets are components. With no such components the fibre has finitely many points, so 0BFP(3) forces the base case. If f=b g, every curve contracted by g is contracted by f. At the generic point of the exceptional curve of b, 0BFP makes g an isomorphism. Closing its inverse image gives a curve of X mapped onto that exceptional curve, hence contracted by f but not by g. This proves the strict inclusion; it uses no finite normalization, excellence or resolution theorem. I checked the analogous first paragraphs of 0BBT only to compare the existing excellent nodes, not to import excellence into this assertion.

For the rigidity statement I checked the hypothesis that the target centre has local dimension two. A source centre of local dimension one gives a Cartier-point blowup and a one-point exceptional fibre, so the nonconstant hypothesis excludes that case automatically. In the remaining case 0AGQ gives both exceptional fibres as projective lines, with conormal bundles of degree +1. The ambient map is an isomorphism at the generic point of the target exceptional curve by 0BFP. Its restriction is therefore a proper birational map to a regular projective line and is an isomorphism; the field identification can be read on the global sections of these projective lines, so no algebraic closedness is assumed. The canonical map on conormals is nonzero at the generic point and is a map of degree-one line bundles on the same projective line, hence is everywhere an isomorphism. The exact conormal/differential sequence 067L and Nakayama imply vanishing of the ambient relative differentials along the source exceptional fibre; 02GF and 02V5 give quasi-finiteness there. Clause (4) of 0BFP gives the isomorphism open containing the entire target fibre. The restriction isomorphism then identifies its full preimage with the source exceptional fibre. This is exactly the nonconstant subargument of 0C5R, now with its assumptions recorded. It is not the factorization theorem itself.

Step 1 cites the count node for finiteness, the zero case and the existence/location of a bad point; these assertions are literally present in that statement. It explicitly quantifies the induction over all surface morphisms, so changing the target in step 7 is permitted. Step 2 cites the sfb global point-blowup geometry for regularity, integrality, Noetherianity, properness, the isomorphism off the centre and the local dimension at closed exceptional points. The dimension conclusion follows by chains: all closed stalks have dimension at most two and a closed stalk above y has dimension two. The latter point exists by closedness and dominance of the proper blowup. No excellent-only geometry node is used.

Step 3 uses the exact rational-map resolution statement: arbitrary base Y, integral regular Noetherian two-dimensional source X, proper target Y', dense open U and centres outside U. It supplies regularity of every intermediate surface, not merely the final surface. The point-blowup properness node and proper-composition statement justify partial composites, which are not being extracted from the proof of the resolution node. The proper-to-separated-target statement applies to the triangle X_n -> Y' -> Y; b is separated because it is proper. Surjectivity is the elementary closed-and-dense-image argument, and birationality follows by the same generic identification. Choosing n minimal is legitimate in the nonempty set of natural-number lengths supplied by resolution.

Step 4 uses only the new rigidity statement, whose two blowup bases, proper birational map and target-centre dimension have all been checked. The fibre morphism is scheme-theoretic: x maps to y and the equation b h=f p_n factors its restriction through the residue-field fibre F. No factorization is inferred merely from topological containment. The rigidity conclusion is exactly the full inverse-image equality needed. The two subsequent singleton-fibre deductions use surjectivity of the two source projections, established in step 3. They do not assume an intermediate composite is an isomorphism at y.

Step 5 opens and uses 0BFP clause (3), not its codimension-one clause: the singleton underlying fibre suffices. The regular-normal node supplies normality of the target local ring, and integrality of X supplies reducedness. The dense-open definition of birationality implies the generic-local-ring definition in the cited normal-modification statement. This corrects the ambiguous final point-name in the source.

Uniqueness applies also with target Y to the two composites b h_{n-1} and f p_{n-1}; both compose with a to f p_n, so the descended map is over Y. Thus minimality really reduces n and yields the lift on X itself. Step 7 checks properness again in the triangle X -> Y' -> Y, birationality on the common open, and all surface hypotheses before using the strict-count clause; the strong induction hypothesis then applies to g. This finishes the stated factorization, rather than merely producing a dominating modification.
