Step 2 uses the representable diagonal clause of `def-trf-algebraic-space` to get a scheme equalizer. `lem-pc-yoneda` states the actual natural bijection Hom(h_T,B)=B(T), so it justifies identifying its points and morphisms, with compatibility. Step 3 uses the atlas in the algebraic-space definition, the full etale base-change and scheme-representability statement of `lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers`, and the disjoint-union-of-finite-separable-fields conclusion of `lem-weil-pbc-etale-fibre-discrete-separable-points`. Step 4 checks each algebraic hypothesis: maximal-ideal completeness is precisely the input to `lem-weil-pbc-complete-pair-henselian`; its conclusion is a henselian pair and hence a henselian local ring. `lem-weil-pbc-henselian-finite-etale-field-lift` allows the chosen finite separable k'/k and supplies a finite etale algebra with that exact closed fibre. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-over-complete-local-product` requires Noetherian completeness and finiteness as a module, which hold. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-local-radical` says every maximal ideal contains mA'. The fact that the quotient is the nonzero field k' then forces a single maximal ideal and a single nonzero complete local factor, with maximal ideal exactly mA'. This justifies using maximal-ideal powers in step 5.

Step 5 uses precisely the affine square-zero lifting assertion of `lem-sfb-etale-space-infinitesimal-lifting`, not the converse criterion in the similarly named lfp/formally-etale node. I checked that the ideal m^nA'/m^(n+1)A' squares to zero for every n >= 1, and that the two maps in each lifting square commute by induction and compatibility of b_n. `def-sbc-first-order-thickening` defines exactly this hypothesis. The induction starts at the selected k'-point and the unique lifts form a compatible family.

Step 6 cites the already proved calculation of step 1 on the scheme U and the ring A' whose completeness and locality step 4 established. The equality with b'_n follows from the commuting square of step 5; no injectivity for maps into B is presumed to algebraize U.

Step 7 verifies the overlap, rather than asserting descent compatibility. The etale base-change and composition nodes really give etaleness of Spec(A' tensor_A A') over Spec A; finiteness follows directly from tensor products of finite generating sets. The finite-product spectrum node identifies the disjoint scheme pieces, and the sheaf uniqueness condition glues the equalities.
