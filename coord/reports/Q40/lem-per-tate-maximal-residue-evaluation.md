## Argument reserved for the coordinate lemma

This is explanatory report material, not a proof inside the unsplit node. Normalize f by a coefficient of maximal absolute value. Its coefficients then have norm at most one. The finite set of norm-one coefficients is nonempty; all other coefficients have a uniform bound rho<1, by splitting off the finite set of coefficients of norm at least 1/2. Choose positive triangular weights so the weighted degrees nu_r+sum N_i nu_i are distinct on that finite set. Such weights can be chosen by taking successive powers of an integer larger than every coordinate in the finite set. The substitutions x_i -> x_i+x_r^{N_i} and their minus-sign inverses are norm-nonincreasing on polynomials; completeness and polynomial density extend them to mutually inverse isometric maps on restricted series. The norm-one monomial of largest weighted degree yields the unique top x_r term c*x_r^d; every other contribution from these finitely many monomials has smaller x_r-degree. All contributions from the remaining tail are bounded by rho. Thus the x_r^d coefficient is c plus a coefficient-series of norm at most rho, hence a unit by geometric inversion; coefficients above d have norm at most rho. Multiply by its inverse to get a_d=1, lower coefficient norms at most one and strict upper-tail bound. This includes constants, d=0, r=1, and nondiscrete valuations. This normalization argument is a real reusable comparison lemma, not a two-line elementary step or a clause of the item.
