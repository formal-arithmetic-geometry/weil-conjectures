### Original step 1

Put $A_Z=p_Z^*\underline\Omega$, with the finite-subextension-valued convention of [sk-def-sfb-qlbar-condensed]. The coefficient construction [def-weil-pbc-condensed-qbar-coefficient-construction] identifies $A_Z$ with the sheafification of the bounded finite-subextension-valued function presheaf. This is exactly $\mathcal R_Z$: a function whose values span a finite-dimensional $\mathbf Q_\ell$-space takes values in the field generated by a finite basis of that space, a finite extension since every basis element is algebraic. Conversely a finite-subextension-valued function has finite-dimensional span. A locally finite-span function is therefore locally a bounded function. Every bounded function is locally finite-span, and the two inclusions induce inverse maps after sheafification (use the open neighbourhoods in the definition of locally finite-span). Denote this ring-sheaf isomorphism by $c_Z:A_Z\cong\mathcal R_Z$. No claim about all continuous $\Omega$-valued functions is used.

### Original step 2

These $c_Z$ respect the specified scheme coefficient maps. Indeed at each finite $E$ the chart equation of [lem-weil-pbc-continuous-chart-scheme-naturality] says that the coefficient pushforward, followed by the chart on the source, is the chart on the target followed by precomposition with the first fibre-product projection. Take the filtered coefficient colimit, which is $A_Z$ by [sk-prop-sfb-qlbar-colimit-x]; equality on its structure maps gives the same equation for $c_Z$. The bounded-function charts of the coefficient construction are these colimit charts. Thus, for every $v:Z\to W$, $c_Z\alpha_v=\rho_v v^{-1}c_W$, where $\alpha_v:v^{-1}A_W\to A_Z$ is the canonical coefficient isomorphism and $\rho_v:v^{-1}\mathcal R_W\to\mathcal R_Z$ is adjoint to the precomposition map in the item. By [sk-prop-sfb-cartesian-coefficients], $\alpha_v$ is invertible, so $\rho_v$ is invertible too. In particular $h^{-1}\mathcal R_T\cong\mathcal R_{T'}$ and $h'^{-1}\mathcal R_P\cong\mathcal R_{P'}$ use exactly the ring maps of this square, and their composition comparisons agree.

### Original step 4

$$h^{-1}p_*I\xrightarrow{\operatorname{bc}_I}p'_*h'^{-1}I\xrightarrow{p'_*b}p'_*J.$$
The ordinary $\operatorname{bc}$ is the counit adjunct in the item, formed degreewise; its naturality in $a$ gives
$\operatorname{bc}_I h^{-1}p_*a=p'_*h'^{-1}a\,\operatorname{bc}_{M[0]}$.
Consequently precomposing the displayed map with $h^{-1}p_*a$ gives $p'_*(b h'^{-1}a)\operatorname{bc}_{M[0]}$. The two resolution arrows are precisely $h^{-1}(\delta_{p,M})$ and $\delta_{p',h'^{-1}M}$, proving the asserted normalization equation. The resolving derived-adjunction normalization [lem-sfb-resolving-derived-adjunction-normalization], with exact inverse image as the left functor, identifies this construction with the derived counit mate of the pullback-square comparison. In particular it is independent of the resolving choices.
