I read BLR §4.4 Lemma 2 and the diagonal argument of Theorem 1 on the online primary scan, and the local Stacks proofs just named. The item is the local section version of that argument. The source need only be reduced for uniqueness; r is separated relative to B, and the structural composites agree because both candidate extensions are required to be B-morphisms. On any open overlap, D remains dense. The maximum domain V is constructed as a union and gluing; it is not taken from an unstated rational-map theorem.

Step 2 uses only the relative equality statement. The open T-circle contains all generic points by the dimension-zero case, hence is dense, and its intersection with the open T_V is dense. T is reduced because each stalk is a domain. This proves the actual equality of scheme morphisms v_V a=b, not just equality of their underlying point maps.

Step 3 uses affine opens as a basis, factorization through an open, quasi-compactness of an affine scheme, the descending-chain characterization supplied by the Noetherian closed-subsets node, and the finite clopen normal integral components theorem. The selected affine W1 is locally Noetherian by step 1, quasi-compact by the affine theorem, and normal integral because it lies in one normal component. This local component restriction avoids assuming X is integral.

Step 4 uses smoothness under open restrictions, Noetherianity of open subschemes, and factorization through an open. T' is an open of the original Noetherian T, and W is an open of Noetherian W1, so both applications have their hypotheses. Normality restricts because stalks are unchanged; a nonempty open of an integral scheme is integral by its definition. J is dense because it is open and nonempty in integral W: its nonemptiness is witnessed by any section point over T' intersect T-circle, where the already proved equality with b forces its image into H. No constructibility of a rational map's image was assumed.

Step 5 uses the full new normal-affine indeterminacy statement, including its glued map h_E and its assertion about generic points of every boundary component. W is integral normal Noetherian and J is a dense open, exactly its hypotheses; H can have an arbitrary coordinate ring, since Y is not assumed finite type. To use relative uniqueness with target Y, I first verify B-linearity of h_E. For this I compare two maps into the affine B0, which is separated over the arithmetic base by the explicit surjective multiplication description of its diagonal. This is a one-line affine definition calculation, not a global separatedness assumption on B. Both comparisons agree on dense J. It follows E is actually a candidate in the definition of V.

Step 6 uses only the statement of the new smooth-section-divisor-intersection node. All its hypotheses are now available from step 4 and the pure codimension assertion of step 5. Open restriction preserves the local rings, so the original codimension-at-most-one hypothesis applies to every required point of T'. This supplies the contradiction excluding the boundary; the dimension theorem is not smuggled into an elementary step.
