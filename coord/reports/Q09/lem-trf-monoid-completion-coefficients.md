For the first new node, the full argument retained from old steps 1--2 is as follows. Choose nonzero monoid generators, allowing the empty list for P={0}. Every nonzero summand has positive integral weight, so a q-term decomposition has q at most w(p); for p=0 only the empty decomposition exists. Thus the nonempty finite set of possible lengths has a maximum. Concatenation gives superadditivity. A bounded-weight element has a generator word of bounded length, so there are finitely many such elements; this bounds each coordinate of a tuple summing to a fixed p. A word for an exponent with nu(p)<n has length less than n, giving finite S_n. Expand a product of n ideal generators. If k factors are nonzero monomials, the coefficient at p is in I^(n-k); any remaining monomial factor only increases the available decomposition length, so k<=nu(p). This proves the coefficient containment. Conversely, a coefficient in I^(n-nu(p)) is a finite sum of products of that many ideal elements; combine them with an attained nu(p)-term decomposition. If nu(p)>=n, group a decomposition into n nonzero summands (or keep a residual monomial). Each monomial is therefore in J^n, and finite polynomial support permits summation. The zero exponent uses nu(0)=0.

For the second new node, the full inverse-limit argument retained from old steps 3--4 is as follows. Choose finite representatives F_N of compatible residue classes. For N>=j+nu(p), the coefficient at p is well-defined modulo I^j by the assumed finite-level criterion. Compatibility makes these residues a compatible system as j varies; completeness and separatedness of A give one and only one coefficient a_p. Conversely, the finite sum over S_n with coefficients a_p defines a residue modulo J^n. For m>=n, omitted exponents have nu(p)>=n and hence vanish at level n, so the residues are compatible. The criterion shows that these constructions are inverse. Coefficientwise addition and the monomial/scalar formulas follow at every level. For a product and a fixed p,j, take N>=j+nu(p). Superadditivity bounds nu(u),nu(v) by nu(p) for u+v=p, so every factor coefficient in the finite convolution is determined modulo I^j at this level. Multiplying representatives gives the claimed convolution modulo every I^j; separatedness gives equality in A. Finite pair decompositions also imply finite triple decompositions, so the usual finite-sum associativity calculation defines the convolution ring. The unique isomorphism is unique subject to the stated coefficient condition; no uniqueness among unrelated algebra automorphisms is claimed.

The factorization retained from old step 5 is already present in the cited node: choose finite generators of the maximal ideal, and a finite nonzero generator list of P. For low nu, expand a coefficient using the maximal-ideal generators and an attained decomposition, obtaining n-factor terms; there are finitely many low-nu exponents. For high nu, extract n generators from n nonzero summands and place each exponent in the first eligible tuple of generators in a fixed ordering. Cancellation in P subset G makes its residual exponent unique. The residual coefficient families are arbitrary elements of A^P. This is a finite sum of the chosen n-fold monomial products times those families, so it lies in the nth power of the family ideal. These are exactly steps 4--7 of the existing node, not new work to duplicate.
