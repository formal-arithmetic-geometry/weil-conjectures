## Source error

This is an error in an agent-created prerequisite, not a false assertion of Hemo–Richarz–Scholbach. Here is a counterexample with positive degree 1 (no unbounded Brauer-index input is needed).

Let k=Q and let R be the ring of eventually constant sequences in k indexed by positive integers. Equivalently, R is the filtered union of k^{F union {infinity}} over finite subsets F of the positive integers, with the tail factor split as F grows. Put X=Spec R. Its space is the one-point compactification S=N union {infinity}; all residue fields are k. The points m in N are clopen, and a neighborhood of infinity contains all sufficiently large m. This is an affine non-Noetherian scheme. Let j be the open immersion of the disjoint union of those isolated points into X. It is used only to calculate the example, not as a support-chain arrow; take the support chain to be empty, v=id_X, B=Z_2, pi=2, n=1, and L=M=B_X. Both modules are global free scalar local models of rank one. The continuous function a(m)=2^m, a(infinity)=0 defines a section of B_X and a module endomorphism f. Let I be its actual abelian image. As a subsheaf of the torsion-free B_X, I has no 2-torsion, so C_1(I) is quasi-isomorphic to G=I/2I in degree zero.

We compute G on the weakly contractible affine basis V of X_proet. Write P=pi_0(V), an extremally disconnected profinite space, P_m for its clopen inverse image of m, O=union_m P_m, and D=closure(O), which is clopen. Exactness of sections on V computes I(V) as the ordinary image of multiplication by a on C(P,Z_2). Its annihilator consists precisely of functions vanishing on D: a is nonzero on O, and continuity then forces a function annihilating it to vanish on D. Consequently the image, as a module, is C(D,Z_2), by restriction to the clopen D. Exactness of sections also computes G(V)=C(D,F_2).

Every continuous F_2-valued function on O extends uniquely to D. In fact the closures in P of its two disjoint open fibers are disjoint clopen sets (extremal disconnectedness), and their union is D. Thus

G(V)=C(D,F_2)=product_m C(P_m,F_2)=product_m Gamma(V_m,F_2).

These identifications respect pullback, since they are induced by restricting the quotient to each V_m; the displayed bijections prove this restriction map is an isomorphism on the basis. Hence G=j_proet,* F_2 as abelian sheaves. Moreover every V_m is clopen in the weakly contractible affine V and is weakly contractible, so H^{q>0}(V_m,F_2)=0. Cohomology on their disjoint union is the product of their cohomologies. Therefore Rj_proet,* F_2 is already this degree-zero G. Comparison on each isolated field and composition of right-derived direct images give

R nu_X,* C_1(I) = Rj_et,* F_2.

To see R^1 j_et,* F_2 is nonzero at infinity, enumerate distinct rational primes p_m and choose the tuple of quadratic torsors Q(sqrt(p_m))/Q in product_m H^1(Q,F_2)=H^1(N_et,F_2). No étale neighborhood of a geometric point above infinity kills its germ. Indeed take an affine étale neighborhood V of that point. Its finitely presented R-algebra descends to one of the finite partition rings k^{F union {infinity}}. On the entire remaining tail it is a single finitely presented étale k-algebra, hence a finite product of finite separable fields. At least one tail field K is nonzero, since V meets infinity. A fixed finite extension of Q contains only finitely many quadratic subfields (pass to its finite normal closure). Thus for arbitrarily large m the torsor for p_m remains nontrivial over K, so the restricted tuple is not zero on V. This remains true after shrinking to any affine étale neighborhood. By the sheafification description of higher direct images, its nonzero germ lies in (R^1 j_et,* F_2)_infinity. Thus H^1(R nu_X,* C_1(I)) is nonzero, contradicting the original claim.

The only general properties used in this calculation are the continuous-function description of pulled-back coefficients, the weakly contractible affine basis and exact evaluation (HRS Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.3; Bhatt–Scholze Sections 2 and 4), bounded classical comparison (Stacks 099V), and finite presentation over a filtered union. The rational-prime argument supplies the actual nonzero germ, rather than just noting that G is nonclassical: nonclassicality alone would not refute nonpositivity.
