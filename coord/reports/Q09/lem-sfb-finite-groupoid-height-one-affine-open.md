## Review by Q09

I read Stacks `more-groupoids.tex`, Tag 0ABI (`lemma-find-affine-codimension-1`), lines 2806--2949, in full. I compared the full absolute hypotheses and the conclusion with the item, including the interpretation of field-valued arrows after a common extension. I also read the statements of 09NN in varieties.tex, 0ABA/0ABE/0ABF/0ABH in more-groupoids.tex, 03JE/03BH in groupoids.tex, 03HV in morphisms.tex, 00HS in algebra.tex and 01J7 in schemes.tex. No new node has a separate source to audit. All non-elementary tools below have existing nodes, and I opened and read every cited node's statement; citations.json copies those statements verbatim.

Step 1 uses the groupoid laws to identify the orbit and the finite-fibre clause of `lem-weil-pbc-finite-closed-map-finite-fibres` to show it is finite. `pqf-lem-affine-open-locnoeth`, the affine-stalk-localization statement and localization-Noetherianity give Noetherian stalks. Local finiteness of components gives finiteness on quasi-compact opens by taking a finite subcover. These are exactly the extra hypotheses of `lem-trf-codimension-one-points-affine-neighbourhood` (09NN); separatedness and the dimension bound are already assumed. No field or finite-type-over-field condition is used.

Step 2 uses the closed-map clause for the finite target and `lem-sfb-groupoid-subset-saturation-invariant` for the closed saturation of the complement of A_0. The identity arrow gives the containment in A_0, and orbit disjointness shows the distinguished orbit survives. The Noetherian-open statement applies because A_0 has Noetherian coordinate ring. This produces a Noetherian scheme with an immersion into an affine scheme, without asserting that an arbitrary intersection of affine opens is affine.

Step 3 applies `lem-sfb-integral-orbit-affine-descent` to the reduction. Its orbit-cover hypothesis is immediate because the reduction has identical underlying points. `def-weil-pbc-scheme-reduction` gives the reduction closed immersion, and closed-immersion finiteness, finite base-change permanence and finite-implies-integral verify the hypotheses. The restricted arrow scheme is the double pullback, not just a source pullback; the extra closed immersion is finite because its affine algebra is a quotient of a finite module. Reduction does not change prime chains.

Step 4 uses the complete statement of `lem-sfb-finite-groupoid-almost-dense-flat-locus` (0ABA), including both the thickening and orbit-saturation clauses. On reduced W the thickening ideal vanishes: it is contained in the nilradical on each affine chart. The reduced closure T exists by `lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme`; its integral inclusion meets every orbit because the saturation of the closure is U. Thus 0ABE really applies a second time. Quotient prime chains preserve the local dimension bound, and the open W remains unchanged after restriction to its closure.

Step 5 treats u in W using 09NN and `lem-sfb-locally-free-groupoid-invariant-affine` (03JE). The common affine is justified by the original orbit dimension bound, not by an unproved blanket assertion about quasi-affines. Noetherianity of quotient charts follows by lifting ideals to the Noetherian original chart. `lem-trf-immersion-over-separated-base` verifies separatedness of the locally closed replacements and W. Its statement has no finite-type assumption. Component finiteness and local Noetherianity therefore give all the 09NN hypotheses again. The remembered integral descents complete this case.

Step 6 uses the local dimension definition and the affine-stalk and localization-prime correspondences: a further proper generalization would give a prime chain of length two in O_{U,v}. Component cover and scheme sobriety turn a point with no proper generalization into a component generic point. Incomparability of groupoid orbits (0ABG) keeps these generic points out of the distinguished orbit. The Noetherian finite-component lemma makes the list finite, possibly empty. A dense open contains every component generic point.

Step 7 first uses integral incomparability for the finite source, so an arrow over a component generic point has no proper generalization. For the flat target it uses the definition of flatness on stalks, the exact prime-chain assertion of `lem-sc-flat-going-down` (00HS), and clauses (1)--(2) of `lem-weil-pbc-spec-local-ring-canonical-morphism` (01J7 and the local-ring identification). The maximal ideal contracts to the maximal ideal since a scheme stalk map is local. The lifted prime gives r' generalizing r and mapping to the specified target generalization; hence r'=r. Component cover and sobriety then give the generic-orbit hypothesis needed at step 8. This explicitly repairs the direction error described above without citing the false statement.

Step 8 uses finite-over-locally-Noetherian implies finite presentation and the full generic-orbit function assertion of `lem-sfb-finite-groupoid-almost-invariant-function` (0ABF). In an affine immersion, j(U) is open in its closure H. Hence H minus j(U) and H minus j(W) are closed ambient subsets and define I and J; the required disjointness and closed-union hypotheses follow. The resulting f in J vanishes on U minus W, so F is contained in W and its invariance there extends to U.

Step 9 uses the reduced induced closed subscheme and the same double-pullback finite-arrow calculation from step 3. It does not assume the reduced complement Z is scheme-theoretically invariant: only its support is invariant. The full orbit remains in Z, and all proper generalizations were removed in F. Component cover and sobriety therefore give component generic points in Z, as required for the next function lemma. Step 10 applies finite-presentation-over-Noetherianity and 0ABF again, now on Z with the ideal I. The union V(I) with j(Z) is H minus j(F), closed since F is open in U and U is open in H. Thus its exact boundary-ideal hypothesis holds; it gives the invariant affine Z_h containing the full distinguished orbit.

Step 11 uses finite morphism permanence to ensure R_F is affine, the finite-locally-free norm charts to construct g, and `lem-a1-affine-groupoid-invariant-norm` (03BH) for equality of the two pullbacks of g. `lem-pd-finite-free-norm-prime-criterion` is applied on finite-free charts, so varying local rank is allowed. It says exactly that nonvanishing of g means h is nonzero at every arrow target. The identity arrow gives F_g contained in D(h). A principal open in affine F is affine by the spectrum definition.

Step 12 is the explicit set-theoretic assembly V=F_g union Z_h: both pieces are unions of full orbits, V contains the distinguished orbit, and V is constructible since U is Noetherian. If a generalization stays in Z, h remains nonzero there. Step 13 handles a generalization outside Z: the norm criterion produces an arrow whose target has h=0. The closed-map property of the finite source and `lem-trf-closed-map-specializing` lift its specialization to an arrow above z in Z_h. Continuity preserves h=0, contradicting the invariance of Z_h. The exact conclusion of `lem-trf-spectrum-constructible-generalization-open` makes V open on each affine chart. Closed-map specialization lifting is used in its correct direction, distinct from flat going down.

Step 14 applies `lem-sfb-immersion-principal-affineness` (0ABH). Quasi-compactness of the immersion follows since the open V in Noetherian U is Noetherian. The principal part for the image of f is exactly F_g, already affine. Because h belongs to I, j(U) intersect D(h) equals H intersect D(h), closed in Spec A_h; cutting by f identifies the complementary image with j(Z_h), closed there. These verify both nonformal hypotheses of 0ABH. Finally the two remembered 0ABE descents and inclusion into the initial invariant open recover an invariant affine in the original U. Such an invariant open containing one orbit point contains the entire orbit by the groupoid laws.

If u lies in W the proof terminates before using f or h. There are no hidden field, normality, irreducibility or flatness assumptions on the original groupoid.
