## What changed and what was wrong

The imported item was a statement-only prerequisite. Its full relative statement is correct and has been left byte-for-byte unchanged. In particular the kernel and central subgroup need not be smooth. The proof now constructs the commutator morphism and descends it, proves the homomorphism identities, and uses existing relative character duality to kill it on connected geometric fibres. This makes the Hom-functor rigidity argument of Conrad 3.3.1(2) explicit without creating a second rigidity node. Only after this has proved centrality, and hence commutativity of the preimage, is the extension theorem applied.

Conrad kills the Hom-functor by passage to finite torsion and finite etale duals. Our local coefficient/character formulation is a variant below the unit level: the existing ring-valued character-duality node already says that the Hom data is locally constant even over nonreduced parameter rings. Thus no torsion-density, representability-of-Hom or Cartier-duality theorem is silently being invoked in the parent proof. The commutator is hxh^{-1}x^{-1}, including its last factor; it descends to N, not to E.

The existing commutative-extension and action-rigidity nodes are over fields. Reducing our assertion to geometric fibres alone would leave a gap over a general base, so those nodes are not cited for a relative conclusion. The new extension input is genuinely relative and includes the fppf exactness hypothesis. The fppf map in its statement is flat and locally of finite presentation; since the quotient is of multiplicative type, it is fppf over the base, and composition makes the middle group fppf over the base. This is exactly the hypothesis in Conrad B.4.2(2), not an assertion that arbitrary fibrewise multiplicative groups are of multiplicative type.
