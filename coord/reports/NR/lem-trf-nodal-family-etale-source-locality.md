### First new node: retained arguments, old steps 1--4

Old step 1: Etale maps are flat by [def-weil-etale-morphism]. Apply [lem-trf-relative-flatness-source-cover] to the structure sheaf O_X: it is quasi-coherent because on each affine it is the sheaf associated to the free rank-one module, and h_i*O_X=O_U_i since B tensor_A A=B on stalks. Flatness of this sheaf over S is precisely flatness of the structure morphism. Therefore f is flat if and only if every f∘h_i is flat.

Old step 2: Each h_i is flat and locally of finite presentation by [def-weil-etale-morphism]. The jointly surjective family thus satisfies [lem-trf-lfp-fppf-source-locality], so f is locally of finite presentation if and only if every f∘h_i is. In either direction of the desired implication we may henceforth use local finite presentation on both f and the composites.

Old step 3: Fix an algebraically closed field Ω and a geometric point Spec Ω→S. Write C=X×_S Spec Ω and D_i=U_i×_S Spec Ω. Associativity of fibre products identifies D_i with U_i×_X C: both represent compatible pairs of maps to U_i and Spec Ω over S. By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-covering-base-change], the maps e_i:D_i→C are etale and jointly surjective. These are exactly the geometric fibres of the composites used in the statement.

Old step 4: The schemes C and D_i are locally of finite type over Ω. Indeed, choose finite-presentation affine charts for f and f∘h_i; after base change their rings are Ω tensor_R A, which are finitely presented by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-presentation-base-change-composition](1), and hence finitely generated simply by retaining the finitely many presentation generators. These affine charts cover the fibres by the definition of fibre product. Since Spec Ω is Noetherian (its ring is a field), [pqf-lem-locnoeth-lft] shows that C and D_i are locally Noetherian.

For the future splitter, these citations were opened and read: def-weil-etale-morphism explicitly includes flatness and local finite presentation; lem-trf-relative-flatness-source-cover explicitly gives the family equivalence for a quasi-coherent module, applied to the structure sheaf; lem-trf-lfp-fppf-source-locality gives exactly the arbitrary-family finite-presentation equivalence. lem-weil-pbc-etale-covering-base-change states both etaleness and joint surjectivity after base change. lem-weil-pbc-finite-presentation-base-change-composition (1) gives the finitely presented tensor-product algebra; pqf-lem-locnoeth-lft gives local Noetherianity over the Noetherian field. ### Second new node: retained arguments, old steps 5--8

Old step 5: For y∈D_i with x=e_i(y), the map e_i is flat and locally of finite type by the definition of etale (a finite presentation in particular supplies finitely many generators). Apply [lem-sbc-flat-composite-fibre-dimension-at-point] to D_i→C→Spec Ω. Its relative fibre over x has dimension zero by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-smooth-zero-dimensional-fibres]. Thus dim_y(D_i)=0+dim_x(C)=dim_x(C), where the dimensions mean local topological dimension at the indicated points, not the Krull dimension of the local rings.

Old step 6: Take the whole schemes as the open sets in the two local-dimension criteria. If C is pure of dimension one, [lem-sbc-equidimensional-open-local-dimension] gives dim_x(C)=1 at every x; step 5 gives dim_y(D_i)=1; [lem-sbc-constant-local-dimension-components] then gives pure dimension one for each D_i. Conversely, if all D_i are pure of dimension one, the first criterion gives dim_y(D_i)=1, and each x has some lift y by step 3. Step 5 gives dim_x(C)=1, and the second criterion gives purity of C. The finite type and local Noetherian hypotheses of the criteria were supplied in step 4. Empty fibres have no components, so the assertions remain valid.

Old step 7: Assume f is nodal. Steps 1, 2 and 6 give all the structural properties of every composite. If y is a nonsmooth point of D_i, the pointwise assertion of [lem-trf-etale-source-smoothness], applied to D_i→C→Spec Ω, implies that x=e_i(y) is nonsmooth on C. The nodal hypothesis on f gives the completed local Ω-algebra Ω[[u,v]]/(uv) at x. By [lem-trf-nodal-point-etale-locality], the same completed local algebra occurs at y. Its hypotheses hold: C is locally finite type by step 4, pure of dimension one by hypothesis, and e_i is etale by step 3. This proves nodality of each composite.

Old step 8: Conversely assume every composite is nodal. Steps 1, 2 and 6 give flatness, local finite presentation and pure dimension one for the geometric fibres of f. Let x be any nonsmooth point of C. Step 3 supplies i and y∈D_i above x. By the pointwise equivalence in [lem-trf-etale-source-smoothness], y is nonsmooth on D_i, so nodality of the composite supplies its completed local Ω-algebra Ω[[u,v]]/(uv). Apply [lem-trf-nodal-point-etale-locality] backwards: C is locally finite type by step 4 and pure of dimension one by step 6, and e_i is etale. The completed local algebra at x is therefore Ω[[u,v]]/(uv). This works for every Ω and x, proving the required nodal fibre condition.

For the future splitter, lem-sbc-flat-composite-fibre-dimension-at-point supplies the dimension equality when the first map is flat; it applies because etale is flat and locally of finite presentation and hence locally finite type. lem-weil-pbc-etale-smooth-zero-dimensional-fibres gives dimension zero for the relative fibre. lem-sbc-equidimensional-open-local-dimension gives pointwise dimension from purity over a field, and lem-sbc-constant-local-dimension-components gives purity from constant local topological dimension on a locally Noetherian scheme. Both apply to the whole open scheme. lem-trf-etale-source-smoothness explicitly includes the pointwise equivalence; its statement, not its proof, is used. lem-trf-nodal-point-etale-locality explicitly gives the equivalence of nodal completed local algebras at corresponding arbitrary points for a locally finite type, pure one-dimensional base curve. These two pointwise comparisons and the cover's lifts prove the last clause in each direction. All these old proof inputs are guidance for splitting the new nodes; they are not proof_deps on a statement-only node.
