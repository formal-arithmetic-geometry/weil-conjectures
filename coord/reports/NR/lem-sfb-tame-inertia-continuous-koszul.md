### Original step 1

Allocation: New fixed-summand preparation: finite averaging, residual action, adapted basis and scalar-extended internal invariants.

Put $L=\mathbf Z_\ell^r$ and $Q=\rho(H)$. The finite sum $e=|Q|^{-1}\sum_{h\in Q}h$ is a continuous $E$-linear idempotent: multiplication by any element of $Q$ permutes its summands, and $ev=v$ for a $Q$-fixed vector. Thus $\operatorname{im}e=M^H=N$. Each $\rho(\gamma_i)$ commutes with $e$ because the $H$ and $L$ actions commute. Hence $N$ is a finite-dimensional continuous $L$-representation. Choose an $E$-basis of $N$ and extend it by a basis of $\ker e$; in these coordinates $e=\operatorname{diag}(1,0)$. Over both $F$ the same matrix acts on continuous functions. Its image is exactly $N_F$, and its fixed vectors under $H$ are the same image. This verifies the coefficient objects to be used in the reduction, without an infinite average.

### Original step 2

Allocation: Finite-quotient argument goes to new fixed-summand preparation; application of the existing analytic reduction is repaired parent step 1.

Every finite quotient of $H=(\prod_{q\ne p,\ell}\mathbf Z_q)^r$ has order prime to $\ell$: its open kernel contains a basic product neighbourhood, leaving finitely many finite $q$-power factors, all with $q\ne\ell$. Apply the prime-to-ell reduction with $B=H$, $L=\mathbf Z_\ell^r$ and $V=M$. The finite-image and prime-to-ell hypotheses are precisely those of the item. It gives the inflation isomorphism $a_F:R\Gamma_L(N_F)\xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma_G(M_F)$. This step invokes the analytic reduction for the actual internal condensed modules; the finite sum in step 1 alone does not prove the vanishing of higher $H$-invariants.

### Original step 3

Allocation: Application of existing dense-lattice comparison is repaired parent step 2.

Apply dense-lattice restriction to the representation $V=N$ from step 1, with $D=\mathbf Z^r$ and the coordinate homomorphism $\iota:D\to L$. It gives $b_F:R\Gamma_L(N_F)\xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma_D(\operatorname{Res}_\iota N_F)$. All coefficients here still consist of all continuous norm-topological functions. The input is a derived-category isomorphism, so no inference from isomorphisms of separate cohomology groups is needed.

### Original step 4

Allocation: Strict subset/sign/square-zero construction goes to new norm-coefficient Koszul realization; application of the existing derived Koszul resolution is repaired parent step 2.

Use the discrete-lattice Koszul resolution with $R=C_F$, $P=\operatorname{Res}_\iota N_F$, and $T_i=\rho(\gamma_i)|_{N_F}$. These are commuting automorphisms by the product-group action. Its complex is literally $K_F(M)$: label each exterior basis vector by its increasing subset $I$. Removing the $i$th element of $J$ has position $1+\#\{j\in J:j<i\}$, giving exactly the displayed sign. In $d^2$ each unordered pair $\{i,j\}$ occurs twice with opposite signs; the two operators commute, so they cancel. Thus the lattice input provides $\lambda_F:K_F(M)\xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma_D(\operatorname{Res}_\iota N_F)$ with the required convention, not just its cohomology.

### Original step 5

Allocation: Full composite, naturality and degree-zero normalization are repaired parent step 3.

Define $\kappa_F=a_F\circ b_F^{-1}\circ\lambda_F$ in the derived category. All three factors are invertible by steps 2--4, so this is an isomorphism of the required direction. Naturality follows by composing the naturality squares of those three maps and inverting the square for $b_F$. On invariant elements, $\lambda_F$ is their inclusion in degree zero, $b_F$ is the identity under the invariant identification, and $a_F$ is induced by $N_F\hookrightarrow M_F$. Therefore this composite is compatible with the prescribed degree-zero inclusion.
