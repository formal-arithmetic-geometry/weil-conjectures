## Review by NR

I read the online Gepner–Haugseng definitions and both stated results, including the proofs of Lemma 3.1.8 and Proposition 4.2.3. The item assumes exactly local Kan operation/morphism complexes, small object sets in its stated universes, unital associative ordered substitution, and includes nullary operations. The displayed sums are over monotone simplex maps. Strings are nonempty sequences indexed from 0 through n; operator lists can be empty. No symmetry, nonempty-color, invertibility or nonempty-nullary-space hypothesis is used for the ordinary operad or general string assertions. Groupoid invertibility is used only in step 7.

1. Step 1 uses the definition of simplicial categories from `lem-coef-coherent-nerve-comparison`. It writes the actual substitution and composition rules, including which indexed operations are discarded and how a nullary block appears. Those equations prove associativity and units from the input data. It is an elementary construction, not an uncited operadic nerve theorem.
2. Step 2 uses the locally Kan, ordinary-nerve and mapping-space clauses of `lem-coef-coherent-nerve-comparison`, the horn definitions of `def-coef-infinity-fibrations`, and the ordinary nerve/horn/product definitions of `def-cbc-enhanced-diagrams-cones`. Each is stated there, not merely in its proof. Coordinate horn filling proves finite-product Kan closure, and a horn maps into a single coproduct summand because it is nonempty and connected. The relative inner lifting is checked using uniqueness of inner fillers in an ordinary nerve. It requires no unproved claim that an arbitrary map of quasicategories is an inner fibration.
3. Step 3 uses the composition-compatible mapping comparison, `lem-coef-infinity-cocartesian-mapping-criterion`, and `lem-coef-kan-homotopy-pullback-invariance`. The criterion explicitly applies to an inner fibration between quasicategories with one specified edge, precisely the hypotheses established in step 2; it does not require a cocartesian fibration in advance. The block equality proves the strict pullback, and discreteness of the base gives the path-space pullback directly. The invariance statement supplies passage through the natural mapping-space equivalences. I checked m=0 separately: there is one empty target list, and every product in the relevant mapping square is a point, including the multiple possible inert maps to [0].
4. Step 4 uses the coherent-nerve definition/right adjunction and the precise ordinary nonsymmetric operad definition in `lem-coef-operadic-algebra-presentability`; it takes no presentability conclusion from that node. The functor formula for the nerve proves that it commutes with fibers and finite products. Identity-base operations are unary, giving U^n, while the displayed arbitrary-base operations give exactly the target-coordinate mapping-space factors. These are both Segal conditions, rather than just the fiber condition. For n=0 the fiber is terminal even if P has no colors. For a constant monotone map all nonempty target factors are nullary operation spaces; they are allowed to be empty, and the proof does not assert otherwise.
5. Step 5 uses the same comparison, criterion and homotopy-pullback invariance as step 3, plus the actual definition of a cocartesian fibration. Its component formula remains valid when phi is noninjective: the pullback remembers the choice of psi even when different psi have the same composite phi psi. Hence repeated coordinates introduce no missing summands. Since lifts are constructed for every base arrow, this establishes the full cocartesian assertion.
6. Step 6 uses the nerve's enriched-functor definition and `lem-coef-cocartesian-straightening`. The latter states that classification has the fibers as values and cocartesian transport as arrows. I checked the n+1-fold fiber on both objects and all mapping simplices. The selected lifts give reindexing of morphisms as well as objects; their composition identities and constant coherence cubes verify the coherent diagram, so this step is stronger than a comparison of isolated fibers. At n=0 it is C; the map out of [0] to [m] repeats the same coordinate m+1 times. At [1] there are two entries, consistent with the input definition.
7. Step 7 uses the comparison's explicit groupoid-to-Kan clause, `lem-cbc-inner-fibration-equivalence-outer-horn-lift`, `lem-coef-infinity-cocartesian-composition-initiality`, and the horn definitions. The former relative theorem actually supplies fillers over the prescribed base simplex, so it does imply that a vertical equivalence is cocartesian; a theorem saying merely that groupoid nerves are Kan would not suffice. Factor an arbitrary string edge through the chosen identity-component lift and its vertical edge. That vertical edge lies in a Kan fiber, so it is an equivalence in the total space; the cited composition theorem then makes the original edge cocartesian. Initial one-horns use the already constructed lifts. This proves the literal left-horn lifting property. A discrete two-object C, the terminal C, and the empty C all satisfy the claim; the empty case has no horn maps and empty string fibers.
8. Step 8 uses comparison naturality, straightening functoriality, and the same composition/initiality and relative equivalence-edge tools. Initially I had only checked preservation of the specified lifts. I repaired this in the self-review: initiality compares any other cocartesian lift to the specified lift by a fiber equivalence, the induced functor preserves that equivalence, and the outer-horn and composition results show that string functors preserve every cocartesian edge (operator multifunctors need and give preservation only over inert arrows). This supplies the exact morphism hypothesis of straightening. The coordinate formulas then prove naturality, including the higher identity coherence simplices. It invokes no unsupported claim that arbitrary total-space functors preserve cocartesian arrows.
