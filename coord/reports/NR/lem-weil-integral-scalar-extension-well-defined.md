**lem-pur-compatible-coefficient-tower.** Source: Stacks 03EK, sites-modules.tex, section-tensor-product, including its displayed sectionwise tensor construction, sheafification and bilinear universal property. The strict-tower enhancement is agent-written. For each U, the map x tensor z to x tensor b_n(z) is R(U)-balanced since b_n r_(n+1)=r_n, and is S(U)-linear since b_n p_(n+1)=p_n. These section maps respect restriction and hence sheafify uniquely. Pure tensors generate the presheaf tensors, and the sheafification universal property proves equality of sheaf maps; it is not assumed that global sections of a sheaf tensor are generated by global pure tensors. Applying f tensor 1 commutes with these transitions and preserves identities, composition and sums. The termwise homotopy equation and the integer-shift reindexing commute with transitions because the transitions have degree zero. Restriction of scalars has the same underlying abelian sheaf; kernels and sheafified cokernels therefore give the same cohomology sheaves before and after restriction. This proves preservation and reflection of quasi-isomorphisms without flatness. Finally (a x) tensor z equals x tensor r_n(a)z, and the two k-actions agree in B_n, proving k-linearity. This lemma is generic in a site and compatible ring maps, and asserts no derived descent or completed scalar-extension theorem.

**lem-pur-strict-roos-homotopy-functor.** Sources: Stacks 014E, derived.tex, definition-cone, and 08TC, definition-derived-limit; explicit strict-model homotopy calculation, with the model of def-weil-strict-roos-model. A strict map f gives g=product f_n with g s_X=s_Y g, by composing with each product projection. Thus its diagonal map is a chain map and respects sums, identities and composition. For compatible h, the same projection test gives v_Y h_P=h_P v_X. With D(y,x)=(-dy-vx,dx) and H(y,x)=(-h_P y,h_P x), direct multiplication gives DH+HD=((dh_P+h_Pd)y,(dh_P+h_Pd)x); the cross term is h_Pv_X-v_Yh_P=0. Consequently homotopic strict maps give homotopic Roos maps, so the additive cochain functor passes to the homotopy quotient. Product projections and biproduct projections show that diagonal maps preserve scalar multiples in a k-linear category. Exactness of countable products is not required for this homotopy assertion. The lemma does not assert functoriality of an arbitrary chosen triangulated derived limit.

**lem-pur-additive-localization-descent.** Source: Stacks 04VG, categories.tex, lemma-properties-left-localization for localization background, with the stronger natural-transformation interface explicitly stated in lem-weil-pbc-localization-universal-property. Since q and U are additive, they carry a chosen zero object and the canonical biproduct diagram X→X+Y←Y, with its projections, to zero objects and biproduct diagrams. The natural isomorphism Gq≅U shows that G sends this particular q-image diagram to a biproduct diagram. Every pair in D is isomorphic to a pair qX,qY by the explicit essential-surjectivity assumption. Transporting these diagrams proves that G preserves binary biproducts and sends a zero object to zero, hence preserves zero morphisms. It then preserves the diagonal, codiagonal, and the block-diagonal map f+g expressed as the composite of diagonal, f direct-sum g, and codiagonal. Thus G(f+g)=Gf+Gg. This uses no chain-map lifting and no calculus of fractions. The elementary preservation-of-biproducts criterion is also visible in Mathlib CategoryTheory/Preadditive/AdditiveFunctor.lean:227–232; it does not alone state the comparison/essential-surjectivity lemma being introduced.
