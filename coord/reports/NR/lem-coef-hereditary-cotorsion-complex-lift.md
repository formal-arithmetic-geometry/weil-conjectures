## Source error

In the online arXiv version of Gillespie's Proposition 3.7, the displayed identification of sphere Ext with cycle Ext is used before exactness of the target complex is established. As an unrestricted identity this is false. In abelian groups take $A=\mathbf Z$ and $X=S^{n+1}\mathbf Z$. The nonsplit complex extension
$$0\to S^{n+1}\mathbf Z\to D^n\mathbf Z\to S^n\mathbf Z\to0$$
has nonzero class, while $Z^nX=0$ and $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\mathbf Z,0)=0$. It cannot split as complexes: a section is identity in degree $n$, which does not commute with the nonzero disk differential. Steps 4 and 8 first obtain exactness from the generator-sphere vanishing and the disk short exact sequence, and apply the sphere–cycle formula only afterward. This repairs the order of the argument without changing the theorem or its hypotheses. The new disk/sphere node explicitly includes the exactness hypothesis. This is a source-proof issue, not a statement dispute about the item.

## Technical arguments for the unsplit auxiliary nodes

These are explanations for the reader, not proof fields in the new statement-only nodes.

For the disk/sphere calculus, the disk functor in degrees $n,n+1$ is left adjoint to evaluation in degree $n$ and right adjoint to evaluation in degree $n+1$. On extensions these exact adjunctions give the two displayed disk formulas. For an extension of a sphere by an exact complex, taking degree-$n$ cycles gives an extension of $A$ by $Z^nY$; conversely such an extension is glued into degree $n$ and the next differential is obtained by its pushout along $Z^nY\to Y^n$. The exact-source formula is the dual construction in the opposite abelian category, where the relevant object is $\operatorname{coker}d^{n-1}=Z^{n+1}X$. If the degreewise extension groups vanish, choose splittings; the differential of the middle complex has matrix with diagonal entries $d_Y,d_X$ and off-diagonal entry $t^n:X^n\to Y^{n+1}$. Squaring it gives $d_Yt+td_X=0$. Changing a splitting changes $t$ by a Hom boundary. Conversely every such cocycle supplies a middle differential, and a boundary change supplies an equivalence of extensions; this proves the Yoneda $H^1$ classification. For a sphere on an injective $I$, degreewise splitting holds and exactness of $\operatorname{Hom}(-,I)$ identifies the resulting first Hom cohomology with $\operatorname{Hom}(H^{n-1}X,I)$.

For cycle-orthogonal splitting, a short exact extension of exact complexes is exact in the middle, and its cycle sequences are short exact. Choose sections $s_n:Z^nX\to Z^nE$ of those cycle sequences using the assumed Ext vanishing. In degree $n$ consider the subobject of $E^n$ consisting categorically of the kernel of
$$E^n\longrightarrow Z^{n+1}Y,\quad e\longmapsto d_Ee-s_{n+1}d_Xp(e).$$
The difference factors into $Z^{n+1}Y$, and the restriction on $Y^n$ is the epimorphism $Y^n\to Z^{n+1}Y$. The projection of this kernel onto $X^n$ is therefore epic with kernel $Z^nY$; this can equally be expressed by a pullback diagram, without elements. Since $X^n$ is an extension of $Z^{n+1}X$ by $Z^nX$, the first-variable Ext sequence gives $\operatorname{Ext}^1(X^n,Z^nY)=0$. Choose a section in degree $n$. Its restriction to $Z^nX$ differs from the already chosen $s_n$ by a map into $Z^nY$. That difference extends to $X^n$, since $\operatorname{Ext}^1(Z^{n+1}X,Z^nY)=0$. Subtract it. The resulting term section restricts to $s_n$ and has differential $s_{n+1}d_X$. All these independently chosen, adjusted term sections thus commute with the differentials. There is no induction from a nonexistent lowest degree.

For the augmented-tail test, every map $f:X\to E(T)$ has $f^0$ factoring through $X^0/Z^0X=Z^1X$. Every map $a:Z^1X\to T$ extends to a cochain map: begin with $a d_X^0$ in degree zero, extend its composite with $T\to I^0$ across $Z^1X\hookrightarrow X^1$ using injectivity of $I^0$, and inductively extend the resulting cycle maps into $I^1,I^2,\ldots$. A null homotopy changes $a$ by the restriction of a map $X^1\to T$. Conversely, after subtracting this first homotopy term, extend subsequent homotopy terms into the successive injectives; injectivity and exactness of $X$ provide the extensions at each positive degree. Thus the homotopy-class group is the cokernel of $\operatorname{Hom}(X^1,T)\to\operatorname{Hom}(Z^1X,T)$. The exact sequence $0\to Z^1X\to X^1\to Z^2X\to0$ identifies this cokernel with $\operatorname{Ext}^1(Z^2X,T)$ because $\operatorname{Ext}^1(X^1,T)=0$. The index two in the node was checked by this computation.

The Yoneda comparison is precisely the content of Stacks `derived.tex`, Tag 06XU, `lemma-yoneda-extension`, read with the definition preceding it and the degree-one consequence 06XV. The Baer sum is obtained by direct sum followed by diagonal pullback and addition pushout; those operations give the addition of derived classes. For injective targets the first monomorphism of a positive extension splits, so its Yoneda class is zero. This is a genuine bridge beyond the existing degree-one classification or the mere definition of derived Ext.
