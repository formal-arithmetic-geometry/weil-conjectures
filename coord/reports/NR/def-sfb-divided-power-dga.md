## Source error

Stacks Tag 09PI (`example-adjoining-even`) says that the extended structure satisfies `gamma_n(T^(i)) = T^(ni)` and subsequently gives a sum formula without the corresponding factorial coefficients. Taken literally, this contradicts 09PG(7). For an even generator T over Q, take n=i=2. The item correctly imposes

\[
\gamma_2(T^{[2]})=\frac{4!}{2!(2!)^2}T^{[4]}=3T^{[4]},
\]

whereas the sentence in 09PI gives only `T^[4]`. In the free divided-power polynomial algebra over Q this basis vector is nonzero and 3 times it is different from it. This also contradicts the displayed sum formula when its only nonzero coefficient is x_2=1. Tag 07H5 (`lemma-divided-power-polynomial-algebra`) and its torsion-free computation confirm the factorial coefficient. I read 07H5 and the preceding monomial construction in section 07H4 to check this interpretation. The source's intended construction is the standard divided-power polynomial algebra, not the incorrectly normalized formula. The item's original iterated-power axiom already has the correct coefficient, so the item requires no substantive statement correction and is not STATEMENT-DISPUTED.

## Review by NR

The first server dry run returned one precise deficiency: the strict graded-algebra node named no node for its underlying grading. I repaired this by adding `def-sfb-nonnegative-graded-algebra`, a Mathlib leaf, and citing it in the strict node. Before creating it I searched the whole DAG for `GradedAlgebra`/`GradedRing` declaration matches and the whole blueprint for `nonnegative` and `GradedAlgebra`; no existing definition matches an arbitrary noncommutative N-graded R-algebra. I reran the semantic, Loogle and grep searches specifically for this leaf and read Basic.lean:48-67 and 163-172. The Mathlib fields supply the exact decomposition and multiplication grading. The scalar-image condition is immediate: algebraMap(r)=r times 1 lies in the R-submodule A_0. There is no extra connectedness, generation, commutativity or positivity assumption. For edge cases, A concentrated in degree zero and the zero ring are allowed. This third new node is complete as a Mathlib leaf; the other two remain unsplit. The next dry run required this leaf itself to name its direct-sum notion. I opened the existing `def-weil-pbc-direct-sum-modules` and read its entire statement: it defines the finitely supported families, module structure, canonical inclusions and finite-sum expression used by the grading. I linked that existing Mathlib leaf in the new grading definition. This is the actual direct sum used in the statement, with I=N and M_i=A_i. It adds neither a new construction nor a new mathematical hypothesis.

For the item I read section 09PF and definitions 09PG/09PJ in full, both adjoining-variable examples 09PH/09PI, and remark 0F4I for arbitrary sets of variables. The item keeps the homological grading d:A_i to A_(i-1), rather than substituting a cohomological convention; its negative terms are zero. The existing chain-complex/homology node explicitly defines that direction, extension by zero and the quotient ker d / im d, so it says exactly what the homology sentence takes from it. The two new definition statements were read back against the item: the first gives its precise strict graded algebra, and the second gives each of the seven graded divided-power conditions. The item explicitly cites both. There are no proof steps: the C1/C4 per-step audit and the long-step audit are vacuous; the nontrivial conditions are stated as data and axioms, not claimed as consequences of a definition or as uncited theorems.

For `def-sfb-nonnegative-strict-graded-algebra` I used the introductory paragraphs of 09PF immediately before 09PG. I verified that R is only a commutative ring, A need not be commutative as an ungraded algebra, scalars and the identity have degree zero, and the grading has no connectedness assumption A_0=R. The even-subalgebra and ideal descriptions follow directly from degree addition and the sign (+1) in even degrees. The source and new node both impose odd homogeneous squares zero separately. This matters over F_2: the ordinarily graded commutative algebra F_2[t] with t of degree 1 satisfies the sign identity but fails t^2=0. It must be excluded. A ring concentrated in degree zero, the zero ring, and an exterior generator in degree 1 meet the stated conventions without a restriction on characteristic.

For `def-sfb-graded-divided-power-structure` I used all seven conditions of 09PG. I checked that only positive n have codomain A_even,+, while gamma_0=1 lives in degree zero; n=m=1 and n=1 in the iteration identity give the required identities. The scalar a is any element of A_even, not merely an element of R, and u,v in the odd-product condition are homogeneous. It would be wrong to impose vanishing on every sum of odd products: in an exterior algebra on four odd generators gamma_2(z_1 z_2+z_3 z_4)=z_1 z_2 z_3 z_4 may be nonzero, as the paragraph following 09PG notes. The new node retains precisely this distinction. Factorials act as integer coefficients, so there is no hidden characteristic-zero hypothesis. With no positive degrees, the positive ideal is zero and its divided powers are the trivial ones, with gamma_0(0)=1. A single even generator has gamma_n(T)=T^[n] and the binomial multiplication law of 07H4, which is consistent with the corrected iteration coefficient discussed above. Polynomiality concerns only the underlying graded divided-power algebra, not the differential.

I read the Mathlib hits rather than treating their names as evidence of a match. `GradedAlgebra` at RingTheory/GradedAlgebra/Basic.lean:170 (underlying `GradedRing` at line 67) has no strict signed-commutativity axiom. `GradedTensorProduct.comm` at LinearAlgebra/TensorProduct/Graded/Internal.lean:363 swaps two tensor factors; it does not define strict graded commutativity of A. `DividedPowers` at RingTheory/DividedPowers/Basic.lean:78-92 supplies ordinary ideal axioms but neither the degree condition nor odd-product vanishing; the rational and square-zero special cases do not cover general R and A. The semantic searches used two different phrasings for each notion, supplemented by Loogle and source grep. Accordingly neither new node is classified as a Mathlib leaf. All searches recorded in the new files were actually run through msearch.py, and their unmodified result records are preserved.

The original item was not mathematically weakened. I added references to the two notions without deleting its formulas. The only correction made in the mathematical reading was to reject the erroneous normalization printed in 09PI; no new node copies that error.
