## Review by NR

I read the current source of the item and new node online: Serre §10 Proposition 11 computes the two coefficient stalks and invokes §6 Proposition 4 for the local analytic quotient. I read Proposition 4 and its proof on printed pp.10–11, and checked its scope: it concerns the analytic ideal of an algebraic subvariety. Here both schemes are smooth over C and thus reduced, and the closed immersion realizes exactly such a subvariety, so there is no issue with Serre's older reduced-variety convention. The new node states only that ideal/local-ring result; it contains no proof or embedded proof sketch. Its surjectivity is the analytic restriction to the induced closed analytic subspace, and its kernel is precisely what Proposition 4 supplies. I also read the latest blueprint chapter/unit and direct-image proof: this is a supporting GAGA input in the existing chain, not a replacement of the six-functor route. The item is not the unit key statement, so no unit realization record is changed.

For step 1, I opened the full statements of the analytification/cotangent node, the topological inverse-image definition, the inverse-image module definition, the sheaf tensor definition, and the sheafification definition. They provide, respectively, the analytic spaces and maps, the directed representative colimit, multiplication of representatives and its semilinear unit, the sheafified tensor, and its universal factorization into sheaves. The argument constructs the actual map from representatives, verifies balancing and restriction compatibility, and then sheafifies. I marked it elementary because it is a direct instance of these definitions with the balancing equation written out. In particular I did not invoke the scheme-only module-pullback adjunction for an analytic map.

For step 2, I read the statements of the general ringed-space tensor-stalk node (Stacks 01CB), the topological inverse-image stalk node including its unit germ formula (008H), the inverse-image module definition (008X), the algebraic module closed-pushforward stalk node, and the topological abelian closed-pushforward stalk node (both 00AE). I checked the local Stacks sources: `modules.tex` lemma-stalk-tensor-product and `sheaves.tex` lemma-stalk-pullback, lemma-stalk-tensor-sheaf-modules, lemma-stalk-pullback-modules and lemma-stalks-closed-pushforward. The tensor lemma is applicable over the ringed space with structure sheaf pi-inverse O, not only over a scheme. The inverse-image stalk comparison's normalization identifies the map on pure germs, not just its endpoints. For the algebraic pushforward I use the statement's explicit semilinearity; for the analytic pushforward I use the genuinely topological node and write the scalar compatibility as restriction of products. This avoids claiming that a scheme-only node applies to a complex manifold.

For step 3, I reopened the closed-immersion definition: its surjective structure-sheaf map gives local lifts of germs, so A maps onto R with kernel I. I then checked the new node's exact statement gives the required analytic B/IB identification through the restriction map. The remaining claim is elementary tensor algebra, not a second unstated theorem: the inverse sends [b] tensor m to b tensor m; an element of IB changes the result by sums of b'a tensor m = b' tensor am = 0. The same relation verifies balancing over A/I. Both composites fix generators. No flatness, coherence theorem, or exactness of analytification is being assumed here.

For step 4, I checked the same closed-immersion definition, the two distinct closed-pushforward stalk nodes, and the tensor/inverse-image stalk nodes against their statements again. The off-image assertion is made about a complex point, not an arbitrary map with image in a closed subset. Locally it is the elementary factorization of a ring map into the field C through its quotient: the ideal lies in the point's prime, hence maps to zero. I wrote this to justify passage from being outside the analytic image to being outside the algebraic image. The two zero stalk calculations then use exactly the off-image clauses of the cited nodes.

For step 5, I read the statement of the topological sheaf-of-sets stalkwise isomorphism criterion and Stacks 007T, lemma-points-exactness(3). Applied to the underlying sheaves of the constructed linear map it gives bijectivity on sections; the inverse is linear by cancellation. I computed the requested canonical-unit equation on every algebraic open and every section, and wrote the equality of the inverse-image opens that identifies the two composed direct images. Thus naturality is not asserted in place of checking this compatibility.

I tested the item and the new statement on an empty source, identity immersion, inclusion of a point in affine space, and a coordinate subspace with positive codimension. The empty case has only zero pushforward stalks and the new statement has no x to quantify over. The identity has I=0 and the tensor comparison is the identity. For the origin in affine n-space, B is the ring of holomorphic germs, IB is generated by the coordinate germs and B/IB=C; the comparison is the usual reduction of a tensor. A disconnected smooth scheme causes no problem because the argument is entirely pointwise and imposes no constant dimension or rank. A zero coherent module gives the zero map between zero sheaves. I did not extend the local-ideal statement to singular or nonreduced objects that Serre's convention would leave ambiguous.
