## What was wrong and what changed

The imported proof had empty uses lists and null elementary flags, so neither the finite cohomology induction nor derived evaluation preserving the truncation tower was actually split. Its bare reference to the pro-étale Milnor node would not state the Milnor sequence of abelian groups needed after evaluation. I used lem-pc-derived-limit-milnor-sequence for that sequence, with all its hypotheses verified, and kept the pro-étale left-completeness route exactly as unit step 2 prescribes.

The statement's two hypotheses and two numerical conclusions are unchanged. I made b explicitly an integer, spelled out the unbounded derived category, canonical truncations, constant ring sheaf, left modules, derived evaluation, and a common small-site universe. In particular I did **not** add commutativity to the finite-ring assumption: classicality is the underlying set-sheaf condition of sk-def-pe-classical-definition. The module condition is equivalent because the canonical section map is linear, and a bijective linear map has a linear inverse. This avoids applying that node's commutative coefficient clause to an arbitrary finite ring. All groups can be computed by evaluating left-module K-injective resolutions and forgetting the scalar action. Step 1 identifies this with the usual right derived evaluation on degree-zero sheaves using a bounded-below injective resolution. The natural A-actions are retained, and the final step explicitly checks that the inverse of the canonical group isomorphism is A-linear. Thus the conclusions hold as A-module statements too. These are conventions and justification for the existing statement, not a weakening or a new mathematical hypothesis.

The proof now explicitly handles b<-n, spells out the base, induction step and terminal identification of each bounded truncation, identifies product projections under evaluation, and checks that the transition endomorphism becomes the transition endomorphism of the evaluated tower. It regards the derived limit as an object with a Roos triangle and asserts no canonical functorial choice of arbitrary cones. The final step checks the quotient-to-good-truncation map and thus proves invertibility of the canonical map asked for in clause (2).
