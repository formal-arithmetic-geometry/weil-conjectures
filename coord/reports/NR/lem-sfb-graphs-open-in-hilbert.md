### Original step 1 — now lem-sfb-hilbert-graph-family-base-change

Fix an $S$-scheme $T$. By [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence](b), [lem-weil-pbc-flat-morphism-base-change] and [lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence](3), $Z_T\to T$ is proper, flat and finitely presented. The diagonal of $X_T\to T$ is the base change of the closed diagonal of $X\to S$ ([def-weil-proper-morphism]); it is closed by [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence](b). Thus $X_T\to T$ is separated. For $f:Z_T\to X_T$ over $T$, the square with upper arrow $\Gamma_f=(\mathrm{id},f):Z_T\to Z_T\times_TX_T$, lower arrow $\Delta_{X_T/T}:X_T\to X_T\times_TX_T$, and right arrow $(f\circ\operatorname{pr}_1,\operatorname{pr}_2)$ is cartesian: a pair $(z,x)$ lifts uniquely exactly when $f(z)=x$. This is the fibre-product universal property [def-pc-fibre-product]. Hence $\Gamma_f$ is a closed immersion by the same base-change statement. Its domain is $Z_T$, so its resulting closed subscheme has proper, flat, finitely presented structure map to $T$. It therefore belongs to the Hilbert functor specified in the item.

Original cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence, lem-weil-pbc-flat-morphism-base-change, lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence, def-weil-proper-morphism, def-pc-fibre-product.

### Original step 2 — now lem-sfb-hilbert-graph-family-base-change

Pullback along $T\prime\to T$ sends admissible closed families to admissible closed families: closedness and properness follow from [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence](b), flatness from [lem-weil-pbc-flat-morphism-base-change], and finite presentation from [lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence](3). Iterated fibre products agree with the single base change by [def-pc-fibre-product], so pullback respects identities and composition. Pulling back the two graph projections yields $(\mathrm{id}_{Z_{T\prime}},f_{T\prime})$. Its first projection is the identity and its second projection is $f_{T\prime}$; the uniqueness clause of the fibre-product property identifies it with $\Gamma_{f_{T\prime}}$. This proves naturality of the graph transformation.

Original cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence, lem-weil-pbc-flat-morphism-base-change, lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence, def-pc-fibre-product.

### Original step 3 — now lem-sfb-graph-projection-functor-comparison

For any closed subscheme $j:D\hookrightarrow Z_T\times_TX_T$, write $k=\operatorname{pr}_1\circ j$ and $l=\operatorname{pr}_2\circ j$. If $D$ is a graph then $k$ is an isomorphism, with inverse given by the graph parametrization. Conversely, if $k$ is an isomorphism, put $f=l\circ k^{-1}$. The two projections of $\Gamma_f\circ k$ are $k$ and $l$, so [def-pc-fibre-product] gives $j=\Gamma_f\circ k$. Thus $D$ is precisely the graph of $f$ as a closed subscheme. In particular the graph determines $f$ uniquely, proving injectivity.

Original cited nodes: def-pc-fibre-product.

### Original step 4 — now lem-sfb-graphs-open-in-hilbert

Now let $D$ be a Hilbert family over $T$. Apply [lem-sfb-universal-isomorphism-open] with $B=T$, $A=D$, $C=Z_T$, and $f=k$. The structure map $D\to T$ is flat, finitely presented and proper by membership in this Hilbert functor, hence locally of finite presentation and universally closed. By Step 1, $Z_T\to T$ is proper, hence separated, locally of finite type and closed: these implications are part of [def-weil-proper-morphism], since universal closedness includes the identity base change. All six hypotheses of the cited lemma hold; no flatness or finite-presentation hypothesis on $X\to S$ is used. It supplies an open $U\subseteq T$ such that for every $h:T\prime\to T$, $k_{T\prime}$ is an isomorphism exactly when $h$ factors through $U$. This is the required universal open locus.

Original cited nodes: lem-sfb-universal-isomorphism-open, def-weil-proper-morphism.

### Original step 5 — now lem-sfb-graph-projection-functor-comparison

The fibre product of the morphism functor with the represented functor $h_T$ over the Hilbert functor has, on a test scheme $T\prime$, pairs $(h:T\prime\to T,f:Z_{T\prime}\to X_{T\prime})$ for which $\Gamma_f=D_{T\prime}$. By Step 3 such an $f$ exists uniquely if and only if $k_{T\prime}$ is an isomorphism, and Step 4 identifies these $h$ exactly with maps $T\prime\to U$. The correspondence commutes with pullback by Step 2; hence this fibre product is represented by $U$, and its map to $T$ is the open inclusion. This proves representability by open immersions. On $U$, the map corresponding to $D_U$ is exactly $l_U\circ k_U^{-1}$.

Original cited nodes: none (earlier steps and definitions).

### Original step 6 — now lem-sfb-graph-projection-functor-comparison

If a scheme $H$ represents the Hilbert functor, apply Steps 4 and 5 to its universal family over $T=H$. The resulting open $M\subseteq H$ represents the morphism functor. The inverse first projection of the restricted universal family followed by its second projection gives the evaluation $Z_M\to X_M$. Every other family is its pullback under the representing map to $M$. To check arbitrary base change explicitly, the pullback of $k_U^{-1}$ is inverse to the pullback of $k_U$, because their two composites are the pullbacks of identity maps; uniqueness of inverses therefore gives $(k_U^{-1})_{T\prime}=(k_{U,T\prime})^{-1}$. Consequently evaluation pulls back to $l_{U,T\prime}\circ(k_{U,T\prime})^{-1}$. The same test-map criterion of Step 4 shows the open locus itself pulls back to the corresponding open locus after every base change.
