## Worker report

The module `Sheaf.AlgebraicGeometry.FineFanBlowup` defines the fine-fan framework of
`def-trf-fine-fan-blowup`, culminating in
`AlgebraicGeometry.FineFan.SmoothFunctorialDesingularization`. All its data are explicit;
there are no new theorems, axioms, `sorry`s, or choices of an object from a postponed existence
proof. Blowup is specified as a property of a supplied fan morphism by its exact affine atlas,
including its overlap opens and its canonical maps. A desingularization is a structure of
actual towers and coherent comparison data, not an assertion of their existence.

Source read: Illusie--Laszlo--Orgogozo, Travaux de Gabber, Exposé VIII, printed pp. 98--102,
§§3.1.1--3.1.26, in the supplied PDF. In particular, 3.1.4 explains the finite spectrum,
its generic point and minimal affine neighborhoods; 3.1.13--3.1.16 gives smoothness;
3.1.17--3.1.24 gives localization-compatible saturated chart blowups and pullback towers;
3.1.26 gives smooth functoriality. The node's saturated contraction convention is retained.
No change to the natural-language statement or its dependencies is needed.

The code uses `CommMonCat`, as do the recorded log-structure and face-sharpening dependencies.
Its multiplicative notation represents the node's additive monoids through `Multiplicative`:
`1` is `0`, product is addition, quotient is difference, and `g ^ n` is `n • g`.

Clause-by-clause rendering:

1. **Group completion, fine, sharp, saturated.** `Algebra.GrothendieckGroup.of` is the
   canonical completion map already used by `def-trf-fs-log-blowup`.
   `IsFine P` is `Monoid.FG P` and injectivity of that map. `IsSharp P` says every
   `IsUnit` element equals `1`. Saturation uses Mathlib's `Submonoid.PowSaturated` on
   the range of the completion map in `Fan.IsSaturated`: its `n = 0 ∨ ...` formulation
   is exactly the node's implication for `n > 0`, and does not impose a condition at zero.
2. **Localization and saturation construction.** The existing `Algebra.faceLocalization`
   is reused for the monoid localized at `Submonoid.closure S`; its carrier consists
   exactly of fractions `p/s`. Thus for a submonoid this is the node's `P-S`, and for
   a singleton it is `P[a⁻¹]`. `saturation Q` takes the submonoid closure of the specified
   positive-root subset inside the supplied group. That subset is already a submonoid:
   `1` has root witness 1, and positive witnesses `n,m` for `x,y` give witness `n*m`
   for `x*y`. Taking closure therefore changes no elements; it supplies the structure
   without postponing any closure proof or choosing any data.
3. **Units and sharpening.** Mathlib `IsUnit`/`Units` are the elements admitting inverses;
   `Associates` is exactly quotient by multiplication by a unit, also for a monoid with
   no zero. `sharpenMap` is induced by `Associated.map`. `sharpPresheaf` takes this
   quotient on sections, and `sharpSheaf` applies the recorded `presheafToSheaf`.
   The commutative monoid operations, including the locally induced addition, are
   transported by the algebraic sheafification itself.
4. **Ideals, prime ideals, spectrum, basic opens.** Mathlib's `SemigroupIdeal P` supplies
   absorption; commutativity identifies left absorption with `I+P ⊆ I`.
   `MonoidPrime` adds properness and closure of the complement under multiplication.
   It does not require a nonempty ideal, so the empty prime is included.
   `spectrumTopology` is generated by the sets in `basicOpen a`, namely `a ∉ p.carrier`.
5. **The monoid structure sheaf and its restrictions.** `spectrumSections U` consists of
   pointwise local fractions which agree in the common ambient completion. Each nonempty
   open of a fine monoid spectrum contains its unique generic point, so this condition
   is the usual agreement of localized sections. Every fine spectrum has finite topology
   and minimal affine neighborhoods (ILO VIII 3.1.4); at a point those fractions come from
   that neighborhood. Consequently these are exactly the ordinary structure sections,
   and on `D(a)` they are `P[a⁻¹]`. The empty open has its singleton empty function.
   `spectrumRestriction` restricts the functions, and `spectrumPresheaf` uses these maps.
   `spectrumSheaf` applies the existing sheafification functor; on the fine spectra used
   in charts this does not change the already sheaf-like fraction construction.
6. **Sheaves and germs.** Sheaves are Mathlib `TopCat.Sheaf CommMonCat` on
   `Opens.grothendieckTopology`, exactly the open-cover site. `stalkDiagram`, `stalk`,
   and `germ` use the neighborhood colimit, with the actual quotient colimit of Mathlib
   `CommMonCat.FilteredColimits`. Equality in this filtered colimit is equality after
   further restriction. The helpers generalize the existing `TopCat.Presheaf.stalk` API
   to the filtered-colimit hypotheses available for `CommMonCat`: Mathlib's topological
   stalk API requires **all** small colimits, an instance it does not supply for this
   category. `monoidFilteredColimits` installs Mathlib's actual explicit construction,
   and `stalkMap` is induced by naturality and inverse images of neighborhoods.
7. **Affine models and fine fans.** `affine P` is the spectrum with the sharpened monoid
   sheaf. `Chart` is an open subset and an actual sheafed-space isomorphism to `affine P`
   for fine `P`. `Fan.space`, `Fan.sharp`, and `Fan.locallyAffine` give exactly the
   topological space, sharp sheaf, and local affine charts. All open subsets are allowed.
8. **Morphisms and fan properties.** `Hom.map` is a sheafed-space morphism: a continuous
   base map and natural homomorphisms on inverse-image opens. `Hom.isLocal` uses the
   existing `IsLocalHom` on the induced maps of germs. `Fan.IsTorsionFree` tests positive
   powers in every stalk completion; `Fan.IsSaturated` tests root saturation in that
   completion. `Fan.IsSmooth` permits every finite rank, including rank zero.
   `Hom.IsSmooth` requires an isomorphism from the source stalk times the free monoid
   to the target stalk, identifying the actual map with the first-factor inclusion.
9. **Coherent nonempty ideals and pullback.** `IdealFamily`, `idealStalk`, and
   `IsIdealSheaf` give section subsets with absorption, restriction and local membership.
   Local membership together with the ambient sheaf supplies the subsheaf gluing axiom.
   `IsNonemptyCoherentIdeal` requires locally a positive finite list of ideal sections
   generating exactly the ideal germs. Repetitions in the list are harmless and cover
   exactly finite nonempty sets. `pullbackAlong`/`pullbackIdeal` generate the image ideal
   at germs and then test membership locally, so generation is sheaf-local.
10. **Chart blowups.** `blowupChart P E a` is the submonoid generated by the image of `P`
    and all `b/a` with `b ∈ E`. `saturatedChart` saturates inside the original completion.
    `chartInclusion` and `overlapElement` are explicit ambient-group inclusions and ratios.
    A generator of the ideal outside `E` has form `b*p` with `b ∈ E`, so the construction
    is equivalent to using all elements of the ideal, exactly as in the node.
11. **Canonical affine maps and gluing.** `affineSection` uses the numerator map, the
    sheafification units, and the unit quotient; `affineGerm` is its actual germ.
    `IsAffineMap` fixes the inverse images of primes and the images of all numerator
    germs. `GeneratedOnChart` identifies the given ideal in these canonical coordinates.
    `BlowupPatch` retains a sharp fine base monoid and a finite nonempty generating set,
    the actual affine open charts `Spec(C_a)`, canonical maps over the base, coverage of
    precisely the inverse-image base open, and intersections exactly `D(b/a)`.
    These conditions give precisely the specified quotient gluing: an open atlas
    identifies the ambient space with the quotient of its disjoint union, and the ambient
    sheaf gives agreeing tuples of sections. The transition maps are the identity on
    common localizations. Indeed the maps agree on `P` by `overBase`; fractions are
    forced by inverses, and saturation roots are unique in the sharp fs target stalks.
    Those stalks are torsion-free by the recorded fs face-sharpening theorem. Thus the
    atlas permits no different transition over `P`. `IsSaturatedBlowup` requires such
    patches around every base point, together with the precise coherent ideal condition.
    This describes the exact construction up to its canonical isomorphism; it does not
    replace the canonical construction by an arbitrary map or choose an existence witness.
12. **Towers and their recursive pullback.** `Tower n` has `Fin (n+1)` levels, `Fin n`
    ordered centers and arrows, each required to satisfy `IsSaturatedBlowup` for that
    center. `PullbackTower` requires the successive centers to be `pullbackIdeal` and
    all squares to commute. Each new level is the saturated blowup of the preceding
    level by its `Tower.blowup`, which is exactly the stipulated recursive pullback.
13. **Contraction.** `Contraction.index` is an order-preserving surjection of level sets,
    preserving endpoints. `adjacent` advances by zero or one stage. `deleted` says that
    advancing by zero is equivalent to the center being `wholeIdeal` **and** the actual
    arrow being an isomorphism. `deletedMap`, `keptMap`, and `keptCenter` transport arrows
    and the surviving ordered centers through the supplied level isomorphisms. A
    nontrivial saturation at a unit center cannot be deleted.
14. **Smooth-functorial desingularization.** `ResolutionInput` restricts exactly to fine
    torsion-free fans with finite affine covers. `Resolution` supplies an actual finite
    tower starting at that input and with smooth last level. `ResolutionComparison`
    supplies the recursive pullback starting at the source of the smooth map, fixes its
    base map, and contracts it to the source's assigned tower, fixing the base
    identification. `SmoothFunctorialDesingularization.comparison` is provided for every
    smooth morphism. `identityIndex`, `identityMap`, `compositionIndex`, and
    `compositionMap` impose the identity and composition laws for the index contractions
    and induced level maps. `HEq` expresses transport along the stated index equalities;
    it does not forget centers, because the contraction and pullback structures preserve
    them explicitly.
