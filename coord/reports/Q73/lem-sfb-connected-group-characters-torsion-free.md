The first supplies both regularity and local Noetherianity, not merely regular stalks. The second requires nonempty connectedness: the identity supplies a point, and connectedness is a hypothesis. Step 2 uses def-weil-affine-spectrum for the unique contravariant coordinate map and def-sc-gm-ga for its Laurent coordinate and comultiplication. The latter explicitly says m*(t)=t⊗t, which proves the coordinate of the nth power character is u^n by induction. It therefore gives the equation in the ring, not just on rational points. Both assertions are in the quoted statements.

Step 3 takes only the displayed polynomial factorization from lem-weil-alg-closed-linear-factorization. T^n−1 is nonzero and monic because n≥1 and F is a field. Substitution into the coordinate ring gives a zero product. The short induction from the domain axiom produces a scalar u; this elementary calculation is written in the proof and is not disguised as a new node. It works when the factors repeat, so no prime-to-characteristic hypothesis is needed.

Step 4 uses the identity section and group homomorphism notion from def-sc-group-scheme, the affine-map correspondence from def-weil-affine-spectrum, and finite Laurent sums from def-weil-laurent-polynomial-ring. Preservation of the identity is checked by cancellation of the idempotent value in F×. The counit fixes F, so the scalar is 1. Agreement on scalars, t and t^{-1} gives agreement on every finite Laurent sum, and then on scheme morphisms. Thus this is scheme-level equality, not an unsupported promotion of rational-point equality. The cited Laurent definition is sufficient here since the ring in question is the field-coefficient coordinate ring F[t,t^{-1}].
