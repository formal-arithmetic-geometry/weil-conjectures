It uses smooth differential finite freeness, not smoothness of X. Step 2 extends an independent family of actual differentials by actual differentials from their spanning set. `lem-pur-etale-at-point-from-differentials` states precisely that such a basis gives an etale coordinate map over any field, without requiring x closed. The coordinate-zero fibre is a base change, so the etale base-change node applies. The affine-space smoothness, etale-implies-smooth and smooth-composition nodes give smoothness of M_0; none is taken from a mere definition or an uncited Jacobian assertion.

Step 3 is the explicit cokernel calculation with the same quotient and tensor-exactness nodes. I checked the formula d(sum a_j f_j) at x, including the terms that vanish. This proves exactly the specified differential isomorphism. It does not incorrectly require injectivity of I/I² -> Omega for singular X.

Step 4 uses `sbc-thm-etale-local-normal-form` for M_2, then the affine scheme/ring etale equivalence to satisfy the two zero-cotangent hypotheses of `lem-trf-formally-etale-differentials-base-change`. That last node really states the absolute differential base-change isomorphism needed here. Polynomial differentials are free on dT_j by their universal derivation. Minimality identifies both fibres with Omega_X, hence the lifted functions on M_1 have independent spanning differentials. The chosen-coordinate node supplies etaleness of their map. Agreement on X is agreement of all coordinate functions in the quotient ring, not a tangent-space assertion.

Step 5 uses etale base change for both projections. I checked the statements of the affine-source-separatedness, closed-immersion-base-change, clopen etale-section and etale-composition/cancellation nodes. Their hypotheses hold because the fibre products start affine, their projections to X are etale, and the marked diagonal point has exactly the residue field of x. Each section is clopen in a closed fibre, so its complementary component is closed in the ambient fibre product. Removing both complements retains X and produces the two Cartesian closed-pair squares. This repairs the possible gap of claiming that a mere extension map identifies the embedded subschemes.

Step 6 uses the ordinary pointed-neighbourhood existence node and `lem-per-pointed-etale-limit-henselization` solely for existence and its universal-property identification. I checked their whole statements, including the directedness, marked primes and unchanged residue-field hypotheses. The existence node supplies these. For the actual projection maps, `lem-weil-pbc-map-into-henselian-colimit` explicitly permits a single etale algebra, and permits a henselian local target with no module-finiteness restriction; it supplies both existence and uniqueness of the pointed map. The universal property supplies the extensions to local henselizations and the two inverse identities. `lem-trf-henselization-quotient-at-prime` really identifies the quotient by the extended defining ideal with the henselization of X. Consequently the ambient isomorphism has the given quotient restriction. I did not replace this with a statement only about completed rings.

Step 7 invokes the smooth-source conormal fibre sequence specifically for N, which is smooth. This node has arbitrary field k, so it is applicable here. The coordinate chart for N and the formally etale differential comparison give its quotient basis. Lifts of a basis of the conormal fibre complete that basis in the ambient differential fibre, and the chosen-coordinate criterion gives the displayed etale map h. I checked that no regularity/reducedness hypothesis on the original X has entered this argument.

Step 8 constructs the retraction by an actual fibre product with N, removes the complementary closed component by the same section theorem, and identifies the map to N times A^r as a restriction of a base change of h. I corrected the first draft's phrase “is the base change” to “is the restriction of the base change”, since the neighbourhood has been restricted. `lem-sbc-smooth-relative-dimension-permanence` states relative-dimension addition and says explicitly that an etale first map has dimension zero. Together with affine-space smoothness it proves pure relative dimension r, which is a hypothesis of the next step.

Step 9 uses the full statement of `lem-sc-smooth-section-affine-coordinates`: it supplies a centred etale chart, a Cartesian zero-section square, generation of the section ideal by the actual pulled-back coordinates, regularity of that sequence and the section quotient. Its base is an arbitrary scheme, not only a curve or finite-field scheme. The section in Step 8 is closed, and its retraction is smooth of exactly the required relative dimension. Therefore all hypotheses match. Restriction and composition preserve the ambient etale map by the cited etale-composition node.
