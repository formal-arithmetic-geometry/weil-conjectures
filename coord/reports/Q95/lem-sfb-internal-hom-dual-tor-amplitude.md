For lem-sfb-finite-scalar-perfect-refinement: choose a finite affine open cover of the nonempty quasi-compact S. Over each affine, apply the defining fpqc condition of the original covering to choose finitely many affine opens in its members whose images cover that affine. Their combined composites form a covering by 098A or the site axioms/098C; their union is finite, and nonemptiness follows because their images cover nonempty S. Choose the indicated original index for each selected affine. For each of the finitely many chosen bounded scalar complexes choose a finite integer support interval, using [0,0] for a zero complex if necessary. The minimum of the lower endpoints and maximum of the upper endpoints give common a<=b; neither depends on a later tensor test sheaf. Restrict each given derived representation to its selected affine. The constant-sheaf package sends the chosen scalar splitting maps i,p to splitting maps of constant sheaves and identifies the constant finite free module with a finite direct sum of the constant ring sheaf. Termwise scalar extension to B_j sends these to I,P and the equality pi=1 to PI=1, and sends the zero terms to zero terms. Tensor with B_j need not preserve arbitrary exact sequences. These are actual split representatives, not data chosen from a mere undefined existence theorem.

For lem-sfb-split-perfect-dual-tor-window: tensor with B^N is a finite direct sum of the identity functor. Tensor with E^r is its functorial retract through i_r,p_r, and therefore sends a short exact sequence to a direct summand of an exact short complex. Similarly Hom(E^r,-) is a retract of Hom(B^N,-), which is the finite direct sum functor by lem-sc-finite-free-internal-hom-exact. Thus the tensor functor and internal Hom functor out of each E^r are exact. Apply lem-pc-bounded-exact-term-hom-quasi-iso to the K-injective augmentation B[0]->I: boundedness and termwise exactness give the quasi-isomorphism from ordinary Hom(E,B[0]) to Hom(E,I), and the latter is the defining derived internal Hom model. In the Hom product only the term of B[0] in degree zero survives, giving D^n=Hom(E^{-n},B). In the Hom differential d_B phi-(-1)^n phi d_E the first term is zero, giving (-1)^{n+1} phi d_E^{-n-1}. The identity d_E d_E=0 gives d_D d_D=0 with this sign. Outside [-b,-a], E^{-n}=0, hence D^n=0. Precomposition with p_{-n}, respectively i_{-n}, exhibits D^n as a finite-free retract, and the composite applied to phi is phi p_{-n} i_{-n}=phi. The finite-free internal Hom identification turns the middle term into B^{N_{-n}}. The same tensor-retract exactness argument proves D^n flat. Apply implication (2)->(1) of lem-sp-ringed-site-tor-flat-model to E in [a,b] and D in [-b,-a], obtaining both universal vanishings for arbitrary module sheaves M. ## Original proof retained verbatim

Old step 1 (uses: lem-sfb-module-object-ringed-site-derived-comparison):

Use the module-object/ringed-site derived comparison with $X=S$ and $B=\Lambda_S$. It leaves underlying abelian sheaves unchanged, identifies relative derived tensor and internal Hom with their ordinary ringed-site versions, and is compatible with slice restriction. We may therefore work throughout with sheaves of modules over the section-ring sheaf of $B$, retaining the notation $B$. In particular the dual in the question is the ringed-site $R\mathcal Hom_B(P,B)$. No replacement of $B$ by the discrete constant sheaf $\underline R$ is made.

Old step 2 (uses: def-weil-finite-type-morphism, def-weil-pbc-proetale-site):

If $S=\varnothing$, all site objects are empty and their empty covering forces every module sheaf to be zero, so both required vanishings hold with $a=b=0$. Suppose henceforth $S\ne\varnothing$. Finite type over the affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}k$ gives a finite affine open cover of $S$. For each of these affines, the covering condition for $\{U_i\to S\}$ supplies finitely many affine opens in the $U_i$ mapping into that affine and covering it. Combining these finite families gives a finite covering refinement $\{V_j\to S\}_{j\in J}$ subordinate to the original cover. This is a cover by the transitivity axiom of the site. It is nonempty because $S$ is nonempty. On $V_j$, choose the bounded scalar model supplied by its original $U_i$, and choose integers $a_j\le b_j$ outside which that model is zero. Set $a=\min_{j\in J}a_j$ and $b=\max_{j\in J}b_j$. Then $a\le b$ and $[a_j,b_j]\subseteq[a,b]$ for every $j$.

Old step 3 (uses: def-weil-pbc-strict-perfect-coefficient-package, def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules, lem-sc-finite-free-internal-hom-exact):

Write $B_j=B|_{V_j}$ and $E_j^\bullet=B_j\otimes_{\underline R}\underline{F_j^\bullet}$, so $P|_{V_j}\simeq E_j^\bullet$. The strict-perfect coefficient package gives, in each degree $r$, splittings $F_j^r\xrightarrow{i_r}R^{N_r}\xrightarrow{p_r}F_j^r$ with $p_ri_r=1$. Its constant-sheaf construction, followed by scalar extension to $B_j$, gives $E_j^r\xrightarrow{I_r}B_j^{N_r}\xrightarrow{P_r}E_j^r$ with $P_rI_r=1$. Scalar extension preserves this equality by functoriality; it need not be exact or flat. Since tensor with $B_j^{N_r}$ is a finite direct sum of the identity, tensor with its retract $E_j^r$ is exact, so $E_j^r$ is flat. Likewise $\mathcal Hom_{B_j}(E_j^r,-)$ is an exact retract of $\mathcal Hom_{B_j}(B_j^{N_r},-)$, which is the finite direct sum functor by the cited finite-free Hom lemma. Exactness passes to these retracts because the corresponding short complex is a direct summand of an exact short complex.

Old step 4 (uses: lem-weil-pbc-ringed-internal-hom-exact-restriction, lem-pc-bounded-exact-term-hom-quasi-iso, def-pur-derived-internal-hom, def-pur-hom-complex):

Derived internal Hom commutes with slice restriction, hence $P^\vee|_{V_j}\simeq R\mathcal Hom_{B_j}(E_j^\bullet,B_j[0])$. By the bounded exact-term Hom lemma, applying $\mathcal Hom^\bullet(E_j^\bullet,-)$ to a K-injective resolution $B_j[0]\to I^\bullet$ is a quasi-isomorphism: boundedness and the termwise exactness were proved in step 3. By the definition of derived internal Hom, the target computes the displayed derived object. Thus $P^\vee|_{V_j}$ is represented by $D_j^\bullet=\mathcal Hom^\bullet_{B_j}(E_j^\bullet,B_j[0])$. Explicitly $D_j^n=\mathcal Hom_{B_j}(E_j^{-n},B_j)$ and $d_{D_j}^n(\phi)=(-1)^{n+1}\phi\circ d_{E_j}^{-n-1}$. In particular $D_j^n=0$ unless $n\in[-b_j,-a_j]$.

Old step 5 (uses: lem-sc-finite-free-internal-hom-exact, def-pur-sheaf-hom, def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules, lem-sp-ringed-site-tor-flat-model):

Applying $\mathcal Hom_{B_j}(-,B_j)$ to the splitting in step 3 exhibits each $D_j^n$ as a retract of $\mathcal Hom_{B_j}(B_j^{N_{-n}},B_j)\cong B_j^{N_{-n}}$: the inclusion is precomposition with $P_{-n}$, the retraction is precomposition with $I_{-n}$, and their composite is precomposition with $P_{-n}I_{-n}=1$. Hence $D_j^n$ is flat by the same finite-direct-sum and retract argument for tensor used in step 3. Apply implication (2)$\Rightarrow$(1) of the ringed-site flat-model criterion first to $E_j^\bullet$ in $[a_j,b_j]$ and then to $D_j^\bullet$ in $[-b_j,-a_j]$. For every sheaf $M$ of $B_j$-modules, it gives $H^q(P|_{V_j}\otimes_{B_j}^{\mathbf L}M)=0$ outside $[a_j,b_j]$ and $H^q(P^\vee|_{V_j}\otimes_{B_j}^{\mathbf L}M)=0$ outside $[-b_j,-a_j]$.

Old step 6 (uses: lem-pur-site-restriction-module-adjunction-exact, lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-derived-tensor-pullback):

Fix any sheaf $N$ of $B$-modules. Slice restriction is exact, so it commutes with cohomology, and its derived pullback is its termwise restriction. The derived tensor-pullback comparison therefore gives, for $T=P$ and for $T=P^\vee$, $H^q(T\otimes_B^{\mathbf L}N)|_{V_j}\cong H^q(T|_{V_j}\otimes_{B_j}^{\mathbf L}N|_{V_j})$. Taking $M=N|_{V_j}$ in step 5 shows these restrictions vanish for $q\notin[a,b]$ in the first case and $q\notin[-b,-a]$ in the second, since both local intervals are contained in the corresponding global intervals.

Old step 7 (uses: lem-sfb-sheaf-cover-local-isomorphism, sk-def-sfb-constructible):

The family $\{V_j\}$ covers the final object $S$, so its pullbacks cover every object of the small site. Apply the cover-local isomorphism criterion to the map from each of the cohomology sheaves in step 6 to zero, using underlying abelian sheaves: every slice restriction is an isomorphism to zero, hence the original map is an isomorphism. This proves both global vanishings for the arbitrary sheaf $N$. The same integers $a,b$ were fixed before choosing $N$, and the reflected interval is exactly $[-b,-a]$. By the definition of Tor amplitude these are the asserted finite Tor bounds. The comparison in step 1 transports the vanishings back to module objects without changing their cohomology sheaves.
