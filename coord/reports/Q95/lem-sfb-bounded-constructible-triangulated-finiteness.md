Steps 5-6 establish both directions of the finite coefficient identification. The internal-module-in-Ab node supplies the scalar equivalence G, including zero rings. The finite-discrete constructible-encoding theorem supplies the internal-to-intrinsic implication for any scheme. Its statement does not supply the converse, so step 6 proves the converse: enumerate all elements of a finite module to generate it and all elements of the finite kernel as relations; apply lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-constant-exact to that finite presentation; use the restriction-encoding theorem to transport the witnesses on each stratum and each covering member. Neither the Noetherian-scheme hypothesis of lem-sfb-finite-discrete-constructible-model nor an unproved equivalence between the general and constant-model predicates is used.

Step 7 uses lem-weil-proetale-constructible-exact-closure only after both encoding directions are available. There is no inference from an affine-local partition to a global finite partition on an arbitrary scheme.

Step 8 uses lem-sbc-cohomology-in-weak-serre-subcategory. Its statement is for any abelian category and explicitly includes the bounded version and inherited triangles; it needs zero, strict fullness, kernels, cokernels and extensions, all supplied earlier. def-sfb-bounded-constructible-complexes specifies the identical full subcategory with bounded cohomology and the same module predicate, with no completeness condition. Thus applying the generic theorem proves the item under exactly its existing hypotheses. It does not require a finite Tor amplitude or a stronger geometric finiteness assumption.
