### lem-trf-fibre-generic-localization-residue-flat

Returned step 1:

Fix $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}R$, put $A=R_{\mathfrak p}$, $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak pA$, $C=(R\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}B$, and $F=B\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. By [def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime], $A$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ and $C/\mathfrak mC=F$. By hypothesis $F$ is a nonzero domain. The map from the field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ into $F$ is injective: its kernel is an ideal of a field and cannot contain $1$. Thus the inverse image $\mathfrak q=\ker(B\to F)$ is a prime with contraction $\mathfrak p$. Every element of $R\setminus\mathfrak p$ therefore becomes a unit in $D=B_{\mathfrak q}$, giving the canonical map $u:C\to D$.

Returned step 3:

The $A$-module $D$ is flat. Indeed, for an injection of $A$-modules $L_1\to L_2$, regard it as an injection of $R$-modules and tensor with the flat $R$-module $B$ [def-weil-pbc-flat-ring-map]. Localizing this injection of $B$-modules at $T=B\setminus\mathfrak q$ is still injective: if $x/t$ maps to zero, some $v\in T$ kills its image numerator, so injectivity before localization gives $vx=0$ and $x/t=0$ [def-weil-pbc-module-localization]. Identify $T^{-1}(L_i\otimes_R B)$ with $L_i\otimes_A D$ by $(l\otimes b)/t\mapsto l\otimes(b/t)$, with inverse $l\otimes(b/t)\mapsto(l\otimes b)/t$. Balancing over $A$ follows because each $r\notin\mathfrak p$ acts invertibly on $L_i$, with $r^{-1}l\otimes b=l\otimes(b/r)$ in the localized tensor product. The maps are inverse on generators [def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product]. The resulting injection is exactly the tensor of $L_1\to L_2$ with $D$, proving the claim.

Returned step 4:

The reduction of $u$ modulo $\mathfrak m$ is injective. In fact $D/\mathfrak mD$ is the localization of $F=C/\mathfrak mC$ at the images of $B\setminus\mathfrak q$. The identification sends the class of $b/t$ to $\bar b/\bar t$; its inverse sends $\bar c/\bar t$, with $c=b/r\in C$, to the class of $b/(rt)$. Changing $c$ by an element of $\mathfrak mC$ changes this fraction by an element of $\mathfrak mD$, and clearing denominators verifies both inverse equations [def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime, def-weil-pbc-module-localization]. Every such $\bar t$ is nonzero by the definition of $\mathfrak q$. Thus $F\to D/\mathfrak mD$ is injective: $\bar b/1=0$ means $\bar t\bar b=0$ for one nonzero $\bar t$ in the domain $F$, whence $\bar b=0$. This is the actual reduction map of $u$, not an arbitrary injection of the two rings.

### lem-trf-projective-local-flat-family-injectivity

Returned step 2:

Apply [lem-trf-affine-geometrically-integral-projective] to the given $R\to B$. Its hypotheses are exactly the flatness, finite presentation and all-field-extension domain hypotheses of the item. Consequently choose a free $R$-module $E=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R$ and maps $j:B\to E$, $s:E\to B$ with $sj=1$, using [def-weil-pbc-projective-module]. Localize these maps at $R\setminus\mathfrak p$; the fraction formulas preserve $sj=1$. The localized free module is $\bigoplus_{i\in I}A$: the isomorphism sends $(r_i)/t$ to $(r_i/t)$, and its inverse uses the product of the denominators of the finitely many nonzero coordinates. Hence $C$ is a direct summand of a free $A$-module, and is projective over $A$ by the same definition.

Returned step 5:

This proves the local inclusion used in the final paragraph of the source without introducing another node for that inclusion.

Returned step 6:

Let $b\in B$ map to zero in every $B_{\mathfrak q_{\mathfrak p}}$. Step 5 shows that $b/1=0$ in $(R\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}B$ for every prime $\mathfrak p\subset R$. If $b\ne0$, its $R$-annihilator $J=\{r\in R:rb=0\}$ is proper, since $1\notin J$. By [lem-weil-pbc-proper-ideal-contained-in-maximal] choose a maximal ideal $\mathfrak p$ containing $J$; it is a prime. The fraction criterion [def-weil-pbc-module-localization] applied to $b/1=0$ supplies $r\notin\mathfrak p$ with $rb=0$. This puts $r\in J\subseteq\mathfrak p$, a contradiction. Hence $b=0$. The kernel of the displayed product homomorphism is zero, which proves its injectivity (apply the zero-kernel assertion to the difference of two elements with equal images). If $R$ is the zero ring then so is its unital algebra $B$ and the empty-product map is injective as well.

For the module-family statement, localization of the splitting of M yields projectivity of M_p by exactly the old finite-support denominator argument. Apply the existing 05FQ criterion to each u_p, then take the tensor test module A_p. If x lies in the kernel of all v_p, those local injections give x/1=0 in each M_p. The proper annihilator J of a hypothetical nonzero x is contained in a maximal prime p; the zero-fraction equation provides r outside p killing x, contradicting J contained in p. For R=0 every unital module is zero. These are the old steps with module variables, including their hypotheses and the actual fraction formulas, rather than a shorter replacement argument.
