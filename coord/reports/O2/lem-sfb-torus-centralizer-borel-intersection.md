## Review by O2

I read the item against Milne 17.46 word for word, including the closed transporter footnote and the two displayed conjugation identities. Its field is algebraically closed; G and B are smooth connected affine groups of finite type; S need not be maximal. The scheme-theoretic statements require testing all algebra extensions, and the proof retains those tests. The result 17.72 confirms the same interpretation. There are no new node statements or new source claims to review.

For each step I opened the statement of every cited node (the full exact statements are also in citations.json), checked its hypotheses, and compared what the step takes from it:

1. `torus-centralizer-smooth-connected` supplies actual representing smooth geometrically connected closed subgroups, not just their rational points, for both G and B. The algebraic-closure connectedness test verifies its hypothesis over F. The fibre-product definition identifies H with the intersection on all algebras. `smooth-connected-derived-subgroup` supplies the derived subgroups; minimality of the commutator subgroup gives D^iH contained in D^iB and hence solvability. This is not an application of a subgroup-solvability theorem absent from the citations.
2. `homogeneous-orbit-quotient-geometry` gives the represented quotient, local lifts and coset-fibre identity; `borel-quotient-proper-geometry` gives properness once its quotient hypotheses are verified. I explicitly checked finite presentation with the finite-type-to-separated-target, Noetherian finite-type, locally-finite-type-to-finite-presentation and qcqs permanence statements. The base field is Noetherian because it has only two ideals. `smooth-flat` is explicitly cited before the flat-lfp-open result is applied. `smooth-group-orbit-geometry` gives a locally closed reduced orbit for the smooth connected C even though a general stabilizer need not be smooth. Its stabilizer is exactly H on all algebras; the homogeneous quotient statement then gives O isomorphic to C/H. This avoids the circular flag-variety node.
3. The derived-subgroup node converts the commutator-series definition to the normal-series definition used by `perfect-solvable-unipotent-torus`, so that no change of meaning of solvable is hidden. F is perfect, and B satisfies all of that node's hypotheses. `multiplicative-type-largest-subtorus` gives smoothness/connectedness of tori; `solvable-normal-subgroup-products` supplies dimension bounds and strict dimension growth. Thus S can be enlarged to a maximal torus of B. `solvable-torus-complements` says exactly that the restriction to this maximal torus is an isomorphism onto D; it is not cited for conjugacy, which it does not state. The decomposition b=ut is explicitly computed, and kernel membership is checked using the sheaf quotient and faithful flatness of its local tests.
4. The homogeneous quotient node permits lifting geometric cosets, so the inverse image of the C-orbit really is the image of multiplication CB. The geometrically-connected-product-components statement applies to C times B, with C geometrically connected. Image connectedness and connectedness of closure are proved in the step using the definition. The reduction and closed-immersion nodes give the reduced closed affine scheme Z; its coordinate algebra is a quotient of a finitely generated F-algebra.
5. The constant-source closed-factorization-locus statement applies with source S, parameter G, target G and closed subscheme B. I checked separatedness from `group-scheme-separated-field`. The conjugation formula on C times B satisfies the universal property after every extension. The reduced-source factorization node, whose statement assumes reduced source, is applied only to Z with its deliberately chosen reduced structure. Thus the conjugation family into B is a morphism, not an unsupported inference from rational-point containment.
6. `diagonalizable-group-geometric-criteria` identifies split tori with D_F(Z^r). The `diagonalizable-hom-family-rigidity` statement allows a connected affine finite-type parameter scheme, with neither smoothness nor reducedness assumed. Z has those hypotheses and the point 1. The group-homomorphism condition is checked by conjugation followed by the quotient homomorphism. This gives the required identity on arbitrary algebra-valued points, not just F-points.
7. Right translation by a fixed b in B(F) is an isomorphism and preserves q's fibres, hence M, its closure and its reduced structure. This is the explicitly written elementary continuity argument, not a new group-theoretic input.
8. The same dimension statements enlarge y^{-1}Sy to a maximal torus of B. The existing `borel-maximal-torus-conjugacy` statement applies to the ambient group B itself, which is smooth connected; its maximal-torus clause is independent of reductivity. It supplies b with b^{-1}T_yb=T as subgroup schemes. The decomposition from step 3 yields b=ut, and the displayed conjugation by t removes t. No claim about unipotent conjugators is taken from a node that only asserts ordinary conjugacy.
9. The earlier all-algebra rigidity identity applies at yu because step 7 places that F-point in Z. Step 8 and S contained in T put both arguments in T on every algebra extension. The torus complement isomorphism is injective on those points, so yu commutes with S scheme-theoretically. This is an elementary assembly of earlier steps, as recorded.
10. A locally closed subset is open in its closure, so the possible boundary Z minus M is closed. The Jacobson closed-point density node gives a closed point if it is nonempty; the algebraically-closed residue-field node turns it into an F-point. This contradicts step 9. The quotient-map argument for the open surjection is written out. A locally closed immersion with closed support is a closed immersion by the ideal-sheaf gluing argument in the step. The closed-immersion-proper node then applies to O inside proper Q. No properness of G or of Z is assumed.
11. `solvable-proper-fixed-point` applies to J acting on the nonempty proper finite-type scheme O. It gives a scheme-theoretic fixed point and hence, by the quotient's rational lifting statement, a c in C(F). The coset-fibre identity gives c^{-1}Jc contained in H on every algebra. Strict dimension growth for smooth connected subgroups rules out H strictly contained in J. Thus maximality is proved rather than relabelled as a new lemma.

I tested the argument in the cases S=1 (C=G and CB=G), S central (again C=G), G=B solvable (C contained in B and H=C), G a torus (B=G), and G=1. The zero-rank torus is allowed at every use of the torus results. For the upper triangular Borel in GL_2, a diagonal subtorus with unequal weights has its usual diagonal centralizer, while a scalar subtorus has centralizer GL_2; the intersection conclusion holds in both cases. No characteristic-zero hypothesis was introduced, and nonreduced test algebras remain allowed in steps 1, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 11.
