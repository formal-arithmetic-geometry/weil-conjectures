For the new node itself, the source I read was Stacks 0ETT, final paragraph. For a point y of Y outside U, 0CM2 supplies a trait whose generic point lies in U and whose closed point maps to y. On its base-changed finite-type scheme, universal submersivity forces a specialization from the generic fibre to the closed fibre: otherwise the closed fibre is stable under generalization and is constructible, hence open in this Noetherian scheme, contradicting the quotient topology on the trait. Every closed subset containing the generic preimage therefore meets that fibre. Mapping this point to X gives a point of Z over y. Points in U are immediate from surjectivity. This is a non-elementary support lemma, strictly smaller than finite generation, and so it is a new unsplit statement, not an elementary node or an item clause. Its future proof will need the trait and constructibility inputs; they have not been smuggled into its statement as a proof sketch.

Step 4 uses the affine form of the closed-immersion definition, the 02KL flat-source descent statement, and the all-affine-pairs finite-type criterion. The strict transform on each chart is a quotient of C tensor_B B_i, generated over C by finitely many ratio variables, hence finite type over k. Thus the composite to Spec(k) is locally finite type. Surjectivity was proved in step 3; flatness and local finite presentation came from step 1. Source descent gives local finite type of T, and the affine-pair node gives finite generation of every B_i. Neither flat base-change descent nor a finite map is substituted here.

Step 5 is the written elementary coefficient argument: adjoining the finite lists of coefficients and the f_i makes A[J/f_i] contain a generating set of B_i, and the reverse inclusion is immediate. There is no unproved assertion that finite type descends along an arbitrary filtered colimit. Step 6 uses the chart cover and the canonical homogeneous localization overlap node; the affine chart-map definition identifies D_+(f_i t) with the fraction chart. Both overlaps invert the same fraction f_j/f_i, and both maps are the identity in Frac(B). This writes the gluing identification and its structure map explicitly. These are short computations with the cited charts, so no extra comparison lemma is made.

Step 7 uses Noetherian permanence, H-projectivity of blowups over a projective affine base (with X = S = Spec(A), identity embedded in P^0_A), the coherent finite-type quasi-coherent criterion for O_T, and the q=0 clause of projective coherent finiteness. A is Noetherian because it is finite type over the field; T is locally Noetherian by its already identified charts. This supplies exactly the coherent-module and projectivity hypotheses. The weaker projective-bundle convention is not silently passed to the H-projective cohomology node.

Step 8 uses the Noetherian-submodule-finiteness statement. B embeds into the finite A-module of global sections because on any nonempty fraction chart its restriction is the inclusion into Frac(B). It then spells out why finite module generators over the finitely generated k-algebra A are algebra generators over k. This is not an application of Artin--Tate without its module-finiteness hypothesis: the needed finiteness has been proved here.

Step 9 is the explicit quotient/base-change application of the integral auxiliary argument. Quotient rings are Noetherian because ideals lift to ideals of R; E/pE is a quotient of a finite-type k-algebra, and its further base changes are exactly the original R-algebra base changes. The auxiliary proof did not require B to inject into C, so the passage to R/p is valid, including possible nonreduced E/pE.

Step 10 uses the existing prime-filtration theorem precisely with M = R, a cyclic finite R-module. It chooses a single finite-type k-subalgebra D surjecting onto all the finitely many quotient rings R/p_i. These are cyclic D-modules. Lifting one generator of each successive quotient and subtracting its D-multiple proves finite generation up the filtration, and adjoining these generators to the algebra generators of D proves the conclusion. This also covers nilpotents and reducible R without a hidden reduction theorem.
