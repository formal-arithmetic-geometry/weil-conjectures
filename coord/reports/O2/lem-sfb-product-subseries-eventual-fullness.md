Step 1 uses the full fibre criterion in lem-weil-pbc-invertible-generating-sections-fibre, the invertibility and fibre-product clauses in lem-weil-pbc-invertible-pullback-tensor, and properness in lem-pc-projective-proper. The pure products of finite bases span S, so the cited finite-family fibre criterion applies. The line's tensor powers exist as invertible modules. No claim about all global sections being generated by degree-one products is made here.

Step 2 uses def-weil-closed-immersion for the surjection on local rings and def-sc-scheme-tangent-space for tangent vectors, with the elementary local coordinate calculation written out. For an embedded curve, distinct projective vectors are separated by a linear form; a surjection on local rings makes coordinate-ratio differences detect every nonzero tangent vector. Multiplication by a factor nonzero at the relevant point preserves point separation. In a local frame the derivative of v times the other factors is precisely their value times dv because v vanishes. The nonzero factor is chosen at y for two-point separation and at x for tangent separation, which are different choices and must not be confused. The complete statement of lem-sc-point-tangent-separation-closed-immersion supplies exactly generation plus the closed embedding and polarization after these hypotheses are checked. def-weil-pbc-morphism-to-projective-space-by-sections fixes the coordinate normalization, so a coordinate pulls back to the corresponding section of S. Properness is from step 1. This argument also proves the separation conditions for the complete linear system if an ampleness convention requires that observation, but we use the actual closed embedding directly.

Step 3 takes only the restriction-surjectivity sentence of lem-sfb-projective-polarization-section-algebra, applied to projective space itself with O(1). Its hypothesis of a positive-power closed embedding is met by the identity with exponent one. The closed subscheme is j(C), with the scheme structure provided by the closed immersion, not merely its point-set image. lem-pc-projective-space-twist-cohomology gives the actual monomial/symmetric-power description, not just a dimension count. lem-pc-twist-tensor and lem-weil-pbc-invertible-pullback-tensor supply compatible twist powers and pullbacks. On each homogeneous monomial, restriction is the product of its coordinate sections in S; linearity proves equality with the required multiplication map. This avoids an unjustified inference from equality of dimensions. The rank-one P^0 case is explicitly covered; in fact a nonempty one-dimensional curve cannot embed there.

Step 4 applies the existing closed-immersion Serre-vanishing theorem, not a definition of ampleness or a source tag alone. The base ring is the field k, hence Noetherian; j is the closed immersion already constructed. Its sheaf O_C is quasi-coherent and finitely presented as a module over itself: it has the local one-generator free presentation. The theorem vanishes every positive cohomology degree after a sufficiently large restricted twist. The two tensor-identification statements identify that twist with M^{tensor n}; hence h^0 equals the Euler characteristic. No smoothness or positivity assumption has silently been inserted into this vanishing theorem.
