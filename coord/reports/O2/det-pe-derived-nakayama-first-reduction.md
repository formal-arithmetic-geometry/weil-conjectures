### lem-pe-principal-reduction-cone-calculus

Source: literature/blueprint/main-20261015/blueprint/sec-proetale-coefficients/sk-lem-pe-derived-nakayama/sk-lem-pe-derived-nakayama-first-reduction.proof.md; Cones paragraph, first two sentences and the cone/shift commutation sentence; explicit strict principal-reduction comparison. Supporting cone convention: Stacks Tag 014E (definition-cone).

Proof (2101 characters):

1. By [det-pe-derived-nakayama-resolution] the quotient is resolved by $[R\xrightarrow{\pi^n}R]$. Its transition to the preceding resolution is $\pi$ in degree $-1$ and $1$ in degree $0$: the chain equation is $\pi^n\pi=\pi^{n+1}$ and the augmentation is the quotient map. Thus the reduction, transition and unit are exactly $D_n(M)$, $(x,y)\mapsto(x,\pi y)$ and $j_n(m)=(m,0)$, not independent identifications at each level. This verifies the strict tower of [sk-def-pe-derived-completion].
2. For $C=\operatorname{Cone}(f)$ the source differential on $(b,a,b',a')$ is $(d_Bb+fa+\pi^nb',-d_Aa+\pi^na',-d_Bb'-fa',d_Aa')$. Its signed permutation $(b,b',a,-a')$ has exactly the differential of $\operatorname{Cone}(D_n(f))$, by [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]. The permutation is its own inverse after reordering. Cone inclusion gives $(b,0,b',0)$, which maps to $(b,b',0,0)$. On the boundary, $D_n(-p)$ gives $(-a,-a')$ and the shift comparison changes this to $(-a,a')$, the negative projection of the permuted tuple. The transition multiplies both primed coordinates by $\pi$, so commutes with the permutation. Applied to $j_C$, the primed coordinates are zero and it becomes the cone map of $(j_A,j_B)$.
3. Direct substitution in $d(x,y)=(dx+\pi^ny,-dy)$ proves that $(x,y)\mapsto(x,(-1)^r y)$ is the shift comparison. Its zero and addition laws are $(-1)^0=1$ and $(-1)^{r+s}=(-1)^r(-1)^s$. It fixes $(m,0)$, commutes with transitions, and is natural in chain maps. These checks prove all stated compatibilities.
4. For a quasi-isomorphism $a$, its natural scalar square gives a morphism between the two distinguished scalar cone triangles; the first two vertical maps are $Qa$, hence the third $QD_n(a)$ is an isomorphism by [lem-sf-triangle-morphism-iso]. The localization property [lem-weil-pbc-localization-universal-property] therefore descends $D_n$ and its natural comparisons. Additivity is coordinatewise. Every distinguished triangle is isomorphic to a localized cone triangle [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]; step 2 identifies its image with a cone triangle, proving exactness.


### lem-pe-roos-cone-calculus

Source: literature/blueprint/main-20261015/blueprint/sec-proetale-coefficients/sk-lem-pe-derived-nakayama/sk-lem-pe-derived-nakayama-first-reduction.proof.md; Cones paragraph, Roos-fibre sentence and cone/shift commutation sentence. Supporting sources: Stacks Tag 08TC (definition-derived-limit) and Tag 07KC (lemma-products).

Proof (2134 characters):

1. Products and their diagonal maps are functorial by [def-weil-category-product]. Tower compatibility gives $s_{N'}P(f)=P(f)s_N$, so the displayed diagonal map on $H$ commutes with the differential, identities and composition. The cone triangle of [def-weil-abstract-derived-category], rotated by TR2 [def-weil-triangulated-category] with its sign isomorphism, is $QH(N)\to QP(N)\xrightarrow{s_N}QP(N)\to QH(N)[1]$. By [det-pe-products-derived], $QP(N)$ is the product of the $QN_n$. This is exactly [def-weil-derived-limit], with no arbitrary choice of a map into an abstract triangle.
2. A levelwise quasi-isomorphism $f$ gives a quasi-isomorphism $P(f)$ by [det-pe-products-derived]. The two Roos triangles and their diagonal maps form a morphism of triangles by the cone differential formulas; their product components are isomorphisms. Thus $QH(f)$ is an isomorphism by [lem-sf-triangle-morphism-iso]. This uses neither boundedness nor surjectivity of transitions.
3. The product universal property [def-weil-category-product] identifies $P(\operatorname{Cone}(f))$ with $\operatorname{Cone}(P(f))$: the inverse maps are $((b_n,a_n))\mapsto((b_n),(a_n))$ and $((b_n),(a_n))\mapsto((b_n,a_n))$. In the stated source order the differential on $H(\operatorname{Cone}(f))$ is $(db+fa,-da,-db'-fa'-s_{N'}b,da'-s_Na)$. Signed permutation gives the target differential on $(b,b',a,-a')$. Inclusions give $(b,0,b',0)\mapsto(b,b',0,0)$. The source boundary has coordinates $(-a,-a')$; after the shift comparison these are $(-a,a')$, exactly the target negative projection. Naturality follows from diagonal maps and $s_{N'}P(f)=P(f)s_N$.
4. Substitution into $d(v,u)=(dv,-du-s_Nv)$ proves the shift formula $(v,u)\mapsto(v,(-1)^r u)$; multiplying signs proves its zero and addition laws. A compatible augmentation satisfies $s_N(j_n(m))_n=0$, so $m\mapsto((j_n(m))_n,0)$ is a chain map. In the cone comparison the zero fibre coordinates remain zero and the two nonzero coordinates are the given cone augmentations. In the shift comparison the zero fibre coordinate is fixed. This proves the augmentation identities, including their signs.


### lem-pe-completion-triangle-comparison

Source: literature/blueprint/main-20261015/blueprint/sec-proetale-coefficients/sk-lem-pe-derived-nakayama/sk-lem-pe-derived-nakayama-first-reduction.proof.md; Cones paragraph, sentence that the three canonical completion maps form a morphism of distinguished triangles; shifts sentence. Localization convention: Stacks Tag 05RU (definition-unbounded-derived-category).

Proof (2272 characters):

1. Compose the strict functors $D_\bullet$ of [lem-pe-principal-reduction-cone-calculus] and $H$ of [lem-pe-roos-cone-calculus]. The first sends every quasi-isomorphism to a levelwise quasi-isomorphism and the second sends these to quasi-isomorphisms. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-localization-universal-property] with source the category of complexes, target $D(R)$ and localization $Q$ [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]. It gives $F$ and descends all the natural transformations and isomorphisms below, including their equalities. Diagonal maps show additivity.
2. The principal-reduction lemma supplies the actual quotient tower and compatible $j_n(m)=(m,0)$. The Roos lemma makes $c_M(m)=(((m,0))_n,0)$ a natural chain map into its derived limit, hence it represents the canonical reduction-completion map of [sk-def-pe-derived-completion]. This fixes the strict augmentation, rather than choosing a lift solely from its evaluations. Compose the two lemmas' shift comparisons to obtain $\sigma_{M,r}$. Both are natural and coherent. Each fixes every zero second coordinate of $c$, so $\sigma_{M,r}c_{M[r]}=c_M[r]$.
3. For a chain map $g:A\to B$, apply the reduction cone comparison levelwise and then the Roos cone comparison. Their composite identifies $F(\operatorname{Cone}(g))$ with $\operatorname{Cone}(F(g))$, preserves the inclusion and boundary with $\sigma$, and sends $c_{\operatorname{Cone}(g)}$ to the cone map of $(c_A,c_B)$. These conclusions are in the statements of the two cited lemmas. Thus the localized cone triangle has distinguished image and its three $c$ maps form a triangle morphism.
4. Any distinguished triangle $T$ is isomorphic to such a localized cone triangle $T_0$ by [def-weil-abstract-derived-category]. Write its isomorphism as $(a,b,e):T_0\xrightarrow{\sim}T$. Naturality of $\sigma$ identifies the image triangles by $(F(a),F(b),F(e))$, including the connecting square, so $F(T)$ is distinguished by TR1 [def-weil-triangulated-category]. Naturality of $c$ gives $c_K=F(a)c_Aa^{-1}$, $c_L=F(b)c_Bb^{-1}$ and $c_C=F(e)c_{\operatorname{Cone}(g)}e^{-1}$. Conjugating the triangle morphism of step 3 therefore gives exactly $(c_K,c_L,c_C)$, including its last square with $c_K[1]$. This proves the assertion for every distinguished triangle.
