## New node: why the integration statement is true

The new statement concerns an arbitrary finite set and an arbitrary commutative coefficient group, with a function on disjoint triples; it mentions no scheme, line bundle, induced cubical structure, transposition of a nine-variable array, or cubical morphism. It is the local algebraic input in step 3, not the requested geometric conclusion moved into another node. Here is a complete argument for the later worker; it is kept out of the statement-only node.

Work multiplicatively. A third alternating difference of any function c on subsets is symmetric, normalized, and satisfies the four-disjoint-block cocycle: expand the factors, and the two sides have identical c factors. Construct c by increasing cardinality of subsets, with c=1 on cardinalities at most two. Maintain that t divided by the third difference of c is 1 for triples with total union of size less than n; values of c on larger subsets have not been fixed and may temporarily be 1. This residual, say q, still has the symmetry, normalization and cocycle identities. For each S with |S|=n, consider its partitions into three nonempty blocks. For four nonempty disjoint blocks A,B,C,D with union S, q(A,B,D)=q(B,C,D)=1 by the induction hypothesis, so the cocycle gives

q(A union B,C,D) = q(A,B union C,D).

With symmetry this says the value on a three-block partition does not change when one element is moved from a block of size at least two into another block. The graph of unordered three-block partitions is connected. Explicitly choose distinct a,b in S and target blocks {a},{b},S minus {a,b}. First separate a and b if they lie in the same block: transfer b to one of the other nonempty blocks (the source still contains a). Transfer every other element from a's block to the third block, leaving {a}. Transfer every element other than b from b's block to the third block, leaving {b}. Each transfer has a nonempty source remainder and two other nonempty blocks, so it is an instance of the displayed four-block equality; symmetry lets us relabel the blocks. For n=3 there is only one unordered partition and no transfer is needed. Thus q is constant on all three-block partitions of S. Define c(S) to be that constant, multiplying any temporary value of c(S) if necessary. The coefficient of c(S) in the third difference for those triples is +1; every other subset involved is proper in S. This makes the residual 1 on all triples with union S, without changing any smaller union. Distinct subsets of size n do not interfere. Induction proves the formula for all triples; triples with an empty block follow by cancellation. No roots, division by an integer, divisibility, freeness or characteristic assumption is used.
