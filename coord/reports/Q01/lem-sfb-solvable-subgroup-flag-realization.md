I reviewed the eight steps against the open node statements and sources, not against their names or the content hidden in their proofs. The new node is self-contained, with a nonzero finite-dimensional space, a group-scheme representation, the all-algebras meaning of stability, the terminating commutator convention, and explicit smoothness, connectedness and algebraic-closedness hypotheses. Step 1 uses exactly Chevalley's all-algebras equality, the prescribed-kernel theorem with N=1, and the homomorphism factorization identifying its image with the fppf quotient. Affineness, finite type and closedness of H are present; G and GL(W) are separated, as required by the factorization. Quotienting the sheaf by the trivial subgroup is the original group, so this proves a closed immersion rather than merely injectivity on field points. I specifically corrected the initial plan after the existing Chevalley node became available: no second such node is created.

Step 2 is elementary affine matrix algebra. The direct summand retains the exact line stabilizer on every algebra. The closed graph over s(G), followed by the block diagonal inclusion, makes the representation faithful. I wrote the equations and the block-determinant observation rather than assuming a direct-sum representation is automatically a closed immersion. The trivial group and H=G cases are included, and E contains a line even when G is trivial.

Step 3 applies the new node only to nonzero modules in the induction; the zero module is handled as the base case. H remains the same smooth connected solvable group throughout, and the quotient module has a rational action defined by the lower-right matrix block. Taking preimages is valid after every scalar extension because these are split vector-space exact sequences. This explicitly proves the flag in V/L and its lifts; it does not put the entire flag clause into a new node. Preservation of V_1=L gives the reverse inclusion in the stabilizer without relying on density of F-points.

Step 4 uses the Grassmannian node's precise universal-quotient statement, with quotient rank n-i rather than i, to obtain rank-i kernels. The local splitting and quotient argument explains why it represents locally direct summands. The incidence equations are the matrix entries of S_i to Q_{i+1}; their ideals are frame-independent. Pulling those ideals back is equivalent to vanishing even over nonreduced algebras. The argument that successive quotients remain locally free is written out. This is one routine incidence argument, not an unnamed independent theorem. In dimensions n=1 and n=2 the empty product or empty incidence condition gives Spec F or P^1, respectively.

Step 5 uses the Grassmannian node's actual Plücker closed embeddings and the Segre node's actual closed embedding into one projective space. The affine ideal-sum and surjective-ring-map arguments justify products and compositions of closed immersions here. It then applies projective-implies-proper, and the proper definition includes finite type; it does not silently extract finiteness from projectivity. This works for the empty closed subscheme and for nonreduced closed subschemes as well as the reduced ones required by the item. The two off-unit statements, Segre and projective-implies-proper, are generic geometric tools with no unit-specific replacement, and are declared in split_report.tool_uses.

Step 6 describes the stabilizer by actual below-diagonal equations, hence a finite-type closed subgroup, before applying the identity-component node. That node explicitly states smoothness and connectedness of the reduced identity component over an algebraically closed field, without assuming K reduced; its hypotheses are met. Reduction and restriction to the open identity component commute since both are computed locally by the nilradical. This identifies the two orders of operations. A zero-dimensional representation can only be faithful for G=1 and was treated separately. Infinitesimal subgroup schemes such as mu_p can occur in K and cause no problem; we never claim K itself smooth.

The quotient embeds in a torus, hence is commutative, and I wrote the induction from a normal series to the terminating derived series explicitly to match the item's solvability convention. No theorem about characteristic-zero Lie algebras is used.

This proves dim J=dim K without assuming equal local tangent dimensions or reducedness. Zero-dimensional stabilizers, including nonreduced ones, have dimension zero and are covered.
