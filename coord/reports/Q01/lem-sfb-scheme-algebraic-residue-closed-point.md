Step 1 uses lem-weil-pbc-affine-opens-basis exactly for affine neighbourhoods inside a prescribed open, twice, and def-weil-affine-spectrum exactly for the ring map corresponding to U -> V. I checked that z is a specialization of y, so an open about z contains y; choosing a chart merely about y would not suffice. Step 2 uses the closure formula in clause (1) of lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-closed-subsets-primes and the precise existence assertion of lem-weil-pbc-proper-ideal-contained-in-maximal to prove that the base prime is maximal. It uses clauses (1),(2) of lem-weil-pbc-spec-field-point-residue-field for the affine residue-field identifications and embedding of B/q in kappa(y). The induced map A -> B/q has kernel p because it represents the point y mapping to s. The field A/p therefore embeds in B/q. Since A/p is already a field, its fraction-field identification is canonical. def-weil-residue-field states that the structural map is the one induced by the local stalk map: the section/germ/quotient composites here give exactly that map, not an arbitrary field embedding. This is the only non-Mathlib tool node outside the unit, declared in split_report.tool_uses; it is generic scheme infrastructure, not a different six-functor proof route.

Step 3 uses only the definition of algebraicity and the embeddings established in step 2. I expanded the subring-is-a-field assertion from 0BID instead of citing a tag without a node. For nonzero b, cancelling the initial power of b makes the constant coefficient nonzero; its inverse belongs to the base field contained in C. The displayed inverse lies in C and proves C is a field. I also wrote the ideal argument showing q is maximal, so no unquoted quotient-field/maximal-ideal criterion is hidden here.

Step 4 uses exactly the same spectrum-closure clause to turn specialization into inclusion q <= r. Maximality and properness give equality. Because z was arbitrary in the global closure, this proves the global closedness, including for nonseparated schemes, without a Jacobson assertion.

Small-case checks: the existence of y excludes an empty source and empty chosen charts; an empty ambient scheme has no such y. Infinite algebraic and purely inseparable extensions work, since the polynomial inverse needs neither finite degree of the extension nor separability. Nilpotents disappear in B/q, a domain embedded in kappa(y), without a reducedness hypothesis. On a doubled-origin scheme each specialization is still handled by a chart about that specialization; no global separation is used. Dropping the closed-base-point hypothesis fails for the identity on Spec k[t] at its generic point. Dropping algebraicity fails for the generic point of A^1_k over Spec k. These tests verify the necessity and orientation of the assumptions rather than adding any.
