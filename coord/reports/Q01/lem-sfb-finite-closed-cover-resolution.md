## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the expression “every X minus Z and X minus Z_a is a quasi-compact open” can mean only that the open subspace is quasi-compact. This does not imply that its inclusion is a quasi-compact morphism on a non-quasi-separated scheme. Stacks 09AH (proetale-lemma-ses-associated-to-open) expressly assumes the latter, and 09BM (proetale-lemma-closed-immersion-complement-retrocompact-exact) assumes a retrocompact complement. Corrected this phrase to say that the complementary open immersions are quasi-compact morphisms. Added def-weil-finite-type-morphism and def-pc-fibre-product for the morphism convention and actual intersections. All conclusions are retained.

Explicit test of the weaker interpretation: let R=k[x_1,x_2,...] and W=union_n D(x_n) in Spec R. W is not quasi-compact: no finite subfamily covers, since the prime generated by a prescribed finite set of the variables misses another variable. Glue two copies of Spec R along W. Each copy U is an affine, hence quasi-compact, open of X; its intersection with the other affine chart is W. Thus U→X is not a quasi-compact morphism. Its closed complement has a scheme structure: use the unit ideal on U and the ideal (x_1,x_2,...) on the other chart, which restrict to the same unit ideal on W. Therefore the weaker reading really fails the cited lemma's hypothesis, even though X is a scheme and U a quasi-compact open space. This is a hypothesis/type check, not a claimed counterexample to every possible version of closed-cover descent. No quasi-separatedness or global affine hypothesis has been silently inserted. The correction states exactly what is needed and what the original dependency assumed.
