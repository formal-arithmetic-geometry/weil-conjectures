### Old Step 1

Put $\mathcal A=\mathcal P^{op}$ and $\mathcal B=\mathcal D^{op}$. A presheaf is a covariant module functor on the corresponding non-opposite category. The bar theorem identifies the stated derived colimits with derived colimits on these small categories. To use the simplicial-computation theorem, identify its first-vertex bar convention with the bar theorem's last-vertex convention: their differentials in degree $-n$ differ by $(-1)^n$, and multiplication by $\lambda_n=(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}$ is a chain isomorphism since $\lambda_n=(-1)^n\lambda_{n-1}$. This is the identity in degree zero and commutes with every reindexing map and augmentation. Work in these identified models. Choose the standard polynomial resolution $P_\bullet\to B$ supplied by the resolution-coherence theorem, within the fixed size bound, and set $U_n=(P_n\to B,P_n[E]\to C)$. Each is an object of $\mathcal A$, and extending every simplicial operator by the identity on $E$ makes $U_\bullet$ a simplicial object. Transport to the chosen small representatives by isomorphisms when necessary.

### Old Step 2

The resolution-coherence theorem says that every $P_n\to B$ is surjective and $s(P_\bullet)\to B[0]$ is a quasi-isomorphism. For constant $B$, the alternating differential is zero in odd positive chain degree and the identity in even positive degree, so $s(\operatorname{const} B)\to B[0]$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Therefore $s(P_\bullet)\to s(\operatorname{const} B)$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Applying the surjective-quasi-isomorphism criterion to the underlying simplicial abelian groups proves that $P_\bullet\to\operatorname{const} B$ is a trivial Kan fibration, with the boundary lifting property and vertex surjectivity of the cited definition.

Uses: lem-trf-cotangent-resolution-coherence, lem-trf-surjective-qis-simplicial-group-trivial-kan, def-weil-pbc-trivial-kan-fibration.

### Old Step 3

The map $P_\bullet[E]\to\operatorname{const}(B[E])$ is also termwise surjective and a quasi-isomorphism on alternating complexes. Indeed let $\mathcal M=\mathbf N^{(E)}$ be the set of finitely supported monomial exponent functions. Coefficients identify its underlying simplicial abelian-group map with $\bigoplus_{m\in\mathcal M}(P_\bullet\to\operatorname{const} B)$, because simplicial operators act on coefficients and fix all variables. The alternating complex is the same direct sum of complexes. A cycle in a direct sum has finite support and is a tuple of coordinate cycles; a boundary is a tuple of coordinate boundaries. Choosing preimages only on that finite support proves $H_n(\bigoplus C_m)=\bigoplus H_n(C_m)$. Thus Step 2 gives the required homology isomorphisms. Termwise surjectivity follows by lifting the finitely many coefficients of a polynomial. Apply the same surjective-quasi-isomorphism criterion. This proves the trivial Kan fibration needed for the top polynomial algebra, including infinite $E$, without a filtered-colimit preservation theorem.

Uses: lem-trf-surjective-qis-simplicial-group-trivial-kan.

### Old Step 4

For $W=(P\to B,Q\to C)$ choose polynomial coordinates $P=A[S]$, $Q=P[T]$. A simplex of $X_W=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(W,U_\bullet)$ consists precisely of elements $x_s\in P_\bullet$ over $\beta(s)$ for $s\in S$, and $y_t\in P_\bullet[E]$ over $\gamma(t)$ for $t\in T$: the second algebra map extends the first and is determined freely by the $y_t$. This coordinate description commutes with all faces and degeneracies. A boundary lifting problem for $X_W\to *$ is therefore a family of boundary lifting problems for the maps in Steps 2 and 3 over the indicated constant simplices. Fill each coordinate, choose the fillers for the sets $S,T$, and extend uniquely by the polynomial universal property. Vertex lifts are obtained in the same way. Consequently $X_W\to *$ is a trivial Kan fibration by the cited definition, and the homotopy-equivalence theorem makes $X_W$ homotopy equivalent to a singleton. Empty generator sets give empty products, hence a singleton; infinite generator sets cause no restriction because each chosen coordinate defines an algebra map out of a free polynomial algebra.

### Old Step 6
