### Old step 1 → lem-trf-small-sheaf-coproduct-labels

Work in the small universe-sized model of the site specified in [def-weil-size-convention]. Define the presheaf $P$ by $P(V)=\coprod_{i\in I}G_i(V)$, with restrictions preserving the label $i$. By [lem-trf-sieve-set-sheafification-exists], its sheafification $C=P^\#$ exists. For any sheaf $H$, a natural map $P\to H$ is exactly a family of natural maps $G_i\to H$, by evaluating on the labelled pairs $(i,s)$. The sheafification adjunction therefore gives $\operatorname{Hom}(C,H)=\prod_i\operatorname{Hom}(G_i,H)$. This is precisely the coproduct universal property [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit], with $c_i(s)=\eta(i,s)$. This argument works also for empty $I$.

Uses: `def-weil-size-convention`, `lem-trf-sieve-set-sheafification-exists`, `def-weil-pbc-category-colimit`.
Source: literature/stacks-project/sites.tex, Tag 00WI (lemma-colimit-sheaves), its left-adjoint argument; Tag 00ZM (proposition-sheafification-adjoint-topology).

### Old step 2 → lem-trf-small-sheaf-coproduct-labels

By [lem-weil-pbc-site-sheafification-sections-local], every $a\in C(T)$ is represented on an fppf covering $\{g_\alpha:U_\alpha\to T\}$ by labelled sections $(i_\alpha,s_\alpha)$ of $P$. Also, if $c_i(s)=c_j(t)$ on a scheme $V$, that lemma gives an fppf covering on which $(i,s)$ and $(j,t)$ are equal in $P$. If $i=j$, the sheaf equalizer condition [def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology] implies $s=t$. Hence every $c_i$ is injective on every section set; componentwise cancellation also proves it is monic. If $i\ne j$, no member of this further covering can exist, since equality of labelled pairs would imply $i=j$. Thus it is the empty covering; joint surjectivity [def-pc-fppf-site] forces $V$ empty. Every sheaf has one section over the empty scheme, by its empty covering and the same equalizer condition.

Uses: `lem-weil-pbc-site-sheafification-sections-local`, `def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology`, `def-pc-fppf-site`.
Source: literature/stacks-project/spaces.tex, Tag 0F15 (lemma-coproduct-sheaves-open-and-closed), local representatives and empty mixed intersections; sites.tex Tag 00WK.

### Old step 3 → lem-trf-fppf-labelled-cover-descent

The images $g_\alpha(U_\alpha)$ are open by [lem-weil-pbc-flat-lfp-open], since covering morphisms are flat and locally of finite presentation [def-pc-fppf-site]. When $i_\alpha\ne i_\beta$, the restrictions of $a$ to $U_\alpha\times_TU_\beta$ are simultaneously in the two different summands, so Step 2 makes this fibre product empty. Their images in $T$ are disjoint: indeed let $W=g_\alpha(U_\alpha)$ with its open subscheme structure [def-weil-open-immersion]. The factorization $U_\alpha\to W$ is surjective. Its base change along $U_\beta\times_TW\to W$ is surjective by [lem-weil-pbc-surjective-base-change], while its source is $U_\alpha\times_TU_\beta$ (the fibre-product universal property [def-pc-fibre-product]). It follows that $U_\beta\times_TW$ is empty. This scheme is the inverse image open of $W$ in $U_\beta$, proving the claimed disjointness.

### Old step 4 → lem-trf-fppf-labelled-cover-descent

For each $i$ put $T_i=\bigcup_{\alpha:i_\alpha=i}g_\alpha(U_\alpha)$, with its open subscheme structure [def-weil-open-immersion]. Step 3 shows these opens are pairwise disjoint, and joint surjectivity of the covering [def-pc-fppf-site] shows that they cover $T$. Each $T_i$ is also closed because its complement is the union of all the other $T_j$. This is a scheme coproduct decomposition: a family of maps $T_i\to Y$ glues uniquely to $T\to Y$ by [lem-weil-pbc-scheme-morphism-gluing], since all mixed intersections are empty. Thus the disjoint open inclusions have the required coproduct universal property, with arbitrary empty pieces allowed.

### Old step 5 → lem-trf-fppf-labelled-cover-descent

Base change the covering $\{U_\alpha\to T\}$ to $T_i$. By the site axiom [def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology], this is an fppf covering. Its members with label $i$ are $U_\alpha$ and its other members are empty, by Step 3. On two members with label $i$, the sections $s_\alpha,s_\beta$ have equal images under $c_i$, since both images restrict $a$; their restrictions agree by the injectivity proved in Step 2. Supply the unique $G_i$ section on every empty member. The sheaf equalizer condition therefore glues these sections uniquely to $a_i\in G_i(T_i)$. The same condition for $C$ gives $c_i(a_i)=a|_{T_i}$, since this equality holds on the pulled-back covering.

### Old step 7 → lem-trf-disjoint-sheaf-partition-pullbacks

Fix $i$ and a test scheme $Z$. A section of $h_T\times_CG_i$ on $Z$ is a pair $(b,s)$ with $b:Z\to T$, $s\in G_i(Z)$ and $c_i(s)=b^*a$; this is the sectionwise fibre-product equation. On the inverse image open $Z_j=b^{-1}(T_j)$, it identifies $c_i(s|_{Z_j})$ with $c_j(b^*a_j)$. Step 2 makes $Z_j$ empty for $j\ne i$. The $Z_j$ cover $Z$, so $b^{-1}(T_i)=Z$, and $b$ factors uniquely through the open subscheme $T_i$ [def-weil-open-immersion]: its underlying map has image in that open and its structure-sheaf map restricts there. These assignments are inverse and commute with precomposition in $Z$, since both use only composition and restriction of sections. They give the natural identification $h_{T_i}\simeq h_T\times_CG_i$, and hence the asserted cartesian square. Here a section $a$ gives the natural transformation $h_T\to C$ explicitly by $b\mapsto b^*a$, so no additional identification theorem is being assumed.
