## Source error

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported theorem is false for the separate one-sided definition of operadic colimit in `def-coef-operadic-correspondence`. GH A.2.1 states the same separate tests, and GH A.2.4 refers to the symmetric HA proof without addressing the simultaneous-context problem. HA's symmetric statement is not disputed: symmetry moves both contexts onto one side. In an arbitrary nonsymmetric monoidal category, separate stability of an individual cocone does not imply simultaneous left/right stability.

Here is an explicit finite counterexample, including all category data. Take the 156 words of length at most three in the alphabet `{a,b,c,l,r}`, including the empty word, and two further objects `Z` and `TOP`. Multiplication concatenates words of total length at most three; otherwise it is `TOP`. The empty word is the identity, `TOP` is absorbing, and multiplying `Z` by any nonidentity object on either side gives `TOP`. Order words of equal length componentwise using `a < c` and `b < c`, with all other letters incomparable; also impose `lar < Z`, `lbr < Z`, and put every object below `TOP`. There are no other nonidentity comparisons. This is a strict ordered monoid and hence a finite strict monoidal poset category. Its nerve is a monoidal infinity category of the required kind.

For every object `u`, `uc` is the join of `ua,ub`, and `cu` is the join of `au,bu`. For a word of length at most two, componentwise order gives the assertion; `Z` cannot be a competing upper bound because words below `Z` start with `l` and end with `r`, whereas the first pair ends in `a,b` and the second starts in `a,b`. For longer words or `u=Z,TOP`, the relevant products are all `TOP`. Thus the binary discrete cocone `(a,b) -> c` passes all one-sided contextual tests, even for contexts that are arbitrary tuples, since their tensor is an object `u` of this monoid. The singleton identity cocone at `r` also passes all those tests.

Concatenating these two cocones gives `(ar,br) -> cr`. Its left context `l` has base `lar,lbr` and proposed apex `lcr`. Both base objects map to `Z`, but `lcr` does not. Hence restriction of active arrows from the proposed apex to the color `Z` is the map from the empty space to a point, not an equivalence. The output is not operadically colimiting. This disproves the original two-input assertion with nonempty finite indexing shapes.
