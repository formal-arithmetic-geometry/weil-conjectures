I reread delivery-errors B–E with both JSON files and the sources open. Step 1 uses def-weil-ring-frobenius, which explicitly says that the power maps are ring homomorphisms. For a field they are injective, and their images are subfields. The argument that the unique pth root of an intersection element lies in every power image is written out using a p^(n+1)-th root and injectivity. This makes C perfect without treating it as an additional black-box lemma.

Step 2 uses def-weil-pbc-relative-separable-closure for the exact assertions S is a subfield of K^sep and is algebraic over C. It uses lem-per-algebraic-extension-perfect for the stated conclusion that an algebraic extension of a perfect field is perfect. C was proved perfect in Step 1. Taking finitely many successive pth roots gives the required p^n-th root for each n inside S; no root in an unspecified algebraic closure is substituted. Both quoted statements provide precisely these facts.

Step 3 uses def-pd-separable-closure for the statement that K^sep is algebraic and separable over K, and def-weil-pbc-separable-algebraic-extension for monic minimal polynomials and elementwise separability. Thus each chosen root x_n satisfies the hypothesis of the new leaf. The leaf is applied with F=K, E=K^sep, a=x_n, and the actual integer n; both fields have characteristic p via the given embedding. Its displayed equality is exactly the one used, and makes every individual coefficient of f a p^n-th power in K for every n. This proves coefficient membership in C; it does not claim x_n itself belongs to C. The polynomial has positive degree because it is a nonzero monic minimal polynomial of an algebraic element.

Step 4 uses def-weil-pbc-separable-algebraic-extension for irreducibility of the minimal polynomial over K and the meaning of separable algebraic; def-pc-separable-polynomial explicitly gives the derivative and Bezout criterion; def-weil-pbc-relative-separable-closure gives the final membership condition in S. Irreducibility descends since a factorization over C would remain a factorization of the same positive degrees over K. The derivative remains nonzero over C by coefficient injectivity. A common divisor of f and f' would be either constant or associated to f, and the degree inequality rules out the latter; the Euclidean algorithm gives the displayed Bezout equality. This is a short elementary polynomial argument, not a new descent lemma. It shows f is the separable minimal polynomial over C and justifies exactly the membership claim needed. I corrected the derivative typography while checking it.
