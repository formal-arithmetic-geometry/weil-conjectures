### lem-trf-parameter-product-strict-local-branches

Old Step 1 (including the parent geometric-to-algebraic interface, which remains in assembly Step 1):

Fix $u\in U$ and put $A=\mathcal O_{U,u}$ and $I_u=\{j:t_j\in\mathfrak m_A\}$. The other factors are units. By [def-weil-regular-scheme], $A$ is a regular Noetherian local ring. Extend the factors indexed by $I_u$ to a regular system of parameters. By [lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-generators-regular-sequence], after any permutation every quotient by an initial subfamily is regular local, and the remaining parameters are successive nonzerodivisors. In particular $A/(t_j)$ is regular local for $j\in I_u$. Let $H=A^{\mathrm{sh}}$ for the separable closure selected by $\bar u$. The geometric-to-algebraic presentation [lem-sbc-geometric-strict-henselization-local-presentation] identifies this with the strict local ring at $\bar u$: on an affine chart the prime is $u$, as seen by contracting the kernel of the residue map induced by $\bar u$. Apply [lem-sbc-strict-henselization-surjective-quotient] to $A\twoheadrightarrow A/(t_j)$: its kernel lies in $\mathfrak m_A$, so $H/t_jH$ is a strict henselization of $A/(t_j)$. It is regular local by [lem-sc-strict-henselization-regular], and a nonzero domain by [lem-sc-regular-local-domain]. Thus $P_j=t_jH$ is a proper prime ideal.

Old Step 2:

For distinct $i,j\in I_u$, reorder the parameter system with $t_j$ first and $t_i$ second. By [lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-generators-regular-sequence], $t_i$ is a nonzerodivisor in the nonzero ring $A/(t_j)$, hence $t_i\notin(t_j)$. The map $A\to H$ is faithfully flat by [lem-sc-strict-henselization-faithfully-flat]. By [lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-ideal-contraction], $t_i\notin t_jH$. Thus the primes $P_j$ of Step 1 are pairwise incomparable. Put $F=\prod_{j\in I_u}t_j$. If $I_u$ is nonempty, the local ring of $D_U$ at $u$ is $A/(F)$, since discarded factors are units. By [lem-sbc-strict-henselization-surjective-quotient] its strict henselization is $H/(F)H$, identified with its geometric strict local ring by [lem-sbc-geometric-strict-henselization-local-presentation]. Every prime of $H$ containing $F$ contains some $t_j$, hence contains $P_j$. Conversely each $P_j$ contains $F$; incomparability shows that these, and only these, are the primes minimal over $(F)H$. Passing to the quotient and then killing nilpotents does not change this prime poset: every prime contains each nilpotent, and inverse image along the quotient is an inclusion-preserving bijection onto primes containing its kernel. Consequently the reduced strict local ring of $D_U$ has exactly $|I_u|$ minimal primes. When $I_u$ is empty, $F=1$ and $u\notin D_U$, giving branch number zero by convention. This proves the intrinsic description of $b$ and its independence of coordinate equations.

### lem-trf-etale-coordinate-intersection-geometry

Old Step 3:

For $u\in C$, all $s$ factors are in $\mathfrak m_A$ and form part of a regular system of parameters, so $s\le\dim A$. The closed-subscheme description [def-weil-closed-immersion] identifies $\mathcal O_{C,u}$ with $A/(t_1,\ldots,t_s)$. By [lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-generators-regular-sequence] this ring is regular local. The scheme $C$ is locally Noetherian: on an affine Noetherian chart of $U$ its ring is the quotient by the finitely generated ideal of the restricted functions, and ideals in a quotient correspond to ideals containing the kernel, so ascending chains stabilize. Hence [def-weil-regular-scheme] implies that $C$ is regular. Every displayed local ring is a domain by [lem-sc-regular-local-domain]; a nilpotent in a domain is zero, so [def-pc-reduced-scheme] implies that $C$ is reduced. If $C$ is empty these local assertions hold vacuously.
