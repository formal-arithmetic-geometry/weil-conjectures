## Review by O1

I read Anantharaman 4.1 in the primary scan for the field-effectivity claim. For the quotient node I read Stacks groupoids.tex 0B7R (quasi-compact geometrically irreducible neutral subgroup), bootstrap.tex 06PH (free flat group quotient) and stacks-morphisms.tex 0DU1 (open relation gives an etale quotient). I also read Milne Algebraic Groups, Propositions 1.29-1.34 and 1.52, printed pp.15-21, to check the intrinsic component property. The relation defined by xy^{-1} in H^0 is clopen; over an algebraic closure its quotient is the discrete set of components, and its fibres are translates of H^0. This explains why the quotient is separated, its map is quasi-compact, and the assertion is intrinsic under isomorphisms even after a base change to a nonfield such as L tensor_k L. Field-extension compatibility alone would not have justified step 2, so the auxiliary statement explicitly includes this stronger compatibility.

For the ample-open node I read the proof of Stacks 0BF7 and varieties.tex 0BDC. The former reduces to translates of the neutral group over an algebraic closure and deals with nonreduced groups; the latter descends existence of an ample module, not a chosen module. A quasi-compact open meets finitely many clopen geometric components; restricting ample modules on those translates gives an ample module on that open after algebraic closure. This is why the node allows U to be a non-subgroup and the total group to have infinitely many components. It is not the existing finite-type-group ampleness statement.

I opened the JSON statement of every node cited by a proof step. Step 1 uses exactly base-change preservation of separation and local finite presentation, the field-extension fpqc lemma, and full Yoneda. The field L is a residue field of a point of the nonempty cover T, so it need not be algebraic or finite over k; none of those cited statements requires that. Local finite presentation implies local finite type directly from their affine definitions. The descent cocycle is obtained by composing the representing identifications, not postulated.

Step 2 uses the new quotient statement over the double and triple tensor products, the separated locally quasi-finite effectivity statement (which explicitly permits non-quasi-compact V), and descent of etaleness for a scheme already constructed. The cited etale-fibre classification gives an open covering of E by spectra of finite separable extensions of L. Each is finite type with a singleton fibre, hence quasi-finite by pqf-def-quasifinite. I added both citations and this explicit verification in the final audit. No fpqc-to-fppf algebraicity implication is used. Step 3 uses the representable fpqc descent statement to glue actual morphisms V_L to Q, with equality on the double overlap supplied by quotient equivariance.

Step 4 uses the full etale-fibre classification over Spec k, not just finiteness of a single residue field. Its components are spectra of finite separable fields, so their L-pullbacks are affine and quasi-compact even when k_a tensor_k L is not a field. The quotient lemma supplies quasi-compactness of the preimage, and the ample-open lemma supplies exactly finite type and an ample module. This is the place where falsely assuming the whole group finite type would break the argument.

Step 5 uses the existing field ample-scheme effectivity statement with its actual hypotheses: finite-type X_a, ample module, arbitrary extension L/k, and a compatible datum. Its statement explicitly requires no linearization of that module. I checked the BLR proof's first paragraph online. The equalizer argument identifying Y_a with G_a is written out; representable descent supplies the equalizer for Y_a and the fpqc sheaf condition supplies it for G_a. No unsupported descent equivalence of categories is cited. Step 6 checks each hypothesis of the schemes criterion: Zariski sheaf, represented open subfunctors, and covering on every test scheme. Yoneda gives all group operations and identities; the two final descent statements then give separatedness and local finite type. These results do not require quasi-compactness of Y.
