## Findings and corrections

The statement is correct, including infinite algebraic Galois extensions and infinite-dimensional vector spaces. I read Milne A.64 on printed p.580 and its consequence A.65 on printed p.581. A.64 asserts continuous semilinear vector-space descent, with a proof through finite Galois extensions, independence of characters and Morita equivalence. Instead of treating that whole descent theorem as an unsplit restatement of this item, the delivered proof gives a direct finite-support argument for the canonical action. It is explicitly marked `proof_origin.by = agent`; the source place distinguishes the source's assertion from this proof.

The argument picks a vector of minimal nonempty support within the coordinate support of a given element of W, normalizes a nonzero coefficient to one, and subtracts each conjugate. A nonzero difference would have strictly smaller support, so the vector is fixed. The infinite Galois fixed-field theorem puts its coefficients in k. Cancelling one coordinate now proves generation by strong induction. Extending a basis of W_0 to a basis of M proves tensor injectivity. This avoids invoking exactness of tensor products, continuity of an arbitrary semilinear action, averaging by the group order, or an unstated finite-dimensionality condition.

Step 1 cites `def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product`. I opened its statement: it gives bilinearity, the universal property and generation by pure tensors. Those are exactly what constructs F and identifies its image with the L-span N. Taking a preimage, checking the formula for L-linearity and proving N is contained in W use just the definitions and the given subspace closure properties. The step is marked elementary.

Step 2 cites `lem-weil-pbc-vector-space-basis-through-vector` and `def-weil-vector-space-scalar-extension`. The former explicitly gives a basis from an empty independent subset; the latter explicitly gives the extended basis with the correct second-factor scalar action. The finite-support coordinates and coordinatewise action are written out. Although the latter node is called a definition, its statement actually includes the basis theorem and it is an accepted Mathlib leaf, rather than a mere definition being used as a theorem it does not state.

Step 3 cites `lem-exc-etale-galois-fixed-field-subgroup`. I opened its statement and checked that its top extension need not be finite and that its separability, algebraicity and normality hypotheses hold for L/k. Its intermediate field is specialized to k, so its fixing subgroup is the full group Gamma and the fixed field is k. No arbitrary subgroup fixed-field assertion is substituted for this. Minimality is taken among supports contained in the fixed finite set s(x), which is nonempty because x is an eligible nonzero vector. Normalization preserves membership in W and preserves support. Every conjugate difference is in W, has no coordinates outside s(u), and loses the normalized coefficient. Its vanishing therefore follows from the exact minimality chosen, before the fixed-field theorem is used. The recovered k-coefficients have finite support and give an actual element of W_0 by tensor bilinearity.

Step 4 has no external input: it assembles step 3 by strong induction on the support cardinality, explicitly with zero as base case and y=x-bu as the smaller-support element. It is marked elementary because all ingredients have just been proved in steps 1–3. The smaller support is inside s(x) minus its pivot; it cannot grow outside the support because u was chosen inside it. Thus the induction never assumes finite dimension of W or stability of an arbitrary finite-dimensional span.

Step 5 uses the same two basis nodes as step 2. The extension theorem applies because an independent family in the subspace W_0 remains independent in M under its injective inclusion. The scalar-extension basis theorem applies separately to W_0 and M. A finite coordinate sum in the domain maps into a subset of a basis of the codomain, and vanishing forces its coefficients to vanish. This proves the required injectivity without an uncited flatness theorem. Combining it with step 4 proves exactly the given map's isomorphism onto W.

I checked the small cases M=0, W=0, W=M_L, and L=k: empty bases and empty supports are allowed, and the zero-support induction case applies. For a nontrivial stable line, the normalized minimal-support vector has base-field coordinates as claimed. The argument works in positive characteristic, including when a finite Galois group's order is divisible by the characteristic, since it uses no division by the group order. It also works for infinite bases and infinite Galois groups because it only minimizes finite support and applies the existing infinite fixed-field theorem.
