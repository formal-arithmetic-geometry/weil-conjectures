The finite algebra, its multiplication, unit and morphisms descend on a presentation; representability by finite schemes, full faithfulness and the normal etale source atlas are included in the new statement. For the second new node I read stacks-geometry.tex Tag 0DQZ and etale.tex Tag 039M, including the paragraph explaining that the completed local map is an isomorphism when the residue field is separably closed. This is a comparison for a supplied effective formal atlas, not an existence theorem for quotient charts. An etale atlas lifts the closed Omega point; its pullback over the complete strictly henselian local chart has a section, and formal etaleness identifies the pointed completions. The assertion includes compatibility of the actual effective map, which is needed for tensoring the finite algebra in step 6. General stack lifting/completion comparison is the substantive new prerequisite; the existing scheme etale completed-local-ring isomorphism does not already assert it.

For the third new node I read spaces-groupoids.tex Tags 04M9 (the 2-cartesian presentation square) and 044W (quotient-stack identification), algebra.tex Tags 00MA and 07N9, and the cited Pikaart--de Jong paragraph. Apply the presentation square at each infinitesimal neighbourhood: finite pullbacks are the finite algebras C/m^n C with their canonical action descent. Completeness identifies the inverse system with C. The statement includes the induced semilinear action and completion of the entire inverse-image gerbe. It does not assert normality of C or identify C with an integral closure; these are separate steps of the item. It is a theorem about arbitrary finite representable maps, so singular or nonnormal C is an allowed small case.

4. Etale-integral-closure-base-change says exactly that tensoring the integral closure in an arbitrary ambient algebra gives the new integral closure. The field-product and normal-domain-factor nodes identify the ambient base-changed rational algebra, and flat density rules out discarded components. 5. Perfect-closure criterion is applied with P=k, which meets its purely inseparable and perfect hypotheses. It supplies normality for EVERY extension k'/k, including transcendental ones. Flat density preserves all generic components of the open after extension. The finite normal algebra is its integral closure by the two written integrality inclusions. Finite-stack-descent glues the canonical comparisons. This does not apply arbitrary base-change invariance of normalization.
6. The new completed-atlas statement supplies the comparison R=hat(A) AND the compatible map to the stack. Fields-excellent and finite-type-localization-excellent give excellence of A and of each local C_q, since finite algebras are finite type. Completion-finite-extension explicitly gives the product of completed local C_q. The final normal-completion assertion of henselian-normal-completion-preparation applies to ANY normal excellent Noetherian local domain, not only its earlier henselization. Finite-product-normal then gives normality of D. I verified that these statements handle zero products and that no unproved new completion lemma remains.
7. The excellence definition includes the G-ring property. G-ring-completion-regular explicitly supplies flatness of A to R, and flatness is preserved by the displayed tensor base change. Dense-flat-pullback therefore applies to the open in Spec C. The two integrality inclusions identify D with B in the product of ALL rational fields, not in a single arbitrarily chosen component.
8. Finite-formal-quotient-pullback applies to the already constructed representable finite map. It gives exactly the completion along the full inverse-image gerbe and the canonical induced H-action. The agreement on E fixes the identification with the B action, since both algebras embed in the same rational algebra.
9. This uses only the quotient definition: arrows from b to itself are stabilizing group elements. A closed point of the finite Omega algebra has residue Omega; hence fixing that point is the same as fixing the geometric point. No faithfulness or tameness assumption on H was added.
