### Returned Step 1

Put $V=\mathbf F_\ell^{2g}$ and $\mathcal H=R^1f_*\underline{\mathbf F_\ell}$ for a smooth family $f:X\to T$ in the moduli groupoid. Its projective-bundle hypothesis implies properness by the cited projective-bundle theorem. This includes the H-projective families in the statement: their projective space is the bundle of a finite free module. Apply the smooth-proper local-system theorem to the constant coefficient sheaf. Apply prime-coefficient proper base change and take degree one: for every $v:T^\prime\to T$, the canonical map $v^{-1}\mathcal H\to R^1f^\prime_*\underline{\mathbf F_\ell}$ is an isomorphism. In particular its geometric stalk at $\bar t$ is $H^1(X_{\bar t},\mathbf F_\ell)$. The proper-curve computation gives dimension $2g$ at every such stalk. A local constant vector-space model therefore has dimension $2g$ wherever its base is nonempty; shrinking to its connected pieces if necessary gives $\mathcal H\cong V_T$ etale locally. This proves constant rank and arbitrary-base-change compatibility without assuming $T$ Noetherian.

Inputs: lem-sfb-smooth-proper-finite-cohomology-local-system, lem-trf-projective-bundle-proper, lemma-weil-pbc-prime-proper-base-change, thm-sc-proper-cohomology.

### Returned Step 2

Let $\mathcal F=\underline{\operatorname{Isom}}_{\mathbf F_\ell}(V_T,\mathcal H)$ on $T_{\mathrm{et}}$. On any etale open trivializing $\mathcal H$, this is the constant sheaf $\operatorname{GL}_{2g}(\mathbf F_\ell)$: a frame is its invertible matrix in that trivialization. This finite set contains the identity. Finite locally constant representability therefore supplies a finite etale scheme $P\to T$. Its geometric fibres are the corresponding stalk sets of frames, so every geometric fibre is nonempty and $P\to T$ is surjective. For an arbitrary $v:T^\prime\to T$, inverse image of the represented sheaf is represented by $P\times_TT^\prime$. In the same local trivializations inverse image of $\mathcal F$ is the frame sheaf of $v^{-1}\mathcal H$; Step 1 identifies this with the frame sheaf of $X_{T^\prime}/T^\prime$. Evaluating at the terminal object $T^\prime$ proves $\operatorname{Mor}_T(T^\prime,P)=\operatorname{Isom}(V_{T^\prime},\mathcal H_{X_{T^\prime}/T^\prime})$. These identifications are natural in $T^\prime$. The empty base gives the empty representing scheme and satisfies the same formula.

Inputs: lem-galois-flc-representable, lem-galois-stalk-representable, lem-galois-inverse-image-representable.

### Returned Step 3

First work with the level groupoid, before asserting that it is a scheme. Use the definition of the 2-fibre product, inverting its comparison isomorphism to orient it from the level family to the fixed family. Over $T^\prime\to T$ an object is $(Y,\lambda,\phi)$, where $\phi:Y\xrightarrow{\sim}X_{T^\prime}$ is an actual family isomorphism. Send it to the frame $(\phi^*)^{-1}\lambda$ on $X_{T^\prime}$. Conversely a frame $\alpha$ gives $(X_{T^\prime},\alpha,\mathrm{id})$. An arrow between $(Y,\lambda,\phi)$ and $(Y^\prime,\lambda^\prime,\phi^\prime)$ must be $u=(\phi^\prime)^{-1}\phi$; it preserves frames exactly when the transported frames agree, since $\phi^*=u^*(\phi^\prime)^*$. Thus these functors are fully faithful and essentially surjective, with the displayed isomorphisms supplying the quasi-inverse comparison. Under base change use the composite through $\mathrm{BC}^1$ prescribed in the definition for each frame. Naturality of that comparison with family isomorphisms preserves the displayed transport and arrow equations. Consequently $T\times_{\mathcal M_{g,0}}\mathcal L_{g,\ell}$ is the discrete frame functor, even before using rigidity of the level objects themselves.

### Returned Step 4

The coarse-moduli input makes $\mathcal L_{g,\ell}$ a smooth finite-type Deligne--Mumford stack and supplies its coarse morphism $c$ to a scheme $C_{g,\ell}$. For an object $(C,\lambda)$ over an algebraically closed field, a frame-preserving automorphism satisfies $u^*\lambda=\lambda$. Since $\lambda$ is an isomorphism, $u^*$ is the identity on $H^1(C,\mathbf F_\ell)$. Geometric level faithfulness gives $u=\mathrm{id}_C$. The DM geometric-inertia criterion now gives an algebraic space $E$ naturally equivalent to $\mathcal L_{g,\ell}$, and also trivial automorphisms for its objects over every test scheme, including nonreduced schemes. This is where the restrictions $g\geq2$ and $\ell\geq3$ are essential.

Inputs: lem-sfb-abelian-level-coarse-moduli, lem-sfb-geometric-curve-level-faithfulness, lem-sfb-dm-geometric-inertia-trivial.

### Returned Step 5

Regard the coarse map as $c:E\to C_{g,\ell}$. Apply the defining bijection between maps $C_{g,\ell}\to E$ and maps $\mathcal L_{g,\ell}\to E$ to the equivalence $\mathcal L_{g,\ell}\to E$ (identified with $\mathrm{id}_E$); obtain $d:C_{g,\ell}\to E$ with $dc=\mathrm{id}_E$. The two maps $cd$ and $\mathrm{id}_{C_{g,\ell}}$ have the same composite with $c$, namely $c$, so uniqueness in that same initial property gives $cd=\mathrm{id}_{C_{g,\ell}}$. Hence $E\cong C_{g,\ell}$ and the latter scheme represents the full groupoid. Set $L_{g,\ell}=C_{g,\ell}$. It is finite type by the coarse input and smooth because the equivalence identifies it with the smooth stack $\mathcal L_{g,\ell}$ (smoothness is tested on its scheme atlases). This argument uses the coarse universal property only after the algebraic-space conclusion; a bijection of geometric isomorphism classes alone would not give $d$.
