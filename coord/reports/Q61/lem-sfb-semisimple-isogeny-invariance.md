Step 1 uses the affine homomorphism factorization, not the proper-source image result. N is affine and finite type because it is a closed subgroup of G_1. The new lemma is applied to f and that very factorization; all of its smooth, connected, solvable and normal hypotheses are exactly the hypotheses imposed on N, while the original f is faithfully flat. It is here that the non-elementary image geometry is isolated, with no semisimple hypothesis hidden in it.

Step 2 uses only the definition of semisimplicity already written in the item's statement. Since the whole image scheme is trivial, the restriction is the identity-section composite, giving factorization through the kernel as a group scheme. I explicitly excluded the invalid shortcut from equality on rational points alone.

Step 3 uses only the affine characterization in def-weil-closed-immersion. With ideals J_K contained in J_N, the resulting map A/J_K to A/J_N is surjective; the finite-dimensionality therefore passes to B. This neither assumes reducedness of K nor centrality of K. The quotient-of-a-finite-vector-space argument is elementary.

Step 4 applies the smooth-geometrically-reduced statement to N over Spec F, so that its only fibre really is N. Ideals in the finite-dimensional F-vector space B satisfy the descending chain condition by decreasing dimension. The existing reduced-Artinian product statement therefore applies with exactly its two hypotheses. The residue fields are F by the *first* clause of the existing affine Nullstellensatz node, which allows arbitrary finitely generated F-algebras and all characteristics; I did not use the characteristic-zero-only geometry-definition node. Nonemptiness comes from the identity section. A finite product with at least two factors has the explicit idempotent open-and-closed partition, contradicting connectedness. These are elementary applications of the cited statements, not an uncited classification of finite etale groups.

Step 5 converts the resulting F-algebra isomorphism B=F to triviality of N, and quantifies over all N. A one-point F-scheme has unique group structure. G_1 already has smoothness, connectedness, affineness and finite type, so the conclusion is the full stated semisimplicity condition.

For future splitting of the new node, the proof route is as follows (not inserted into its statement): I=N/ker(q) by the homomorphism theorem; smooth quotient and the continuous surjective image of connected N give smoothness and connectedness. A finite solvable series maps to a solvable series by subgroup correspondence, or use the equality between the image of each derived subgroup and the derived subgroup of the image; 6.27 and 6.30 give precisely this passage. Normality is checked on every F-algebra A by lifting h in H(A) and i in I(A), after a common faithfully flat cover, to g in G and n in N. The equality h i h^{-1}=f(gng^{-1}) puts the conjugate in I locally, since N is normal; vanishing of the ideal of the closed subgroup I descends through a faithfully flat ring map. Inverting h gives equality, so this is scheme-theoretic normality. The statement does not assume the kernels smooth: quotient geometry must not introduce that hypothesis.

I tested the new statement with N=1, N=G, f=id, and Frobenius G_a to G_a in characteristic p, whose kernel alpha_p is not smooth. All satisfy the conclusion. Dropping surjectivity of f would make its normality conclusion false: include a Borel subgroup in SL_2 and take N to be that Borel. For the item I checked G_0=1 (the source becomes a finite smooth connected group), identity isogenies, and SL_p to PGL_p in characteristic p (nonreduced kernel mu_p). Alpha_p and mu_p show why a finite connected group alone need not be trivial; smoothness of N is essential. A nontrivial finite constant group shows why connectedness is essential. No kernel centrality or reducedness is used.
