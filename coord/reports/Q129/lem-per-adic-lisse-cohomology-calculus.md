## Review by Q129

I read each cited node's statement, rather than taking facts from its proof. The following is my check of what each step actually uses.

1. The common-stage definitions really represent objects by finite coefficient fields. The connected-components lemma gives finitely many open connected pieces for a Noetherian space, including the empty case. The two clauses of the lisse representation equivalence give both the rational representation and finite quotient systems; the stable-lattice lemma gives a finite *free* integral lattice for a continuous action of a compact group. The fundamental group is profinite, so compactness and continuity are present. This avoids assuming the stronger, false claim that every pro-etale lisse E-module globally has a lisse lattice. The input here is the classical adic lisse category of def-pod.
2. The completed-system comparison explicitly gives an inverse-limit locally finite-free B-module with the prescribed reductions and transitions. The stratified-perfect characterization is stated for the precise category with compatible etale-origin witnesses, and its one-stratum local model A^r applies. The integral-limit coefficient node explicitly identifies the *ring sheaves* R_X and B. Thus the next use of the generic perfect-factor lemma does not silently change coefficient categories.
3. Exactness of slice restriction and the restriction/tensor isomorphism are explicitly stated by the two cited generic site tools. Finite sums preserve an exact sequence; kernels/cokernels vanish if they vanish on a cover, by the sheaf axiom. Tensor therefore preserves acyclic complexes. This is the elementary local finite-sum argument, not a new flatness or cohomology node.
4. Cohomology is defined as kernel/image quotient by the cited definition. The proof writes the induced cycle and boundary maps and their quotient, making the comparison canonical and natural. The characterization supplies finite strata and finite constant module models for H^a(K_A); tensor with a free local rank r gives the finitely presented model M^r and the same cohomological bounds. Its converse then supplies membership in the stronger integral category, including all witness conditions. This is not inferred from local boundedness alone.
5. I checked the *statement* of det-lem-int-tensor-product carefully: its final “Moreover, let S be a scheme” paragraph gives sequential-derived-limit preservation for a locally finite-free perfect factor on an arbitrary scheme. I do not apply its earlier characteristic-p finite-type tensor-closure assertion to this X. Step 2 supplies its ring identification and N has the required model A^r[0]. The reconstruction node explicitly identifies a complete object's canonical quotient map with the derived limit of its quotient tower. The balanced levelwise comparison is written out, and its compatibility with each quotient transition is checked before taking the limit. Hence the result is the completed operation defined by def-weil-derived-pullback-tensor-hom, not a merely similar ordinary tensor.
6. The localized-tstructure proposition states that Q is t-exact and cohomology is Q applied to integral cohomology. This matters: an integral representative can contain uniformizer-torsion cohomology that is zero rationally. The proof uses that actual proposition and does not define the rational heart by vanishing of the representative's unlocalized cohomology. The formula for rational morphisms agrees with the bilinear formulas in the operations definition.
7. The integral characterization gives finite-module local models for the cohomology sheaf. During review I noticed that this alone did not cite the theorem descending its finite reductions to etale local systems; I repaired the gap by citing det-pe-finite-local-systems-twist, whose equivalence also descends the normalized maps and identities. The finite-coefficient tensor lemma explicitly gives the tensor-germ isomorphism. The finite-module limit lemma gives completeness and recovery of normalized finite systems, even when the first factor has torsion. Only the lisse factor is required to be free; tensor with it is a finite sum, so its commutation with this inverse limit is the written elementary calculation. This computes the adic fibre on a stratum, not the ordinary stalk of an infinite inverse limit.
8. Finite-level linear duals of free modules commute with quotient maps: the inverse-transpose formula proves compatibility on overlaps and transitions. The completed-system comparison therefore applies to the dual tower. The integral characterization supplies its degree-zero membership. The fibre limit lemma and the fibre definition identify its fibre with the integral dual and then the field dual. The construction gives all local ranks, including 0, without requiring a single global basis.
9. The exact slice restriction and the globally-free internal-Hom exactness lemma show Hom(N,-) exact on a local trivialization, hence globally. The bounded-exact-term Hom lemma applies to the one-term complex N[0] and the quasi-isomorphism B[0] to its K-injective resolution. The definition of derived internal Hom says exactly that the latter Hom complex computes it. The resulting comparison is evaluation-normalized and gives degree zero. The operations definition allows rational internal Hom for this pair because Step 8 proved its constructibility.

The local-field integral-basis lemma supplies a finite free A-module A'; the cofinal uniformizer powers identify the completed ring extension as a finite free module. The kernel/image calculation then proves t-exact scalar extension for cohomology. I wrote the fibre pure-tensor and dual evaluation formulas, so compatibility with extension is an actual check rather than the phrase “by transport”. Common-field representatives and the explicit tower equations make every construction independent of the stage and of rational morphism denominators.
