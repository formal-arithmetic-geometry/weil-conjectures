## Preserved argument and allocation for the two future workers

### Original step 1: item edge cases

If $T=0$, its spectrum is empty and has dimension $-\infty<n$, as in [def-weil-pbc-krull-dimension-ring]. Hence assume $T\ne0$. If $n=0$, the polynomial ring is $k$ and a nonzero element of the kernel is a unit: applying $\varphi$ to its inverse times itself gives $1_T=0$, a contradiction. Thus it remains to treat $n\ge1$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-krull-dimension-ring.

### Original step 2: lem-trf-polynomial-nonzero-ideal-quotient-dimension-bound

Put $P=k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$. The field $k$ is Noetherian because its only ideals are $(0)$ and $k$, and has dimension zero because its sole prime ideal is $(0)$. Apply [lem-sf-noetherian-polynomial-dimension] to get $\dim P=n$. Also $P$ is a domain directly from the polynomial multiplication [def-weil-pbc-mv-polynomial-ring]: order exponent vectors lexicographically; in a nonzero polynomial choose the largest exponent in its finite support. For two nonzero polynomials with largest exponents $a,b$, the largest exponent of their product is $a+b$, with coefficient the product of their nonzero leading coefficients. This coefficient is nonzero in the field. Thus $(0)$ is a prime ideal of $P$.

Cited nodes: lem-sf-noetherian-polynomial-dimension, def-weil-pbc-mv-polynomial-ring.

### Original step 3: lem-trf-quasi-finite-kernel-quotient-dimension-comparison

Let $I=\ker\varphi$, choose $0\ne h\in I$, and put $R=P/I$. The formula $\psi(p+I)=\varphi(p)$ is well defined because $I$ is the kernel, and factors $\varphi$ through $R$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-finite-four-rings] to $P\to T$, $P\to R$, $R\xrightarrow{\psi}T$ and $T\xrightarrow{1}T$. The square commutes. Its finite-sum hypothesis holds because each $t\in T$ is the single product $t\cdot1$, with the first factor in the image of $T\xrightarrow{1}T$ and the second in the image of $R$. For each prime $\mathfrak q$ of the bottom $T$, its inverse image in the top $T$ is the same $\mathfrak q$, where the original map is quasi-finite by hypothesis. The cited statement therefore gives that $\psi:R\to T$ is finite type and quasi-finite at every prime of $T$.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-quasi-finite-four-rings.

### Original step 4: lem-trf-polynomial-nonzero-ideal-quotient-dimension-bound

Every strict prime chain $\mathfrak r_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak r_\ell$ of $R$ pulls back to a strict prime chain $\widetilde{\mathfrak r}_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\widetilde{\mathfrak r}_\ell$ of $P$ containing $I$. Primality follows by testing products modulo $I$; strictness follows by lifting an element in each successive ideal difference along the surjection $P\to R$. Each lifted ideal contains $h\ne0$, so prepending the prime $(0)$ makes a chain of length $\ell+1$ in $P$. By $\dim P=n$ and the prime-chain definition [def-weil-pbc-krull-dimension-ring], $\ell+1\le n$. Thus every prime chain of $R$ has length at most $n-1$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-krull-dimension-ring.

### Original step 5: lem-trf-quasi-finite-kernel-quotient-dimension-comparison

Fix a prime $\mathfrak q$ of $T$ and put $\mathfrak r=\psi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$. The localization/chain formula [lem-weil-pbc-localization-dimension-chains-below-prime] applied to $R,\mathfrak r$, together with step 4, gives $\dim R_{\mathfrak r}\le n-1$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-dimension-inequality-quasi-finite] to $\psi$ at $\mathfrak q$: step 3 verified precisely its finite-type and quasi-finiteness hypotheses. It gives $\dim T_{\mathfrak q}\le\dim R_{\mathfrak r}\le n-1$.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-localization-dimension-chains-below-prime, lem-weil-pbc-dimension-inequality-quasi-finite.

### Original step 6: lem-trf-quasi-finite-kernel-quotient-dimension-comparison (ring bound), then item (spectrum comparison)

For any strict prime chain of length $\ell$ in $T$, let $\mathfrak q$ be its last prime. By [lem-weil-pbc-localization-dimension-chains-below-prime], $\ell\le\dim T_{\mathfrak q}\le n-1$ by step 5. Taking the supremum of all chain lengths [def-weil-pbc-krull-dimension-ring] gives $\dim T\le n-1<n$. Finally [lem-weil-pbc-spec-topological-krull-dimension-ring] identifies this ring dimension with $\dim\operatorname{Spec}T$, the dimension in the conclusion.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-localization-dimension-chains-below-prime, def-weil-pbc-krull-dimension-ring, lem-weil-pbc-spec-topological-krull-dimension-ring.

For the second node's generalized statement, the same localization argument proves dim R_r <= dim R without a finite bound: chains below r are chains of R. Every chain in T ends at a prime q and hence has length <= dim T_q <= dim R. Taking the supremum gives the stated global inequality even when dim R is infinite. If T is zero there are no q and its dimension is bottom; if A/I is zero then the induced unital map forces T zero. Thus no extra finite-dimension hypothesis was introduced.
