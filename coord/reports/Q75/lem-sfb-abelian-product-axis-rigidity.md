## Review by Q75

I read Milne `literature/milne-abelian-varieties_AV.pdf`, I.3.2–3.6, printed pp.17–19, especially the proof of Theorem 3.4 on p.19. The theorem explicitly treats noncomplete factors: for a curve it compactifies and extends, then applies proper rigidity; for higher dimension it uses curves through the fixed smooth point and normalization. The item keeps all of its assumptions and conclusion. The new node states the normalized pointed-curve density input of Lemmas 3.5(b) and 3.6, rather than claiming each closed curve is globally smooth. Normalization has a point over the specified nonsingular point, is an isomorphism there, and over algebraically closed k is a smooth integral curve. Its image is dense in the original curve, so taking the dense union gives the new statement. Its `u_i` are allowed to be constant: when X is a point, a constant map from the affine line supplies a nonempty dense image. This repairs the dimension-zero omission that a literal 'union of one-dimensional closed subcurves' statement would have had. I did not alter the item to exclude that case.

For step 1 I opened `lem-pc-proper-base-change` (clause 2), `lem-trf-smooth-composition`, `lem-sfb-finite-type-composition-base-change`, and `lem-trf-smooth-geometrically-normal`. They say exactly the base-change, composition, finiteness and reducedness facts used. I also read `def-sc-abelian-variety` and `def-weil-proper-morphism`: properness supplies the separated finite-type target needed by all later equality and rigidity statements. Step 2 is an elementary diagonal calculation, not an uncited permanence assertion: the affine-local quotient by the sum of the two diagonal ideals proves that the product diagonal is closed, using precisely the affine-local criterion in `def-weil-closed-immersion` and the definition of separatedness in `def-weil-proper-morphism`.

For step 3 I opened `lem-sc-compactification`, which applies because the chosen C has dimension one and is smooth, separated and connected. I opened `lem-sbc-connected-rational-point-geometrically-connected`: j(c) is the required section, so it gives geometric connectedness, not merely connectedness. I opened `lem-weil-pbc-geometrically-connected-product-components`: its component bijection makes the product connected because W is connected and nonempty. Smoothness and finite type come from step 1, and regularity comes from `lem-trf-smooth-geometrically-normal` at the identity field extension. The statement of `lem-sc-regular-connected-integral` requires nonempty connected regular source, all verified. Separatedness was proved in step 2. Thus the exact source hypotheses of `lem-sfb-weil-extension-to-abelian-variety` (nonempty integral separated finite-type smooth scheme, dense open, abelian target) hold. The open product contains (c,w0), so is nonempty and therefore dense in the integral product. Extension applies to an actual map on that open, with the stated target, and does not invoke extension to an arbitrary proper variety.

For step 4 the same regular-connected-integral check makes the compactified curve integral, hence the nonempty open j(C) dense. The statement of `lem-sfb-relative-separated-dominant-ext` requires a reduced source, a separated target over the same base and equality after a dense-image morphism; these are precisely the hypotheses here, including equality of morphisms on C. I read `lem-sc-rigidity` in full: it needs X proper geometrically connected and geometrically reduced, Y connected, and Z separated locally of finite type. Each is verified in the step; geometric reducedness follows from the smoothness node and is not confused with ordinary reducedness. The zero restriction at w0 gives its constant-fibre hypothesis. Its factorization conclusion gives g, and composing with the section j(c) identifies g with the given zero axis as a morphism, not just a set map.

For step 5 the new node gives the dense family, with exactly the smooth geometrically integral curves required by step 3. I read `lem-weil-pbc-scheme-coproduct-family`: it allows an arbitrary set, not just a finite family, gives all summand inclusions, the union of their images, and the unique morphism out. Thus it combines the u_i and makes the two composites equal on the coproduct. The dense-image equality node then applies with reduced V. A set of representatives suffices for these finite-type k-schemes and their maps, so this is a small coproduct, not a proper class of curves.

For step 6 I read `lem-pc-rational-points-dense`, whose hypothesis is simply smoothness over a separably closed field; k is algebraically closed. It applies to the smooth product. The fibre-product universal property identifies every k-point with a pair, and step 5 proves equality of evaluations as k-morphisms. The coproduct node again packages this set of points. The final dense-image equality citation uses the reduced product proved in step 1. This avoids the incorrect inference that equality on a dense set alone implies equality of structure-sheaf maps on a nonreduced source.
