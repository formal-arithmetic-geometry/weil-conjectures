### Original Step 1 — lem-trf-proper-curve-normalized-component-cover

The structure map of $X$ is of finite type by [def-weil-proper-morphism]. A field is Noetherian (its ideals are $0$ and $(1)$), so [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian] makes $X$ Noetherian; [lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-finite-components] gives finitely many irreducible components. Give each its reduced induced closed structure using [lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme]. Each is integral, since it is reduced and irreducible, by [def-pc-integral-scheme]. These components cover $X$, and their dimensions are at most one: a chain of irreducible closed subsets of a closed subspace is such a chain in $X$ [def-kun-topological-krull-dimension]. Write the one-dimensional components as $C_1,\ldots,C_t$. Every remaining component is zero-dimensional and hence a single closed point: the generic point provided by [lem-weil-pbc-scheme-sober] would otherwise specialize along a chain of length one. It is isolated because it is disjoint from all other components, a finite closed union. Denote these reduced point components by $P_1,\ldots,P_r$.

### Original Step 2 — lem-trf-proper-curve-normalized-component-cover

The inclusions of these reduced components into $X$ are closed immersions, hence finite by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-finite], proper by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-proper], and proper over $k$ by [lem-weil-pbc-proper-composition]. In particular each $C_i$ is an integral finite-type proper curve. Apply [lem-sc-normalization-exists] to $\nu_i:\widetilde C_i\to C_i$: it is finite and surjective, $\widetilde C_i$ is proper over $k$, and $\dim\widetilde C_i=1$. It is integral directly from [def-sc-normalization]: on a nonempty affine $\operatorname{Spec}A\subset C_i$ its ring is the integral closure of the domain $A$ in $k(C_i)$, hence a domain, and the generic points on these charts agree in the same function field. Thus the proper integral curve criterion will apply to $\widetilde C_i$ over the original field $k$, even when $k$ is imperfect.

### Original Step 3 — cover construction to lem-trf-proper-curve-normalized-component-cover; descent application to existing lem-trf-finite-surjective-ample-reflection

### Original Step 4 — lem-trf-finite-coproduct-ample-with-points

For a finite disjoint union, the affine nonvanishing-locus definition [def-weil-pbc-ample-invertible-module] makes ampleness equivalent to ampleness on every summand. Indeed, in the forward direction restrict a witnessing section to a summand. Its nonvanishing locus is the inverse image of the original affine nonvanishing locus under a closed immersion; [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-finite] says that immersion is affine, so this inverse image is affine. Each summand is quasi-compact because it is closed in the quasi-compact union. Conversely, extend a witnessing section on one summand by zero on the others, using the sheaf gluing axiom on this disjoint open cover. Its nonvanishing locus is the same affine open, and a finite union of quasi-compact summands is quasi-compact. Hence this verifies both directions directly from the definition.

### Original Step 5 — lem-trf-finite-coproduct-ample-with-points

Every $P_j$ is the spectrum of a field: choose an affine neighbourhood in its singleton space; its ring $A$ is a nonzero domain with only the prime $(0)$, so any nonunit would lie in a maximal ideal and hence be zero, and $A$ is a field. An invertible module on $P_j$ is a one-dimensional vector space by [def-sc-invertible-module]; choose a nonzero basis section. With exponent $1$ its nonvanishing locus is all of the affine scheme $P_j$. Thus this restriction is ample by [def-weil-pbc-ample-invertible-module], and there is no condition on point components.
