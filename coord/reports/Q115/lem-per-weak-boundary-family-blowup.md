## STATEMENT-DISPUTED: exact tower length

Take any field k, X=Spec k[t], B empty, and three members Y_1=V(t(t-1)), Y_2=V(t), Y_3=V(t-1), with reduced structures. All are nowhere dense and regular, and every nonempty scheme-theoretic intersection is one of these or empty, hence regular. At each point each nonempty ideal is a parameter ideal, so the boundary condition holds. The distinct nonempty intersections form an intersection-closed family with three members. Its minimal members are Y_2 and Y_3, and their disjoint union is Y_1, an effective Cartier divisor. Blowing up its invertible ideal is the identity, but every Y_i is contained in the center, so every strict transform is empty. The tower has one step. There is a strict chain Y_2 strictly contained in Y_1 with two members (represented by index subsets {1,2} strictly containing {1}). Thus the imported assertion that the number of steps *equals* the maximal chain length is false.

ILO VIII Proposition 4.2.11, printed pp.121--122, makes the same equality claim without an irreducibility assumption. This is a source error in that generality, not merely a transcription issue. The source's intended termination and Cartier conclusion require only an upper bound, also consistent with its earlier use in VIII 4.2.7/4.2.13 and the subgroup-chain bound in the preceding construction. I replaced equality by “at most the maximal number of members in a strict chain of distinct nonempty intersections,” with height zero for the empty poset. I added no hypothesis and retained every geometric and principalization conclusion. Step 8 proves this corrected bound without assuming that a nonminimal disconnected member survives. In particular the parent equivariant-modification application still obtains its required finite canonical tower. No target, unit, or chapter statement was edited.
