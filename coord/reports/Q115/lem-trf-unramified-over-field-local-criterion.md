## Original proof and redistribution

### Old step 1

Suppose $f:Y\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is unramified. By [lem-trf-unramified-field-fibre-decomposition], $Y$ is a disjoint union of open subschemes $Y_i=\operatorname{Spec}L_i$, with $L_i/k$ finite separable. Each $Y_i$ has one point $y_i$ by [def-weil-affine-spectrum]; its complement is the union of the other open components, so $y_i$ is closed in $Y$. The neighbourhood $Y_i$ is the smallest open neighbourhood of $y_i$, so the stalk of the structure sheaf is its section ring $L_i$ (the colimit defining the stalk has this final neighbourhood). Its maximal ideal is zero, and the canonical quotient to its residue field [def-weil-residue-field] is consequently an isomorphism. These identifications respect the $k$-structure, since the disjoint-union isomorphism is over $k$. This proves every assertion in the forward implication, including the vacuous case of an empty index set.

Assigned to lem-trf-disjoint-field-spectra-point-stalk-data. The statement includes the actual germ map and the composite canonical residue map, so its future proof must establish compatibility over k, not only abstract field isomorphisms.

### Old step 2

Conversely assume the pointwise conditions. Fix $y\in Y$ and put $L=\kappa(y)$. The equality $\mathcal O_{Y,y}=L$ means that its canonical residue map is an isomorphism of $k$-algebras, not just an abstract ring isomorphism. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-separable-algebraic-differentials-base-change] to the tower $k\subseteq k\subseteq L$; its finiteness and separability hypotheses are exactly the hypotheses on $L/k$. It gives $\Omega_{L/k}\cong L\otimes_k\Omega_{k/k}=0$: the universal derivation of $k$ over itself is zero, so its representing module is zero by [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials]. Transport through the canonical residue isomorphism gives $\Omega_{\mathcal O_{Y,y}/k}=0$. By [lem-weil-pbc-formally-unramified-ring-iff-kahler-zero], the local ring map $k\to\mathcal O_{Y,y}$ is formally unramified. The local ring of the single point of $\operatorname{Spec}k$ is $k$, since the whole one-point spectrum is the smallest neighbourhood and its section ring is $k$. Thus this is the stalk map of $f$.

Remains verbatim in item step 2.

### Old step 3

Apply [lem-sbc-formally-unramified-stalk-neighbourhood] to $f$ at $y$, using its given local finite type hypothesis and the formally unramified stalk map proved in step 2. It gives an open neighbourhood $V_y$ of $y$ for which $V_y\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is formally unramified. These neighbourhoods cover $Y$ as $y$ varies. No finite cover or quasi-compactness assumption is needed.

Together with old step 4, assigned to lem-trf-formally-unramified-from-all-stalks. Its Mathlib match proves the same conclusion more generally without finite type. For the original locally finite type route, the existing neighbourhood theorem gives the required opens exactly as written.

### Old step 4

Here is the local-to-global uniqueness argument. Given a first order thickening $T\subset T'$ of affine schemes over $k$ and two lifts $a,b:T'\to Y$ agreeing on $T$, the topological equality $|T|=|T'|$ from [def-sbc-first-order-thickening] shows that $a$ and $b$ have the same underlying point map. The opens $a^{-1}(V_y)=b^{-1}(V_y)$ therefore cover $T'$. Cover each of these opens by affine opens $W$. The closed subscheme $T\cap W$ is affine, being cut out in the affine scheme $W$ by a square-zero ideal. The restrictions of $a,b$ to $W$ are lifts into $V_y$ of the same map on $T\cap W$. Formal unramifiedness of $V_y\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ gives their equality by [def-weil-pbc-formally-unramified-morphism]. Equality on this open cover is equality of morphisms: both their continuous maps and their sheaf maps agree locally. Hence $a=b$, proving that $f$ is formally unramified. This argument also applies to $Y=\varnothing$: a map $T'\to\varnothing$ exists only when $T'$ is empty, and is then unique.

The full original argument above remains the explicit lifting/gluing realization of lem-trf-formally-unramified-from-all-stalks under the item’s finite type assumption. In general Mathlib uses prime-local detection of the ring property (04E8) and affine locality (0HAL).

### Old step 5

The morphism $f$ is locally of finite type by hypothesis and formally unramified by step 4. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-lft-formally-unramified-unramified] to conclude that its diagonal is an open immersion and hence that $f$ is unramified. Together with step 1 this proves the equivalence. Closedness was needed only in the conclusion of the forward direction; the converse has in fact used only the stronger local-ring and residue-extension conditions already assumed.

Retained as the final assembly, now numbered 4, with step-reference updates only.
