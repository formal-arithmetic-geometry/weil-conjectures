### Original step 1

Put $K=\kappa^{\mathrm{sep}}$. For a finite separable subfield $e/\kappa$ of $K$, apply the finite separable field-lifting theorem over the henselian local ring $H$ to obtain a module-finite etale $H$-algebra $B_e$ with $B_e/\mathfrak m_HB_e\simeq e$. Every maximal ideal of $B_e$ contains $\mathfrak m_HB_e$ by the finite-algebra radical lemma. Since the quotient is the nonzero field $e$, its zero ideal is the only maximal ideal, so $B_e$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak n_e=\mathfrak m_HB_e$. Its inverse image in $H$ is $\mathfrak m_H$, so the structure map is local. This proves locality rather than assuming that an arbitrary finite etale algebra is local.

Cited statements: `lem-weil-pbc-henselian-finite-etale-field-lift`, `lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-local-radical`.
Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex, Tag 0BSL (remark-construct-sh-from-h), first sentence; Tag 04GK (lemma-henselian-cat-finite-etale), essential surjectivity.

### Original step 2

Reduction on homomorphisms between finite etale $H$-algebras is bijective. Lift each residue inclusion $e\subset f$ to its unique $H$-algebra map $u_{ef}:B_e\to B_f$. For $b\in B_e$, its residue maps to zero in $f$ exactly when its residue in $e$ is zero; hence $u_{ef}^{-1}(\mathfrak n_f)=\mathfrak n_e$, so the lift is local. The composite $u_{fg}u_{ef}$ and $u_{eg}$ have the same residue map, as do $u_{ee}$ and the identity; injectivity of reduction gives the diagram laws. For two lifts of the same identified field $e$, lift the identity in both directions; their composites are identities by the same argument. This gives precisely uniqueness up to unique residue-compatible isomorphism. The finite separable subfields of $K$ form a small directed poset containing $\kappa$, as in the filtered-colimit description of a separable closure. Thus this is a filtered diagram.

Cited statements: `lem-weil-pbc-henselian-finite-etale-map-bijective`, `def-pd-separable-closure`.
Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex, Tag 0BSL, arrows sentence, using Tag 04GK full faithfulness.

### Original step 3

The small diagram has a colimit $C$ in commutative $H$-algebras, which is also its colimit in commutative rings; every element of $C$ comes from some $B_e$. The residue maps $B_e\to e\subset K$ give $\psi:C\to K$. This map is surjective because every element of $K$ belongs to some $e$ and $B_e\to e$ is surjective. If $c$ is represented by $b\in B_e$, injectivity of $e\to K$ shows that $\psi(c)=0$ exactly when $b\in\mathfrak n_e$. Consequently $I=\ker\psi$ is the union of the images of the $\mathfrak n_e$. If $c\notin I$, its representative $b$ is outside the maximal ideal of the local ring $B_e$, hence is a unit, and its inverse maps to an inverse of $c$. Since $C/I=K$ is nonzero, $I$ is the unique maximal ideal. Thus $C$ is local with the specified residue identification, and each $B_e\to C$ is local.

Cited statements: `lem-weil-pbc-commutative-algebras-colimits-exist`, `lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-algebras-elementwise`, `def-pd-separable-closure`.
Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex, Tag 0BSL, union and residue sentences; Tag 04GI (lemma-colimit-henselian), omitted locality/residue argument expanded.

### Original step 4

Each $B_e$ is henselian by the finite-local-algebra permanence theorem applied to $H\to B_e$. Hence $(B_e,\mathfrak n_e)$ is a henselian pair. Step 2 shows that the transitions carry these ideals into one another, and Step 3 identifies their image union with $I=\mathfrak m_C$. Apply preservation of henselian pairs under directed colimits to obtain that $(C,\mathfrak m_C)$ is henselian. Since $C/\mathfrak m_C=K$ is separably closed, $C$ is strictly henselian. No Noetherian or completeness assumption is used.

Cited statements: `lem-weil-pbc-finite-local-henselian`, `lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-henselian-pair`, `def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology`, `def-pd-separable-closure`.
Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex, Tag 0BSL, henselianity sentences, using Tags 04GH (lemma-finite-over-henselian), 04GI (lemma-colimit-henselian); pair formulation: more-algebra.tex Tag 0FWT (lemma-filtered-colimits-henselian).

### Original step 5

As a filtered colimit of the etale $H$-algebras $B_e$, $C$ is ind-etale over $H$. The henselization ind-etaleness theorem applied to the maximal ideal of the local ring $A$ gives that $A\to H$ is ind-etale (identifying $A_{\mathfrak m}$ with $A$). Stability under composition therefore makes $A\to C$ ind-etale too. In particular both $H\to C$ and $A\to C$ have the filtered-etale presentations required by the henselian mapping lemma; their being filtered colimits is not being inferred from a universal-property definition of henselization alone.

Cited statements: `def-weil-pbc-ind-etale-algebra`, `lem-trf-henselization-prime-ind-etale`, `lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability`.
Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex, Tag 0BSL, last two sentences, using Tag 04GN (lemma-henselization) and Tag 0BSI (lemma-composition-colimit-etale).

### Original step 6

Let $D_A$ be the colimit of etale residue triples over $(A,\mathfrak m,K)$. The strict-henselization triples theorem gives that $D_A$ is henselian local, ind-etale over $A$, and has its canonical residue map onto $K$; the triples-colimit identification makes it a strict henselization in the pointed-algebra sense. Apply the henselian mapping lemma first with source $C$, target $D_A$, and then with source $D_A$, target $C$, over the base $A$, prescribing the identity of $K$ on residue fields in both cases. Its hypotheses hold by Steps 3--5 and the triples theorem, and both maximal ideals lie over $\mathfrak m$. The resulting local maps have composites with residue identity. Uniqueness in that same mapping lemma, compared with each identity map, proves that both composites are identities. Thus $C\simeq D_A$ canonically as an $A$-algebra respecting the chosen residue identification.

Cited statements: `lem-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-triples`, `lem-sc-etale-triples-colimit-is-strict-henselization`, `lem-weil-pbc-map-into-henselian-colimit`.
Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex, Tag 0BSL, final uniqueness sentence; Tags 08HR (lemma-map-into-henselian-colimit), 08HT (lemma-uniqueness-henselian), 04GW (lemma-strict-henselization-different).

### Original step 7

Apply the same comparison over the local base $H$, with its identified residue field $\kappa$, to the strict-henselization triple colimit $D_H$. Steps 3--5 make $C$ henselian local, ind-etale over $H$, with residue field $K$; the triples theorem gives these properties for $D_H$. The two maps provided by the henselian mapping lemma with residue identity are inverse by its uniqueness, giving $C\simeq D_H$ as an $H$-algebra. The comparison in Step 6 respects the canonical $H$-structure on $D_A$: the composite $H\to C\to D_A$ is local and extends $A\to D_A$, so the universal property defining $H$ identifies it with the canonical map $H\to D_A$. ### Original step 8

To compare with geometric neighbourhoods even for imperfect $\kappa$, choose an algebraic closure $\Omega$ of $K$ and the geometric point $\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ through $A\to\kappa\to K$. The relative separable closure of $\kappa$ in $\Omega$ is exactly the given $K$: one inclusion follows from separable algebraicity of $K/\kappa$; if $x\in\Omega$ is separable over $\kappa$, its minimal polynomial over $K$ divides a separable polynomial over $\kappa$, so $K(x)/K$ is finite separable and therefore trivial because $K$ is separably closed. For every etale neighbourhood with residue embedding into $\Omega$, the etale-residue-field theorem says this residue field is finite separable over $\kappa$. Every element of its image is therefore in $K$. This verifies, rather than assumes, the image-containment hypothesis of the chart/triple equivalence used next.

Cited statements: `lem-pc-algebraic-closure-exists-unique`, `def-pd-separable-closure`, `lem-pc-sepclosed-finite-sep-trivial`, `def-weil-pbc-relative-separable-closure`, `lem-weil-pbc-etale-algebra-residue-field-separable`.
Source: literature/stacks-project/etale-cohomology.tex, Tags 04HT (remark-etale-neighbourhoods), 04HX (lemma-describe-etale-local-ring), residue-closure choice and proof first paragraph.

### Original step 9

Use the affine-chart/triple equivalence with $Y=U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and the geometric point of Step 8. Every affine neighbourhood already factors through $U$, and Step 8 verifies the required residue-image condition. The equivalence identifies the opposite affine pointed-neighbourhood category with the residue-triple category and identifies their ring diagrams, including the structural and residue maps. A cocone on either diagram gives a cocone on the other by this equivalence; their universal factorization properties are identical. Thus their colimits are canonically isomorphic, with the same maps from $A$ and to $K$. Taking a small representative category as in the neighbourhood-colimit definition does not change that universal property. Hence the ring $D_A$ of Step 6 is the strict henselization ring of the pointed scheme-neighbourhood definition, and Step 6 gives the claimed comparison with $C$.

Cited statements: `lem-weil-pbc-etale-chart-residue-triples-equivalence`, `def-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-limit`, `def-weil-pbc-category-colimit`.
Source: literature/stacks-project/etale-cohomology.tex, Tag 04HX (lemma-describe-etale-local-ring), proof first paragraph and displayed triple colimit.

### Original step 10

The separable-closure Galois theorem shows that $K/\kappa$ is Galois. Choose a finite $\kappa$-basis of any finite separable subfield $e$; the finite-set-in-finite-Galois-subextension theorem supplies a finite Galois subfield $g\subset K$ containing that basis, hence containing $e$. It also supplies a common finite Galois overfield for two such $g$'s, by applying it to the union of their finite bases. To see directly that restriction gives the same colimit, extend any cocone on the Galois subdiagram by sending $B_e$ to its target through $B_e\to B_g$ for a chosen $g\supset e$. A common Galois overfield shows independence of this choice and compatibility for every $e\subset f$. Extension and restriction are inverse on cocones and their maps. Their universal properties therefore give the same colimit, canonically and respecting both the $H$-structure and the residue maps.

Cited statements: `lem-weil-pbc-separable-closure-exists-galois`, `lem-weil-pbc-finite-set-in-finite-galois-subextension`, `def-weil-pbc-category-colimit`.
Source: literature/stacks-project/fields.tex, Tag 09DT (lemma-normal-closure), Tag 0EXM (lemma-normal-closure-galois), applied inside the separable closure; cofinal restriction of Tag 0BSL.

`lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-local-radical`: Let $(R,\mathfrak m)$ be a local ring and $R\to B$ a ring map making $B$ a finitely generated $R$-module. Then every maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$ of $B$ has preimage $\mathfrak m$ in $R$, hence contains $\mathfrak mB$; consequently $\mathfrak mB$ is contained in the Jacobson radical of $B$, i.e. $1+x$ is a unit of $B$ for every $x\in\mathfrak mB$.

`lem-weil-pbc-henselian-finite-etale-map-bijective`: Let $(R,\mathfrak m,\kappa)$ be a henselian local ring [def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology] and S,T finite etale R-algebras [def-weil-etale-morphism]. Reduction gives a bijection from R-algebra homomorphisms S to T to kappa-algebra homomorphisms $\kappa\otimes_R S$ to $\kappa\otimes_R T$.

`def-pd-separable-closure`: Let $k$ be a field. A *separable closure* of $k$ is a field $k_s$
together with a $k$-embedding $k\hookrightarrow k_s$ such that $k_s$ is
separably closed and algebraic and
separable over $k$; it is the compositum of the finite separable subextensions
$K/k$ of $k_s$, so that

$$
k_s=\colim_{K/k\ \text{finite separable}}K
$$

as a filtered colimit of $k$-algebras, and $\Spec(k_s)\to\Spec(k)$ is
faithfully flat. We fix one such $k_s$ for each field $k$ and write $C_{k_s}$
for the base change of a $k$-scheme $C$ along
$\Spec(k_s)\to\Spec(k)$.

`lem-weil-pbc-commutative-algebras-colimits-exist`: Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $\mathcal C_R$ the category of commutative $R$-algebras (commutative rings $A$ with a ring homomorphism $R\to A$; morphisms are ring homomorphisms commuting with the structure maps). Then every diagram $D:I\to\mathcal C_R$ indexed by a small category $I$ (in particular every directed system $(A_i,\varphi_{i'i})_{i\le i'}$ over a directed set $I$) has a colimit [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit] in $\mathcal C_R$. For $R=\mathbf Z$ this says that every small diagram of commutative rings has a colimit.

`lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-algebras-elementwise`: Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $I$ a filtered category and $i\mapsto A_i$ a functor from $I$ to the category of commutative $R$-algebras, with a colimit $A=\varinjlim_IA_i$ in that category [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit] and coprojections $\iota_i:A_i\to A$. Then (1) $A$ with the maps $\iota_i$ is also a colimit of the underlying diagram of commutative rings; (2) every element of $A$ is of the form $\iota_i(a)$ for some $i\in I$ and some $a\in A_i$.

`lem-weil-pbc-finite-local-henselian`: Let R be a henselian local commutative ring [def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology] and A a local commutative R-algebra which is finite as an R-module. Then A is henselian [def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology].

`lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-henselian-pair`: Let $\Lambda$ be a directed set (a nonempty preordered set in which any two elements have a common upper bound, Stacks Categories Definition Tag 00D3) and $(A_\lambda,I_\lambda)_{\lambda\in\Lambda}$ a directed system of pairs: commutative rings $A_\lambda$ with ideals $I_\lambda$ and ring maps $\varphi_{\mu\lambda}:A_\mu\to A_\lambda$ for $\mu\le\lambda$ ($\varphi_{\lambda\lambda}=\mathrm{id}$, $\varphi_{\lambda\nu}\circ\varphi_{\mu\lambda}=\varphi_{\mu\nu}$) with $\varphi_{\mu\lambda}(I_\mu)\subseteq I_\lambda$. Assume every pair $(A_\lambda,I_\lambda)$ is henselian [def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology]. Let $A=\varinjlim_\lambda A_\lambda$ be the colimit of commutative rings [def-weil-pbc-category-colimit], with coprojections $\alpha_\lambda:A_\lambda\to A$, and $I=\bigcup_\lambda\alpha_\lambda(I_\lambda)$. Then $I$ is an ideal of $A$ contained in the Jacobson radical of $A$, and $(A,I)$ is a henselian pair.

`def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology`: A pair $(A,I)$ consisting of a commutative ring and an ideal is *henselian*
if, for every finite $A$-algebra $B$, every idempotent of $B/IB$ lifts
uniquely to an idempotent of $B$. A local ring is *henselian* when its
maximal-ideal pair is henselian, and *strictly henselian* when it is
henselian and its residue field is separably closed. For
$T=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and an ideal $I$, write
$T_0=\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)$ [def-weil-affine-spectrum] for the closed subscheme.

`def-weil-pbc-ind-etale-algebra`: Let $A$ be a commutative ring and let $B$ be an $A$-algebra. The
$A$-algebra $B$ is *ind-etale* if it is a filtered colimit of etale
$A$-algebras of finite presentation [def-weil-pbc-scheme-conventions]. It is
*ind-Zariski* if such a presentation can be chosen with terms finite products
of localizations $A_f$. The corresponding affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}B$
is an inverse limit of affine etale schemes, with affine transition maps. The
ring map $A\to B$ is *weakly etale* when the induced map
$\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is weakly etale in
[def-weil-pbc-scheme-conventions]; equivalently, $B$ is flat as an $A$-module
and is flat as a module over $B\otimes_A B$ via the multiplication map
$B\otimes_A B\to B$.

`lem-trf-henselization-prime-ind-etale`: Let $A$ be a commutative ring, let $\mathfrak p\subset A$ be a prime ideal, and let $H$ be an ordinary henselization of $A_{\mathfrak p}$ [def-weil-pbc-henselization-at-prime]. Then the canonical composite $A\to A_{\mathfrak p}\to H$ is ind-etale [def-weil-pbc-ind-etale-algebra]. No Noetherian hypothesis is imposed.

`lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability`: The classes of ind-etale [def-weil-pbc-ind-etale-algebra] and ind-Zariski
maps are stable under base change, composition, and filtered colimits. An
ind-etale map is weakly etale. If $B,C$ are ind-etale over $A$, every
$A$-algebra map $B\to C$ is ind-etale. Weakly etale maps are stable under
composition and base change.
