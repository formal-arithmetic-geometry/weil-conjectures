## Preserved argument for the new lemma

The following is the retained former Step 8 verbatim, for the future worker, not a proof placed in the unsplit node. Its reference to Step 7 means the site-comparison adjunction calculation still present in the parent. That calculation can be developed as its own dependency when splitting the new lemma.

**Arbitrary-base rigidity claim.** For every scheme $S$, put $X=\mathbf A^1_S$, let $p:X\to S$ be the projection, and let $e:S\to X$ be the unit section. If $M$ is a finite locally constant pro-etale $\Lambda$-module sheaf on $S$, then every endomorphism $h:p^*M\to p^*M$ with $e^*h=\operatorname{id}_M$ is the identity. No separatedness, finite-type, quasi-compactness, or flatness hypothesis on $S$ is part of this claim. By [lem-sf-proetale-locally-constant-origin](b), $M\simeq\nu_S^*G$ for a finite locally constant etale module sheaf $G$. Step 7, applied to $p$, gives $p^*M\simeq\nu_X^*p_{\mathrm{et}}^*G$. The full faithfulness of [thm-weil-pbc-site-comparison] on bounded-below derived categories, together with [lem-weil-pbc-derived-single-fully-faithful] for degree-zero objects, identifies endomorphisms of $p^*M$ with endomorphisms of $p_{\mathrm{et}}^*G$, and identifies their restrictions along $e$.

Put $H=\mathcal Hom_{\Lambda_S}(G,G)$ using [def-pur-sheaf-hom]. There is a canonical isomorphism $p^{-1}H\simeq\mathcal Hom(p_{\mathrm{et}}^*G,p_{\mathrm{et}}^*G)$: on an etale cover trivializing $G$ with finite module value $N$, both sheaves are the constant sheaf with value $\operatorname{End}_\Lambda(N)$, and the canonical map sends an endomorphism to its pullback. For a finite constant module $N$, choose a finite presentation $\Lambda^r\to\Lambda^s\to N\to0$; this exists because a finite generating set for $N$ has a kernel contained in the finite set $\Lambda^s$, hence finitely generated. The sheaf of maps $N\to N$ is the kernel of the corresponding map $\underline N^s\to\underline N^r$; inverse image preserves this finite limit and constant modules. Naturality in the presentation and the trivialization globalizes the comparison. The base changes of the etale trivializing cover cover $X$ by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-covering-base-change].

Thus $h$ corresponds to a section $z\in\Gamma(X,p^{-1}H)$. The geometric fibres of $p$ are $\operatorname{Spec}(\Omega[t])$ by [def-sbc-affine-space], and are connected because $\Omega[t]$ is a domain and its zero prime is dense. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-etale-connected-fibres-section-compatible] to $z$: it gives $p_1^*z=p_2^*z$ on $X\times_S X$, with no finiteness assumptions. Pull back along $r=(\operatorname{id}_X,e\circ p):X\to X\times_S X$. Since $p_1r=\operatorname{id}_X$ and $p_2r=e\circ p$, the unit-compatible comparisons [lem-weil-pbc-sets-etale-inverse-image-composition-units] give $z=p^*e^*z$, hence $h=p^*e^*h$. Under the hypothesis $e^*h=\operatorname{id}_M$, this says $h=\operatorname{id}_{p^*M}$. This is the rigidity principle used below.

The former Step 9 is also retained here to preserve the interrupted work:

Let $b:B'\to B$ be an arbitrary base change and let $b_1:\mathbf A^1_{B'}\to\mathbf A^1_B$ be its affine-line base change. The canonical comparisons of [lem-pur-dnc-section-geometry](5) identify the primed family, section invariant and punctured comparison with the pullbacks of $F,C(g,s),\kappa^\circ$; clause (1), together with the composition comparison of [lem-weil-pbc-proetale-derived-pullback-composition], identifies its fibre comparison with $b^*\theta$. Therefore $b_1^*\kappa:F'\xrightarrow{\sim}a'{}^*C(g',s')$ is a normalized comparison and extends the primed punctured comparison. By exact termwise inverse image [def-weil-derived-pullback-tensor-hom], Step 3 pulls back to $F'\simeq b_1^*M_F[2d]$ and $C(g',s')\simeq b^*M_1[2d]$. These sheaves are finite locally constant: by [lem-sf-proetale-locally-constant-origin] choose etale finite local models, pull their trivializing covers back using [lem-weil-pbc-etale-covering-base-change], and use Step 7 to compare the two sites. A constant finite module stays the same constant module under inverse image, since inverse image preserves the final object and coproducts. In particular $M'=b^*M_1$ is finite lisse.

For any other normalized comparison $\eta$, the automorphism $h=\eta\circ(b_1^*\kappa)^{-1}$ of $a'{}^*C(g',s')$ becomes, after cancelling the common shift by [lem-weil-pbc-derived-single-fully-faithful], an automorphism of $a'{}^*M'$ whose restriction along the unit section is the identity. Apply the **arbitrary-base rigidity claim of Step 8** with $S=B'$ and $M=M'$; its hypotheses hold for this arbitrary scheme $B'$, and it gives $h=\operatorname{id}$. Hence $\eta=b_1^*\kappa$. No use of [lem-pur-lisse-uniqueness] is made over $B'$; that finite-type lemma occurs only in Step 5 over the separated finite-type coordinate bases $B_i$. For two successive base changes the comparison and the iterated pullback have the same normalization, so the same arbitrary-base rigidity claim identifies them; identity base change is immediate. Etale localization is a special case. This proves clause (4).
