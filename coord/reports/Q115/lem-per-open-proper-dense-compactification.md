For Step 1 I read every cited statement. The alteration definition supplies finite type, maximal dominance, and finite residue extensions, exactly as ILO 1.2.2 requires. pqf-def-locnoeth uses an affine Noetherian covering, so the finite covering in the item supplies its hypothesis directly. The spectrum quasi-compactness leaf explicitly supplies compactness of each affine, and taking a finite union gives compactness of S. The locally-Noetherian quasi-separatedness leaf supplies the remaining Nagata base hypothesis (Stacks properties.tex 01OY). Separatedness of f is not inferred from the definition of properness alone: its diagonal is identified by the displayed fibre-product universal property with the proper map z's diagonal, using that an open immersion is a monomorphism. The open-immersion statement explicitly identifies its source with its open image, which proves that monomorphism claim. No separatedness of S over an absolute base is needed.

For Step 2 the dense Nagata statement gives the full sheaf injection, not merely topological dominance. def-nagata-compactification gives precisely j open, b proper and the factorization. I checked the empty-complement argument using def-pc-scheme's locally ringed space condition: all rings on opens inside a missing W would be zero, and those opens are cofinal for a stalk at a point of W, forcing a forbidden zero local ring. This is the same direct stalk argument used in the reviewed proper-dense-open lemma; no affine-neighbourhood shrinking is silently used. The properness definition gives finite type of b, and the finite-type-over-Noetherian leaf (Stacks morphisms.tex 01T6) gives Noetherian B. Its locally-Noetherian-plus-quasi-compact convention agrees with Step 1's interpretation of the finite affine-cover hypothesis.

For Step 3 the open-preimage fibre-product leaf explicitly identifies B_U with b^-1(U). k's open-immersion structure is the restriction of the already given isomorphism Z onto j(Z), whose image lies in that open. The separated-base-change leaf is applied to the separated proper map b; the proper-to-separated leaf is applied to Z proper over U and B_U separated over U, not to an arbitrary target. The proper-closed-image statement then supplies exactly the closedness used. Density in B_U follows from the elementary definition of density on nonempty opens of an open subset. A surjective open immersion is an isomorphism by the defining isomorphism onto its open image. I checked Stacks 01JR (open fibre products), the proper/closed-image part of morphisms.tex 01W6, and the statements of all six cited nodes. There is no appeal to scheme-theoretic density after a non-flat base change.

For Step 4 I read the finite-component leaf (Stacks properties.tex 0BA8) and the scheme-sobriety leaf (Stacks schemes.tex 01IS). They supply finiteness and existence/uniqueness, which the definition of generic point alone would not supply. In a finite component set, removing every other component gives a nonempty open containing the fixed generic point. Density forces a point of j(Z) there. Every nonempty open of the fixed component contains its generic point, by the closure definition. Under an open-subscheme isomorphism, the intersection C intersect j(Z) is irreducible and dense in C; maximality in the open follows by closing any putative larger irreducible subset in B. These are direct topology arguments from the cited component/generic-point definitions. Equality of the structure sheaves on an open gives equality of local rings and residue fields, so the alteration object's generic-point and finite-field-extension clauses transfer to b. This does not assume B is reduced, although schematic density of reduced Z would imply that stronger assertion.

For Step 5 I reopened lem-per-generic-fibre-finiteness-criterion and read its final clause: it asserts finiteness of the actual scheme fibre, including possible nilpotents. Its hypotheses are precisely a Noetherian S, finite-type b, maximal dominance, and finite extensions at component generic points. Steps 1, 2 and 4 have established each. ILO II 1.1.7 is the source, with generic points lying in its dense finite locus because the base is Noetherian. I do not use merely finite cardinality of the fibre.

For Step 6 the component-cover node explicitly asserts every point lies in a component, and the sobriety node gives its generic point. Continuity carries specialization to specialization. Two component generic points cannot be proper specializations of one another: their component closures would be nested, contradicting maximality. Sobriety on S then identifies the points. The resulting generic point belongs to U since its preimage is in j(Z), contradicting the chosen s outside U. This proves that no B-point maps to s; the commuting fibre projections then prohibit any fibre point, without needing a converse point-lifting theorem for fibre products. Thus this elementary step proves the extra clause rather than moving it into a new node.

I tested the statement and the argument on S empty; U empty; Z empty; S=Spec(k); U=S; a disconnected reduced base with U missing one component; and the union of two crossing irreducible curves with U missing the generic point of one component. In the last case a boundary may lie over the intersection of the components, while the omitted component's generic fibre is empty, as Step 6 asserts. In the disconnected case no component can be added over the omitted base component since density would fail. A proper map of positive-dimensional generic fibre is correctly excluded by the alteration-object finite-extension hypothesis. Nonreduced finite fibres are allowed, and finiteness in Step 5 concerns their coordinate algebra, not just their support.
