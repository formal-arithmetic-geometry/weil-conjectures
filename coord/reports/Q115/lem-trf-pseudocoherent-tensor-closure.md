### Original step 1

Allocation: Existing lem-weil-pbc-source-derived-tensor-upper-bound (exact reuse; no duplicate node).

First record the upper-bound calculation used below. If $X,Y\in D(R)$ have cohomology zero above $u,v$, choose representing complexes and replace them by their good truncations $\tau_{\le u}X^\bullet,\tau_{\le v}Y^\bullet$. The truncation inclusions are quasi-isomorphisms, since they retain all the possibly nonzero cohomology. Apply the bounded-flat replacement statement on the one-object site with only the identity covering and structure ring $R$: ordinary $R$-modules identify with module sheaves on this site. It supplies flat complexes $F,G$, zero in degrees above $u,v$, respectively. They are K-flat by the bounded-flat criterion. The derived tensor is represented by $\operatorname{Tot}(F\otimes_R G)$. In degree $k>u+v$ each summand has $p>u$ or $q>v$ and is zero. Thus $H^k(X\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}Y)=0$ for $k>u+v$. This is just the total-degree calculation after applying the cited replacements.

Original citations: def-kun-truncations, lem-pc-kflat-bounded-flat-replacement, lem-pc-module-bounded-flat-kflat, lem-pc-derived-tensor-second-factor-functor, def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves.

### Original step 2

Allocation: Assembly step 1.

Put $t=\max(m+a,n+b)$. If $n>a$, the preceding calculation gives vanishing of $K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}L$ above $a+b<t$ because $t\ge n+b>a+b$. The map from the zero finite-free complex is therefore an isomorphism in degrees greater than $t$ and surjective in degree $t$. It witnesses $t$-pseudo-coherence. If $m>b$, use instead $t\ge m+a>a+b$ and the same zero witness. Hence it remains to treat $n\le a$ and $m\le b$.

Original citations: def-pc-ring-m-pseudo-coherent.

### Original step 3

Allocation: lem-trf-derived-tensor-approximation-comparison; the final Q-bound deduction stays in assembly step 2.

Choose bounded finite-free witnesses $\alpha:P^\bullet\to K$ and $\beta:Q^\bullet\to L$ from the definition of $n$- and $m$-pseudo-coherence. Write $C_\alpha,C_\beta$ for cone objects in distinguished triangles $P\to K\to C_\alpha\to P[1]$ and $Q\to L\to C_\beta\to Q[1]$, with $P,Q$ the images of the complexes in $D(R)$. ### Original step 4

The cone of $\alpha\otimes 1_Q:P\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}Q\to K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}Q$ is $C_\alpha\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}Q$, whose cohomology vanishes above $(n-1)+b$ by step 1. It is its own inverse; the tensor differential formula gives equal coefficients $(-1)^{pq+q}$ on $y\otimes d x$ and $(-1)^{pq}$ on $d y\otimes x$ on both sides. This natural chain isomorphism descends through K-flat models. Thus the cone of $1_K\otimes\beta:K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}Q\to K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}L$ is $K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}C_\beta$, with cohomology zero above $a+(m-1)$ by step 1.

### Original step 5

For a triangle $A\xrightarrow f B\to C\to A[1]$ with $H^i(C)=0$ for $i\ge t$, its long exact sequence makes $H^i(f)$ an isomorphism for $i>t$ and $H^t(f)$ surjective: the adjacent groups $H^{i-1}(C),H^i(C)$ vanish when $i>t$, and $H^t(C)=0$ suffices for surjectivity. In step 4 both cones vanish in degrees $i\ge t$, since $t\ge n+b$ and $t\ge m+a$. Each of the two maps is therefore an isomorphism above $t$ and surjective at $t$. Their composite $\alpha\otimes^{\mathbf L}\beta:P\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}Q\to K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}L$ has the same properties, by functoriality of tensor and composition of surjections.

### Original step 6

Each finite-free module is flat: after a choice of finite basis its tensor functor is a finite direct sum of the identity functor, and exactness is checked coordinatewise. Hence the bounded complexes $P^\bullet,Q^\bullet$ are K-flat by the cited criterion, and $P\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}Q$ is represented by $E^\bullet=\operatorname{Tot}(P^\bullet\otimes_R Q^\bullet)$. This is bounded and finite free in each degree. Indeed, for finite bases indexed by $I_p,J_q$, the tensors of their basis vectors indexed by $I_p\times J_q$ form a basis of $P^p\otimes_R Q^q$, with inverse sending each pure tensor to its coordinate products; the finite disjoint union over $p+q=k$ gives a finite basis of $E^k$. Bounds on the degrees of $P^\bullet,Q^\bullet$ give the sum of these bounds for $E^\bullet$. Together with step 5, the map $E^\bullet\to K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}L$ is the required witness in the definition of $t$-pseudo-coherence.
