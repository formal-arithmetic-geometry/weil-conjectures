## Auxiliary arguments and Mathlib audit

For the positive-grading input, use a lattice basis and a finite nonzero generating list. Sharpness excludes a nonzero relation with nonnegative integer coefficients summing to zero. The rational linear equations then exclude zero from the convex hull of the generators (a nonnegative real solution of rational linear equations admits a rational one). The nearest point of that compact convex hull to zero is nonzero and has strictly positive inner product with every generator. Approximate this functional by a rational one, preserving finitely many strict inequalities, then clear its denominators. This proves the stated integral functional. The empty generating list allows the zero homomorphism. This is a convex separation lemma, not the domain or dimension clause of the item.

For the coefficient input, positivity bounds every decomposition length by w(p). Expanding b^n in the monoid basis shows that its coefficient ideal at p is m^{max(n-nu(p),0)}. A maximum-length decomposition yields that ideal, and any term contributing to b^n has at least the required coefficient power. A finite generating list with maximum weight M shows nu(p)>=w(p)/M and hence finitely many p have nu(p)<n. Thus each finite quotient is the stated direct sum. For fixed p the compatible quotients determine an element of the complete coefficient ring A; conversely an arbitrary family defines all finite quotient elements because there are finitely many surviving indices. These maps are inverse and respect convolution. Every fixed coefficient sum is finite because w(u),w(v)<=w(p), and the positive-weight sets are finite. The projection kernel is the actual ideal power, not just its closure, by Stacks 05GG for the finitely generated ideal b. This is an identification and filtration lemma for arbitrary complete Noetherian local A; it assumes no regularity and asserts neither a domain nor a dimension formula. It cannot replace the item's monomial growth or least-term arguments.

For the Hilbert--Samuel input, Stacks 00K8 supplies the eventual polynomial chi(n)=length(R/n^{n+1}). Definition 00KA and Proposition 00KQ identify its degree with dim R. Substituting n-1 gives the stated polynomial. Since the lengths are positive and the polynomial is nonzero on all sufficiently large integers, its leading coefficient is positive. This is a theorem for every nonzero Noetherian local ring, not a monoid-ring restatement.
