Old steps 2–3 are assigned to the first new node. The future proof has the following complete argument available, retained here rather than put in the statement-only node. For an S–R bimodule P, apply the existing mixed right-unitor theorem to its tensor with the regular R–R bimodule, then forget the right action. The defining coequalizer is the displayed coequalizer for T_P(R). This gives an S-linear c with c q_R = r_P and c inverse equal to q_R after insertion of the R-unit and the ambient right-unitor inverse. Both linearity and invertibility are part of that mixed-unitor result; R and S need not coincide. To pass to finite rank, tensoring P and P tensor R preserves finite coproducts. Distribute both objects in the coequalizer defining T_P(R^k) over the k regular summands. A map out of this coequalizer is exactly a family of maps out of P tensor R coequalizing the regular pair, one for each summand: the compatibility is checked on each injection of the source coproduct. Thus the induced T_P(injection) exhibit T_P(R^k) as the coproduct of k copies of T_P(R), in left S-modules as well: the module linearity equation is checked after the quotient, using preservation of its coequalizer by S tensor –. Define c_k using c on every summand and define its inverse using c inverse on every summand. Their two composites become identities after each injection, hence are identities by coproduct uniqueness. Substitution of the regular inverse gives exactly the inverse-component formula stated in the new node. The empty family proves T_P(0) is initial and its unique map to zero is an isomorphism. This extraction includes the nontrivial comparison of the balanced coequalizer with the finite coproduct; it does not merely name the rank-one specialization of the already existing mixed-unitor leaf.

Old step 4 is assigned to the second new node and the presentation transport in parent step 3. For an H preserving zero maps and the cokernel of f, H(q_f) has the cokernel universal property for H(f). Given a:H(A) ≅ A' and b:H(B) ≅ B', set g = b H(f) a inverse and q' = H(q_f) b inverse. Then q'g = 0. If h:B' → N kills g, the map h b kills H(f) because b H(f) = g a. There is a unique v with v H(q_f) = h b, equivalently v q' = h. Hence q' is a cokernel of g. Cokernel uniqueness gives u and its inverse with the two equations stated in the new leaf. The exact Mathlib implementation is the composite of `PreservesCokernel.iso H f` and `cokernel.mapIso (H.map f) g a b`; `PreservesCokernel.π_iso_hom`, the definition of `cokernel.map`, and `cokernel.π_desc` give the normalization. The inverse equation is obtained by composing with the inverse isomorphisms; uniqueness follows from the cokernel projection being epi. Applying E to a chosen presentation isomorphism p and composing with u gives the desired presentation of E(M).

Old step 5 remains parent step 4. The ranks stay finite and f_S is S-linear, so the resulting presentation is precisely the definition's witness. No use of kernel preservation or flatness is hidden here.
