## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported phrase "its tensor with any A-module" does not specify derived tensor. On the ordinary reading, the claimed equivalence with a resolution bound r is false. Take A = F_p[t] and I = (t). All P_n = F_p[t]/(t^n) are finite sets and perfect, with the two-term resolution A --t^n--> A. Ordinary P_1 tensor_A M is in degree zero for every module M, so the ordinary-tensor condition with r = 0 is automatic. But P_1 is not projective: a splitting of A -> A/(t) would send 1 to an element a with ta = 0, forcing a = 0 in the domain A, inconsistent with its quotient being 1. Also Tor_1^A(P_1,P_1) = P_1 is nonzero. The source's projective-resolution/Tor intent therefore requires derived tensor.

The replacement statement writes P_n tensor_A^L M explicitly and defines it by a projective resolution, with H^(-i) = Tor_i. It preserves the hypotheses, all n >= 1, the uniform bound, finite projectivity and the zero quotient. It replaces the reference "as in def-weil-pbc-adic-coefficient-datum" by the actual algebraic objects A/I^n and their quotient/power definition: the original statement only borrows these ring quotients and does not introduce a scheme, a sheaf coefficient ring or completed coefficients. A finite ring is automatically additive torsion, so even that clause of the coefficient datum introduces no lost assumption here. The removed sheaf datum would be an irrelevant statement dependency and a risk of confusing algebraic A-modules with completed-coefficient sheaves.
