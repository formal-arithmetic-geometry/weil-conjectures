## Review by NI

I checked every citation against the statement of its node, rather than its proof. The following records the mathematical use, including the hypotheses that matter. `citations.json` supplies verbatim statement quotations for every one of the 32 step/node pairs.

- Step 1 uses `lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme` for the affine quotient description, reducedness and closed support. Its radical-ideal description applies to any closed subset, hence to the given component. A quotient of a finitely generated algebra is finitely generated, so the finite cover of C supplies a finite affine cover of R. `def-weil-finite-type-morphism` supplies precisely this finite-cover criterion. `pqf-lem-locnoeth-lft` and `pqf-lem-lft-fp-locnoeth` apply over Spec k: a field is Noetherian since its only ideals are zero and the unit ideal. `lem-weil-pbc-locally-noetherian-quasi-separated` gives quasi-separatedness of R, and clause (1) of `lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence` passes it to the structure morphism. This proves finite presentation even for a nonseparated C; no affine intersection was assumed affine.
- Step 2 uses the chosen-closure criterion `lem-trf-geometric-reducedness-algebraic-closure-test`, whose statement allows any chosen algebraic closure and imposes no finite-type restriction. Here the identity k/k is an algebraic closure. `lem-sbc-subset-geometric-irreducibility` applies with Omega=k, X=R and subset the whole space. Algebraic closedness implies separable closedness, and R is nonempty and irreducible by construction. These statements therefore give the required reducedness and irreducibility of R_L; no descent of reducedness from a nonreduced C is assumed.
- Step 3 uses `lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra` for the ring on each inverse-image chart, `lem-weil-pbc-field-extension-base-change-faithfully-flat` for B to B tensor_k L (arbitrary B is allowed), and `lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-surjective-spectrum` for actual point-surjectivity. Their statements fit in succession and prove the global assertion on a cover.
- Step 4 uses `lem-weil-pbc-field-tensor-irreducible` with base k and fields L,E to verify geometric irreducibility of Spec L. The same affine-fibre-product statement identifies its field base changes. The statement of `lem-sc-product-irreducible-components-bijection` then applies to U=Spec L and V=C; V is allowed to be any scheme. To identify the corresponding component with the inverse image, the proof writes the affine support as the vanishing locus of the extended ideal, using the explicit tensor-quotient isomorphism, and then uses maximality of an irreducible component together with Step 2. This is an argument from the stated bijection, not an extra clause borrowed from its proof.
- Step 5 uses `lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change` for the base-changed closed immersion and clause (3) of `lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme` for uniqueness. Reducedness and support were already proved. I marked this step elementary because it is just substitution into these two statements and the established component correspondence; there is no hidden reducedness/base-change lemma.
- Step 6 uses the actual two-chart description in `def-sc-p1-standard`; each chart is polynomial affine and the intersection is explicitly the Laurent affine chart. `lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs` supplies compactness of that intersection, and clause (2) of `lem-weil-pbc-quasi-separated-affine-cover-criterion` supplies quasi-separatedness from the cover. `pqf-lem-lft-fp-locnoeth` supplies local finite presentation over the field, and qcqs morphism permanence supplies the morphism finiteness conditions. `def-nagata-projective-space-base-change` identifies subsequent projective lines by reassociation of fibre products; it is not used for a theorem about Proj charts that its statement omits.
- Step 7 uses `lem-kun-algebra-union`, whose assertion is precisely a filtered union of finitely generated k-subalgebras, and `lem-weil-pbc-spec-colimit-limit` for the resulting scheme limit and affine transitions. The affine-scheme and qcqs-permanence statements give the remaining hypotheses of approximation and preservation of finite presentation. The index is a set of subalgebras of the fixed set L, contains k, and is directed by adjoining the union of two finite generating sets, so there is no size or nonemptiness gap.
- Step 8 uses clause (2) of `thm-pd-spreading-out` twice, once in each direction. The statement explicitly guarantees that the descended morphism base changes to the given one. The models are R and P over the stage k, both finitely presented by Steps 1 and 6. The stages are affine and qcqs by Step 7. Taking the algebra generated by the two stages gives a common stage; there is no use of separatedness or properness.
- Step 9 uses clause (3) of that same theorem for each of the two inverse equations. The maps in each pair have the same finitely presented source and target model, and their base changes agree by the two identities of alpha,beta. A common upper stage preserves both equations because base change preserves compositions and identities. Thus the prescribed isomorphism and its inverse, not merely two unrelated maps, descend.
- Step 10 uses `lem-weil-common-extension-fields-maximal-ideal` for a nontrivial A (it is a subalgebra of a field). `lem-weil-pbc-closed-point-residue-field-base` applies to the closed maximal point of Spec A, which is locally finite type over the algebraically closed k. It gives an isomorphism of the structural residue-field map, so the resulting map A to k is a k-algebra map. Pullback of the two inverse equations yields the k-isomorphism, with the projective-line identifications from Step 6. This is not evaluation at an arbitrary non-rational point.
- Step 11 uses only the base-change definition of projective space and the definition of an irreducible component. Pullback of mutually inverse maps remains mutually inverse. The empty scheme has no components, so all statements indexed by a component are vacuous. I marked this assembly step elementary.
