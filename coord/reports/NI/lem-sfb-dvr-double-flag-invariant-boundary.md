## Corrections and mathematical issues

There is no statement dispute in this item. I retained the imported definitions and every conclusion. In particular no reduced-fibre or completeness hypothesis was added, and no inference is made that invariants commute with reduction modulo the maximal ideal. The character convention is essential: the right multiplier is +rho, the line is M_rho (equivalently the usual L_{-rho}), and the normalized function satisfies the inverse left Borel transformation law. Using the opposite sign would reverse the ample line.

The original item had no proof. Its source sentence in Alper motivates the double-flag argument but does not by itself establish the integral torsor and coaction assertions. The new proof makes these assertions explicit. Two substantial supporting arguments have their own statement-only nodes: a comparison of relative parabolics, and primitive integral normalization. Neither new node asserts a double-flag boundary or repeats a clause of the item's conclusion. The assembly constructs that section and the quotient itself.

During self-review I replaced an incorrect shortcut from projective-bundle projectivity to a free ambient bundle. The existing projectivity theorem only gives a finite-type quasi-coherent module, not a locally free one. Finite generators give a polynomial-to-symmetric-algebra graded surjection, and the cited relative-Proj functoriality gives the required finite-projective-space embedding. I also added explicit citations for the affine flatness criterion and flat-implies-torsionfree where integrality of the total space is deduced from the geometric generic fibre.

## Intended proof retained for the new normalization node

This is supporting proof material for a later worker, not a split of the new node. Put R=A[H], choose a uniformizer pi, and fix m≥1. The existing DVR rho-section node gives a finite free module W_m of actual regular functions and its full rational coaction. The coordinate algebras of H and B are A-flat since the groups are smooth. The defining right-character equation is the kernel of the difference of two A-linear maps R→R⊗A A[B]. Its target is torsion-free. Consequently W_m is pi-saturated in R: if pi r satisfies the equation, cancel pi to obtain the equation for r. Thus W_m/pi W_m injects into R/pi R.

Let P be the kernel in W_m of the left Borel coaction minus the character m rho: equivalently f(bg)=rho(b)^{-m}f(g), as a regular-function identity on B×H. The target W_m⊗A A[B] is torsion-free, so P is also pi-saturated in W_m. It is finite over the Noetherian DVR and torsion-free, hence finite free. Kernel formation commutes with localization and then algebraic closure E of the fraction field, since these base changes are flat. The same flat-kernel argument identifies W_m⊗E with the actual induced module E_B(-m rho), which is nonzero because W_m is finite free of positive rank. The existing field primitive-line lemma says that its unipotent invariants are a one-dimensional torus eigenspace with weight w_0(-m rho)=m rho. Since B=T⋉V, this is exactly the left Borel character kernel defining P_E. Therefore P has rank one.

Choose a generator f of P. It is not in pi W_m: otherwise cancellation in the coaction equation would put f/pi in P, contradicting that f generates a free rank-one A-module. By the previous saturation injection, its reduction is a nonzero regular function on H_kappa. On the dense longest cell the two Borel transformation laws give f(u n_0 b)=rho(b)^m f(n_0). If f(n_0) were in the maximal ideal, its reduction would vanish on this dense open of the integral group H_kappa, hence everywhere, a contradiction. Thus f(n_0) is a unit of A. Divide f by it. This gives the normalized function in the new statement, for every m≥1, without assuming anything about nonflat base change of invariant kernels. All equations were Hopf coaction equations, so hold on every test algebra. The open-cell hypothesis is already available before this node is applied; the proof is not circular through the double-flag item.

On Spec of a local ring an open neighborhood of its closed point is the whole base. For Borels the zero tangent weight is Lie(T), and each nonzero weight is the relevant root line, giving the stated Borel consequence. For the normalization node I read Milne Proposition 22.22 and wrote out the integral saturation argument above; I did not attribute that extra lattice argument to a theorem about fields.
