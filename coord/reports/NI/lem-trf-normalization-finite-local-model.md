## Source error

The proof of Lemma 4.8.4 calls the localized inclusion a “local module-finite extension”. The inference from the preceding unique-prime construction establishes **local integral**, not module-finite. The hypotheses supply no module generators of the normalization; uniqueness of a prime and integrality are not a module-finiteness criterion. In particular the usual nonfinite unibranch normalization examples (Akizuki's one-dimensional Noetherian local domains) would violate that extra conclusion: take C=R and its unique normalization prime. The item's statement does not assert this extra conclusion, and needs no correction. Steps 6–9 instead prove integrality after localization and the exact equality with N_Q. Stacks 00OK then gives dimension equality for an injective integral extension without a finite-module assumption. No new finite-normalization input is delivered.

1. The finite-prime-fibre statement says precisely that the primes of N over q form a finite set. Its completion bound is unused. The integral-closure definition gives the integral extension R to N, and the incomparability statement applies to these primes with their identical contraction. No result about the Noetherian topology of Spec N is used, since that downstream theorem depends on this branch.
2. Prime avoidance supplies one element in Q avoiding all the other primes, because incomparability verifies every noncontainment. The r=1 case uses x=0 separately; a nonempty-family lemma is never applied to an empty family.
3. This is an elementary construction, not a restated input: the proof contracts a putative second prime first to R, uses the complete finite list, then uses membership of x to exclude every other member. Primality of p is checked directly.
4. The finite-type Noetherian statement applies to the explicit polynomial quotient C. The prime-localization definition supplies the local ring A. Domain and fraction-field assertions follow from the inclusions in K, without a hypothesis that R is normal.

6. The transitivity statement says exactly that integral ring maps compose. The integral closures in the definition are rings integral over their bases; applying transitivity to R, C, D proves D contained in N, while monic equations prove the other inclusion. 7. The localization-of-integral-closure statement gives the actual image equality and injectivity inside the localized ambient ring. Here S contains no zero and the ambient field remains K. Thus it gives B as the integral closure of A, hence an injective integral inclusion A into B.
8. The maximal-contraction statement applies to this integral extension. The localization prime correspondence says M is the extension of its contraction to N. Contraction to C equals p because contraction to A is pA. The already proved uniqueness identifies it with Q. B is nonzero, so it has a maximal ideal; therefore it has exactly the stated one. 9. The proof writes out both inclusions B=N_Q. Inverting an element outside Q is justified by its being outside the unique maximal ideal of B; a nonunit would generate a proper ideal and hence be contained in a maximal ideal. This is an elementary local-ring argument with the cited prime correspondence, not an additional normalization theorem.

The dimension definition supplies the supremum convention, including dimension zero. No height equality for an arbitrary finite extension is presumed; it follows only after the local identification.
