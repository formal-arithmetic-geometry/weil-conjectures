## Proof retained for the new algebra lemma

This is an independent agent argument, not a claim that Stacks 0EKK states the auxiliary. Let B=A_0, and for m in M let K_m be the ideal of B consisting of finite sums of products a b with a in A_m and b in A_{-m}. This is an ideal since multiplication by B preserves both graded pieces. Suppose K_m is proper. Choose a prime p of B containing K_m and put k=kappa(p) and C=A tensor_B k. Since the projection A to B onto degree zero is B-linear and splits B into A, the grading base changes to C=direct sum C_n with C_0=k, nonzero. No flatness of A or its pieces is needed: tensor preserves the split direct sum. The equality I=J persists after this base change because both ideals are generated by the images of the stated generators; the maps A_n to C_n generate C_n after scalar extension.

Choose a maximal proper homogeneous ideal H of C. It exists by Zorn: the union of a chain of proper homogeneous ideals is homogeneous and proper because it does not contain 1. Put D=C/H. Its zero component is k because a proper homogeneous ideal cannot contain a nonzero element of the field C_0. Every nonzero homogeneous d in D is invertible with a homogeneous inverse: the homogeneous ideal (d) must equal D by maximality, and projecting an equation d c=1 onto degree zero gives d c_{-deg d}=1. Thus N={n:D_n is nonzero} is a subgroup of M (products of homogeneous units are nonzero units).

The generator ideal in D[M] is exactly (t^n-1:n in N). For each n in N choose a nonzero d in D_n and cancel this unit in d(t^n-1); outside N there is no nonzero homogeneous generator. Consequently I=J implies (t^n-1:n in N)=(t^l-1:l in M). The quotient on the left is D[M/N]: map t^l to the coset of l; its kernel is precisely the displayed ideal, since a finite sum whose coset coefficients sum to zero can be rewritten as sums of c(t^l-t^{l_0}) within each coset. In D[M/N] the coefficient basis elements of distinct cosets remain distinct because D is nonzero. The equality with J therefore forces M/N=0, hence N=M.

But K_m maps to zero in C_0=k, so every product from C_m C_{-m} is zero. Since m is in N, a nonzero element d of D_m has inverse in D_{-m}. Lift these two homogeneous elements to C_m and C_{-m}; their product is zero by the preceding observation, whereas its image is d d^{-1}=1. Contradiction. Thus K_m=B and a finite expression 1=sum a_j b_j exists, which is exactly the new statement. For A=0 use the empty sum; for m=0 use 1 times 1. This proof works for arbitrary abelian M and does not assume finite generation, Noetherianness, torsion freeness, reducedness, or a field coefficient ring.
