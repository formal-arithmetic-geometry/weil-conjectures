## Extracted arguments retained for the next splits

### Identity compact-support normalization

Choose the compactification $s\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}s\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}}s$. The formula $Rf_{!,n}=Rp_*j_!$ of [def-compact-support-direct-image-detailed] is the identity here, since direct image and extension by zero along the identity act termwise as the identity. At the integral stage the formula of [def-weil-integral-rational-qbar-compact-support] is $R\varprojlim_n\operatorname{Res}_n C_n\cong C$ by derived completeness [def-weil-derived-complete]. The natural unit into this total limit is the reduction/limit adjunction unit [lem-coef-derived-system-evaluation-calculus]; its composites to all levels are the original reduction maps. The normalized reduction tower has the canonical counit isomorphisms of [lem-coef-normalized-dvr-effectivity]. Write these functors as $\mathcal R\dashv\mathcal L$, with unit $u$ and counit $e$. Since $e_N$ is invertible for normalized $N$, the triangle equation $\mathcal L(e_N)u_{\mathcal L N}=1$ makes $u_{\mathcal L N}$ invertible. Derived completeness supplies some isomorphism $h:C\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal L\mathcal R C$; naturality gives $u_{\mathcal L\mathcal R C}h=\mathcal L\mathcal R(h)u_C$, so $u_C$ is invertible as well. Thus the canonical natural unit, rather than a separately chosen completeness isomorphism, identifies integral identity compact support with the identity functor. The natural isomorphism $u^{-1}$ descends to the rational category: naturality for an integral arrow $v$ gives naturality for $v\otimes a$ after multiplying both sides by $a$, and hence for sums of such arrows. For descent to the common-stage category, check compatibility with $E\subset L$. The completed scalar comparison [lem-weil-finite-free-completed-scalar-comparison] identifies integral scalar extension with ordinary derived tensor by the finite free completed coefficient sheaf. Tensor by this sheaf commutes with the products and cones in the total-limit model: after a choice of basis it is a finite direct sum, so the identification acts componentwise. On a flat-term K-flat representative $P$, the canonical completion map has the reduction maps as its product component and zero as its shifted-product component, exactly as specified in that comparison. Tensoring this map and applying the coefficient comparison yields the completion map for the extended representative, with the same reduction and zero components. Thus the square formed by scalar extension, the compact-support scalar comparison, and the two units $u$ commutes as an equality of chain maps, not merely after projecting to each reduction. Its inverse square gives the same compatibility for $u^{-1}$. At a common field the naturality square is therefore the rational naturality square already proved; extending that field preserves its equality. This gives a natural identity normalization of $Ra_{s!}$ in all four point categories. 

### Point reconstruction

These exact functors act termwise on derived categories. For a finitely presented $R$-module $M$, apply the two exact functors and sections to a presentation $R^a\to R^b\to M\to0$. Since $\Gamma(s,B)=R$, the adjunction unit identifies $M\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma(s,L_RM)$. Exactness then makes the unit an isomorphism for every complex whose cohomology modules are finitely presented. Noetherian finite modules are finitely presented [lem-sc-noetherian-finite-module-finite-presentation]. At finite level, $L_R$ sends a complex with bounded finite cohomology to a complex with constant finite cohomology sheaves. Their geometric stalks are finite by [lem-weil-proetale-constant-sheaf-stalk], so this complex is constructible by [def-weil-finite-torsion-constructibility]. At integral level every bounded complex with finitely generated cohomology over the DVR $R$ is perfect: [lem-weil-pbc-finite-free-resolution-noetherian] gives $R^a\twoheadrightarrow M$ with finitely generated kernel; the kernel is torsion free as a submodule of a free module, so [lem-sc-dvr-fg-torsionfree-free] makes it finite free. Thus each cohomology module has a length-one finite-free resolution, and [lem-sf-perfect-cohomology-assembly] gives a bounded finite-projective representative $P$ of the complex. The integral stratified-perfect characterization [lem-weil-pbc-integral-stratified-perfect-characterization], applied to the single stratum $s$, puts $L_RP=B\otimes_R P$ in $D^b_c(s,R)$. Here $s$ is Noetherian of dimension zero and $\ell\ne p$. Now take $C\in D^b_c(s,R)$ and $c=\Gamma(s,C)$. Its bounded finite/finite-generation properties are part of the target of [def-weil-finite-coefficient-point]. We have proved $L_Rc$ is constructible. The adjunction triangle identity makes $\Gamma(s,L_Rc\to C)$ inverse to the unit at $c$, hence an isomorphism. Conservativity of the equivalence [lem-weil-point-fibre-equivalence] therefore makes the actual adjunction counit $L_Rc\to C$ an isomorphism. This justifies replacing $C$ by $L_Rc$, without treating all pro-etale sheaves as coefficient modules.

### Scalar-Hom comparison diagram

To identify the internal-Hom comparison itself, use the completion comparison [lem-weil-finite-free-completed-scalar-comparison] on the bounded finite-projective model $B\otimes_R P$ of step 3 and its dual model from step 5. Ordinary completed-ring extension then acts termwise on these models. Take the internal-Hom scalar comparison of [lem-pur-integral-operations-scalar-extension] to be the transpose of scalar-extended evaluation. For a finite free term, evaluation on a basis gives $f\otimes a\mapsto(x\otimes b\mapsto f(x)\otimes ab)$; passage to the image of a splitting idempotent gives the identical formula for a finite-projective term. There are finitely many terms, so these formulas identify the total Hom complexes and commute with their signed differentials. Sections of this map have the displayed module scalar-Hom formula, since the point-sections comparison is the inverse of the sections-tensor map followed by completion. Thus this calculation identifies the comparison used by the common-stage construction, beyond merely giving an abstract isomorphism of its source and target. Precomposition by a map of models commutes with this formula. For $E\subset L\subset M$, both successive scalar comparisons send $((f\otimes a)\otimes b)(x\otimes t)$ to $f(x)\otimes abt$. The internal-Hom comparisons and fibre comparisons have these same identity and composition compatibilities [lem-pur-integral-operations-scalar-extension, lem-weil-point-sections-scalar-extension]. Hence the two stage representatives give the same arrow after extension to $M$, and an arrow represented over $L$ has the same naturality square there. This proves the common-stage formula with the specified, rather than arbitrary, coefficient comparisons.
