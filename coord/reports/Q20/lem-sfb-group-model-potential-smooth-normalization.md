## Review by Q20

I checked the unchanged item against the normalization construction in Prasad--Yu Section 3.3, Proposition 3.4, and Conrad's Appendix A.2--A.4. The group construction is not made a new node or simply attributed to a clause of the item. Steps 4--6 spell out why the product is integrally closed in its generic algebra, why all three Hopf maps preserve the normalization, and why their identities hold. The generic connectedness hypothesis is retained although the normalization construction itself does not require it.

For the first new node I read the completion step in Stacks algebra.tex Tag 0328 and the residue-extension construction Tag 03C3. The general regular-generator form is stated explicitly as a generalization, not misquoted as the p-adic theorem. Its mechanism is cancellation by t in S/t^nS: this identifies the kernel of projection with tD and shows t remains regular in the inverse limit. The limit is complete and separated with principal maximal ideal and unchanged residue. Every nonzero element then has finite t-order and is t^n times a unit. This implies the domain and DVR assertions. In particular, no unjustified Noetherianity or separatedness of S is used. The p-adic project theorem is only a special case, not an attachment for the new statement.

For the second new node I read Appendix A.2, its specialization in A.4, and the finite-normalization discussion A.6. The finite extension is not required separable; the complete DVR case is within the theorem. Flatness embeds E and its base extensions in their generic algebras. Generic geometric normality identifies the normalization with integral closure in that generic algebra; the normality and finite-normalization assertions are the ones used by A.4. The resulting C is torsion-free over the DVR, hence flat. This is a reduced-fibre theorem with real content, not an isolated restatement of the group-normalization item: it applies to arbitrary affine flat models and asserts no group law or smoothness.

For the third new node I read Appendix A.3's Serre-criterion argument and Stacks Tag 031S for the normality criterion. At special minimal primes the reduced special fibre is a field locally and the local maximal ideal is generated by the regular element pi, giving R1. At other special primes reducedness supplies S1 on the special fibre, and the regular pi raises depth by one, giving S2. On the generic open normality supplies both. Normality also makes T integrally closed in its generic localization. The normality input is weaker than the existing normal-fibres theorem and stronger than the existing special-generic-point DVR statement; it is neither duplicate. This node does not assert smoothness or a group structure.

Step 1 uses exactly the flat local prescribed-residue theorem and the new completion criterion. The former says its maximal ideal is the extended maximal ideal; hence it really is pi S. Flatness makes pi regular. Units and powers of pi show A embeds in D and that the map is local, including the specified residue identification.

Step 2 uses the smooth-morphism lifting definition, smooth geometric normality, and the new normalized-reduction theorem. The tensor identity proves the needed flatness of E directly. The generic fibre is the field base change of the given smooth generic group; the brief base-change/product lifting calculation supplies smoothness without an uncited purity or smoothening theorem. Geometric normality is exactly the hypothesis required by the reduced-fibre input.

Step 3 uses the complete-DVR finite-normalization theorem and the complete-pair henselian theorem. I read both statements: the former permits arbitrary finite field extensions and gives a local map with complete DVR target, so no separability assumption is hidden. Finiteness gives a finite residue extension, and algebraic closedness of the already fixed kbar identifies it with kbar. Completion is for the target's maximal ideal, as required for henselianity.

Step 4 uses smooth geometric normality, geometrically-reduced flat pullback, the finite-type-algebra Noetherianity leaf, and the new DVR normality theorem. Both tensor factors are flat, their generic product is smooth, and both special factors are geometrically reduced. The pullback theorem's reduced and locally Noetherian test-scheme conditions hold because the special factor is a finite-type algebra over a field. This is why I added the existing Noetherianity citation. The theorem then yields the precise integral-closedness needed in the next step, not merely reducedness of a normalization.

Step 5 uses the PID integral-closedness leaf for R; for the other two maps it uses the result of Step 4. The monic equation with coefficients in B_R is written explicitly. Applying each generic Hopf map gives coefficients in the right original target ring. Thus restriction to C is justified for multiplication, inverse and identity separately. There is no appeal to a definition of normalization as if it supplied an unstated universal property.

Step 6 is the elementary equality check: every target tensor power is flat, hence injects into its generic localization. All group diagrams can therefore be checked on the generic fibre. This is not a separate comparison theorem and is not made a node.

Step 7 uses the geometrically-reduced-group smoothness leaf, the finite-type finite-presentation leaf, and the fibrewise smoothness criterion. I read all three: the first is over a field and requires finite type, which is known; the second applies over the Noetherian DVR; the last requires flatness and local finite presentation and smoothness of every fibre. A trait has just the generic and closed point, and both fibres have now been addressed. No connectedness assertion for the special fibre is inferred here.

Step 8 uses integral injective lying-over. It is applied to B_R inside C before reduction; primes above special primes necessarily contain the uniformizer. This proves surjectivity after reduction without an unwarranted injectivity hypothesis on the quotient ring. Reduction of a finite module generating set proves finiteness of the special map. The generic-isomorphism and homomorphism clauses were proved in Steps 2 and 6.

I tested the statements on the trivial group and smooth affine additive/multiplicative models; neither a positive-dimensional generic fibre nor special connectedness is assumed by this proof. I checked the normality criterion against a reduced nodal special fibre (xy=pi has normal total space), an empty special fibre such as R[1/pi], and the zero algebra. The generic normality condition is essential; reduced special fibre is not silently replaced by normal. In the completion criterion, t cannot be zero or a unit in a nonzero local ring under the given hypotheses. Imperfect residue fields and nonexcellent A are permitted: the residue construction takes the entire specified algebraic closure and reduced-fibre normalization is performed only after passage to complete D. No step uses excellence of A.
