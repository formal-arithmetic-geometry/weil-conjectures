## Independent argument behind the two new local inputs

The independently delivered completed-chart node on main is used exactly as stated, on the strict local ring of the locally Noetherian log regular scheme W. Its sharp-chart replacement is allowed. The strict-local base change is compared with Z only through the existing ind-etale strict-local invariance theorem. Sharp charts for the same characteristic differ by a multiplicative unit character in the integral associated log monoid. Retaining that character is essential: the presentation theorem does not promise compatibility with the original R-chart.

For the formal-extension model, first complete the local rings; finite presentation of the monoid algebra lets this be computed by completing the base-completed tensor algebra at the induced prime. This comparison does not require the chart extension to be flat. Split R_G into G^gp and its sharp quotient S. The difference between the original R chart and the S chart is a unit character on R^gp; since h^gp is an isomorphism it extends uniquely to Q^gp, and rescaling each Q monomial by that character removes the twist. After inverting F, quotienting the common G^gp gives a monoid with unit group F^gp/G^gp and sharp quotient T. The unit-group quotient is finite free: it embeds in the finite free R^gp/G^gp and has finitely many generators. Its split group-algebra directions form a Laurent torus coefficient algebra. A chosen prime of that torus need not be closed; use its local ring and complete it. Over a field, or a Cohen ring with its uniformizer retained, this coefficient ring with the old t variables is regular. Completion yields D[[T]]. In mixed characteristic it yields D[[T]]/(eta); the reduction of eta modulo the T monomials is congruent to p modulo the old t variables. The coefficient ring before adding t is unramified over the Cohen ring, so this reduction is in m_D but not m_D^2, even for imperfect residue fields. This explains the exact parameter hypothesis, the retained unit-face tensor relations, and why the new model node asserts a presentation, not a dimension formula. No preservation theorem is used to establish the model.

For the formal monoid dimension theorem, use a strictly positive integral functional on the sharp fs monoid's rational cone. Its sublevel sets are finite. Finite monoid generators compare this degree filtration with powers of the monomial maximal ideal. Counting lattice points gives upper and lower polynomial growth of degree rank(T^gp); convolution with the coefficient ring's Hilbert--Samuel growth gives degree dim D + rank(T^gp). The Hilbert--Samuel dimension theorem (00KQ) therefore supplies the dimension equality, not just a prime-chain lower bound. A monomial order refining the positive functional shows the formal series ring is a domain: leading nonzero coefficients multiply nontrivially over the domain D. Noetherianity follows from finite generation before completion. Finally eta with nonzero parameter reduction is a nonzero nonunit in this domain, so quotient dimension drops by one; its stratum is D/(eta-bar), regular of dimension dim D-1. This argument works for arbitrary complete regular coefficient rings, without a log-regular source or any monoid extension. It is not a renamed toric-refinement assertion.

## Intended argument for the new monoid input

Write L=Q^gp/P^gp, and let S be the image of Q in L. It is finitely generated and generates L as a group. It is saturated: if n*l belongs to S, lift l to z in Q^gp and write n*z=q+g(p2)-g(p1), with q in Q and p1,p2 in P. Then n*(z+g(p1))=q+g(p2)+(n-1)*g(p1) belongs to Q; saturation of Q gives z+g(p1) in Q, whose image is l. The finitely generated torsion-free group L is a finite free abelian group. Identify it with Z^r. A saturated finitely generated monoid generating that lattice is the set of lattice points in its full-dimensional rational polyhedral cone: for a lattice point in the cone, rational coefficients and clearing denominators give a positive multiple in the monoid; saturation finishes. Choose a primitive lattice vector v in the interior of the cone (scale a rational interior point to an integral vector and divide its coordinate gcd), and extend v to a lattice basis v,b2,...,br. For each i choose a sufficiently large nonnegative integer Ni so that bi+Ni*v lies in the cone. The basis v,b2+N2*v,...,br+Nr*v is contained in S. Lift it to Q. The resulting P^gp plus Z^r map to Q^gp is bijective, since its quotient on L is a basis and its kernel on P^gp is zero. Restrict to P plus N^r. This argument is not the log regularity clause in different words: it is a purely monoid-theoretic input valid without any scheme. The case r=0 uses no cone or primitive vector.
