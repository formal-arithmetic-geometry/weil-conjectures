Together their statements justify the resulting R-module short exact sequence. I did not use the etale-only finite-ring exactness lemma.

Step 2 uses the unit R in the statement of `lem-pc-sheaf-modules-tensor-closed`. The explicit unitor is multiplication, so tensor by R is exact; the zero case follows sectionwise from `def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product`. This verifies the exact-tensor definition of flatness in `def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules`. The statement of `lem-pc-bounded-above-flat-kflat-site` applies to arbitrary ringed sites with flat terms; the bound is 0. Thus no bounded-below substitution or unjustified flatness of L enters. This is marked non-elementary because it invokes the K-flatness theorem.

Step 3 is elementary from the cohomology formula in `def-pc-acyclic-complex-modules` and the criterion in `def-pc-quasi-iso-modules`: only kernel in degree -1 and cokernel in degree 0 survive. The chain-map equation is qt=0 and the induced degree-zero map is the specified quotient identification. Step 4 uses precisely preservation of colimits in `lem-weil-pbc-independent-sheaf-tensor-colimits`; the finite cokernel diagram has both object and arrow types in the coefficient universe, and the given small site satisfies its size hypothesis. The unitor from `lem-pc-sheaf-modules-tensor-closed` identifies the tensor differential with t. The two induced cokernel maps are inverse by their universal properties. Their formula is x tensor bar(a) sent to a[x]. Importantly, no surjectivity on global sections of the sheaf quotient is assumed, and L/tL means the quotient sheaf. All maps in this step exist for arbitrary L, including torsion L.
