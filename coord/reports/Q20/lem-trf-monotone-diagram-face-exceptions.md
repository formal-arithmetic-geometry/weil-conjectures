Step 1 uses no cited node: the finite box below v is nonempty because it contains v, and any strict coordinatewise decrease strictly lowers the sum. Its minimal element is therefore one of the listed vertices. Additive closure proves the converse. This is written out, not a use of an unrecorded antichain theorem.

Step 2 uses only the definitions in the item. Exiting the i-th quotient support means that some earlier vertex divides the translated vector. Since no earlier vertex divided before translation, the failed inequality must be at coordinate k, and its exact integer value is alpha_h,k=alpha_i,k+beta_k+1. This verifies both positivity needed for the later lex comparison and beta_k<M. No earlier vertex exists for i=1, so the same witness argument simply rules out any exception there.

Step 3 uses the monotonicity hypothesis and Step 2. I checked each coordinate of the moved vector explicitly, especially coordinate j, where the sum of TWO vertex coordinates is bounded by the total degree, hence by M. The large coordinate belongs to the quotient exponent beta; no bound on alpha_i is improperly substituted for it.

Step 4 uses Step 1's minimal vertex below the moved vector. Its degree is at most the earlier vertex's degree. A strict degree decrease puts it earlier immediately. Equal degrees and coordinatewise comparison force equality with the moved vector; that vector agrees before k and strictly decreases at k, so it is earlier lexicographically. Thus its index is less than h, hence less than i, contradicting quotient support. This explicit case split is essential: it does not silently suppose monotonicity preserves vertexhood. No non-elementary comparison is hidden or outsourced.

Step 5 treats the complementary region separately, using Step 1 and additive closure. The translated vector lies in D and the original does not. This again forces equality at coordinate k and gives beta_k<M, with no assertion about the ordering needed. Step 6 transfers the witnessing vertex's mass to j and checks coordinatewise domination by beta itself. Additive closure gives the contradiction beta in D. These are direct calculations, not appeals to the parent's stabilization theorem.
