For Step 1 I checked `lem-weil-pbc-monic-quotient-free`, whose statement gives the powers of the root as a basis over any commutative coefficient ring. It applies to every stage, since the polynomial is monic of positive degree n, including n=1. Expanding the coefficients recursively proves existence and uniqueness of the multivariate basis; this is a finite iteration of the cited result. I checked `lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-is-integral` against algebra.tex Tag 00GK (`lemma-finite-is-integral`) and `lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-noetherian` against the Noetherian permanence statement, Tag 00FN. R is Noetherian and the displayed basis is finite, so the hypotheses hold. The coefficient of 1 supplies injectivity before lying over is ever applied.

For Step 2 I checked `lem-weil-pbc-finite-over-henselian-local-product` against algebra.tex Tag 04GH (`lemma-finite-over-henselian`), especially its henselian factors and local structural maps. R is henselian by hypothesis and B is finite by Step 1. Computing the fibre directly from its quotient presentation produces the truncated polynomial ring with nilpotent variables and unique maximal ideal. Each factor's reduction is nonzero because its structural map is local; this rules out a hidden use of Nakayama or an assumption that finiteness alone implies locality. A product with two such factors has a nontrivial idempotent, while the computed fibre is local. The unique factor is therefore B itself. The residue map is identified by evaluation, not just by an unspecified abstract isomorphism. `def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology` supplies exactly the final implication from henselian and separably closed residue field to strictly henselian, checked against Tag 04GF.
