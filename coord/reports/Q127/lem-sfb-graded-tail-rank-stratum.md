## Returned proof retained verbatim

Returned step 1:

Apply [lem-sfb-graded-fibre-hilbert-functions-finite] to $(\mathcal A,M)$ over $U$. Its hypotheses are exactly the Noetherian, degree-one finite generation, and finite graded generation hypotheses here. Thus every $M_d$ is coherent and the functions $h_u(d)=\dim_{\kappa(u)}(M_{d,u}\otimes\kappa(u))$ lie in a finite set $H$. No flatness of $M$ over $U$ is being assumed.

Returned step 2:

For $d\geq m$ apply [lem-sfb-coherent-universal-rank-locus] to $(M_d,P(d))$ and denote its open by $W_d$. The nonnegative integral-value hypothesis on $P$ makes this application legitimate. For every $h\in H$ for which some $d\geq m$ satisfies $h(d)>P(d)$, choose one such integer $e_h$. There are finitely many chosen integers; put $V=\bigcap_hW_{e_h}$, taking $V=U$ if none were chosen. Then $V=\{u:h_u(d)\leq P(d)\text{ for all }d\geq m\}$. Indeed a bad function $h_u$ fails its selected test, while a good function passes every test. Hence $V$ is open. This proves the stationarity required in the source without needing a common bound for the eventual Hilbert polynomials.

Returned step 3:

On $V$, every fibre of $M_d|_V$ has dimension at most $P(d)$. The last clause of [lem-sfb-coherent-universal-rank-locus], now applied over the Noetherian open scheme $V$, gives a closed subscheme $C_d\hookrightarrow V$ cut out by a quasi-coherent ideal $\mathcal I_d$, universally imposing rank $P(d)$ on the pullback of $M_d|_V$. This is an application to one coherent module at a time, not an assertion that infinitely many open conditions can be intersected arbitrarily.

Returned step 4:

Form the ideal sheaf $\mathcal I=\sum_{d\geq m}\mathcal I_d$ on $V$. It is quasi-coherent: on an affine $\operatorname{Spec}R\subseteq V$ it is the sheaf associated to $\sum_d I_d\subseteq R$, and localization commutes with sums since every element of a sum is a finite sum. The corresponding affine descriptions agree on principal restrictions, which is the affine-local quasi-coherence criterion. By [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-ideal-closed-subscheme] let $Z\hookrightarrow V$ be the closed subscheme cut out by $\mathcal I$. More explicitly, Noetherianity also gives the source\textquotesingle s finite intersection: each ideal $\sum_dI_d$ has finitely many generators, each involving finitely many $d$; choose a finite affine cover of $V$ and take the union of these finite degree sets. Their maximum $N$ satisfies $\mathcal I=\sum_{m\leq d\leq N}\mathcal I_d$. If the sum is zero, take $N=m$.

Returned step 5:

Let $g:T\to U$ be arbitrary. If all $g^*M_d$ have the required ranks, [lem-sfb-coherent-universal-rank-locus] applied to each selected degree forces $g$ to factor through every $W_{e_h}$, hence through $V$. For a map $T\to V$, the same lemma says that the rank condition in degree $d$ is equivalent to factoring through $C_d$. On affine charts of $T$ mapping into an affine $\operatorname{Spec}R\subseteq V$, this means precisely that the induced ring map kills $I_d$. Killing every $I_d$ is equivalent to killing their sum $I$, hence to a unique factorization through $\operatorname{Spec}(R/I)$. The affine models in [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-ideal-closed-subscheme] identify these with $Z$. These local factorizations agree, since a map out of a quotient ring is determined by its composite; they therefore glue. Conversely a factorization through $Z$ kills every $I_d$, so the lemma gives all the required locally free ranks. This argument uses no Noetherian hypothesis on $T$.

Returned step 6:

The map $j:Z\to V\to U$ is an immersion by [def-weil-closed-immersion]. It is of finite type, directly from [def-weil-finite-type-morphism]: on any affine $\operatorname{Spec}R\subseteq U$, the open $V\cap\operatorname{Spec}R$ is a finite union of principal opens $D(f_i)$ because $R$ is Noetherian. Indeed write that open as $\bigcup_{f\in J}D(f)$; finite generation of $J$ gives a finite subcover. Over each $D(f_i)$, $Z$ is $\operatorname{Spec}(R_{f_i}/I_i)$, a finite-type $R$-algebra, generated by the image of $f_i^{-1}$. Thus these finitely many affine charts verify both local finite type and quasi-compactness. The affine rings $R$ are Noetherian by [pqf-lem-affine-open-locnoeth].

Returned step 7:

Every test scheme has at most one factorization through $j$, as shown by the quotient-ring and open-subscheme descriptions in Step 5. Hence its image on functors of points is exactly the specified rank subfunctor. Any two such representing immersions have a unique compatible isomorphism by [lem-pc-representable-unique], which proves uniqueness as a subfunctor.

Returned step 8:

For any $U\prime\to U$, form $Z\prime=Z\times_UU\prime$. The base-changed closed part is a closed immersion by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change]; the open part is the inverse image open $V\prime\subseteq U\prime$. Thus $Z\prime\to U\prime$ is an immersion. A map $t:T\to U\prime$ factors through $Z\prime$ if and only if its composite $g:T\to U$ factors through $Z$, by the fibre-product universal property. By Step 5 this is equivalent to $g^*M_d$ being locally free of rank $P(d)$ for every $d\geq m$. The composition isomorphism of [def-pc-module-pullback] identifies this with the pullback along $t$ of the pieces pulled back to $U\prime$. This proves the asserted property without applying any Noetherian theorem over $U\prime$.
