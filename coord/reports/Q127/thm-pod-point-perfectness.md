## Source error

The sentence in main-20261015 Proof Step 1, “the sheaves of A-modules on s are the A-modules”, is false for arbitrary sheaves on the pro-étale site. The current node `lem-cbc-proetale-stalk-conservative`, clause (4), supplies a concrete counterexample: for a nonzero finite coefficient module A, the sheafified quotient Q_A of all set maps to A by locally constant maps is nonzero, but Q_A(s)=0 and every geometric-point stalk is zero. Nonconstancy on an infinite profinite object witnesses its nonzero values; on the one-point final object every map is locally constant. Thus evaluation at s cannot identify all pro-étale module sheaves with ordinary modules. The target only uses bounded constructible objects; the repaired Step 2 passes those through the étale-origin equivalence and so repairs the proof without changing the target statement. This is a proof-source error, not a STATEMENT-DISPUTED about the theorem being split.

4. **Dimension of the compactification.** `det-nagata-theorem-dense` asserts existence with a quasi-compact open immersion and a proper second map over a qcqs base. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian` and `lem-sc-noetherian-closed-subset-finite-components` justify finite components. `lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme` provides the scheme structure for each closed component, and on affine charts its quotient algebra is finite type. `lem-sc-dimension-locally-algebraic` actually asserts equality of dimension on every nonempty open of an irreducible locally finite-type scheme. This is an argument for compact perfectness, not a new node restating that clause.

6. **Finite free models and duals.** `lem-trf-finite-tor-amplitude-perfect` uses finite **cohomology**, which is what Steps 3/5 prove. It yields a bounded free complex; only after choosing that complex do I apply `lem-weil-bounded-finite-tor-perfect`, whose hypothesis is a bounded complex of finitely generated modules and whose conclusion really has terms in the desired interval. Finiteness of the ring makes it Noetherian, and its local-ring hypothesis is known. `lem-sc-affine-projective-complex-derived-hom` is explicitly about ordinary modules on the one-point ringed site, with bounded-above projective source and bounded-below target. A bounded free complex and the degree-zero ring satisfy those conditions. The degree-i dual term is Hom(P^{-i},A), giving the sign reversal of the endpoints without any new geometric duality input. This also distinguishes perfectness from mere bounded finite cohomology over the nonregular quotient A_n.

7. **Integral images.** The common input Tor interval is already in the target's integral Dctf hypothesis. `lem-sf-integral-direct-image-constructible` explicitly provides ordinary-image constructibility and the canonical comparisons at every n; it is not just a definition of a limit. `lem-coef-integral-compact-support` gives both assertions for shriek under exactly the separated finite-type and common-amplitude hypotheses we have, with d nonnegative. `lem-pod-point-sections-derived-reduction` explicitly compares ordinary-module derived reduction with sections of the sheaf reduction; it is applied to each constructible integral object at s. No unsupported general assertion about all cohomology modules of all complete complexes is necessary.

8. **Integral duals.** The same projective Hom-complex theorem computes the dual. `lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-submodule-finite` gives finite-generation of kernels, so the cohomology modules are finite too. Finally `lem-pc-base-change-rhom-perfect` explicitly covers every commutative coefficient map and a perfect first argument; here that argument is C, not K on X, and the second is R[0]. Therefore the dual reductions, not only the original reductions, are the claimed finite-level complexes, with the uniform reversed interval from Step 6.

9. **Fields.** The rationalization definition has exactly the same objects as the integral category; it does not adjoin retracts. The operation definitions specify rationalization and common finite coefficient stages, and the point definition supplies the corresponding fibre. Tensoring a bounded finite projective matrix complex with E produces finite-dimensional terms. Its ordinary cohomology and the cohomology of its termwise dual are subquotients of finitely many finite-dimensional terms, so are bounded and finite-dimensional. The same finite matrices at a chosen E' define the algebraically closed stage. This uses the coefficient categories actually in the item, with no imposed Weil structure and no replacement by an unrelated condensed coefficient category.
