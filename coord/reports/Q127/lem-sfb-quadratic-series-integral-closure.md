## Allocation of the complete returned argument

The following is the proof retained verbatim from the repository item, not abbreviated replacement prose. It remains in the report because the new nodes must have no proof steps.

### Returned step 1 → lem-sfb-quadratic-homogeneous-substitution

Write $\beta=b/a$, $\gamma=c/a$, $p(Z)=Z^2+\beta Z+\gamma$, and $q=aX^2+bXY+cY^2$. Multiplying a generator of a principal ideal by $a^{-1}$ does not change that ideal. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-monic-quotient-free] to $p$: $L$ has $F$-basis $1,z$ and $z^2=-\beta z-\gamma$. In particular $F\to L$ is injective, and each coefficient in $L$ has a unique expression $u+vz$ with $u,v\in F$.

### Returned step 2 → lem-sfb-quadratic-homogeneous-substitution

For $H=\sum_{n\geq0}H_n\in F[[X,Y]]$, where $H_n$ is its homogeneous polynomial of total degree $n$, define $\phi(H)=\sum_{n\geq0}H_n(z,1)t^n$. This is well defined: each $H_n$ has only $n+1$ coefficients. The convolution formula of [def-weil-pbc-multivariate-power-series] gives $(HK)_n=\sum_{i+j=n}H_iK_j$, hence $\phi(HK)=\phi(H)\phi(K)$; addition and the $F$-algebra structure are coefficientwise. Since $\phi(q)=a\,p(z)t^2=0$, this homomorphism factors through the displayed map $j:R\to L[[t]]$, sending $x$ to $zt$ and $y$ to $t$.

### Returned step 3 → lem-sfb-quadratic-homogeneous-substitution

If $\phi(H)=0$, then $H_n(z,1)=0$ for every $n$. By the definition of $L=F[Z]/(p)$, $H_n(Z,1)=p(Z)U_n(Z)$ for a polynomial $U_n$. For $n<2$ this forces $H_n=0$; for $n\geq2$, comparison of leading coefficients gives $\deg U_n\leq n-2$. Homogenizing this equality to degree $n$ gives $H_n=qG_{n-2}$, with $G_{n-2}=a^{-1}Y^{n-2}U_n(X/Y)$ a homogeneous polynomial (all exponents are nonnegative). Thus $H=q\sum_{n\geq2}G_{n-2}$, where the sum is a genuine formal series by its degrees. Conversely multiples of $q$ map to zero. This proves $\ker\phi=(q)$ and injectivity of $j$. Here the degree-zero and degree-one assertions also follow from the independence of $1,z$ in step 1; no division theorem for complete rings is used.

### Returned step 4 → lem-sfb-quadratic-homogeneous-substitution

The constant term of $\phi(H)$ lies in $F$. Conversely an arbitrary series in $F+tL[[t]]$ has the form $\alpha_0+\sum_{n\geq1}(\alpha_n+\delta_n z)t^n$, with $\alpha_n,\delta_n\in F$. Its explicit preimage is $\alpha_0+\sum_{n\geq1}\alpha_nY^n+\sum_{n\geq1}\delta_nXY^{n-1}$. Therefore identify $R$ with the subring $A=F+tB$ of $B=L[[t]]$ using $j$. In particular $F[[t]]\subseteq A$, and $tB\subseteq A$. These are coefficient identities in the power series ring of [def-weil-pbc-multivariate-power-series].

### Returned step 5 → lem-sfb-quadratic-series-field-factors

We next describe the coefficient algebra without any restriction on the characteristic. If $p$ has a root $r\in F$, put $s=-\beta-r$; then $p=(Z-r)(Z-s)$ and $(r-s)^2=\beta^2-4\gamma\ne0$. Evaluation gives $L\cong F\times F$, with inverse $(u,v)\mapsto u(z-s)/(r-s)+v(z-r)/(s-r)$, as is checked on the basis $1,z$. If $p$ has no root, $L$ is a field: for $u+vz\ne0$ its norm $N=u^2-\beta uv+\gamma v^2$ is nonzero, since for $v\ne0$ it equals $v^2p(-u/v)$ and for $v=0$ it equals $u^2$. Multiplication shows its inverse is $(u-\beta v-vz)/N$. Thus $L$ is a field or a product of two fields. Applying the coefficient isomorphism to each coefficient identifies $B$ with $\prod_i D_i$, where $D_i=K_i[[t]]$ and there are one or two field factors $K_i$. The power series convolution shows this is a ring isomorphism.

### Returned step 6 → lem-sfb-quadratic-series-field-factors

Each field $K_i$ is a Noetherian normal domain: its ideals are $0,K_i$, and its fraction field is itself. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-power-series-normal-domain] separately to each $K_i$. It follows that every $D_i=K_i[[t]]$ is an integral domain and is integrally closed in its fraction field $E_i=\operatorname{Frac}(D_i)$. Consequently $B=\prod_iD_i$ is reduced, and the injection $A\subseteq B$ makes $R\cong A$ reduced too (a nilpotent maps to a nilpotent in each domain).

### Returned step 7 → lem-sfb-quadratic-series-field-factors

Multiplication by $t$ shifts coefficients and is injective on $B$, hence also on $A$. Under $j$, the extended ideal $(x,y)B=(zt,t)B=tB$. For finiteness, write every $h\in B$ uniquely as $h=u+zv$ with $u,v\in F[[t]]\subseteq A$, by separating its coefficients in the basis $1,z$. Thus $1,z$ generate $B$ as an $A$-module in the sense of [def-weil-finitely-generated-module]. There is also an explicit integral equation, avoiding any unstated finiteness-to-integrality theorem: this $h$ satisfies $h^2+(\beta v-2u)h+(u^2-\beta uv+\gamma v^2)=0$. Its coefficients belong to $F[[t]]\subseteq A$, so every $h\in B$ is integral over $A$ by [def-weil-pbc-integral-closure].

### Returned step 8 → lem-sfb-regular-conductor-fraction-normalization

The total fraction ring is compatible with these explicit maps. If $s\in A$ is a non-zero-divisor on $A$ and $sb=0$ in $B$, then $tb\in tB\subseteq A$ and $s(tb)=0$; hence $tb=0$, so $b=0$. Thus $s$ has a nonzero coordinate in every domain $D_i$ (otherwise it kills $te_i\in A$, where $e_i$ is the corresponding nonzero coordinate idempotent of $B$). Fractions define an injective map $Q(A)\to\prod_iE_i$, $r/s\mapsto(r_i/s_i)_i$: a fraction mapping to zero has $r_i=0$ for all $i$, hence $r=0$. Every $h\in B$ belongs to its image as $(th)/t$, since $th\in A$ and $t$ is a non-zero-divisor. This embeds $B$ in $Q(A)$ extending $j$, not merely as an abstract isomorphic algebra. Moreover any denominator regular on $B$ is invertible in $Q(A)$: if $s\in B$ is regular, $ts\in A$ is regular on $A$, and $s^{-1}=t/(ts)$. Hence these maps also identify $Q(A)$ and $Q(B)$.

### Returned step 9 → lem-sfb-regular-conductor-fraction-normalization

Let $w\in Q(A)$ be integral over $A$. Embed it in $\prod_iE_i$ as in step 8, and project its monic equation to coordinate $i$. All the coefficients now lie in $D_i$, since $A\subseteq\prod_iD_i$. The conclusion of [lem-weil-pbc-power-series-normal-domain], applied with the same $K_i$ as in step 6, implies $w_i\in D_i$. Thus the coordinate tuple of $w$ belongs to $B$. The embedding $B\to Q(A)$ of step 8 has exactly this tuple as its image, so injectivity gives $w\in B$. Step 7 supplies the reverse inclusion: every element of $B$ is integral over $A$. By [def-weil-pbc-integral-closure], $B$ is exactly the integral closure of $R\cong A$ in $Q(R)\cong Q(A)$. The constructions used the original substitution throughout, so the required identifications, ideal extension and non-zero-divisor assertion all respect $j$.

The two elementary pieces which occur alongside these extracted arguments stay explicit in the assembly: reducedness of R from its injection (old step 6), and the extended coordinate ideal and regularity of y (old step 7).

For the generic third node, k is a ring map, not just a set-theoretic assignment: in Q(A), (tb)/t+(tb')/t=t(b+b')/t and ((tb)/t)((tb')/t)=t(bb')/t after multiplying numerators and cancelling the invertible image of t. It sends 1 to 1 and a∈A to a/1. If k(b)=0, multiplying by t gives tb=0 in A (A embeds in Q(A) since its denominators are regular), hence b=0. Any compatible map must send b to tb/t because t is invertible; this proves uniqueness. For s regular on B, ts belongs to A and is regular on A: (ts)a=0 first gives sa=0 by regularity of t, then a=0. Thus k(s)·(t/(ts))=1. Fraction maps Q(A)→Q(B) and Q(B)→Q(A) are mutually inverse because they fix A and B respectively, and every fraction is its numerator times the inverse of its denominator. A projected monic equation for w places w_i in D_i; since the map Q(A)→∏E_i is injective and iota(k(b)) is b, the tuple gives equality w=k(b). Conversely, if B is integral over A, the same monic equations remain true after applying k. This proves the third statement under precisely its stated hypotheses.
