One detail must not be copied literally from an irreducible-base reading of the source: a compactification of a surjection over U need not surject onto a component of S missed by U. For example S is the disjoint union of two field spectra and U is just one component. I retain the item's explicit remedy: adjoin the reduced closed complement to each compactification before taking the product. This is construction bookkeeping, not an extra hypothesis. For S with intersecting components, the complement can have vertical components; the dominant-part operation is applied only after forming the proper surjective product, and removes those vertical components. No claim is made that the complemented factors themselves are objects of alt/S.

## New node: source and argument retained for its next worker

`lem-per-open-proper-dense-compactification` is the compactification sentence of ILO II 3.2.1 together with its needed generic-finiteness check. Read Stacks Tag 0F41 (`flat.tex`, `theorem-nagata`), and ILO II Proposition 1.1.7, Definition 1.2.2 (p.24), as well as the ordinary-case compactification paragraph (p.29). A Noetherian scheme is qcqs; its open immersion U into S is quasi-compact and separated. Thus the proper Z -> U, composed with U -> S, is separated and finite type, so Nagata and its dense refinement supply Z open dense in B proper over S. Over U the map Z -> B_U is proper because its source is proper over U and its target is separated over U; it is an open immersion with closed image, hence its dense image is all of B_U and it is an isomorphism. The existing OverRefinement theorem states this comparison exactly.

For the generic-fibre clause, Noetherian B has finitely many irreducible components. A dense open Z contains all their generic points; their images in S are component-generic by the alt/S assumption on Z, with finite residue extensions. If a point of B lies above a component-generic s, a component-generic generization of that point has image a generization of s, necessarily s itself. That generization belongs to Z, hence its image s lies in U. Thus B_s is empty for s outside U. For s in U, B_s equals Z_s, which is finite by ILO II 1.1.7 (finite type, maximal dominance and finite residue extensions). This argument does not require U dense in S, and it works for the empty source. Only the statement goes into the new node; this argument is retained here for its future split.
