### Previous step 1

Choose an affine open neighbourhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}S$ of $z$. By [lem-weil-pbc-lft-affine-pairs], $S$ is a finitely generated $F$-algebra. The rational point gives an augmentation $S\to F$ with maximal kernel $\mathfrak p$. Put $A=S_{\mathfrak p}=\mathcal O_{X,z}$, $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak pA$, and $K=\overline F$. The induced point $\bar z$ corresponds to the maximal kernel $\mathfrak q$ of $S\otimes_FK\to K$, $s\otimes\lambda\mapsto\bar s\lambda$; put $B=(S\otimes_FK)_{\mathfrak q}$ and write $\mathfrak n$ for its maximal ideal. Apply [lem-trf-separable-closed-point-formal-presentation] with ground field and residue field both $F$, and extension field $K$: $F/F$ is finite separable. It supplies Noetherian local rings $A,B,\widehat A$, a faithfully flat local map $A\to B$ with $\mathfrak mB=\mathfrak n$ and closed fibre $K$, and a formal presentation of $\widehat A$, whose maximal ideal is $\mathfrak m\widehat A$ with residue field $F$. The same facts for $\widehat B$ (Noetherian local, maximal ideal $\mathfrak n\widehat B$, residue field $K$) come from the second application of this node, to $S\otimes_FK$ over $K$ at $\mathfrak q$, in step 3. The coefficient section $F\to\widehat A$ is the structural $F$-algebra map, because the section is itself an $F$-algebra map.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-lft-affine-pairs, lem-trf-separable-closed-point-formal-presentation.

### Previous step 2

Write $M_A=\Omega_{A/F}$ and $M_B=\Omega_{B/K}$, with differentials as in [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials]. These are finite modules. Indeed, if $s_1,\ldots,s_d$ generate $S$ over $F$, additivity and Leibniz express every $\mathrm ds$ as an $S$-linear combination of the $\mathrm ds_i$; the symbols presentation in that definition says these differentials generate $\Omega_{S/F}$. The same calculation applies after extending the ground field. By [lem-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials-localization], localization gives the claimed finite modules. Let $J_A=\operatorname{Fit}_1(M_A)$ and $J_B=\operatorname{Fit}_1(M_B)$, defined by the presentation-minor construction of [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change] with $k=1$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials-base-change] to $F\to K$ and $F\to S$, and then [lem-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials-localization] at $\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak q$. They identify $M_B$ with $M_A\otimes_AB$. Clause (2) of [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change] therefore gives $J_B=J_AB$.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials, lem-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials-localization, lem-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials-base-change, lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change.

### Previous step 3

Apply [lem-trf-separable-closed-point-formal-presentation] also to the finitely generated $K$-algebra $S\otimes_FK$ and its rational point $\mathfrak q$. Choose its presentation $\widehat B=K[[t_1,\ldots,t_N]]/(g_1,\ldots,g_r)$ with $N\ge2$ and $(g_1,\ldots,g_r)\subseteq(t_1,\ldots,t_N)$. Its Jacobian cokernel is $M_B\otimes_B\widehat B$. The assumed ring isomorphism $\widehat B\cong K[[u,v]]/(uv)$ satisfies clause (c) of [lem-trf-formal-jacobian-quadratic-criterion]: take $a=c=0,b=1$, so the discriminant is $1$ in every characteristic. Implication (c) to (a) gives $\operatorname{depth}(\widehat B)=1$ and identifies the ideal of the $(N-1)$-minors of this Jacobian with $\mathfrak n\widehat B$. By clauses (1) and (2) of [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change], the same ideal is $J_B\widehat B$: use the displayed cokernel as a presentation and extend $J_B$ along $B\to\widehat B$. In the kernel-row formulation of that node, every row is a linear combination of the displayed relation rows; expanding a determinant multilinearly shows that its minors generate exactly the same ideal. Consequently $J_B\widehat B=\mathfrak n\widehat B$. No $K$-linearity of the hypothesized ring isomorphism has been assumed.

Uses: lem-trf-separable-closed-point-formal-presentation, lem-trf-formal-jacobian-quadratic-criterion, lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change.

### Previous step 4

The completion map $B\to\widehat B$ is faithfully flat by [lem-weil-pbc-local-completion-faithfully-flat], since $B$ is Noetherian local by step 1. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-ideal-contraction] to $J_B$ and $\mathfrak n$. Their extensions agree by step 3, so $J_B=\mathfrak n$. Now $A\to B$ is faithfully flat and $\mathfrak mB=\mathfrak n$ by step 1, while $J_AB=J_B$ by step 2. Contracting again with [lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-ideal-contraction], this time along $A\to B$, gives $J_A=\mathfrak m$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-local-completion-faithfully-flat, lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-ideal-contraction.

### Previous step 5

Both completion maps $A\to\widehat A$ and $B\to\widehat B$ are flat by [lem-weil-pbc-local-completion-faithfully-flat]. By [lem-trf-separable-closed-point-formal-presentation], all four rings are Noetherian local, the extended maximal ideals are the maximal ideals of the completions, and their residue fields are respectively $F,K$. Thus the three local flat maps $B\to\widehat B$, $A\to B$, and $A\to\widehat A$ have closed fibres $K,K,F$. Each field has depth $0$ by [def-weil-pbc-depth]: its maximal ideal is zero, and zero is not a nonzerodivisor on a nonzero field, so no positive-length regular sequence lies in that ideal. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-flat-local-depth-formula] to the three maps, in that order. The depth-one calculation of step 3 yields $\operatorname{depth}(B)=1$, then $\operatorname{depth}(A)=1$, and finally $\operatorname{depth}(\widehat A)=1$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-local-completion-faithfully-flat, lem-trf-separable-closed-point-formal-presentation, def-weil-pbc-depth, lem-weil-pbc-flat-local-depth-formula.
