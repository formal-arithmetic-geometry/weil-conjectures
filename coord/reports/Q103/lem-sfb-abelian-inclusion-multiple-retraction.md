For the quotient node, 5.14 supplies the quotient by the normal subgroup (commutativity ensures normality), 5.27 supplies the torsor identity and faithful flatness, and 1.63 supplies smoothness for the smooth kernel. The quotient is a separated finite-type group scheme; after any field extension its surjective image from the integral group is irreducible, and smoothness makes it reduced, hence geometrically integral. Smoothness and finite type/quasi-separatedness make the quotient map finitely presented. The torsor identity persists under every base change. The trivial subgroup gives the identity quotient, and H=G gives the point quotient, consistent with the theorem.

For the torsor node I read the complete proof of 8.22, not just the stated integer quantifier. A smooth nonempty torsor has a finite separable point. Over a Galois closure the sum of differences v-P_j is Galois-invariant and descends as a scheme map. Translation adds one h to each of n summands, giving the morphism identity of weight n, with n the positive degree. No division by n is made. A rational point gives n=1. Positive characteristic and a degree divisible by that characteristic are allowed. The node defines torsor inline by a faithfully flat structure map and the action-difference isomorphism, so it does not silently mean the etale torsors in other nodes.

A rational map P^2 dashed to P^1 illustrates why a general proper target is not substituted. The statement has no proof text, only its precise extension conclusion.
