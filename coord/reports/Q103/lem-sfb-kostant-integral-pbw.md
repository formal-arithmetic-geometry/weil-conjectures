Step 1 uses exactly the existing integral coroot-span statement to write h_alpha with integer simple-coroot coordinates and to know the root pairings are integers. It then applies the new Newton statement only to rational polynomials that take integer values, including binomial polynomials of integer affine forms and products of such polynomials. The h_i commute in the enveloping algebra, so evaluation is legitimate. The degree bounds quoted are explicitly in the Newton node, not borrowed from its future proof.

Step 2 uses the rank-one identity with E=e_alpha_i, F=e_-alpha_i and H=h_i, whose three commutators are among the Chevalley hypotheses. With m=n=b the last summand is exactly binomial(h_i,b), without a leading denominator. All the other summands have Cartan binomial index t<b. Newton expansion controls their shifted polynomials by indices at most t, so strong induction really proves toral membership in the root-generated algebra. This is not an appeal to an integral-form theorem that already includes the desired basis.

Step 3 uses the rank-two node only for distinct nonopposite roots. Opposite roots use the exact rank-one formula, with a specified orientation; its t=0 summand swaps the blocks and t>0 has degree m+n-t after the explicitly cited coroot-coordinate/Newton expansion. Root/torus commutation is derived directly from the defining bracket, with shift a*alpha(h_i). That shift is an integer, and subtracting the unshifted binomial polynomial lowers degree. Thus every lower-degree assertion needed for termination has been checked rather than hidden in the word 'straighten'.

Step 4 is the actual assembly argument. I checked its induction on (total degree, inversions of expanded types): same-degree swaps strictly reduce inversions; every correction has smaller degree regardless of the new word's order. Sorted equal root types merge with the elementary factorial binomial coefficient. Sorted Cartan blocks merge by the cited Newton statement without introducing a new type or an inversion. This works for an arbitrary chosen root ordering. There is no circular invocation of M being a subalgebra, no reliance on rational independence to prove integrality and no standalone normalization lemma secretly carrying the item's conclusion.

Step 5 uses just the definition of a generated integer subalgebra and the two inclusions proved in Steps 2 and 4, so it is elementary. Step 6 uses exactly ordinary rational PBW. Replacing powers of h_i by their binomial polynomials has a nonzero diagonal coefficient and lower-total-degree remainder. In a finite relation its largest degree consequently has distinct PBW monomials, and descending degree gives independence over Q, hence over Z. The integral basis assertion is proved in the item; no child restates it. Step 7 specializes the universal-property leaf to End_Q(V), with the Lie representation identity from the definition node, and uses the empty PBW basis for the zero Lie algebra. This verifies every clause, including uniqueness of the representation extension and U_Z=Z in rank zero.
