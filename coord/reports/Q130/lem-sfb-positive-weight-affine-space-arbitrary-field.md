Step 1 uses the statement of lem-pc-affine-finite-type-algebra to make the affine ring finitely generated; the scheme is affine, over a field and finite type as required. The smooth-scheme-over-field-regular node provides both regularity and local Noetherianity, not just domains. The regular-connected-integral node assumes precisely those regularity conditions and nonempty connectedness; the k-point supplies nonemptiness and geometric connectedness implies connectedness over k. The definition of an integral scheme then gives that the nonempty affine chart has domain A. The localization definition supplies its local maximal ideal, and the spec-map-stalk-localization statement explicitly identifies the localization with the stalk. Thus R is the required Noetherian regular local ring. These are generic geometry tools from other units, declared in split_report.tool_uses where needed; no six-functor or alteration route is replaced.

Step 2 cites the relative grading theorem, whose statement includes multiplication of degrees, weight zero for base scalars, unique finite decompositions and the formula on every algebra extension. The given action satisfies its identity and associativity hypotheses. Evaluation at the fixed point is invariant for all those extensions, so the Laurent polynomial identity used in the step follows from its stated action formula. Comparing its coefficients, rather than values on the finite group k^×, proves the homogeneity assertions. Powers of a homogeneous ideal are homogeneous by expanding products; this is elementary algebra written in the step. We do not use any proof-only property of the grading node.

Step 3 uses only the explicit fraction definition of localization. I checked surjectivity and injectivity of the cotangent map separately: c-s is in m, and su in m² forces epsilon(s)u in m². The localization equality witness is multiplied into the denominator when necessary; it stays outside m. The polynomial generators minus their augmentations generate m by subtracting the constant evaluation of each monomial, so the quotient really is finite dimensional. This comparison is a short fraction computation, not an uncited smoothness or completion theorem.

Step 4 uses the actual naturality clause of the rational tangent/cotangent duality node, not a mere definition of tangent spaces. Both points in that lemma are rational when applying an action automorphism; its vector-space identification is combined with step 3. The dual-number formula is the tangent definition already cited. In an eigenbasis the coefficient equality lambda_j(a_t^*u_i)=t^(n_j)delta_ji proves that pullback has the same weights n_i as tangent vectors, rather than their negatives. The identity is read over the universal Laurent coefficient ring using the algebraic action, so weights divisible by the characteristic remain nonzero characters. Homogeneous lifting uses only the quotient grading and finite linear algebra; there is no unproved equivariant splitting lemma hidden here.

Step 5 uses exactly the Jacobson-radical part of the Krull-intersection node. R is Noetherian, its unique maximal ideal equals its Jacobson radical, and R is finite as an R-module. The fraction definition proves injectivity of A→A_m from the domain property. Thus global separatedness is actually proved; applying the local theorem directly to the affine ring without its radical hypothesis would have been invalid.

Step 6 has no external theorem. Each element of m^j is a finite sum of products of j elements of m with an arbitrary coefficient. Replacing the coefficient by its augmentation changes the class by m^(j+1); replacing each factor by its cotangent representative has the same effect. This proves the spanning claim explicitly. All resulting degrees are positive sums, so every nonpositive homogeneous element in m lies in all powers, hence vanishes by step 5. I checked the induction starts at j=1 and also works when there are no cotangent basis vectors. The conclusion A_0=k and A_q=0 for q<0 is derived here, not delegated to a new node that repeats the item.

Step 7 checks every hypothesis of the positive-graded-polynomial node: a finitely generated commutative k-algebra; a nonnegative grading; scalars exactly its degree zero piece; regularity at the homogeneous maximal ideal; and homogeneous lifts of a finite cotangent basis. Its statement gives the evaluation isomorphism for these very lifts, with d equal to the basis size and the zero-variable case. The spectrum definition gives contravariant functoriality, so the inverse ring map supplies the inverse scheme map. The affine-space definition identifies the polynomial spectrum over Spec k, including d=0. Evaluation at the given point sends all selected b_i to zero, so the point is carried to the origin, rather than merely to an unspecified rational point.

I tested the edge cases: X=Spec k and d=0; a weighted affine space with unequal weights 2 and 5; k finite; and weight p in characteristic p, where the derivative on the Lie algebra is zero although the algebraic character is positive. A negative or zero tangent weight invalidates step 6 and is excluded by the statement; a non-smooth cusp invalidates the regularity input; a disconnected extra component invalidates the global domain injection and is excluded.
