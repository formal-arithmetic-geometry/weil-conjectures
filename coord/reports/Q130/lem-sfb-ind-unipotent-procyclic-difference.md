### Original step 1

Apply [lem-sfb-relative-cyclic-restriction-model] to $R=A$, $H=\mathbf Z_\ell$ and $u:\mathbf Z_{\mathrm{disc}}\to H$, $k\mapsto k$. For every module $N$ with commuting $G,H$ actions this constructs the actual morphism $c_N^G:R\Gamma_H^G(N[0])\to Q[N\xrightarrow{\tau-1}N]$. It belongs to a transformation of triangulated functors on $D^+$, and forgetting $G$ takes it to the scalar-action version $c_N^1$ and reflects its invertibility. It therefore suffices to establish that $c_M^1$ is an isomorphism after forgetting $G$. This use does not assume that dense restriction is an isomorphism.

Uses: lem-sfb-relative-cyclic-restriction-model.

### Original step 2

First take the trivial $H$-module $A$. Form the underived scalar bar $U_A^q(S)=C(S\times H^q,F)$ and its canonical augmentation $j_A:QU_A^\bullet\to\mathscr B_A(H)$. For $F=E$, [lem-sfb-normed-scalar-profinite-hom-acyclic] applies to every profinite $P=H^q$ and gives the columnwise resolution quasi-isomorphisms. The bar faces respect them by that node's parameter naturality. The first-quadrant column-comparison theorem makes their total $j_A$ an isomorphism; the source columns are concentrated in resolution degree zero. ### Original step 3

By [lem-sfb-procyclic-scalar-cochain-evaluation], the map $\epsilon:U_A^\bullet\to[A\xrightarrow0 A]$ that is the identity in degree zero and evaluates a one-cochain at $1$ in degree one is a quasi-isomorphism of the explicit function-sheaf complexes. The cited statement gives equality of image and kernel sheaves, allowing local primitives after a finite jointly surjective parameter cover; no global sectionwise exactness is required. The formal normalization in [lem-sfb-relative-cyclic-restriction-model] gives $c_A^1\eta^{-1}j_A=Q\epsilon$, where $\eta:R\Gamma_H A\simeq\mathscr B_A(H)$. ### Original step 4

Fix a finite-dimensional norm-topological $E$-vector space $W$ and a nilpotent $D_W$. A choice of basis identifies $C(-,F\otimes_E W)$ with a finite biproduct of $A$ as internal modules. In the case $D_W=0$, $H$ acts trivially, so the same is an equivariant isomorphism. The two functors defining $c^1$ are additive and the transformation is natural by [lem-sfb-relative-cyclic-restriction-model]. On a finite biproduct its components are therefore copies of $c_A^1$. This proves invertibility for every trivial finite-dimensional stage, including $W=0$.

Uses: lem-sfb-relative-cyclic-restriction-model.

### Original step 5

For a general finite stage put $W_0=\ker D_W$. It is $H$-stable and $H$ acts trivially on it. The quotient $\overline W=W/W_0$ has the induced nilpotent operator and exponential action. Choose an $E$-linear complement to $W_0$ by extending a basis; in finite-dimensional product norm coordinates inclusion and quotient are continuous and the chosen section is continuous. After $F\otimes_E-$ and $C(S,-)$ this gives a split exact sequence on every $S$, hence a short exact sequence of internal $H$-modules $0\to M(W_0)\to M(W)\to M(\overline W)\to0$. The splitting is used only to prove underlying exactness, and is not asserted equivariant. Exponentials commute with the maps because they are finite polynomials in $D_W$.

### Original step 6

Induct on $\dim_E W$. The zero-dimensional case was covered in step 4, and $D_W=0$ is also covered there. If $D_W\ne0$, nilpotence gives $\ker D_W\ne0$: for a nonzero vector take its last nonzero iterate under $D_W$. Thus $\dim\overline W<\dim W$; the kernel has trivial action and the quotient satisfies the same finite-stage hypotheses. The short exact sequence of step 5 gives a distinguished triangle by [lem-weil-pbc-complex-ses-triangle]. ### Original step 7

Now forget $G$ from the original sequence and take its sheaf colimit $M$. [lem-sfb-profinite-invariants-filtered-continuity], applied to $J=H$ and $I=\mathbf N$, identifies $\operatorname{colim}_n H^q(R\Gamma_HM_n)$ with $H^q(R\Gamma_HM)$ by the actual coprojection maps, for every $q\ge0$. ### Original step 8

Naturality of $c^1$ for every coprojection gives, for each $q\ge0$, a commutative square whose top arrow is $\operatorname{colim}_n H^q(c_{M_n}^1)$ and whose bottom arrow is $H^q(c_M^1)$; its two vertical arrows are exactly the comparisons of step 7. The top arrow is an isomorphism by step 6, so the bottom arrow is an isomorphism. In negative degrees both sides have zero cohomology, since right derived invariants on a degree-zero module and the two-term complex are bounded below by zero. Thus $c_M^1$ is an isomorphism in the ordinary derived category, in which quasi-isomorphisms are inverted. No infinite nilpotent induction or uniform nilpotence exponent was used.

Uses: lem-sfb-relative-cyclic-restriction-model, def-weil-abstract-derived-category.

### Original step 9

Restore the $G$-action. By the conservative forgetting and comparison clause of [lem-sfb-relative-cyclic-restriction-model], the image of $c_M^G$ under forgetting $G$ is the isomorphism $c_M^1$ just proved. Hence $c_M^G$ is invertible in $D(\mathcal A_G(A))$. Its target is the required complex: at stage $n$, $\tau$ acts by the given finite exponential $\exp(D_n)$, and compatibility with transition maps identifies its colimit action with $\exp(D)$. Thus $\tau-1=\exp(D)-1$. This proves the derived-object assertion with its commuting residual action.

Uses: lem-sfb-relative-cyclic-restriction-model.

### Original step 10

The formal comparison has its invariant-inclusion normalization, so its degree-zero map identifies the two subobjects by their inclusions in $M$. One can also check their equality directly without assuming separatedness of the colimit topology: on a finite stage write $\exp(D_n)-1=D_n U(D_n)$ where $U(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{e_n}X^{j-1}/j!$. The endomorphism $U(D_n)$ has the form $1+$ a nilpotent polynomial and is invertible by a finite geometric sum, commuting with $D_n$. Hence its difference kernel is $\ker D_n$, which is fixed by every $\exp(tD_n)$, including continuously varying $t$. A section of the sheaf colimit is locally from a stage; if its difference is zero, refine locally to a later stage where that equation holds. The finite-stage argument then proves it fixed after every base change. Conversely $H$-fixed sections are fixed by $1\in H$. Thus the common inclusion is exactly the required one. All arguments include zero stages and $D_n=0$, and only finite-stage nilpotence was used.

Uses: lem-sfb-relative-cyclic-restriction-model, lem-sfb-profinite-invariants-filtered-continuity.
