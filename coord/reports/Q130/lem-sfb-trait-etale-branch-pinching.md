### Old step 2 → lem-sfb-finite-centre-proper-pinching

Apply [lem-sfb-ferrand-scheme-pushout] with $(X,Z,T)=(C,B,N)$; finite implies integral. Obtain $Y$ and maps $a:C\to Y$, $b:N\hookrightarrow Y$. The theorem gives the cartesian square, the universal property for maps to every scheme, and the isomorphism $C\setminus B\simeq Y\setminus N$, since $B\to N$ is surjective. Its quotient-space description also makes $a$ surjective. On a simultaneous affine chart write $P=A\times_DE$, with $\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq C$, $\operatorname{Spec}E\subseteq N$, $\operatorname{Spec}D\subseteq B$. The map $E\to D$ is finite. Choose $E$-module generators $d_1,\ldots,d_r$ of $D$ and lifts $x_i\in A$. For $x\in A$ write its image as $\sum e_id_i$ and lift each $e_i$ to a pair $(u_i,e_i)\in P$, possible because $A\to D$ is surjective. Then $x-\sum u_ix_i$ belongs to $\ker(A\to D)$ and is the $A$-coordinate of a pair in $P$. Thus $1,x_1,\ldots,x_r$ generate $A$ over $P$. The affine morphism $a$ is finite.

Uses: lem-sfb-ferrand-scheme-pushout.

### Old step 3 → lem-sfb-finite-centre-proper-pinching

The finite surjection $a$ and quasi-compactness of $C$ make $Y$ quasi-compact, so $Y/S$ is of finite type. ### Old step 4 → lem-sfb-flat-trait-pinching-base-change

$$0\longrightarrow P\longrightarrow A\oplus E\xrightarrow{(u,v)\mapsto\bar u-j^*v}D\longrightarrow0$$
is exact, because $A\to D$ is surjective. Since its last term $D$ is $R$-flat, [lem-weil-pbc-flat-quotient-tensor-injective] keeps it exact after tensoring with every $R$-algebra $R'$, so $$P\otimes_RR'\cong(A\otimes_RR')\times_{D\otimes_RR'}(E\otimes_RR'),$$ and $A\otimes_RR'\to D\otimes_RR'$ is still surjective. Now let $S'=\operatorname{Spec}R'$ be the generic point or a geometric point of $S$. The base-changed diagram $C_{S'}\hookleftarrow B_{S'}\to N_{S'}$ satisfies the hypotheses of [lem-sfb-ferrand-scheme-pushout]: $B_{S'}\to C_{S'}$ is a closed immersion by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change]; $B_{S'}\to N_{S'}$ is finite, since on affine charts the finite module generators persist under $-\otimes_RR'$; and the inverse image $U\times_SS'$ of the affine open $U\supseteq B$ of Step 1 is affine, being $\operatorname{Spec}$ of a tensor product of rings, and contains $B_{S'}$. So the pushout $P'=C_{S'}\amalg_{B_{S'}}N_{S'}$ exists, and the base changes of $a,b$ induce a canonical map $\varphi:P'\to Y_{S'}$. For a chart $W$ of Step 2, the open $W_{S'}=\operatorname{Spec}(P\otimes_RR')$ has inverse images $\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_RR')$, $\operatorname{Spec}(E\otimes_RR')$, $\operatorname{Spec}(D\otimes_RR')$ in $C_{S'},N_{S'},B_{S'}$. By the affine clause of [lem-sfb-ferrand-scheme-pushout] and the displayed identity, $W_{S'}$ is the pushout of these three affine schemes. By its open-restriction clause, applied in $P'$, so is $\varphi^{-1}(W_{S'})$, which has the same three inverse images. Hence $\varphi$ is an isomorphism over every $W_{S'}$, and these opens cover $Y_{S'}$. So $Y_{S'}$ is the pushout of the base-changed diagram, and its square is the Ferrand square of that diagram. This includes the nonflat base change to a geometric point of the special fibre.

### Old step 6 → lem-sfb-finite-branch-ample-descent
