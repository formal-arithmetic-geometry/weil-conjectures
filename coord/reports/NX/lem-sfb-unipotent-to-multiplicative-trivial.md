## New-node arguments for future workers

The fixed-vector node is precisely Theorem 14.5(b)⇒(a). Milne proves it by filtering the coordinate algebra of the unitriangular group by the weights j-i of the upper matrix coordinates, passing that coconnected filtration to a quotient Hopf algebra for the closed subgroup, and proving that every nonzero comodule has a nonzero invariant vector. This applies to infinitesimal subgroup schemes as well as smooth ones; the invariant is fixed on every algebra, not just on k-points. The new node is strictly smaller than the item: it has a unitriangular embedding supplied over the ground field and asserts fixed vectors, without any multiplicative-type target, extension-invariance assertion or vanishing conclusion.

For existence descent let F be algebraically closed, let L/F be any extension, and write F[G]=B, F[H]=C. A given closed group embedding over L gives a surjective Hopf map C⊗L→B⊗L. Spread its finitely many algebra-generator images to a finitely generated F-subalgebra R⊂L. Include coefficients of preimages of finitely many F-algebra generators of B, and spread the resulting finitely many identities expressing surjectivity. Spread the finite algebra relations and group-law compatibilities as well. Equalities descend after increasing R (Stacks 01ZM(2)–(3)); equivalently choose finite expressions and finitely many relation witnesses. The resulting map C⊗R→B⊗R is a surjective Hopf map. A maximal ideal of nonzero R has residue field finite over F by 00FV, hence equal to F, and specialization gives a closed group embedding over F. The reverse direction is base change. This argument proves existence only; it does not purport to descend the original embedding as a specified morphism. The new node is not a clause of the item: it is an arbitrary pair G,H and a general finite-presentation specialization fact.
