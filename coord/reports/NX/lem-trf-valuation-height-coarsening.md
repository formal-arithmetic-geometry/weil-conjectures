The relative group statement is my explicit refinement of the convex-chain/rational-rank argument underlying Temkin's height reasoning. To check its truth, consider a chain C_0<...<C_n in Gamma. Intersections with H are convex in H. At most h transitions change their intersection. At each of the remaining transitions i choose x_i in C_i outside C_{i-1}. The classes of these x_i are rationally independent in (Gamma/H) tensor Q. Indeed a relation, after clearing rational denominators and the torsion killed by rationalization, gives a nontrivial integer combination in H. At its largest index j the resulting H element belongs to C_j; since this transition has unchanged H intersection, that element belongs to C_{j-1}. All earlier terms belong there too, so a nonzero integer multiple of x_j belongs to C_{j-1}. A convex subgroup of an ordered group is saturated: if m|x| is in it, then 0<=|x|<=m|x| puts x in it. Contradiction. The unchanged transitions number at most e, giving n<=h+e. This is the substantive independent lemma; none of this proof has been put into its statement-only node. It specializes to the absolute rational-rank bound when H=0 but is not that bound in general. No convexity of H or order on Gamma/H is assumed or needed.
