Step 1 uses the first sentence of `lem-weil-pbc-z-localizations-dvr`, which explicitly gives Noetherianity, integral closedness and dimension one of Z. The localization-normal and localization-Noetherian nodes give exactly those two properties for R, and clause (1) of the localization-prime-residue node supplies the bijection with its extension inverse. Both directions preserve inclusion, so the dimension bound follows. Its multiplicative subset excludes zero because N is positive. The realization of R as the subring of C is justified by the fraction formula, so the later structural injections are the actual ones.

Step 2 applies `lem-sfb-unit-binomial-finite-etale` with positive n=N, the unit N, and u=1. Its statement gives both the module basis and the scheme-theoretic etale map; no change of etale definitions is assumed. Evaluation is well-defined by zeta^N=1 and surjective by the definition of A. Images of the displayed basis generate A. The finite-algebra-Noetherian node can therefore be applied to B and A separately over the already Noetherian ring R.

Step 3 uses clause (c) of the affine-open-stalk-localization node, not just the definition of Spec, to identify the actual stalks with R_r. Normal-domain localization applies since R is a domain and each prime complement excludes zero. The etale-smooth node really asserts smoothness, and the smooth-normal-ascent node really asserts integral closedness and the domain property of every source stalk; it imposes no missing finiteness or surjectivity assumption.

Step 4 verifies all hypotheses of `lem-sbc-normal-sections-finite-product`: local Noetherianity from the single Noetherian affine chart, the quasi-compact whole open from the quasi-compact-spectrum node, and normal domain stalks from step 3. The affine-spectrum definition explicitly supplies the global-section ring identification used. The component theorem itself does not assert that the factors are finite R-modules: this follows here by projecting the finite list of module generators of B, with each factor's R-action defined by that very projection.

Step 5 is the explicit finite coordinate-idempotent argument. Since A is a nonzero domain, there is exactly one coordinate with image 1; all others have image 0. This also rules out an empty product. Thus the factor map is genuinely surjective and R-linear, and its kernel is prime.

Step 6 applies finite-implies-integral to the selected component, whose module-finiteness was proved in step 4. The inclusion R into A makes both the component structural map and its composite injective. In particular (0) and ker h are comparable prime ideals of the domain D_i with the same zero contraction. This is exactly the statement of `pqf-lem-incomparability`; hence ker h=0. No assertion that arbitrary quotients of normal rings are normal is used.

Step 7 applies the same finite-implies-integral node to A. Incomparability turns strict prime chains into strict contraction chains, so dimension is at most one. When p is nonzero, (0)<p gives dimension exactly one, and no larger prime can exist. All hypotheses of `lem-sc-dedekind-localization-dvr` are now present: a Noetherian integrally closed domain of dimension one and a maximal ideal. Step 8 uses the fraction definition from `def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime`; the inverse of a nonzero a/s is s/a. This proves the field clause without applying the nonzero-prime DVR theorem at zero.
