### Original step 1

Choose flat-term K-flat complexes $Q\to K$ on $U$ and $F\to P$ on $X$. The open semilinear tensor-model lemma gives a flat-term K-flat representative $j_!Q$ of $j_!K$ and a chain isomorphism
$G=\operatorname{Tot}(j_!Q\otimes F)\cong j_!\operatorname{Tot}(Q\otimes j^*F)=j_!G_U$.
Restriction and coefficient pullback preserve flat-term K-flat representatives, so $j^*F$, $Q_n=Q\otimes B_{n,U}$ and $F_n=F\otimes B_{n,X}$ compute the indicated derived objects. Here $B_{n,W}=(A_n)_W$; coefficient pullback is applied on the identity topos with its map $B_W\to B_{n,W}$. The chain isomorphism is the ordinary slice tensor isomorphism, in the inverse-open-unit-transpose normalization of $\gamma^B$. All complexes remain unbounded; these are computational K-flat choices, not a finite-Tor assumption on $K$ or $P$.

Uses: lem-pc-kflat-resolution, lem-sf-open-semilinear-tensor-model, lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-derived-tensor-pullback, lem-sf-open-tensor-normalization.

### Original step 2

Apply the strict semilinear tensor-model statement to $Q$. It supplies a single $A_\bullet$-linear chain isomorphism $r_Q:((j_!Q)\otimes B_{n,X})_n\to J(Q_n)_n$ with formula $[x]\otimes a\mapsto[x\otimes a|_U]$, commuting with all quotient maps and scalar units. Its evaluations localize to the canonical open scalar comparisons. Their inverses are the open-unit transposes by the open tensor normalization, hence have exactly the normalization specified by the coefficient-mate definition. Since $K$ is complete, the integral open reduction comparison identifies this localization with the already specified $r_j(K)$ and proves that $j_!K$ is complete. This constructs $r_\bullet$ as a simultaneous system map; it is not assembled from unrelated derived evaluations.

Uses: lem-sf-open-semilinear-tensor-model, lem-sf-open-tensor-normalization, def-sf-open-reduction-coefficient-mate, lem-sf-integral-open-reduction-comparison.

### Original step 3

Scalar restriction commutes with the open extensions in the statement: on each extension-presheaf summand it retains the same underlying abelian group, with $B_X$ acting through $B_X\to B_{n,X}$; this is the same action obtained by first restricting scalars on the open and then extending. The restriction maps and quotient transitions are the same maps on these summands. Sheafification has the same underlying additive sheaf with this induced action, so the identification also holds for the module sheaves and termwise for complexes and strict systems. In particular $r_Q$ retains its $A_n$-linear identification before scalar restriction, as well as its strict $B_X$-linear tower identification afterwards.

Uses: def-weil-extension-by-zero-presheaf, lem-pur-site-restriction-module-adjunction-exact, lem-sf-open-semilinear-tensor-model.

### Original step 4

The field $k$ is Noetherian (its ideals are $0$ and $k$), so finite type over $k$ implies that $X$ is locally Noetherian by the finite-type Noetherian lemma. The definition of finite type gives quasi-compactness over $\operatorname{Spec}k$, hence $X$ is Noetherian. The open $j$ is therefore quasi-compact. For each $n$, apply the finite open constructibility-interval lemma to $K_n$: via $r_n$ it gives constructible cohomology of $(j_!K)_n$, the equality $\mathcal H^q(j_!^{A_n}K_n)\cong j_!^{A_n}\mathcal H^q(K_n)$, the same interval as $K_n$, and exactly the asserted partition with the reduced closed complement and zero sheaf. The single interval in the integral definition therefore works for every $n$. Combining this with completeness from step 2 proves $j_!K\in D^b_c(X,R)$. Apply the immersion completed-pullback comparison to the open immersion $j$ and $P$: it gives ordinary exact $j^*P\in D^b_c(U,R)$ with the same uniform interval and quotient identifications. Its strata are the intersections of the original strata with $U$, omitting empty intersections.
