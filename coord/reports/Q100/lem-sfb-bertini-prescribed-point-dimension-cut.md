## Source and route

I read Charles–Poonen, Bertini irreducibility theorems over finite fields, Theorem 1.8, printed p.4, including all three proof paragraphs. A variety there means separated finite type, not necessarily quasi-projective. The theorem has a finite prescribed closed-point set and gives the codimension-one subvariety. Over algebraically closed k the reduced induced structure is integral. Corollary 1.9 is not the item: it iterates this codimension-one result.

The source first compactifies by Nagata, then applies Chow, adds a point in the birational isomorphism open, and takes a projective Bertini section. The existing lem-sfb-integral-chow-envelope-compactification gives the same projective situation more directly: P is proper over X, P is dense open in integral projective Z, and P is isomorphic over a nonempty open U of X. Take the Bertini divisor in Z, restrict to P, then take its proper image in X. This is the same open-restriction and proper-image argument in the source, with their order reversed. The point p0 over U ensures both operations preserve dimension. The latest blueprint unit and direct-images proof (3) were read: they give the six-functor context, not this auxiliary interpolation argument. The inherited chain has no surviving imported ancestor; blueprint_refs.py ancestor returns None, so all from.node fields honestly retain null and the full via chain.

For the new lemma I read Stacks more-morphisms.tex, Tag 0G4F, label lemma-bertini-irreducible, especially hypotheses (3) and (4), and varieties.tex, Tag 0A21, lemma-dimension-locally-algebraic, including maximal-chain and specialization statements. Also read 0FD5, lemma-generate-over-complement, for the actual application. The lemma says only that an immersive system off a finite set satisfies those two hypotheses; it gives neither a good general member nor an integral divisor through the prescribed points.

A proof of the new lemma, retained here for its future worker: Since the sections generate off S, the common zero set is contained in finite S. Its irreducible components are single closed points; in an integral d-dimensional variety each has codimension d by 0A21. For the witness choose a nonempty smooth affine open A in T minus S, possible because k is perfect and T is integral finite type. This uses the dense smooth-locus theorem (Stacks varieties.tex, lemma-geometrically-reduced-dense-smooth-open); it is an ingredient of the new lemma, not an uncited step of the item's proof. Choose a closed k-point a of A. On a projective coordinate chart where a section t is nonzero, the ratios of the system's sections give an immersion. Its cotangent map is surjective, so two k-linear combinations f,g of these ratios have independent cotangent classes at a, since d>=2. Subtract their values at a, corresponding to replacing the numerator sections by u-f(a)t and v-g(a)t. In the regular local ring O_T,a, these two functions are part of a regular parameter system; their quotient is a regular local domain of dimension d-2. Thus the intersection of the corresponding two global zero schemes has an irreducible component W through a, locally of codimension two. The local-ring/dimension formula in 0A21 gives codimension two for W in T. The section t is nonzero at a, so W is not contained in its zero scheme. This supplies (a), and the preceding finite-support argument supplies (b). All uses of smoothness are on the auxiliary open A, not assumptions on T or on S. For an implementation the cotangent/regular-parameter and dense-smooth-open results will be the next-level dependencies when this node is split.

## Review by Q100

I checked B1–B10, C1–C8, D1–D5 and E1–E7 with the JSON files and the sources open, and made the following specific checks.

Step 1 uses the integral envelope statement, not the much weaker Chow-surjection node: the envelope explicitly gives integral P and Z, a dense open inclusion, a closed projective embedding, a proper surjection and a nonempty isomorphism open U. Its affine Noetherian base is Spec k. The dimension lemma applies to integral locally finite-type schemes and nonempty opens; all three schemes and both opens meet these hypotheses. The finite-type-over-Noetherian lemma supplies Noetherianity where quasi-compactness of opens is needed. I read Stacks 0208's proof and 0200 context to check the envelope's precise role.

Step 2 uses the Jacobson statement for existence of closed points in nonempty closed fibres and in a nonempty open. It does not infer that an arbitrary point of a fibre is closed. The closed-point residue statement gives k at each chosen p_x; lem-kun-closed-point-field then proves it is closed in Z, even though P is only open in Z. The point p0 is chosen directly in Z's open over U, and can coincide with another marked point. If F is empty, p0 remains. This is precisely the source's important nonemptiness safeguard.

Step 3 uses existence of reduced induced closed structure for the finite closed subset, very-ample-implies-ample for the closed embedding Z into projective space, and 0FD5 for the full kernel system generating off S and giving an immersion there. Proper coherent finiteness at q=0 supplies the finite-dimensional section space and its linear subspace. An invertible sheaf is quasi-coherent and finitely presented, locally free of rank one; Z is proper as required. H^0 is the global-sections functor in degree zero. The new lemma is applied only to integral finite-type Z of dimension m>=2, and its statement gives both hypotheses taken from it. I did not use the smooth-affine finite-cover versions outside their hypotheses.

Step 4 checks geometric irreducibility rather than assuming it. The algebraic-elements argument makes k algebraically closed in k(Z); 037P gives the geometric irreducibility of Spec k(Z), and 054Q gives that of Z. The irreducible-member node's full statement explicitly supplies a nonempty coefficient open over every extension. Its codimension-two witness and base condition come from the new node. Choosing a nonzero coefficient is legitimate: the kernel system is not the zero system since it generates off finite S in dimension >=2; a nonempty open in irreducible affine space meets the complement of zero. The Jacobson and residue statements supply a k-point of that open. All vanishing at S is retained.

Step 5 uses only the local description of zero schemes and integrality to see that s defines a Cartier divisor: its nonzero germ in the function field cannot restrict to zero on any nonempty affine trivialization, whose ring is a domain. The Cartier purity statement assumes integral finite type of pure dimension m; Z is integral, so its sole irreducible component has that dimension. H is nonempty since it contains S, and irreducible by Step 4; purity therefore gives dim H=m-1. Reduced induced structure gives actual reduced D with the same underlying space, not an unspecified existence object.

Step 6 uses E=D intersect P as a scheme-theoretic open restriction of D and closed subscheme of P. It is not an alleged factorization from mere set containment. It contains every marked point and hence is nonempty integral of dimension m-1. The cited closed-immersion-proper and proper-composition statements prove the actual map E to X is proper; the proper-closed-image statement is then applicable. Irreducibility of its image is proved by the elementary inverse-image-of-closed-covers argument. The reduced-induced-structure statement creates Y as an actual closed integral subscheme containing F.

Step 7 does not assume birational maps preserve dimension on every subvariety. The added p0 ensures E meets the open over U. Over U the map is an isomorphism, and both relevant subschemes have the same reduced induced structure; the uniqueness clause of the cited reduced-structure theorem identifies them. The dimension-of-opens lemma then proves dim Y=m-1. The final finite-type and separated assertions follow from affine quotient rings and the closed diagonal of X, as written out, with no properness of X added.

I tested the new lemma on the minimal dimension d=2, including a singular surface with S containing all chosen singular points: the witness is sought on a different smooth open, and its intersection is locally zero-dimensional. Finite base locus has codimension 2 in this case, exactly the boundary needed in 0G4F. S empty gives empty common zero set and a vacuous base condition; the witness argument still works. A one-dimensional variety would fail the two-independent-cotangent-directions argument, and is explicitly excluded. A system composed with a pencil can fail, but such a system is not an immersion of a d>=2 variety off S. For the item I checked F empty, one point, several points, m=2, points in the exceptional image of the Chow map, and singular X. Closed fibre points exist over every prescribed point because the envelope map is surjective. The extra point over U prevents dimension collapse even if all original points lie in the exceptional image. All seven proof steps are shorter than 2000 characters.
