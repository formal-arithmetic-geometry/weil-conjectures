## Source errors and corrections

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported gloss “scheme-theoretic intersection of the image of c with the diagonal” is false for a general correspondence. Let X=s=Spec k, C=Spec(k[ε]/(ε²)), and take the unique map c:C→s=X×_sX (one can take coefficient complex zero and coefficient map zero). The defining pullback is C. The scheme-theoretic image of c is s and its intersection with the diagonal is s, which is reduced and is not C. The corrected statement says scheme-theoretic inverse image of the diagonal along c, retaining its scheme over C. It preserves the displayed defining fibre product exactly.

STATEMENT-DISPUTED (typing): if “h:X→X a morphism” permits arbitrary scheme endomorphisms, (id_X,h):X→X×_sX need not exist. It requires a_X∘h=a_X. For X=s=Spec(k), a_X=id, take h induced by the nonidentity p-power automorphism of the algebraic closure k of a finite field. Then a_X∘h=h≠id. The graph formula is ill-typed. I made the intended k-endomorphism condition explicit, in agreement with the existing graph-correspondence definition and the project graph constructor. This is a correction of the omitted base compatibility, not a change to the intended relative fixed scheme.

The phrase “the set of open-and-closed connected components” could be misread as discarding nonopen components. I defined π₀(F) to mean all maximal nonempty connected subsets and the stated condition to mean that this set is finite and all its members are clopen. The subscheme on a clopen component is its open restriction, including nilpotents. No automatic finiteness theorem about finite-type schemes is being invoked or added; the coproduct description remains conditional on the stated finite clopen condition.

1. **Diagonal and fixed projections.** `def-pc-fibre-product` states existence, the commuting projection relation, the universal property for *every scheme T*, and uniqueness of the induced morphism. Apply it to a_X twice to obtain Δ from (id,id). Apply it to c and Δ to construct F,i_F,d. If p₁,p₂ are the two projections of X×_sX, compose c i_F=Δ d with each: p_j c i_F=p_j Δ d=d. Thus c₁i_F=d=c₂i_F, without an immersion or properness assumption on c. The cohomological data are supplied by `def-trf-correspondence`; I read its statement and kept its separated finite-type X,C hypotheses and the original finite-field/coefficient convention dependencies. `def-weil-scheme-morphism-properties` really gives the separated/finite-type conventions used here, not existence of products; the latter now has its own exact citation. The statements of `def-trf-coefficient-conventions` and `def-weil-finite-field-fiber` were also opened and checked.

2. **Graph/equalizer description.** With h over s, (id,h) exists by the same fibre-product universal property. For a test scheme T, a map to its pullback is a pair (t,v) with (t,h t)=(v,v). Composing with the two product projections gives t=v and h t=v. Conversely h t=t gives the pair (t,t), which satisfies the product equality because both projections agree; existence and uniqueness follow from `def-pc-fibre-product`. This proves precisely the stated scheme-theoretic equalizer description for all test schemes, including nonreduced ones. It does not identify a scheme equalizer with only its fixed underlying points. No separate node restating this clause is necessary: this is a direct two-projection calculation from the cited construction.

3. **Components and coproduct.** `def-weil-connected-scheme` states nonempty connectedness by the clopen-separation condition. The definition of maximal connected subset is spelled out inline. For completeness, for x in the underlying space form the union of all connected subsets containing x. In a separation of this union, every such subset must lie on the side containing x, so the union is connected and maximal. The same separation argument shows a union of two intersecting connected subsets is connected. Hence each point lies in one component, and distinct components are disjoint. These are the elementary arguments in Tag 004T, written out here rather than assumed from the connectedness-definition node. Under the condition expressly stated in the item, every component is an open subset. `def-weil-open-immersion` explicitly identifies its open subscheme with (β,O_F|β). `lem-weil-pbc-scheme-coproduct-family` explicitly gives the coproduct and its universal mapping property. For arbitrary morphisms β→Y, `lem-weil-pbc-scheme-morphism-gluing` explicitly gives a unique morphism F→Y from compatible maps on an open cover. The cover here consists of the components just proved disjoint and exhaustive, and compatibility on distinct overlaps is vacuous. Thus F satisfies the same coproduct mapping property, proving the canonical identification; alternatively glue the component inclusions into the coproduct to obtain the inverse and check both composites on each component. There is no unproved assumption of local connectedness, reducedness, smallness of an unbounded indexing category, or Noetherian finiteness here. The index is the set of components, assumed finite. Empty F has no components and is the empty coproduct. There are no non-elementary hidden facts beyond the two exact existing coproduct/gluing nodes, both Mathlib leaves.
