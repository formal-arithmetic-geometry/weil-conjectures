## Proof guidance for the new prerequisites

For the stable-image lemma, the matrices commute, so the binomial formula makes P(X)=X^p additive and Frobenius-semilinear. For a basis e_i, W_r is the span of the e_i^(p^r); consequently these spans commute with every scalar extension, without any perfectness assumption. They form a decreasing finite-dimensional sequence. Over the algebraic closure, every n by n matrix has nilpotent Jordan part killed by a common exponent p^r >= n. Thus high p-powers are semisimple; their finite linear combinations are semisimple because commuting diagonalizable matrices are simultaneously diagonalizable. Conversely the semisimple elements of W over the algebraic closure form a vector space S. P restricts to an injective Frobenius-semilinear endomorphism of S (a semisimple nilpotent matrix is zero), hence is surjective there because that field is perfect. Therefore S lies in every p-power image. This identifies the stable image with S over the algebraic closure. The span base-change formula and injectivity of scalar extension then give exactly the stated W_infinity and its geometric equality. The argument does not claim P is surjective on k-rational points.

For the characteristic-zero criterion, Engel gives nilpotence of the adjoint Lie action. One can integrate its full flag of trivial successive Lie quotients to the connected group in characteristic zero (stabilizers and kernels are smooth and a full-tangent closed subgroup equals the connected group, Milne 10.15). The adjoint group therefore acts unitriangularly. The kernel of Ad is the centre in characteristic zero: each fixed-conjugation closed subgroup with full tangent is the whole connected group. A central extension of a unitriangular group has a finite central series; the same construction over k, or descent of the canonical descending central series, gives the stated k-subgroups. This general integration criterion is the non-elementary prerequisite, not something concealed in an elementary step of the item. Its node does not assume semisimple-centrality, does not conclude positive characteristic, and does not restate a clause of the item.

For the stabilizer lemma, Milne 17.76 proves the tangent equality by (1+epsilon Y)X(1-epsilon Y)=X+epsilon[Y,X], and proves smoothness by the semisimple-derivation zero-scheme argument. Its existing general derivation node can be used when that prerequisite is split. The item uses its statement only, without taking a construction from the proof of the existing noncentral-element reduction.

For each of the three new nodes I read these source places and distinguished the author's assertion from my general auxiliary formulation. Milne leaves both the positive-characteristic exclusion and the imperfect-field subspace detail implicit; I expose the former as a general group integration criterion and the latter as a commuting-matrix theorem, rather than attributing new numbered results to the PDF.

Step 1 uses the statement of algebraic-lie-additive-jordan, which gives rational Jordan parts over a perfect field and nilpotence in every rational group representation. Characteristic zero makes k perfect, and Ad is such a representation. Its semisimple part is central by the actual geometric hypothesis. The new charzero-ad-nilpotence-group-criterion then supplies exactly the contradicting group central series, so the step does not confuse nilpotence of a Lie algebra with nilpotence of a group.

Step 2 uses the faithful tangent-injectivity assertion of algebraic-lie-additive-jordan and the precise restricted-rational-differentiation statement: rational differentials preserve p-powers and rho(X^[p])=rho(X)^p. Applying the latter to the fixed embedding and Ad proves that the matrix Lie centre is p-closed. The equality of Lie centres under base extension is the kernel of the explicit bracket matrix, so ordinary row reduction proves it. No group centre or smoothness of the centre is being assumed here.

Step 3 uses only commuting-p-power-stable-image: step 2 verified its positive characteristic, commutation and p-closure hypotheses. Its stable subspace equals all semisimple matrices in the centre over k and over the algebraic closure. The item's hypothesis identifies those sets with all semisimple tangent vectors, and the zero bracket proves bracket closure. This is an application of a general matrix theorem to the Lie centre, not a new node that restates the item with J renamed.

Step 4 uses the new semisimple-tangent-stabilizer statement for each actual X, including zero. Centrality gives its entire tangent space. I checked all four other cited statements: connected-rational-point-geometrically-connected applies because the identity is a k-section; finite-type-group-identity-subgroup supplies an open and closed connected subgroup with no field restriction; open-immersion-smooth-composition gives its smoothness; smooth-subgroup-tangent-generation applies over the algebraically closed field with the singleton family and with C as the containing subgroup. It does not require that containing subgroup to be connected. The final coordinate-ideal calculation explicitly descends C_F=J_F to C=J, establishing equality of group schemes, not merely geometric points. The two citations from other units are declared generic tools in split_report.tool_uses.

Step 5 is elementary A-linear algebra. Scheme-theoretic stabilizer equality in step 4 gives fixed basis vectors for every commutative k-algebra, and the written sum calculation gives triviality on all of A tensor s(J). It does not infer triviality from Lie-level invariance in positive characteristic.
