## Preserved argument for lem-trf-invariant-coordinate-transfinite-filtration

This node is the transfinite construction and exhaustion argument of Stacks Tag 058X, theorem-kaplansky-direct-sum, proof lines 20500–20533 and 20555–20565. It assumes the chosen invariant countable blocks D_j, so the existing countable-coordinate-closure lemma remains a separate input in the parent. It does not assume that the modules themselves are countably generated: after the invariant blocks have been supplied, only their coordinate support and finite support are used. Its conclusion includes the actual recursive equations, monotonicity, the initial-segment invariant, countable differences, invariance, both endpoints, and continuity of the coordinate submodules.

The two full arguments of the returned split, with ordinal indices identified with J along the bijection η in the new statement, were:

Choose a well-order of $J$ and identify it with an ordinal $\kappa$. Define sets $A_\alpha\subseteq J$ for $\alpha\leq\kappa$ by transfinite recursion: $A_0=\varnothing$; $A_\lambda=\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}A_\beta$ at a nonzero limit; if $A_\alpha=J$, put $E_\alpha=\varnothing$, and otherwise put $E_\alpha=D_j$ for the least $j\notin A_\alpha$; set $A_{\alpha+1}=A_\alpha\cup E_\alpha$. Each $E_\alpha$ is at most countable. Let $M_\alpha=M_{A_\alpha}$. Inductively $e(M_\alpha)\subseteq M_\alpha$: at a successor every element of $M_{A_\alpha\cup E_\alpha}$ is a sum of an element supported in $A_\alpha$ and one supported in $E_\alpha$, both preserved by $e$; at a limit any finite support in the increasing union lies in one earlier $A_\beta$, so $M_\lambda=\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}M_\beta$ and is preserved as well. The base is $e(0)=0$.

The recursion exhausts $J$ by stage $\kappa$. More precisely, prove $\{\beta:\beta<\alpha\}\subseteq A_\alpha$ for every $\alpha\leq\kappa$ by ordinal induction. At $0$ this is empty. At a successor all indices below $\alpha$ are already in $A_\alpha$; if $\alpha$ is absent then it is the least omitted index, so $E_\alpha=D_\alpha$ contains it. If it is already present, nothing needs adding to cover the initial segment through $\alpha$. At a nonzero limit $\lambda$, any $\beta<\lambda$ lies in $A_{\beta+1}\subseteq A_\lambda$, since $\beta+1<\lambda$. Thus $A_\kappa=J$ and $M_\kappa=M$. This argument still enumerates indices of zero summands, so no inference that a missing summand must be nonzero is needed.

The well-order in the new statement is supplied existentially by η; the least omitted index is measured in this enumeration. The statement includes the initial-segment invariant explicitly, rather than merely claiming exhaustion. At a successor, the old-stage module plus the selected invariant block spans the new coordinate submodule even if the two subsets overlap. At a nonzero limit, a finite support is contained in an earlier stage of the increasing family. These two arguments establish invariance and module continuity and are assigned to this node, not suppressed in a parent step. Empty J gives κ=0 and the single empty coordinate set. Zero coordinate modules do not affect the recursion, which enumerates indices rather than testing whether a missing coordinate contributes a nonzero module.

## Preserved argument for lem-trf-idempotent-coordinate-filtration-quotients

This node is the induced-filtration and quotient-generation argument of Stacks Tag 058X, theorem-kaplansky-direct-sum, proof lines 20489–20499, using the coordinate construction at 20500–20533. It assumes an arbitrary supplied continuous invariant coordinate filtration with countable differences. The full original arguments were:

Put $P_\alpha=P\cap M_\alpha$. The equality $P_\alpha=e(M_\alpha)$ follows in both directions: stability places $e(x)$ in $M_\alpha$ and in $P$; conversely $p\in P\cap M_\alpha$ satisfies $p=e(p)$. Hence $P_0=0$, $P_\kappa=P$, and $P_\lambda=\bigcup_{\beta<\lambda}P_\beta$ at every nonzero limit, using the union equality for $M_\lambda$. For each $\alpha<\kappa$, the map $r_\alpha:P_{\alpha+1}\to P_\alpha$ given by $p\mapsto e(\pi_{A_\alpha}(p))$ is linear and lands in $e(M_\alpha)=P_\alpha$. It fixes $P_\alpha$, since $\pi_{A_\alpha}(p)=p=e(p)$ there. Thus every successor inclusion of the $P_\alpha$ has an explicit retraction. No commutation of $e$ with the coordinate projection is assumed.

Fix $\alpha<\kappa$ and put $B_\alpha=A_{\alpha+1}\setminus A_\alpha\subseteq E_\alpha$. Choose at most countable generating subsets $G_j\subseteq N_j$ for $j\in B_\alpha$. The union of their canonical images is at most countable and generates $M_{B_\alpha}$, because each coordinate family has finite support [def-weil-pbc-direct-sum-modules]. The map $M_{B_\alpha}\to P_{\alpha+1}/P_\alpha$ sending $z$ to $e(z)+P_\alpha$ is linear with the quotient operations of [def-weil-pbc-quotient-module]. It is surjective: for $p\in P_{\alpha+1}$ decompose its coordinates as $p=y+z$ with $y\in M_\alpha$ and $z\in M_{B_\alpha}$; then $p=e(p)=e(y)+e(z)$ and $e(y)\in P_\alpha$, so its class is the class of $e(z)$. The images of the displayed countable generators therefore generate $P_{\alpha+1}/P_\alpha$. If $B_\alpha$ is empty this quotient is zero and has the empty generating set.

Here P is defined in the node itself as im(e), so no object from the parent's proof is tacitly imported. Idempotence implies e(p)=p on P. Invariance gives P∩M_A=e(M_A); monotonicity and limit continuity then give the endpoints and continuous image filtration. The retraction eπ_A is stated with its exact domain P_(α+1) and codomain P_α. Its restriction is the identity because π_A fixes an element already supported in A and e fixes an element of P. No commuting-projection assumption occurs.

The quotient map is explicitly z↦e(z)+P_α on the new-coordinate submodule M_B. For a representative p=y+z, coordinate restriction gives y∈M_A and z∈M_B, and p=e(p)=e(y)+e(z) has the same quotient class as e(z). The countable union, over j∈B, of chosen countable generating sets remains countable, and finite-support coordinates show that it generates M_B. The explicit surjection carries it to generators of the quotient. This countable-generation argument is entirely in the new node; it is not inferred from a definition-only citation in the parent. No countability assumption on R or the underlying sets of N_j is used.
