## Review by Q100

I re-read the item against 036S and the ordinary-fibre criterion in 01UE/01UG. It makes no Noetherian or separation assumption on the original schemes. This is consistent with the proof: only local algebras on fibres are essentially of finite type over a field. The new algebra statement is the essentially finite type field-algebra specialization of 09Q7, with 09Q6 converting all three local rings to the exact field-relative CI notion used here. Such local algebras are localizations of finite type field algebras, hence Noetherian. The quotient C is again essentially finite type over the same field. Thus the new statement does not require a theorem for non-Noetherian rings; no completeness or separability is smuggled into its hypotheses.

In step 1, I opened lem-sfb-scheme-etale-source-target-properties. Its clauses explicitly include precomposition and descent for flatness and local finite presentation, including arbitrary jointly surjective families and the empty family. Those are exactly the two properties used; it is not cited for CI descent.

In step 2, I checked each affine-chart citation. The lfp affine-opens theorem gives a finite presentation for D over R for compatible affine opens; the affine fibre-product theorem computes D tensor k; the open-immersion base-change theorem makes these charts opens of the fibre. The finite-presentation base-change theorem applies to arbitrary R to k and therefore needs no finite-type assumption on S. The stalk-localization theorem identifies each fibre stalk with a prime localization. Finite type follows directly from the displayed finite generators, and essentially finite type follows from its definition. This is a routine chart computation, not an additional descent theorem.

In step 3, I checked the individual etale base-change and etale covering base-change statements. The second gives joint surjectivity on the ordinary fibre itself, so descent does not rely on lifting a point without a compatible residue field. The fibre-product identification is written by mutually inverse pairs and preserves the maps to the original schemes. Etale implies flat by the cited etale definition, and the flat-morphism definition gives precisely the flat local homomorphism A to B required by the new algebra theorem. Both rings are essentially finite type over k by step 2.

In step 4, I opened the stalk-localization, fibre-ring-local-ring-isomorphism, and etale-fibre statements.
