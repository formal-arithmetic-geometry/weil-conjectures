I reviewed this continuation myself under the direct-delivery brief, with the source passages and cited node statements open. This is not a claimed independent second-reader review. The maintainer supplies that independent reading after delivery.

Step 1 uses the Cohen presentation statement, including its residue-field isomorphism and complete Noetherian regular ambient ring. In residue characteristic p the coefficient Cohen ring is a complete DVR. In residue characteristic zero the coefficient-field case is converted to a trait by C=k[[t]], with t sent to zero; the existing power-series theorem gives completeness and dimension one, and lem-sc-regular-dim1-dvr gives the DVR assertion. A prime invertible in R is nonzero in k, hence a unit in the local C. I checked that this does not require R to have mixed characteristic or be a domain.

Step 2 uses exactly the ambient system theorem and complete-local excellence. The closed immersion comes from Step 1; the formal ambient completion is C[[x_1,...,x_m]]. The complete base is excellent, and the dense open has nowhere-dense complement, which are the uniformization hypotheses. Strict henselianity of R implies that k is separably closed, as the ambient theorem requires.

Step 3 uses the regular alteration hypercover theorem for reduced quasi-excellent X, invertible ell, and finite L. It uses the SNC parameter criterion to choose actual branch equations; it does not take this from the boundary definition. Finitely many levels and Noetherian closed fibres let us mark a finite collection of charts and equations. Degenerate simplices and matching maps remain in the entire diagram.

Step 4 uses the relative diagram theorem for the closed X in the formal ambient, including the affine open, functions, charts, matching maps and covering certificates. Its statement now explicitly supplies ordinary recovery of the finite diagram, as well as the dominant reduced hypercover comparison. Its close-section clause is what will justify a hypercover after b; arbitrary local base change is not assumed to preserve generic finiteness.

Step 5 uses smooth ascent of regularity only for the ambient affine-space charts; it does not assert that the hypercover levels are smooth over X. The regular-local quotient theorem supplies independent generators of the closed immersion's ideal. Their generation equalities are among the finite data descended by the relative theorem. Since corresponding closed-fibre residue fields agree, any linear relation among descended cotangent classes would map to a forbidden relation among the original ones. The hypersurface criterion applied successively gives the regularity of the intermediate levels. At each stage the remaining classes are still independent in the quotient cotangent space.

Step 6 uses the chosen parameter equations and the same residue-field comparison to prove independence for the intermediate boundary functions. The finite diagram retains their support equalities. Nakayama applies to the finitely generated maximal ideal of each Noetherian local ring, so the independent classes extend to a generating parameter list.
