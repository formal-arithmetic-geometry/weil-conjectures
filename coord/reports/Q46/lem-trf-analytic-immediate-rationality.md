## Review by Q46

I read Temkin's original Theorem 6.3.1 proof on printed p. 50, the definition of special cosets before Lemma 6.1.2 on p. 37, Proposition 6.1.4 on p. 38, Proposition 6.2.4 on p. 42 and its polynomial-reduction proof on pp. 47–48, Lemma 6.2.8 and its preceding completed-tensor-product calculation on p. 45, and Lemmas 6.3.2–6.3.3 on p. 48. I also read the ramification discussion and Lemma 2.1.5 on p. 9. The new ramification statement keeps completeness, compatible norms, algebraically closed residue and divisible values; it does not mistakenly use only a prime-power degree to infer a tower. Its tower conclusion is exactly the separate wild-ramification input supplied by the source. Residue characteristic zero is a separate conclusion, not a degree-zero or degree-one convention for p.

For the new model statement, I checked that the auxiliary tame (p-1)st-root extension of Proposition 6.1.4(ii) is trivial under its residue/value hypotheses. This permits normalization to a=1,s=1 without enlarging K. The critical-coset norm identity is explicitly included from Lemma 6.1.2, since otherwise the later distance and small-p bounds would lack a citation. The model states its assertions for every representative of the coset, because Step 2 changes that representative. Its a=1 clause includes both an exact root and the field-generation assertion of Proposition 6.1.4(iii). It does not claim topological rationality. Algebraic closedness of k allows the constant representative to be excluded directly by a root in k, a simpler argument than the source needs for its more general split-ground-field setup.

For the reduction node, I specialized Proposition 6.2.4 with the moderately ramified extension equal to the rational field itself; the statement requires that field already have a topological coordinate and be immediate over k. For the root-obstruction node, I retained precisely the strict bound |pz|<inf|z-k| from Lemma 6.2.8 and the rational-coordinate hypothesis. It is not a claim that all nonconstant elements of an arbitrary extension are nonpowers.

Step 1 uses the opened statement of finite-normed-space-complete to make every algebraic intermediate field complete; its inherited multiplicative absolute value is a norm over K0. The closed rational subfield K0 is complete by the explicit Cauchy-limit argument for closed subsets. The type-alternatives node is used only for its stated algebraic closedness of the residue field and divisibility of the ground value group, with precisely the item's hypotheses. The ramification-tower node then supplies the actual finite tower. The proof's induction is explicit, with y as the base coordinate, and it preserves immediacy by inclusions between equal residue fields and value groups.

Step 2 cites exactly the model and reduction statements for the coset and improved representative. Every hypothesis is available for Ki and Ki+1; in particular each is complete, immediate over k, and the former has the induction coordinate. Constant exclusion uses only a root of a nonconstant polynomial over algebraically closed k and contradicts the cited positive infimum.

Step 3 uses only the model's exact a=1 root and ordinary field-generation conclusion. Its remaining argument is elementary containment: a closed subfield containing alpha also contains v and the closure k(v). Step 4 uses the model's approximate a=0 root, positive infimum and small-p bound. It explicitly compares that infimum to distance from k using pth roots of every constant. The distance equality under a perturbation smaller than the infimum follows for each constant separately from the strong triangle inequality. The elementary marking records these written inequalities, not a hidden analytic lemma.

Step 5 uses the opened close-embedding-degree statement, which requires isometric embeddings, a uniform multiplicative error constant strictly below one, and finite degree. It receives all three: the factor/inverse calculation proves the same error bound for every rational function; extension on Cauchy sequences proves an isometric inverse pair on the closures; delta/r<c0<1 gives the uniform bound. This avoids taking only pointwise small errors as sufficient. Its conclusion is the finite degree p over K'. Step 6 checks that -v' is itself a coordinate of K', that K'/k is immediate by inclusions, and that the strict small-p bound survives; therefore the root-obstruction statement really applies. The Mathlib leaf then supplies irreducibility in either characteristic. Equal finite degrees give K'(alpha)=L, and the two field containments explicitly give L=k(alpha)^wedge.

I tested the statements against L=K (the tower has n=0), residue characteristic zero, equal and mixed characteristic with p>0, p=2, and the possibility that the approximate root were constant. The latter would force v' into k, already excluded by the strict distance comparison. A special coset cannot have s=0 or contain zero; these are retained explicitly, so it is not a vacuous or split-coset reduction. A constant coordinate is excluded from the root obstruction, and replacing z by -z preserves its distance and closure. The binomial statement correctly excludes t=0 through the no-root hypothesis and assumes p prime, not an arbitrary positive degree. No statement dispute remains.
