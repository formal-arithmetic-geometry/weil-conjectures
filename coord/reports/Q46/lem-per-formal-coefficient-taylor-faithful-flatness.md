For clarity, the finite-jet calculation is justified as follows. An element of (Z)^k has no coefficient below total degree k. Conversely, partition all monomials of degree at least k by a chosen divisor of degree k. There are finitely many such divisors, and each part is one such divisor times a formal power series. This proves the kernel description for the truncation map over either coefficient ring. The remaining monomials form a finite basis. Thus extending their coefficients gives the actual algebra isomorphism (R/I^k) tensor_A B to T/J^k, with multiplication checked by finite convolution. For any module N over R/I^k, the tensor reassociation is given by x tensor (c tensor b) mapping to cx tensor b; its inverse maps x tensor b to x tensor (1 tensor b). Flatness of B over A therefore proves quotient flatness directly from the definition, without an uncited base-change theorem.

To see that all maximal ideals of T contain J, for h in J and t in T the series sum_r (th)^r exists coefficientwise: only finitely many terms affect each coefficient. It inverts 1-th. A maximal ideal omitting h would provide an inverse to h modulo that ideal, contradicting that 1-th is a unit. Hence 0523 applies at every maximal ideal of T, and the existing maximal-localization detection node then proves global R-flatness of T. This argument does not assume B local. In the final local clause, the already-existing multivariate local-ring node identifies the maximal ideal through the constant coefficient; splitting off the constant term identifies it with (m_B,Z)T.
