### Retained old step 1

The kernels of $q_0$ and $q$ are finitely generated ideals in the respective maximal ideals, since regular local rings are Noetherian. Complete $P$ and $Q$ at their maximal ideals. ### Retained old step 2

Use the Cohen structure power-series surjection to fix $c_0:T_0=\Lambda[[x_1,\ldots,x_n]]\twoheadrightarrow A$. Here $\Lambda$ is a field of characteristic zero when the residue field of $A$ has characteristic zero, and a Cohen ring otherwise. The power-series regular-completeness lemma says that $T_0$ is complete regular local; its unique maximal ideal is $\mathfrak n_0=\mathfrak m_\Lambda T_0+(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$. A surjection from a local ring to a nonzero local ring carries the source maximal ideal onto the target maximal ideal (its inverse image is the unique maximal ideal), so $c_0$ is local. By the power-series formal-smoothness lemma, $T_0$ is formally smooth for the $\mathfrak n_0$-adic topology over $B=\mathbf Q$ in the characteristic-zero case, and over $B=\mathbf Z$ otherwise.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-cohen-structure-power-series-surjection, lem-weil-pbc-power-series-field-cohen-regular-complete, lem-weil-pbc-power-series-field-cohen-fs-adic.

### Retained old step 3

Let $r:R\twoheadrightarrow A$ be either completed surjection from step 1, and put $J=\ker r$. Apply the continuous lifting lemma with its base ring $B$, source $T_0$, target ring $R$, ideal of definition $I=\mathfrak m_R$, quotient ideal $J$, and exponents $t=1$ and $r_{\mathrm{exp}}=1$. The ring $R$ is $I$-adically complete and $J\subseteq I$. The ideal $J$ is closed: if $z\in\bigcap_{N\ge1}(J+\mathfrak m_R^N)$, then $r(z)\in\bigcap_N\mathfrak m_A^N=0$ by completeness of $A$, so $z\in J$. Identify $R/J$ with $A$ via $r$. Since $c_0$ is local, $c_0(\mathfrak n_0)\subseteq\mathfrak m_A=r(I)$, as required. In the $B=\mathbf Q$ case $R$ is a $\mathbf Q$-algebra because every nonzero integer has nonzero residue (the residue fields of $R$ and $A$ agree) and hence is a unit; the quotient and $c_0$ respect this structure. The lifting lemma gives $f:T_0\to R$ with $r\circ f=c_0$ and $f(\mathfrak n_0)\subseteq\mathfrak m_R$.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-fs-adic-lift-into-complete, def-weil-pbc-adically-complete-ring.

### Retained old step 4

Choose generators $b_1,\ldots,b_s$ of $J$, possible by Noetherianity, with $b_j\in\mathfrak m_R$. Put $T=\Lambda[[x_1,\ldots,x_n,y_1,\ldots,y_s]]=T_0[[y_1,\ldots,y_s]]$, where the equality is coefficient regrouping. The power-series regular-completeness lemma makes $T$ complete regular local. Apply power-series adic evaluation with coefficient ring $T_0$, coefficient map $f$, target ideal $\mathfrak m_R$, and $y_j\mapsto b_j$ to obtain $h:T\to R$. Let $\pi:T\twoheadrightarrow T_0$ set every $y_j$ to zero. Then $r\circ h=c_0\circ\pi$: for any series, terms of positive $y$-degree evaluate into $J$ modulo every $\mathfrak m_A^N$ and vanish in $A$, which is separated; the constant coefficient maps by $r\circ f=c_0$. Moreover $h$ carries the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_\Lambda T+(x_1,\ldots,x_n,y_1,\ldots,y_s)$ into $\mathfrak m_R$, so $h$ is local.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-power-series-field-cohen-regular-complete, lem-weil-pbc-power-series-adic-evaluation, def-weil-regular-scheme.

### Retained old step 5

We prove the omitted surjectivity by a direct recursive expansion. Fix $z\in R$. Starting with $S_0=0$, construct polynomials $S_N\in T_0[y_1,\ldots,y_s]$ of degree less than $N$ satisfying $z-h(S_N)\in J^N$. Write this residual as $\sum_{|\nu|=N}d_\nu b^\nu$ with $d_\nu\in R$, using the monomial generators of $J^N$. For each coefficient choose $a_\nu\in T_0$ with $c_0(a_\nu)=r(d_\nu)$, possible since $c_0$ is surjective. Then $d_\nu-f(a_\nu)\in J$. Hence $S_{N+1}=S_N+\sum_{|\nu|=N}a_\nu y^\nu$ satisfies $z-h(S_{N+1})\in J^{N+1}$. This proves the induction, including $N=0$.

### Retained old step 6

The corrections in step 5 have strictly increasing $y$-degree, so their coefficients define a series $S\in T_0[[y_1,\ldots,y_s]]=T$. The evaluation formula says $h(S)-h(S_N)\in\mathfrak m_R^N$, while $z-h(S_N)\in J^N\subseteq\mathfrak m_R^N$. Therefore $z-h(S)\in\bigcap_N\mathfrak m_R^N=0$, proving that $h$ is surjective. If $s=0$, then $J=0$, the first constant coefficient already maps to $z$, and no positive-degree corrections are needed.

### Retained old step 7

Apply regular-local-surjection-kernel-comparison to $T\xrightarrow{h}R\xrightarrow{r}A$: these maps are surjective local and $T,R$ are regular local.
