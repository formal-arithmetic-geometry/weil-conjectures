## Review by Q46

I checked the item against both propositions on Milne p.427, not just their conclusions: 21.7 first uses C_H(T)=T and then the quotient ker(Ad)/Z(H); 21.8 uses the genuine T-character weight decomposition. The field here is algebraically closed, so the smooth connected reductive hypothesis and geometric maximality needed by the existing self-centralizer statement apply. I kept the character weights, never their differentials; replacing them by infinitesimal weights would lose the nonreduced centre in small characteristic.

For step 1 I opened lem-sfb-affine-group-centre-base-change and lem-sfb-reductive-torus-self-centralizer. The former's statement supplies precisely the closed subgroup functor whose points commute after every algebra extension; the latter supplies equality with T on that same functor of points. Central conjugation is the identity on every base algebra, so differentiating it is an elementary way to prove the inclusion in ker(Ad), with no assumption of smoothness of the centre.

For step 2 I opened lem-sfb-reductive-rank-one-reflections: its statement gives the direct sum Lie(H)=Lie(T) plus all root spaces and explicitly gives each root space dimension one. The torus acts by the stated characters on the summands; comparing a basis over A proves alpha(t)=1 without reducedness of A. I also read def-weil-closed-immersion: its affine quotient-ring clause gives the closed equations alpha-1. This step does not cite a definition for a hidden theorem about flatness or tensor products.

For step 3 I opened lem-sfb-split-torus-subgroups-diagonalizable and both new statements. The subgroup node gives K=D_F(M) with M finitely generated, exactly the source of the new vanishing input. The adjoint-kernel input gives q with its scheme-theoretic kernel Z(H), exactly the needed converse inclusion, and gives a closed unitriangular target, exactly the vanishing input's target hypothesis. H meets smoothness, connectedness, affineness and finite type; K and U need not be smooth, connected or reduced. The proof does not replace the kernel by its set of F-points or assert surjectivity of q on A-points.

For step 4 I opened lem-sfb-multiplicative-character-antiduality and def-weil-affine-spectrum. Antiduality supplies the natural evaluation T=D_F(X); affine spectrum identifies algebra maps out of F[X] with points. I wrote the maps e_x to h(x) and back, the integral-span argument (negative coefficients use inverses), the factorization through X/L, and the inverse scheme maps obtained from the universal coordinate-algebra points. Their composites are identities on the character generators. The Hopf compatibility is checked on e_x. Thus a general Yoneda theorem or an unquoted exactness assertion is not being used as a black box.

For step 5 I reread the natural character identification in the same antiduality statement. The inclusion sends e_x to e_[x], so the pullback character is [x]. That checks the specific map, not merely the existence of an abstract isomorphism. The only elementary step is this final substitution and checking the allowed empty-root case. No step is longer than 2000 characters, and no long step conceals a further comparison theorem.

I checked the new adjoint-kernel statement against Milne 14.23 and 14.5: q is the quotient by the closed centre, followed by the unitriangular embedding. It is stated for smooth connected G, which is sufficient here and is within 14.23; no reductivity is imported into this input and no nonsmooth kernel is declared smooth. For the second new node I read 14.18(b), its image argument, 14.16 and 12.9(c). It applies even when M has characteristic torsion: diagonalizable does not mean reduced. I tested M=0 and U=1, and M=Z/pZ (mu_p) against alpha_p and Ga; the assertion is zero as required, even though mu_p has only the identity geometric point. For the first new input, a torus and Ga have trivial adjoint action and equal their own centres, hence the target may be the trivial unitriangular group. For the item, an empty root system gives L=0 and centre T; GL_n gives X/L=Z and scalar G_m, while SL_2 in characteristic 2 gives X/L=Z/2Z and the nonreduced group mu_2. These checks expose precisely why a field-point-only proof or Lie-algebra-centre calculation would fail.
