1. Projectivity and geometric integrality are exactly the conclusions of the two existing abelian-variety nodes. The new projective Picard node applies at the identity point. It gives a proper connected group P without assuming smoothness, so this step does not assume the conclusion.
2. Only the representing property of the new Picard node is used. All translations are in the connected smooth variety A, and the zero parameter gives the trivial bundle; thus algebraic equivalence has a stated witness. The normalization cancels on both axes, including for L trivial.
3. The existing cube statement is the seven-factor isomorphism on A cubed, exactly the family identity used. Its restriction and normalization give a homomorphism on schemes, not only on rational points.
4. The same cube, with the third variable fixed at b, gives equality of the two rigidified families. The Picard node gives uniqueness of the induced morphism.
5. Pullback of each generating family for algebraic equivalence preserves its smooth connected parameter scheme. The identity a t_y=t_{a(y)} a is an identity of scheme maps. Tensor product and the representability clause give both a-dual and the desired formula with the correct direction.
6. The cited rigidity theorem allows a connected, possibly nonreduced second factor. Its target must be separated and locally finite type, supplied by the Picard input; its first factor must be proper, geometrically connected and geometrically reduced, supplied by abelian characterization. It proves universal translation invariance of algebraically trivial families before inversion is used.
7. The reduced-component node supplies an abelian B of the same dimension as the full kernel, including nonreduced kernels. The restriction of a very ample embedding proves restriction ampleness directly. The ample tensor node supplies tensor ampleness; the H0-proper-variety statement supplies constants because B is geometrically integral. This replaced an invalid draft attempt to use rigidity for arbitrary functions. The contradiction is from the actual definition of ampleness, not an unproved positivity assertion.
8. The fibre/image dimension node covers arbitrary finite-type schemes over a field and requires only the established fibre dimension bound. The new Picard node supplies tangent H1; the new H1 node gives its dimension. The existing group-identity-local-dimension and regular-local-cotangent-dimension statements apply to the Noetherian local ring at the identity, so neither smoothness nor reducedness of P is presupposed.
9. The regular closed-point smoothness node has exactly the algebraically closed field and finite-type algebra hypotheses present. Closed-point residue fields are k by its cited node. Translations move the identity to each such point; the Jacobson density statement rules out a nonempty closed nonsmooth complement. Abelian characterization supplies irreducibility after, not before, smoothness is proved.
10. The image-dimension node is used on the closed reduced image with zero-dimensional fibres. Extending chains of irreducible closed subsets proves the strict dimension drop directly; no false assertion about nonreduced full-dimensional subgroup equality is used. Isogeny is the cited definition: surjective with finite kernel.
11. The stabilizer node supplies both the closed subgroup and its explicit Cartier-divisor equality, not merely a definition of a set stabilizer. The reduced-component node applies to S. Restriction along 0 times C removes the constant line and shows phi_L vanishes on C as a scheme morphism, making it factor through the finite scheme-theoretic kernel.
12. The cube yields the triple-translate identity. Proper coherent finiteness applies to the invertible sheaf L cubed, which is quasi-coherent and finitely presented. The projective quotient node classifies the surjective evaluation map and gives the exact pullback of O(1). Its general quasi-coherent hypothesis includes the finite-dimensional constant module here. The rational-point density node is applied only to nonempty opens in the smooth abelian variety A.
13. Failure to be in the support stabilizer gives a translate containing x and avoiding y. Two more freely chosen translates avoid y and retain the sum-of-translations-zero condition; the section separates their projective images. The positive-dimensional-closed-points lemma applies to fibres over closed target points, whose residue fields are k by the cited node. Proper fibre-dimension upper semicontinuity and Jacobson density extend this to all target points. This avoids an unstated field-extension base-change comparison.
14. Proper plus locally quasi-finite is exactly the hypothesis of the cited finiteness theorem. Finite means affine by the cited definition. The ample-pullback node therefore applies; the absolute-ample-to-projective-immersion statement is used to return to the classical proper-scheme definition of ample, not silently conflate two notions.
