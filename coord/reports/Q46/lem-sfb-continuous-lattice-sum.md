### Old step 1

Apply [lem-sfb-finite-sum-lattice] with the given finite nonempty $J$ and its full lattices. Choose an $A$-basis $b_1,\ldots,b_r$ of $\Lambda$ which is also an $E$-basis of $E^r$. Its coordinate map $\beta:A^r\to\Lambda$, $(a_i)\mapsto\sum_i a_i b_i$, is a homeomorphism. The same lemma gives such coordinate homeomorphisms for each $\Lambda_j$. The hypotheses are exactly those of that lemma, including the product/subspace topologies and the permitted case $r=0$.

### Old step 2

For each $i$, membership $b_i\in\sum_j\Lambda_j$ gives vectors $v_{ji}\in\Lambda_j$ with $b_i=\sum_jv_{ji}$: group the finitely many summands by $j$, inserting zeros. Define $s_j(\sum_i a_i b_i)=\sum_i a_i v_{ji}$ and $s=(s_j)_j$. These are $A$-linear, and $q(s(\sum_i a_i b_i))=\sum_i a_i\sum_jv_{ji}=\sum_i a_i b_i$, so $q s=1$. Each map $A^r\to E^r$, $(a_i)\mapsto\sum_i a_i v_{ji}$, is continuous: each ambient coordinate is a finite sum of products of a coordinate $a_i\in A\subset E$ with a fixed scalar in $E$. Its image lies in $\Lambda_j$, so it is continuous into its subspace topology. Compose with $\beta^{-1}$ to obtain continuity of $s_j$; finitely many components give continuity of $s$ for the finite product topology. Here the topological field and subspace ring are those of [def-weil-l-adic-coefficient-extension], and the continuity of $\beta^{-1}$ is supplied by [lem-sfb-finite-sum-lattice]. The sum map $q$ is itself continuous by the identical finite-coordinate addition calculation.

Cited nodes: lem-sfb-finite-sum-lattice, def-weil-l-adic-coefficient-extension

### Old step 3

For any topological space $Z$ and continuous $f:Z\to\Lambda$, put $f_j=s_j\circ f$. Each $f_j$ is continuous into $\Lambda_j$, and $\sum_j f_j=(q\circ s)\circ f=f$. As $J$ is finite the tuple $(f_j)_j$ is an element of the displayed direct sum. Conversely any finite sum of such functions is continuous and takes values in $\Lambda$. Thus the continuous-function sum map is surjective for every $Z$, with no compactness or connectedness assumption.

### Old step 4

For an object $U$ of $S_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}$, postcomposition with $\beta$ gives an $A_S(U)$-linear bijection $A_S(U)^r=C(|U|,A)^r\xrightarrow{\sim}C(|U|,\Lambda)$; its inverse is postcomposition with $\beta^{-1}$ followed by the $r$ coordinate projections. Continuous functions into a finite product are exactly tuples of continuous functions. These maps commute with every restriction because they are postcomposition by fixed maps. Here $A_S$ is the coefficient ring sheaf of [def-weil-pbc-continuous-integral-coefficients]; that $U\mapsto C(|U|,A)$, and likewise $U\mapsto C(|U|,E)$, $U\mapsto C(|U|,\Lambda)$ and $U\mapsto C(|U|,\Lambda_j)$ for the topological abelian groups $E$, $\Lambda$, $\Lambda_j$ (addition is continuous as a restriction of that of $E^r$), satisfy the sheaf condition is [lem-cbc-continuous-functions-fpqc-sheaf]: covering families of $S_{\mathrm{pro\acute et}}$ are fpqc coverings and its fibre products are those of schemes. Thus this identifies the presheaf $F_\Lambda(U)=C(|U|,\Lambda)$ with the sheaf $A_S^r$: the sheaf gluing condition for a finite tuple is checked in each coordinate. In particular $F_\Lambda$ is a free rank-$r$ module sheaf. Apply the coordinate homeomorphisms of [lem-sfb-finite-sum-lattice] in exactly the same way to each $\Lambda_j$ to see that each $F_j(U)=C(|U|,\Lambda_j)$ is a module sheaf.

### Old step 6

Finally let $B\in\operatorname{GL}_r(E)$ have columns $b_i$, using the $E$-basis furnished by [lem-sfb-finite-sum-lattice].
