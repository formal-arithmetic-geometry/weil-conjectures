### Original step 1

Put $\Delta A=g^{-1}A$ on $\mathcal T$. Factor $p=g\circ r$, where $r:(\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal T),B)\to(\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal T),\Delta A)$ is the identity on underlying topoi with ring map $\Delta A\to B$, and $g:(\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal T),\Delta A)\to(\operatorname{Sh}(\mathcal C),A)$ is projection. The sequence-site equivalence identifies $r_*$ with termwise scalar restriction $U_0$. Kernels and cokernels are unchanged on underlying abelian sheaves and computed componentwise, so $U_0$ is exact and $Rr_*=U$. Also $g_*=\lim$ on $A$-module towers, hence $Rg_*=R\lim$ in the specified total-limit model. All sites are small: the sequence site is a product of the given small category with the countable index category.

### Original step 2

Apply the ringed-topos composition theorem to $r,g$ to define the specified natural isomorphism $c:Rp_*\xrightarrow{\sim}Rg_*Rr_*=\mathcal L$. More explicitly choose a K-injective resolution $N\to I$ over $B$ on $\mathcal T$, then a K-injective resolution $j:UI\to J$ over $\Delta A$. These resolutions exist on the two small ringed sites. Now $Rp_*N$ is represented by $\lim UI$, and $\mathcal LN$ by $\lim J$. The composition theorem says precisely that $\lim j:\lim UI\to\lim J$ is a quasi-isomorphism whose class is $c_N$, and that this prescription is natural in derived $N$. Thus this comparison is fixed by actual resolution maps.

### Original step 3

Write $a_n:N_n\to I_n$ for the evaluated resolution. It is a quasi-isomorphism because exactness in strict systems is componentwise. Likewise $j_n:UI_n\to J_n$ is a quasi-isomorphism. In the model $\mathcal LN=\lim J$ the actual projection is $\pi_n=[Ua_n]^{-1}[j_n]^{-1}[\operatorname{pr}_n^J]$. The chain equality $\operatorname{pr}_n^J\circ\lim j=j_n\circ\operatorname{pr}_n^{UI}$ gives
$$\pi_n c_N=[Ua_n]^{-1}[\operatorname{pr}_n^{UI}].$$
The right side is exactly the projection of $p_*I$ to $UI_n$, followed by identification with $\operatorname{Res}_nN_n$. This proves preservation of the actual total-limit projections by the specified comparison; it does not choose maps between unspecified cone objects.

### Original step 4

For later transposition at level $n$, let $T_n=-\otimes_A^LB_n$. The ordinary module adjunction for the identity topos with ring map $A\to B_n$ is extension/restriction, sending $f:Q\otimes_A B_n\to K$ to $x\mapsto f(x\otimes1)$. Restriction is exact since its underlying abelian sheaf is unchanged. K-flat resolutions exist, and change of rings preserves quasi-isomorphisms between K-flat complexes. Apply the resolving-class derived adjunction to this ordinary adjunction and the class of K-flat complexes. It gives $T_n\dashv\operatorname{Res}_n$ and its representative transpose formula: the derived adjunct of a chain map $f:Q\otimes_A B_n\to K$, after the canonical K-flat comparison, is the class of $x\mapsto f(x\otimes1)$. This formula applies to arbitrary target complexes $K$, without K-injectivity of $K$.

### Original step 5

Let $\eta,\varepsilon$ be the unit and counit of $\mathcal R=Lp^*\dashv Rp_*$ supplied by the sequence-projection adjunction. If $\Phi(f):P\to Rp_*N$ denotes its Hom bijection, define $\Psi(f)=c_N\Phi(f)$. Composition with an isomorphism is a natural bijection, so it defines an adjunction $\mathcal R\dashv\mathcal L$ in these derived categories. Its unit and counit are explicitly
$$u_P=c_{\mathcal RP}\eta_P,\qquad e_N=\varepsilon_N\mathcal R(c_N^{-1}).$$
For example naturality of $c$ gives $\mathcal L(e_N)c_{\mathcal R\mathcal LN}=c_N Rp_*(e_N)$; naturality of $\eta$ and the old right triangle identity then give $\mathcal L(e_N)u_{\mathcal LN}=1$. The other identity is $e_{\mathcal RP}\mathcal R(u_P)=\varepsilon_{\mathcal RP}\mathcal R(\eta_P)=1$, after cancelling $\mathcal R(c_{\mathcal RP}^{-1})\mathcal R(c_{\mathcal RP})$. Thus the identities hold as actual derived morphisms.

### Original step 6

Choose a flat-term K-flat representative $Q\to P$, so $\mathcal RP$ is represented by the strict complex $(Q\otimes_A B_n)_n$. Choose its system resolution $a:\mathcal RP\to I$. The fraction-extension formula in the sequence-projection adjunction computes $\eta_P$ by the ordinary transpose of $a$: the chain map $Q\to\lim UI$ sends $x$ to $(a_n(x\otimes1))_n$. By step 3, the projection of $u_P=c_{\mathcal RP}\eta_P$ to level $n$ is the class of $x\mapsto a_n(x\otimes1)$ followed by $[Ua_n]^{-1}$. It is therefore the class of $Q\to\operatorname{Res}_n(Q\otimes_A B_n)$, $x\mapsto x\otimes1$. By step 4 this is exactly the scalar-extension unit, i.e. the required reduction unit.

### Original step 7

Represent $N$ by its K-injective system $I$. Choose a flat-term K-flat resolution $q:Q\to p_*I=\lim UI$. The sequence-projection Hom formula computes $\varepsilon_N$ by the system chain map with components
$$Q\otimes_A B_n\longrightarrow I_n,\qquad x\otimes b\longmapsto b\operatorname{pr}_n^{UI}(q(x)).$$
Indeed its ordinary transpose is $q$, the representative of the identity of $Rp_*N$. By step 4 its $n$th component has scalar-extension adjunct $[\operatorname{pr}_n^{UI}]$, after the resolution identifications. Step 3 identifies this with $\pi_nc_N$. Precomposing $\varepsilon_N$ with $\mathcal R(c_N^{-1})$ therefore makes the adjunct of its $n$th component $\pi_nc_Nc_N^{-1}=\pi_n$. This is the required description of $e_{N,n}$. It holds without assuming that $UI$ or $I_n$ is K-injective.

### Original step 8

For $h:P\to\mathcal LN$, the inverse of the transported Hom bijection is $e_N\circ\mathcal R(h)$: its transpose is $\mathcal L(e_N)\mathcal L\mathcal R(h)u_P=\mathcal L(e_N)u_{\mathcal LN}h=h$ by naturality of $u$ and the right triangle identity. The sequence-projection functor is derived scalar extension at level $n$, naturally on derived morphisms. Consequently this adjunct has component $e_{N,n}\circ(h\otimes_A^LB_n)$. The construction used one natural Hom bijection and the specified comparison throughout, so its unit, counit, and formulas are natural in $P,N$.
