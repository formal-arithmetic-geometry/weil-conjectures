Step 1 cites the pointed-map generated-subgroup construction for the actual minimal closed subgroup and its geometric reducedness and connectedness. I checked that the source space is affine finite type, pointed, geometrically reduced and geometrically connected, and that its image is inversion-stable on every test algebra. Step 2 uses the perfect pairing explicitly stated in the reductive Borel/root-datum structure node, applied to a given J_alpha; this legitimizes clearing rational denominators to choose the cocharacters. The new cell node says exactly the contraction identities, open immersion and intersection needed later; its nonempty and mixed-commutation hypotheses are verified here.

Step 3 uses the new retraction node for every kernel, fixed subgroup and limit action. The orbit node receives every listed input, including the normalizer action, character scaling, kernel invariance and nonnegative limit actions. Its conclusion is kept distinct from the actual roots until step 6.

Step 4 uses the reduced-subgroup detector, the orbit node's sign rule and the cell node's open immersion. If a smooth connected solvable group properly contains TV, its intersection with the cell forces a nonidentity U-point. Reduction of that intersection therefore meets the detector's exact hypothesis. Opposite conjugate root groups generate the conjugated rank-one J by the existing simple-root-generation statement. The perfect-solvable-unipotent-torus theorem rules out its being solvable, since reductivity kills its unipotent part and a torus has no roots. Thus TV is maximal solvable; the longest conjugation gives TU. This is the Borel argument, not a lemma merely stating reductivity.

Step 5 uses the smooth-unipotent-solvable node, the general Borel flag variety (which does not assume reductivity), and the solvable fixed-point theorem on that nonempty proper scheme. I checked scheme-theoretic stabilizers in the flag statement: an N-fixed point gives containment of the whole subgroup scheme, not only its rational points. Normality transports fixedness to every G-translate. The intersection from the new cell node is exactly T, so a smooth normal unipotent subgroup is trivial. This establishes reductivity with the item's full definition.

Step 6 uses the root-subgroup parametrizations for additive smoothness and exact character scaling, and the limit-group tangent-space statement to exclude zero pairing with lambda. The detector then identifies every actual root group with an orbit subgroup. Its use on the negative side is through the specified longest conjugation, so no unproved negative-side detector is assumed. The cell node supplies U=U_G(lambda) and V=U_G(-lambda), not merely containments.

Step 7 uses the actual statement of the rank-one-reflections node: the centralizer G_gamma is rank one, with the unique coroot determined by its reflection. Uniqueness of the canonical root subgroups identifies the embedded J root groups with those of G. Simple-root generation then makes J and G_gamma the same closed subgroup, which proves compatibility of coroots as cocharacters in every characteristic. No comparison of Lie-algebra differentials of coroots is used.
