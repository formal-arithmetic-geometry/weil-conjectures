### Original Step 1

Work with $r\ge1$; if the inverse limit includes $r=0$, its extra term is $\Omega_{0/F}=0$ and adds no condition, by [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials]. Put $P=F[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$ and $J=(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\subset P$, with $P\subset D$ the finite-support series [def-weil-pbc-mv-polynomial-ring, def-weil-pbc-multivariate-power-series]. Specialize [lem-weil-pbc-power-series-combined-ideal-coefficients] to coefficient ring $F$ and zero coefficient ideal: a series is in $\mathfrak n^r$ exactly when all its coefficients of total degree $<r$ vanish. Every series therefore has the same class modulo $\mathfrak n^r$ as its finite polynomial truncation in degrees $<r$. For a polynomial, vanishing in these degrees is equivalent to membership in $J^r$: the latter ideal is generated by the monomials of degree $r$, and each higher-degree monomial is divisible by one of these. Thus $P\to Q_r=D/\mathfrak n^r$ is surjective with kernel $J^r$, identifying $P/J^r$ with $Q_r$ by the coordinate classes.

### Original Step 2

Let $M=D^{\oplus n}$, and define the $D$-linear map $\theta_r:M\to\Omega_{Q_r/F}$ by $a\mapsto\sum_i(a_i\bmod\mathfrak n^r)\,\mathrm d(t_i\bmod\mathfrak n^r)$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-jacobi-zariski-differential-maps] to $F\to P\to P/J^r=Q_r$, using the surjectivity and kernel proved in Step 1. By [lem-weil-pbc-polynomial-ring-kahler-basis], $Q_r\otimes_P\Omega_{P/F}$ is $Q_r^{\oplus n}$: explicitly $c\otimes\sum_i p_i\mathrm dt_i\mapsto(c\bar p_i)_i$, with inverse $(c_i)_i\mapsto\sum_i c_i\otimes\mathrm dt_i$. The conormal map sends the class of $g\in J^r$ to $(\partial_i g\bmod J^r)_i$, by that basis node's derivative formula. Consequently $\theta_r$ is surjective and its kernel $K_r$ is $\mathfrak n^rM$ plus the $D$-span of the vectors $(\partial_i g)_i$ for polynomial $g\in J^r$. Indeed, exactness describes the kernel in $Q_r^{\oplus n}$; lift its finitely many scalar coefficients to $D$, and the remaining difference lies in $\mathfrak n^rM$.

### Original Step 3
