## STATEMENT-DISPUTED: unrestricted coefficient base change

The original sentence said that ideal-generation and flatness assertions were preserved by coefficient base change, without specifying the coefficient rings. The field-extension and diagonal-completion instances are valid. If the phrase is read as allowing arbitrary coefficient rings, it is false already for the zero ideal in one transverse variable.

Take the original datum over a characteristic-zero field k with N=Spec(k), U=A^1_k, a=the origin, J=0 and an empty generator family. Its quotient k[[Z]] is flat over k. Put S=k direct-sum V, with V a k-vector space with basis e_0,e_1,..., multiplication (c,v)(d,w)=(cd,cw+dv); thus V^2=0. Let x=e_0. Then Ann_S(x)=V. In M=S[[Z]], the series y=sum_{i>=0} e_i Z^i is killed by x, but is not in V M. Indeed every element of V M is a finite sum v_j h_j(Z), and its coefficients lie in the finite-dimensional span of the v_j, since V kills every V-valued coefficient. The coefficients e_i of y do not lie in such a span. Flatness of M would preserve exactness at the middle term of V -> S --x--> S, forcing ker(x:M->M)=V M. This contradiction proves that the pulled-back, completed zero quotient is not flat over S.

I have therefore made the coefficient-base-change clause explicit for Noetherian coefficient rings, including residue-field extensions and the subsequent diagonal completions. This is a correction of a false unrestricted claim, authorized by the worker brief's statement-dispute rule. It preserves all geometric coefficient rings used by the parent: local finite-type characteristic-zero coefficient charts and field power-series diagonal rings. Equality of finite ideal identities itself transports through any coefficient map; it is flatness after completion over a completely arbitrary ring that fails. The main hypotheses and all neighbourhood, ideal-generation, pointwise flatness, and empty-family conclusions are retained.

## Proof of the new coefficient-extension lemma (for the next worker)

Source read: `literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex`, Tag 0523, `lemma-flat-module-powers`, its full hypotheses and proof, lines 23808 onward. Also read Tag 0306, `lemma-Noetherian-power-series`, and the flat-local faithful-flat statement used by the existing node `lem-pc-local-flat-faithful-flat-reflects-surjective`. These are background facts, not claims that 0523 literally states the helper.

For a flat map rho:A->B of Noetherian rings, put R=A[[Z]], T=B[[Z]], I=(Z)R. Both rings are Noetherian by the existing power-series Noetherian lemma. For every r>=1 there is a canonical isomorphism

T/I^r T = B tensor_A (R/I^r).

Both sides are the finite direct sum of B indexed by monomials of degree <r, and the isomorphism sends each coefficient and each such monomial to itself. Flat base change of rho shows this is flat over R/I^r. Apply 0523 with its coefficient ring R, algebra T, module T (finite over itself), and ideal I. Every maximal ideal of T contains (Z): for every t in T and each Z_j, the series 1-Z_j t has a geometric-series inverse. Hence 0523 makes T localized at every maximal ideal flat over R. To deduce flatness of T, tensor an arbitrary injection of R-modules with T. Its kernel is a T-module; localization at every maximal ideal is zero by the preceding flatness and the localization-tensor identity, so the kernel is zero. A nonzero module element has a proper annihilator contained in a maximal ideal and remains nonzero there. This proves the global flatness assertion. If B is local, T is local with maximal ideal (m_B,Z), by the recursive inverse for a series with unit constant term. Localizing R at the inverse image of this maximal ideal gives a flat local homomorphism, hence a faithfully flat one. Empty Z is the given flat map and its flat local localization. The zero-ring case in the first assertion is vacuous; the local assertion explicitly assumes B nonzero.

This is a genuine extension lemma below the item. Its proof does not assume coherent ideal generation, existence of a geometric retraction, or any conclusion of the item. It is not a mere one-line specialization of 0523: it needs the truncated coefficient pushout, the Jacobson-radical observation, and the localization detection argument.
