For step 1 I opened `lem-sbc-section-component-cartesian`: its statement gives the actual equality of inverse-image component loci, not merely a map between components. Its local-finite-type hypothesis holds for smooth N/S. I opened `lem-sfb-locally-algebraic-group-identity-component`: it assumes an algebraically closed field and local finite type, both satisfied by each geometric fibre, and it states subgroup structure, geometric irreducibility and finite type. The smooth affine comparison and smooth-ring base-change statements justify the use after residue-field extension. The surjective-base-change statement applies to Spec of a field extension, which is surjective since both spectra have a single point; it then gives a surjective fibre projection. Combined with the component equality it makes the image exactly C_s. The continuous-image quasi-compactness statement transfers quasi-compactness. This is not an assertion that N itself is quasi-compact.

For step 2 I checked the full quasi-compact and quasi-separated hypotheses of `lem-sbc-smooth-section-component-open`. The entire image e(S), as well as C_s, is covered by finitely many affine opens; neither is silently assumed to be a single affine. The resulting finite union W has the restricted section and contains all of C_s, so its component in that fibre equals C_s. Quasi-compactness over affine S is checked on principal base opens, and separatedness of N/S makes chart intersections affine by the closed diagonal inside their affine tensor-product spectrum. This uses exactly the affine-spectrum fibre-product and closed-immersion statements cited. An arbitrary affine chart would not carry the section, so that initial draft approach was corrected. The local lifting definition of smoothness restricts to an open: a lift from a first-order thickening lands in the same open since the thickening has unchanged points. The output W^0 is open in N and contained in C, proving openness by neighbourhoods.

For step 3 the field theorem states that each geometric component is a subgroup, so no assertion about product connectedness is borrowed from a definition. I read the factorization-through-open statement: its precise hypothesis is set-theoretic containment of the image, established fibre by fibre. It gives unique factorizations for all three operations. Equality of the group diagrams follows from the same uniqueness and the diagrams on N; there is no circular invocation of an already constructed subgroup.

For step 4 I opened `sbc-lem-smooth-criterion`, which actually gives flatness and local finite presentation, and `lem-weil-pbc-flat-lfp-open`, which actually gives an open map. It follows that the image of an affine neighbourhood A of e(s) contains an affine principal base neighbourhood B. The fibre-product affine-spectrum theorem makes U=A×_SB affine. Surjective base change guarantees nonemptiness after all algebraically closed extensions, and the irreducibility already obtained makes these opens dense. No finite-type or quasi-compactness hypothesis for all of N is introduced, and there is no unproved spreading-of-density step.

For step 5 I checked an arbitrary underlying point x, not just a closed point in a geometric fibre. Choosing an algebraic closure of its own residue field produces the needed point g. The translate-inverse open has explicit inverse, so its intersection with U is nonempty by irreducibility. Passing to the residue field L of an intersection point gives actual L-points u,v with uv=g_L and an actual point of the scheme fibre product over x. This is an elementary group-law and fibre-product argument from steps 1–4, not an application of the corresponding result for ordinary topological groups. It proves product, not only difference, surjectivity.
