## Problems found and corrected

The original node was statement-only, without a proof or Lean attachment. It is mathematically sound as an ordinary, not normalized, desingularization theorem. Its source does not itself prove the main theorem: ILO VIII 2.3.9 invokes Temkin. Accordingly the proof now exposes two proper intermediate inputs from that construction. It does not make a new node restating Theorem 1.2.1, and it does not cite a theorem about normalized towers to obtain regular ordinary centers.

No STATEMENT-DISPUTED change was needed. The added clarifications preserve meaning. A potential pitfall was finite-dimensional induction: Noetherian schemes need not have finite Krull dimension. Step 2 instead uses Noetherian minimal closed sets and pointwise finite prime height. Another pitfall was omitting every isomorphism stage: a nonempty Cartier center can give an isomorphism and must be retained. The proof removes only unit ideals.

## Review by Q21

I read the archived ILO VIII printed pp.88,91--94, including ordinary resolution definition 2.3.1, functoriality 2.3.4, Remark 2.3.5 and Theorem 2.3.10. The centers remain part of the tower datum throughout, so regularity of centers is taken from the ordinary algorithm, not lost through normalization. I read Temkin online: §§1.3.1--1.3.2 for the strong variety input, §§2.2--2.3 for centered towers and contracted functoriality, Theorem 3.4.1 with the precise pair category, Lemma 4.1.3, and §4.3 through the regular-morphism check. The first new node states Theorem 3.4.1's smaller pair problem with the existence of its strong variety input included. The second isolates §4.3's finite approximations, not its final stabilization. Equicodimensionality and schematic density are written explicitly, rather than used as undefined terms. The theorem allows centers that are only regular in the required codimensions until the final stabilization; I did not incorrectly demand globally regular intermediate approximations.

Step 1 uses the full statements of the two new nodes. Their categories match: reduced Noetherian qe schemes over Q, all regular morphisms, finite disjoint unions. The successor clause retains the prior prefix, so it gives one sequence on the whole category rather than unrelated objectwise choices. Step 2 uses precisely the existing minimal-closed-subsets statement and finite-prime-height statement, both opened and read. It applies minimality to the decreasing closed unresolved sets supplied by the approximation node; localization turns prime chains into the finite bound needed at a point. No uniform dimension bound is assumed. Step 3 uses exactly the off-unresolved-locus agreement, coherence and nonempty centers asserted by the approximation node. Once the unresolved set is empty the agreement is global, and its definition gives regularity of both centers and final scheme.

Step 4 uses the approximation node's regular functoriality at a common stabilized index, not at different indices. I opened and read the flat base-change comparison definition, the theorem saying that this comparison is an isomorphism, the flat-morphism base-change lemma, and the invertible-ideal blowup isomorphism corollary. The level maps are flat by the cited lemma. The unit ideal is invertible, so the corollary applies; this does not justify removing a nonunit Cartier stage, which is explicitly retained. Step 5 uses the concrete comparison definition, whose coefficientwise Rees map and glued affine charts supply the generator formula. It writes the extended-ideal and original-index calculation for two successive contractions; units stay units, and later unit centers are precisely the additionally deleted indices. Identity and composition therefore hold for the actual comparison, not just terminal schemes. Step 6 uses the exact existing surjective-base-change statement twice, first for each level and then its center, ensuring that every nonempty center survives. The full cited statements are copied verbatim in citations.json so each use can be checked.

I tested empty schemes and pairs, regular zero-dimensional inputs, disjoint unions with regular and singular components, a regular open restriction missing a center, a nonempty Cartier center whose blowup is an isomorphism, surjective regular maps, and the possible infinite-dimensional Noetherian case. The empty input uses R_0; different components are processed by the single global rule, not independently concatenated; a missed center gives exactly a unit ideal; and only pointwise finite heights enter termination.
