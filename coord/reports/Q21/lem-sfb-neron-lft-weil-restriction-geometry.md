## Review by Q21

I read BLR's primary scan, especially 7.6 Theorem 4 on printed pp.194–195, all of Proposition 5 on pp.195–198, the Néron adjunction argument of Proposition 6, and the discussion following 10.1 Definition 1 and first paragraph of Proposition 4's proof on pp.290–291. The source distinguishes quasi-compactness from local finite type and requires a locally Noetherian base for the former; the new geometric-properties statement preserves that hypothesis. It does not assert preservation of flatness or properness along a ramified finite locally free map. I also read 6.4 Theorem 1 and its discussion of the neutral open. BLR 10.1 Proposition 4 explicitly uses the finite-set affine-neighbourhood property of the lft-model, and the preceding paragraph explicitly supplies the S-dense affine open and its surjective product map. Those are the sources of the two Néron-specific inputs, rather than an unsupported claim that any separated scheme is quasi-projective. The four new files contain only statements, with all objects introduced, no proof text, and no proof dependencies. The ordinary scheme, smooth, finite-type, affine, fibre and group notions have their definition dependencies or explicit inline meanings.

For step 1 I opened the exact finite-morphism and faithfully-flat definitions, the affine flatness bridge, the locally Noetherian definition, the finite-flat affine-basis theorem, the finite-free-spectrum theorem, the localization finite-map and localization base-change statements, and the affine fibre-product formula. These say precisely what is used: the basis theorem requires a locally Noetherian target, supplied by D being Noetherian; it does not require D' to be free globally. The tensor-localization identification is cited separately, not taken from the finite-map lemma. A finite-dimensional domain is a field by the injective/surjective multiplication argument written in the step. This proves the generic base is E', without a separability assumption. No excellence or henselianity enters.

Step 2 applies the finite-set-affine input to the image of a finite fibre set in M'; this accommodates several points of S' over one point of S. The representability criterion says scheme, not merely algebraic space. The preservation theorem says smooth and separated without additional assumptions. Smoothness supplies local finite presentation, hence local finite type. No Néron property is used for arbitrary base-changed models.

In step 3 the only external input is Yoneda full faithfulness, whose statement I opened. The pointwise operations are explicitly given, are natural under precomposition, and the group diagrams follow by injectivity on every test scheme. This is an elementary use of that cited theorem, not a separate group-transport lemma. In step 4 I checked both directions of each displayed Hom identification using the fixed structural map and projections; this proves arbitrary base-change compatibility on the nose at the functor level, including successive changes. The same preservation node supplies local finite type and quasi-compactness at the generic fibre. Here the base is Spec E, which is locally Noetherian, and V' is finite type by hypothesis; the conclusion would not follow just from M' being lft.

Step 5 opens and uses the smooth-affine/ring equivalence and smooth-ring base-change theorem solely to ensure Y' is smooth. It then writes all four Hom sets and identifies the middle arrow as the assumed Néron bijection. The composite is the generic restriction map, so W really satisfies the Néron mapping property. In particular I did not apply the neutral-chart lemma to W on the strength of smoothness alone. Step 6 uses its exact statement after all its hypotheses are checked, including finite-type generic group and the compatible group identification. Its subset is the same geometric fibrewise identity subset as the item's W^0.

For step 7 I opened the affine fibre-product, affine quasi-compactness, continuous-image quasi-compactness, and finite-type definition nodes. The surjective affine product gives quasi-compact W^0 on each affine base neighbourhood; quasi-compactness of S gives finitely many such neighbourhoods. A finite union of quasi-compact opens is quasi-compact by the open-cover argument written out. The final basic-open argument explains quasi-compactness of the morphism to the affine base, not merely of the source. Restricting lft affine charts and adjoining one inverse proves local finite type of the open subgroup. These are elementary steps with the stated inputs.

I tested the new statements on D'=D (ordinary model), trivial group (neutral chart S), a split torus with its infinite-component Néron lft-model (the whole model need not be finite type, but its neutral chart is), disconnected finite-type generic groups, several primes above a base prime, ramified extensions and purely inseparable generic extensions. The affine-neighbourhood criterion also permits rank zero and empty Z: its representing functor is terminal when S' is empty and empty when a positive-rank test fibre is forced into empty Z. No connectedness, finite component group, global free basis, étale extension, perfection, completeness or excellence hypothesis has been inserted. The proof never transfers the Néron property by arbitrary base change; only the representing restriction functor commutes with that base change.
