## Moved proof guidance (report only, for the future workers)

For the first new node, tensoring an acyclic R_E-complex with the regular R_E[0] is termwise the identity and hence preserves acyclicity; the regular module and zero modules are flat. On each local tensor, u_m(r tensor z)=rho_m(r)z and its inverse is z to 1 tensor z. The two composites are identities by the balancing relation; the maps respect restriction, sheafification and the target coefficient action. Applying a quotient transition before or after u_m gives the same rho_m(r) times the reduction of z, since rho_m is the reduction of rho_(m+1). Thus the identifications are tower identifications, not merely isolated derived isomorphisms. For E=L, the map from the source regular module becomes pi_m under u_m; this checks the actual reduction units. Let g_m:R_L[0] to T_m be u_m^{-1} pi_m. Their product g is killed by 1-s; hence the strict mapping-cocone lift with components (g,0) is a chain map eta. It is R_L-linear, and its quotient coordinate on any section s is 1 tensor pi_m(s). A module-sheaf map out of R_L is fixed by its value on the unit over every site object. In other degrees the source is zero. The two graded cone projections determine a morphism, so the given first-coordinate formula and zero second coordinate prove uniqueness. This accounts for the strict unit proof that the current Lean module isolates separately.

For the second new node, the old local lifting argument proves epimorphy of the constant quotient tower. After the first node's u_m, the tower is exactly Res_m(A_(L,m))_Y in degree zero. A compatible family of its sections is, by definition, a section of R_L; module structure is the limit module structure. Apply the existing surjective-tower-derived-limit node with ring sheaf R_L, using its clause 1 for surjectivity of 1-s and its kernel, and clause 2 for the product in the derived category and distinguished Roos triangle. The normalized strict eta has degree-zero map equal to this kernel inclusion, by its quotient coordinates, and zero shifted coordinate. Its induced H^0 is therefore the identity of the limit, and H^1 of the target is zero since 1-s is epi; all other degrees vanish. This proves eta is a quasi-isomorphism, not just that the two objects admit some unspecified isomorphism. Its inverse after Q identifies the concrete scalar cone with R_L and retains the quotient projections. At E=L the first map of the resulting Roos triangle is exactly the prescribed reduction-unit map, proving derived completeness in the current definition. No general uniqueness of triangulated lifts is used. This distributes both the old diagonal completeness and the E-to-L completion comparison to one reusable tower argument.

## Original proof retained for extraction audit

### Old step 1

Finiteness and the tower. By [lem-weil-valued-dvr-finite-quotients] (applied to $\mathcal O_E$, a discrete valuation ring with finite residue field by [def-weil-l-adic-coefficient-extension]), each $A_{E,n}$ is a finite ring and each reduction $A_{E,n+1}\to A_{E,n}$ is surjective. Hence the morphism of constant sheaves $(A_{E,n+1})_Y\to(A_{E,n})_Y$ is surjective: every section of $(A_{E,n})_Y$ over $U$ is, on the members of some covering of $U$, a constant section $a\in A_{E,n}$ (sheafification [def-weil-pbc-proetale-site]), and $a$ lifts to $A_{E,n+1}$.

### Old step 2

Part 1. Take the partition of $Y$ with the single stratum $Y_1=Y$ ($N=1$), with immersion $i_1=\mathrm{id}_Y$. The set $Y$ is open and closed in $Y$, hence locally closed, and constructible (it is open and retrocompact in $Y$) [def-weil-scheme-morphism-properties]. The restriction $\mathrm{id}_Y^*(A_{E,n})_Y$ is $(A_{E,n})_Y$ itself (inverse image along the identity), and it is constant, hence locally constant (take the one-member covering $\{\mathrm{id}:Y\to Y\}$ of $Y_{\mathrm{proet}}$) [def-weil-proetale-locally-constant-sheaf]. Its stalk at every geometric point of $Y$ [def-weil-proetale-geometric-stalk] is $A_{E,n}$ by [lem-weil-proetale-constant-sheaf-stalk], a finite $A_{E,n}$-module by Step 1. So $(A_{E,n})_Y$ is a constructible sheaf of $A_{E,n}$-modules [def-weil-proetale-constructible-sheaf].

### Old step 3

The complex $(A_{E,n})_Y[0]$ has $\mathcal H^0=(A_{E,n})_Y$ and $\mathcal H^i=0$ for $i\neq0$ [def-weil-derived-cohomology-sheaves]: finitely many nonzero cohomology sheaves, all constructible by Step 2. Hence $(A_{E,n})_Y\in D^b_c(Y,A_{E,n})$ [def-weil-finite-torsion-constructibility], and its cohomological support [def-weil-cohomological-support] is contained in $\{0\}$ (it is $\{0\}$ if $Y\neq\emptyset$ and empty if $Y=\emptyset$). This proves Part 1.

### Old step 4

Reductions of $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$. The one-term complex $P=\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$ in degree $0$ is K-flat over $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$: for every acyclic complex $M$ of $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$-modules, $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}}M\cong M$ termwise (unit isomorphism of the tensor product of sheaves of modules [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product]), which is acyclic. So the identity $P\to\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$ may be used in [def-weil-integral-derived-reduction], and $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}\otimes^L_{\mathcal O_E}A_{E,n}=\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}}(A_{E,n})_Y\cong(A_{E,n})_Y$, the isomorphism being the unit isomorphism; this is the canonical map of Part 2, which is therefore an isomorphism. Under these isomorphisms the transition maps of the system $(\operatorname{Res}_n(\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}\otimes^L_{\mathcal O_E}A_{E,n}))_n$ of [def-weil-derived-complete] are the reduction maps $\operatorname{Res}_{n+1}(A_{E,n+1})_Y\to\operatorname{Res}_n(A_{E,n})_Y$, and the maps $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}\to\operatorname{Res}_n(A_{E,n})_Y$ are the limit projections $\pi_n$ [def-weil-completed-integral-coefficients] (the unit isomorphism is natural in the second factor).

### Old step 5

By Step 4 and Part 1, every reduction $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}\otimes^L_{\mathcal O_E}A_{E,n}\cong(A_{E,n})_Y$ lies in $D^b_c(Y,A_{E,n})$, and their cohomological supports are all contained in the single interval $[0,0]$, independent of $n$ (Step 3).

### Old step 6

$\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$ is derived complete. The $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$-modules $F_n=\operatorname{Res}_n(A_{E,n})_Y$ form an inverse system with surjective transition maps (Step 1; $\operatorname{Res}_n$ does not change the underlying sheaf), and their limit in sheaves of $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$-modules is $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$ itself with projections $\pi_n$ (limits of sheaves of modules and of rings are computed on the underlying sheaves [def-pur-sheaf-inverse-limit, def-weil-completed-integral-coefficients]). By [lem-weil-proetale-surjective-tower-derived-limit] (clause 2), with $\mathcal R=\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$, $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$ in degree $0$ is a derived limit $R\varprojlim_n\operatorname{Res}_n(A_{E,n})_Y$ in $D(Y_{\mathrm{proet}},\mathcal O_E)$ [def-weil-derived-limit]; by clause 3, the canonical map $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}\to R\varprojlim_n\operatorname{Res}_n(A_{E,n})_Y$ of [def-weil-derived-complete], which is the map compatible with the $\pi_n$ of Step 4, is the unique such map, namely the identity of $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}=\varprojlim_n(A_{E,n})_Y$, an isomorphism. Hence $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$ is derived complete [def-weil-derived-complete].

### Old step 7

Part 2. By Steps 4, 5 and 6, $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$ is derived complete, its reductions lie in $D^b_c(Y,A_{E,n})$ and have cohomological supports in $[0,0]$ for every $n$; so it is an object of $D^b_c(Y,\mathcal O_E)$ [def-weil-derived-complete-integral-detailed].

### Old step 9

Integral scalar extension of $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$. Let $E\subset L$ be finite extensions of $\Ql$ in $\Qlb$, $e$ the ramification index, so $\varpi_E\mathcal O_L=\varpi_L^e\mathcal O_L$. ### Old step 10

Independence of the stage. The scalar-extension functor $-\otimes_EL:D^b_c(Y,E)\to D^b_c(Y,L)$ is, on objects, $K\mapsto K\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}\mathcal O_L$, the integral scalar extension of an integral representative, viewed in the rationalization [def-weil-rationalization-karoubi]. On the representative $\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}$ of $E_Y$, Step 9 gives an isomorphism $\phi:\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,Y}\otimes_{\mathcal O_E}\mathcal O_L\cong\widehat{\mathcal O}_{L,Y}$ in $D(Y_{\mathrm{proet}},\mathcal O_L)$, between objects of the full subcategory $D^b_c(Y,\mathcal O_L)$ (Part 2 for $L$), and Step 9 with $E=L$ gives $\psi:\widehat{\mathcal O}_{L,Y}\otimes_{\mathcal O_L}\mathcal O_L\cong\widehat{\mathcal O}_{L,Y}$; their images in $D^b_c(Y,L)$ are isomorphisms $\phi:E_Y\otimes_EL\cong L_Y$ and $\psi:L_Y\otimes_LL\cong L_Y$ (a functor carries isomorphisms to isomorphisms). So $u=\psi^{-1}\phi:E_Y\otimes_EL\to L_Y\otimes_LL$ represents a morphism $[L,u]:(E,E_Y)\to(L,L_Y)$ of $\mathcal C_Y$, and $[L,u^{-1}]$ a morphism back; their composites are represented over $L$ by $u^{-1}u$ and $uu^{-1}$, identities, which represent the identities of $(E,E_Y)$ and $(L,L_Y)$. Hence $[L,u]$ is an isomorphism $(E,E_Y)\cong(L,L_Y)$ in $D^b_c(Y,\Qlb)$ [def-weil-algebraically-closed-coefficients]. This proves Part 3.
