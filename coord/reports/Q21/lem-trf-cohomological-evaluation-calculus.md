For `lem-trf-exceptional-tensor-open-restriction` I read 4.2.2(a)'s initial locality sentence and the displayed counit/projection diagram (4.2), together with the existing open normalization, exceptional composition and projection definitions. Its proof to be split next is as follows. Write the equality of morphisms as Gu=vg. Exceptional composition, with the conjugate adjunction normalization, identifies the right adjoints of R(Gu)_! and R(vg)_!. The exceptional counit of a composite is the first counit followed by the other counit after direct image. Substituting this composite counit into the definition of T, and using the composition/projection coherence, shows that T for a composite is the composite of the two T transformations. For an open immersion the canonical PF is the inverse of the open tensor isomorphism; its restriction is the identity by lem-sf-open-tensor-normalization. Thus the exceptional tensor transformation for an open is the ordinary pullback tensor identification. Apply the composite formula to Gu and vg and cancel these open identifications. The resulting equation is exactly the kappa/lambda equation in the new node. This needs no Cartesian assumption: equality Gu=vg and composition suffice. The node itself carries only the statement, not this proposed proof.

For `lem-trf-dualizing-projection-open-restriction` I read 4.2.4(f)'s locality sentence, the definition of its BC map before the proof, and its exceptional-adjoint diagram (4.7), interchanging projection indices as in the unchanged item. The locality argument factors j times j through X times Y. First restrict the first product factor by compact-support base change; compatibility of the normalized exchange with pasting identifies the restricted inverse exchange for the product square with the inverse exchange for the rectangular product X times Y. Next restrict the second factor by j. Transpose with respect to that open adjunction and use compact-support composition. The composite counit for a_X=a_Y j is the counit of j followed by that of a_Y. Consequently, after inserting j_!, the resulting adjunct is exactly a_X^*(epsilon_aX) B_X, with the open-unit identification. The exchange-pasting equation identifies the B factor, and the composite-counit equation identifies the remaining factor. Bijectivity of adjunction yields J^*chi_Y=chi_X. This proves locality of the map; it does not assert that restricting B_Y alone produces B_X, nor that j^*Ra_Y! on arbitrary inputs equals Ra_X!j^*. The dualizing object restricts by exceptional composition j^!=j^*, independently of an extension-by-zero choice. This distinction matters for clause (d).
