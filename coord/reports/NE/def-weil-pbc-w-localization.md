## Statement dispute and correction

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported definition leaves the ordering of denominator data unspecified, and its purported localization maps do not exist for arbitrary refinements with arbitrary allowed new denominators. Here is an explicit failed type check. Take A = Z, coarse parts D(2) and V(2), with denominators 2 and 1. Refine to the three nonempty constructible parts D(6), D(2) intersect V(3), and V(2). Choose their denominators 3, 1, and 1 respectively. These choices all satisfy the stated nonvanishing condition, and the second partition refines the first. But the component for D(6) would require a Z-algebra homomorphism Z[1/2] → Z[1/3]. Such a homomorphism would send the inverse of 2 to an inverse of 2 in Z[1/3], whereas 2 is not a unit there: an equation 2a = 3^n is impossible in Z. Thus refinement alone is insufficient.

The correction keeps the blueprint's objects and adds the intended preorder: refinement together with divisibility of the parent's denominator into each child's denominator. It makes the phrase “multiplying its denominators” precise, rather than replacing the colimit with a new object. The index is a preorder, not necessarily a partial order, since unit-related denominators can be mutually divisible. There is at most one arrow for each relation. The formula for each map is explicit. If g = fh, the inverse of f in A_g is h/g, so the formula may also be written a/f^n ↦ ah^n/g^n. Different witnesses h give the same map by the localization universal property; the witness is not extra index data.

In particular, denominator 1 is allowed and causes no error by itself. The single-part index with denominator 1 is an initial index, not a terminal index, so its presence does not collapse the diagram to A. My initial concern about that choice is resolved by specifying the direction and ordering of the maps.

The commutative-ring hypothesis is now explicit. The empty spectrum/zero-ring case is explicit as well: its empty partition gives the empty product, the zero ring. The correction neither adds a condition on A nor asserts w-locality or faithful flatness as part of the definition. The ind-Zariski clause remains, since it is precisely a presentation by finite products of A_f.
