For step 1, I opened the field, characteristic, algebraic-closure, integral-scheme and smooth-morphism definition nodes. Characteristic is the least positive annihilator, and field invertibility rules out a nontrivial factorization of it. Algebraic closedness supplies roots of T^(p^n)-c, and the characteristic-p binomial identity gives injectivity. Integrality supplies the single component. The smooth definition explicitly gives the equivalent equidimensional-fibre formulation, so an integral dimension-one curve satisfies relative dimension one. These are short arguments from those definitions, and this is the only step marked elementary.

For step 2, the smooth-relative-dimension permanence node explicitly permits arbitrary base change, and clause (b) of the proper-fp permanence node explicitly preserves properness under any base change. I checked their statements, not their proofs. The fibre-product definition supplies the universal pair, which constructs the inverse to the first projection using s_n inverse. Its two composites are checked against the projections, so no geometric-integrality base-change theorem is being assumed. Integrality and dimension are preserved by this scheme isomorphism. The proper-morphism definition explicitly includes separatedness and finite type, matching the curve definition. These hypotheses apply for every n, including zero.

For step 3, the absolute-Frobenius definition explicitly states that the underlying map is the identity and the sheaf map raises sections to p-th powers. The fibre-product universal property then constructs exactly the map in the item. The projection isomorphism makes this map a homeomorphism, which cannot have singleton image on a one-dimensional target. The existing proper-curve/function-field node gives an embedding for precisely these smooth proper integral curves and this definition of nonconstant. The function-field definition identifies their fields with generic stalks. The displayed composite on stalks is x to x^(p^n), so the image is K^(p^n); I did not assume the scheme isomorphism is k-linear. This repairs the potential ambiguity in identifying twist fields.

For step 4, I checked that the smooth-differentials-free-rank node applies to a smooth scheme morphism at any point, including the generic point, with rank equal to fibre dimension there. Step 1 gives that rank as one. The affine-fraction node supplies K = Frac(A) for the nonempty affine W. The localization node allows any multiplicative subset of the algebra, so A minus zero is allowed. Its conclusion is the module isomorphism needed to turn the rank-one module into Omega(K/k) of dimension one. This is the exact use quoted in citations.json, not merely a definition of differentials.

For step 5, the p-basis-exists node is an absolute statement about any characteristic-p field, applied to K. The differential-basis node is a relative statement about K/k. I checked their hypotheses coincide here because k is contained in K^p, by the roots already proved in step 1. Hence their respective coefficient fields K^p and kK^p agree. A basis of a one-dimensional space has a single index: the proof explains both nonemptiness and impossibility of two independent members. The p restricted monomials are then a literal basis, proving degree p from the degree definition. No field p-rank theorem is being hidden or moved into a duplicate node.

For step 6, the Frobenius power is an isomorphism onto its own image, not onto K. It maps the two fields K and K^p onto L_i and L_(i+1). The proof explicitly sends the basis along that isomorphism and checks spanning and independence, so no unstated invariance-of-degree comparison is assumed. The degree-multiplicativity node has finite intermediate extensions as hypotheses; both adjacent degree p and the inductively finite degree p^n are established before applying it. The base n=0 is the identity extension, and the induction produces the exponent n+1 rather than merely claiming iteration. Combining this with step 3 yields exactly the function-field image in the question.

The degree calculation is genus-independent, so positive-genus curves require no added hypothesis.
