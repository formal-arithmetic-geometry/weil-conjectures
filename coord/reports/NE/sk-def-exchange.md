## Supporting theorem: argument and edge cases

The supporting theorem is not split in this delivery. For the next reader, its intended comparison is the following. Exact inverse image has a right adjoint that preserves K-injectives; the derived Hom adjunction on a K-injective target is the homotopy adjunction, using the K-injective Hom bijection. Therefore the derived counit on I or J is represented by the corresponding termwise sheaf counit, with the canonical comparisons retained. Naturality of the sheaf counit and the defining underived transpose give

$$
\varepsilon^0_{f',J}\circ f'^{-1}e
=j\circ\varepsilon^0_{f',g'^{-1}I}\circ f'^{-1}\Ex^0_I
=j\circ g'^{-1}\varepsilon^0_{f,I}\circ\theta^0_{f_*I}.
$$

Localizing this identity and substituting the written source/target comparisons gives the defining transpose of the fixed unbounded module mate. Injectivity of the derived adjunction Hom bijection identifies the arrows. In particular this comparison involves actual module Hom groups; it never assumes that forgetting coefficients or taking inverse image preserves injectivity. Replacing any representative gives the same specified mate under its canonical comparisons, which yields choice independence. This argument requires the K-injective and derived-adjunction comparison facts, rather than just the definition of a resolution; that is why a module representative comparison theorem was needed. This argument is placed in the report, not in the new unsplit node's statement or proof fields.

I checked both statements on the identity square, where theta is identity and the triangle identity makes Ex identity. For K=0, Lambda the zero ring, and empty schemes, the derived objects and all displayed arrows are zero as appropriate. The new theorem also permits a nonzero acyclic K-injective resolution of zero; its canonical comparisons identify its image with zero and the same equality holds. For an ordinary sheaf placed in degree zero, the replacement g' inverse image I -> J remains essential; neither the theorem nor the item identifies derived direct image with ordinary direct image. Unbounded I,J are explicitly allowed in the new theorem, whereas the item uses its bounded-below injective specialization. The target J need not be termwise bounded below to model the bounded-below derived object. No properness, smoothness, torsion, invertibility, nonzero-ring, or nonempty-scheme assumption enters the comparison.
