I checked the statements of every cited node, not their proof internals, against each use. The citation file contains the full verbatim statement for each of the 15 step/node pairs, so it also contains every clause used in a step.

Step 1 uses `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian` for a finite-type scheme over the Noetherian one-point scheme Spec Q, `lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-open-subscheme-noetherian` for each preimage of an affine open, and `def-weil-finite-type-morphism` for the meaning of quasi-compactness. The cited statements give exactly these facts. No separatedness assumption is needed, since the preimage is an open in the Noetherian source. The assertion that Spec Q is Noetherian is elementary: its ring has two ideals and its spectrum one point.

Step 2 uses `lem-trf-conductor-smooth-base-change` for the finite normalization quotient's conductor being coherent, nowhere dense, and unit exactly on normal input. Its cokernel is the same canonical cokernel in the item. It then uses `lem-trf-normalized-blowup-calculus` with that coherent nowhere dense ideal, obtaining normality, reducedness and finite type of P(X), and its unit-ideal specialization on normal X. Each hypothesis is already present or just supplied by the conductor node. It does not assert that a unit normalized blowup on an arbitrary reduced scheme is identity.

Step 3 writes the concatenated finite list and checks its centers and endpoint directly. The only cited node, `def-trf-normalized-blowup`, states the construction and explicitly its empty-scheme convention. All facts about the tail, including regularity and nonempty centers, are assumptions on N, now applicable to the normal scheme P(X). A proper ideal sheaf has a stalk inside a maximal ideal, which proves the nonemptiness assertion without a new lemma. This is an elementary list construction, not a hidden resolution-existence theorem.

Step 4 applies the conductor smooth comparison only after step 1 established quasi-compactness. Its equality of extended ideals permits using the normalized-blowup smooth comparison with exactly D_Y, rather than an unrelated ideal. The calculus statement explicitly gives the induced smooth map and the identity/composition compatibilities. All input schemes have the required finite-type rational and reduced hypotheses. This establishes the precise smooth normal-input map needed for the assumed N comparison.

Step 5 checks all three possible unit/nonunit cases; extension of a unit ideal rules out the fourth. It uses the calculus statement only for the unit stage on a now-normal Y, and invokes no converse saying every isomorphism has unit center. In particular a normalized blowup of a nonunit invertible ideal can be an isomorphism and must still be retained: the item and proof contract exactly unit ideals.

Step 6 invokes the hypothesis on N, not an unproved assertion about arbitrary tower algorithms. The finite induction has a stated base (the step-4 comparison at P(Y)) and step (the same ideal and the same normalized-blowup construction on the identified preceding scheme). The definition node states that construction. After deletion of a unit stage, normality of its source comes from P(Y) or the previous normalized level; the calculus then proves it is an isomorphism. The rest is following and filtering the given ordered finite list, so this is elementary relative to those statements and N's hypothesis.

Step 7 uses the two cited comparison statements' explicit identity/composition clauses and N's assumed coherence. The remaining index argument is written out: an ideal already unit remains unit, extension is associative, and successive filtering keeps precisely those indices nonunit after the composite. This proves compatibility of retained indices as well as of level maps. It does not replace the synchronization data by an assertion about isomorphic endpoints.

Step 8 uses `lem-weil-pbc-surjective-base-change` exactly for the projection from P(X) times_X Y. The definition of a morphism of schemes in `def-pc-scheme` supplies the local homomorphism of stalks. A point above a conductor center therefore still has proper extended center ideal, which proves that stage survives without invoking flat descent of arbitrary ideals. Surjectivity of the induced normal-input map then invokes the no-contraction hypothesis on N; no extra surjective comparison theorem is assumed.
