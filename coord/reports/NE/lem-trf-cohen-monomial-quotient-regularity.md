Zero monoid generators can be removed, including the case of no remaining generators.

Step 2 is a direct coefficient calculation in the monoid algebra as defined in the item's statement. A monomial ideal has a subset of the coefficient basis as a basis, so quotienting kills basis elements and creates no coefficient torsion. Choosing representations in the fixed finite set of monoid generators bounds the number of surviving indices; uniqueness of such representations is neither asserted nor needed. This is why a singular toric monoid and a nonradical I cause no problem. Saturation and cancellation are retained hypotheses, but the calculation does not require their stronger geometric consequences. The p-injectivity is coefficientwise in the domain C.

Step 3 uses precisely the statements of `lem-weil-pbc-completion-exact-finite-modules` and `lem-weil-pbc-completion-finite-module-tensor`, not their proofs. The exact sequence is the ordinary ideal inclusion and quotient; S is Noetherian and K,S,S/K are all finite S-modules by Step 1. Completion is always at a=(p)+J_S, never silently at J_S. The naturality and formula c_K(b tensor k)=b*iota_K(k) in the tensor statement justify that the completed inclusion has image KB. Hence the quotient is the actual inverse limit with the correct componentwise B-action; no surjectivity of an arbitrary inverse limit is assumed.

Step 4 cites the Cohen-ring definition again only for completeness of C. The two displayed containments follow by expansion of the ideal sum and the verified nilpotence Jbar^(m+1)=0 on Q_m. Finite many coefficients allow their compatible p-adic residues to reconstruct a unique vector. The text constructs both directions of the a-adic comparison; it does not assume a completeness change theorem or confuse p-completeness with J-completeness. The index k+m+1 is sufficient also when k=0 or m=0.

Step 5 uses the results already proved in Steps 3 and 4; it needs no fresh theorem citation. Ideal extension respects the displayed sums/products because both sides are finite sums of the same generators, and the double quotient maps are written on representatives. Thus the resulting comparison is the natural map from S required in the statement, not merely some free-module isomorphism.

Step 6 uses the actual inverse-limit description already established in Step 3 with K=IS. Every generator of J^r acts as zero on the rth coordinate because J_S^r is inside a^r; this holds also for arbitrary B-coefficients by componentwise action. A compatible sequence with every coordinate zero is zero by definition. Consequently no Krull-intersection or Jacobson-radical theorem is used without a citation. The old suggested Jacobson/Krull route is unnecessary because this elementary coordinate calculation supplies precisely the separatedness needed.

Step 7 is an explicit induction starting at r=0. Under theta*x=0 and x in J^rN, the equation p*x=-(theta-p)*x lands in J^(r+1)N. Step 5 gives p-torsion-freeness of that full quotient, so the induction advances. It never infers torsion-freeness of a quotient from torsion-freeness of N. The intersection result of Step 6 then kills x, and subtraction gives injectivity.
