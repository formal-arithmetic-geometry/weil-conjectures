I also read `remark-finite-separable-extension` (09E8) and the statement of 09ER through the existing node. The online 09F5 and 09F3 pages confirm the same source text. The following are corrections to the proof, not changes to the imported theorem:

- Initially `P(z)` belongs to L, not necessarily K. After translations it still belongs to the relevant L or L1. Requiring it in K would discard nonconstant principal parts.
- The valuation condition for the first congruence is `pi^n P(c)` integral, not `pi^(-n) P(c)` integral. The identity is `n-e=(l-1)e`, not `(l-1)/e`.
- The maximal perfect core condition is hypothesis (5), not (4).
- Compatible truncated sections give errors modulo `pi^m`; the occurrence of `pi^(n-m)` in the empty-J paragraph is not the needed coefficient congruence.
- For related nonconstant indices `j'=p^r j`, the translation cancelling the j coefficient is the POSITIVE sum `c=sum_{a=0}^{r-1} sigma(lambda_j^(p^a)) pi^(-p^a j)`. Then `c^p-c` is `-lambda_j pi^(-j)+lambda_j^(p^r) pi^(-j')`. The source's displayed negative sum has the opposite sign. The FINAL telescoping translation, which removes high powers and leaves the low terms, does have a negative sign.
- The group law always uses `w^p P(z)P(c)`, not the isolated occurrence of `w P(z)P(c)`.
- In a base-changed DVR of ramification q, the error ideal is `t^((-n+m)q) B_r`, and `v_r(w)=qe`. These two factors of q are essential to the final bound.
- In the final sum, equality at the threshold gives level AT MOST h; a strict bound is not needed. A split tensor algebra must be handled before speaking of a generating field element.

There is also an implicit coefficient issue: the hypothesis puts zeta in B, whereas the empty-J extension is defined by a polynomial over K. The new constants-rigidity input fills this gap. If E is a finite intermediate field of L/K, its residue field is algebraic over k. An algebraic extension of the perfect field k inside l is perfect and therefore lies in the perfect core, so that residue field is k. Its ramification index is one, since it divides the index of A in B. Completeness and 09E8 give `[E:K]=1`. Thus zeta, being algebraic, lies in K and hence in A. This does not require k to be algebraically closed.

The new classification is explicitly restricted to finite-level parameters. Without the condition `w^p eta` in the maximal ideal, the unrestricted source wording needs care (for example `eta=-1/w^p` makes the transformed Kummer right side zero). Here `(1+wz)^p=1+w^p eta`, which is a unit, so all roots are simple and the split-or-degree-p alternative is legitimate. I use this correct Kummer identity, rather than the source proof's `w^p(1+eta)`.

## Arguments retained for the new inputs

The constants-rigidity argument is given above. It is a proper input about constants in an infinite extension, not a restatement of level lowering.

For the pole calculus, 09F2 gives `P=T^p-T+sum a_i T^i`, with `a_i` divisible by w, and `p` a unit times `w^(p-1)`. For a term with `v(c)>=-n/p`, the error has valuation at least `e-in/p>=e-n`; the product error has valuation at least `pe-2n`. Expanding `1+w(X+Y+wXY)=(1+wX)(1+wY)` gives the composition identity. Since `e-n/p>0`, the affine change of generator is invertible, with inverse `(z'-c)/(1+wc)`. This is algebra and valuation theory, independent of any Epp extension.

For the classification, the nonzero unit `1+w^p eta` presents the algebra as a Kummer algebra. A prime-degree Kummer algebra with the roots of unity present is either split or a cyclic degree-p field. In the integral-parameter case P reduces to `T^p-T`, so its normalized branches are unramified. If a negative pole d is prime to p, the valuation of a root is `-d/p`, forcing ramification p. If p divides d and the leading residue is not a pth power, scaling the root by `t^(d/p)` gives an integral monic equation reducing to `U^p-a`, irreducible over the residue field. The resulting DVR has uniformizer t and residue degree p. Completeness supplies finite normalization. The generic identification of the given C is not new: it is delegated to `lem-per-complete-dvr-finite-normalization` in step 2.

For the truncated pole normal form, put `H=n-m`. Because `m<=e`, the quotients by `pi^m` have characteristic p. Compatible sections from 09EZ identify a principal part modulo `pi^(-H)` with coefficients in l. Let J be the indices whose coefficients are outside k. Induct on the finite size of J. If `j'=p^a j` both occur, use the positive telescoping translation described above: the j term disappears and only the j' coefficient changes. Its new value may enter k, so J strictly shrinks. Error terms stay in `pi^(-H)` because `m<=e` and `m<=pe-n`; these are precisely the two bounds controlling the coefficient and product errors. If the leading pole drops, the same window still applies. If J becomes empty, the remaining principal part is `pi^(-n)a` for a in A, giving alternative (i). This is not a new theorem about an extension generated by P: no such extension is present in the statement.

Otherwise no two indices of J differ by a p-power factor. Write each nonconstant coefficient as `lambda_j=mu_j^(p^s_j)` of maximal finite p-height. Finite height follows from the perfect-core equality. Choose r larger than all `s_j+1` and with all `j p^(r-s_j)>n`. Adjoin the root `t^(p^r)=pi`; its polynomial is Eisenstein, and 09ER preserves the upper residue field. Constant coefficients have p^r-th roots modulo `pi^m` because k is perfect; the nonconstant coefficients have the indicated roots modulo `pi^m` by the compatible section. The negative telescoping sums remove the high p-powers. Modulo `t^(-H p^r)` the parameter has terms `x_i t^(-i)` from constants and `sigma(mu_j)t^(-j p^(r-s_j))` from J. All original nonconstant high exponents have disappeared. There is a unique largest latter exponent: equality of two would make their original indices p-power related. It exceeds every constant exponent and is divisible by p. If that exponent is at most `H p^r`, the error bound gives the first valuation alternative. Otherwise it is the actual pole, with leading residue mu_j not a pth power, giving the second. The hypotheses `0<n<pe`, `m<=e,pe-n` ensure every Kummer correction and every mixed binomial error stays beyond the required threshold. This retained argument fixes the signs and scaling in the source.
