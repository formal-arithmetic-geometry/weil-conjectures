## Source error — STATEMENT-DISPUTED

The imported statement calls Qbar_l a **discrete** ring, puts K in the ordinary etale module derived category, and cites `def-weil-pbc-constructible-complexes` to define bounded constructibility. I opened that node: its first sentence is “Let $\Lambda$ be a finite commutative ring and $T$ a scheme.” Substituting the characteristic-zero infinite field Qbar_l fails this explicit ring hypothesis. If one literally retains the definition's finite-stalk lisse condition for Qbar_l-vector spaces, every stalk is zero: a nonzero vector gives an injection from the infinite field into its vector space. This is not the intended adic category, whose constant rank-one object is nonzero.

The upstream Lean docstring proposes obtaining its ordinary discrete-ring conclusion by taking finite coefficient limits and inverting l. That inference changes coefficient categories. For a concrete distinction, continuous cohomology on Spec F_q has H^1 with constant discrete Q_l equal to Hom_cont(Zhat,Q_l^disc)=0 (compact image is finite, and Q_l is torsion free), whereas adic H^1 with constant Q_l is Q_l, from the compatible quotient maps Zhat -> Z/l^n and then rationalization. Thus there is no implicit coefficient identification supporting that proposed passage. I do not claim to disprove an independently defined finiteness theorem for finite-dimensional **discrete** rational local systems; it is not the theorem supplied by this source or by the cited definition.

I read the original master definition and the current definition at `literature/blueprint/main-20261015/blueprint/sec-six-functors-basics/sk-prop-int-brown/def-weil-integral-rational-qbar-compact-support.md`. Both explicitly use derived-complete integral objects, raw rationalization, and common-stage algebraically closed coefficients, and both assume finite-type schemes and a separated finite-type morphism. The item is corrected to exactly this intended objectwise finiteness assertion, with all three constructions and the finite-level tower specified. The finite-field context mentioned in two general coefficient-definition nodes is not imposed on k: the object constructions are restated explicitly over arbitrary k, as in the source's own arbitrary-field compact-support definition and the existing arbitrary-field integral theorem.

Finite type is material for the adic assertion. If Y is the disjoint union of countably many geometric points and X the corresponding disjoint union of P^n, f is proper and both schemes are locally of finite type, but the degree-2n direct image of the constant adic coefficient object is nonzero on the nth component. No global cohomological bound exists. This diagnoses the locally-finite-type gloss; the correction restores the source's finite-type assumptions, rather than claiming a theorem for that disjoint union.
