## Independence of the two foundational tools

The terminal-fibre theorem is stated for a given cartesian fibration and can be proved directly by a comma contraction. Here is the argument behind the new statement, recorded in the report because the new node must be statement-only. For an object r in the fibre at c, an object of (r downarrow k) consists of u:a→b, w:b→c in A and a map r→u^*t_a over w^op. Cartesian factorization identifies this last map with a map r→(wu)^*t_a in the fibre R_c. Replace the object by the corresponding one indexed by wu:a→c. There is a comparison from this replacement to the original object, over the twisted arrow wu→u given by (1_a,w). There is also a comparison from the replacement to the object indexed by 1_c and the map r→t_c, over wu→1_c. The latter uses the unique-up-to-contractible-choice map (wu)^*t_a→t_c in R_c. Thus on the comma category these give a zigzag from the identity to a constant functor. For every parameter simplex the required cartesian factorizations have contractible spaces of lifts, and the terminal maps have contractible mapping spaces, so the zigzag extends coherently to all higher simplices. Its realization contracts the comma category. The horn/Quillen-A proof of finality then gives restriction equivalences on coherent cocone spaces in every universe. This argument concerns only a given cartesian fibration, its cartesian factorizations, and terminal mapping spaces; it does not reconstruct or classify arbitrary space-valued functors. The general comma/finality criterion must be proved independently when this new node is split, rather than cited from a node whose closure reaches this item. Cisinski 5.6.9 supplies the motivating fixed-endpoint twisted-diagonal contraction; it is not being misquoted as this more general theorem.

For the section theorem, HTT 3.3.3.4 is exactly the section/limit assertion, and Remark 3.3.3.5 points out the direct proof route. One can use compatible right-horn lifting over the simplices of K: the space of sections over a simplex is equivalent to the fibre at its final vertex; restriction to faces gives the transport maps and their coherent compositions. Gluing these compatible simplex sections gives the section Kan complex. With a parameter space X the same gluing identifies maps X→Gamma(q) with coherent cones from X to the fibre diagram. This is the limit mapping-space property, including its maps, not just an identification of objectwise fibres. The concrete slice case has fibres Map_C(v(k),c); a lifting triangle composes with v(a), so its transport is precomposition, and higher lifting simplices give precisely the coherent composition data. This is independent of a classification equivalence between all fibrations and presheaves. Neither new statement requires a coherent-nerve/model-localization comparison. They are smaller foundational tasks of independent content, while steps 1–8 below still prove the actual density and extension theorem.

## Additional small-case check

For the terminal-fibre lemma take A=[1], R_0 and R_1 the two-element chain 0<1, and transport R_0→R_1 the constant-0 functor. Transport does not preserve the chosen terminal 1. The three resolution objects are k(id_0)=(0,1), k(0→1)=(1,0), and k(id_1)=(1,1), with the cartesian arrow from the middle object to the first and the fibre arrow from the middle to the last. For r=(0,0), the comma category has only the first vertex; for r=(0,1), the same; for r=(1,0), it is the whole span; for r=(1,1), it has only the last vertex. Each is contractible. This checks precisely the non-preservation hypothesis that a naive terminal-section argument would mishandle. More generally, over a poset A, the comma at r∈R_c is resolved by pairs (a,b) with a≤b≤c and a map r→F(a≤c)t_a. Replacing (a,b) by (a,c) gives maps to both (a,b) and (c,c), in the required directions. This reproduces the two-sided comma contraction without assuming transport preserves terminal objects.
