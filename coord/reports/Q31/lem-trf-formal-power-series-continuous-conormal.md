### Original step 1 — quotient-data

Put $M=\widehat\Omega_{D/F}$ and $T=M\otimes_D R$. By [lem-trf-formal-power-series-continuous-free], $M$ is free on $\widehat{\mathrm d}(t_i)$, so $T$ is generated by $e_i=\widehat{\mathrm d}(t_i)\otimes1$ as an $R$-module: tensoring $\sum_i a_i\widehat{\mathrm d}(t_i)$ with $b$ gives $\sum_i\bar a_i b e_i$. Let $L=\sum_{j=1}^s R(\widehat{\mathrm d}(f_j)\otimes1)\subseteq T$ and $N=T/L$, with quotient map $q:T\to N$. These are real module quotients, and $N$ is a finite $D$-module, generated by the images of the $n$ elements $e_i$. Write $\delta_D(h)=q(\widehat{\mathrm d}(h)\otimes1)$. Componentwise the universal derivations obey additivity, the Leibniz rule and vanish on $F$, so $\delta_D:D\to N$ is an $F$-derivation.

Cited nodes: lem-trf-formal-power-series-continuous-free, def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials, def-weil-module-base-change.

### Original step 2 — quotient-data

Every ideal of the field $F$ is either zero or all of $F$ (a nonzero element has an inverse), hence is generated by zero or one element. Thus $F$ is Noetherian. By [lem-weil-pbc-power-series-noetherian], $D$ is Noetherian, and by [lem-weil-pbc-power-series-variables-complete] it is $\mathfrak n$-adically complete. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-finite-module-complete-over-complete-noetherian] to the finite $D$-module $N$ of Step 1 and the ideal $\mathfrak n$. It gives a bijective canonical map $\iota:N\to\varprojlim_{r\ge1}N/\mathfrak n^rN$. Since the $D$-action on $N$ factors through $R$, the submodule $\mathfrak n^rN$ equals $\mathfrak n_R^rN$. Denote this submodule by $\mathfrak m^rN$, with $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak n_R$.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-power-series-noetherian, lem-weil-pbc-power-series-variables-complete, lem-weil-pbc-finite-module-complete-over-complete-noetherian.

### Original step 3 — quotient-data

Because $I=(f_1,\ldots,f_s)$, any $h\in I$ can be written $h=\sum_j a_jf_j$. Leibniz gives $\delta_D(h)=\sum_j\bar a_j\delta_D(f_j)+\sum_j\bar f_j\delta_D(a_j)=0$: the first terms vanish by the quotient $q$ and the second because $\bar f_j=0$ in $R$. Consequently $\delta_D$ descends to an $F$-derivation $\delta:R\to N$, with $\delta(\bar h)=\delta_D(h)$. In particular $\delta(\bar t_i)=q(e_i)$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials.

### Original step 4 — quotient-data

For each $r\ge1$ put $R_r=R/\mathfrak m^r$ and $C_r=\Omega_{R_r/F}$. Project $M$ to $\Omega_{(D/\mathfrak n^r)/F}$ and then use the quotient $D/\mathfrak n^r\to R_r$. This gives a $D$-linear map $M\to C_r$ with $\widehat{\mathrm d}(h)\mapsto\mathrm d(h\bmod(I+\mathfrak n^r))$. It extends to an $R$-linear map $T\to C_r$ because $I$ annihilates $C_r$, via $m\otimes\bar a\mapsto\bar a\,m_r$. Each $\widehat{\mathrm d}(f_j)\otimes1$ maps to $\mathrm d0=0$. Thus it factors as $\phi_r:N\to C_r$. The quotient maps of rings commute, and the induced differential maps commute since their composites agree on every universal differential. Hence the $\phi_r$ define $\Phi:N\to C=\varprojlim_r C_r=\widehat\Omega_{R/F}$. In particular $\phi_r\delta(\bar h)=\mathrm d(\bar h\bmod\mathfrak m^r)$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials, def-weil-module-base-change.

### Original step 5 — reconstruction

The Leibniz rule implies $\delta(\mathfrak m^{r+1})\subseteq\mathfrak m^rN$. Indeed an element of this ideal is a finite sum of products $a_0a_1\cdots a_{r+1}$ with $a_i\in\mathfrak m$ for $i\ge1$ and $a_0\in R$. Differentiating gives the term $(a_1\cdots a_{r+1})\delta(a_0)$ and, for each $i\ge1$, $a_0\prod_{j\ne i}a_j\,\delta(a_i)$; all coefficients lie in $\mathfrak m^r$. It follows that $x\mapsto\delta(x)\bmod\mathfrak m^rN$ descends to an $F$-derivation $R_{r+1}\to N/\mathfrak m^rN$. The target is an $R_{r+1}$-module because $\mathfrak m^{r+1}$ annihilates it. The universal property therefore gives $\eta_r:C_{r+1}\to N/\mathfrak m^rN$ sending $\mathrm d(x\bmod\mathfrak m^{r+1})$ to $\delta(x)\bmod\mathfrak m^rN$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials.

### Original step 6 — reconstruction

The $\eta_r$ commute with the transitions: the two maps $C_{r+2}\to N/\mathfrak m^rN$ obtained by first applying the differential transition or first applying $\eta_{r+1}$ both send $\mathrm d(x\bmod\mathfrak m^{r+2})$ to $\delta(x)\bmod\mathfrak m^rN$. They agree because the universal differentials generate the Kähler module. Therefore a compatible $c=(c_r)_r\in C$ gives the compatible sequence $(\eta_r(c_{r+1}))_r\in\varprojlim_r N/\mathfrak m^rN$. Compose with $\iota^{-1}$ of Step 2 to obtain an $R$-linear map $\Psi:C\to N$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials.

### Original step 7 — reconstruction

For every $i$, $\eta_r\phi_{r+1}(q(e_i))=\eta_r(\mathrm d(\bar t_i\bmod\mathfrak m^{r+1}))=q(e_i)\bmod\mathfrak m^rN$. The $q(e_i)$ generate $N$, so $\eta_r\phi_{r+1}$ is the canonical quotient map on all of $N$. Consequently for $z\in N$ the $r$-th coordinate of $\iota\Psi\Phi(z)$ is $z\bmod\mathfrak m^rN$. Since $\iota$ is injective, $\Psi\Phi=\mathrm{id}_N$.

Cited nodes: none.

### Original step 8 — reconstruction

Since $\mathfrak m^r C_r=0$, $\phi_r$ factors as $\bar\phi_r:N/\mathfrak m^rN\to C_r$. For $x\in R$, Step 4 gives $\bar\phi_r\eta_r(\mathrm d(x\bmod\mathfrak m^{r+1}))=\mathrm d(x\bmod\mathfrak m^r)$. These generators show that $\bar\phi_r\eta_r:C_{r+1}\to C_r$ is exactly the transition map. If $c\in C$, then $\Psi(c)\bmod\mathfrak m^rN=\eta_r(c_{r+1})$ by construction. Thus the $r$-th coordinate of $\Phi\Psi(c)$ is $\bar\phi_r\eta_r(c_{r+1})=c_r$. Equality in the inverse limit is coordinatewise, so $\Phi\Psi=\mathrm{id}_C$. Hence $\Phi$ is an isomorphism.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials.

### Original step 9 — parent exactness

The map $T\to C$ is the composite $T\xrightarrow q N\xrightarrow\Phi C$. The first map is surjective with kernel $L$ by the definition of $N=T/L$, and $\Phi$ is an isomorphism by Steps 7 and 8. Therefore $T\to C$ is surjective and its kernel is $L$, the image of $R^{\oplus s}\to T$, $e_j\mapsto\widehat{\mathrm d}(f_j)\otimes1$. Its value on $\widehat{\mathrm d}(h)\otimes\bar a$ is $\bar a\,\widehat{\mathrm d}(h\bmod I)$, since every coordinate has that value by Step 4. This is exactly the sequence and the maps in the statement. If $n=0$, then $D=F$, $I=0$ and both differential modules vanish; if $s=0$ or the generating list contains zeroes or redundancies, the same quotient and all the preceding calculations apply. No division by a positive integer, injectivity of the first map, or regular-sequence hypothesis was used.
