The entirety of former Step 2's non-elementary argument belongs to the new node. A future worker can use this proof, replacing termwise inverse limit by the generic termwise functor U:

Choose K-injective representatives I_M and I_N, with derived isomorphisms q_M and q_N as in the new statement. Stacks 070I gives a unique homotopy class bar(h) whose localized image is q_N h q_M^{-1}. The localization is linear by the existing derived-linear node. Hence the localized images of a bar(h) and bar(h)+bar(h') are respectively q_N (ah) q_M^{-1} and q_N(h+h')q_M^{-1}. Injectivity of that same Hom comparison identifies these as the representatives of ah and h+h'. For an additive R-linear U, termwise application preserves those two operations. It also preserves homotopies: if u-v=dH+Hd, then U(u)-U(v)=dU(H)+U(H)d degree by degree, since U preserves sums and composition. Thus it acts on homotopy classes, even if U is not exact. Naturality of the total-derived unit gives eta_N Q_B U(bar(h)) = RU(Q_A bar(h)) eta_M. Conjugating by eta on K-injectives and RU(q_M), RU(q_N) is exactly c_N RU(h)c_M^{-1}=Q_B U(bar(h)). Apply this formula to the sum and scalar multiple, then use target localization linearity and bilinear composition to cancel c_N and c_M. This proves both identities for RU. At no point are the choices of I or q required to be additive or linear. The same unit-normalized formula works for every choice.

In the application U is inverse limit of strict towers of module sheaves. Its scalar action is sectionwise multiplication by the compatible constant sections. A limit is a compatible family, so lim(au)=a lim(u) and lim(u+v)=lim(u)+lim(v); a compatible degree-minus-one tower homotopy H remains a homotopy on compatible families. The tower category is abelian, its derived localization is linear, and its K-injective computations are supplied by the existing strict-tower theorem. These are exactly the former Step 2's remaining, elementary verifications, retained in Parent Step 2.

Former Step 4 remains Parent Step 4 with all substantive formulas: tensor balancing F(ru) tensor e = F(u) tensor re; preservation of composition on pure tensors; extension by bilinearity; identity; uniqueness from u tensor e = e(u tensor 1).
