## New lemma: source and intended argument

The new lemma makes precise the current blueprint proof's TR2 phrase that a distinguished triangle and its signed rotation may be represented over one common finite field, its TR3 phrase about two distinguished rows, and the same representation used for TR4. There is no claim that Stacks has this particular pair-category theorem. Its source is the agent-written blueprint proof, and its unsplit node contains a statement only.

For clarity, the intended argument for its later worker is as follows. For each of the finitely many marked distinguished rows, choose a witness triangle isomorphism to a common-stage cone row. Add its three components, their inverses, the inverse equations, all three triangle-morphism equations, the cone rows, and the finitely many extra arrows and equations to the finite data. Choose a common upper field also containing M_0. The common-stage calculus gives representatives of all these arrows and an enlargement at which every listed equality holds. At a vertex X=(E,K), correct the lifted X[1] by sigma:T(K[1])→(TK)[1]. The shifted-arrow formula converts a lifted triangle equation to the actual stage equation a_M[1](sigma_X p_M)=(sigma_X' p'_M)c_M. Naturality of sigma and its compatibility with the associators give the same correction after further enlargement; this applies iteratively to each of the finitely many shifted expressions. Thus the witnesses become actual triangle isomorphisms, not just componentwise maps. Exact scalar extension carries each witness cone row to a distinguished row at the final stage. Its isomorphic lifted row is distinguished by finite-stage TR1. The chosen representative for each shared vertex is used throughout. The identity representatives give the recovery isomorphisms, and sigma followed by sigma inverse restores the original third map. None of this uses a Verdier axiom for P_T.

The new lemma is stronger than raw lifting of arrows and equations because it ensures that already distinguished rows lift to distinguished rows with their actual shared vertices. Conversely it is strictly less than the item, which must create new arrows and rows by applying the finite-stage axioms. That is why this is a separate auxiliary lemma rather than a relabelled clause of the item.
