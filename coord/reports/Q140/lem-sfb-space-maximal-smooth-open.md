In step 2 I checked that the etale-open-image statement explicitly includes the bijection of open subsets and open subspaces, and that point calculus gives inclusion of subfunctors from inclusion of point sets. Taking the union therefore constructs a real open subspace. The presentation criterion says smoothness on a surjective presentation suffices, and scheme source locality is valid for arbitrary covers, including the empty cover of the empty scheme. Applying those statements to the pulled-back smooth opens proves smoothness on the union; no existence clause is offloaded to a new node.

In step 3 I read the fibre-product/etale-cover statement and the full chartwise calculus statement: the latter applies to algebraic-space morphisms (as representable morphisms of algebraic stacks), and explicitly states etale implies smooth, smooth implies flat, plus flatness stability under composition and arbitrary base change. I verified that T=V×_Y V′ is a scheme because V→Y is representable etale, even though V′→Y need not be etale. Its projection T→Y′ is the composite of an etale pullback and V′→Y′. Likewise U_T→X′ is an etale surjection. The lfp chart-test statement says U→V is lfp from h lfp. Thus the two hypotheses of the new scheme lemma hold without supposing h flat or v lfp.

In step 4 I checked that the new scheme lemma states the iff at every point of U_T, exactly the implication required; it is smaller than the item because both source and target are schemes and it asserts no construction or descent of space opens. The point-calculus statement gives pullback of opens and detects equality of subfunctors; surjectivity of U_T→X′ permits detection of equality after the chosen atlas. The final reassociation is written on test-scheme sections and uses only the definition of the sheaf fibre product.

I tested empty X and empty Y′ (all smooth loci and equalities are empty), an already smooth h (the union is X), a nonflat lfp closed immersion (allowed by the item), and flat base changes that are neither surjective nor lfp (open restrictions and infinite field extension).
