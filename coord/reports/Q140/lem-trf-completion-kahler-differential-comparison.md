## Original seven-step argument (verbatim handover)

### Original Step 1

Every ideal of the field $k$ is zero or contains a unit and is $(1)$, hence is finitely generated. Thus $k$ is Noetherian by [def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring]. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian] to the given finite-type $k$-algebra $S$, then [lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian] to its localization at $S\setminus\mathfrak p$: $S$ and $A=S_{\mathfrak p}$ are Noetherian. The same definition now makes $\mathfrak m$ finitely generated. Put $M=\Omega_{A/k}$. The symbol presentation in [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials] and repeated Leibniz expansion show that the differentials of a finite set of algebra generators generate $\Omega_{S/k}$. By [lem-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials-localization], their images generate $M$ over $A$. Define $N=M^\wedge=\varprojlim_{r\ge1}M/\mathfrak m^rM$. Since $A$ is Noetherian and $M$ finite, [lem-weil-pbc-completion-finite-module-tensor] identifies $M\otimes_A\widehat A$ with $N$. Since $\mathfrak m$ is finitely generated, [lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete], applied to $M$ and to $A$, gives completeness and separatedness, $Q_r:=N/\mathfrak m^rN\cong M/\mathfrak m^rM$, and $B_r:=\widehat A/\mathfrak m^r\widehat A\cong A/\mathfrak m^r$. All identifications are the canonical projections.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring, lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian, lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian, def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials, lem-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials-localization, lem-weil-pbc-completion-finite-module-tensor, lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete

### Original Step 2

For $r\ge1$, Leibniz expansion gives $\mathrm d(\mathfrak m^{r+1})\subseteq\mathfrak m^rM$. For $x\in\widehat A$ choose $a\in A$ lifting $x$ modulo $\mathfrak m^{r+1}\widehat A$ and set $\delta_r(x)=\mathrm da\bmod\mathfrak m^rM\in Q_r$. This is independent of the lift by that inclusion. Lifts of $x+y$ and $xy$ may be chosen as $a+b$ and $ab$; the derivation identities in [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials] then show that $\delta_r$ is a $k$-derivation with scalar action through $B_r$. A lift modulo $\mathfrak m^{r+2}$ is also a lift modulo $\mathfrak m^{r+1}$, so $q_r\delta_{r+1}=\delta_r$, where $q_r:Q_{r+1}\to Q_r$ is reduction. By the completeness bijection of [lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete], these coordinates define a $k$-derivation $\widehat d:\widehat A\to N$ extending $\mathrm d$. They give $\widehat d(\mathfrak m^{r+1}\widehat A)\subseteq\mathfrak m^rN$, proving continuity. For another continuous extension $D$ and fixed $r$, choose $s\ge r+1$ with $D(\mathfrak m^s\widehat A)\subseteq\mathfrak m^rN$ and lift $x$ to $a\in A$ modulo $\mathfrak m^s\widehat A$. Both $D(x)$ and $\widehat d(x)$ equal $\mathrm da$ modulo $\mathfrak m^rN$. Separatedness gives $D=\widehat d$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials, lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete

### Original Step 3

The composite $\widehat d\circ\sigma:F\to M^\wedge$ is a $k$-derivation. By the universal property in [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials] it factors through $\Omega_{F/k}$, which is zero by [lem-trf-separable-field-differentials-vanish]. Thus $\widehat d\circ\sigma=0$, so $\widehat d$ is also an $F$-derivation for the $F$-action induced by $\sigma$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials, lem-trf-separable-field-differentials-vanish

### Original Step 4

Put $D_r=\Omega_{B_r/F}$ and let $t_r:D_{r+1}\to D_r$ be induced by $B_{r+1}\to B_r$. The composite $A\to B_r\xrightarrow{\mathrm d}D_r$ is a $k$-derivation because $\sigma$ is a $k$-algebra section. The universal property in [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials] gives an $A$-linear map $M\to D_r$ sending $\mathrm da$ to $\mathrm d(a\bmod\mathfrak m^r)$. The target is a $B_r$-module, so the map kills $\mathfrak m^rM$. Using [lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete] gives a $B_r$-linear map $\alpha_r:Q_r\to D_r$. On each generator $\mathrm da\bmod\mathfrak m^{r+1}M$ both $t_r\alpha_{r+1}$ and $\alpha_rq_r$ give $\mathrm d(a\bmod\mathfrak m^r)$; hence $t_r\alpha_{r+1}=\alpha_rq_r$. Therefore $\Phi:N\to\varprojlim_rD_r$ is the $\widehat A$-linear map with $(\Phi(y))_r=\alpha_r(y_r)$ for $y_r=y\bmod\mathfrak m^rN$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials, lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete

### Original Step 5

For every $r\ge1$, Step 2 gives $\delta_r:\widehat A\to Q_r$ killing $\mathfrak m^{r+1}\widehat A$; Step 3 shows that it kills $\sigma(F)$. It induces an $F$-derivation $B_{r+1}\to Q_r$, where scalars act through $B_{r+1}\to B_r$. By [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials] obtain a $B_{r+1}$-linear map $\beta_r:D_{r+1}\to Q_r$ with $\beta_r(\mathrm d(x\bmod\mathfrak m^{r+1}))=\delta_r(x)$. On differential symbols the identity $q_r\delta_{r+1}=\delta_r$ yields $q_r\beta_{r+1}=\beta_rt_{r+1}:D_{r+2}\to Q_r$; hence it holds on the generated module. For a compatible sequence $z=(z_r)_r$ in $\varprojlim_rD_r$, define $\Psi(z)$ by $(\Psi(z))_r=\beta_r(z_{r+1})\in Q_r$. Indeed $q_r\beta_{r+1}(z_{r+2})=\beta_rt_{r+1}(z_{r+2})=\beta_r(z_{r+1})$. The completeness bijection in [lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete] identifies this compatible sequence with an element of $N$. The maps are $\widehat A$-linear since the scalar actions on $Q_r$ factor through $B_r$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials, lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete

### Original Step 6

For each $r\ge1$ the following identities have the indicated domains: $\beta_r\alpha_{r+1}=q_r:Q_{r+1}\to Q_r$ and $\alpha_r\beta_r=t_r:D_{r+1}\to D_r$. Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials, lem-weil-pbc-module-completion-complete

For every $x\in\widehat A$ and $r\ge1$, choose $a\in A$ lifting $x$ in $B_{r+1}$. Thus $\Phi\widehat d=\widehat{\mathrm d}_F$ on every element, including those outside $A$. The formula also gives the prescribed value on every $\mathrm da\otimes1$. Since these elements generate $M\otimes_A\widehat A$ over $\widehat A$ (the symbol presentation of [def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials] followed by scalar extension), their prescribed images determine any $\widehat A$-linear map uniquely. This proves the uniqueness in (ii), while Step 2 already proves (i).
