### Original step 1

Use the integral-category identification of [lem-weil-pbc-integral-stratified-perfect-characterization] on the geometric fibre in the coefficient conventions of [def-wf-weil-category]. It identifies the two integral full subcategories inside the same derived category over $B$, with the same reduction functors. Here $W$ is noetherian and finite-dimensional, being of finite type over a field; it is quasi-compact and quasi-separated since it is separated and of finite type, and $\ell$ is invertible since $\ell\ne p$. Thus [lem-fix-constructible-tensor-completion] applies: $T=M\otimes_B^L N$ is complete, and its canonical quotient-tower completion map $\chi^{\mathrm{quot}}$ is an isomorphism natural in both arguments.

Cited statements: lem-weil-pbc-integral-stratified-perfect-characterization, def-wf-weil-category, lem-fix-constructible-tensor-completion.

### Original step 2

Write $C(-)=(-)^{\wedge}_{\mathrm{Kos}}$ and $\eta_A:A\to C(A)$ for the functor and unit represented by [def-weil-pbc-intrinsic-completion]. The unit is natural by [lem-weil-pbc-ringed-koszul-completion-reflector]. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-constant-completion-equivalence] with the constant Noetherian ring $R$, ideal $(\varpi)$ and completed ring sheaf $B$. The hypotheses on the coefficient quotients hold: by [lem-weil-l-adic-extension-integers-dvr], $R$ is a DVR and has finite residue field; hence $R/(\varpi)$ is finite (so additive torsion), and each $R/(\varpi^n)$ is finite. Multiplication by $\varpi^n$ gives the two-term finite-free resolution $0\to R\xrightarrow{\varpi^n}R\to R/(\varpi^n)\to0$, so every quotient is perfect over $R$. The same DVR statement makes $R$ Noetherian (indeed a PID). That theorem's last paragraph identifies Koszul and quotient-tower completions over $B$, respecting their units. Hence transporting $\chi^{\mathrm{quot}}$ through this identification is precisely $\eta_T:T\to C(T)$. This is the $\chi$ in the statement and $C(T)$ is the tensor of [def-wf-tensor-product]. In particular $\chi$ is an isomorphism natural in $M,N$. The comparison theorem, rather than the definition alone, justifies this identification.

Cited statements: def-weil-pbc-intrinsic-completion, lem-weil-pbc-ringed-koszul-completion-reflector, lem-weil-pbc-constant-completion-equivalence, lem-weil-l-adic-extension-integers-dvr, def-wf-tensor-product.

### Original step 3

Put $H=\Phi_W^*$ before completion and rationalization. By [def-wf-weil-category] this is exact coefficient transport. Use precisely the chain models specified in [def-wf-tensor-product]: flat-term K-flat $U,V$, $A=\operatorname{Tot}(U\otimes_B V)$ and $A'=\operatorname{Tot}(HU\otimes_B HV)$. Write $a:HA\to A'$ for the ordinary tensor comparison and $b:HC(A)\to C(A')$ for its completed comparison. The coefficient identification sends $\varpi$ to $\varpi$, so its map $H[B\xrightarrow{\varpi^m}B]\to[B\xrightarrow{\varpi^m}B]$ preserves the degree-zero inclusion $\iota_m$. Consequently the component of the product map applied to $H(1_A\otimes\iota_m)$ is $(1_{A'}\otimes\iota_m)a$. In the mapping-fiber formula of [def-weil-pbc-intrinsic-completion], $\eta_A$ is $z\mapsto((z\otimes\iota_m)_m,0)$. The map $b$ uses the product comparison on both summands, so it preserves this zero second component. Thus, as actual chain maps, $bH(\eta_A)=\eta_{A'}a$. The stated comparisons represent these maps on the derived category, giving
$$\widehat\mu_{\Phi_W}\,\Phi_W^*(\chi_{M,N})=\chi_{\Phi_W^*M,\Phi_W^*N}\,\mu^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\Phi_W}.$$
This calculation uses the specified strict models, without inferring equality of maps merely from their finite reductions.

Cited statements: def-wf-weil-category, def-wf-tensor-product, def-weil-pbc-intrinsic-completion.

### Original step 4

Let $v=\varphi_M\otimes_B^L\varphi_N$ be the ordinary tensor map from $\Phi_W^*M\otimes_B^L\Phi_W^*N$ to $T$. Its completed tensor map is $C(v)$ by [def-wf-tensor-product]. Naturality of the unit [lem-weil-pbc-ringed-koszul-completion-reflector] gives $\chi_{M,N}v=C(v)\chi_{\Phi_W^*M,\Phi_W^*N}$. Compose with $\mu^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\Phi_W}$ and substitute Step 3:
$$\chi_{M,N}v\mu^{\mathrm{ord}}_{\Phi_W}=C(v)\widehat\mu_{\Phi_W}\Phi_W^*(\chi_{M,N}).$$
The left side is $\chi\varphi_T$ and the right side is $\varphi_{\widehat T}\Phi_W^*(\chi)$. This is exactly the morphism condition in [def-wf-weil-category]. Its inverse is a Weil morphism too: multiply this equation on the left by $\chi^{-1}$ and on the right by $\Phi_W^*(\chi^{-1})$. Thus the underlying isomorphism is an isomorphism of Weil pairs. For this to be a morphism of the Weil category, both ends must be bounded constructible: by clause 2 of [lem-pur-integral-operations-preserve-constructible] (applied with $f=\operatorname{id}_W$; $\ell$ is invertible on $W$ by Step 1), $M\widehat\otimes^L_{\mathcal O_E}N\in D^b_c(W,\mathcal O_E)$, and $\widehat T$ and $T$ are isomorphic to it through Step 2 and $\chi^{\mathrm{quot}}$; Step 1 identifies this category with the one of [def-wf-weil-category].

Cited statements: def-wf-tensor-product, lem-weil-pbc-ringed-koszul-completion-reflector, def-wf-weil-category, lem-pur-integral-operations-preserve-constructible.

### Original step 5

Raw rationalization [def-weil-pbc-rational-category] retains objects and localizes Hom groups. The tensor convention of [def-wf-tensor-product] sends $(u/\varpi^r,v/\varpi^s)$ to the integral tensor map divided by $\varpi^{r+s}$, and $\varphi_T$ is read with the same convention. Therefore the naturality and Step 4 equations hold also for rational Weil maps: write $\varphi_M=u/\varpi^r$, $\varphi_N=v/\varpi^s$ with integral numerators, apply the integral equality of Steps 3--4 to $u\otimes v$ (Step 4 used only naturality, so the numerators need not be isomorphisms), and divide by $\varpi^{r+s}$. For the common-stage clause, let $M$ be given at $E$ and $N$ at $F$, and choose a finite intermediate $L\supseteq E,F$ as in [def-wf-weil-category]. Its transition functors give Weil complexes $T_{L/E}M$, $T_{L/F}N$ at $L$; their underlying objects are objects of the integral category at $L$, since rationalization keeps objects. Steps 1--4 and the rational argument just given, with $L$ in place of $E$, give the equation at $L$. Then $[L,\chi]$ and $[L,\chi^{-1}]$ are morphisms of the common-stage Weil category, and its identity and composition rules make them mutually inverse.

Cited statements: def-weil-pbc-rational-category, def-wf-tensor-product, def-wf-weil-category.
