## Complete allocation of the returned proof

The following six steps are the complete baseline proof verbatim. Steps 1 and 2 and the chart-topology part of 3 belong to lem-sfb-t1-finite-hausdorff-affine-cover; the overlap part of 3 and all of 4 and 5 belong to lem-sfb-hausdorff-charts-qc-open-closed. ### Returned step 1

Apply [lem-pqf-finite-affine-cover-qc-open] to the quasi-compact open $X\subseteq X$. Obtain a finite affine open cover $X=\bigcup_{i=1}^nU_i$, and choose presentations $U_i\simeq\operatorname{Spec}A_i$. The family may be empty when $X=\varnothing$. Every singleton in $U_i$ is closed, since it is the intersection of $U_i$ with a singleton closed in $X$.

Citations: lem-pqf-finite-affine-cover-qc-open.

### Returned step 2

For each $i$, every prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ of $A_i$ is maximal. Indeed [lem-weil-pbc-proper-ideal-contained-in-maximal] gives a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m\supseteq\mathfrak p$, which is prime. In the Zariski topology [def-weil-affine-spectrum] write the closed singleton $\{\mathfrak p\}=V(S)=\{\mathfrak q:S\subseteq\mathfrak q\}$ for some $S\subseteq A_i$. Since $S\subseteq\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak m$, we have $\mathfrak m\in\{\mathfrak p\}$, so $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak m$. Thus no closed-point/maximal-ideal correspondence is being used as an unstated lemma.

Citations: lem-weil-pbc-proper-ideal-contained-in-maximal, def-weil-affine-spectrum.

### Returned step 3

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-zero-dimensional-spectrum-profinite] to each $A_i$, with its all-primes-maximal hypothesis proved in step 2. Hence each $U_i$ is Hausdorff. Each $U_i$ is also quasi-compact by [lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs]. Since $X$ is quasi-separated, [pqf-lem-qs-intersection-qc] implies that $U_i\cap U_j$ is quasi-compact for all $i,j$. This is quasi-compactness in the subspace topology, in particular as a subset of $U_j$. No intersection is asserted affine at this stage.

Citations: lem-weil-pbc-zero-dimensional-spectrum-profinite, lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs, pqf-lem-qs-intersection-qc.

### Returned step 4

Fix $i,j$ and put $K=U_i\cap U_j\subseteq U_j$. For $y\in U_j\setminus K$, Hausdorffness gives, for each $z\in K$, disjoint open neighbourhoods $V_z$ of $z$ and $W_z$ of $y$ in $U_j$. Quasi-compactness supplies $z_1,\ldots,z_r$ with $K\subseteq\bigcup_aV_{z_a}$. Then $\bigcap_aW_{z_a}$ is an open neighbourhood of $y$ disjoint from $K$. Thus $K$ is closed in $U_j$, including $K=\varnothing$ with the empty intersection equal to $U_j$. Consequently $U_j\setminus U_i$ is open in $U_j$, hence in $X$. Since the $U_j$ cover $X$, their union $X\setminus U_i$ is open. Therefore every $U_i$ is closed in $X$ as well as open. This is the finite-neighbourhood proof of compact subsets being closed in a Hausdorff space, written out from the definitions.

Citations: .

### Returned step 5

For each $i$, the open inclusion $j_i:U_i\hookrightarrow X$ is an immersion: it factors as the identity closed immersion $U_i\to U_i$ followed by that open inclusion, using [def-weil-closed-immersion]. Its image is closed by step 4. Therefore [lem-weil-pbc-immersion-closed-image-closed-immersion] makes $j_i$ a closed immersion. These are the original affine open subschemes, now also closed subschemes; no induced reduced structure is substituted.

Citations: def-weil-closed-immersion, lem-weil-pbc-immersion-closed-image-closed-immersion.

### Returned step 6

The finitely many affine closed subschemes $U_i\hookrightarrow X$ of step 5 cover $X$ set theoretically by step 1. Apply [lem-sc-closed-affine-union-affine] to this family: $X$ is affine, so there exist a commutative ring $A$ and an isomorphism $X\simeq\operatorname{Spec}A$. Its statement explicitly includes the empty family for $X=\varnothing$. This uses neither a Noetherian nor a finite-type hypothesis. It completes the finite-cover argument using the existing general closed-cover affineness lemma.

Citations: lem-sc-closed-affine-union-affine.
