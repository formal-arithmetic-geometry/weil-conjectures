For the new atlas node I checked 04YC's finite affine subcover of a smooth atlas, then the finite-type and smooth refinements using the stated atlas tests. An affine locally finite-type scheme over a field is finite type because it is quasi-compact. These are general independent inputs, not clauses renamed from this item.

Step 1 uses the precise CFG and full-subcategory conventions and the new representable-descent statement. Fullness and pullback stability give a CFG; scheme-representability is a hypothesis. The new lemma gives exactly effective descent. Step 2 uses the explicit Isom and atlas definitions from the moduli-groupoid node. Fullness identifies the Isom sheaves, so the hypothesis on X's diagonal applies to arbitrary scheme T, not just to atlas points. The displayed pullback identity tests every scheme base change of the etale atlas; its surjectivity comes from the original atlas, not from an open immersion being surjective onto X. These are direct definitions, so step 2 is elementary.

Step 3 uses the new atlas lemma with X algebraic, smooth and finite type; a DM atlas is smooth by the explicitly cited etale-relative-dimension-zero clause of def-sbc-smooth-morphisms. It then uses exactly the existing statement that finite type over a Noetherian base gives a Noetherian source, and that any open of a Noetherian scheme is Noetherian. Spec k is one point with Noetherian local ring, so the base hypothesis is satisfied. It is essential that the entire A is Noetherian: an arbitrary open of an arbitrary finite-type-over-non-Noetherian-base chart would not suffice. Step 4 uses the existing open-immersion smooth-composition statement for A_U -> A -> Spec k. The finite-type claim is written as the finite basic-open cover argument, including generators after localization. The atlas test and the open-cover finite-subcover argument establish global quasi-compactness, rather than claiming local finite type alone implies finite type.

Step 5 uses precisely the new descent lemma, the item's natural scheme-test hypothesis, and the definitions of finite and etale. In particular, Y was merely a CFG at the outset; I did not assume Y's stack property before proving it. Step 6 cites the existing representable-finite stack-properties statement for its DM conclusion only: U is DM by step 2, Y is a stack by step 5, and p is representable finite by the all-schemes hypothesis. No quasi-finiteness or properness of p is needed for the remaining argument.

Step 7 uses the exact statements that a finite scheme morphism is proper, a proper scheme morphism is separated with closed diagonal, a closed immersion is finite, and finite morphisms compose. I checked that I is Isom_U(p y1,p y2) over the same arbitrary T, not the wrong fibre product. Over I the two objects are sections of the same scheme E after inserting the universal base isomorphism. The equality locus is closed by the diagonal of the finite map E -> I, and its functor of points is explicitly Isom_Y(y1,y2): the relative represented groupoid has at most one arrow over a prescribed base isomorphism. Thus J -> T is finite with no assertion that Isom_Y equals Isom_U. This last distinction matters for decorations that kill some stabilizers. The inverse-image closed-immersion assertion is written via the image ideal on affine patches, an elementary defining construction; it is not a hidden uncited descent theorem.
