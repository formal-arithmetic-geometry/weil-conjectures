The first gives finite-type affine charts, the second identifies the stalk with the prime localization compatibly with germs, the third says the residue field is the fraction field of B/q, and the last defines the induced residue-field map. Their statements give exactly what the step uses. The remaining argument is elementary: B is generated by finitely many b_i over A; the image of A lies inside k; every fraction of B/q therefore belongs to k(bar b_i). The reverse inclusion holds in the actual residue field. This does not invoke Zariski's lemma or a closed point. A non-Noetherian base is also allowed, since the chart definition needs no Noetherian hypothesis.
