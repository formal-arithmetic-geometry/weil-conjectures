## Extracted argument, for the next worker

Choose finitely many generators in each P^i and the associated surjection R^r onto P^i. Projectivity lifts its identity map to a section, hence embeds P^i in R^r. Since R is a domain, multiplication by any nonzero scalar is injective on R^r and therefore on P^i. Stacks 0AUW(3), or the existing lem-sc-dvr-fg-torsionfree-free, makes each P^i finite free. For the finitely many degrees in [a,b], choose a finite basis, write its size as n_i and let e_i:R^(n_i) to P^i be its coordinate isomorphism. Outside the interval choose n_i=0 and the unique zero-module isomorphism. Let D_i be the matrix of e_(i+1)^(-1) d^i e_i. The coefficient in row j and column k is the j-th coordinate of d^i(e_i of the k-th standard vector). Then d^i e_i = e_(i+1) D_i. Multiplying consecutive matrices gives e_(i+2)^(-1) d^(i+1) d^i e_i = 0. The coordinate modules and these differentials consequently form a complex, with zero terms outside the same interval; the e_i and e_i^(-1) commute with differentials and are inverse chain maps. This is a strict complex isomorphism. It contains the entire old step-3 split-surjection/torsion-freeness/basis/zero-composite argument; no new proof steps are placed in the unsplit JSON node.

## Original detailed proof retained verbatim

1. Put $R=\mathcal O_E$, $\pi=\pi_E$, $k=R/(\pi)$ and $B_T=\mathcal O_{E,T}$. Use the Noetherian scheme setting and the identification $B_T=\varprojlim_n(R/\pi^n)_T$ in [sk-lem-integral-models]. The coefficient hypotheses for the stratified-perfect theorem applied in step 2 hold with $A=R$ and $I=(\pi)$: [lem-weil-l-adic-extension-integers-dvr] makes $R$ a local PID, hence Noetherian since each ideal has one generator. Here is also the elementary completeness check, rather than an unstated change of coefficient topology. For a compatible sequence of residue classes choose representatives $a_n\in R$. For $m\ge n$, $a_m-a_n\in\pi^nR$, hence $|a_m-a_n|_E\le|\pi|_E^n\to0$. By [lem-weil-l-adic-extension-complete] they converge to $a\in E$. The nonarchimedean inequality of [lem-weil-l-adic-extension-absolute-value] shows $a\in R$ and $a-a_n\in\pi^nR$: both assertions follow by passing to the limit in the corresponding closed-ball inequality, using the DVR factorization to identify the ball of radius $|\pi|_E^n$ with $\pi^nR$. Thus $R\to\varprojlim R/\pi^n$ is onto. It is injective because $|a|_E\le|\pi|_E^n$ for all $n$ forces $a=0$.

2. The meaning of $K\in\Dbc(X,\mathcal O_E)$ is boundedness with constructible cohomology in [sk-def-sfb-constructible]. Apply (1)$\Rightarrow$(2) of [lem-sfb-integral-cohomological-adic-criterion] to this $B_X$-complex. It gives locally derived $(\pi)$-completeness and an isomorphism $K\otimes^L_{B_X}k_X\simeq\nu_X^*C$, with $C$ bounded étale constructible of finite Tor amplitude. These are exactly condition (a) of [lem-sfb-adic-stratified-perfect], not just a residue sheaf constancy assumption. Its implication (a)$\Rightarrow$(b), with the ring hypotheses checked in step 1, gives a finite constructible locally closed stratification of $X$ and on a pro-étale cover of each stratum $S$ an isomorphism $K|_S\simeq B_S\otimes_R P^\bullet$ for a bounded complex of finite projective $R$-modules. The theorem itself identifies the restricted coefficient with $B_S$ and supplies the stratification and the scalar-ring complex. No claim that arbitrary maps between completed free sheaves have constant entries is used.

3. For each term of $P^\bullet$ from step 2, finite projectivity gives a split surjection $R^r\twoheadrightarrow P^i$: choose finitely many generators, and split this surjection by projectivity. Thus $P^i$ embeds into $R^r$; since $R$ is a domain by [lem-weil-l-adic-extension-integers-dvr], it is torsion-free. Apply [lem-sc-dvr-fg-torsionfree-free] to obtain a finite $R$-basis of each $P^i$. There are only finitely many nonzero terms. In these bases each $R$-linear differential is a finite matrix over $R$; conjugating consecutive differentials preserves their zero composite. The resulting bounded finite free $R$-complex $M^\bullet$ is isomorphic to $P^\bullet$, so $B_S\otimes_R M^\bullet$ is the required local model in (1). This is freeness of modules over $R$, not a freeness or constancy assertion about projectors over the sheaf $B_S$.

4. Fix $q$. By [sk-def-sfb-constructible], $H^q(K)$ is already a constructible $B_X$-module sheaf. As a degree-zero complex it has only this cohomology sheaf, so it is bounded with constructible cohomology (the other sheaves are zero, with the zero finite constant model). It therefore belongs to $\Dbc(X,\mathcal O_E)$. Now [sk-lem-integral-models](2) says explicitly that objects of this $\Dbc$ are derived complete, with derived completeness defined in its setup by the canonical isomorphism to the derived limit of reductions. Apply it to $H^q(K)[0]$. This proves derived completeness in the precise sense of [sk-def-pe-derived-completion] used here; no identification of that definition with local internal-Hom completeness is required here. This argument is independent of the local cohomology computation below and does not interchange cohomology and an inverse limit.

5. Use exactly the stratification and charts of steps 2–3. The exact inverse-image functor with its coefficient identification in [sk-prop-sfb-cartesian-coefficients] commutes with cohomology: it preserves the kernels and images defining cohomology, both for restriction to $S$ and for the pro-étale charts. On such a chart $U$ the constant-sheaf functor on $R$-modules is exact by [lem-weil-pbc-constant-module-section-properties](1). The completed coefficient $B_U=\varprojlim_n(R/\pi^n)_U$ is flat over the constant sheaf $\underline R$ by [lem-weil-pbc-completed-constant-sheaf-reduction]. The composite functor $N\mapsto B_U\otimes_{\underline R}\underline N$ is therefore exact. Apply it to the cycles, boundaries and their short exact sequences in $M^\bullet$ to obtain $H^q(K)|_U\simeq B_U\otimes_R H^q(M^\bullet)$. This is the completed module $\widehat{\underline{H^q(M^\bullet)}}$, computed by extension of scalars, not by commuting an inverse limit with cohomology. Finally $\ker(d^q)\subset M^q$ is finite by [lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-submodule-finite], since $R$ is Noetherian by step 1. Its quotient $\ker(d^q)/\operatorname{im}(d^{q-1})=H^q(M^\bullet)$ is finite, because the images of a finite generating set generate any quotient. Together with step 4 this proves every assertion of (2), on the original strata of (1).

6. Choose a finite cohomological interval $[a,b]$ for $K$. By the good-truncation formulas in [def-kun-truncations], the cohomology sheaves of $\tau_{\le q}K$ are $H^i(K)$ for $i\le q$ and zero for $i>q$, and those of $\tau_{\ge q}K$ are $H^i(K)$ for $i\ge q$ and zero for $i<q$. Each list vanishes outside $[a,b]$ and consists of constructible sheaves: this holds for $H^i(K)$ by hypothesis and for zero by its zero finite constant model. Thus both truncations are bounded with constructible cohomology, which is precisely membership in $\Dbc(X,\mathcal O_E)$ by [sk-def-sfb-constructible]. This proves (3) directly in $\Dbc(X,\mathcal O_E)$, without identifying it with a separately defined adic category.
