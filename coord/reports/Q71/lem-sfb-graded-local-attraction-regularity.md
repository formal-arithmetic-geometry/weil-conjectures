The degree-zero criterion for membership in m is exactly the direct-sum
description: decomposition is finite and unique, and all nonzero components
are unrestricted. The maximality instance packages the node's explicit
"hence a maximal ideal" clause. It imposes no further restriction: since m is
an ideal with that membership criterion, reduction of the degree-zero component
induces A/m = A_0/m_0, a field. In particular products of opposite nonzero degrees
lie in m_0 because both factors lie in m. This observation also shows that the
projection followed by reduction is multiplicative. No independent residue
field or arbitrary identification is assumed.

The generator formula implies b ≤ m: negative-degree elements have zero
degree-zero component, and the other generators belong to a ≤ m. Thus both
a and b lie in the specified maximal ideal, as in the node. For either ideal
c ≤ m, the submonoid `m.primeCompl.map (Ideal.Quotient.mk c)` is exactly the
complement of m/c in A/c: any lift of a quotient element belongs to m precisely
when that element belongs to m/c. Hence the two quotient `Localization` types
in the signature are the node's prime localizations, with the actual quotient
maps and fraction constructions. This choice avoids adding proofs of primality
of the quotient ideals as separate assumptions. The equality is also the
specialization of `Ideal.map_primeCompl_comap_of_surjective` to the quotient
map, using `Ideal.comap_map_mk`.
