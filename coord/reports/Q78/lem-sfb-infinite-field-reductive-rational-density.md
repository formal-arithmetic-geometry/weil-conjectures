For step 2 I opened the new self-centralizing node and checked equality on all commutative algebras, not just equality on field-valued points. Both reductivity and maximality hold for the tori selected in step 1. I read `def-sfb-tori-multiplicative-type` and `def-pc-fibre-product`: scalar extension is a fibre product, and the product after scalar extension has the tuple functor; concatenating the finitely many split coordinates gives a split torus. Thus the product itself satisfies the torus definition. This elementary identification avoids assuming that a dense set of rational points behaves like a product of underlying Zariski spaces. I then read the new parametrization node: it supplies exactly a dominant morphism from a nonempty affine-space open, with no separability or splitness assumption. Milne 12.64 and the equivalent formulation in 12.59 give this statement, even over finite or imperfect fields. I did not claim rationality of every torus: the source warns that some characteristic-zero tori are not rational.

Step 3 is only the open-preimage form of dense image, twice. The nonempty open V is supplied by the item, continuity gives open inverse images, and U being open in affine space makes the resulting W an affine-space open. This is a definition-level topological argument, so its elementary marker is justified; no generic smoothness, Chevalley image theorem or product-dominance theorem is hidden in it.

For step 4 I read the full statements of `def-weil-affine-spectrum` and `lem-sfb-laurent-vector-evaluation-test`. The latter explicitly allows arbitrary vector spaces, W=0 and r=0 and concludes that all coefficients vanish when all unit-tuple values vanish. I apply precisely its contrapositive to the scalar polynomial f, embedded by its nonnegative exponents. Choosing f in I outside a prime in W uses only the definition of a Zariski closed subset V(I); it gives f nonzero and D(f) contained in W. Evaluation gives the actual map from Spec k by the spectrum definition. This step is marked non-elementary because it cites the Laurent evaluation theorem; that theorem carries the only polynomial nonvanishing input.
