## Full inherited arguments and allocation

These are worker handoff material, not proof steps in the new nodes. They preserve the returned proof without shortening or blurring it.

### Old step 1 → lem-trf-positive-monoid-convolution-length

Choose nonzero generators $g_1,\ldots,g_s$ of $P$, discarding zero generators; $s=0$ exactly when $P=\{0\}$. For $p\ne0$, a decomposition $p=p_1+\cdots+p_l$ with $p_i\ne0$ satisfies $l\le\sum_i w(p_i)=w(p)$ because each $w(p_i)$ is a positive integer. Such a decomposition exists with $l=1$, so the possible lengths form a nonempty finite subset of $\{1,\ldots,w(p)\}$ and have an attained maximum $\nu(p)$. No nonempty sum of nonzero elements is $0$, since its weight is positive. For any $M\ge0$, an element of $P$ of weight at most $M$ is represented by a word of length at most $M$ in the $g_i$, since each generator has weight at least $1$. There are finitely many such words, including the empty word.

### Old step 2 → lem-trf-positive-monoid-convolution-length

For fixed $p$, if $u+v=p$ with $u,v\in P$, then $0\le w(u),w(v)\le w(p)$. Step 1 gives finitely many possible $u,v$, so convolution is defined. Similarly, the triples $u+v+t=p$ form a finite set; expanding both bracketings of a triple product gives the same finite sum $\sum_{u+v+t=p}a_ub_vc_t$. This proves associativity, and commutativity, distributivity and the identity $z^0$ follow from the corresponding finite-sum formulas. In particular $z^uz^v=z^{u+v}$ and $C(a)z^p$ has coefficient $a$ at $p$ and zero elsewhere. Cancellation in $P\subseteq G$ gives the shift formula: the coefficient of $z^sc$ at $p$ is $c_q$ if $p=s+q$ for some $q\in P$, and is zero if there is no such $q$; that $q$ is unique.

### Old step 3 → lem-trf-positive-monoid-convolution-length

Fix $n\ge1$ and put $S_n=\{p\in P:\nu(p)<n\}$. Any generator word representing $p$ is a decomposition into nonzero summands, so its length is at most $\nu(p)$; for $p=0$ use the empty word. Thus every $p\in S_n$ is the value of one of the finitely many generator words of length less than $n$, and $S_n$ is finite. This argument also covers $s=0$, when $S_n=\{0\}$.

### Old step 4 → lem-trf-finite-support-monomial-ideal-power

By [def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring], choose generators $t_1,\ldots,t_h$ of the ideal $\mathfrak m$. For every $k\ge1$, expanding products of $k$ finite sums from $\mathfrak m=(t_1,\ldots,t_h)$ shows directly that every element of $\mathfrak m^k$ is a finite $A$-linear combination of products $t_{j_1}\cdots t_{j_k}$. Conversely each such product lies in $\mathfrak m^k$ by the definition of ideal multiplication. For $k=0$ use the empty product $1$. When $h=0$ and $k>0$, the ideal is zero and its elements have the empty-sum expression. Only distributivity and the definition of generated ideals are used.

### Old step 5 → lem-trf-finite-support-monomial-ideal-power

For each $p\in S_n$, put $l=\nu(p)$ and choose a decomposition $p=p_1+\cdots+p_l$ attaining this length; when $p=0$, use the empty decomposition. Put $k=n-l>0$. The hypothesis and Step 4 express $a_p$ as a finite sum of terms $c\,t_{j_1}\cdots t_{j_k}$ with $c\in A$. Consequently $a_pz^p$ is the finite sum of $C(c)C(t_{j_1})\cdots C(t_{j_k})z^{p_1}\cdots z^{p_l}$. There are exactly $k+l=n$ displayed factors in $\mathfrak b$ after the scalar $C(c)$, so each term belongs to $\mathfrak b^n$. Summing over the finite set $S_n$ gives a family $a^{<}\in\mathfrak b^n$ equal to $a$ on $S_n$ and zero elsewhere.

### Old step 6 → lem-trf-high-length-prefix-division

Let $T=\{1,\ldots,s\}^n$, a finite set, and put $s_I=g_{i_1}+\cdots+g_{i_n}$ for $I=(i_1,\ldots,i_n)\in T$. If $\nu(p)\ge n$, choose a decomposition of $p$ into at least $n$ nonzero summands, write each of its first $n$ summands as a nonempty word in the $g_i$, and take one generator from each word. The remaining terms sum to $q\in P$, so $p=s_I+q$ for at least one $I\in T$. Fix an order on $T$ and assign $p$ to the first eligible $I$, meaning the first for which $p-s_I\in P$ in the ambient group $G$. This partitions $P\setminus S_n$ into subsets $E_I$. No $p$ is lost or counted twice. If $s=0$, there are no such $p$, so the partition and the sums below are empty.

### Old step 7 → lem-trf-high-length-prefix-division

For $I\in T$ define $c^I\in B$ by $c^I_q=a_{s_I+q}$ when $s_I+q\in E_I$, and $c^I_q=0$ otherwise. Every such family is an element of $B=A^P$; no convergence restriction is imposed. By the shift formula in Step 2, $z^{s_I}c^I$ equals $a$ on $E_I$ and is zero off $E_I$: cancellation ensures that each residual index contributes exactly once. Hence equality of coefficients gives $a-a^{<}=\sum_{I\in T}z^{s_I}c^I$. This is a finite sum, and $z^{s_I}=z^{g_{i_1}}\cdots z^{g_{i_n}}$ is a product of $n$ elements of $\mathfrak b$, so every summand belongs to $\mathfrak b^n$. Step 5 and closure under finite addition now give $a\in\mathfrak b^n$, as required.
