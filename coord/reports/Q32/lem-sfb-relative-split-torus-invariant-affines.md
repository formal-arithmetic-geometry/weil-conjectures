## Supporting arguments retained for subsequent splits

For the embedding input, Thomason Corollary 3.4(3) gives an equivariant immersion into a projective bundle for a normal quasi-projective X and a split reductive group over affine Noetherian S. A split torus satisfies that group hypothesis; S is separated because it is affine. No regularity or normality of S is imposed by case (3). To get the diagonal finite free projective coordinates in the new node, write a finite locally free T-module over affine S as its character summands P_m by expanding its coaction in Laurent monomials. Coassociativity makes the coefficient projections orthogonal idempotents, and the counit makes their sum the identity. A finite generating set of the underlying projective A-module has finite coefficient support, so only finitely many P_m occur; each is a finite projective direct summand. Choose a finite projective complement Q_m with P_m plus Q_m isomorphic to a finite free module. Give Q_m the SAME character m. Their finite sum is a finite free diagonal representation containing the original bundle equivariantly as a direct summand. Projectivization, using the dual if the quotient rather than line convention is chosen, is a closed immersion. This produces the new embedding statement. It does not supply the affine neighbourhood conclusion; the parent must construct a boundary-avoiding equation. The new node is unsplit and has no proof field content; this paragraph preserves the argument for its next worker.

For the boundary input, use the automorphism F(t,p)=(t,tp) of T times P over T. The projection T times P to P is smooth and open. An open surjection has inverse image of a closure equal to closure of the inverse image: any open neighbourhood upstairs has open image meeting the original subset, hence meets its inverse image. Invariance of W therefore makes T times closure(W) invariant under F. Smooth pullback of the reduced structures is reduced (Stacks 034E, descent.tex, lemma-reduced-local-smooth), so uniqueness of reduced induced closed structures identifies F(T times Z) with T times Z as closed subschemes. The same reasoning for the boundary, the complement of the invariant open W in Z, gives T times B. W is the open of reduced Z because a reduced locally closed subscheme is its reduced induced locally closed structure. This works even when A is nonreduced: T times B is smooth over the reduced B, rather than being asserted reduced over A. On each invariant chart D_+(z_i), write the restriction of a degree-d polynomial as f/z_i^d. The ideal of B is preserved by the Laurent coordinate coaction. Expanding its image and comparing the coefficients of the free Laurent monomial basis shows that every character component f_m/z_i^d also lies in the chart ideal (the denominator only shifts every weight by -d w_i). Thus f_m vanishes on every boundary chart. No freeness, flatness, or fibrewise weight decomposition of I(B)_d is needed. The node keeps this non-elementary invariant-boundary comparison as a separate obligation; it is not hidden in a long parent step.
