## Review by Q32

I read HTT Proposition 2.2.2.7 and its proof on printed p. 77, together with Remark 2.2.2.8. The proof first reduces to finite simplicial sets, then inducts on dimension and the number of top-dimensional simplices. The split follows that argument for the two given functors. I also read simplicial.tex at lemma-splitting-simplicial-sets (Stacks 017R), lemma-glue-simplex (018R) and lemma-add-simplices (018S), verifying the tag/label correspondence in tags/tags. No SGA source is used.

Step 1 uses the ordinary-colimit definition to identify both images of the empty diagram with the empty simplicial set. The definition of weak equivalence in def-cbc-enhanced-diagrams-cones makes its component and based-homotopy conditions vacuous. Initial-object uniqueness and realization of an isomorphism are elementary here; no assumption that P or Q preserves weak equivalences is inserted.

Step 2 states the nested induction, including both bases. The nondegenerate-decomposition node says existence and uniqueness of a simplex's nondegenerate ancestor, so absence of such ancestors implies emptiness. The simplex-gluing node explicitly defines the boundary by nonsurjective maps. Factoring a monotone map into its image shows that nondegenerate simplices of the standard simplex are exactly injections, and those in the boundary have dimension strictly less than d. This justifies applying the outer induction to the boundary, rather than incorrectly comparing its number of simplices with that of A.

Step 3 uses precisely the statement of lem-coef-finite-simplicial-top-simplex-removal: its subset has one fewer top simplex and has the three degree conditions needed by the existing Stacks 018R node. The latter's statement supplies the actual pushout with the characteristic map and includes d=0. I checked finiteness and the maximal-dimension hypothesis before removal, and each of the three gluing hypotheses afterwards. It is a tool lemma from sk-prop-h2, declared in split_report.tool_uses; it supplies an ordinary combinatorial pushout and does not replace any geometric unit route. No argument is borrowed from its proof.

Step 4 uses the first clause of lem-coef-sset-gluing-filtered-equivalences. It requires injections only for the boundary-to-simplex legs, not for the attaching maps into U. Both transformed legs are injections by the item's explicit preservation hypothesis. Its other three hypotheses are the outer induction, inner induction and the assumption on standard simplices. The colimit universal property and naturality equations identify the induced comparison with eta_A, on both legs, without an unstated compatibility claim.

Step 5 explicitly proves smallness, nonemptiness, directedness and the union assertion for the poset of finite simplicial subsets. Degeneracy witnesses stay inside subsets because they are faces. Factoring an order map as a surjection and injection gives a finite list containing all nondegenerate simplices of the image of a simplex. This is a finite combinatorial argument, not an infinity-categorical colimit-assembly theorem; the final degreewise union is checked by its ordinary universal property. It uses only the cited decomposition and colimit notions. Thus no hidden finite-subset lemma was moved to a new node.

Step 6 uses the second clause of the same gluing-and-filtered node, whose hypotheses are exactly a small nonempty directed poset and objectwise weak homotopy equivalences. All have been checked. Preservation of colimits identifies the endpoint objects; naturality on each inclusion identifies the endpoint map. There is no replacement of an ordinary colimit by an infinity-categorical one, and no enlargement to a non-small category.
