## Review by Q32

I read Conrad's statement at printed p.126 and the whole proof through p.131. The item keeps arbitrary S, separated commutative Z, merely sheaf-valued E, and an fppf central exact sequence. For the new torus-pairing node I read the D-prime paragraph at p.127. Its hypotheses and conclusion are exactly the pairing vanishing used there. In its source argument, restrictions to mu_n vanish because multiplication by n on Gm is an fppf epimorphism; the Laurent-polynomial torsion family is schematically dense over any ring, so separatedness of Z gives global equality. This also checks nonreduced bases and nonsmooth targets; a statement about field-valued points alone would not suffice.

For the weighted-additive node I read the additive commutator argument at p.127 and Lemma 4.3.2 at p.128. Its statement includes commutativity of F, not an unstated premise for the splitting construction. Invariance of the commutator under simultaneous weight-two scaling reduces fppf-locally to invariance under simultaneous scaling by any unit. Biadditivity and the choices t, t+1, and (t+1)^(-1)-t^(-1)-1 units then kill the pairing. These unit choices have nonempty open fibres even in characteristic two (the relevant polynomial is not identically zero). After commutativity, the action-minus-identity endomorphism kills Z and descends to a lift of multiplication by t^k-1 on Ga; choosing it invertible gives the section. Its equivariance comes from commuting torus actions. An invariant additive map Ga -> Z is trivial: invariance under square scaling, with a square root adjoined fppf-locally, gives f(au)=f(u) for any unit a; choose a and a-1 units and subtract to get f((a-1)u)=1. This proves the final assertion used for uniqueness as well as uniqueness/descent of the local sections. Weight -2 is obtained by inverting t. No square root is assumed etale.

For the lifted-relations node I read Lemma 4.3.3 and the calculation following it, pp.128--130. It assumes the commutativity of D-prime and already supplied equivariant x-prime,y-prime; it constructs only h-prime and the two explicit relations, not the global splitting. The use of squares in proving h-prime multiplicative is legitimate fppf-locally in every characteristic. Removing the unit condition on u uses a local decomposition u=u'+u'' with u',u'',1+u''v units; over each algebraically closed residue field it excludes only finitely many values. The opposite-order formula follows by inverting the first formula with both parameters negated. I retained both formulas so step 4 does not silently use an unquoted comparison.

For the open-cell node I read the final word argument at pp.130--131. It requires partial multiplicativity for every pair whose product remains in the cell, not just on a smaller unspecified dense subset. The elementary root subgroups and the diagonal subgroup all lie in this cell, together with their inverses. The source's two-cell generation can equivalently be checked by right multiplying by y(1): if d is not invertible at a point then c is, and d+c is invertible there, since ad-bc=1. Therefore Zariski-locally every matrix is a product of cell points. For finitely many partial products, the intersection of the corresponding translated cells is a smooth surjective open over the base, hence an fppf cover with local sections: each geometric fibre is a nonempty intersection of opens in irreducible SL2. Choosing one common translating point puts all partial products in the cell. Partial multiplicativity then identifies the two lifted words after left cancellation. Unique gluing gives the claimed homomorphism for arbitrary H; representability or separatedness of H is not required. This verifies that the new node is true for the exact sheaf-valued case needed by the item.
