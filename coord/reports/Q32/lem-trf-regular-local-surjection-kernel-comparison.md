## Previous argument retained for subsequent workers

Old Step 1: Put $I=\ker(v\circ u)$ and $J=\ker u$, so $J\subseteq I$ and $I/J$ identifies with $\ker v$. Because the maps are surjective and local, every kernel is contained in the maximal ideal of its source; because $U$ and $V$ are regular local, they are Noetherian and have finite Krull dimension. The zero ideal is allowed, so all later parameter and empty-sequence arguments include the isomorphism cases.

Old Step 2: Apply the regular-local-quotient parameter theorem to $U\twoheadrightarrow V$. It gives a full regular system of parameters $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ of $U$ and an integer $a$ with $J=(x_1,\ldots,x_a)$. The full parameter sequence is $U$-regular, and each prefix quotient is regular local; since $U/(x_1,\ldots,x_a)\cong V$, its dimension clause gives $a=\dim U-\dim V$. Here $a=0$ exactly when $J=0$.

Old Step 3: We use the following Cohen–Macaulay criterion for either regular local ring $R=U$ or $R=V$: the parameter-regular-sequence theorem supplies a regular sequence of length $\dim R$ in the maximal ideal, so the definition of depth gives $\operatorname{depth}(R)\ge\dim R$; the depth bound [lem-weil-pbc-depth-le-support-dimension] applied to the finite module $R$ gives $\operatorname{depth}(R)\le\dim R$. For any list $z_1,\ldots,z_r\in\mathfrak m_R$, the auxiliary criterion therefore says that $z_1,\ldots,z_r$ is regular exactly when $\dim(R/(z_1,\ldots,z_r))=\dim R-r$.

Old Step 4: Assume $\ker v$ is generated by a $V$-regular sequence $y_1,\ldots,y_b$. The criterion in Step 3 gives $b=\dim V-\dim W$. Lift the $y_i$ to elements of $\mathfrak m_U$ (a lift of an element of $\mathfrak m_V$ cannot be a unit), and adjoin the $a$ parameter generators $x_1,\ldots,x_a$ of $J$ from Step 2. Since $I/J\cong\ker v$, the list $x_1,\ldots,x_a,\widetilde y_1,\ldots,\widetilde y_b$ generates $I$. Its length is $a+b=\dim U-\dim W$, so the fixed-list criterion of Step 3, applied to $U$ and this list, makes it a $U$-regular sequence. This proves the forward implication, including $a=0$ or $b=0$.

Old Step 5: Before the induction, establish the exact generator count. If a regular sequence $z_1,\ldots,z_b\in\mathfrak m_U$ generates $I$, the conormal-freeness lemma gives the basis $[z_1],\ldots,[z_b]$ of $I/I^2$ over $U/I$. Since $I\subseteq\mathfrak m_U$, we have $I^2\subseteq\mathfrak m_UI$, and quotienting this basis modulo the ideal $\mathfrak m_U/I$ identifies $(I/I^2)/(\mathfrak m_U/I)(I/I^2)$ with $I/\mathfrak m_UI$ and sends the coordinate basis of $(U/I)^b$ to that of $(U/\mathfrak m_U)^b$. Thus the images of the given $z_i$ form a basis of $I/\mathfrak m_UI$ over $\kappa=U/\mathfrak m_U$, and its dimension is exactly $b$. Every generating list of $I$ maps to a spanning list in this vector space and therefore has at least $b$ elements; the given regular sequence is a minimal generating list. For $b=0$, both modules and $I$ are zero.

Old Step 6: For the converse, induct on the nonnegative integer $a=\dim U-\dim V$, for all surjective local maps of the stated kind. If $a=0$, Step 2 gives $J=0$, so $U\cong V$ and the regular generating sequence transports across this isomorphism. Suppose $a>0$ and $I$ is generated by a $U$-regular sequence $z_1,\ldots,z_b$. Step 3 gives $b=\dim U-\dim W$, and Step 5 gives $\dim_\kappa(I/\mathfrak m_UI)=b$. Choose $x=x_1\in J$ from Step 2. The parameter list is a minimal generating list of $\mathfrak m_U$ by the parameter theorem; by Nakayama its images form a basis of $\mathfrak m_U/\mathfrak m_U^2$. Hence the class of $x$ there is nonzero. Because $I\subseteq\mathfrak m_U$, membership $x\in\mathfrak m_UI$ would imply $x\in\mathfrak m_U^2$, a contradiction. Thus $[x]\ne0$ in $I/\mathfrak m_UI$ and in particular $b>0$.

Old Step 7: Extend the nonzero vector $[x]$ to a $\kappa$-basis $[x],e_2,\ldots,e_b$ of the $b$-dimensional space $I/\mathfrak m_UI$. Lift each $e_i$ to $z'_i\in I$. The ideal $I$ is a finite $U$-module because the original $b$ elements generate it. Nakayama, applied to this finite module and the displayed basis, now says that $x,z'_2,\ldots,z'_b$ generate $I$. This list has exactly $b$ entries, and every entry lies in $\mathfrak m_U$. No regularity of this replacement list is assumed.

Old Step 8: Put $U'=U/xU$ and let $I'$ be the image of $I$. The parameter theorem makes $U'$ regular local of dimension $\dim U-1$. Since $x\in J$, the maps factor as surjective local maps $U'\twoheadrightarrow V\twoheadrightarrow W$, with the maximal ideal of $U'$ equal to $\mathfrak m_U/(x)$ by the quotient-local-structure lemma. Step 7 gives the generating list $\bar z'_2,\ldots,\bar z'_b$ of $I'$, with exactly $b-1$ entries in that maximal ideal, and $U'/I'\cong W$. Consequently $\dim(U'/I')=\dim W=(\dim U-1)-(b-1)=\dim U'-(b-1)$. The fixed-list criterion of Step 3 applied to the regular local ring $U'$ makes this very list a $U'$-regular sequence. The dimension difference for $U'\twoheadrightarrow V$ is $a-1$, so the induction hypothesis gives a $V$-regular sequence generating $\ker v$. This completes the converse. If $b=1$, the quotient list is empty and $I'=0$; the original empty-sequence case cannot enter the $a>0$ branch by Step 6.
