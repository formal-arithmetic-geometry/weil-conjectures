Original step 1 (uses: pqf-lem-coherent-ft-locnoeth, lem-weil-pbc-nakayama-local-generators, lem-trf-fitting-ideal-generate-locally):

By [pqf-lem-coherent-ft-locnoeth], $F$ is quasi-coherent of finite type. Its stalk at any $s$ is finite: take the germs of finitely many local generators. Nakayama [lem-weil-pbc-nakayama-local-generators] says that the stalk is generated by at most $r$ elements exactly when its residue vector space has dimension at most $r$: lift a residue basis and pad with zeros; conversely images of $r$ generators span the residue space. By clause (2) of [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-generate-locally], these conditions are equivalent to $s\notin|Z_r|$, where $Z_r$ is cut out by $\operatorname{Fit}_r(F)$. Thus $W=W_r(F)=S\setminus|Z_r|$ is open, and $F|_W$ is locally generated by at most $r$ sections.

Original step 2 (uses: def-trf-fitting-ideal-sheaf, lem-trf-fitting-ideal-generate-locally, lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-ideal-closed-subscheme):

For $r>0$, let $\mathcal I=\operatorname{Fit}_{r-1}(F)|_W$, using [def-trf-fitting-ideal-sheaf] and the affine kernel-minor description explicitly stated in [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-generate-locally]. It is a quasi-coherent ideal: restriction to an open is the same affine ideal family, with the same localization compatibility. For $r=0$ put $\mathcal I=0$; here $F|_W=0$, since locally zero sections generate it. In both cases [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-ideal-closed-subscheme] supplies a closed immersion $i:C\hookrightarrow W$ with ideal $\mathcal I$, having affine model $\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathcal I(U))$ over $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq W$. For $r=0$ this is $C=W$.

Original step 3 (uses: lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-finite-type-affine, lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction, lem-weil-pbc-tilde-pullback-base-change, lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change):

Suppose $r>0$. Cover $W$ by affine opens $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ on which $r$ sections generate $F$, padding a smaller set by zeros. By [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-finite-type-affine] and [lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction], $M=F(U)$ is finite and $F|_U\cong\widetilde M$. The chosen sections give a surjection $\pi:A^r\twoheadrightarrow M$: the associated sheaf map is surjective by generation; its cokernel is the sheaf of the module cokernel, and the fully faithful affine correspondence detects that this cokernel is zero. For any $h:T\to W$, cover $T$ by affine opens $V=\operatorname{Spec}B$ mapping into such $U$. By [lem-weil-pbc-tilde-pullback-base-change], $(h^*(F|_W))|_V\cong\widetilde N$ for $N=B\otimes_A M$. Clause (2) of [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change] gives a surjection $B^r\twoheadrightarrow N$ and
$$\operatorname{Fit}_{r-1}(N)=\mathcal I(U)B.$$
Here the ideal on the right is the image ideal under $A\to B$; the affine description of Step 2 identifies the source ideal with the presentation ideal of $M$. The tensor order in the cited statement is changed by $m\otimes b\mapsto b\otimes m$. No flatness of $B$ is used.

Original step 4 (uses: lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction, lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change):

If $h^*(F|_W)$ is locally free of rank $r>0$, refine the affine opens $V$ of Step 3 so that it is free there. By the affine correspondence [lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction], $N\cong B^r$. Compute its $(r-1)$st Fitting ideal using the identity presentation $B^r\twoheadrightarrow B^r$: its kernel is zero, so all the $1\times1$ kernel minors are zero. Presentation independence [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change](1) and Step 3 therefore imply $\mathcal I(U)B=0$ on these opens. These opens cover $T$, so the image of $h^*\mathcal I\to\mathcal O_T$ is zero.

Original step 5 (uses: lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction):

Conversely, suppose $h^*\mathcal I\to\mathcal O_T$ has zero image and $r>0$. In Step 3 every coordinate of every element of $\ker(B^r\twoheadrightarrow N)$ is a $1\times1$ kernel minor. The equality of its ideal with $\mathcal I(U)B=0$ forces each coordinate to be zero. Hence the kernel is zero and the surjection is an isomorphism $B^r\cong N$. The affine correspondence [lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction] gives $h^*(F|_W)|_V\cong\mathcal O_V^r$. These charts cover $T$, proving local freeness of rank $r$. If $r=0$, both image-ideal vanishing and rank-zero local freeness hold automatically for $h:T\to W$, since $\mathcal I=0$ and $F|_W=0$.

Original step 6 (uses: lem-trf-fitting-ideal-generate-locally, lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-monomorphism, lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-lift-ideal-kills-sections):

Let $g:T\to S$ be arbitrary. If $g^*F$ is locally free of rank $r$, it is locally generated by at most $r$ sections, so [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-generate-locally](1) gives $g=j\circ h$ for $j:W\hookrightarrow S$; this $h$ is unique by [lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-monomorphism]. On the affine charts of Step 3, $h^*(F|_W)$ and $g^*F$ are the same tensor module: the maps $A\to B$ agree because $g=j\circ h$. Steps 4-5 (including $r=0$) identify local freeness with vanishing of the image of $h^*\mathcal I$. This vanishing sends every section of $\mathcal I(U)$ to zero on $h^{-1}(U)$, checked on the affine cover in Step 3 and by the sheaf axiom. Thus [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-lift-ideal-kills-sections] supplies a lift $T\to C$. Conversely, a lift through $C$ kills $\mathcal I$ because the affine structure maps of $i$ are the quotients $A\to A/\mathcal I(U)$, so Step 5 gives rank-$r$ local freeness. The lift through $i$ is unique: after covering $T$ by affine opens mapping into the quotient charts, its ring maps out of $A/\mathcal I(U)$ are uniquely determined by their composites with the surjective map from $A$. The underlying map is forced by the closed embedding, and these local equalities give equality of scheme morphisms. Together with uniqueness through $j$, this proves the stated unique factorization through $C\to S$.

Original step 7 (uses: lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-open-subscheme-noetherian, lem-weil-pbc-qc-open-immersion-finite-type, pqf-lem-lft-fp-locnoeth, lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-monomorphism, def-weil-quasi-separated-morphism, def-weil-pbc-scheme-conventions):

For every affine open $U\subseteq S$, the open $W\cap U$ of the Noetherian scheme $S$ is quasi-compact by [lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-open-subscheme-noetherian]. Hence $j:W\to S$ is quasi-compact. By [lem-weil-pbc-qc-open-immersion-finite-type], $j$ is of finite type; [pqf-lem-lft-fp-locnoeth] makes it locally of finite presentation, since $S$ is locally Noetherian. It is also quasi-separated: it is a monomorphism by [lem-weil-pbc-open-immersion-monomorphism]; for a monomorphism the two projections $W\times_S W\to W$ agree, and the diagonal and either projection are inverse, as checked on both components of the fibre product. Thus the diagonal is an isomorphism and in particular quasi-compact. By [def-weil-quasi-separated-morphism] and [def-weil-pbc-scheme-conventions], $j$ is of finite presentation. The same Noetherian-open lemma says that $W$ is locally Noetherian.

Original step 8 (uses: lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-finite, pqf-lem-finite-fp-locnoeth, lem-sbc-locally-finite-presentation-composition, def-weil-pbc-scheme-conventions):

The closed immersion $i:C\to W$ is finite by [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-finite], hence of finite presentation by [pqf-lem-finite-fp-locnoeth], since $W$ is locally Noetherian. By [lem-sbc-locally-finite-presentation-composition], $q=j\circ i$ is locally of finite presentation. For every affine open $U\subseteq S$, $q^{-1}(U)$ is a closed subspace of the quasi-compact space $W\cap U$ of Step 7; it is quasi-compact (adjoin the open complement to an open cover and take a finite subcover). Thus $q$ is quasi-compact. Step 6 proves that $i$ is a monomorphism by the quotient-chart argument, so $q$ is a monomorphism too. The diagonal-inverse calculation of Step 7 shows that $q$ is quasi-separated. Consequently $q$ is of finite presentation in the full sense of [def-weil-pbc-scheme-conventions].

Original step 9 (uses: ):

If every fibre dimension is at most $r$, the defining condition gives $W_r(F)=S$. The closed immersion $C\hookrightarrow W$ of Step 2 is then a closed immersion into $S$, proving the last assertion. The constructions include the empty scheme and the zero module; no step restricts the test scheme $T$ to be Noetherian.
