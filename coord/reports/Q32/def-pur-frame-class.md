## STATEMENT-DISPUTED

The imported frame definition refers to `def-pur-affine-line-datum` for Kummer classes for every coefficient index n. That dependency's current statement assumes e(E/Q_l) divides n, and its current `lem-pur-finite-kummer-level` uses N=n/e. This cannot supply the advertised class at, for example, E=Q_l(sqrt(l)), e=2, n=1: n/e=1/2 is not an integer, although A=O_E/varpi is a legitimate nonzero finite coefficient ring and its Kummer class exists. The problem is the domain of the imported cross-reference, not the existence of frame classes.

I repaired the reference by writing out the same boundary prescription at the fixed, always valid exponent m=l^n. Since l is in (varpi), mA=0, including in the example above. This is not a restriction to e|n or a change to the coefficient twist: A(1) remains exactly the inverse-limit/tensor twist of `def-coefficient-twists-detailed`.

Here is the short coefficient calculation supporting the construction. Put M=Z_l(1). The twist definition gives A(1)=(M tensor O_E) tensor A=M tensor A. Since mA=0 this is (M/mM) tensor_(Z/m) A. Clause (2) of `lem-pur-twist-sheaf-locally-free` identifies M/mM with mu_m; its tensor over the completed coefficient sheaf is the same cokernel of multiplication by m. This gives the displayed theta and the actual coefficient map mu_m -> A(1), v |-> theta^{-1}(v tensor 1), without a flatness or freeness assumption on A over Z/m. These are tensor associativity and the universal property of a quotient applied to an existing theorem, not a new lemma or a clause moved to a new node.

On an index where the old affine-line datum is defined, let N=n/e. The morphism from the l^n-Kummer sequence to the l^N-Kummer sequence has middle map x |-> x^(l^(n-N)) and right map the identity; on kernels it is the same power map. Naturality of the connecting morphism in `lem-weil-pbc-right-derived-universal-delta-functor` sends delta_n(u) to delta_N(u). The projections from M have exactly these transition maps, and inserting 1_A therefore gives the same A(1)-class. Thus the formula preserves the original concrete prescription wherever that prescription was already defined.
