## Retained full original proof

### Original step 1

Put $\mathcal C=\operatorname{Mod}(R)$ and $T=\Phi^*$, so $\mathcal A=\mathcal C^T$, and let $U:\mathcal A\to\mathcal C$ forget the structure [def-weil-endofunctor-fixed-pair-category]. The inverse geometric automorphism gives a quasi-inverse of $T$: the coefficient identification is precomposition of continuous functions, which preserves the locally finite-dimensional-value condition in both directions. Thus $T$ is an additive exact equivalence. Kernels and cokernels of an intertwining map acquire their structure isomorphisms by the kernel and cokernel universal properties; these are invertible since $T$ preserves those universal properties. Their underlying objects are precisely the ordinary kernels and cokernels. The abelian axioms therefore hold in $\mathcal A$ because they hold after the faithful functor $U$, and $U$ is exact and detects zero objects. Hence $H^n(UC)=U H^n(C)$, and $U$ detects quasi-isomorphisms. Products are likewise formed on underlying sheaves: the product structure is the unique map with components $\theta_i T(\pi_i)$; it is invertible because an equivalence preserves products. No exactness of infinite products is asserted or used.

Uses: def-weil-endofunctor-fixed-pair-category

### Original step 2

The small ringed site $(\bar X_{\mathrm{proet}},R)$ satisfies [lem-pur-ringed-site-grothendieck-abelian], so $\mathcal C$ has all small coproducts and exact filtered colimits. Choose a quasi-inverse of $T$ and form integer powers $T^n$, with the natural successor identifications $T T^n\simeq T^{n+1}$. Define $L(M)$ to have underlying object $\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbf Z}T^nM$ and structure map given by these identifications followed by reindexing $n\mapsto n+1$. Define $L(f)$ by $T^n f$ on the summands. The successor identifications show that this map intertwines the structure maps, and identities, composition and addition hold on every summand. This constructs an additive functor $L:\mathcal C\to\mathcal A$.

Uses: lem-pur-ringed-site-grothendieck-abelian

### Original step 3

Restriction to the $n=0$ summand gives $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(L(M),F)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal C}(M,UF)$. Its inverse extends $a_0:M\to UF$ recursively to $a_n:T^nM\to UF$: for $n\geq0$ put $a_{n+1}=\theta_F T(a_n)$, using the successor identifications. For $n<0$ the equation $\theta_F T(a_n)=a_{n+1}$ uniquely determines $a_n$, since $\theta_F$ is invertible and $T$ is fully faithful. The coproduct universal property glues the $a_n$ to a map, and precisely these equations say it is equivariant. Conversely equivariance forces the same recursions, so restriction and extension are inverse. Pre- and postcomposition respect the recursions, giving naturality in $M,F$. Thus $L\dashv U$.

### Original step 4

The functor $L$ is exact. Indeed each $T^n$ is exact; applying $UL=\bigoplus_n T^n$ to a short exact sequence gives the filtered colimit, over finite subsets of $\mathbf Z$, of its finite direct sums. Each finite direct sum sequence is short exact, and filtered colimits preserve exactness by [lem-pur-ringed-site-grothendieck-abelian]. Step 1 reflects this exactness back to $\mathcal A$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-right-adjoint-preserves-kinjective] to the additive adjunction $L\dashv U$: all its hypotheses have now been verified, so $UI$ is K-injective whenever $I$ is K-injective in $\mathcal A$. In particular it applies to every representative $I$ allowed in the item, not merely to degreewise injective resolutions.

### Original step 5

For the given equivariant quasi-isomorphism $\alpha:A[0]\to J$ and K-injective $I$, consider precomposition $c_{\alpha,I}:H^\bullet(J,I)\to H^\bullet(A[0],I)$. Degree $n$ sends $f$ to $f\alpha$, and $d(f\alpha)=(df)\alpha$ since $\alpha$ is a chain map. It is equivariant: substituting the conjugation formula for $H$ and $\alpha\theta_A=\theta_J T(\alpha)$ gives $\theta_I T(f\alpha)\theta_A^{-1}=(\theta_I T(f)\theta_J^{-1})\alpha$. By step 1, forgetting the product Hom complex gives the ordinary product internal-Hom complex over $R$, with the same differential. Step 4 makes $UI$ K-injective and step 1 makes $U\alpha$ a quasi-isomorphism. Apply [lem-pur-rhom-well-defined] on the small representative site with $s=\operatorname{id}_{UI}$ and $t=U\alpha$. The site is small in the common universe containing $R$, and that node allows an arbitrary commutative ring sheaf. It follows that $U c_{\alpha,I}$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Since $U$ detects quasi-isomorphisms, $c_{\alpha,I}$ itself is one, and $Q(c_{\alpha,I})$ is an isomorphism in the native $D(\mathcal A)$ [def-weil-abstract-derived-category].

### Original step 6

In degree $n$, projection to $p=0$ identifies $\prod_p H(A[0]^p,I^{p+n})$ with $H(A,I^n)$: all other factors are $H(0,I^{p+n})=0$, since the only linear map from the zero sheaf is zero. The inverse inserts a section into the $p=0$ factor and zero elsewhere. These maps commute with the Weil structures. Since $d_{A[0]}=0$, the Hom differential becomes just $H(A,d_I)$; hence the projections define a natural isomorphism of complexes $H^\bullet(A[0],I)\simeq H_A(I)$. The same linear-map calculation shows that $H_A$ is additive and preserves chain homotopies. The hypothesis of [def-sfb-right-derived-functor] holds for $\mathcal A$: it is Grothendieck abelian [def-sp-grothendieck-abelian]. Indeed, since $T$ is an equivalence it preserves colimits, so a colimit of fixed pairs is the colimit of the underlying sheaves with the colimit structure isomorphism; filtered colimits in $\mathcal A$ are exact because $U$ detects exactness (step 1) and they are exact in $\mathcal C$ [lem-pur-ringed-site-grothendieck-abelian]; and if $G$ generates $\mathcal C$ [lem-pur-ringed-site-grothendieck-abelian], then $L(G)$ generates $\mathcal A$: for a nonzero $f:F\to F'$ in $\mathcal A$ pick $g:G\to UF$ with $U(f)g\neq0$, whose adjoint $g^\sharp:L(G)\to F$ (step 3) has $fg^\sharp\neq0$. Hence every complex of $\mathcal A$ has a K-injective resolution [lem-sfb-grothendieck-k-injective]; in particular the representatives $I$, $J$ exist. By the K-injective computation of the total right derived functor [def-sfb-right-derived-functor], $Q(H_A(I))$ computes $(RH_A)(N)$, using the chosen identification $QI\simeq N$. Composing this computation with $Q(c_{\alpha,I})$ is the asserted comparison.

### Original step 7

Check independence and naturality inside $\mathcal A$. By [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-hom-derived], maps into any K-injective target in the derived category have chain representatives unique up to homotopy. For two choices $\alpha:A[0]\to J$ and $\alpha':A[0]\to J'$, represent $Q\alpha'\,(Q\alpha)^{-1}$ by $u:J\to J'$. Then $u\alpha\simeq\alpha'$ and $u$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Consequently precomposition by $u$ followed by $c_{\alpha,I}$ equals $c_{\alpha',I}$ after localization. For targets $I,I'$ representing $N$, the same cited bijection represents the identity of $N$ by a homotopy equivalence $v:I\to I'$. Postcomposition by $v$ commutes with precomposition by $\alpha$, by associativity. These comparisons are unique after passing to homotopy and satisfy the composition identities there. To see explicitly that internal Hom respects the homotopies just used, a source homotopy $h$ induces the degree-$-1$ homotopy $f\mapsto(-1)^n f h$ on degree-$n$ Hom terms, and a target homotopy $h'$ induces $f\mapsto h'f$; substituting $d(f)=d_I f-(-1)^n f d_J$ gives the required differences of maps. All are equivariant because the original homotopies lie in $\mathcal A$. For $a:A\to B$, lift $Q\alpha_B\,Q(a[0])\,(Q\alpha_A)^{-1}$ to $u:J_A\to J_B$; then $u\alpha_A\simeq\alpha_B a[0]$, which gives the contravariant naturality square by the same calculation. A derived arrow $N\to N'$ is represented between K-injective targets, giving covariant naturality by associativity. Thus the comparison is independent of both replacements and natural in both variables, in the native derived category.
