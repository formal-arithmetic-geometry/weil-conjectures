## Review by Q84

I read Milne, Algebraic Groups, the paragraph following Definition 17.95 on printed p.382 and the full proof of Theorem 17.96 on p.383. Its fixed-point argument chooses a Frobenius power fixing x, computes two iterates, kills a finite-order inner factor, and contains the desired fixed set in the fixed set of a Frobenius power. Smoothness and connectedness occur elsewhere in the proof of Lang surjectivity; they are absent from this particular argument. I adapted that argument to the functor of points of the given affine group scheme and marked the proof origin as agent for the supplied details. No SGA is used.

In step 1 I checked that the statement of `lem-pc-affine-finite-type-algebra` gives precisely finite type for the global coordinate algebra, with the present affine and finite-type hypotheses. I also read Stacks Tag 01T2, `morphisms.tex`, label `lemma-locally-finite-type-characterize`. The finite-type-algebra definition explicitly gives polynomial generation, so evaluation on generators determines a point without invoking an uncited representation theorem.

In step 2 I checked the algebraic-closure definition gives algebraicity of every coordinate and that `lem-weil-pbc-algebraic-generators-finite-extension` applies to these finitely many elements inside the given closure. I read Stacks Tag 09GH, `fields.tex`, label `lemma-algebraic-finitely-generated`, lines 693ff. Its conclusion is finite-dimensionality, not cardinal finiteness; the proof supplies the basis-coordinate bijection with a finite power of k to obtain the latter.

In step 3 the cited Frobenius definition really gives a k-automorphism of the closure, and the cited group-scheme definition really gives its group-valued functor of points. Naturality of the structural k-morphisms yields compatibility with products and inverse. Frobenius maps the finite field L into itself; injectivity makes its restriction a permutation. Repetition among powers and cancellation gives a positive exponent fixing L, hence x. No uniform exponent fixing the entire algebraic closure is claimed.

In step 4 I reread all three clauses of `lem-weil-unique-subfield-of-closure`. Its first clause gives exactly the finite root field for every positive n; the statement's finite-field datum supplies its prime-power hypotheses. Evaluation injects H_n into that finite field to the t-th power. The subgroup assertion is written out from the Frobenius homomorphism property, not taken from an uncited geometric fixed-locus theorem.

Steps 5 and 6 use the already established facts only. I checked the order of the noncommuting factors in y_n and wrote the induction with x sigma(y_n). Because sigma^m fixes x it fixes every sigma^i(x), hence y_m. Finite H_m gives y finite order by repetition of powers and cancellation; no assertion that arbitrary geometric points lie in a finite subgroup is needed. The second induction uses sigma^m(y)=y, and produces f^(mr)=sigma^(mr) on every geometric point. Step 7 proves the fixed-set containment and applies finiteness of H_(mr), with mr positive. These three steps are elementary group calculations, not new lemmas or citations to source proofs.
