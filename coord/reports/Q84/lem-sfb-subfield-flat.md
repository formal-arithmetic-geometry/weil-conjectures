## Argument for the new worker

Use the internal/sectionwise equivalence [def-weil-pbc-internal-abelian-sheaf-module-equivalence] for R and B; it preserves section groups and restriction maps identically. Its size and monoidal-local-equivalence hypotheses are explicit in the new statement. By [lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-limits-sectionwise], limits in these module categories exist and have the compatible-family section groups. Restriction has the same underlying groups, maps and restrictions and preserves limits by [sk-def-sfb-change-of-scalars].

Let D:J→Mod_R be finite, with limit (L,p_j), and let K=lim_j T(D_j) in Mod_B. Form the actual B-linear comparison a:T(L)→K with projections T(p_j). Restrict it, apply the given alpha_L and the limit isomorphism induced by alpha_(D_j), and identify Res K with lim Res(TD). Naturality alpha_(D_j) Res(Tp_j)=(p_j)^n alpha_L shows that the resulting map is precisely L^n→lim_j(D_j^n), not just some map between isomorphic objects. On sections it sends (x_i)_i to ((p_j x_i)_i)_j. Its inverse takes a compatible tuple family ((x_(i,j))_i)_j to the tuple whose ith entry is the unique section of L represented by (x_(i,j))_j. These maps are R-linear, inverse, and commute with restriction maps by their coordinate formulas. Hence a is bijective on sections.

Its inverse is B-linear: for a section b of B and y of K, a(b a^(-1)y)=b y=a(a^(-1)(by)); injectivity gives b a^(-1)y=a^(-1)(by). Additivity follows the same way. The sectionwise inverses commute with restrictions by uniqueness, so they form a B-module-sheaf inverse, and the equivalence returns an inverse in internal B-modules. Thus the actual comparison a is invertible. This treats n=0 (all coordinate tuples are empty, hence zero) and J empty (the limit in either module category is zero) without excluding them. These are the full old internal/sectionwise, coordinate-reordering, naturality, inverse-linearity and restriction-compatibility arguments, not merely the slogan “finite sums are exact.”

This extraction is needed because the whole argument includes checking the encoding equivalence, constructing the canonical comparison, proving its identification using naturality, and lifting its inverse back to the target module category. It is a separately formalizable argument of comparable size to the existing tensor-coordinate and monoidal-transport tasks, rather than a new node for a bare rearrangement of indices.
