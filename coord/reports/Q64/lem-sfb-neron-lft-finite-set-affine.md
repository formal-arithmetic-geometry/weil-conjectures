I checked the three levels in SETUP, the worker and stage-1 rules, GOAL, and delivery-errors B–E. This is a single-item stage-1 split; only the sandbox has been written. Each new statement introduces all objects, defines the relative group/action data explicitly (the existing group-scheme definition has a field base), explains separatedness and Dedekind domain inline, and cites the Cartier-ideal and boundary-line definitions valid on arbitrary schemes. The curve-only divisor definition was deliberately not used. All three new nodes have empty proofs and proof dependency lists. The non-elementary assembly steps are below 2000 characters each; none hides an uncited geometric lemma.

I read BLR 10.1 Definition 1 and its following paragraph and Proposition 4's proof for the item; 6.4 Proposition 2 and the entire argument after Lemma 4 for the affine and ampleness inputs; and 6.6 Theorem 2 and its open-image/finite-type argument for saturation. I also read local Stacks properties.tex at lemma-ample-finite-set-in-affine (Tag 01ZY) and morphisms.tex at lemma-ample-over-affine (Tag 01VK). The source for the dense-affine lemma is the construction inside the proof of 6.4/1, whose construction uses smooth separated finite type over a Dedekind base, not the torsor law. The source for the action criterion is the broader Proposition 2, not merely the theorem about torsors.

Step 1 cites the full statement of lem-sfb-neron-lft-neutral-affine-product-chart: it really gives an open neutral subgroup and a surjective affine-product map on base neighbourhoods, without assuming connectedness of V. The proof of quasi-compactness from those maps is written out by pulling back covers and taking finite subcovers, then using the quasi-compact affine base. def-weil-finite-type-morphism exactly identifies quasi-compact plus locally finite type with finite type. I opened lem-weil-pbc-smooth-restrict-open-subschemes; clause (a) applies to this open subgroup over S. Restriction of the closed diagonal gives separatedness. The connected-fibre assertion here is the defining property of the geometric neutral component, not a claim about all of N.

Step 2 uses affine neighbourhoods from def-pc-scheme only to choose the finite union W; it does not claim W affine. The new saturation node takes a quasi-compact W and exactly the H constructed in Step 1. Its conclusion supplies every geometric property subsequently used: openness, finite type, smoothness, separatedness, H-stability and fibre-component orbits. I checked that it is not a restatement of the item: it gives no ample module or affine neighbourhood containing W, and works over any affine Noetherian base with a supplied neutral subgroup. The argument to be split later is that (h,w) maps to (h,hw), identifying the action map with a smooth projection; the image is open, is quasi-compact as an image of H times W, and inherits local finite type. Group identities give stability; on geometric fibres a component is a coset of the neutral component, and h=yx^{-1} carries x to y. The source calls on exactly these saturation facts, so I made them a separate geometric statement rather than hiding them in an elementary step.

Step 3 uses the new dense-affine node on this finite-type X. The node states both geometric-fibre density and an effective Cartier boundary, not just existence of an affine open or normality. I checked its expected proof against BLR: around each closed base point select an affine open through generic points of the total space and that fibre, then remove other vertical boundary divisors from the base neighbourhood. A finite base cover is enough. Intersect the resulting generic affine opens, take the closure of their generic effective boundary divisor, and remove that closure. Over each base neighbourhood this open is obtained from the chosen affine by removing a Cartier divisor, hence is affine; affineness is local on the affine base. The horizontal boundary misses fibre components. Smoothness over the regular Dedekind base gives regularity and makes the divisor Cartier. These are substantive inputs deliberately left in the unsplit new lemma, not inferred from a definition of flatness or smoothness in the item.

Step 4 uses the orbit clause already obtained in Step 2 and the density already obtained in Step 3. A nonempty open in a finite-type component over an algebraically closed field contains a geometric point after an algebraically closed extension, so its H-orbit covers the component. This proves actual geometric-point surjectivity of H times A, and hence surjectivity on underlying points, as required by the new boundary-ampleness node. That node has no transitivity assumption on all of X: meeting every orbit is sufficient. All its smoothness, finite-type, separatedness, connected-fibre and Cartier-boundary hypotheses are now in force. Since S itself is affine, its relatively ample conclusion is absolute (01VK); the new node states that absolute result explicitly, so the step does not silently import an extra comparison theorem.

Step 5 cites the statement of lem-sc-ample-finite-set-affine, which allows any scheme with an ample invertible module and any finite subset, including nonclosed points. It gives one affine open containing P. def-weil-open-immersion identifies its structure with the same open subscheme of N; nested open subsets and restriction of the structure sheaf are all this last assertion uses. Every use in all five steps is represented by an unmodified literal statement in citations.json.

I checked small cases of each statement. P empty works with W=X=A empty and the zero Cartier divisor, and the final affine open may be Spec(0). H trivial reduces saturation to W in an etale group. A neutral subgroup already equal to G gives X=G for any nonempty fibrewise W; if W misses fibres, X is allowed to miss them too. Smooth disconnected finite fibres and components defined only after residue-field extension are covered by geometric-fibre density, without requiring rational sections. The dense-affine assertion includes affine X (choose A=X and E=0) and X=P^1_S (choose the affine-line chart with infinity boundary). The action criterion includes E=0 and affine X; it does not assert that an arbitrary divisor is ample without the orbit-meeting hypothesis. For the original item, the G_m Néron lft-model over a DVR has infinitely many special-fibre components; a finite P only needs finitely many of them in the saturation X, so no false finite-type assertion about N follows.
