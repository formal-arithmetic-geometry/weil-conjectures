## Original five-step proof, preserved verbatim

### Original step 1

Write $H=F_D$, $\widetilde H=F_D^{\mathrm{Tow}}$, $M=K_\bullet$, and use right-to-left composition. Put $a_K=\widetilde H(i_K)j_K^{-1}:\Delta_YHK\to\widetilde HM$. The definition of completed pullback first applies pullback to the strict quotient augmentation and then applies the canonical constant-tower/derived-limit unit. Counit normalization identifies this pulled augmentation with $a_K$, on constant towers and on the entire quotient tower. Consequently the actual map is
$$\alpha^F_K=L_Y(a_K)v_{Y,HK}.$$
To check the normalization on representatives, take the flat-term K-flat representative $P$ used by that definition, its quotient tower $P_\bullet$, and a target K-injective tower resolution $r:F(P_\bullet)\to J_\bullet$. The actual construction is the chain map $FP\to\lim J_n$ whose components are $r_nF(P\to P_n)$. The canonical derived adjunction takes exactly this strict augmentation to that chain map: its unit factors the compatible component maps through the ordinary limit. Thus the displayed expression describes the specified map, including its resolution and localization comparisons. This uses the existing K-injective model for total right derived limit and the existing canonical derived adjunction; it does not assert that $F$ preserves K-injectives.

### Original step 2

Set $h=\operatorname{comp}_K:K\to L_XM$. Expand the definition of $\beta_M$ and move $H(h)$ past $v_Y$ by its naturality. Naturality of $j^{-1}$ for $h$ gives
$$\begin{aligned}
\beta_M H(h)
 &=L_Y\bigl(\widetilde H(\varepsilon_{X,M})j^{-1}_{L_XM}\Delta_YH(h)\bigr)v_{Y,HK}\\
 &=L_Y\bigl(\widetilde H(\varepsilon_{X,M}\Delta_Xh)j_K^{-1}\bigr)v_{Y,HK}.
\end{aligned}$$
The first move is $v_{Y,HL_XM}H(h)=L_Y\Delta_YH(h)v_{Y,HK}$, and the second is $j^{-1}_{L_XM}\Delta_YH(h)=\widetilde H(\Delta_Xh)j_K^{-1}$. These are substitutions of naturality and functoriality, with no preservation-of-limit assumption.

### Original step 3

The source completion map is $h=L_X(i_K)v_{X,K}$. Counit naturality for $i_K:\Delta_XK\to M$ and the left triangle identity give
$$\begin{aligned}
\varepsilon_{X,M}\Delta_Xh
 &=\varepsilon_{X,M}\Delta_XL_X(i_K)\Delta_Xv_{X,K}\\
 &=i_K\varepsilon_{X,\Delta_XK}\Delta_Xv_{X,K}
 =i_K.
\end{aligned}$$
Substitution in Step 2 gives $\beta_M H(\operatorname{comp}_K)=L_Y(\widetilde H(i_K)j_K^{-1})v_{Y,HK}$, which equals $\alpha^F_K$ by Step 1. This is an equality of the actual morphisms in the derived category, obtained before evaluating any projection.

### Original step 4

For the canonical target adjunction, the existing counit-projection theorem says $p_n=\operatorname{ev}_n(\varepsilon_{Y,\widetilde HM})$. The formula from Step 1 and counit naturality give
$$\varepsilon_{Y,\widetilde HM}\Delta_Y\alpha^F_K
 =\varepsilon_{Y,\widetilde HM}\Delta_YL_Y(a_K)\Delta_Yv_{Y,HK}
 =a_K\varepsilon_{Y,\Delta_YHK}\Delta_Yv_{Y,HK}=a_K.$$
Evaluate this identity at $n$. Since $\operatorname{ev}_n\Delta_Y=\mathrm{id}$, its left side is $p_n\alpha^F_K$. The counit-normalized exact evaluation comparison and $j$ identify its right side $\operatorname{ev}_n(a_K)$ with $H(q_{n,K})$. Hence $p_n\alpha^F_K=F_D(q_{n,K})$.

### Original step 5

The target counit is a morphism in the derived strict tower category, so its evaluations commute with every evaluation transition. Its source is the constant tower on $L_Y\widetilde HM$, hence has identity transitions. Its target transition is $H(\rho_{m,n})$, by the termwise exact functor and its normalized evaluation identifications. Therefore $H(\rho_{m,n})p_m=p_n$ for all $m\ge n$. For $m\ge n\ge r$, the strict quotient law gives
$$H(\rho_{n,r})H(\rho_{m,n})p_m=H(\rho_{m,r})p_m=p_r;$$
for equal indices the transition is the identity. The quotient law here is induced by the coefficient quotient maps, whose composites are the direct quotient maps. Naturality in $K$ follows by applying the functors and natural transformations $i,j,v,\varepsilon$ to any morphism $K\to K'$; each displayed equality is an equality of their composites. All constructions are on unbounded derived categories. Neither invertibility of $\operatorname{comp}_K$ nor product preservation by $F$ was used.
