### Old step 1 → parent induction setup

We prove a slightly stronger induction claim: for every flat locally finitely presented $g:Y\to S$, every closed $y\in Y_s$ with Noetherian fibre local ring $A$ satisfying $\operatorname{depth}(A)\ge\dim A$, there is a slice with all the conclusions of the item. By [lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-local-finite-krull-dimension], $d=\dim A$ is a finite nonnegative integer. Induct strongly on $d$, uniformly over $g,Y,S,s,y$. The original hypotheses imply this induction hypothesis at $g=f,y=x$, since equality implies the required inequality. The strengthening permits exactly the depth inequality supplied by the quotient lemma below.

Cited statements: lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-local-finite-krull-dimension.

### Old step 2 → lem-trf-zero-dimensional-closed-fibre-open-slice

Suppose $d=0$. Choose affine opens $V=\operatorname{Spec}C\subseteq S$ containing $s$ and $U=\operatorname{Spec}B\subseteq Y$ containing $y$, with $g(U)\subseteq V$ and $C\to B$ finitely presented [def-sbc-locally-finite-presentation]. In particular it is finite type. By [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra], $U_s=\operatorname{Spec}F$ with $F=B\otimes_C\kappa(s)$; the images of any finite set of algebra generators of $B$ generate $F$ over $\kappa(s)$. The point $y$ is closed in $U_s$, hence corresponds to a maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$ of $F$, and $F_{\mathfrak n}=A$. Thus [lem-weil-pbc-closed-point-dimension-local-ring] gives $\dim_y\operatorname{Spec}F=\dim A=0$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-ring-quasifinite-zero-fibre-dimension] to $C\to B$ at the prime of $y$: it is quasi-finite at that prime. The affine pair $(U,V)$ therefore makes $g$ quasi-finite at $y$ by [pqf-def-quasifinite].

Cited statements: def-sbc-locally-finite-presentation, lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra, lem-weil-pbc-closed-point-dimension-local-ring, lem-weil-pbc-ring-quasifinite-zero-fibre-dimension, pqf-def-quasifinite.

### Old step 3 → lem-trf-zero-dimensional-closed-fibre-open-slice

By [lem-sc-quasi-finite-points-open], the quasi-finite locus is an open neighbourhood $W$ of $y$ and $W\to S$ is locally quasi-finite. The map $g$ is locally of finite type because its local finite presentations give finitely many algebra generators. Hence [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-finite-at-point-isolated] makes $\{y\}$ open in $Y_s$. By the subspace-topology assertion of [def-pc-scheme-fibre], choose an open $O\subseteq Y$ with $O\cap Y_s=\{y\}$. Put $Z=O\cap W$ with its open-subscheme structure and take $i:Z\to Y$ to be the inclusion; then $Z_s=\{y\}$. The restriction is locally quasi-finite by [lem-weil-pbc-lqf-restriction-to-opens]. It is flat because the stalk rings and their maps are unchanged on an open subscheme [def-weil-flat-morphism]. It is locally finitely presented: inside each finitely presented affine chart, principal opens form a basis; if $B=C[t_1,\ldots,t_r]/(P_1,\ldots,P_m)$ and $b$ is represented by $Q(t)$, then $B_b=C[t_1,\ldots,t_r,u]/(P_1,\ldots,P_m,uQ-1)$. These principal charts cover the restriction and give its finite presentations [def-sbc-locally-finite-presentation]. This finishes the base case.

Cited statements: lem-sc-quasi-finite-points-open, lem-weil-pbc-quasi-finite-at-point-isolated, def-pc-scheme-fibre, lem-weil-pbc-lqf-restriction-to-opens, def-weil-flat-morphism, def-sbc-locally-finite-presentation.

### Old step 4 → lem-trf-cm-fibre-hypersurface-dimension-drop

Now let $d>0$. By [def-weil-pbc-depth], the inequality $\operatorname{depth}(A)\ge d>0$ implies that there is a nonempty regular sequence in the maximal ideal of $A$: otherwise the supremum of its possible lengths would be zero. Its first element $\bar h$ is a nonzerodivisor in that maximal ideal by [def-weil-pbc-regular-sequence]. By [lem-sbc-scheme-fibre-stalk-quotient], $\mathcal O_{Y,y}\twoheadrightarrow A$ is the quotient by $\mathfrak m_s\mathcal O_{Y,y}$. Lift $\bar h$ to $h\in\mathcal O_{Y,y}$. This lift lies in the maximal ideal: a unit would have unit image in the nonzero local ring $A$, whereas $\bar h$ is in its maximal ideal. Since $g$ is flat at $y$ and locally finitely presented, [lem-trf-fibre-regular-element-flat-hypersurface] supplies an affine open neighbourhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}B$ of $y$, a representative $a$ of $h$, and $D=\operatorname{Spec}(B/(a))\subseteq U$ containing $y$, such that $D\to S$ is flat and locally of finite presentation.

Cited statements: def-weil-pbc-depth, def-weil-pbc-regular-sequence, lem-sbc-scheme-fibre-stalk-quotient, lem-trf-fibre-regular-element-flat-hypersurface.

### Old step 5 → lem-trf-cm-fibre-hypersurface-dimension-drop

Write $y_D$ for the point of $D_s$ mapping to $y$. The affine definition of the closed subscheme [def-weil-closed-immersion] and the fibre stalk quotient [lem-sbc-scheme-fibre-stalk-quotient] identify its local ring as $A_D=A/(\bar h)$. Explicitly, if $T=\mathcal O_{Y,y}$, the local ring of $D$ is $T/(h)$: localization commutes with quotient by sending $b/t\bmod a$ to $(b\bmod a)/(t\bmod a)$, with inverse given by the same fraction before quotient. Its fibre local ring is therefore $(T/(h))/\mathfrak m_s(T/(h))=T/(h,\mathfrak m_sT)=(T/\mathfrak m_sT)/(\bar h)=A/(\bar h)$. These identifications send the original germ to its named class. The point $y_D$ is closed in $D_s$, since $D_s\to U_s$ is the closed subscheme cut out by the image of $a$ (the same quotient computation on affine charts), and $y$ is closed in $Y_s$.

Cited statements: def-weil-closed-immersion, lem-sbc-scheme-fibre-stalk-quotient.

### Old step 6 → lem-trf-cm-fibre-hypersurface-dimension-drop (quotient conclusions), then parent induction assembly

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-cm-local-quotient-nonzerodivisor-cm] to $A$ and $\bar h$. Its hypotheses hold: $A$ is Noetherian local of finite dimension $d$, has depth at least $d$, and $\bar h$ is a nonzerodivisor in its maximal ideal. It follows that $A_D$ is Noetherian local, $\dim A_D=d-1$, and $\operatorname{depth}(A_D)\ge d-1$. Thus the induction claim applies to the flat locally finitely presented map $D\to S$ and the closed point $y_D\in D_s$. It yields $j:Z\to D$ and $z\in Z_s$ mapping to $y_D$, with $Z_s=\{z\}$ and $Z\to S$ flat, locally finitely presented and locally quasi-finite. Compose $j$ with $D\to U\to Y$ to obtain $i:Z\to Y$. The structural morphism $Z\to S$ is exactly the one supplied by induction, so all its three properties persist without any assertion that the immersion itself is flat. Its singleton fibre point maps to $y$. This proves the induction step and, with the first step, proves the item.

Cited statements: lem-weil-pbc-cm-local-quotient-nonzerodivisor-cm.
