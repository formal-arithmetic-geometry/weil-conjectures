There is one precision issue in the source's 0CXA proof: it identifies the base change of an annihilator-defined support with the support after reduction. Equality as closed subsets is sufficient, whereas equality of annihilator subschemes is not valid in general under nonflat base change. For example take R=k[x,y], F=R/(x) direct-sum R/(y), and R -> B=k[t] with x and y both mapping to t. Then Ann_R(F)=(xy) extends to (t^2), whereas Ann_B(F tensor_R B)=(t). These support schemes have the same underlying closed subset but are not equal. The split never needs or asserts an equality of annihilator ideals after arbitrary base change: properness of a finite-type support is independent of the chosen closed-subspace structure (the existing 0CZC node), and Nakayama identifies the underlying supports. The new shrinking statement records exactly the usable conclusion, with no claim about annihilator base change.
