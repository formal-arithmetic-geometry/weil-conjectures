### Returned step 1

Work on an affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ containing $s$, with prime $\mathfrak p$ corresponding to $s$. By [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-finite-type-affine](a), $M=\mathcal F(U)$ is a finite $A$-module; by [lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction](3), $\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$. The germ localization of [lem-sbc-affine-module-germ-localization] identifies $\mathcal F_s$ with $M_{\mathfrak p}$. Write $I=\operatorname{Fit}_r(M)$ using the chosen finite surjection $A^n\twoheadrightarrow M$. By [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change](2), its base change to $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is $IA_{\mathfrak p}=\operatorname{Fit}_r(M_{\mathfrak p})$. Applied to the quasi-coherent Fitting ideal sheaf, [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-ideal-zero-set-affine] says exactly that $s\notin|Z_r|$ is equivalent to $I\not\subseteq\mathfrak p$ and to $\operatorname{Fit}_r(\mathcal F)_s=\mathcal O_{S,s}$.

### Returned step 2

Suppose $I\not\subseteq\mathfrak p$. If $n\le r$, see the end of this step. Otherwise put $a=n-r>0$. Some generating $a$-minor $\delta$ of $I$ is outside $\mathfrak p$, since if every such minor lay in the prime then their generated ideal would lie in it. Choose the $a$ kernel rows giving this minor and reorder the $n$ generator coordinates so that these rows form a matrix $(C\ D)$ with $C$ square of size $a$ and $\det C=\delta$ (up to sign, which does not change $D(\delta)$). In $M_\delta$, writing the generator column as $(x,y)$ with $r$ entries in $y$, the relations say $Cx+Dy=0$. By [lem-weil-pbc-matrix-adjugate-identity], multiplying by $\operatorname{adj}(C)$ gives $\delta x+\operatorname{adj}(C)Dy=0$. Hence $x=-\delta^{-1}\operatorname{adj}(C)Dy$: the $r$ entries of $y$ generate $M_\delta$. Via the affine counit of Step 1 ([lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction](3)) the entries of $y$ are sections of $\mathcal F$ over $U$; restrict them to $D(\delta)$. They generate $\mathcal F|_{D(\delta)}$ in the sense of [def-weil-pbc-generating-sections]: for $t\in D(\delta)$ with prime $\mathfrak q\not\ni\delta$, [lem-sbc-affine-module-germ-localization] identifies $\mathcal F_t$ with $M_{\mathfrak q}$ compatibly with germs, and $M_{\mathfrak q}$ is a localization of $M_\delta$, where the relation $x=-\delta^{-1}\operatorname{adj}(C)Dy$ persists, so the germs of $y$ generate $\mathcal F_t$; a section $\sigma$ of $\mathcal F$ over an open $W\subseteq D(\delta)$ thus has germ $\sum_i f_{i,t}y_{i,t}$ at each $t$, and the two sections $\sigma$ and $\sum_if_iy_i$ agree on a neighbourhood of $t$. This proves generation of $\mathcal F$ by at most $r$ sections on the neighbourhood $D(\delta)$ of $s$. The case $n\le r$ is the same stalk argument applied on $U$ to the $n$ chosen generators.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-matrix-adjugate-identity, lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction, lem-sbc-affine-module-germ-localization, def-weil-pbc-generating-sections. Source: literature/stacks-project/more-algebra.tex, 07ZC (lemma-fitting-ideal-generate-locally), direct kernel-minor proof of (1) implies (4) instead of the residue-field/Nakayama argument; adjugate identity as in Algebra 07DQ.

### Returned step 3

Generation by at most $r$ sections on a neighbourhood implies generation of the stalk by their germs: a local expression as a linear combination passes to the same expression in germs. Conversely, if $M_{\mathfrak p}=\mathcal F_s$ is generated by at most $r$ elements, pad the list by zeros to obtain a surjection $A_{\mathfrak p}^{\oplus r}\twoheadrightarrow M_{\mathfrak p}$. For this presentation the $r$th Fitting ideal is the unit ideal by the $n\le r$ convention. Presentation independence in [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change](1) equates it with the ideal of the localized original presentation, which is $IA_{\mathfrak p}$ by Step 1. Thus this stalk ideal is the whole local ring and $s\notin|Z_r|$ by Step 1. This closes the three implications of clause (2), without a finite-presentation hypothesis.

Uses: lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change, def-weil-pbc-generating-sections. Source: literature/stacks-project/more-algebra.tex, 07ZC (1), (3), (4), with independence 07Z8 and base change 07ZA(3); Divisors 0C3F.

### Returned step 4

Now let $g:T\to S$ be any morphism. Cover $T$ by affine opens $V=\operatorname{Spec}B$ mapping into affine opens $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $S$, and use the ring homomorphism $\varphi:A\to B$ induced by $g$. The affine pullback formula [lem-weil-pbc-tilde-pullback-base-change] identifies $\mathcal F_T|_V$ with $\widetilde{B\otimes_A M}$, where $M=\mathcal F(U)$ as in Step 1. This module is finite: if $m_1,\ldots,m_n$ generate $M$, then $1\otimes m_1,\ldots,1\otimes m_n$ generate it, since $b\otimes\sum a_i m_i=\sum b\varphi(a_i)(1\otimes m_i)$. Thus [lem-weil-pbc-spec-module-tilde-sections] and [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-finite-type-affine](a) show that $\mathcal F_T$ is again finite type and quasi-coherent. By [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change](2), the ideal on $V$ is $\operatorname{Fit}_r(B\otimes_A M)=\varphi(I)B$, where $I=\operatorname{Fit}_r(M)$; the change in tensor-factor order is the map $b\otimes m\mapsto m\otimes b$ with its evident inverse. This proves the needed Fitting base-change formula on each affine chart, without any flatness assumption on $g$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-tilde-pullback-base-change, lem-weil-pbc-spec-module-tilde-sections, lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-finite-type-affine, lem-trf-fitting-ideal-independence-base-change. Source: literature/stacks-project/divisors.tex, 0C3D (lemma-base-change-fitting-ideal), proved on affine charts by More on Algebra 07ZA(3); Schemes 01I9(1).

### Returned step 5

For $t\in V$ corresponding to $\mathfrak q\subset B$, its image $s=g(t)$ corresponds to $\mathfrak p=\varphi^{-1}(\mathfrak q)$. Then $\varphi(I)B\subseteq\mathfrak q$ if and only if $I\subseteq\mathfrak p$: one direction tests $\varphi(a)$ for $a\in I$, and the other uses that $\mathfrak q$ is an ideal and therefore contains every finite $B$-linear combination of these images. By Step 4 and clause (2), already proved in Steps 1-3 and applicable to $\mathcal F_T$, local generation by at most $r$ sections at $t$ is therefore equivalent to $g(t)\notin|Z_r|$. Such neighbourhoods cover $T$ exactly when $g(T)\subseteq S\setminus|Z_r|$. Finally [lem-weil-pbc-morphism-factors-through-open-subscheme] makes this image containment equivalent to a (unique) factorization of $g$ through the open subscheme $S\setminus|Z_r|$. The reverse implication from a factorization to image containment is immediate by composing its underlying maps. This is clause (1).

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-morphism-factors-through-open-subscheme. Source: literature/stacks-project/divisors.tex, 05P8 (lemma-locally-free-rank-r-pullback), part (2) and its proof; open factorization from Schemes 01HI.
