## Review by Q79

I read Milne Proposition 11.3 and its proof on printed p.210, Proposition 2.37 and its proof on pp.51-52, the reduced-subgroup proof in Proposition 1.38 and Corollary 1.39 on p.18, and the explicit split-extension discussion in Proposition 2.34 on p.50. For the single new node I read Propositions 1.29, 1.30(a,b), 1.31(a,b) and Remark 1.33 on pp.15-16. They give precisely the finite etale quotient, its functorial universal property and both comparison maps, as well as nonempty geometrically connected fibres. The new statement restricts to separated finite-type schemes, introduces the second scheme Y explicitly, and does not assume perfectness. It carries no proof sketch or proof dependencies.

For step 1 I opened each cited node. def-sc-group-scheme supplies the finite affine presentation and group diagrams; def-weil-pbc-scheme-reduction identifies B modulo its nilradical; lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme explicitly guarantees the quotient is reduced; lem-a1-finite-reduced-perfect-algebra-etale applies because C is a finite-dimensional quotient and k has surjective p-th power map; lem-weil-pbc-etale-ring-map-iff-etale-affine converts its algebraic conclusion into the scheme conclusion. lem-weil-pbc-perfect-reduced-tensor states reducedness of the tensor product with exactly these hypotheses, and lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra identifies that tensor spectrum with the product being used. def-sc-group-scheme-subgroup defines the conclusion. I read Stacks algebra.tex, lemma-perfect-reduced (00I4), and lemma-etale-over-field (00U3), alongside Milne 1.38/A.30/A.43. The factorization is proved by killing nilpotents, rather than inferred merely from topological containment.

For step 2 the new component-scheme statement is used only for its stated product/terminal comparisons, naturality and description of the fibres. The proof transports the actual group diagrams, then forms the kernel over the rational identity; it does not cite a definition as if that definition proved the subgroup property. def-sc-group-scheme and def-sc-group-scheme-subgroup give these diagrams and the test-scheme kernel description. The finite etale group scheme coordinate classification is explicitly part of def-sc-group-scheme. The identity point selects a k factor, which is why its fibre is open and closed, including the full nilpotent structure. I added def-weil-proper-morphism and the affine-fibre-product lemma to justify separatedness by the surjection B tensor B to B before applying the new theorem. This avoids using a matrix-group component lemma with the wrong base-field or reduced-structure assumptions.

For step 3 the new component lemma gives the comparison with the component map after scalar extension. lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-dim-zero-artinian gives finite discreteness once dimension zero has been checked by the finite-dimensional-domain argument written in the step. lem-per-algebraic-extension-perfect gives perfectness of the separable closure, whose algebraic and separably closed hypotheses are in def-pd-separable-closure. The short derivative/p-th-power argument then verifies it is algebraically closed. The reduction quotient gives R(K)=G(K) for a field K. lem-exc-etale-field-point-functor-fully-faithful states full faithfulness on the category of etale schemes over k, exactly the two objects R and P proved etale in steps 1 and 2. I read Stacks pione.tex lemma-sheaves-point (03QR), whose functor is this geometric-point functor. An inverse equivariant bijection lifts and the two composites equal the identities by faithfulness; I do not turn a pointwise bijection of arbitrary nonreduced schemes into a scheme isomorphism.

For step 4 only the previously constructed kernel, isomorphism u, its inverse, and def-sc-group-scheme are needed. I checked q(a(x))=1, r(hj(z))=z, a(hj(z))=h, and both inverse identities on arbitrary test schemes, not just field-valued points. Commutativity is used exactly in proving multiplication from the direct product is a homomorphism. This is the elementary assembly of the preceding steps, not a new lemma restating the item's conclusion.

I checked the trivial group, a connected infinitesimal group such as alpha_p (R is the identity), a finite etale group (H is the identity), and their product. For a nonconstant example, mu_3 over F_2 is etale but not constant; the argument never replaces etale by constant over k. The new component theorem allows the empty scheme (empty quotient and vacuous fibre assertion), A^1 (quotient Spec k), and Spec of a nontrivial finite separable field extension (quotient itself). Perfectness is used only for the reduced tensor product, etaleness of the reduction, and the closure comparison; it is not silently imposed on the new general component theorem. Milne Example 11.4 explains why dropping it from the item would be wrong.
