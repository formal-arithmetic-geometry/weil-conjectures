## Original returned argument, retained verbatim

### Old step 1

Work at the finite coefficient $A=A_{E,n}$ fixed by the purity conventions. First regard $u:U\to X$ as a covering of $X_{\mathrm{proet}}$. Clause (1) of [det-pe-morphisms-etale-comparison] makes $u$ weakly etale. It is flat and locally of finite presentation by [def-weil-etale-morphism], so [lem-weil-pbc-flat-lfp-open] makes its underlying map open. For every affine open $V\subset X$, choose affine opens $W\subset u^{-1}(V)$ covering $u^{-1}(V)$. The open subsets $u(W)$ cover $V$ by surjectivity. The affine scheme $V$ is quasi-compact by [lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs], so finitely many of these images cover $V$. This is exactly the finite-affine-refinement covering condition in [def-weil-pbc-proetale-site]. Thus the singleton $\{u\}$ is a pro-etale cover. Empty $V$ uses the empty finite refinement.

Uses: def-pur-conventions, det-pe-morphisms-etale-comparison, def-weil-etale-morphism, lem-weil-pbc-flat-lfp-open, lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs, def-weil-pbc-proetale-site.

### Old step 2

The site identification and constant-ring identification in [lem-weil-proetale-weakly-etale-localization] identify $U_{\mathrm{proet}}$ with $X_{\mathrm{proet}}/U$ and $A_U$ with $A_X|_U$. Under these identifications the inverse image along $u$ is sectionwise restriction, as follows directly from [def-weil-proetale-inverse-image]. For $W\to U$ in $U_{\mathrm{proet}}$, $W$ is also a weakly etale $X$-scheme. In the category $\mathcal I_W$ defining the inverse-image presheaf, the pair $(W,\gamma)$, where $\gamma:W\to U\times_XW$ is the graph of $W\to U$, is initial: the unique arrow to $(V,\phi:W\to U\times_XV)$ is $\operatorname{pr}_V\phi:W\to V$. Hence the opposite-indexed colimit is $F(W)$. These identifications commute with restrictions and sheaf morphisms; the resulting presheaf is already the restriction sheaf of [def-pur-localized-ringed-site]. For the coefficient ring they fix each constant section, so they give precisely the canonical localized constant-ring identification, and the induced module pullback is $F|_U$ with its restricted action. This restriction is exact by [lem-pur-site-restriction-module-adjunction-exact].

Uses: lem-weil-proetale-weakly-etale-localization, def-weil-proetale-inverse-image, def-pur-localized-ringed-site, lem-pur-site-restriction-module-adjunction-exact.

### Old step 3

By the finite-level definition [def-weil-derived-pullback-tensor-hom], $Lu^*$ is computed by applying this exact module inverse image termwise to any representative complex. The natural cohomology comparison of [lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-complex-cohomology] therefore gives $\mathcal H^r(Lu^*K)\cong(\mathcal H^rK)|_U$ for every integer $r$. This holds for derived morphisms as well: the comparison is natural on complex maps, and its naturality equation remains true after inverting quasi-isomorphisms and composing the resulting roofs. For $K=M,N$ it identifies $\mathcal H^r(Lu^*\varphi)$ with the restriction of $h_r=\mathcal H^r(\varphi)$. The hypothesis on $Lu^*\varphi$ implies that every $h_r|_U$ is an isomorphism.

Uses: def-weil-derived-pullback-tensor-hom, lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-complex-cohomology.

### Old step 4

Fix $r$ and a test object $T\to X$ of the pro-etale site. The site base-change axiom in [def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology] makes $T\times_XU\to T$ a cover. This object and its double overlap both map to $U$, so step 3 makes $h_r$ bijective on their sections. If two source sections on $T$ have the same image, their restrictions to $T\times_XU$ agree, hence they agree by the separatedness part of the sheaf equalizer axiom in that definition. Given a target section on $T$, invert $h_r$ on $T\times_XU$; the two restrictions of the inverse section to the double overlap agree because their images agree and $h_r$ there is injective. The sheaf axiom glues this section uniquely to $T$, and its image is the original section by separatedness. Thus $(h_r)_T$ is bijective for every $T$. Its inverse is additive, $A_X(T)$-linear and compatible with restrictions, since each of these equations follows after applying the injective map $(h_r)_T$. Consequently $h_r$ is an isomorphism of module sheaves. This also covers $X=\emptyset$: the empty covering forces a module sheaf on the empty object to have just its zero section.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology.

### Old step 5

All the cohomology maps $\mathcal H^r(\varphi)$ are now isomorphisms. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-unbounded-derived-iso-cohomology] to the abelian category of $A_X$-module sheaves on $X_{\mathrm{proet}}$. It follows that $\varphi$ is invertible in the ambient derived category. The bounded constructible category is full, so this inverse is also an inverse in the category of the statement. This proves the required implication without any extra Tor-amplitude or etale-origin hypothesis.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-unbounded-derived-iso-cohomology, def-weil-finite-torsion-constructibility.
