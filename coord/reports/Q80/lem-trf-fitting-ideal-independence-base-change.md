### lem-trf-row-minor-ideal-stable-invariance

Source read: literature/stacks-project/more-algebra.tex, Tag 07Z8 (lemma-fitting-ideal), proof: opening kernel-row description, zero-generator stabilization paragraph, and invertible coordinate change in the final paragraph; row form is the transpose of the source.

Argument assigned to this task (the original returned text, unabridged):

Old step 1: Fix $k\ge0$. For any finite matrix $A$, use $J_r(A)$ as in [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-minor-inclusions]. If $n>k$ and $r=n-k$, then $I_k(\pi)$ is generated by $J_r(A)$ as $A$ ranges over all finite matrices with $n$ columns and rows in $K=\ker\pi$: every $r$-minor uses just $r$ rows, and these are exactly the rows allowed in the defining row-minor description of $I_k(\pi)$. No finite generation of $K$ is needed.

Old step 2: Adjoin $d\ge0$ zero generators, so $\pi^+=(\pi,0):R^{\oplus(n+d)}\to M$ has kernel $K\oplus R^{\oplus d}$. First suppose $n>k$ and put $r=n-k>0$. Pad any $r$ kernel rows by zeros and append the last $d$ standard basis vectors. The minor selecting their $r$ old columns and all $d$ new columns is block diagonal with blocks the original minor and $1_d$; the Leibniz formula [def-weil-pbc-matrix-determinant] makes its determinant the original determinant. Hence $I_k(\pi)\subseteq I_k(\pi^+)$.

Old step 3: Conversely, still with $n>k$, take any $(r+d)$ rows in $K\oplus R^{\oplus d}$, forming a matrix $B$ with $n+d$ columns, and let $A$ be its first $n$ columns. Its rows lie in $K$. Clause (2) of [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-minor-inclusions] gives $J_{r+d}(B)\subseteq J_r(A)\subseteq I_k(\pi)$, proving the reverse inclusion. If $n\le k$, then $I_k(\pi)=R$. When $n+d\le k$ the stabilized ideal is also $R$ by convention; otherwise $0<t=n+d-k\le d$, and $t$ of the added basis vectors give a $t$-minor equal to $1$. Thus $I_k(\pi^+)=I_k(\pi)$ in every case.

Old step 4: An automorphism $u$ of $R^{\oplus N}$ replaces each row $z$ by $zC$ for an invertible $N\times N$ matrix $C$. For $N>k$, clause (3) of [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-minor-inclusions] puts every $(N-k)$-minor of $AC$ in $J_{N-k}(A)$, so the ideal for $u(K)$ is contained in the ideal for $K$. Applying the same statement to $C^{-1}$ reverses the inclusion. For $N\le k$ both ideals are $R$ by convention. Therefore isomorphic free presentations of the same rank have equal ideals.

Original citations checked: def-weil-pbc-matrix-determinant, lem-trf-fitting-ideal-minor-inclusions.

### lem-trf-free-surjections-common-stabilization

Source read: literature/stacks-project/more-algebra.tex, Tag 07Z8 (lemma-fitting-ideal), final proof paragraph: the commuting isomorphism between zero-stabilized free presentations; here made explicit by lifts of standard basis vectors and the displayed invertible shears, rather than an abstract isomorphism of kernels.

Argument assigned to this task (the original returned text, unabridged):

Old step 5: Compare $\pi:F=R^{\oplus n}\to M$ and $\pi':G=R^{\oplus m}\to M$. Lift the images of the finitely many standard basis vectors, using the two surjections, to obtain linear maps $f:G\to F$ and $g:F\to G$ with $\pi f=\pi'$ and $\pi'g=\pi$ [def-weil-pbc-free-module]. The map
$$U:F\oplus G\longrightarrow G\oplus F,\quad U(x,y)=(y+g(x-fy),x-fy)$$
has inverse $U^{-1}(u,v)=(v+f(u-gv),u-gv)$, by substitution, and $(\pi',0)U(x,y)=\pi'(y)+\pi(x-fy)=\pi(x)$. Thus $U$ identifies the kernels of the two zero-stabilized presentations, both of rank $n+m$. Apply Step 4 and then the stabilization equalities in Steps 2-3 to obtain $I_k(\pi)=I_k(\pi')$. This explicit shear calculation supplies the commuting presentation isomorphism used at the Schanuel step of 07Z8; it uses no claim about an abstract isomorphism of kernels lifting automatically.

Original citations checked: def-weil-pbc-free-module.

### lem-trf-row-minor-ideal-scalar-extension

Source read: literature/stacks-project/more-algebra.tex, Tag 07ZA (lemma-fitting-ideal-basics), proof of (3), base-changing the presentation, together with the kernel-row description at the start of Tag 07Z8 (lemma-fitting-ideal); explicit finite-free tensor coordinates and the row-span determinant argument used in that proof.

Argument assigned to this task (the original returned text, unabridged):

Old step 6: For a ring map $\varphi:R\to R'$, apply [lem-weil-pbc-tensor-right-exact] with $M_1=K$, $M_2=R^{\oplus n}$, $M_3=M$, $N=R'$ to $K\hookrightarrow R^{\oplus n}\xrightarrow{\pi}M\to0$. It gives surjectivity of $\pi\otimes1$ and identifies its kernel $K'$ with the image of $K\otimes_RR'$, without asserting this tensor map is injective. The tensor construction [def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product] identifies $R^{\oplus n}\otimes_RR'$ with $R'^{\oplus n}$ by $x\otimes a\mapsto(a\varphi(x_j))_j$; its inverse sends $(a_j)_j$ to $\sum_j e_j\otimes a_j$. Since pure tensors generate, each row of $K'$ is a finite $R'$-linear combination of images of rows of $K$.

Old step 7: If $n\le k$, both base-change ideals are the unit ideal. Otherwise put $r=n-k>0$. Fix $r$ rows in $K'$ and a set of $r$ columns. Write each row as a finite sum $z'_i=\sum_\alpha a_{i\alpha}\varphi(z_{i\alpha})$, with $z_{i\alpha}\in K$, as supplied by Step 6. Substituting these finite sums in the Leibniz formula [def-weil-pbc-matrix-determinant] and distributing products gives
$$\det(z'_i|_J)_i=\sum_{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r}\Bigl(\prod_i a_{i\alpha_i}\Bigr)\varphi\bigl(\det(z_{i\alpha_i}|_J)_i\bigr).$$
Every summand lies in $\varphi(I_k(\pi))R'$. Conversely the image of each original minor is the minor on the image rows, which lie in $K'$. Taking the generated ideals proves $I_k(\pi\otimes_RR')=\varphi(I_k(\pi))R'$. This is a direct finite-sum calculation, with no flatness or finite-presentation hypothesis.

Original citations checked: def-weil-pbc-matrix-determinant, def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product, lem-weil-pbc-tensor-right-exact.
