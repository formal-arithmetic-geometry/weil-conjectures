## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the old final sentence defined r(T) by the maximum over admissible partitions for every T, including the empty cluster. Definition 2.16, printed p.233, separately sets the zero cluster's index to zero.

An explicit counterexample to the claimed equivalence is a curve consisting of two smooth elliptic curves meeting transversely at one point, over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. It is connected, reduced, projective and nodal. Its canonical sections are pairs of regular differentials on the two elliptic components: a differential with at most a single simple pole has residue zero and therefore no pole. Thus the canonical section space is the sum of its two component-supported one-dimensional subspaces. A pair of nonzero differentials is nonzero at both component generic points, so the empty cluster is subcanonical. The two-block partition is admissible, and the old formula gives r(empty)=1, while the source convention gives zero.

Correction: use the unchanged maximum formula when T is nonempty, and explicitly define r(empty)=0. All geometric hypotheses and every notion in the statement are retained. This is not a restriction to two-connected curves. The old maximum remains a legitimate extension for nonempty clusters, and only subcanonical clusters are used downstream.

The definition constructs data rather than choosing an unspecified object by existence. For a block A, its V_A is the intersection of kernels of generic evaluations outside A, so is a k-subspace.
