## Review by Q80

I checked the node files against the complete B--E checklist (both copies in delivery-errors.md), with the source text open. There are no new-node form issues: all ingredients are existing nodes, the item statement is unchanged, and every mathematical expression is TeX escaped once. I did not treat the definitions of flatness or strict transform as theorems asserting exactness.

Step 1 uses the actual cokernel-closure statement to get quasi-coherence of H; finite type follows explicitly by composing finite generators with the quotient. The smooth criterion states flatness, and finite sums are handled by the written tensor argument. Clause (2) of module-admissible-blowup-calculus states both the canonical ordinary-pullback identification for a relatively flat module and quasi-coherence/finite type of arbitrary strict transforms. Its hypotheses hold: S is affine and hence qcqs, U is quasi-compact, the centre is the finite-type specified datum, and the free module is relatively flat. The identification of pullback free modules is the displayed coordinate tensor map.

Step 2 uses exactly the full short-exactness statement of strict-transform-universal-exact, with universal base-change exactness supplied as a hypothesis of this item. It does not try to deduce preservation of kernels from ordinary right exactness. I checked that the ideal retained by admissible-blowup is finite type, and that the induced strict-transform maps are the maps on supported-section quotients. Their two equalities after the quotient maps are written out. These are the requested compatibility, not arbitrary chosen isomorphisms.

Step 3 uses the surjective-base-change node with W to S and S^b to S, with no assumption that b is surjective. Pullback functoriality preserves the given inverse of alpha. An isomorphism on W^b carries the supported-section kernels in both directions, so quotienting preserves the isomorphism even at points lying on the exceptional divisor. This is an explicit two-line restriction argument from the strict-transform definition, not an uncited lemma about purity or flatness. In particular I do not incorrectly assume W avoids the blowup centre.

Step 4 uses affine-morphism-permanence for the *full* inverse image of each affine base open, not intersections of arbitrary affine source opens. The smooth clause of proper-base-change applies without properness: its two clauses are separate. The smooth criterion supplies local finite presentation and stalk flatness. The existing affine-open finite-presentation lemma states the coordinate-ring finite-presentation assertion. The relative-flatness definition explicitly gives equivalence of stalk and affine-section flatness for the structure sheaf. The fibre identification is written using the universal property and actual residue-field extension; geometric integrality, including nonemptiness, survives every such extension by the item's own hypothesis. No Noetherian, finite-dimensional or fixed-relative-dimension assumption is added.

Step 5 uses the unit/counit equivalence and the colimit-preservation clause of affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction, so coker beta really corresponds to H^b, rather than to an unjustified quotient of global sections. The finite-type-affine statement gives finite N, and the affine-germ-localization statement gives the actual stalks. I checked these precise clauses in their node files. Surjectivity of W^b makes its intersection with every fibre nonempty. Each integral fibre has the zero prime as its generic point, lying in every nonempty open; the basic-open argument is written. At these points alpha is an isomorphism of stalks, hence a residue-field basis, and the free stalk is relatively flat by the already established smoothness. This verifies all generic-flatness hypotheses of the cited 05I3 criterion, not just its basis hypothesis.

Step 6 uses the statement of free-subtraction-flatness, which asserts the pointwise equivalence for every prime and all ranks including zero. I matched R,A,N,beta,Q and the generic primes with that statement. The stalk identifications from Step 5 convert its conclusion exactly into the requested base-local-ring flatness equivalence; the affine-basis node covers all base points. The relative-flatness definition then gives the global assertion. No assertion about universal exactness of the transformed sequence is needed or assumed here.
