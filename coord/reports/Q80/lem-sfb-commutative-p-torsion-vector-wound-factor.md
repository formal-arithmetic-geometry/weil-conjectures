For step 3 I read Proposition 1.15 and the straightening statement. V is smooth by step 2, already embedded in E by step 1, and its family generates it also in E by taking an intersection with H. These verify the proposition's actual hypotheses. The result supplies the retraction on E; restricting it to H gives r|H. I wrote the coordinate kernel and both inverse morphisms, including the equality r(x-r(x))=0. No existence theorem for a quotient or for a chosen complement is invoked without a citation.

For step 4 I opened pqf-lem-lft-fp-locnoeth and def-sbc-smooth-morphisms and used their statements verbatim in citations.json. The former applies because W is affine finite type and Spec k is locally Noetherian (its only ideals are 0 and k); its conclusion is exactly local finite presentation, not smoothness. Smoothness is then proved: lift the inclusion followed by a map from a first order thickening's closed term into H, and compose the lift with q. The identity qi=id makes this a lift into W. Thus the definition's two requirements are separately met. Connectedness uses the section to see that q is surjective, so a clopen partition of W would pull back to one of H. This is the only existing non-definition proof citation, and it is already a Mathlib leaf, so no off-unit unsplit theorem is used.

For step 5 I checked the claimed unipotence by writing the faithful first-row matrix representation: its nilpotent part has square zero over every algebra, not just over k-points. I then checked the wound argument as scheme factorization. Translating by f(0) gives a pointed map, so it factors through V_K and W_K and hence through their trivial scheme-theoretic intersection. This proves constancy even over an imperfect separably closed field. It uses the previous construction and explicit formulas, not a new algebraic-group theorem.

For step 6 I checked both inclusions of vector factors. Projection to the other wound factor kills every pointed curve, giving V_K contained in V'_K. Each coordinate axis of V'_K is itself such a curve, and the group law sums the axes to all of V'_K, giving the reverse inclusion as schemes. Equality of defining ideals descends by injectivity of scalar extension for vector spaces. This is not the false assertion that a smooth subgroup's rational points alone determine it over arbitrary k.

For step 7 I checked that V=1 implies H_K wound, then detects zero pointed k-morphisms by k[x] embedding into K[x]. Translation handles unpointed maps. If d>0, the first coordinate axis in V gives a nonconstant k-map into H, contradicting woundness. The assertion about W in the statement is a warning that no uniqueness is claimed; the graph formula records precisely how any nonzero W-to-V homomorphism changes the embedded complement.

Small cases checked before accepting the statements: trivial H and N=0 give V=W=1; H=Ga^n gives V=H and W=1 by its axes; an empty generation family gives the identity subgroup; a nontrivial constant Z/p subgroup embeds in Ga and is allowed by the embedding node, but cannot satisfy the straightening node's pointed-curve generation hypothesis. The straightening conclusion allows h nonlinear; requiring h linear would fail for graphs of Frobenius.
