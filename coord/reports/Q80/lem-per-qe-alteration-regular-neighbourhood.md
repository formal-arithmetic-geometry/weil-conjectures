For the spreading lemma, read Kurano–Shimomoto Lemma 4.4's first proof paragraph, Stacks limits.tex Tag 01ZM (lemma-descend-finite-presentation, objects/arrows/equalities), Tag 07RR (lemma-descend-surjective), and Tag 081F (lemma-eventually-proper). For relative regular-locus openness, read Lemma 4.2 and Claim 4.3, printed pp.4–5. The proof uses Noetherian induction on closed subsets of the closed fibre, the J-2 property on integral quotients, and regular sequences at a regular generic point, then stability under generalization. It proves openness of Reg(X) intersected with the closed fibre, not regularity of the fibre scheme. A future split must retain that distinction. There is no claim that Reg(X) is open everywhere without J-2 for A itself.

I checked B–E with the actual statements and the sources open. All three new nodes introduce their objects and hypotheses, define dominance and generic finiteness inline where used, cite the notions in their statements, and have empty proof lists/dependencies and null proof origins. They are neither elementary consequences nor clauses restating the item. Their blueprint ancestry is the nearest imported ancestor with the complete via chain. Shared chapter and unit records were not changed. Each non-elementary step is under 2000 characters (the longest is 703); no hidden standard comparison or lemma remains inline in a long step.

Step 1 uses def-exc-excellent-ring for excellent implies quasi-excellent and the new weak-uniformization statement for every chart and every covering-witness clause. I verified its base is integral, nonempty, Noetherian, and has a quasi-excellent affine cover, since it is the spectrum of the assumed excellent B_q. Step 2 cites the spreading node, whose statement includes the entire indexed diagram and structural properties; I did not infer them from a definition of alterations or from an etale-only descent theorem.

Step 3 is elementary and has no black-box citation: at a point with contracted prime contained in q, every inverted scalar is already a unit in its ambient local ring. The two local rings are literally the same localizations. The source's equation (4.3) consequently applies to all such points. No identification of the ambient local ring with a closed-fibre local ring is made.

Step 4 uses the relative regular-locus statement for closedness of W_i, def-exc-excellent-ring for the J-2 quotient hypothesis, and def-weil-constructible-subset for constructibility of a closed subset of a Noetherian scheme. The finite-type-to-finite-presentation node states the needed algebra presentation result; on the affine charts the target B[1/a] is Noetherian and the chart rings are finite type. Noetherian spaces have quasi-compact opens, so the finite-type chart morphisms satisfy Chevalley's quasi-compact and locally-finitely-presented hypotheses. Chevalley's cited statement gives constructibility of f_i(W_i) for a constructible subset, not merely constructibility of f_i(X). Read that node's full statement and its source tag 054J.

Step 5 uses the qcqs-scheme-spectral statement for the affine target, then the full closure-specialization statement for a singleton family consisting of E_i. That node does say that a closure point specializes from an actual point of the subset. In Spec(B[1/a]) this point is a prime contained in q; Step 3 contradicts its preimage lying in W_i. This is why it is the closure, not just the image, that can be removed. The two generic tools and finite-presentation/Chevalley are declared off-unit tool uses, not alternate unit routes.

Step 6 is a basic-open choice and restriction argument, explicitly using the spreading node's permanence clause for the integral proper witness, generic fields, and indexed open cover. Regularity is checked by emptiness of the restricted W_i and invariance of local rings on opens. I checked every cited statement again when assembling citations.json; every uses entry has a verbatim quote, without paraphrasing a stronger assertion into the citation.

Small cases: J=B gives empty W_i and a vacuous regularity requirement; J=0 asks for genuinely regular charts on a neighbourhood and the same proof removes all ambient singular points. For q=(0), the local cover is over Frac(B) and generic finiteness is unchanged by any nonempty principal restriction. For B a field, its only prime is (0), one can use the identity chart and witness. The weak-uniformization node excludes an empty integral base, so its finite nonempty charts have meaningful function fields. The spreading node starts from an actual integral nonempty witness; restriction at b outside q is nonempty and cannot discard the generic point. Relative regular-locus openness makes no false assertion that regularity of X modulo I implies regularity of X: for X=Spec(k[e]/(e^2)) and I=(e), the ambient regular locus is empty although the closed fibre is a field; the asserted open subset is correctly empty.

The only correction during this review was to phrase the weak-uniformization hypothesis by an affine cover with quasi-excellent coordinate rings, exactly as the source defines quasi-excellent schemes, avoiding an unnecessary appeal to affine-cover independence at Step 1. No mathematical hypothesis of the item was altered. The literature gap and bibliographic correction are explicit above.
