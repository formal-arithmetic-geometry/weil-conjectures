## Review by Q80

I checked the live statement against the sources just named with the distinction between a strict tower and a list of derived objects kept throughout. R and every S_n are ring sheaves on the same small site, each q_n is a ring map, and t_n q_{n+1}=q_n is exactly what makes the transition balanced over R. No q_n is assumed surjective, flat or Noetherian. A small site relative to modules gives the abelian source category; the countable tower category uses the same module universe and the derived localizations use the stipulated larger morphism universe.

Step 1 uses the statements of `def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product`, `def-pc-sheaf-restrict-scalars`, and `def-weil-inverse-system`. They really give sheafification of the presheaf tensor, the unchanged module sheaf with restricted action, and the transition/naturality equations for a strict tower. The step writes out balancedness, naturality, additivity and the unit equation on pure tensors. Equality after sheafification follows because a morphism out of the sheafification is determined by its presheaf composite. These are direct checks from those definitions, not an assertion of an unspecified scalar-unit normalization.

Step 2 uses `def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category` for sectionwise kernels and sheafified cokernels, `def-weil-inverse-system` for assembly of component maps, `def-pc-sheaf-restrict-scalars` for the unchanged underlying abelian sheaf, and `def-weil-abstract-derived-category` for H^i and quasi-isomorphisms. I checked that the universal maps on kernels and cokernels commute with transitions and hence calculate them in the functor category. Component inverses commute with transitions by the naturality equation. These explicit short universal-property checks justify componentwise cohomology and exact scalar restriction; they do not claim that global sections preserve cokernels.

Step 3 uses precisely the conclusion of `lem-pur-change-of-rings-kflat-quasi-iso`, whose full statement I read: termwise ordinary sheaf extension sends a quasi-isomorphism between any two K-flat complexes to a quasi-isomorphism. This is not the weaker definition of K-flatness, and does not contain a flat-term assumption. The tensor definition supplies the symmetry identification of the two degree-zero-factor conventions, and the scalar-restriction definition is used only through Step 2's explicit kernel/cokernel check. Applying the cited lemma separately at each level yields a map in the already constructed strict tower, not unrelated isomorphisms in derived categories.

Step 4 uses the concrete homotopy, cone and shift conventions of `def-weil-abstract-derived-category`. Applying an additive functor to dh+hd retains homotopies; applying it to the two-by-two cone matrix retains the off-diagonal map and the minus sign. This is why the homotopy-to-derived functor F is triangulated even though T need not preserve short exact sequences of modules. The biproduct and cone calculation is elementary and is written in the step rather than made into a node.

Step 5 uses `lem-pc-kflat-resolution`, whose statement covers every unbounded complex of module sheaves on any ringed site and provides a termwise surjective K-flat resolution with flat terms, and `lem-pur-left-derived-from-kflat-class`, whose statement supplies exactly a universal right Kan extension, its counit and its invertibility on every member of the chosen resolving class. I checked both hypotheses: the resolution arrow points P to X, and Step 3 makes F invert quasi-isomorphisms between all K-flat objects. I choose the entire K-flat class, not just the flat-term subclass; therefore the final counit assertion covers K-flat complexes without flat terms. The use of `def-weil-abstract-derived-category` here is only that its localization inverts quasi-isomorphisms. The universal property's direction agrees with the statement: transformations G Q to F uniquely factor through epsilon from G to LT.
