Step 8 uses the existing uniformizer-root-algebra statement, the unit-binomial-finite-etale statement and the finite-etale-normal-generic-components statement. The latter assumes normal integral Noetherian base; a DVR is a Noetherian PID and is integrally closed by the valuation argument. Both exponents h and m are positive and prime to p, and the element u remains a unit. The formula z=a/y^d gives z^m=u and V=E(y,z), so the chosen generic component is exactly the one needed. Step 9 uses integral-transitive, integral-injective-lying-over, normalization-structure, etale-prime-generates-maximal and etale-algebra-residue-field-separable. Steps 10 and 11 write the valuation calculation instead of hiding it in a tame-permanence node: theta=v beta^b gives h=bc, and repeating it through Z gives the original branch index as a divisor of a prime-to-p integer. The only non-elementary field assertion is transitivity, cited as lem-zl-separable-permanence; subfield separability is proved directly by a minimal-polynomial divisor argument. Every index is positive because the relevant map is injective and local. The residue extension for the original branch is finite by step 2. I checked the actual statement of every cited node, and citations.json copies those statements verbatim. No citation takes a construction from a node's proof instead of its statement.

Small cases: T=F and m=1 give identity and unramified branches. When t is divisible by m, h=1 and the chart only adjoins a root of a unit, giving index one. When gcd(t,m)=1, the calculated bound is a divisor of m. The tame example F_p((r)) with r=s^t and adjoining r^(1/m), p not dividing m, has expected new index m/gcd(m,t). The unramified case allows arbitrary and even inseparable residue extension for S/R: only the finite top residue extensions need be separable.
