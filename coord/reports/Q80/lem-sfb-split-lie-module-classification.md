Step 3 takes cyclicity from `lem-pc-finite-dimensional-trace-cyclic` and the ordinary matrix trace from `def-weil-endomorphism-trace`. I checked the dual-basis signs: if ad(z) has coefficients a_ji on x_i, it has coefficients -a_ij on x^i; the commutator sum cancels by exchanging indices. The trace is d, the dimension of the image algebra, not the dimension of the original g. This is a displayed elementary finite-sum computation.

Step 4 uses the already-verified perfectness to kill the one-dimensional quotient action. It proves c(M) is inside W and hence the trace of c on W is d. Steps 5-6 establish the needed rank-one spectrum from existing statements, rather than creating another rank-one classification node. `lem-sfb-sl2-invariant-complement` imposes no nilpotence or diagonalizability assumption, so it applies before those properties are known. A least-dimensional invariant subspace is simple, and induction splits off finitely many such spaces. Over Qbar, the E-raising vectors have distinct eigenvalues, so their nonzero chain is finite. `lem-sfb-sl2-primitive-lowering-string` works over any characteristic-zero field and gives a nonnegative integer eigenvalue. I use the abstract standard sl2 matrix h, which is nonzero even when its action on a module is zero; no primitive-vector theorem is applied to a triple with zero abstract h. Row reduction then gives a rational primitive vector. The same lowering node provides all nonzero string vectors and raising coefficients, while `lem-sfb-sl2-primitive-string-vanishing` provides the missing endpoint. These are the exact clauses of those two Mathlib leaves. The string span is invariant, so rational simplicity makes it the entire summand. All its distinct H-eigenvalues are integers. In particular the argument has not assumed that a simple rational module is already absolutely simple.

Step 7 uses the triples supplied by `lem-sfb-split-semisimple-root-decomposition` and applies the proved rank-one calculation to their actions on V. `lem-sfb-root-base-rational-integral-spans` applies to that node's reduced crystallographic evaluation pairing and the given sign-coherent simple-root basis. It explicitly supplies both the rational simple-coroot basis and integral spanning by simple coroots. The written commuting spectral projections give ordinary simultaneous eigenspaces, and integrality on all coroots follows by evaluation on those integral combinations. A generalized-weight decomposition alone would not justify this step; I do not cite one for it.

Step 8 uses the finite weight support already proved, checks the order is a partial order, and writes the root-weight shift equation. A maximal weight is therefore killed by every positive root space. `lem-sfb-sl2-primitive-lowering-string` applies to each normalized simple triple with nonzero abstract h_i and gives dominance. `lem-sfb-verma-monomial-weight-basis` defines the particular associative algebra and left ideal being used. The map to S is defined by products acting on the highest vector, and I checked every defining left-ideal generator kills it. Simplicity gives surjectivity, so this is an actual quotient, not an appeal to an unspecified universal module.

Step 9 uses the Verma node's ordinary monomial weights, top line, and cyclic generator, together with the simple cyclic modules supplied by `lem-sfb-rational-highest-weight-modules`. I checked the infinite-dimensional case explicitly: one projects a single vector's finite support, not the whole Verma module by a nonexistent finite global spectral polynomial. A proper submodule cannot have a nonzero top component. Its sum with every other proper submodule still has zero top component, making the sum proper and the unique maximal submodule. Thus every simple highest-vector quotient is the same quotient, including a simple module not initially assumed finite-dimensional. The finite-support projections also justify that the quotient's top space has dimension one.

Step 10 uses the root node for h-stability of the positive-root kernel and the Verma node for the weight bound of a module generated by any homogeneous highest vector. The rational-highest-weight node independently supplies the opposite upper bound in the chosen L(lambda). If a singular vector has weight mu, these give lambda <= mu and mu <= lambda, forcing equality. Decomposing the invariant kernel into its h-weights is justified by the finite projections already written. This proves every singular vector, not merely every singular eigenvector, lies on the one line.

Step 11 is an elementary consequence of that kernel line and cyclicity: an isomorphism preserves its weight, and an endomorphism is determined by its value on the generator. The scalar may be zero. This proves pairwise nonisomorphism and End_g(L(lambda))=Q without using algebraically closed Schur's lemma.

Step 12 uses the root node's finite positive-root spaces to form a single finite rational matrix for their common kernel; row reduction proves its kernel commutes with every field extension. The weight projectors have rational coefficients and continue to separate the weights over every extension of Q. A nonzero invariant subspace over K therefore has a homogeneous maximal-weight vector, which is in the extended singular line. The rational-highest-weight node gives cyclic products spanning L, and their tensor extensions span L_K. This proves simplicity for arbitrary K, including transcendental extensions; it does not assume an algebraic extension, a descent theorem for subspaces, or Weyl's theorem over K.

Step 13 assembles the proved clauses and uses the rational-highest-weight node's explicit rank-zero choice. For g=0 a simple module has dimension one because every subspace is invariant. Empty root sets, zero modules, repeated copies of one simple module, lambda=0, and weights on chamber walls are all allowed. No positivity argument silently assumes strictly dominant lambda or a nonempty root set.
