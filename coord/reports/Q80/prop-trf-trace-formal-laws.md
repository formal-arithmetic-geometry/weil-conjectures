## Source error

The current blueprint's scalar-extension proof calls arbitrary `B` a K-flat A-complex. For `A = Z/4`, `B = Z/2`, tensoring the injection `2A -> A` with B gives a zero map from a nonzero B-module, so B is not flat and is not K-flat in degree zero. Step 5 uses the bounded projective representative M as the K-flat factor. Its extended finite projective splitting and explicit dual frames prove the required scalar trace equality without that false claim.

The scalar-extension proof also lists a passage $A_{E,n}\to\mathcal O_E$ as a ring homomorphism. Such a unital map generally does not exist: for $A=\mathbf Z/4$ and $\mathcal O_E=\mathbf Z_2$, it would send $4\cdot1=0$ to $4\cdot1\neq0$. Adic inverse-limit reconstruction is a different construction. Clause (3) only quantifies over ring homomorphisms that exist, so the repaired proof does not use this invalid claimed passage.

The Stacks Tag 087J source uses dual numbers and endomorphisms `(1+epsilon,1,1)`, rather than literally the blueprint's Z/4 variant. Step 4 verifies the latter variant directly, including the left-square homotopy, chain-map equation and last triangle square; it is not attributed as a verbatim Stacks example.

The inherited `lem-trf-chain-supertrace` statement describes a basis of a finite projective module, although finite projective modules over a general ring need not be free. Only its stated finite-projective supertrace and independence conclusions are used here; the defining model comes from the reviewed `def-trf-categorical-trace`, which correctly uses finite dual frames. This dependency should retain that interpretation when it is split; I did not modify another worker's node.

Reading their current statements showed that the former is about pro-etale sheaves and does not define internal Hom, while the latter constructs fibre functors and has cohomological target categories. Those C4 mismatches are removed. The generic module-derived representative theorem and an explicit vector-space complement construction supply what is actually used.
