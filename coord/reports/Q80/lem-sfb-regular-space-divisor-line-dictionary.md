Regularity makes the charts reduced and normal (spaces-properties.tex lemma-regular-normal, 0BGT); the reduced case has weakly associated points exactly at codimension zero (0EN1). Noetherianity supplies the finiteness condition of 0BB1. Quasi-separatedness supplies decentness and the schematic generic points required by 0ENA. The chart component assertion uses the normal Noetherian component result 033M, which already exists in rem-pc-regular-normal. The generic product gives both injectivity and dense-open detection; no extension of a regular section across a divisor is asserted.

For the second new node I read spaces-properties.tex lemma-pre-dimension-local-ring (0BAM) and lemma-reduced-closed-subspace (03IQ), including its atlas construction, and spaces-chow.tex lemma-length (0EE1), lemma-etale-pullback (0EPY), and lemma-etale-pullback-principal-divisor (0EQ2). The global Chow statements have an additional dimension-function setup, so the new source explicitly says this is an agent-formulated codimension-one interface from their pointwise proof, not a specialization that drops those hypotheses. The equalizer argument is: a coefficient at a codimension-one point is constant on its atlas lifts because any two lifts have a common lift on the relation; a finite compatible support descends as a closed saturated support and its generic points give the prime subspaces. Reduced induced closed subspaces give their structures. Etale local dimensions agree, and reduced etale inverse images have multiplicity one. For orders, an etale local extension of DVRs has maximal ideal generated by the image of a base uniformizer, so the orders agree, with no residue-degree multiplier. This is a genuine cycle-descent prerequisite, not a restatement of Cartier/Weil bijectivity. I corrected a draft tag 04N8 to the actual tag 0BAM before delivery.

For step 1, the generic-chart model gives precisely the product of generic fibres and their one-dimensionality. Choosing a basis in each fibre and checking multiplication componentwise is elementary. The scheme-etale comparison gives ordinary local frames, with all morphisms and compatible restriction. It is not cited as a generic Yoneda or descent theorem.

For step 2, I opened the full statement of lem-trf-normal-domain-principal-divisor-kernel: it gives finite support, additivity, the normalized valuation/length agreement, and exactly the zero-divisor/unit criterion used. The charts are Noetherian normal domains by the model, so its hypotheses hold. The new cycle lemma gives the exact atlas equalizer and etale invariance of orders used to descend. A finite chart cover exists by quasi-compactness. The sheaf quotient is used locally; no surjectivity on global units is presumed.

For step 3, I opened lem-per-regular-prime-divisor-local-equations: it applies to an integral regular locally Noetherian scheme with an integral prime divisor of local dimension one, and states both the effective Cartier ideal and its prime generator/uniformizer. All those hypotheses hold on the integral chart components, with codimension one furnished by the cycle interface. The proof writes the product of local prime equations with the actual signed coefficients; the kernel lemma makes the overlapping ratios units. No new node merely says that the item's Cartier/Weil clause is true.

For step 4, I opened lem-trf-algebraic-space-invertible-descent, which states effective descent and full faithfulness for line modules on an etale scheme cover with the specified cocycle, including their ordinary-scheme interpretation. The inverse-equation modules coincide inside the same meromorphic sheaf on overlaps; therefore their identity identifications satisfy the required cocycle. The rational sections are glued in the meromorphic sheaf by its sheaf axiom. This does not misapply line-module descent to a meromorphic sheaf as though it were itself a line module over O.

For step 5 the same descent statement glues the actual maps a -> as. In a frame s=g e the basis g^{-1} goes to e, proving regular invertibility without any unproved extension theorem. Injectivity into the meromorphic sheaf, supplied by the model, proves overlap agreement and uniqueness. Step 6 uses exactly the kernel clause on normal affine domains; zero divisor means units, not merely nonnegative orders. Step 7 applies the model's dense-open injection to the line of homomorphisms, whose local coefficients describe maps by the definition of a line; the comparison's full faithfulness handles the scheme clause. These are elementary consequences of the cited interfaces.

For step 8 I opened lem-trf-flat-birational-normalization-comparison. Its first conclusion explicitly says every non-zero-divisor maps to a non-zero-divisor under the flat map and gives the fraction formula. Take its finite intermediate algebra to be A itself; no normalization or integrally closed conclusion of that statement is used. The actual arbitrary-space pullback is supplied by lem-trf-space-enlarged-etale-ringed-site-model, whose statement I read through the ringed-topos and composition clauses. Refine the pulled-back chart by affine etale charts of X'; flatness is defined chartwise here, so the required ring maps are flat. A map of total fraction rings need not be an isomorphism or injective; only its action on units is used. Step 9 computes the tensor basis map and checks that 1 maps to 1. The orders identity follows from the already established Cartier/Weil dictionary and the definition by pulled-back equations; it does not invoke a dimension-graded flat cycle pullback or assume constant relative dimension.

Edge cases checked: the empty space uses empty products and zero divisor groups; finite disconnected unions use a generator on every generic component, not just a single nonzero coordinate. For a field there are no prime divisors and every nonzero scalar is a unit. For a DVR, f=pi^n gives O(D)=pi^{-n}O and order n for the rational section 1, including n<0. On a two-dimensional regular local ring the local products of prime equations have no extra codimension-two condition. For a flat ramified map of traits pi -> u t^e, the last step gives e times the order, whereas an etale map has e=1; the proof does not confuse these cases. For a non-surjective flat map a whole component may disappear, but units still map to units and the argument uses no faithfulness. A closed immersion of a point on the divisor is not flat, and no pullback through a zero denominator is claimed.
