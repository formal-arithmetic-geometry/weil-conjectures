### lem-sfb-pointed-galois-limit-geometry

Old step 1:

Use the finite-etale equivalence and the pointed open-subgroup correspondence. For each open normal subgroup $N\triangleleft\Pi$, choose the pointed connected cover $(Y_N,y_N)$ with fibre $\Pi/N$ and marked coset $N$. An equivariant endomorphism of $\Pi/N$ is right translation; these act simply transitively. Conversely, for a pointed transitive fibre $\Pi/U$, its equivariant automorphisms have orbit $N_\Pi(U)/U$ at $U$, so simple transitivity forces $U$ normal. Full faithfulness makes pointed arrows $Y_M\to Y_N$ unique and identifies them with $aM\mapsto aN$ for $M\subseteq N$. Thus this small directed poset (ordered by reverse inclusion, with common refinement $M\cap N$) is a skeleton of the pointed Galois covers. A cone on either indexing system is a compatible family on the other, by these pointed isomorphisms; the same holds for a cofinal system. We may therefore compute the specified limit with this poset. The finite-set equivalence also supplies a small skeleton within the stipulated universes.

Original uses: thm-galois-fundamental, cor-galois-open-subgroups, def-weil-size-convention.

Original source: {"document": "literature/stacks-project/pione.tex", "place": "Theorem 0BND (theorem-fundamental-group), (1); Lemma 0BN2 (lemma-galois); finite coset calculation"}.

Old step 2:

Every $Y_N\to X$ and every transition is finite etale: these are morphisms in the finite-etale category. They are affine by the definition of finite. Their domains are quasi-compact because the inverse images of a finite affine cover of $X$ are affine, hence quasi-compact. They are quasi-separated because finite morphisms are separated and the base is quasi-separated: the separated diagonal is a closed immersion, hence affine and quasi-compact, and the quasi-separated composition property applies. ### lem-sfb-profinite-scheme-product-components

Old step 10:
