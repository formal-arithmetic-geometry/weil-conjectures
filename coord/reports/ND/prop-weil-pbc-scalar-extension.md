## Argument handoffs for the four new unsplit nodes

These handoffs preserve the actual arguments for their future workers. They are not included as proof fields of the delivered unsplit nodes.

For complete-dvr-coefficient-basis: the valuation of t=wv^e makes the intersection of t^nB zero. B/tB has a length-e filtration with factors B/vB, hence is finite as a set and finite over A/tA. Apply Stacks 031D to the A-module B, then use its torsion-freeness and the DVR freeness theorem 0AUW. Reduce any resulting basis modulo t^n; B/t^nB=B/v^{en}B. Constant sheafification preserves this finite direct-sum isomorphism and its quotient maps. Limits commute with finite products in sheaves; the indices en are cofinal. This gives the specific basis map, with all projections, rather than an unspecified isomorphism. No completeness of B was assumed.

For finite-free-strict-tower-transport: scalar restriction is exact, reflects isomorphisms, and preserves K-injectives because its left adjoint tensor is exact. The derived restriction is a right adjoint and preserves derived products. After restriction, tensor with S is a finite sum of identities; this identifies the canonical product comparison as invertible. Construct the comparison on strict tower complexes from the ordinary tensor/limit comparison and the universal right-derived comparisons, using one resolution of the entire tower. The derived-product identification takes the full product arrow 1-s to the corresponding product arrow over S. Functorial total derived inverse limit supplies the cone map, including its connecting arrow; the two product maps and the triangle imply invertibility. The original chain-level augmented comparison gives the unit equation after derivation. This cannot be replaced by choosing a cone map in a bare triangulated category: such a choice does not determine a natural unit-compatible lambda. Restriction is used to detect invertibility, not to claim faithfulness on arbitrary derived morphisms. A basis is used only to prove that the canonical comparison is invertible, so it does not affect its definition.

For finite-free-completion-transport: apply strict-tower transport to M tensor [R --t^n--> R]. Balancing identifies its extended tower, including the t-transition in degree -1 and identity in degree zero, with CM tensor [S --a(t)^n--> S]. Stacks 0A0E identifies both total limits with their specified Koszul reflectors and identifies their augmentations. Because a(t)=wv^e, the maps S to S[1/a(t)] and S to S[1/v] agree under the canonical localization isomorphism. Thus the augmented extended Cech complexes are identified; derived internal Hom identifies their reflectors and units. This gives kappa on arbitrary M. Any two such maps preserving the unit agree by the reflector Hom bijection, since the target is complete. The same bijection applied after a further scalar extension proves coefficient-tower coherence; applying it to shifted units proves shift coherence. On complete M the unit equation proves preservation of completeness. The result does not assert equality of t^n and v^n quotients.

For reflected-adjunction-coefficient-squares: for local N, compose Hom(Lambda F M,N)=Hom(F M,N)=Hom(M,G N) to construct the reflected adjunction and fix its counit. Apply this description to delta, insert kappa C(eta)=eta, and transpose: the result is exactly beta restricted to local objects. For the displayed ambient gamma/kappa formula, precompose its two routes with C_A eta at G_0M. Naturality of beta and the two unit equations identify both composites with G_1(C_B eta_M) beta_M. C_A Lambda G_0M is a reflection of C_A G_0M, and the common target G_1 C_B Lambda M is local. Injectivity of the reflector Hom bijection gives the required equation. For pasting, expand the Hom bijections of the composed reflected adjunctions: they are the composites of the same original adjunction bijections and unit precompositions. Ordinary mate pasting therefore becomes reflected mate pasting. The identity case is the identity Hom bijection. Shift comparisons reduce in the same manner to their supplied unit and adjunction compatibility. This proves a reusable theorem about four arbitrary categories, without any finite-field, sheaf or base-change assumption.
