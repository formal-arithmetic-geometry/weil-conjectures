For step 3 I opened the basic-open statement again, `lem-weil-pbc-lfp-affine-opens-finite-presentation`, `def-weil-pbc-finitely-presented-algebra`, and `lem-weil-pbc-finite-presentation-base-change-composition`. The cited composition clause then really gives finite presentation over the original R, which is the hypothesis the final criterion needs.

For step 4 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-generic-fibre-localization-domain-irreducible` and `def-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra`. The former explicitly identifies the tensor product with the localization and says it is a domain; injectivity supplies its nonvanishing hypothesis. The rest of the step is elementary from that statement: the fraction s/r maps to the identical element of L, every fraction s/t in L is a quotient of nonzero-denominator elements from C, and the given finite set of algebra generators works over K. I marked this step elementary relative to the cited localization theorem, rather than introducing separate nodes for these fraction computations.

For step 5 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-smooth-at-generic-point-over-field`. The local criterion's three hypotheses are all present: a nonzero b with finite presentation over R (step 3), flatness of the map of local rings K to L, and smoothness of the correct fibre at its corresponding prime (step 5).
