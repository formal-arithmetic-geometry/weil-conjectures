I read `morphisms.tex` at 02FZ and its surrounding discussion defining dimension at a point, `algebra.tex` at 00QD and 00QH (including its proof via 00QE and 00QG), `schemes.tex` at the affine fibre-product formula and 01K0/01K1, and `topology.tex` at 0055 and the following open-neighbourhood remark. These sources distinguish the minimum of dimensions of open neighbourhoods from the dimension of a local ring. I also opened the statements of all seven nodes cited in the proof; citations.json copies their statements verbatim. There are no new-node statements or new-source obligations to review.

Step 1 uses the actual affine cover in `def-weil-finite-type-morphism`, not an unproved claim about arbitrary affine opens. Its statement supplies a cover with finite type R-algebras over each affine target open, so choosing the member containing x supplies exactly the finite type hypothesis later required by 00QH. `def-weil-affine-spectrum` identifies the chosen chart with its prime spectrum. This cover-member choice is elementary. It does not require a finite cover, quasi-compactness, or a Noetherian base.

Step 2 uses the last sentence of `def-pc-scheme-fibre`, which says that each fibre is homeomorphic to the subspace of points mapping to s. The fibre of the restricted morphism consequently has the topology of W intersected with that subspace, an open subset because W is open in X. `lem-weil-pbc-dimension-at-point-open-subspace` states precisely the equality of local dimensions on that open subset. `lem-sbc-affine-coordinate-fibre-dimension` has both source and target affine, exactly as constructed in step 1, and identifies the distinguished fibre point and its local dimension with the ring-map fibre prime. This is its statement, not a construction taken from its proof. I made the tensor-factor order explicit: swapping the factors sends the residue-field evaluation kernel to the same distinguished prime, and the resulting homeomorphism preserves open neighbourhoods and irreducible chains. This avoids silently identifying a local-ring dimension with the required topological dimension.

Step 3 uses `def-kun-topological-dimension-at-point` for its explicitly stated range of nonnegative integers and infinity. Membership in U_n excludes infinity, so there is an actual integer d between 0 and n. The existing 00QH node requires a finite type ring map and equality of the local fibre dimension with d at a specified prime; step 1 gives the former and step 2 gives the latter. Its conclusion supplies an open neighbourhood with bound d at every prime in it. I checked that it assumes neither a Noetherian ring nor a finite-presentation map. Applying it with d rather than directly with n is necessary, since its hypothesis is equality, not an inequality.

Step 4 uses the affine spectrum identification to pull that open neighbourhood back to W. An open subset of the open W is open in X. The dimension equality proved for every y in step 2 transfers the bound from the ring spectrum back to the original fibre, and d <= n gives the desired bound. Taking the union of these neighbourhoods is elementary and proves openness, including the empty-locus case.

I tested the proof on the empty source (empty union), n=0 (d=0 and the same 00QH application), the identity morphism (point fibres of dimension zero), affine space over a field (constant local topological fibre dimension), and a locally finite type morphism with a non-quasi-compact source (only one cover member is chosen at each point). No step needs properness, global finite type, flatness, or finite dimension of all fibres. The key correction to the otherwise terse source reduction was to explain open-subspace invariance, the distinguished prime, and the equality hypothesis d of 00QH explicitly. No statements were corrected or weakened.
