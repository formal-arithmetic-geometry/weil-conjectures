For step 1 I read all four cited statements. `lem-sfb-solvable-normal-subgroup-products` explicitly says that smooth connected groups remain so after every field extension and that solvability remains true. Thus Lie–Kolchin's algebraically closed, smooth, connected, solvable hypotheses hold over F. The new triangular-kernel node gives the actual intersection with U_n, its scheme normality, unipotence and smooth connectedness, and the closed diagonal image. The existing multiplicative-type node explicitly says a smooth connected group of multiplicative type is a torus. I do not infer smoothness of a kernel merely from smoothness of its source: Milne 16.10 obtains it from the splitting in 16.26(a).

For step 2, the orthogonality node says the composite W to J/N is the trivial group-scheme morphism, even for a nonsmooth unipotent W. The quotient-geometry node says the kernel of the quotient morphism is exactly N scheme-theoretically. These give containment, then equality by applying containment twice. The argument is written in the step; it is not a new uniqueness node. This excludes the erroneous geometric-point-only inference that would fail for infinitesimal subgroups.

For step 3, I checked Section 1.54(b): it permits infinite Galois extensions and requires stability of the defining ideal, not merely of the rational-point set. Perfectness makes the algebraic closure a separable, hence Galois, extension; uniqueness from step 2 gives equality of Galois conjugate subgroup schemes and therefore ideal stability. The descent node gives N with N_F=V and descent of normality. The existing Mathlib smooth-descent leaf requires a flat quasi-compact surjective base morphism: Spec F to Spec k has all three properties for a field extension. Connectedness descends by the displayed clopen-set argument. Orthogonality's field-extension and geometric-point comparison assertions supply unipotence with the item's exact meaning. Finally quotient-geometry gives the fppf representing object and its base change D. Since D is already a split torus over the algebraic closure, this is the definition of a torus over k; the proof does not rely on a separate torus-descent theorem.

For step 4 I specifically checked imperfect extensions K/k. One must not apply the perfect-field theorem again over K. Instead the base-changed kernel is smooth and connected by the existing scalar-extension node, is unipotent by the new scalar-extension comparison, and has quotient Q_K by quotient-geometry. Multiplicative type persists under scalar extension by the explicit new node clause. Step 2, which has no perfectness hypothesis, gives its universal characterization, maximality and uniqueness. Step 5 writes both inclusions under an automorphism and its inverse, so it is elementary relative to the preceding characterization; it needs no additional cited theorem. The faithful representation can be pulled back along the automorphism, so the item’s representation-based definition is respected.

For step 6 the generic normality criterion is exactly equality of subgroup schemes under each geometric conjugation. H normal in G gives an actual automorphism of H_F; step 5 preserves the subgroup. The criterion then gives normality over every k-algebra. I checked that G is smooth as required, and did not silently replace this with connectedness or drop smoothness. The source explicitly warns that the analogous assertion may fail for nonsmooth ambient groups.
