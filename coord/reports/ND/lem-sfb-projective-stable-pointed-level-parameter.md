### Returned Step 3, verbatim

Construct by induction $M_r$ with a map $a_r:M_r\to\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,r}$ and an H-projective structural morphism to $\operatorname{Spec}k$. The base is $M_0,a_0$ from Step 2. Given them, let $X_r\to M_r$ be the stable $r$-pointed family they specify, and put $M_{r+1}=X_r$. By [lem-sfb-knudsen-pointed-forgetful-universal-curve], this scheme represents $M_r\times_{\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,r}}\overline{\mathcal M}_{g,r+1}$ and hence supplies $a_{r+1}$. Its map to $M_r$ is projective in the bundle sense, flat and finitely presented. The induction hypothesis gives a closed embedding $M_r\hookrightarrow\mathbf P^N_k$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-projective-ample-invertible] with $A=k$ and this embedding: $M_r$ admits an ample invertible sheaf. Thus [lem-trf-projective-bundle-to-h-projective] applies to $X_r\to M_r$ and makes it H-projective. Now [lem-trf-h-projective-composition] makes $M_{r+1}\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ H-projective, establishing the induction. These structural morphisms are proper by [lem-pc-projective-proper], hence of finite type by [def-weil-proper-morphism]. Take $M=M_n$ and its specified family $(\mathcal U\to M,t_1,\ldots,t_n)$. An H-projective embedding is a bundle-projective embedding using $\mathcal O^{N+1}$ in [def-trf-projective-bundle-morphism]. This proves the required projectivity conventions, including $n=0$, without auxiliary markings.

### Returned Step 5, verbatim

Fix $F/k$ and the smooth marked curve $(C,p_1,\ldots,p_n)$ from (b). For any $F$-scheme $B$, repeated use of the 2-cartesian squares in Step 3 identifies an $m:B\to M_n$ with a stable $n$-pointed family $Y/B$, a map $m_0:B\to M_0$, and an isomorphism from the stabilization of $Y$ after forgetting all markings to $X_{0,m_0}$. If $\alpha:Y\xrightarrow{\sim} C_B$ preserves each marking, forgetting them leaves the smooth genus-$g$ curve $C_B$, already stable because $g\geq2$. By the smooth-locus clause of [lem-sfb-knudsen-pointed-forgetful-universal-curve], all these stabilizations are the identity. The pair $(m,\alpha)$ therefore gives exactly $(m_0,\beta:X_{0,m_0}\xrightarrow{\sim}C_B)$. Conversely, from $(m_0,\beta)$ put the sections $\beta^{-1}(p_{i,B})$ on $X_{0,m_0}$ and apply those same 2-cartesian squares successively. They are smooth and disjoint, so stabilization changes nothing; they give $m$ and $\alpha$. The two constructions undo one another, including their isomorphism data, and commute with pullback in $B$. Uniqueness here is in the discrete functor of points of the representing schemes, so no quotient by $\operatorname{Aut}(C)$ or by permutations of the labels is taken.

Uses: lem-sfb-knudsen-pointed-forgetful-universal-curve, def-sfb-stable-pointed-moduli-groupoids

### Returned Step 6, verbatim

Since $C_B$ is smooth, the 2-cartesian square of [lem-sfb-stable-abelian-level-normalization] identifies pairs $(m_0,\beta)$ in Step 5 with the 2-fibre over $C_B$ of $L_{g,\ell}\to\mathcal M_{g,0}$. The composite of these equivalences gives, for every $F$-scheme $B$, the required bijection between pairs $(m,\alpha)$ and $F$-maps $B\to P_C$, naturally under pullback. Surjectivity over the nonempty scheme $\operatorname{Spec}F$ implies nonemptiness of $P_C$. A frame exists geometrically because $\mathcal H$ has rank $2g$, even if it has no frame over $F$ itself. Thus (b) holds for all field extensions, without assuming $k$ or $F$ algebraically closed and without any rationality of level structures.
