## Sources and retained arguments for the new nodes

The first three nodes expose the comparisons HA B.2.4(a) leaves implicit. The fourth exposes the section calculation behind B.2.4(c). These are finer technical lemmas, not a restatement of the edge-detection theorem: none concludes that the pattern mapping test detects equivalences or that all marked domains are good.

For local/global comparison: a global lift remains cocartesian on pullback by its horn lifting property. If a local edge u and a chosen global lift v share a source and base edge, both are initial lifts in the edge pullback. The initial-lift node gives their comparison triangle and an equivalence of targets in the vertex fibre. A fibre equivalence is globally cocartesian by the mapping-space criterion (top map an equivalence and bottom map homotopic to the identity). Cocartesian composition transfers the property from v to u. Over an identity edge, compare with the degenerate lift; this proves marked equals fibre-equivalence for vertical edges. Global lifts exist over S by the pattern definition. I have not asserted global/local agreement for an arbitrary inner fibration without that lift hypothesis.

For invariance over the interval: apply the cocartesian mapping criterion to an edge and every target. An ordinary quasi-inverse to the underlying equivalence gives equivalences on mapping spaces by conjugation with the inverse natural equivalences; composition is associative and unital up to homotopy by the existing composition node. The lower mapping spaces are unchanged since the map is strictly over Delta^1. Essential surjectivity covers remaining targets, and their equivalences lie over identities because Delta^1 has no nonidentity equivalences. Homotopy-pullback invariance gives preservation and reflection. This does not choose or assume a quasi-inverse over the base.

For mapping components: in a simplex W^flat times (Delta^n)^sharp, vertical marked edges become fibre equivalences. The naturality squares for a marked edge of W have those vertical equivalences as sides. Cocartesian composition/cancellation transfers its marked status between parameter vertices. The triangles also mark every diagonal product edge once all vertex diagrams preserve the marking. Thus the marked mapping complex is the full subset on marking-preserving vertices and a union of components. The item itself retains the elementary homotopy-group argument that a weak equivalence restricts to corresponding unions; that assertion was not moved wholesale into this node.

For section cores: projections over Delta^0 or Delta^1 are isofibrations since base equivalences are identities. Exponentiation at the empty-to-B monomorphism gives isofibrations Fun(B,E) and Fun(B,F) over Fun(B,B). Postcomposition by an underlying equivalence is an equivalence using its ordinary quasi-inverse and pointwise inverse transformations. The base-change node at id_B gives equivalent strict section quasicategories. Their quasi-inverses restrict to cores. For the identification, an n-simplex in the displayed marked mapping object is a family of sections with vertical components fibre equivalences. The pointwise-equivalence criterion detects these transformations as equivalences. Conversely, inverse witnesses remain in the section fibre: Fun(B,B) is the nerve of a poset, so every simplex all of whose vertices equal id_B is constant. This also shows the equality naturally in postcomposition. The new statement introduces E,F,B,u and the vertical-only marking itself; it does not leave X,Y undefined or assume (2) from the item.
