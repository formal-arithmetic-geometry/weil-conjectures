This is a definition node, so there are no proof steps to audit, no proof dependencies, and no hidden application of a theorem in a proof. I audited each clause against the STATEMENTS of the definition dependencies, rather than treating a definition as a theorem that establishes something else:

- For reversal, `def-weil-reverse-polynomial` states the coefficient reversal and the notation T^deg(P) P(1/T). `def-weil-polynomial-ring` and `def-weil-polynomial-coefficient` supply the degrees and coefficient extraction. `def-weil-polynomial-root` supplies evaluation in an arbitrary field extension. I did not use the specialized `def-weil-ordinary-reciprocal-roots` as a general field theorem: its statement fixes a geometric cohomological family. Instead the generic normalized-polynomial definitions remain explicit in this item. The normalization P(0)=1 makes every ordinary root nonzero and avoids a spurious zero reciprocal root of the reverse: the reverse's constant coefficient is the nonzero leading coefficient of P. For nonzero alpha, the identity rev(P)(alpha)=alpha^deg(P) P(alpha^(-1)) is just the finite-sum formula, so the two root conventions agree.
- `def-weil-algebraic-closure` genuinely defines fixed algebraic closures, and `def-weil-l-adic-numbers` genuinely defines Q_l; the parenthetical l-adic application means the same formulas over that field. I did not assert or use an isomorphism of valued/topological fields Q_l-bar with C. Iota is a fixed abstract field embedding. The integral factors are taken as given; neither the theorem that constructs them nor the geometric canonical Betti-number construction is a dependency of these formulas. This also prevents the forward reference from becoming a cycle.
- For Weil numbers, `def-weil-numbers` states exactly the all-complex-embeddings condition for a nonzero algebraic number and the fixed prime power q. It does not require algebraic integrality; I have not added such a requirement. The existing complex-absolute-value and positive-real-power nodes state the norm and exponent notions actually used. The generic purity clause retains q,w >= 0 and the usual real exponents, not natural-number division. Since the existing power node states only the positive-base definition, the zero-base convention is written explicitly in the item.
- Universal purity uses `def-weil-algebraic-element`, whose statement is precisely existence of a nonzero rational annihilating polynomial, and the existing minimal-polynomial definition in `def-weil-pbc-separable-algebraic-extension`. I read Stacks `fields.tex` at `fields-definition-minimal-polynomial`, Tag 09GM, lines 832–863: it constructs the unique monic generator of the annihilator ideal and characterizes it by least degree. I read Mathlib `FieldTheory/Minpoly/Basic.lean:28–55,89,134` and `FieldTheory/Minpoly/Field.lean:51`, and `RingTheory/Algebraic/Integral.lean:63–78`: `minpoly` has the indicated properties under IsIntegral, and `IsAlgebraic.isIntegral` supplies precisely this hypothesis over a field. This confirms the existing node supplies the standard minimal-polynomial notion, including x=0; its first sentence assumes algebraicity, not separability. The roots used for scalar universal purity are roots of the rational minimal polynomial mapped to C, not roots of a polynomial over C with no rational coefficients. The polynomial universal-purity predicate quantifies over ORDINARY roots, as do both the source text and the code at Weights.lean:83; that definition's docstring incorrectly says reciprocal roots, and I did not follow that docstring.
- The Betti formula, determinant-constant formula and alternating degree sum are explicit finite formulas over the cited polynomial/degree/coefficient notions. I checked that `alternatingNatDegreeSum` uses integer signs and coerced natural degrees. The general family is allowed to contain zero polynomials, so the natural degree of zero is explicitly 0, while the normalized integral factors are nonzero. I avoided citing `def-weil-determinant-constants` for this arbitrary family because its statement includes geometric determinant identities and the special range 0..2d that this definition does not need.
- `def-weil-hankel-matrix` states both the entries c_(r+s) and the supremum of ranks of finite square submatrices, exactly the meanings used here. Its statement, not any row-span theorem from its proof, is what the dependency supplies. The item does not assert the non-elementary equivalence of finite Hankel rank with finite-dimensional shift span.
- For the shift span I read the exact source clause and the project construction `ShiftSpan.lean:57–77`: m iterates of the left shift have nth entry c_(n+m), so the source's displayed family is correct. The new general span leaf is the literal Mathlib construction in `LinearAlgebra/Span/Defs.lean:48`, an infimum of all submodules containing the subset; `subset_span:77` and `span_le:82` state the defining least-submodule property. The formal-power-series dependency is used only to identify this sequence module with coefficient sequences, exactly as `def-weil-formal-power-series` states; it is not used as a span theorem. No finite generation or field hypothesis was introduced, and no recurrence or rank theorem is used.
- `def-weil-normalized-integral-fraction` states normalization, coprimality in Q[T], and the Taylor-series meaning of A/B, exactly the last clause. It is a predicate on a given pair, not an existence theorem: I did not use Fatou's lemma or claim this leaf produces integral factors. Its statement expands BZ=A in Z[[T]], which is the precise meaning now written in the item.
