### Original step 2 → lem-dl-a1-field-point-full-faithful-galois-map

For a finite etale $X$-scheme $P$, restrict to $\operatorname{Spec}K$ and act on its geometric generic lifts by $\sigma\cdot t=t\circ\operatorname{Spec}(\widetilde\sigma)$. The field Galois equivalence identifies this with the continuous action on $\operatorname{Hom}_K(\operatorname{Spec}\Omega,P_K)$; the extension to $\widetilde\Omega$ does not change these lifts. Naturality in $P$ gives an automorphism of its fibre functor, hence an element $q_X(\sigma)$ of $\pi_1(X,\bar\eta)$ under the finite-etale/fibre equivalence. Composition satisfies $(\sigma\tau)\cdot t=\sigma\cdot(\tau\cdot t)$, since Spec reverses compositions. Every evaluation is continuous on a finite discrete fibre, so $q_X$ is continuous for the evaluation topology. Functoriality identifies this map with the one induced by $\operatorname{Spec}K\to X$.

### Original step 3 → lem-dl-a1-field-point-full-faithful-galois-map

Put $\Pi=\pi_1(X,\bar\eta)$ and $H=q_X(G)$. The group $G$ is compact: by the Krull limit theorem (applicable since $\Omega/K$ is Galois) it is isomorphic as a topological group to the inverse limit of the finite discrete groups $\operatorname{Gal}(L'/K)$, a closed subset of their product, which is compact. $\Pi$ is profinite, hence Hausdorff. The image $H$ is therefore compact and closed. If $H\ne\Pi$, profinite subgroup separation gives a proper open subgroup $W\supset H$. Its coset set $\Pi/W$ is finite (its discrete cosets cover compact $\Pi$) and continuous, and the fibre equivalence realizes it by a finite etale cover $P$ of $X$. The coset $W$ is fixed by $H$, hence gives a $G$-equivariant map from a singleton to the generic fibre of $P$. The field equivalence gives a section over $\operatorname{Spec}K$. Generic-point full faithfulness extends it to a section over $X$. Under the fibre equivalence the chosen coset is then fixed by every element of $\Pi$, forcing $W=\Pi$, a contradiction. Thus $q_X$ is surjective.

### Original step 4 → lem-dl-a1-strict-local-inertia-generic-fibres

Let $N$ be the smallest closed normal subgroup containing the $J_x$. For a finite etale $P\to X$, its pullback to $\operatorname{Spec}A_x^{\mathrm{sh}}$ is finite etale. The strict henselization $A_x^{\mathrm{sh}}$ is a strictly henselian local ring, so it is henselian with separably closed residue field. Henselian finite-etale equivalence identifies its algebra with a finite etale algebra over the separably closed residue field. The field product classification says the latter is a finite product of that residue field; full faithfulness of the equivalence therefore identifies the original algebra with a finite product of $A_x^{\mathrm{sh}}$. Its generic fibre is consequently a disjoint union of copies of $\operatorname{Spec}K_x^{\mathrm{sh}}$. Since $J_x$ fixes this field, it acts trivially on the geometric fibre of $P$. This holds for every $P$, so $q_X(J_x)=1$. The kernel of the continuous homomorphism is closed and normal, and therefore $N\subseteq\ker q_X$.

### Original step 5 → lem-dl-a1-open-normal-inertia-fixed-field

Fix an open normal subgroup $U\supseteq N$ of $G$. The open-subgroup fixed-field theorem gives a finite separable extension $L=\Omega^U$ and $U=\operatorname{Gal}(\Omega/L)$. For $g\in G$, $u\in U$, $a\in L$, one has $u(g(a))=g((g^{-1}ug)(a))=g(a)$, so $g(L)=L$. Any $K$-embedding $L\to\Omega$ extends to a $K$-endomorphism of $\Omega$, which is an automorphism because $\Omega/K$ is algebraic. Thus all embeddings carry $L$ into itself; $L/K$ is normal and hence finite Galois. We will prove that $\ker q_X\subseteq U$ by constructing its finite etale normalization cover.

### Original step 6 → lem-dl-a1-strict-local-inertia-generic-fibres

Two $K$-embeddings $i,j:K_x^{\mathrm{sh}}\to\Omega$ have conjugate fixing subgroups: the isomorphism $j\circ i^{-1}:i(K_x^{\mathrm{sh}})\to j(K_x^{\mathrm{sh}})$ extends to a $K$-endomorphism of $\Omega$ by the embedding-extension theorem, and the algebraic-endomorphism theorem makes it an automorphism $g$. Pointwise evaluation then gives $\operatorname{Aut}_{j(K_x^{\mathrm{sh}})}(\Omega)=g\operatorname{Aut}_{i(K_x^{\mathrm{sh}})}(\Omega)g^{-1}$. For a prime over $x$ in the integral closure in $\Omega$, localize the affine base at $x$. Integral closure commutes with this localization and its prime over the maximal ideal is maximal. The local inertia comparison identifies the inertia of this prime with the fixing subgroup of one such embedding. Localization does not change its fraction residue field or stabilizer. Hence every such inertia subgroup is conjugate to the chosen $J_x$, and is contained in $N$.

### Original step 7 → lem-dl-a1-open-normal-inertia-fixed-field (lying over and restriction), lem-dl-a1-strict-local-inertia-generic-fibres (normal affine coordinate ring)

Take a nonempty affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $X$. Its ring is a domain with fraction field $K$, and is integrally closed: an element $z\in K$ integral over $A$ belongs to every $A_{\mathfrak p}$ by normality of the stalks; the ideal $\{a\in A:az\in A\}$ is therefore contained in no maximal ideal, so contains $1$. ### Original step 8 → lem-dl-a1-trivial-inertia-finite-etale-normalization

Let $\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$. It acts on $B$ because it preserves monic equations over $A$. Its invariant ring is $A$: an invariant element of $B$ belongs to $K$ by finite Galois fixed-field theory and then belongs to $A$ by the integrally closed property proved in step 7; the reverse inclusion is immediate. At every prime $\mathfrak q$ of $B$, step 7 gives $I_{\mathfrak q}=1$, so the inertia-invariant-ring theorem says that $\operatorname{Spec}(B)=\operatorname{Spec}(B^{I_{\mathfrak q}})\to\operatorname{Spec}(B^\Gamma)=\operatorname{Spec}A$ is etale near $\mathfrak q$. These neighbourhoods cover $\operatorname{Spec}B$. The map is integral by construction and locally of finite type because it is etale, hence is finite. Apply this on every affine open $V$. The relative normalization $Y\to X$ of $X$ in $\operatorname{Spec}L$ is given there by these same rings $B$, so is finite etale globally. Here $\operatorname{Spec}L\to X$ is qcqs: its inverse image over every nonempty affine is $\operatorname{Spec}L$, and it is separated. No finiteness of normalization has been assumed.

The generic fibre of $Y$ is $\operatorname{Spec}L$: integral closure commutes with localization to $K$, and the integral closure of $K$ in the algebraic field extension $L$ is all of $L$. ### Original step 10 → parent step 5

Finally $N$ is the intersection of its open normal overgroups. Indeed, profinite subgroup separation expresses $N$ as the intersection of its open overgroups $W$. For each such $W$, its finite coset action has open normal kernel $U_W\subseteq W$.
