## Findings and corrections

The original item is true as stated; neither its hypotheses nor its conclusions were changed. It was an unsplit child of constructibility descent. I read the current main-20261015 ancestor, `lem-trf-local-term-locality`, and the recorded chapter and unit. This is infrastructure for its existing nearby-cycle constructibility branch; it does not replace the unit's contracting-correspondence argument. The item is not the unit's key node, so no realized fields were edited.

A potential citation error was avoided: `lem-trf-oriented-product-points-conservative` states conservativity for module sheaves and derived categories, whereas this item concerns inverse image on sheaves of sets. I did not use its proof as though it were its statement. Instead step 4 uses the existing finite-site enough-points theorem, the universal property, and the existing classification of small-etale-topos points; step 7 applies that set-valued enough-points statement directly.

The abstract coherence lemma was checked without assuming that the site is subcanonical. Its final formulation uses the finite-limit closure *in the sheaf category* of the associated representables. It does not claim that arbitrary sheaf morphisms between these generators come from morphisms in the original site. The initial draft's stronger formulation was corrected before delivery.

The lifting statement explicitly retains the specified geometric specialization. Merely lifting a Zariski specialization and forgetting the arrow would not justify the identification of the oriented-product point. Field extensions are allowed and are removed by canonical stalk isomorphisms, rather than by an unjustified equality of chosen geometric points. The proof does not choose an arbitrary point of the special fibre and demand a generalization of that chosen lift.

I read Orgogozo §8.1's triple-site discussion and the whole proof of Lemma 9.1.5. Its two short claims, coherence on the sites and lifting points, are precisely the claims expanded here. I also read the local Exposé XI construction, universal inverse-image formula, point description and proof of 2.5, and the Stacks source at 01T6 (`lemma-finite-type-noetherian`), 01TS (`lemma-base-change-finite-presentation`), 01OY (`lemma-locally-Noetherian-quasi-separated`), 01KE (`lemma-lift-specializations-valuative`) and 0GJ5 (`remark-can-lift`). I checked the actual statements of every cited DAG node, not just their sources or proofs.

1. Step 1: `def-weil-proper-morphism` explicitly includes finite type. The finite-type-over-Noetherian node gives Noetherian source, exactly what is used for T, X and X_T. Finite presentation implies finite type immediately from the affine algebra presentations in the conventions. Clause (3) of `lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence` explicitly preserves finite presentation under arbitrary base change, so the argument does not extract that fact from `lem-pc-proper-base-change`, which does not state it. The locally-Noetherian-quasi-separated node gives exactly the extra scheme hypothesis needed for the global sites. No separatedness of S or X is added.

2. Step 2: the new triple presentation applies to qcqs diagrams. Step 1 supplies qcqs for all four schemes and for f and f_T; the identity arrows meet the same hypotheses. The new abstract criterion applies to small sites with finite limits and finite-cover pretopologies. These are expressly supplied by the site statement. Its conclusion gives the coherent generators and coherent topoi in the definitions. Both assertions are cited, not inferred from the definition of coherence alone.

3. Step 3: I checked the three components of Z_T against the new site's exact base-change formula. In the vanishing case Y=S, Y'=T, the third component is W times_S T; the first is U times_X X_T. The formula is about associated representables and requires no subcanonical-site assumption. The morphism criterion explicitly promotes preservation of the chosen generators to preservation of every coherent object; that promotion is not left implicit. Properness and surjectivity are not used here.

4. Step 4: `lem-trf-finitary-site-enough-points` states joint reflection on *sheaves of sets*. Its finite-limit and finite-refinement assumptions hold for the global site. The universal-property node gives existence and uniqueness of a point from the projections and structural 2-arrow. `lem-trf-etale-topos-points-geometric` states that every abstract point of each small etale topos is geometric. Conjugating the arrow, rather than discarding it, proves the claimed point description.

5. Step 5: the new lifting node introduces arbitrary schemes, a proper surjective map, both geometric points and the prescribed arrow, with the same direct-image 2-morphism convention as the universal-property node. It supplies extensions as actual embeddings and the arrow compatibility used later. Its source's omitted geometric lifting detail remains a genuine smaller scheme lemma, not an elementary claim or a restatement of vanishing-topos conservativity. For its eventual proof one first works over the strict localization at the special geometric point, encoding the given arrow, lifts the general point through the proper surjection, specializes its closure to the closed fibre, and uses compatible strict-local geometric points. Working over that strict localization is what preserves the chosen specialization.

6. Step 6: the fibre-product definition's statement gives a unique map from the common source Spec(Omega'_s), since p s' = f x r_s. Thus the construction requires no field amalgamation theorem. The universal-property node makes the resulting triple a point. The geometric-point-morphism-stalk-bijection node states precisely the canonical isomorphism of stalk functors under each field extension. Combined with the already stated arrow compatibility, universal uniqueness identifies v z_T with z. This is an elementary assembly of the cited constructions, not a new lifting theorem.

7. Step 7: the finite-site enough-points statement is applied to the set-sheaf map a. For each point in its family, step 6 gives an actual lifted point up to isomorphism, so composition of inverse images identifies the relevant stalk map with the image of v^{-1}a. The latter has an inverse, hence so do all stalk maps. The cited criterion returns an inverse for a; `def-trf-coherent-topos` then identifies this as conservativity. No passage through modules or derived categories occurs.
