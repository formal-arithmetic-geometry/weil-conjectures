For Step 1 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-vector-space-free`: its statement explicitly gives a basis of any vector space. For Step 2 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-intersection-subfields-subspace`: it is about any K-linear subspace of K^n, not about just scalar elements or differential kernels. Our relation space is genuinely K-linear because the target L is a K-vector space. Its stated nonempty-index and n>=1 hypotheses both hold. The intersection assumption implies that K^p lies in every K_alpha; downward directedness is assumed. The contrapositive gives one beta killing every coefficient relation, and smaller coefficient fields inherit this property. We do not infer eventual vanishing from mere pointwise differential detection.

Step 3 cites the actual universal-property statement of `def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials`. The displayed derivation calculation and the inverse formula show that derivations kill the entire generated subfield, not just its generators. The two universal comparison maps are inverses because both fix every exact differential. This explicitly handles K' that does not contain K^p, which the item's unrestricted quantifier requires. E remains contained in K_beta; F=EL^p follows because the embedded K^p is contained in L^p.

For Step 4 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-frobenius-basis-compositum`: it asserts that the field generated by L^p and S is exactly the S-span of the pth powers of any finite K-basis. It does **not** assert independence. Its sole extra condition S contains K^p holds for E by construction; independence comes from Step 2. The resulting degree is n.

For Step 5 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-field-degree-multiplicativity`: its statement gives finiteness and the tower equality. The original K'-spanning set spans K over E, and any E-spanning set of L also spans over F, so every application has finite extensions. I checked both towers E<=K<=L and E<=F<=L separately. Cancellation uses n>0, rather than cancelling a possibly zero finrank in an infinite-dimensional space.

For Step 6 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-p-independent-extends-to-p-basis` and `lem-weil-pbc-p-basis-differentials-basis`. The former is relative, with arbitrary characteristic-p base field, so applying it to the empty family really constructs the required p-bases of K/E and L/F. The latter states exactly that their differentials form bases. Since E contains K^p and F contains L^p, the bases of monomials are over E and F themselves. The index sets must be finite by the independent single-index monomials in finite-dimensional vector spaces; counting all p-bounded exponent choices gives p^r and p^s. Thus injectivity of natural-number powers with p>=2, together with Step 5, identifies r and s. I marked this step non-elementary to make explicit its reliance on both cited p-basis theorems, while leaving the short counting and cancellation arguments in the step.
