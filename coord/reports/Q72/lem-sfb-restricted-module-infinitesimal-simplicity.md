The normalized-algebra lemma's hypotheses are met: S is smooth connected affine finite type, V is rational simple and nonzero, A is a unital subalgebra of End(V), T is split, and step 1 gives the multiplicity-one weight space. For this first new node I read Milne Exercise 4-2 (Clifford theory, p.97), the Grassmannian construction 7.29, torus decomposition Theorem 12.12 (pp.234--235), and Borel fixed point Corollary 17.3 (p.353). The exact operator-algebra formulation is an agent lemma. Its argument is as follows. The sum of simple A-submodules (the A-socle) is nonzero by choosing an A-submodule of minimal positive dimension, and is G(k)-stable because G(k) normalizes A. This is a fixed vector subspace; smoothness and algebraic closedness make G(k) dense, so its closed subspace stabilizer is all G (Milne 4.3--4.5). Rational simplicity forces the socle to be V. A finite sum of simple submodules is semisimple: adjoining a simple summand either intersects the previous sum trivially or is already contained in it. Hence V is semisimple as an A-module. In a nonzero T-stable A-submodule choose the minimal positive dimension d of an A-submodule. The A-stable d-subspaces form a nonempty closed subset of the projective Grassmannian: stability is the vanishing of the finitely many maps from the tautological subbundle into the universal quotient, one for each element of a basis of A. Each point is A-simple by minimality, and the subset is T-stable since T normalizes A. Borel fixed point for the smooth connected solvable torus gives a T-stable simple A-submodule E. An A-linear retraction onto E exists by semisimplicity. The T-action by conjugation makes Hom_A(V,E) a rational T-module. Restriction to E is T-equivariant; take the weight-zero component of the retraction, whose restriction remains id_E. Its kernel is a T-stable A-linear complement. Repeat on that complement to obtain a direct-sum decomposition into T-stable A-simple modules. The associated projections preserve each T-weight space. If v lies in a one-dimensional weight space, only one projection of v can be nonzero: otherwise two nonzero vectors in distinct direct summands would lie in that same line. Thus v lies in a single A-simple summand and Av is that summand. This argument also explains why merely citing Clifford theory for a normal abstract subgroup would have been inadequate. No unproved characteristic-zero complete reducibility is used.

The formula consequently lands in D_R for all R; it is not only a formula on rational points.

For the second new node I read Milne Propositions 22.14--22.17 (root-orbit coefficients, Weyl symmetry, primitive cyclic weight bound) and the SL2 lattice description in 22.33. Here is the independent argument I checked. The orbit x_-(a)v is polynomial because the action is rational on the affine line; expand it with finitely many vector coefficients. Work in the rational cyclic SL2-submodule generated by v. For SL2 the integer weights are at most m by 22.17 (the root is integer 2), and are at least -m because the Weyl element exchanges weight mu with -mu. Conjugating x_-(a) by diag(t,t^-1) shows coefficient w_j has character m-2j, so a nonzero coefficient must have j<=m. This is a character-lattice equality, valid also when the differentials of two characters coincide in small characteristic. Constant coefficient is v. Compare the epsilon coefficient in x_-(a+epsilon)v=x_-(epsilon)x_-(a)v to obtain f w_j=(j+1)w_(j+1). Induction gives f^j v=j!w_j, without division. For m<p, all factorials used are invertible, giving the claimed truncated exponential. No assumption that the restricted SL2 module is simple or semisimple is used. This is more than an elementary restatement of the weight-bound node: it adds orbit degree and the exact differential-coefficient relationship in arbitrary characteristic.

Step 4 uses the elementary normalization identity on Av and also on inverse root elements (parameters -a), so gets equality, not only inclusion. The same argument works for T because lambda(t) is a unit. The stabilizer is written explicitly with both matrix and inverse-matrix lower-left blocks, so it is a closed subgroup scheme even on nonreduced test rings. The cited simple-root generation node explicitly concerns the smallest closed subgroup containing T and all positive/negative simple root subgroup schemes; it therefore applies exactly to this K. It also states the empty-base case. The universal R=F[S] point translates preservation into the coaction inclusion, so rational simplicity can be applied in step 5. Step 5 then only unfolds the generated algebra: any Lie-stable subspace is A-stable. It does not use an unproved assertion that all Lie-stable spaces are rational submodules.
