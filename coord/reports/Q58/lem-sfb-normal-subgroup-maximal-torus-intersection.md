For step 1 I opened each cited statement. The base-change node says the projection of a closed immersion along any scheme morphism is closed; its application is M -> G base changed along T -> G. The subgroup definition tests on all commutative algebras, where the fibre product is exactly intersection. The closed-immersion and finite-type definitions justify the quotient-coordinate argument without any image-factorization theorem. The identity-component node says the neutral component is open and closed and its reduction is a smooth connected integral subgroup. The reduction definition is the actual quotient by the nilradical, with unchanged underlying space. Restricting that quotient to the open identity component proves H = (D_red)^0. The largest-subtorus statement explicitly says that every smooth connected closed subgroup scheme of a torus is a torus; that is the clause used. No claim that D is reduced or smooth is made. Composites of the affine closed inclusions are closed because their coordinate maps are composites of surjections.

For step 2 the same largest-subtorus statement supplies smoothness and connectedness of a torus, and the smooth-geometrically-reduced statement applies to the structure morphism over F, giving reducedness. The nilradical maps to zero in a reduced source, so the map factors through reduction. A connected image containing the identity is in that component, which is open by step 1, giving the further factorization. The induced map is a closed immersion since its coordinate map is a surjection factoring through a quotient. This is a short argument from the quoted results and definitions, not an uncited theorem about scheme-theoretic images.

For step 3 the finite-dimension node assumes only finite type over a field, which holds for both G and its closed affine subgroup M by step 1. I used no hypothesis of reductivity. Applying the identity-component statement to a connected torus gives irreducibility; smooth-geometrically-reduced makes it reduced, so its affine coordinate algebra is a domain. The chain definition supplies the bound for every closed torus and nonnegative integer dimensions because the identity makes each nonempty. These statements were all opened and recorded verbatim in citations.json.

Step 4 is the elementary finite-integer maximum argument, using the already proved facts of step 3. A strict closed inclusion into an integral affine torus has proper support: an ideal with full support lies in every prime, including the zero prime, so is zero. Appending the irreducible ambient torus to a longest chain strictly increases dimension. This establishes maximality rather than assuming that a maximal-dimension torus is maximal. No separate maximal-containing-torus node was created for this few-line consequence of the cited statements.

Step 5 applies that argument twice, starting with the identity torus in M, then with its chosen maximal torus S in G. The identity is closed because the counit is a surjective F-algebra homomorphism. The composite inclusions remain closed by the coordinate-surjection definition. The first maximum ranges over all torus subgroups of M, so S is maximal in M; the second ranges over those containing S, and step 4 makes R maximal in G.

For step 6 I checked the actual statement of the Borel/maximal-torus conjugacy node: its conjugacy clause needs smooth connected affine finite-type G over an algebraically closed field and explicitly imposes no reductivity. It states equality as subgroup schemes on every commutative algebra. Normality in the cited subgroup definition therefore gives c_g(M)=M on all algebras, not just F-points. The group definition supplies the inverse conjugation. An overtorus of gSg^{-1} pulls back to an overtorus of S inside M, proving maximality under the automorphism without a separate transport lemma.

Step 7 factors this conjugate torus through H by step 2, then uses maximality in M to identify it with H. It uses only preceding steps. Thus it proves the conclusion by an argument, rather than relabelling a new node that states the conclusion. All steps have fewer than 2000 characters.
