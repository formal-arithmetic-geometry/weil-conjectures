The hypotheses below are the hypotheses in those statements, rather than conclusions taken from their proofs.

Step 1 uses smooth-unipotent-solvable to exclude a smooth connected normal unipotent subgroup under the given semisimplicity hypothesis. This supplies reductivity for reductive-torus-self-centralizer, whose statement really gives scheme-theoretic centralizer equality. Torus-centralizer-smooth-connected supplies smoothness of that centralizer, and hence of S. Both results allow the algebraically closed base and the given maximal split torus. H cannot be solvable, since H itself would violate semisimplicity and S has dimension one.

Step 2 is the elementary dual-number calculation, with the G_m and tangent-space definition nodes explicitly cited. Its units based at 1 are exactly 1+a epsilon. The coordinate basis gives dimension r; the tangent-dimension-smoothness statement explicitly gives equality for the already established smooth group S, so r=1. This proves the actual isomorphism of group schemes, not just equality of dimensions. Rank zero gives a zero-dimensional tangent space and is excluded by the dimension-one hypothesis.

Step 3 uses general-borel-flag-variety for existence, smoothness, projectivity, transitivity on rational points and the full stabilizer B. Smooth-group-orbit-geometry gives a locally closed smooth orbit. Closed-point-residue-field-base and lft-over-field-jacobson-space apply because X is finite type over algebraically closed F; they show that this orbit contains every closed point and equals X. I wrote the dense locally closed/open/complement argument rather than equating schemes from rational points alone. Smooth-geometrically-reduced supplies reducedness. Homogeneous-orbit-quotient-geometry explicitly identifies the orbit with H/B, without requiring B normal.

Step 4 uses torus-normalizer-borel-transitivity only for conjugacy of Borels containing S. Split-torus-hom-matrices supplies all group-scheme endomorphisms of G_m with their integral exponents. An inverse exponent forces m=+1 or -1. The self-centralizer node then shows that equal induced automorphisms produce equal conjugate Borels, since their quotient lies in S contained in B. These statements bound the entire fixed-point set, and do not assume an identification from the proof of another node.

Step 5 uses the strict-dimension clause of solvable-normal-subgroup-products, which explicitly concerns any strict inclusion of smooth connected groups (the clause does not require B normal). Homogeneous-quotient-krull-dimension says exactly dim H=dim B+dim(H/B), so the Borel quotient has positive dimension. General-borel-flag-variety identifies its fixed rational points with Borels containing S. Proper-homogeneous-torus-fixed-bound gives at least d+1 fixed points for this proper H/B, allowing an arbitrary closed stabilizer and no finite-fixed-locus hypothesis. Their number is at most two by step 4, so d=1. Borel-quotient-proper-geometry explicitly makes this smooth homogeneous quotient integral; B is smooth connected, as required.

Step 6 applies complete-curve-connected-linear-action only after establishing that X is smooth proper integral, finite type, dimension one, and that its H-action is nontrivial. Projective-frames-pgl represents the automorphism sheaf, not only the rational-point automorphism group; thus the action gives a group-scheme map to PGL_2. Algebraic-group-homomorphism-factorization explicitly supplies the closed normal finite-type kernel. General-borel-flag-variety gives the stabilizer equality after every commutative algebra extension, so K lies in B scheme-theoretically.

Step 7 uses locally-algebraic-group-identity-component for the smooth connected reduced identity subgroup of K. Since rational conjugations preserve both the identity component and reduction, smooth-ambient-normality-geometric gives its normality in smooth H over algebraically closed F; this avoids the false unrestricted assertion that reduction of a normal group is always normal. The derived-subgroup node gives existence and smooth connectedness of all successive derived subgroups. The written restriction-of-commutator induction shows solvability inside B. Semisimplicity therefore makes J trivial. Neutral-component-translates identifies every component of K with K^0 and supplies finiteness of components, proving dimension zero without assuming the kernel smooth.

Step 8 uses group-hom-dimension-kernel-image in its full nonseparable form, with arbitrary nonreduced kernel. Projective-matrix-central-cover explicitly states dimension r^2-1 for PGL_r in every characteristic. The dimension of its closed image is bounded above by chains of irreducible closed subsets, directly from the cited definition of Krull dimension. Connected-group-dimension-two-solvable applies to the original smooth connected affine H and gives the lower bound three. These inequalities prove dim H=3 without needing to identify the kernel with the centre or to assert separability of the map.

Step 9 uses precisely the equality-if-smooth clause of group-tangent-dimension-smoothness for H, and combines it with the explicitly constructed lambda. This proves every clause of the unchanged statement.
