### Old step 1 -> lem-a1-qc-open-intersection-spectral-presentation

Fix an index $i_0$. Each open subscheme $U_i$ is quasi-separated: its affine open cover is also a family of affine opens of $X$, whose pairwise intersections are quasi-compact by clause (1) of [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-separated-affine-cover-criterion]; clause (2) applied to this cover gives the claim. Since $U_i$ is quasi-compact by hypothesis, [lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-scheme-spectral] makes every $U_i$ spectral. In particular $Y=U_{i_0}$ is spectral. Let $J$ be the set of distinct opens among the $U_i$ contained in $Y$, ordered by reverse inclusion, and put $V_j=j$ for $j\in J$. It is a set, is nonempty (it contains $Y$), and is directed by the given refinement condition. Every original $U_i$ contains a member of $J$, by applying that condition to $i,i_0$. Consequently $E=\bigcap_{j\in J}V_j$. For the stipulated finite-intersection refinement, use nonempty finite intersections. Two quasi-compact opens have finite affine covers, so their intersection is covered by finitely many pairwise affine-open intersections, each quasi-compact by clause (1) above. Induction therefore makes every nonempty finite intersection quasi-compact. Their total intersection is unchanged and their family is directed by reverse inclusion.

### Old step 2 -> lem-a1-qc-open-intersection-spectral-presentation

For $j\le k$ let $f_{jk}:V_k\hookrightarrow V_j$ be inclusion. These maps are spectral: a quasi-compact open $W$ of $V_j$ is a quasi-compact open of $Y$ (inclusion preserves its topology and open covers), and $f_{jk}^{-1}(W)=W\cap V_k$ is quasi-compact by [def-weil-spectral-space], since $W,V_k$ are quasi-compact opens of $Y$. Identities and composition are those of inclusions. The subspace $E$ is the topological limit of this diagram, with projections $p_j:E\hookrightarrow V_j$. Indeed, the coordinates of a compatible tuple all coincide in $Y$, since any two indices have a common upper bound. That common point lies in every $V_j$, hence in $E$; conversely any point of $E$ gives such a tuple. The initial topology of the $p_j$ is the subspace topology: all inclusions are continuous for that topology, and the projection $p_Y:E\hookrightarrow Y$ alone induces the subspace topology. This also proves the limit assertion when $E$ is empty.

### Old step 3 -> lem-a1-ring-inverse-image-underlying-sets

We may compute the underlying sets of the ring-valued inverse images by the set-valued inverse-image construction, with the same units. To check this, let $h:T\to Z$ be any of the continuous maps used here and let $G$ be a sheaf of commutative rings on $Z$. For an open $W\subseteq T$, the opens $B\supseteq h(W)$ form a small nonempty directed poset under reverse inclusion. By [def-weil-pbc-topological-inverse-image], the presheaf inverse image is $h_pG(W)=\varinjlim_B G(B)$ in rings. By [lem-pc-filtered-colimit-commrings-elementwise] its elements are represented by sections on such $B$, and two representatives agree precisely after restriction to a smaller such open. By [lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-sets-elementwise] these are exactly the representatives and equality relation for the set colimit. Thus the canonical map from the set colimit to the underlying ring colimit is a bijection, commuting with restriction and with the coprojections. Sheafification commutes with underlying sets, including its unit, by [lem-weil-pbc-ring-sheafification-space-underlying-abelian]. Applying this to $h_pG$ gives the claimed identification of $h^{-1}G$ and its unit on underlying sets.

### Old step 7 -> lem-a1-directed-ring-cocone-recognition

Zero, negatives and the unit are checked at one stage. Each $\rho_i$ preserves these operations because it is a ring inverse-image unit. Uniqueness of $q$ follows from (a). Hence this same section ring, or its underlying abelian group, is the specified colimit, with the specified canonical isomorphism.
