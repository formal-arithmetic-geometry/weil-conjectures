I read Tag 02WU in `spaces.tex`, from the full hypotheses through the final test-morphism argument, and compared the node statement with it. The definition of equivalence relation and its restriction in `groupoids.tex`, Tag 02V8, supplies step 1: on T-points this is the inverse image of an equivalence relation along g. This is an elementary argument using the universal property in `def-pc-fibre-product`; there is no hidden etaleness requirement. I also read the actual statements of each node cited below, not merely their titles or proof lists.

For step 2 I read Tag 045Z, including its proof, and Tag 045Y immediately preceding it. `lem-weil-pbc-site-sheafification-sections-local` states local representatives for sections of the associated sheaf, including the specified universe convention. It is applied to the presheaf of orbit sets, then representatives of those sets are chosen. `lem-trf-etale-relation-kernel-pair` explicitly assumes a monomorphism whose test-point image is an equivalence relation, with no etale hypothesis, and explicitly identifies h_R with h_U ×_F h_U. This gives the r used here, after local lifting. The same argument gives equality in F' by the restricted relation, so the use of injectivity later is justified before any representing open is built. I avoided the sibling restriction-quotient node, whose current statement requires etaleness and would not cover our flat-lfp case (C4).

For step 3 I checked both projection directions in the fibre product: E → R is the base change of g, and p then uses t. The flat base-change node states exactly this preservation. Clause (3) of the broad qcqs permanence node includes local finite presentation along arbitrary base changes, with no global qcqs hypothesis on that clause. The flat composition and local finite presentation composition nodes give exactly the two composite properties. The flat-lfp-open node includes openness and all base changes. Factoring through W preserves flatness because stalk maps do not change, and preserves local finite presentation by taking the same affine presentations over smaller basic target opens. Thus this factorization is an elementary restriction argument, not an assumed theorem about arbitrary locally closed images. I read the relevant source statements in `morphisms.tex`: Tags 01U9, 01TS, 01TR, and 01UA, and the composition-of-flat-morphisms lemma.

For step 4, the surjective-base-change node says precisely that pulling back a surjection remains surjective. The flat base-change and broad permanence nodes give the remaining two conditions in the explicit definition of an fppf covering. They are applied to E → W, not E → U: only the former is surjective. The section over this cover is the projection to U' and its image in F agrees via the actual R-point supplied by E. Step 2 makes the two local F' sections agree on the overlap. The sheaf condition glues them, exactly as in the displayed cover diagram of Tag 02WU.

For step 5 I checked the reverse direction on an arbitrary Z → V, not merely on points. The local-sections node again supplies b_k, and the kernel-pair node gives an actual R-map r_k with the two required composites. These maps define Z_k → E by the defining fibre-product equation. Only after this scheme map exists do I pass to its image in W. Joint surjectivity of the covering gives the image containment for Z itself. Factoring through an open subscheme is justified by restricting both parts of a scheme morphism; no analogous unjustified factorization through a closed or locally closed subscheme is used. The uniqueness in step 2 shows the two directions are inverses and natural. This is the substantive local representing-property argument of the item, and has not been moved into a new node restating the conclusion (D1/D2).

For step 6 I verified that local lifting applies to a completely arbitrary section a ∈ F(T). The two opens on every overlap represent the same pullback subfunctor by steps 4–5. As embeddings into the overlap they have equal images, so their open subscheme structures agree; this is the elementary uniqueness of the restricted structure sheaf on an open. No quasi-separatedness or affineness of overlaps is assumed.

For step 7 I read Tag 022C (`topologies-lemma-zariski-etale-smooth-syntomic-fppf-fpqc`) in full. The four cited statements give exactly flatness, the definition of the desired fpqc condition, openness of each fppf map, and quasi-compactness of an affine scheme. Affine opens covering each inverse image give an open cover of each target affine, and its finite subcover is precisely the fpqc condition. This is the short elementary deduction from those statements, written out rather than made into another node. It verifies the fpqc hypothesis before the descent node is applied, rather than silently identifying fppf and fpqc coverings.

For step 8 I read Tag 03N0 (`descent-lemma-open-fpqc-covering`) and the descent-of-morphisms statement Tag 023Q. `lem-weil-pbc-open-subscheme-fpqc-descent` gives the unique descended open with exactly the equal-overlap hypotheses of step 6. `lem-weil-pbc-fpqc-representable-descent` states existence and uniqueness of a morphism into an arbitrary target scheme from compatible local morphisms, so together with step 7 it verifies that h_(W_T) is an fppf sheaf. The last step checks the resulting isomorphism on every Z → T using the pulled-back cover and unique gluing of F' sections; it is not an unsupported appeal to descent of general schemes. The final map is the open embedding in `def-weil-open-immersion`, giving both representability and the asserted type of immersion.

I tested the construction on g with empty source (E and every representing open are empty), on g the identity (the reflexive relation makes the image W all of U), and on g surjective (E → U is surjective by base change and t, so every representing open is all the test scheme). For the equality relation and g: A¹ → Spec k the restricted relation is the full fibre relation, so the quotient map is still an isomorphism; the proof never treats g itself as injective or an open immersion. Nonreduced test schemes are retained in the r_k and E argument. The argument also handles empty covers over the empty scheme by the sheaf condition. There are no new statements with untested small cases.
