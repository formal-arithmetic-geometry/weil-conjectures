## Problems and corrections

The imported statement is retained. The original integral cover need not have finite fibres: only its finite approximating covers do. Thus choosing an affine neighbourhood of its whole orbit without using the quasi-affine hypothesis would be wrong. The argument instead spreads the given quasi-affine chart, obtains whole-fibre containment at a finite stage by constructible containment, and takes its closed saturation complement. There is no assumption that the finite cover Q/S is flat.

The finite-stage sheaf F_Q is not initially a scheme. I corrected an early use of the scheme orbit-saturation lemma in step 3: the step now proves invariance directly from identity, inverse, cocycle and common residue-field extensions on scheme tests. The scheme orbit lemma is used only in step 7, after F_0,Q is represented by Z. I also made the finite-presentation hypothesis on the fpqc scheme form explicit in representation spreading, and made Z Noetherian in the semilocal prerequisite to justify quasi-compactness of the localization morphism. These are hypotheses of new supporting statements, verified in the item, not new hypotheses on the item.

I retained that ancestry, filled the actual place and searched the current latest blueprint, including its whole imported DAG, for equivalent statements. ## Review by Q66

I read Raynaud Theorem 4.1's reductions 1–6 and Lemma 4.3 in the primary scan, the latest blueprint's ctf direct-image argument and the parent split. For chart spreading I checked reduction 3 against Stacks 01ZC (limit preservation), 01ZM (spreading finite-presentation schemes), 0EUU (eventual open immersion) and the distinction between a scheme and a scheme-valued chart into a sheaf. These scheme results do not themselves state the new sheaf chart assertion. Its subsequent proof will descend a finite affine-chart presentation and its map after the fpqc cover, then spread the open-immersion condition and equality of descent maps. For representation spreading, reduction 4 uses the same limit-preservation argument for the whole representation; separatedness and finite presentation must hold on the cover, and the compatibility with the specified limit representation is part of the new statement. Stacks 01ZM and 01ZQ supply the scheme approximation and separatedness ingredients, but not the sheaf representability assertion. For the semilocal statement I read the start of reduction 6 and Lemma 4.3, and Stacks 00E3 (localization spectrum) and 00DS (prime avoidance). One first puts the finite subset in an affine open of its quasi-affine neighbourhood, localizes away from the union of its primes, and uses prime avoidance to identify the resulting spectrum with all their generalizations. Under either finite relation map, a generalization lifts to a generalization of a point above E by going up. Invariance of E therefore gives invariance of this subscheme; localization local rings identify its two pullbacks. This substantive construction and compatibility is the new prerequisite, not merely the localization-flatness fact already in the DAG.

Step 1 uses the actual surjective-base-change, fpqc-covering and fpqc-descent-of-local-finite-presentation statements to obtain Y/T locally of finite presentation. The qcqs permanence and affine-qcqs statements make the quasi-compact separated V finitely presented over affine T. The integral-limit node explicitly gives finite finitely presented stages and finite transition maps over a qcqs base. The new chart node accepts exactly this affine inverse system and gives an open subfunctor, without requiring scheme representability at a stage. Surjectivity of each stage follows from the factorization of the original surjection.

Step 2 uses the fibre-product-points and surjective-base-change statements to compare residue-field representatives over x. The descent-datum statement gives the actual transport of the same F-section. The finite permanence and finite-fibre statements justify finite field fibres, and the locally Noetherian finite-type statement justifies their Noetherian property. I read the constructible-set definition and the limit-contained-in-constructible statement: the former makes an open in this Noetherian space constructible, and the latter applies to the affine inverse system C_j with that constructible open. This gives whole-fibre containment, not merely membership of one chosen lift.

Step 3 uses finiteness and closedness of the two base-changed projections on scheme tests. Its displayed closed image is taken inside the actual scheme V_i, not inside an unrepresented sheaf. The datum's cocycle and inverse show that the set of further transports is unchanged. The field-point comparison and surjective-base-change statements verify that the orbit of z is exhausted by the fibre used in step 2. Finite Q over Noetherian S and finite presentation of V_i give Noetherian V_i; the cited Noetherian-open and qcqs statements make its open Z quasi-compact and finitely presented. No new quasi-affineness theorem is hidden here: composing the two open inclusions embeds Z as a quasi-compact open in the same affine scheme.

Step 4 uses the finite-closed-map statement to take the complement of the image of the closed complement, and the scheme-test transport argument gives whole-fibre invariance. The base-change and fibre-product-points statements justify the residue-field comparisons, hence the equality of the pulled-back opens. I checked the open-fpqc-descent statement: it really descends compatible open subschemes, rather than just defining descent data. Its hypotheses are supplied by the fpqc cover obtained from A/S. The equalizer definition of the original sheaf constructs the subfunctor F_0 with the stated representations.

Step 5 uses the separated-cancellation and affine-separated statements to pass from absolute separatedness of quasi-affine Z to Z/Q and Z/S. Base change makes Z_A/A separated. The universally-closed-surjection theorem explicitly descends relative separatedness to P_0/A; a definition of separatedness would not suffice here. Finiteness gives a closed surjection. Qcqs permanence gives quasi-compact Z_A; a cover of its continuous image P_0 has a finite subcover by pulling it back. The definition of open immersion and separated-implies-quasi-separated then give finite presentation of P_0/A from local finite presentation, quasi-compactness and quasi-separatedness.

Step 6 applies the representation-spreading statement only after establishing all its cover hypotheses in step 5. The inverse system has a small directed indexing set of finite polynomial presentations; generators and relations give the asserted limit directly. The scheme at the limit is P_0 over A, not the whole possibly non-quasi-compact P. The fibre-product and descent-datum definitions show on every test scheme that both comparison and datum preserve the same F_0-section. Finite permanence, integrality and base-change surjectivity then give exactly the claimed finite integral surjection g. There is no appeal to a mere definition of descent for an unproved existence theorem.

Step 7 uses finite fibres to make the orbit finite and uses the orbit-saturation statement only for the scheme Z with its now-established datum. Z is Noetherian and quasi-affine, so all hypotheses of the new semilocal lemma hold. That lemma states equality of the pullbacks as subfunctors, as required, rather than just equality of point sets. Finally the base-change image statement gives the incidence after passing from z in j(L) to the corresponding point of Z_B. Every cited node was opened and its statement read; citations.json contains a verbatim portion for every use. No proof step is longer than 2000 characters.

I tested the assertions on the empty base (the item's pointwise claim is vacuous), identity covers, constant inverse systems and empty E (Spec of the zero ring). If E contains comparable points its generalization set is controlled by the maximal members, so the semilocal construction does not assert a distinct maximal ideal for every listed point. A finite cover such as Spec(k) to Spec(k[epsilon]/epsilon^2) is surjective but nonflat; none of the finite-cover steps uses fpqc descent along that cover. The localization map is allowed to fail finite presentation, so no false open-immersion assertion is inferred from its flat monomorphism.
