### Old Step 1

Write $R_W=\widehat{\mathcal O}_{E,W}$ and $B_{n,W}=(A_{E,n})_W$. Write $f_n$ for $f$ acting with the finite coefficient sheaves $B_{n,Y},B_{n,X}$. Put $U=Lf^*K(d)[2d]$ and $U_n=Lf_n^*K_n(d)[2d]$. Integral pullback and completed tensor preserve bounded constructibility and have the stated canonical reductions by [lem-pur-integral-operations-preserve-constructible]. By [prop-weil-twist-preserves] the integral twist is a locally free rank-one invertible $R_X$-module; hence locally it is the coefficient sheaf and tensoring by it preserves the same cohomological and Tor intervals. The integral twist's reductions are the finite twists by [def-coefficient-twists-detailed]; for its positive power $d$, trivializing the rank-one sheaf reduces both this assertion and the tensor comparison to $R_X\otimes_{R_X}B_{n,X}=B_{n,X}$. If $[a,b]$ is the common Tor interval of the $K_n$ from [def-weil-finite-tor-amplitude], [lem-sf-pullback-tor-amplitude] gives that interval for every $Lf_n^*K_n$. Tensoring by the locally free twist leaves it unchanged, and shifting by $[2d]$ changes it to $[a-2d,b-2d]$, since $\mathcal H^i(C[2d])=\mathcal H^{i+2d}(C)$. Finite pullback is the exact module inverse image in [def-pur-integral-derived-operations], so it preserves the input cohomological window. The rank-one twist also preserves it; the shift changes its endpoints by minus $2d$. Thus $U\in D^b_{ctf}(X,\mathcal O_E)$ and its canonical reduction is $U_n$ at every level. This proves the finiteness actually needed; it does not infer common integral local models from boundedness alone.
