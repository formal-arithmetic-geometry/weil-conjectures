## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: a nonzero lisse sheaf on an arbitrary positive-dimensional scheme can be simple. Over an algebraically closed field k let X be the disjoint union of A¹_k and Spec k. Let Lambda=F₂ and let L be zero on A¹_k and constant F₂ on the isolated point. The two components are open, so L is lisse, is nonzero, and is simple: its sole nonzero stalk is the simple module F₂ at an algebraically closed point. It is already a length-one extension, while dim X=1. Thus even in the finite-type case connectedness is needed for the claimed final consequence.

Finite type (or an appropriate Jacobson replacement) is also needed if the final sentence was meant to apply to every connected Noetherian scheme. Take X=Spec k[[t]] with k algebraically closed and Lambda=F₂. The generic point is open and the closed point is closed. The constant sheaf F₂ has the open-closed filtration with factors j_!F₂ and i_*F₂; both factors are simple, since the corresponding one-dimensional representations have no proper nonzero submodule. X is connected, Noetherian, and one-dimensional. Thus the imported Noetherian hypothesis alone does not repair the sentence. The corrected final assertion explicitly assumes connectedness AND finite type over a field, matching the preceding Jacobson argument and the Lean candidate's hypotheses. No target declaration is edited.

Simplicity of M means simplicity in finite continuous Galois representations on Lambda-modules. It cannot mean simplicity of the underlying Lambda-module. For a finite field K and Lambda=F₂, take M=F₄ as a two-dimensional F₂-space and let the absolute Galois group act through its quotient C₃ by multiplication by a generator of F₄^×. This has no invariant line (the generator has irreducible polynomial T²+T+1) and is a simple representation, but F₂² is not a simple F₂-module. The corrected statement explicitly gives the stable-submodule condition, preserves arbitrary finite rings, specifies all functors, and defines finite extension by a finite filtration.
