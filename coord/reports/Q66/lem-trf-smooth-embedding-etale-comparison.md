### Original step 1

Work on affine neighbourhoods of the given point, and represent the identified scheme germs by the same $k$-scheme germ $X$. An affine finite-type $k$-algebra is generated by finitely many elements, so its presentation as a quotient of $k[T_1,\ldots,T_a]$ gives a closed embedding into $\mathbf A^a_k$, which is smooth. Now start with any smooth ambient embedding, write $X=\operatorname{Spec}(B/I)\subset M=\operatorname{Spec}B$ near $x$, and put $L=\kappa(x)$. The quotient differential sequence and right exactness of tensor give
$$ (I/I^2)\otimes_{B/I}L\longrightarrow\Omega_{B/k}\otimes_B L\longrightarrow\Omega_{(B/I)/k}\otimes_{B/I}L\longrightarrow0.$$
The first map sends $f$ to $df$. Smooth differentials are locally finite free; put $n=\dim_L(\Omega_{B/k}\otimes_B L)$. Choose $f_1,\ldots,f_r\in I$ whose differentials form a basis of the kernel of the displayed surjection. Coefficients in $L$ lift to the local ring at $x$, so representatives can be taken regular after shrinking. This uses no smoothness or reducedness of $X$.

### Original step 6

Henselian form. First, henselizations exist. Let $R$ be a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ and let $(R^h,\mathfrak mR^h)$ be its pair henselization: a henselian pair with $R/\mathfrak m\xrightarrow{\sim}R^h/\mathfrak mR^h$. If a maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$ of $R^h$ did not contain $\mathfrak mR^h$, the finite algebra $R^h/\mathfrak n$ would reduce to the zero ring modulo $\mathfrak m$, and both idempotents $0\neq1$ would lift its unique idempotent. So $R^h$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak mR^h$, hence a henselian local ring, and $R\to R^h$ is local. For a local map $g:R\to T$ into a henselian local ring, $(T,\mathfrak m_T)$ is a henselian pair containing $g(\mathfrak m)$. The pair universal property gives a unique map $R^h\to T$ under $R$. It is local, since it sends $\mathfrak mR^h$ into $\mathfrak m_T$. Thus $R^h$ is a henselization of $R$. On an affine chart let $A\to C$ represent $p_i$ and let $\mathfrak p,\mathfrak q$ be the marked primes. The residue fields are identified. By the pointed henselian lifting theorem, the map $A\to A_{\mathfrak p}^h$ extends uniquely to a pointed map $C\to A_{\mathfrak p}^h$; it localizes to $C_{\mathfrak q}$ and extends by the henselization universal property to $C_{\mathfrak q}^h\to A_{\mathfrak p}^h$. This inverts the natural map $A_{\mathfrak p}^h\to C_{\mathfrak q}^h$: one composite is the identity by the universal property for $A_{\mathfrak p}^h$; the other agrees with the identity on $C$ by uniqueness of the pointed etale lift, then on $C_{\mathfrak q}^h$ by its universal property.
