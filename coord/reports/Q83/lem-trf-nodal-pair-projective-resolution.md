Properness and the equality of reduced inverse-image boundaries finish the argument. The unused stronger hypotheses of connected fibres and geometrically integral smooth generic fibre are retained in the item.

## Review by Q83

I checked B–E with the node files and the following source text open. For the item I read Exposé X Lemma 2.2 and its proof, pp. 149–150. For the definition I read VI 1.1–1.4, pp. 69–70: the log structures are on the etale site, fine saturated means finitely generated/integral/saturated charts, and the strict-local quotient dimension formula is the stated criterion. I corrected the chart pushout notation to use the inverse image of the units, rather than treating an abstract monoid as a subset of the local ring. All auxiliary notions in this definition are explicitly explained or are existing definition dependencies; it does not introduce log smoothness as an unexplained prerequisite.

For the chart lemma I read VI 1.4–1.5(i) and Stacks 0BIA (`etale.tex`, `lemma-strict-normal-crossings`, including its proof). The smooth-intersection convention implies the parameter convention, and the local equations of the distinct components give characteristic monoid N^r. This lemma includes the nontrivial divisorial-chart identification rather than pretending it follows from the name “strict normal crossings”. For nodal log regularity I read the whole statement and proof of VI Proposition 1.9, pp. 70–71, and Stacks 0CBY (`curves.tex`, `lemma-etale-local-structure-nodal-family`). The latter is the available non-branch-labelled etale local model uv=h. The proposition assumes a proper flat nodal curve, smooth away from the boundary, and an etale divisor in the smooth locus; these are exactly the hypotheses of the new statement. The additional finite-presentation and Noetherian restrictions do not change the application. I kept the exact connected one-dimensional wording of the assigned statement in the auxiliary theorem: its nodal fibres are curves of pure dimension one because a zero-dimensional smooth component would be an isolated open-and-closed point (contradicting connectedness), whereas both branches at an ordinary double point are one-dimensional.

For regular modification I read VIII 3.4.6(i), 3.4.8–3.4.9 and 4.1.1, and VI 1.7. Saturated log blowups give normalized blowups, the total space becomes regular, and the reduced boundary is only normal crossings. There is no characteristic-zero assumption in VIII 3.4.9; the characteristic-zero independence theorem later in 3.4.15 is not being used. For strictification I read VIII 4.1.2–4.1.8, including the drop in depth of the strict transform when the deepest stratum is blown up, and the accumulated exceptional divisor's strict normal crossings property. I also read Remark 4.2.2(i), Stacks 0B8X (`varieties.tex`, `lemma-dense-smooth-open-variety-over-perfect-field`), and the statements of the two nodes sharing that tag. The smoothness conclusion is valid because F is algebraically closed, hence perfect, and both the regular ambient scheme and its regular boundary intersections are of finite type. No separatedness of the structure morphism to F is silently assumed. Each new theorem has a complete self-contained statement, no proof or proof dependencies, and status unsplit.

Step 1 uses the full statement of `lem-kun-projective-implies-proper`, then the finite-type clause of `def-weil-proper-morphism`; `lem-sfb-finite-type-composition-base-change` explicitly supplies composition; `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian` supplies both Noetherian conclusions. I opened these four statements: none requires a separated base, and the projectivity convention is the item's original closed-immersion convention. Spec F is Noetherian directly since a field has only two ideals. This step is marked non-elementary because it invokes those theorems. The two off-unit unsplit generic tools are declared in `split_report.tool_uses` with their reasons; the other generic tools are Mathlib leaves.

Step 2 takes the chart lemma's explicit log regularity conclusion, with smoothness, finite type and the codimension-r intersection hypothesis checked against the item's exact wording. Step 3 takes the nodal theorem's log regularity and nowhere-density conclusions. Properness and Noetherianity were obtained in step 1; D is etale without needing a choice of its sections, and finite etaleness certainly implies the required etaleness. I do not claim that etale branch splitting is a global splitting. Step 4 uses precisely regular modification, not strictification, and checks integrality, the same F, finite type and log regularity before applying it. Step 5 uses precisely the regular ambient / ordinary normal crossings hypotheses supplied by step 4; it returns smoothness and strict normal crossings as explicitly stated in the new node.

Step 6 takes proper composition from `lem-weil-pbc-proper-composition`, whose statement I opened. Birationality is checked on the particular dense open X minus B, not inferred merely from dominance. That open remains nonempty upstairs, since both maps are isomorphisms there, and any nonempty open of an integral scheme is dense. I opened `def-weil-pbc-scheme-reduction`: it states explicitly that reduction is the identity on the underlying set. Thus the boundary equality uses only composition of inverse images and uniqueness of a reduced closed subscheme with a prescribed support; it makes no claim that reduction commutes with arbitrary base change. The complete cited statements are copied verbatim into `citations.json`, one entry for each use in each step. No step exceeds 2000 characters; the longest is 705 characters. None hides an additional comparison lemma.

Small cases checked: empty T and D give trivial log structures and the smooth case; dimension-zero Y permits no vertical nodes since f must then be smooth; uv=t already has regular total space, but its two branches are retained in the boundary; uv=t^n (n>1) requires the genuine log-regular desingularization; uv=t1*t2 checks the crossing-degeneration case without assuming a single base parameter. A geometrically irreducible nodal special component can have self-intersections, so regularity alone does not finish the item; this is why Step 5 is separate. A non-etale or colliding marked divisor would fail the nodal theorem's hypotheses and is never admitted here. The regular-to-smooth conversion is restricted to the algebraically closed base; it is not claimed over an imperfect field. The closed and generic fibre assumptions of the original item remain intact.

No statement dispute was found. There was no returned delivery to repair. The first dry run flagged the phrase “the item’s” in a proof step as an external-setting reference; I replaced it by “the given hypotheses”, without changing the argument. I corrected the potential normal-versus-strict gap by making two different theorem nodes and retaining an explicit strictification step; I also avoided claiming projective-space projectivity for the output.
