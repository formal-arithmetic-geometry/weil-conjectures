For the next worker on the product lemma, here is the argument, kept out of its unsplit statement. On affine opens U = Spec R and V = Spec Q, evaluation on A intersect U and B intersect V separates R and Q: a function vanishing there has closed zero locus containing a dense subset, so lies in every prime; it is zero because the ring is reduced. Given z in R tensor_k Q, write z = sum_i r_i tensor q_i with the q_i linearly independent over k. If all paired evaluations of z vanish, first fix a in A intersect U. Separation on Q implies sum_i r_i(a) q_i = 0. Independence gives r_i(a) = 0 for each i. Separation on R then gives r_i = 0. Thus the evaluations on pairs separate R tensor Q. Nilpotents map to zero in k under every evaluation, hence are zero. Further, if a basic open D(z) contains no paired point then z has all evaluations zero and is zero, so D(z) is empty. The paired points are therefore dense. The affine products U times_k V cover the scheme product, proving both conclusions. This argument does not tensor an infinite product of fields or confuse scheme products with topological products. Milne 1.10 and 1.14 give precisely the evaluation method; A.43 confirms the reducedness conclusion by the geometric-reducedness route.
