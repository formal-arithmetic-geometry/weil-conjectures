## Argument preservation and tasks for subsequent workers

The incidence node has its own hypotheses (integral base and flat map; no finite type or purity assumption) and conclusion (specialization plus component incidence; no dimension conclusion). Its proof task is exactly the following retained argument from old steps 1--2:

Old step 1: Fix $s\in S$ and an irreducible component $C$ of $X_s$. By sobriety choose its generic point $x$, regarded also as a point of $X$ using the fibre homeomorphism. Choose affine neighbourhoods $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $s$ and $U=\operatorname{Spec}R\subset f^{-1}(V)$ of $x$. The affine flatness criterion makes $A\to R$ flat. Since $S$ is integral, $A$ is a domain and $\eta\in V$ corresponds to $(0)$. If $\mathfrak q$ represents $x$ and $\mathfrak p$ represents $s$, going down applied to $(0)\subseteq\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak q$ gives $\mathfrak q_0\subseteq\mathfrak q$ contracting to $(0)$. Thus there is a point $y\in X_\eta$ specializing to $x$ in $X$.

Old step 2: Choose an irreducible component $B$ of $X_\eta$ containing $y$, using the component-cover theorem, and let $z$ be its generic point by sobriety. In the fibre $z$ specializes to $y$; its continuous inclusion into $X$ shows that $z$ specializes to $x$ in $X$. Put $Z=\overline{\{z\}}$ in $X$ and $Z_s=Z\cap f^{-1}(s)$. This is closed in $X_s$ and contains $x$, hence contains $C=\overline{\{x\}}$ in $X_s$. Any irreducible subset of $Z_s$ properly containing $C$ would also be an irreducible subset of $X_s$ properly containing $C$. Maximality of $C$ therefore makes $C$ an irreducible component of $Z_s$.
