## Source error

The last paragraph of 09EY says that, when no finite obstruction exists, u is a pth power in the completion of B_1 and then in B_1, although no B_1 is defined in this branch. Interpreting this as B is false: in B=k[[t]] with perfect characteristic-p k, u=1+t has all uniformizer digits pth powers, but u is not a pth power in B (its t coefficient is nonzero). Its root is 1+s in B[s], s^p=t. The new digit lemma states the precise true conclusion in the complete root ring S; the item's steps 2 and 3 show why this still gives exactly C=B[t^(1/p)]. This repairs the proof, not the item statement. The finite-obstruction computation in the printed proof is omitted; step 5 supplies it with error exponent (n-m-1)p after division. For n=m+2 this is positive, including m=0. No STATEMENT-DISPUTED change to the item is needed.

The proof plan behind the new digit statement is successive extraction: put u_0=u; if the residue of u_i is a pth power, choose b_i lifting a root and set u_(i+1)=(u_i-b_i^p)/t. If extraction stops it gives the displayed finite expansion. Otherwise the sums w_N=sum_(i<N)b_i s^i form a compatible Cauchy family in S, and w_N^p=u-t^N u_N. Completeness and separatedness give w^p=u. This argument belongs here for the next worker; the new node itself contains only its self-contained statement.

For the approximation node the source constructs a root of Y^p-t^(n+p-1)Y-t. Its derivative is the nonzero scalar -t^(n+p-1), and the polynomial is Eisenstein. The equation and t associated to s^p give the claimed approximation to order np. This is a genuinely smaller construction, with no B or C or Epp alternative. For the nonpower-residue node, the mod-t polynomial T^p-bar(a) is irreducible, the monic root algebra has residue field k[bar(z)] and maximal ideal generated by t, hence is a DVR and its own normalization. This applies in mixed characteristic too; no perfection assumption is inserted.
