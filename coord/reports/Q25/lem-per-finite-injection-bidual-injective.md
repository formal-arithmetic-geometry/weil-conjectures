I read the entire proof of Stacks Tag 0EB8 in more-algebra.tex, especially the claim that j is injective. The source first passes restriction of duals to the fraction field, observes surjectivity there by splitting the vector-space injection, and concludes that the cokernel is torsion and its R-dual zero. My five steps follow this argument, with the last implication written directly on an arbitrary bidual kernel element. I also read Algebra Tag 0583 (algebra-lemma-hom-from-finitely-presented), Tag 00CS (algebra-proposition-localization-exact), and Tag 00HT(1) (algebra-lemma-flat-localization). The five cited node JSON statements were opened and read, not inferred from their titles. All are already Mathlib leaves, so no off-unit non-leaf tool declaration is needed.

Step 1 uses lem-weil-pbc-localization-module-exact exactly for preservation of the exact pair 0 → U → V. Its statement also defines fractions, their equality relation and the induced localized map. Thus it supplies both injectivity of f_K and the specific fraction model used later. lem-weil-pbc-localization-flat states flatness for an arbitrary multiplicative set; S = R minus zero is multiplicative because R is an integral domain. Its localization is the usual fraction field K, with inverse of a nonzero fraction a/s equal to s/a. No torsion-free assumption on U or V is introduced.

Step 2 uses lem-weil-pbc-localization-tensor-product for the general multiplicative-set tensor localization statement, not the separate principal-localization node. Its general statement applies with M = R, N = P, M' = K and N' = S^{-1}P. The tensor unit maps and the two displayed fraction/tensor formulas give the identifications used. lem-weil-pbc-flat-base-change-hom-finitely-presented states the actual bijective canonical Hom comparison, with the pure-tensor formula, and explicitly includes finite modules over a Noetherian ring. It therefore applies to U and V without a new finite-presentation lemma. The target module is R; its base change is K. I checked the comparison square by evaluating both routes on u/t, obtaining the same psi(f(u))/(st). The statement requires no finiteness of U* or V*, and none is used.

Step 3 uses precisely the statement of lem-trf-vector-subspace-complement: every subspace of a vector space over a field has a complement, without a finite-dimensionality assumption. The subspace is the image f_K(U_K). The definition beta(f_K(u)+c) = alpha(u), uniqueness of decomposition and injectivity of f_K prove the extension and its linearity in a few lines. This is not a new functional-extension lemma hidden in the step; it is the direct application of the quoted complement statement. It is marked non-elementary because that complement theorem is substantive.

Step 4 uses the fraction equality explicitly present in lem-weil-pbc-localization-module-exact. Surjectivity and compatibility from the preceding two steps give psi/s with the required localized equality. The equality criterion supplies a second nonzero t. The identity (t psi) composed with f = ts phi is then an explicit calculation, with no unquoted right-exactness theorem, cokernel-base-change theorem or finiteness-of-Hom assertion. This step is elementary relative to those already cited statements. It explains exactly how the source's torsion cokernel is obtained.

Step 5 uses only the defining formula for f** and R-linearity: ts x(phi) = x((t psi) composed with f) = 0. Nonzero ts cancels because R is a domain. Applying this to x-y proves injectivity, not only triviality of the kernel. No left-exactness of Hom is invoked without citation: the entire needed calculation is present.

For small cases I checked U = 0, V = 0 (then injectivity forces U = 0), torsion modules such as U = V = Z/n, and the injection Z → Z given by multiplication by a nonzero n. In the latter case f* is multiplication by n and need not be surjective over Z: this confirms why the proof must go to the fraction field and clear denominators, rather than assert surjectivity over R. Over a field S already consists of units and the proof reduces to vector-space duality. Mixed torsion/free modules cause no problem because localization need not be injective on U itself; only the localized map f_K needs to be injective. I did not assume reflexivity or that either module is free.
