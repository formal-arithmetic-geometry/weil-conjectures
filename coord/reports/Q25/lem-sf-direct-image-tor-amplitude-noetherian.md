I have preserved this route. In particular I have not applied `lem-exc-residue-tor-test`, assumed that a Noetherian ring is Artinian, assumed every Λ-module sheaf is torsion, or assumed the base field is separably closed.

The exact statement of `lem-sf-projection-finite-constant` was read: it requires finite cohomological dimension on **all** coefficient module sheaves and a finitely presented constant module. The torsion-abelian dimension theorem proves neither of those hypotheses for a general Noetherian ring. It therefore cannot justify the needed projection step directly. The one new node isolates that genuine missing constant-module projection input, with the finite Tor hypothesis already available on the item. It has no Tor-amplitude conclusion for the output and does not merely rename the item.

The new node's source is explicitly an agent-stated extension of the argument of Stacks Tag 0F12 (`lemma-pull-out-constant-mod-n`), not a claim that 0F12 already states the theorem for a nontorsion ring or a relative direct image. The existing `lem-kun-0f12` states the torsion-ring global-sections case. A future split of the new node can use finite free constant modules, finite syzygies over the Noetherian ring, the way-out argument, and filtered coefficient colimits. For the way-out comparison, tensoring the input with any module has a common finite cohomological interval, and its cohomology is killed by N; hence the needed direct-image bound is the bound on torsion cohomology, not a bound on every Λ-module sheaf. The canonical maps are natural in the coefficient module. For arbitrary modules take the filtered system of finitely generated submodules. Exact filtered colimits, tensor's colimit compatibility, and qcqs higher-image continuity on the common bounded torsion interval pass the canonical comparison to the colimit. This is substantial work for the next item; it is not hidden inside a step of the present split.
