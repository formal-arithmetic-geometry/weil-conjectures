The retained argument for the future worker on the new statement is as follows. With $T_t(K)=R\varprojlim(K\xleftarrow t K\xleftarrow t\cdots)$, the regular-principal reduction tower gives a triangle $T_t(K)\to K\xrightarrow{c_K}C_t(K)\to T_t(K)[1]$, with the quotient augmentation specified in the statement. Thus completeness is equivalent to $T_t(K)=0$. Write $A=\ker g$, $D=\operatorname{coker}g$ and $V=\operatorname{Cone}(M[0]\to N[0])$. Exactness of $T_t$ gives $T_t(V)=0$, and the truncation triangle $A[1]\to V\to D[0]\to A[2]$ then gives $T_t(D[0])\cong T_t(A[0])[2]$. The Roos complex of a degree-zero module tower lies in degrees 0 and 1. The left side lies in degrees 0 and 1, while the right side lies in degrees -2 and -1; hence both vanish. The kernel and cokernel are complete. Apply the cokernel assertion to $A\to M$ to obtain completeness of the ordinary image. Exactness of $T_t$ also proves extension closure from a short exact sequence. This is the substance of Lemma 3.4.14 specialized to one regular generator; its future one-level split should expose the principal completion/telescope comparison and the Roos amplitude input as cited dependencies.
