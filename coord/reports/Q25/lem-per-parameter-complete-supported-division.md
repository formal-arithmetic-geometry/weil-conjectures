As small cases, with one transverse variable and f=Z-W the quotient of h=Z is 1 and the remainder is W; thus a naive transverse-order-preserving or total-degree-d_i argument would be wrong. When m=0 the construction is exactly the existing field theorem. For the necessity of the flatness hypothesis, take A=L[[W]], B=A[[X,Y]], f_1=Y^2, f_2=XY+WY, f_3=X^2 and the degree-lex vertices (0,2),(1,1),(2,0). The diagram is (X,Y)^2, so the reduced indexed family is the partitioned monomial basis and the reduced ideal has supported division. But Y f_3-X f_2+W f_2=W^2Y belongs to J. Its supported remainder is W^2Y itself, since Y lies outside the diagram. Also Y is nonzero modulo J because it survives modulo (W,J)=(W,X^2,XY,Y^2); hence W^2 kills a nonzero class in B/J. Multiplication by W^2 is injective on A, so the definition of flatness shows that B/J is not flat. This explicitly tests the hypothesis used in Steps 6--7.
