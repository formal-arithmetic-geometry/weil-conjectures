### lem-trf-affine-toric-equivariant-ideals

Original step 1:

We work on affine monoid charts $\operatorname{Spec}_{\rm mon}P$ using [def-trf-monoscheme-toric-realization]. The ring $k[P]$ has the specified monomial basis. Localization at $a\in P$ sends $e^p$ to the same symbol and identifies $k[P]_{e^a}$ with $k[P[a^{-1}]]$: its inverse sends $e^{p-na}$ to $e^p/(e^a)^n$. The two assignments respect the defining equality in the monoid localization. They also identify the ideal extended from $I$ with $k[I+\mathbf N(-a)]$. We will glue these explicit monomial identifications on overlaps; all maps send each monomial to the monomial with its image exponent.

Source: literature/illusie-laszlo-orgogozo-travaux-de-gabber_arXiv-1207.3648.pdf; Expose VIII, Proposition 3.2.2 proof and 3.2.14, printed pp.103,105; explicit localization calculation.

Original cited nodes: def-trf-monoscheme-toric-realization, def-trf-fine-fan-blowup.

Original step 2:

On a connected component let $L$ be its generic group and put $A=k[P]$, $B=A\otimes_k k[L]$. By [def-trf-monoscheme-toric-realization], equivariance of $J$ says $JB=\mu(J)B$, where $\mu(e^p)=e^p\otimes e^p$. Thus for $f=\sum_p c_pe^p\in J$, $\mu(f)\in J\otimes_k k[L]$. Apply the coefficient functional at $e^p$ on the second tensor factor: $c_pe^p\in J$, and hence $e^p\in J$ whenever $c_p\ne0$, since $k$ is a field. Set $I=\{p\in P:e^p\in J\}$. Multiplication by $e^q$ shows $I+P\subseteq I$, and the coefficient calculation gives $J=\bigoplus_{p\in I}ke^p=k[I]$. Conversely, for $J=k[I]$, $\mu(e^p)=(e^p\otimes1)(1\otimes e^p)$ and $e^p\otimes1=\mu(e^p)(1\otimes e^{-p})$; these equations give $\mu(J)B=JB$. This proves both equivariance and the inverse formula, including $I=\varnothing$, $J=0$.

Original step 3:

Coherence in this bijection is also local. If $I$ has finite generators, the corresponding monomials generate $k[I]$. Conversely choose finite ring generators of a coherent $J$ on an affine chart, and collect their finitely many support exponents. Step 2 puts every corresponding monomial in $J$; these monomials generate $J$, since they contain the chosen generators and lie in $J$. Their exponents generate $I$: expanding products in a monomial generating set shows that a monomial in the generated ring ideal has exponent equal to a generator plus an element of $P$. The inverse formula and Step 1 therefore glue uniquely on overlaps. The basis shows $k[I]=k[P]$ exactly when $0\in I$, equivalently $I=P$, and $k[I]\ne0$ exactly when $I\ne\varnothing$. Check these assertions on the affine charts of each component. This finishes (i).

### lem-trf-toric-blowup-chart-normalization

Original step 4:

For (ii), choose finite generators $a_1,\ldots,a_s$ of $I$ on an affine chart. Here $s>0$: nonemptiness on a component persists to every nonempty monoid chart since localization cannot turn a nonempty monoid ideal into the empty set. Put $J=k[I]$. By [prop-nagata-affine-blowup-chart-cover], the ordinary blowup is covered by the charts $\operatorname{Spec}k[P][J/e^{a_i}]$. In its fraction field this chart ring is precisely $k[C_i]$, where $C_i=P[I-a_i]\subset P^{\rm gp}$: the generators $e^p$ and $e^{b-a_i}$ are exactly the generators on both sides. This is also the ordinary monoscheme blowup chart in [def-trf-monoscheme-toric-realization]. On the overlap of charts $a_i,a_j$, both identifications invert $e^{a_j-a_i}$, respectively its inverse on the other chart, so they agree on every monomial. Together with Step 1 this gives the canonical ordinary toric blowup comparison on all of $M$.

There are finitely many such charts on a finite affine cover of $M$. Inverting all monomials gives $k[P^{\rm gp}]$ on each side, and all comparison maps act identically there. ### lem-trf-monoscheme-smooth-local-charts

Original step 6:

For smooth morphisms use smallest affine neighborhoods, so a stalk splitting is an actual local chart splitting. Indeed, in $\operatorname{Spec}_{\rm mon}P$ at a prime $\mathfrak p$, its complementary face is generated by those members of a finite generating set of $P$ lying outside $\mathfrak p$. If $a$ is their sum (or $0$ for an empty set), then $D(a)$ is contained in every neighborhood of $\mathfrak p$ and its section monoid is the stalk $P_{\mathfrak p}$. This gives a smallest affine neighborhood at each point, also in a general fine monoscheme. A morphism maps the smallest neighborhood of a point into the smallest neighborhood of its image, and its map of sections is its stalk map. Consequently a smooth monoscheme morphism has on these neighborhoods the chart $P\to P\oplus\mathbf N^r\oplus\mathbf Z^t$ of [def-trf-monoscheme-toric-realization]. Its realized ring map is
$$k[P]\longrightarrow k[P][x_1,\ldots,x_r,y_1^{\pm1},\ldots,y_t^{\pm1}].$$
This map is smooth by the definition [def-sbc-smooth-morphisms]: it has the finite presentation with inverse variables $v_j$ and relations $y_jv_j-1$; across a square-zero thickening lift the $x_i$ arbitrarily and lift each $y_j$ as a unit, whose inverse then supplies $v_j$. A lift of a unit is a unit because $1+n$ has inverse $1-n$ when $n^2=0$. These local morphisms cover the source realization, so [lem-weil-pbc-smooth-local-on-source] proves that $k[N]\to k[M]$ is smooth. This proves the first assertion of (iii).

### lem-trf-saturated-blowup-product-charts

Original step 7:

We can now check smooth pullback in (ii) on the same smallest neighborhoods. In a chart $Q=P\oplus H$, $H=\mathbf N^r\oplus\mathbf Z^t$, the extended ideal is $I+H$. Its chart at $(a,0)$ is
$$Q[(I+H)-(a,0)]=P[I-a]\oplus H.$$
Saturation of this chart is $P[I-a]^{\rm sat}\oplus H$: if a positive multiple of $(g,h)$ lies in the product, saturation of $H$ gives $h\in H$, and the first coordinate gives $g\in P[I-a]^{\rm sat}$; conversely take a multiple witnessing the first coordinate. Thus the realized saturated chart is obtained from $k[P[I-a]^{\rm sat}]$ by the same polynomial and invertible variables. The normalized scheme chart is the very same algebra by [lem-trf-affine-monoid-algebra-normalization]. The base changes and comparison maps send $e^p,x_i,y_j$ to themselves; hence the chart squares commute and so do their localizations and gluings. This is the asserted smooth-pullback compatibility, including identities and composites, since substitution on these generators composes. No general assertion about normalization under arbitrary base change is needed.

### lem-trf-smooth-toric-saturated-sharp-reduction

Original step 8:

For the converse smoothness assertion, restrict to any affine monoid chart $P$ of $M$. Smoothness restricts to its open realization by [lem-weil-pbc-smooth-local-on-source]. ### lem-trf-sharp-monoid-vertex-cotangent

Original step 10:

Let $A$ be the set of atoms of the sharp monoid $R$, namely the nonzero elements not expressible as a sum of two nonzero elements. It is finite and generates $R$. To see this without an infinite descent, choose an inclusion-minimal finite generating set of nonzero elements. If a generator $b$ decomposes, expand its two summands in these generators. An occurrence of $b$ in the expansion would cancel to give a sum of nonzero elements equal to $0$, impossible in a sharp integral monoid, unless one summand were $0$. Thus the expansion omits $b$, contradicting minimality. All generators are therefore atoms; conversely an atom must be one of them, since an expression of length at least two decomposes it. This proves the claim, including $R=\{0\}$ and $A=\varnothing$.

Source: literature/illusie-laszlo-orgogozo-travaux-de-gabber_arXiv-1207.3648.pdf; Expose VIII, proof of Lemma 3.2.12(ii), printed pp.104--105, assertion that the indecomposable nonunits generate; finite minimal-generators argument written out.

Original step 11:

Using $P\cong R\oplus\mathbf Z^t$, put $B=k[P]=k[R][y_1^{\pm1},\ldots,y_t^{\pm1}]$ and let $\mathfrak m$ be the maximal ideal generated by $e^q$ for $q\ne0$ in $R$ and by $y_j-1$. Its residue field is $k$. Modulo $\mathfrak m^2$, the classes of $e^a$ for $a\in A$ and $y_j-1$ span: decomposable exponents give products of two nonunits, and $y^n\equiv1+\sum_j n_j(y_j-1)$ modulo the square, also for negative $n_j$. They are independent. Indeed map $B$ to the square-zero algebra $k\oplus\bigoplus_{a\in A}k\epsilon_a\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^t k\delta_j$ by
$$e^{(q,n)}\longmapsto\begin{cases}1+\sum_jn_j\delta_j&q=0,\\ \epsilon_q&q\in A,\\0&q\ne0\text{ decomposable}.\end{cases}$$
The rule is multiplicative: a sum of two nonzero exponents is decomposable, and multiplication by a unit does not change the first-order atom term. It sends the displayed classes to the distinct basis vectors. Localization at $\mathfrak m$ does not change this quotient, since $B/\mathfrak m=k$ and every denominator acts there by a nonzero scalar. Thus the local cotangent space of $B_{\mathfrak m}$ has dimension $|A|+t$.

### lem-trf-toric-vertex-dimension-freeness

Original step 12:

The finite torsion-free group $P^{\rm gp}$ has a basis by [lem-trf-fg-torsionfree-group-free], of size $r+t$ by Step 9. Its group algebra is a Laurent polynomial algebra in that many variables. Inverting all monomials identifies the fraction field of $B=k[P]$ with the rational function field in those variables: monomials with distinct lattice exponents are linearly independent, and the basis of the group generates all fractions. Its transcendence degree over $k$ is $r+t$. The algebra $B$ is finite type, generated by the monomials of a finite generating set of $P$, and it is a domain by [lem-trf-affine-monoid-algebra-normalization]. Thus [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-domain-dimension-trdeg] gives $\dim B_{\mathfrak m}=r+t$. This local ring is regular by Step 8, so [lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-cotangent-dimension] and Step 11 give $|A|+t=r+t$, hence $|A|=r$. The atoms generate $R$ by Step 10 and hence generate the free group $R^{\rm gp}$. A generating $r$-tuple in a rank-$r$ free abelian group is a basis (its surjective integer matrix has an integer right inverse, so determinant is a unit). Therefore the surjection $\mathbf N^A\to R$ is injective, as any equality would be an integer relation in that basis. It follows that $P\cong\mathbf N^r\oplus\mathbf Z^t$. Applying this to the smallest affine neighborhood of each point proves $M$ smooth.
