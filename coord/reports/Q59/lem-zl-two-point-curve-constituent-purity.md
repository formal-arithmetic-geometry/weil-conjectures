### Returned step 1

Fix the anchor $x_*$ and a closed point $x$, allowing $x=x_*$. Apply [lem-zl-curve-testing-irreducibility] to the nonempty finite set of the chosen geometric lifts (a singleton if the lifts coincide) and to the whole finite family $\mathcal S_1,\ldots,\mathcal S_r$. Its hypotheses are exactly the smooth, geometrically connected, affine, positive-dimensional hypotheses on $X_0$ and the geometric irreducibility hypotheses on the quotients. It gives one smooth connected affine geometric curve $i:C\hookrightarrow X$ through both lifts on which every $i^*\mathcal S_j$ is irreducible. This also applies to the empty family when $r=0$.

Existing inputs: lem-zl-curve-testing-irreducibility.

### Returned step 2

Apply [lem-zl-bertini-finite-field-descent] to this closed immersion to obtain $m\ge1$ and $C_m\hookrightarrow X_{0,m}=X_0\times_{k_0}k_m$, with $k_m=\mathbf F_{q^m}$ and geometric base change $C$. Since $X_{0,m}$ is affine and of finite type, its closed subscheme is affine of finite type: writing $X_{0,m}=\operatorname{Spec}A$, it is $\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)$. Write $B=A/I$. The map $B\to k\otimes_{k_m}B$ is faithfully flat: choose a $k_m$-basis of $k$ containing $1$; as a $B$-module the target is a nonempty direct sum of copies of $B$, so tensoring preserves and reflects exactness, the definition in [def-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-ring-map]. Thus [lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-surjective-spectrum] supplies a geometric prime above every prime of $B$.

Existing inputs: lem-zl-bertini-finite-field-descent, def-weil-closed-immersion, def-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-ring-map, lem-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-surjective-spectrum.

### Returned step 3

For each prime $\mathfrak p$ of $B$, choose a prime of $k\otimes_{k_m}B$ above it as in Step 2. The latter algebra is the coordinate ring of the smooth curve $C$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-smooth-at-prime-field-extension-descent] with base field $k_m$, extension $k$, and finite-type algebra $B$: $B$ is smooth at $\mathfrak p$. These neighborhoods cover $\operatorname{Spec}B$, hence $C_m$ is smooth. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-dimension-at-point-field-extension] at the same primes; its geometric dimension is one, so $C_m$ has dimension one at every point. Finally [lem-weil-geometrically-connected-algebraic-closure] applies because $(C_m)_k=C$ is connected. Thus $C_m$ is a smooth geometrically connected affine curve over $k_m$, exactly the type of curve required by the curve-purity theorem.

Existing inputs: lem-weil-pbc-smooth-at-prime-field-extension-descent, lem-weil-pbc-dimension-at-point-field-extension, lem-weil-geometrically-connected-algebraic-closure.

### Returned step 4

First restrict the given filtration to $X_{0,m}$ using [lem-zl-constant-extension-weil-heart-equivalence](a), then pull it back along $C_m\hookrightarrow X_{0,m}$ using [lem-zl-immersion-weil-local-behavior](1), now over $k_m$. Both functors are exact, so the resulting quotients are precisely $\mathcal S_j|_{C_m}$. Lissity is preserved by pulling back their finite-level locally constant trivializations in [def-pod-lisse-l-adic-sheaves]. The heart restrictions realize the classical ones with their Weil maps: [lem-per-lisse-completed-system-comparison] (classical systems vs lisse completed modules) and [lem-tw-lisse-rational-realization] (lisse heart vs lisse $E_X$-modules) commute with pullback along any scheme map, including non-$k$-linear Frobenius, with composites, and with point fibres and iterated point transports, so the heart local operators below are the classical ones. Their geometric restrictions are the irreducible local systems on $C$ from Step 1. For a closed point $z\in C_m$ over $x\in X_0$, let $y$ be its image in $X_{0,m}$. The immersion identifies $\kappa(z)=\kappa(y)$ and the local operators. Constant restriction identifies the latter operator with $F_x^{[\kappa(y):\kappa(x)]}$. The eigenvalue multiset, including algebraic multiplicities, is given by [lem-fd-weil-pullback-local-frobenius] applied to $g_m:X_{0,m}\to X_0$ as a morphism over $k_0$: clause (b) of [lem-zl-constant-extension-weil-heart-equivalence] identifies the local operators of $g_m^*\mathcal F$ and constant restriction. The immersion then preserves that multiset. Thus the eigenvalues at $z$ are the corresponding $f$th powers and $q_z=q_x^f$, where $f=[\kappa(z):\kappa(x)]\ge1$. The same applies to $\mathcal G$ and to every quotient. The chosen geometric lifts give points $z_*,z$ of $C_m$: their residue fields embed into $k$ and are algebraic over $k_m$, hence these are closed by [lem-kun-closed-point-field](a).

Existing inputs: lem-zl-constant-extension-weil-heart-equivalence, lem-zl-immersion-weil-local-behavior, def-pod-lisse-l-adic-sheaves, lem-kun-closed-point-field, lem-fd-weil-pullback-local-frobenius, lem-per-lisse-completed-system-comparison, lem-tw-lisse-rational-realization.

### Returned step 5

The restriction $\mathcal G|_{C_m}$ is real. Indeed [def-wf-iota-real] says its original local eigenvalue multiset is stable under complex conjugation, including multiplicities. For every positive integer $f$, $\overline{a^f}=\overline a^{\,f}$; hence the multiset of $f$th powers is also conjugation-stable, even when powers of distinct eigenvalues coincide. Apply the local power formula of Step 4 at every closed point of $C_m$. No diagonalizability is required.

Existing inputs: def-wf-iota-real.

### Returned step 6

Pass to Weil representations by the exact equivalence [prop-wf-weil-sheaf-representation-equivalence] on the curve $C_m$ over $k_m$, under which subobjects, quotients and short exact sequences of lisse Weil sheaves correspond to those of the Weil fundamental representations of [def-wf-fundamental-representation]. The restriction of the representation of $\mathcal S_j|_{C_m}$ to the geometric subgroup $\pi_1(C)$ is, by [def-wf-fundamental-representation], the stalk representation of the local system $i^*\mathcal S_j$, which is irreducible by Step 1. A nonzero proper $W$-stable subspace would be $\pi_1(C)$-stable, so the representation of each nonzero quotient $\mathcal S_j|_{C_m}$ is irreducible. By Step 4 the restricted filtration of $\mathcal G|_{C_m}$ is exact with these quotients, so its image under the equivalence is a composition series of the representation of $\mathcal G|_{C_m}$, and every $\mathcal S_j|_{C_m}$ is an irreducible constituent in the sense of [def-real-weil-representation-operations]. Apply [thm-rp-real-lisse-purity-affine] over the finite field $k_m$ to the real lisse sheaf from Step 5 on the smooth geometrically connected curve from Step 3. This gives a real weight $v_j(C_m)$ for which each restricted quotient is punctually $\iota$-pure. The theorem applies to constituents and assumes no semisimplicity of $\mathcal G$.

Existing inputs: prop-wf-weil-sheaf-representation-equivalence, def-wf-fundamental-representation, def-real-weil-representation-operations, thm-rp-real-lisse-purity-affine.

### Returned step 7

Fix $j$. Its stalk at $x_*$ has positive rank and invertible Frobenius, so it has a nonzero eigenvalue $\alpha$ over the algebraically closed coefficient field. For a point $z_*\in C_m$ under the chosen lift of $x_*$ put $e=[\kappa(z_*):\kappa(x_*)]\ge1$. Steps 4 and 6, together with [def-weil-punctual-weights], give $|\iota(\alpha)|^e=(q_{x_*}^{e})^{v_j(C_m)/2}$. Taking real logarithms and dividing by $e\log q_{x_*}>0$ gives $v_j(C_m)=2\log_{q_{x_*}}|\iota(\alpha)|=:w_j$. The same equality applies to every eigenvalue at the anchor, so all have this modulus. This defines $w_j$ independently of the eigenvalue, of the curve, and of the finite extension. To initialize it one may use Step 1 with $x=x_*$, so no existence of a second distinct closed point is needed.

Existing inputs: def-weil-punctual-weights.

### Returned step 8

Let $x$ be any closed point and $\beta$ any eigenvalue of $\mathcal S_j$ there. Choose the two-point curve as above and its point $z$ over $x$, with residue degree $f\ge1$. By Step 4 and curve purity, $|\iota(\beta)|^f=(q_x^f)^{w_j/2}=q_x^{fw_j/2}$. Taking the unique positive $f$th root yields $|\iota(\beta)|=q_x^{w_j/2}$. This includes the anchor itself by Step 7 and proves purity everywhere by [def-weil-punctual-weights]. For $r=0$ there are no weights or eigenvalue assertions to check, and Steps 1-5 still give the required curve and reality. Both Frobenius-power and residue-size clauses are already proved in Step 4. Thus all assertions hold simultaneously for the supplied filtration without choosing a semisimplification.

Existing inputs: def-weil-punctual-weights.
