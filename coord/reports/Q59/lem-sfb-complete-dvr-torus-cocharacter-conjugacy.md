## Arguments retained for the two statement-only inputs

For the field input I read Milne, Algebraic Groups (2017), Theorem 12.9(b),(c), printed pp.233--234; Definition 12.14 and the paragraph about split-torus quotients, p.236; and Theorem 25.10 with its proof, p.546. Factor a split-torus homomorphism through its scheme-theoretic faithfully flat image (the existing affine homomorphism theorem). The image is the quotient by its scheme-theoretic kernel; diagonalizable duality makes its character group a subgroup of the free character lattice of the source. Such a subgroup is free, so the image is a split torus even for inseparable kernels. Choose a split torus containing it of largest dimension; dimensions are bounded by the finite dimension of G. A strict inclusion between split tori strictly increases dimension: on characters it is a surjection of free lattices, and equal rank would make its kernel zero. Thus the chosen torus is maximal split. Milne 25.10 conjugates it into the prescribed maximal split torus by an element of G(k). Compose the image factorization with that conjugation to obtain v. No algebraic-closure descent of a chosen point is being asserted. The statement is a genuine residue-field input even for finite or imperfect fields.

For the fixed-point input I read Milne Theorem 13.1 and the weight-splitting proof of Lemma 13.6, printed pp.254--256. The relative infinitesimal assertion is agent-written; here is the algebra argument to retain for the later worker. The multiplicative-group action is a Z-grading C=direct sum C_d, with R in degree zero. A fixed B-point is precisely a graded map to B concentrated in degree zero. Take a polynomial presentation P onto C by finitely many homogeneous generators with homogeneous kernel K. Smoothness of C over R gives a split conormal sequence K/K^2 -> Omega_(P/R) tensor_P C -> Omega_(C/R) -> 0. All modules and the inclusion are graded. The degree-zero component of an ordinary C-linear retraction of the inclusion is again a C-linear retraction: on a homogeneous vector take the component in that vector's degree; multiplication by a homogeneous scalar respects this operation. Thus the retraction may be chosen graded. To lift a graded map C -> B_0 along B_1 -> B_0, first map the degree-zero polynomial generators to arbitrary lifts in B_1 and all nonzero-degree generators to zero. This gives a graded map phi:P -> B_1. Since phi(K) lies in J and J^2=0, phi on K induces a graded C-linear map K/K^2 -> J, where C acts through the given map to B_0. Compose with the graded conormal retraction to extend it to Omega_(P/R) tensor C -> J, hence to a graded R-derivation delta:P -> J. Then phi-delta is a graded algebra map because products of J vanish, and it kills K. It descends to the required graded lift C -> B_1. This proves precisely the square-zero statement, without invoking field-only regularity arguments or an unmentioned representability theorem. A future split of this input must cite the conormal and grading facts; they are not silently treated as elementary steps in the present item.
