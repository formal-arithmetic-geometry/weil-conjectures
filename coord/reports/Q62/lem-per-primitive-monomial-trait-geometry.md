Step 3 uses exactly E and U. The coefficient ring is the polynomial ring in all variables other than j, not the fraction field of that ring. The ideal pi A is prime because its quotient is a polynomial domain. Its square does not contain pi, checked by coefficient comparison and cancellation. The leading monomial does not vanish mod pi. Primitivity follows by examining a common divisor of the leading coefficient and pi, a prime element. These verifications are essential: the leading coefficient need not be a unit, so a monic Eisenstein citation would not suffice. The variable separation is the elementary polynomial reindexing identification. The quotient-prime and integral clauses of U finish integrality; there is no appeal to geometric integrality of the generic fibre, which can fail.

Steps 4 and 5 use the explicit monomial partial-derivative definition and the smoothness lifting definition.
