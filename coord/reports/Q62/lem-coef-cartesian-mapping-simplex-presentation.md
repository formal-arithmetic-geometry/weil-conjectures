## Retained proof plans for extracted arguments

### Original step 7

We next justify composition on $\mathfrak C[Y]$ without assuming it is locally Kan or that the pinched resolution preserves composition. For ANY quasicategory $S$, put $D_S=\mathfrak C[S]$ and take the object-preserving locally Kan replacement $r:D_S\to RD_S$ of [lem-coef-coherent-nerve-comparison]. Write $W=N_{hc}(RD_S)$ and let $a:S\to W$ be the adjoint of $r$. Every simplicial set is Joyal-cofibrant, since cofibrations are monomorphisms. The map $r$ is a Bergner weak equivalence and $RD_S$ is fibrant: its mapping sets are Kan and the equivalence-lifting condition for its map to the terminal category is satisfied by identity arrows. The weak-equivalence criterion for the stated Quillen equivalence therefore makes $a$ a categorical equivalence. By rigidification detection $\mathfrak C[a]$ is a Dwyer--Kan equivalence. Apply the natural squares of $\theta_{S,x,y}$ and $p_K:|K|_Q\to K$ from [lem-coef-pinched-rigidification-resolution] to $a$: their horizontal maps are weak equivalences, and $\mathfrak C[a]$ is a local weak equivalence, so two-out-of-three makes every $\operatorname{Map}_S(x,y)\to\operatorname{Map}_W(ax,ay)$ a weak equivalence. This uses only naturality of $\theta$, not composition compatibility.

The same coherent-nerve node supplies equivalences $RD_S(x,y)\simeq\operatorname{Map}_W(ax,ay)$ that DO preserve enriched composition and send vertices to their corresponding edges. The vertex $r([h])$ corresponds exactly to $a(h)$ by the adjunction defining $a$. The triangle formula for composition is natural under $a$, since applying $a$ commutes with all three edge restrictions and with the pinch maps. Consequently, if postcomposition by an edge $h:y\to z$ is a weak equivalence $\operatorname{Map}_S(w,y)\to\operatorname{Map}_S(w,z)$, it is a weak equivalence on the corresponding mapping spaces of $W$ (all the maps induced by $a$ are equivalences), then on $RD_S(w,y)\to RD_S(w,z)$ (the locally Kan comparison preserves composition), and finally on $D_S(w,y)\to D_S(w,z)$ (the maps $r$ are local weak equivalences and commute STRICTLY with enriched composition). This is the required cited route through $R\mathfrak C[S]$ and $N_{hc}$.

On components it also identifies $\pi_0\mathfrak C[S]$ with $hS$, fixing objects and edge classes and respecting composition: use $r$, the locally Kan comparison and the fully faithful map $a$, all identity on this object set. Thus an isomorphism class between vertices in $\pi_0\mathfrak C[S]$ is represented by an equivalence edge of $S$, by the inverse-triangle characterization in [lem-coef-infinity-mapping-composition].

### Original step 9

We prove the marking-reflection assertion, using both directions of Step 6's criterion. First record its consequence for equivalences over an identity of the base. If $u$ is an equivalence in a fiber, postcomposition by $u$ on every mapping space has homotopy inverse given by an inverse edge, by associativity and the inverse triangles. Composing an edge $b$ before or after such fiber equivalences changes its Cartesian mapping square by equivalences of corners, with the same base corners. The path-space homotopy-pullback criterion is invariant under these changes by [lem-coef-kan-homotopy-pullback-invariance]; the coherent comparison homotopies enter the path coordinate by concatenation, and the inverse fiber equivalences give inverse comparisons up to the associativity and inverse-triangle homotopies. The iff direction therefore says that the new edge is Cartesian exactly when $b$ is. This establishes the closure and reflection under fiber equivalences used here; it is not an extra assertion of the mapping-fiber lemma.

Every $y\in Y_j$ is equivalent within $Y_j$ to some $q(a)$, $a\in(A_j)_0$. Indeed rigidification detection makes $\mathfrak C[A_j]\to\mathfrak C[Y_j]$ essentially surjective on $\pi_0$, and Step 7 identifies the latter category with $hY_j$. Given a Cartesian $d:x\to y$ over $i\to j$, let $c=q(t_{j,i}a)$ and choose a fiber equivalence $v:q(a)\simeq y$. The marked edge $e_a:t_{j,i}a\to a$ has Cartesian image, so choose a triangle composite $b= vq(e_a):c\to y$; it is Cartesian by the preceding square argument. The cited mapping-fiber lemma is now used ONLY with test vertices in $Y_i$, where its hypothesis is satisfied. Applied to $d$ at $w=c$ it gives $u:c\to x$ in $Y_i$ with $du\simeq b$ over $i\to j$; applied to $b$ at $w=x$ it gives $t:x\to c$ with $bt\simeq d$. Since the postcomposition maps are equivalences, their injectivity on components at $w=x$ and $w=c$ gives $ut\simeq1_x$ and $tu\simeq1_c$, respectively. Hence $u$ is a fiber equivalence. This explicitly supplies an equivalence of arrows $d\simeq vq(e_a)u^{-1}$.

For any simplicial map $f:Y\to Z$ over $\Delta^n$, with $Z$ Cartesian, functors preserve equivalences and inverse-triangle equations. If $fq$ preserves marked edges, $f(q(e_a))$ is Cartesian. The square argument applied in $Z$ to the fiber equivalences $f(u),f(v)$ then recognizes $f(d)$ as Cartesian. The reverse implication holds because $q$ is marked. Thus $f$ preserves all Cartesian edges exactly when $fq$ preserves the marked edges. For a coherent equivalence of functors, its endpoint equivalences and naturality triangles give the same comparison of the image of each edge; the same square argument proves invariance of the preservation condition.

### Original step 10

Let $Z\to\Delta^n$ be any small Cartesian test fibration. Its total space is a quasicategory. Step 8 and [lem-coef-joyal-functor-equivalence-test] give an equivalence $q^*:\operatorname{Fun}(Y,Z)\to\operatorname{Fun}(M(A),Z)$. The relative flat marked mapping objects are full subcategories of these two functor quasicategories. To see fullness, a simplex between vertex maps over $\Delta^n$ projects on every vertex $(x,t)$ to the fixed base vertex of $x$; a map to $\Delta^n=N([n])$ is uniquely determined by its vertices. Hence it is over the base in every parameter degree, and the flat marking asks only that each parameter-vertex map preserve the specified edges. The selected objects are invariant under functor equivalences: projected equivalence edges in $[n]$ are identities, and Cartesian preservation is invariant by Step 9. Moreover a functor $f:Y\to Z$ lies in the selected full subcategory exactly when $fq$ does. For the base-map condition this follows from the fiberwise essential surjectivity in Step 9 and uniqueness of simplices in $N([n])$; for the marking condition it is precisely Step 9. Choose a coherent quasi-inverse R to the absolute restriction equivalence. If h is a selected object on the mapping-simplex side, then q^*R(h) is equivalent to h and hence is selected, so the reflection just proved makes R(h) selected on the Y side. Thus R restricts to the selected full subcategories. Their fullness also retains every simplex of the units and counits, so these restricted maps are coherent quasi-inverses. This proves that the restricted functor is a functor equivalence directly from its definition. Relative marked calculus identifies their cores with the sharp relative mapping complexes. Here is the core argument explicitly. A simplicial functor preserves equivalences by sending the two inverse triangles to inverse triangles, so each restricted quasi-inverse maps cores to cores. A coherent natural equivalence $H:F\simeq G$ restricts to a simplicial homotopy on cores: for an edge $(a,0\to1)$ of the core times $\Delta^1$, the triangle in that product gives $[H(a,0\to1)]=[H_{\mathrm{target}(a)}][F(a)]$, a product of equivalences. Edges with constant parameter are also equivalences. Thus every simplex lands in the core by its defining edge condition. Apply this to both unit and counit. Their restrictions give two simplicial inverse homotopies. The realization-cylinder homeomorphism turns them into continuous inverse homotopies, and [lem-coef-homotopy-equivalence-weak] makes the map on cores a weak homotopy equivalence. We obtain
$$\operatorname{Map}^\sharp_{\Delta^n}(Y^\natural,Z^\natural)\simeq\operatorname{Map}^\sharp_{\Delta^n}(M^m(A),Z^\natural).$$
This is the defining test for $q$ to be a Cartesian equivalence, and proves the criterion for every $q$ satisfying the hypothesis.

### Original step 11

Finally, for any simplicial set $B$, quasicategory $E$ and map $b:B\to E$, the absolute marked test of $B^\flat\to E^\natural$ against a naturally marked quasicategory $C$ is exactly the restriction of cores $\operatorname{Fun}(E,C)^\simeq\to\operatorname{Fun}(B,C)^\simeq$. Indeed all simplicial maps $E\to C$ preserve equivalences by applying them to the two inverse triangles; the minimally marked domain imposes no edge condition, and the absolute flat mapping objects are therefore $\operatorname{Fun}(E,C)$ and $\operatorname{Fun}(B,C)$. The marked mapping calculus identifies their sharp versions with these cores. If $b$ is categorical, [lem-coef-joyal-functor-equivalence-test] gives coherent quasi-inverses on these functor quasicategories, and Step 10's explicit core-homotopy argument makes the displayed core restriction a weak homotopy equivalence. Conversely, assume this core restriction is weak for every quasicategory $C$. Fix $C$, put $D=\operatorname{Fun}(E,C)$, $F=\operatorname{Fun}(B,C)$, and let $p:D\to F$ be restriction. For every small simplicial $H$, the test with the quasicategory $\operatorname{Fun}(H,C)$ says that
$$\operatorname{Fun}(H,D)^\simeq\longrightarrow\operatorname{Fun}(H,F)^\simeq$$
is a weak homotopy equivalence: interchange $H$ with $E$, respectively $B$, in the product defining an internal Hom. These isomorphisms commute with simplicial operators and restriction and hence preserve cores. Take $H=F$. Surjectivity on components gives $s:F\to D$ and a coherent equivalence $ps\simeq1_F$. Take $H=D$. Injectivity on components, applied to $psp\simeq p$, gives $sp\simeq1_D$. Here these cores are Kan by marked mapping calculus, so a path of vertices compresses to an equivalence edge by successive two-dimensional horn fillers; thus the component assertions give actual coherent natural equivalences. Therefore $p$ is a coherent functor equivalence. This works for every $C$ in a universe containing $B,E,C,D,F$, so [lem-coef-joyal-functor-equivalence-test] makes $b$ categorical. This proves the absolute marked equivalence iff categorical equivalence directly from the point-model test. Apply this with $B=A_i$, $E=Y_i$. Pullback and induced-marking compatibility of relative marked calculus identify the marking on $Y_i$ with the Cartesian edges of $Y_i\to\Delta^0$. Step 6's IFF criterion for this map says that an edge $h:y\to z$ is Cartesian exactly when $h\circ-:\operatorname{Map}_{Y_i}(w,y)\to\operatorname{Map}_{Y_i}(w,z)$ is an equivalence for every $w$. If $h$ is invertible these maps have inverse postcomposition by $h^{-1}$. Conversely surjectivity on components at $w=z$ gives $g:z\to y$ with $hg\simeq1_z$; injectivity at $w=y$, applied to $hgh\simeq h$, gives $gh\simeq1_y$. The inverse-triangle characterization then makes $h$ an equivalence. Thus the induced marking is exactly the natural equivalence marking, with both implications justified. Thus this is the claimed equivalent fiber hypothesis. The formulas and both inductions included $n=0$; empty fibers impose no nonemptiness requirement in any lifting or equivalence test. In particular $Y_i=\varnothing$ forces $A_i=\varnothing$ whenever a fiber equivalence is given, and if all fibers are empty the sharp mapping complexes out of the empty total space are points, not empty spaces. No part of the argument asserts fibrancy of $M^m(A)$.
