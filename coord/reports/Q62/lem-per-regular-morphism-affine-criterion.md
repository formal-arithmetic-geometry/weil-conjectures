For step 1 I opened and read each of the six cited statements. The residue-field identification explicitly commutes with the germ/residue map, so the affine tensor product uses the same A-algebra structure as the scheme fibre. The open fibre-product statement gives the actual open subscheme, not merely equality of underlying point sets. The affine fibre-product statement supplies both the Spec tensor presentation and compatibility with projections. The locally Noetherian definition then applies to this covering of X_y. Choosing affine neighbourhoods inside an open is the affine basis property of a scheme; joint covering follows pointwise from the inverse-image opens. No intersection of affine opens is assumed affine.

For step 2 I read def-per-regular-scheme-morphism and def-weil-pbc-g-ring in full. The former uses every finite residue-field extension and flatness, exactly the hypotheses of condition (1) of the new comparison. The flatness criterion's statement applies to every affine pair, not only one selected covering. The new comparison's condition (2) gives geometric regularity of the coordinate ring of the affine open U_y constructed in step 1. Its structural field is kappa(y), identified compatibly with kappa(p) there. The regular-ring-map definition is flatness plus Noetherian geometrically regular residue fibre rings, precisely what these arguments give.

For step 3 the same definition yields flatness of all coordinate maps, so the converse of the existing flatness criterion applies. Each y has a cover by the U_y from step 1; these are genuine affine opens, so condition (3) of the new comparison applies to X_y, whose local Noetherianity was already checked. Its conclusion gives regularity after every finite field extension, not just after algebraic closure. Together with flatness this is exactly the project's definition of a regular morphism.
