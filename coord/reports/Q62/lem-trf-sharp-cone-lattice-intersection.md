## Exact previous six-step proof

1. Identify $P$ with its image in $G$ using integrality in [def-trf-fs-log-blowup]. Choose finite monoid generators $p_1,\ldots,p_m$. If $ng=0$ for an integer $n>0$, both $ng$ and $n(-g)$ lie in $P$. Saturation puts both $g$ and $-g$ in $P$. They are additive inverses in $P$, so sharpness gives $g=0$. Consequently $G$ is torsion-free.

2. Apply [lem-trf-fg-torsionfree-group-free] to the finitely generated torsion-free abelian group $G$ established in Step 1. It gives $r\in\mathbf N$ and a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_r$: every $g$ is uniquely $\sum_k n_ke_k$ with $n_k\in\mathbf Z$. This proves the finite-free assertion in (a).

3. For each basis coordinate $k$, let $\lambda_k:G\to\mathbf Z$ send $\sum_i n_i e_i$ to $n_k$. The balanced bilinear map $(g,q)\mapsto q\lambda_k(g)$ induces $L_k:G\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\mathbf Q\to\mathbf Q$ with $L_k(g\otimes q)=q\lambda_k(g)$. If $g\otimes1=0$, then every coordinate $\lambda_k(g)=L_k(g\otimes1)$ is zero, whence $g=0$. This proves the injection in (a). Also every tensor is a finite sum of pure tensors, and $g\otimes q=\sum_k q\lambda_k(g)(e_k\otimes1)$, so every element of $V$ is a rational combination of the $e_k\otimes1$. For $r=0$, the same description says $G=V=0$.

4. Write $v_j=p_j\otimes1$. Each $p\in P$ is $\sum_j a_jp_j$ with $a_j\in\mathbf N$, so substituting this expression into any nonnegative rational combination of elements of $P$ shows $C=\{\sum_j q_jv_j:q_j\in\mathbf Q_{\ge0}\}$. This proves rational polyhedrality with the lattice generators $p_j\in G$. Each $e_k$ is an integer combination $\sum_j b_{kj}p_j$ by Step 1. For $x=\sum_k t_k(e_k\otimes1)$, set $c_j=\sum_k t_kb_{kj}\in\mathbf Q$. Then $x=\sum_j\max(c_j,0)v_j-\sum_j\max(-c_j,0)v_j$, a difference of two elements of $C$. This proves the spanning assertion in (b), including the empty generating family.

5. Certainly $P\subseteq C\cap G$. Conversely, let $g\in G$ have $g\otimes1=\sum_j q_j(p_j\otimes1)\in C$ with $q_j\in\mathbf Q_{\ge0}$. Write $q_j=a_j/b_j$ with $a_j\in\mathbf N$ and $b_j\in\mathbf N_{>0}$. Put $N=\prod_j b_j>0$ and $A_j=a_j\prod_{i\ne j}b_i\in\mathbf N$. This proves (c); the empty family has $N=1$ and gives $g=0$.
