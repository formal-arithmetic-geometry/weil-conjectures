## Review by NS

I read the sources above with the actual node statements open and retained every hypothesis of the item. Milne's proof supplies the compactification argument, while the Bhatt–Scholze comparison must justify its use with the specified adic categories. I did not replace these categories with discrete rational etale sheaves, nor infer a support statement for an inverse limit merely from levelwise supports.

For step 1, sk-thm-nagata-compactification says exactly that a separated finite-type map to a qcqs base has a dense compactification, and sk-def-nagata-sketch-data spells out the quasi-compact open immersion and proper map. Spec k satisfies qcqs. Properness gives the same separated finite-type setting on the compactification; no smooth or descent condition is required.

For step 2, the new comparison statement explicitly supplies each object, fibre identification, Hom identification and the equality of the compact-to-ordinary arrow with restriction. It is not being cited just for the existence of an equivalence. I checked its hypotheses against the compactification from step 1, and its common-finite-stage clause against the Qbar coefficient stage of the item. Exact extension by zero and the degree-zero normalization are both necessary: an arbitrary equivalence without the named arrow would not suffice. The future proof can combine the existing continuous realization, exact module extension on the slice site and its integral constructibility with ordinary direct-image realization and the point Hom formula; Remark 6.8.15 is the source for these operation comparisons. This generic comparison has no assertion of injectivity or support, so the argument has not been moved wholesale into it.

For step 3, lem-per-adic-heart-pullback-fibres explicitly states exactness and joint conservativity, for arbitrary separated finite-type schemes and identity pullback in particular. The proof tests the image of t using exactness, then uses conservativity on that object. Only fibres of constructible heart objects are used; no assertion about arbitrary pro-etale sheaves is made. Interior fibres vanish from the hypothesis alpha(c)=0 and boundary fibres from the comparison node.

For step 4, the new support lemma states closedness of the nonzero-fibre locus of the actual section t and vanishing on its complement. Mere constructibility of J's support would not say this. It applies on the entire compactification to J, which is constructible but need not be lisse. The zero boundary fibres from step 2 exclude every boundary point from S. In particular we take S itself, not a closure inside X which might later acquire boundary points.

I checked the support lemma against the source's integral and rational models: choose a constructible integral model and clear the denominator of the unit morphism. Quotient out its uniformly bounded integral torsion, which does not change its rationalization. On a finite stratification this torsion-free model is a finite-free integral local system; refine into finitely many connected pieces. A nonzero integral section on such a piece is detected by one finite reduction at a geometric fibre, and the invariant-vector description at that finite level detects it everywhere on the piece. Taking the maximum of these finitely many levels makes the nonzero rational-fibre locus equal to the support of one finite-level section. The latter is closed: a zero geometric germ of a section is zero on an etale neighborhood, whose image is open. This explains why a single support works. The finite-stratification/local-system comparison and torsion removal are the substantive content left to the new unsplit node, not an unrecorded lemma hidden inside an item step.

For step 5, the cited proper-closed-subset-properties statement actually contains (1) equivalence with some closed scheme structure being proper and (2) inheritance by a smaller closed subset. These first make the whole compactification proper as a closed subset and then S proper. The reduced closed subscheme on S restricts to precisely itself on the open X containing all of S, so this is the definition of properness of Z required in the item. The argument does not infer properness merely from topological compactness. The elementary restriction of the defining ideal is written in the step, rather than a new node.

For step 6, the comparison node explicitly has open-restriction and unit normalizations. The existing lem-tw-lisse-rational-realization explicitly realizes unit morphisms and respects pullback and coefficient extensions for all finite-type schemes, so it normalizes a lisse restriction without adding a smoothness hypothesis. I use lem-a1-degree-zero-compact-support-sections only for its ordinary lisse H0 comparison where its smooth finite-field/Weil hypotheses hold, never its compact-support or proper-support conclusions. All these maps are the scalar-extension/unit maps, which gives their agreement rather than an unspecified transported identification.

Edge cases: X empty and G zero give zero Hom and compact-support spaces and Z empty. If X is proper, the identity compactification is permitted and alpha is the ordinary H0 identification. A skyscraper sheaf has a closed point support; a sheaf j!L may have nonclosed sheaf support, but any global unit morphism still satisfies the new section-support assertion. On the affine line with the constant sheaf, a nonzero section would have full nonproper support, so Hc0 vanishes, as the endpoint route needs. Disconnected and nonreduced schemes are allowed; no connectedness or reducedness is used, and proper subsets always receive their reduced induced structure. No Frobenius-stable integral lattice is assumed.
