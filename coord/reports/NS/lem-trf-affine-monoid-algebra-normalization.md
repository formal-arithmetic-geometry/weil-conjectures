## Returned proof verbatim

1. Put $Q=P^{\mathrm{sat}}$. Since $P$ is fine, it is finitely generated and embeds in $G$. By [lem-trf-fine-monoid-finite-saturation], $Q$ is finitely generated, integral and saturated, its group completion is $G$, and $k[Q]$ is a finite $k[P]$-module. This proves the finiteness clause and supplies the finite-generation hypothesis for $Q$ used next.

2. Apply [lem-trf-monoid-algebra-domain-fraction] to $P\subseteq G$ and to $Q\subseteq G$. Both monoids are finitely generated and generate the torsion-free group $G$. The monomial maps embed $k[P]\subseteq k[Q]\subseteq k[G]$, all three are domains, and their fraction fields identify with $K=\operatorname{Frac}(k[G])$. In particular the inclusion is $k[G]\subseteq K$.

3. For each $g\in Q$, the monomial criterion [lem-trf-monomial-integrality] gives that $e^g$ is integral over $k[P]$. The final clause of that node gives that every element of $k[Q]$ is integral over $k[P]$. Thus $k[Q]$ is contained in the integral closure of $k[P]$ inside $K$.

4. The finite generators of $P$ generate $G$ as a group. Since $G$ is torsion free, [lem-trf-fg-torsionfree-group-free] gives a finite basis $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ of $G$. Put $B_0=k$ and $B_i=B_{i-1}[X_i]$ for $1\leq i\leq r$. The field $k$ is a normal domain: its fraction field is itself. Inductively [lem-weil-pbc-polynomial-ring-normal-domain] says that $B_i$ is a normal domain whenever $B_{i-1}$ is one. Hence $B=B_r=k[X_1,\ldots,X_r]$ is a normal domain, including $B=k$ when $r=0$.

5. Let $S=\{X_1^{b_1}\cdots X_r^{b_r}:b_i\in\mathbf N\}\subset B$. Since $B$ is a domain and these monomials are nonzero, $0\notin S$. The map $X_i\mapsto e^{u_i}$ defines $B\to k[G]$; the elements of $S$ map to units, with $(e^{u_i})^{-1}=e^{-u_i}$, so it extends to $\theta:S^{-1}B\to k[G]$. Define its inverse on the monomial basis by $e^{\sum_i n_i u_i}\mapsto\prod_iX_i^{n_i^+}/\prod_iX_i^{n_i^-}$, where $n_i^+=\max(n_i,0)$ and $n_i^-=\max(-n_i,0)$, and extend $k$-linearly. The unique basis coordinates make this well defined; multiplying fractions adds the exponent vectors, so this is a ring map. Both composites fix the coefficients, all $X_i$ and $X_i^{-1}$, respectively every $e^g$, and hence are identities. Thus $k[G]\simeq S^{-1}B$.

6. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-localization-normal-domain] to the normal domain $B$ and the multiplicative subset $S$, which does not contain zero. It gives that $S^{-1}B$ is a normal domain. The explicit isomorphism of the previous step therefore shows that $k[G]$ is integrally closed in its fraction field $K$: every $z\in K$ satisfying a monic polynomial over $k[G]$ lies in $k[G]$. This is the ambient-field assertion needed before discussing support.

7. Let $x\in K$ be integral over $k[P]$. A monic equation $x^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_0=0$ with $a_i\in k[P]$ is also a monic equation over $k[G]$, because $k[P]\subseteq k[G]$. The ambient-field assertion just proved implies $x\in k[G]$. Only now write its finite support $x=\sum_{g\in T}c_ge^g$. By [lem-trf-integral-support-saturation], every $g\in T$ belongs to $Q$. Therefore $x\in k[Q]$. Together with Step 3, this proves that $k[Q]$ is exactly the integral closure of $k[P]$ in $K$.

8. Finally let $y\in K$ be integral over $k[Q]$. Its monic equation also has coefficients in $k[G]$, so Step 6 gives $y\in k[G]$. By Step 1, $Q$ is finitely generated and saturated, with torsion-free group completion $G$. Apply [lem-trf-saturated-monoid-algebra-normal] to $Q$ and this element $y$ of $k[G]$: its statement gives $y\in k[Q]$. This proves integral closedness in the full field $K$, rather than only in the group algebra. All arguments allow $r=0$ and negative basis coordinates, so neither the zero-rank case nor monoids with units require an exception.
