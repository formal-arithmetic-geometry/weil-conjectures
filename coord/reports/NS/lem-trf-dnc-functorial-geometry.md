## Statement typing clarification — STATEMENT-DISPUTED

Taken as an arbitrary morphism of underlying schemes, the original phrase “let f:X'→X satisfy …” does not make `f_D` a D-morphism. For example choose the permitted field k=the algebraic closure of F_p, take X=X'=Spec k and I=J=0, and let sigma be its nonidentity Frobenius automorphism. The resulting scheme automorphism satisfies the ideal inclusion but is not a k-morphism: the two composites to Spec k are different. Thus f×_k id_D is not a fibre-product morphism, and its generic fibre cannot be the stipulated k(t)-morphism. Varshavsky Lemma 1.4.3 explicitly starts with a “morphism of schemes over k”. I inserted “be a k-morphism”, spelling out that source hypothesis already implicit in the intended ambient category. I also replaced the final `Fix(c)` by `Fix(c)_eta=F×_k eta`, and spelled out the relative fibre-product definition of Fix(tilde c).
