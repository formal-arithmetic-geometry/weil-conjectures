## Review by NS

I read Milne Definition 18.1 (pp. 118–119) and Remark 29.7 (p. 166) for the compactification model and the actual degree-zero support argument. I read Bhatt–Scholze 6.8.2, 6.8.4, 6.8.14 and 6.8.15 for the three coefficient models. For the new lemma I read 7.4.7 and 7.4.10: on a normal Noetherian component the pro-etale fundamental group is the profinite etale group, so the continuous finite-dimensional E-representation has compact image. Such an image preserves an integral lattice (the orbit of a standard lattice is bounded, and its A-span lies between two scalar multiples of the standard lattice). The integral local system corresponding to that lattice realizes as a lisse completed module by 6.8.4. Finitely many clopen components allow the lattices to be combined. I checked Stacks Tags 056T, 01VB and 0BQ3 for the geometric input: smoothness survives field extension, the geometric fibre is normal, and normal implies geometrically unibranch. This is a real monodromy/lattice lemma, not a clause about cohomology or proper support, and it is not a two-line consequence of any cited DAG statement. It therefore belongs as the single new node rather than as an unsupported inline claim.

The exact statement of lem-pur-compact-support-integral-coefficient-comparison then supplies compact-support extension of E with identity and composition laws and finite-level normalizations. lem-weil-point-sections-scalar-extension applies to that bounded constructible output at the geometric point. Ordinary sections commute with the finite free coefficient extension because, in a basis, it is a finite direct sum. I corrected the initially mismatched citation here instead of treating its title as evidence.
8. lem-sfb-noetherian-connected-components-open supplies finitely many clopen connected components. On each, cor-galois-lisse-representations(1) is applied only to finite etale locally constant modules: full faithfulness of the fibre functor detects whether a section map from the unit is zero. Thus each support is a union of whole components, and increasing supports stabilize. The fibre convention is exactly def-pod-lisse-l-adic-sheaves; finite-free fibres ensure rationalization detects nonzero elements. This proves closedness and properness by equality to one finite-level support, not merely containment in a union.
9. Each algebraic-closure coefficient expression uses finitely many scalars, so one finite extension and one uniformizer denominator suffice. Step 7 permits that common stage. An invertible scalar preserves precisely the nonzero-fibre locus. This final step is elementary and uses the preceding steps, rather than introducing a new cohomology comparison.
