## Review by NS

I read the item against Tag 0EY2 and checked the local/global distinction: topological dimension at a point is the dimension of small open neighbourhoods, not the Krull dimension of the local ring. Invariance in Tag 02FY(1) concerns exactly the former. Purity of a fibre by itself on an arbitrary topological space would not suffice for the local equality; Step 3 supplies the locally finite type morphism to its residue field so the locally algebraic dimension theorem applies.

For Step 1 I opened `lem-trf-retrocompact-bounded-fibre-dimension` and read its statement: it gives both openness and retrocompactness for every nonnegative integer bound under locally finite presentation, with no qcqs base hypothesis. For d greater than zero the bound is d-1, hence nonnegative. At d=0 I did not apply that theorem at the invalid bound -1: `def-kun-topological-dimension-at-point` explicitly places dimension at an existing point in {0,1,...,infinity}, so the low locus is empty. Empty is open and its intersection with each quasi-compact open is empty, hence retrocompact.

For Step 2 I opened `def-weil-constructible-subset`. Its statement defines constructible subsets as finite unions of U intersect the complement of V, with U and V retrocompact open. The complement here is exactly one such piece with U equal to the entire space and V equal to the Step 1 locus; the entire space is retrocompact since its intersection with W is W. Thus this is an elementary definition calculation, not an uncited complement theorem or new lemma. It works also for empty X and d=0.

For Step 3 I read `def-weil-finite-type-morphism`, which explicitly includes locally finite type in finite type; I read the statement of `lem-weil-pbc-lft-base-change`, which preserves locally finite type for any morphism of bases. Applied once along g and once along the residue-field point of T', it supplies the needed structure map of the fibre. `def-pc-scheme-fibre` explicitly identifies that object as the relevant fibre product over the residue field. No property of g other than being a morphism is used, and no finite type hypothesis on T' is introduced.

For Step 4 I read `def-weil-pure-dimension` and `lem-sbc-equidimensional-open-local-dimension`. The former says every component has dimension d; the latter, for a scheme locally finite type over a field, says that equal dimensions of all components of an open O give local dimension d at every point of O. I take the residue field as k and O=Y, the whole fibre. A fibre containing the chosen point is nonempty, so the item's nonempty-fibre hypothesis applies. This proves the local equality at every point, including generic points; no closed-point assumption appears in the cited statement.

For Step 5 I opened `lem-sc-fibre-dimension-after-base-change`: its statement gives exactly the equality of local dimensions at x' and its image x, for an arbitrary cartesian square with f locally finite type. All these hypotheses are available. It also identifies the fibre as the residue-field base change, so no separate unrecorded comparison is needed. Substituting the local equality from Step 4 and observing that d is not at most d-1 gives image containment. This does not claim the image is constructible; only E is constructible.

For Step 6 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-dimension-supremum-local-dimensions`: it explicitly gives the global dimension as the supremum of local dimensions for any topological space, with empty supremum -infinity. This statement says exactly what Step 6 takes from it. Bounding each term by d is elementary order reasoning once that theorem is cited. Thus empty fibres have dimension at most d and do not require a purity inference at an absent point.
