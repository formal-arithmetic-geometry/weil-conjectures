### Argument retained for lem-a1-local-trivialization-residue-rank

Original step 2: Fix $i$ and choose a principal trivializing open $D(f)$ containing $\mathfrak q_i$, so $f\notin\mathfrak q_i$ and $P_f\cong E_f^{\oplus r}$ by the hypothesis. Here $\mathfrak q_i$ is a point of $\operatorname{Spec}E$ [def-weil-affine-spectrum]. Write $S_i=E\setminus\mathfrak q_i$. Localizing that isomorphism further gives $P_{\mathfrak q_i}\cong E_{\mathfrak q_i}^{\oplus r}$: the identification $S_i^{-1}(P_f)\to S_i^{-1}P$ is $(p/f^a)/u\mapsto p/(f^a u)$, with inverse $p/u\mapsto(p/1)/u$. These are inverse because $f\in S_i$ is invertible after localization. The same formulas for scalars identify $S_i^{-1}(E_f)$ with $E_{\mathfrak q_i}$, and localization of $E_f^{\oplus r}$ is coordinatewise. In particular $P_{\mathfrak q_i}$ is generated by $r$ elements. These are direct fraction computations using [def-weil-pbc-module-localization-away], [def-weil-pbc-module-localization], and [def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime].

Original step 3: The residue map identifies $P_{\mathfrak q_i}/\mathfrak q_iP_{\mathfrak q_i}$ with $F_i$: on fractions it is $[p/u]\mapsto\bar u^{-1}\pi_i(p)$; its inverse is $\pi_i(p)\mapsto[p/1]$. For well-definedness, equality of fractions is killed by some element outside $\mathfrak q_i$, whose image in $k_i$ is invertible. A summand with numerator in $\mathfrak q_iP$ maps to zero; conversely, if $\pi_i(p)=0$ then $p\in\mathfrak q_iP$, so $p/u\in\mathfrak q_iP_{\mathfrak q_i}$. The analogous formula identifies $E_{\mathfrak q_i}/\mathfrak q_iE_{\mathfrak q_i}$ with $k_i$. Reducing the coordinate isomorphism of step 2 now gives $F_i\cong k_i^{\oplus r}$: quotienting a finite tuple is coordinatewise, with kernel the tuples in $\mathfrak q_iE_{\mathfrak q_i}$. Hence $\dim_{k_i}F_i=r$ [def-weil-vector-space-dimension].


### Argument retained for lem-a1-simultaneous-residue-bases

Original step 1: Put $k=D/\mathfrak m$, and enumerate the finitely many maximal ideals of $E$ as $\mathfrak q_1,\ldots,\mathfrak q_s$; $s=0$ is allowed. For each $i$ put $k_i=E/\mathfrak q_i$ and $F_i=P/\mathfrak q_iP$, with quotient map $\pi_i:P\to F_i$. Since $\mathfrak mE\subseteq\mathfrak q_i$, the map $D\to k_i$ factors through a unital map $k\to k_i$. It is injective: its kernel is an ideal of the field $k$ and cannot contain $1$. Thus $F_i$ is also a $k$-vector space, and $\pi_i(dn)=\bar d\pi_i(n)$ for $d\in D$ and $n\in N$.

Original step 4: Choose a coordinate basis of each $F_i\cong k_i^{\oplus r}$. Lift each of its finitely many vectors to $P$ using the quotient map. As $N$ spans $P$ over $E$, each lift is a finite $E$-linear combination of elements of $N$. Collect all elements appearing in these expressions into one finite list $x_1,\ldots,x_L\in N$. Then for every $i$, the elements $\pi_i(x_\ell)$ span $F_i$ over $k_i$: reduction of the chosen expressions already spans every coordinate basis vector. If $s=0$, take $L=0$. Set $V=k^{\oplus L}$, a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space, and define $T_i:V\to F_i$ by $T_i(c)=\sum_{\ell=1}^L c_\ell\pi_i(x_\ell)$, using $k\hookrightarrow k_i$. Each $T_i$ is $k$-linear, and the $k_i$-linear span of $T_i(V)$ is $F_i$ because $T_i(e_\ell)=\pi_i(x_\ell)$.

Original step 5: Construct $n_1,\ldots,n_r\in N$ by induction, maintaining that $\pi_i(n_1),\ldots,\pi_i(n_t)$ are linearly independent over $k_i$ for every $i$. The empty family gives $t=0$. If $t<r$, let $W_{i,t}$ be their $k_i$-linear span in $F_i$; it is proper since its dimension is $t<r$ [def-weil-vector-space-dimension]. Its inverse image $H_{i,t}=T_i^{-1}(W_{i,t})$ is a $k$-linear subspace of $V$. It is proper: if it were all of $V$, the $k_i$-span of $T_i(V)$ would lie in $W_{i,t}$, contradicting step 4. Apply [lem-pc-finite-linear-avoidance] to the finite family of proper subspaces $H_{i,t}$ in the finite-dimensional space $V$ over the infinite field $k$. Choose $c\in V$ outside their union. Lift each $c_\ell$ to $d_\ell\in D$ and set $n_{t+1}=\sum_\ell d_\ell x_\ell\in N$. Step 1 gives $\pi_i(n_{t+1})=T_i(c)\notin W_{i,t}$. A linear relation with nonzero coefficient of this last vector would put it in $W_{i,t}$; a relation with last coefficient zero vanishes by induction. Thus independence persists. At $t=r$ the $r$ independent vectors form a basis of each $r$-dimensional $F_i$. When $s=0$, the union to avoid is empty and $c=0$ works at each stage.


### Argument retained for lem-a1-basis-of-maximal-residue-bases

Original step 6: Let $\varphi:E^{\oplus r}\to P$ send $(a_j)$ to $\sum_ja_jn_j$. At $\mathfrak q_i$, coordinatewise localization identifies its source with $E_{\mathfrak q_i}^{\oplus r}$ and its formula with $(a_j/u_j)\mapsto\sum_j(a_j/u_j)(n_j/1)$ [def-weil-pbc-module-localization]. By [def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime], $E_{\mathfrak q_i}$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak q_iE_{\mathfrak q_i}$. Its module $P_{\mathfrak q_i}$ is finitely generated by step 2. By steps 3 and 5, the images of $n_1/1,\ldots,n_r/1$ form a basis of its residue module. Therefore [lem-weil-pbc-nakayama-local-generators] implies that they generate $P_{\mathfrak q_i}$, so $\varphi_{\mathfrak q_i}$ is surjective.

Original step 7: Choose the isomorphism $\psi_i:P_{\mathfrak q_i}\xrightarrow{\sim}E_{\mathfrak q_i}^{\oplus r}$ obtained in step 2. Then $\psi_i\varphi_{\mathfrak q_i}$ is a surjective endomorphism of the finitely generated $E_{\mathfrak q_i}$-module $E_{\mathfrak q_i}^{\oplus r}$, which is generated by the standard coordinate vectors. By [lem-weil-pbc-finite-module-surjective-endomorphism-bijective] it is bijective. Composing with $\psi_i^{-1}$ shows that $\varphi_{\mathfrak q_i}$ is bijective. This verifies injectivity as well as the generation supplied by Nakayama.

Original step 8: Every maximal ideal of $E$ occurs in the list. Hence [lem-weil-pbc-linear-map-bijective-at-maximal-localizations], applied to $\varphi:E^{\oplus r}\to P$, gives that $\varphi$ is bijective. Its inverse is $E$-linear by uniqueness of preimages, so it is an isomorphism. Equivalently, every element of $P$ is uniquely an $E$-linear combination of $n_1,\ldots,n_r$, which is exactly the required basis, and each $n_j$ belongs to $N$ by construction. The local criterion includes the empty maximal spectrum; in particular, for the zero ring $E$, the constructed zero vectors give the isomorphism $E^{\oplus r}\cong P=0$.

The abstract second node takes Q=N. Finite target bases have preimages in Q up to finite spanning combinations: choose a finite list of elements of Q whose images span each target over K_i. This is precisely old step 4 with x_l in N, and the induction uses the same fixed k^L, inverse-image subspaces, coefficient lifts and independence argument as old step 5. No finite generation of Q over D, no surjectivity of rho_i as a D-linear map, and no finite field-extension degree are assumed. In the third node, the canonical residue identification is the same fraction formula as old step 3, valid for any module and maximal ideal; it transports the hypothesis on P/qP to the residue of P_q. The local free hypothesis gives finite generation without assuming P globally finite. The three existing lifting/locality facts are used exactly as in old steps 6–8.
