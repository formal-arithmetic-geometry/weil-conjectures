## Argument for the new unsplit node

This paragraph is guidance for its next worker, not a proof embedded in its statement-only node. Read Milne Proposition 21.75, printed p.448, together with 21.68 on positive root coordinates, pp.445--446, and 21.24 on opposite-root SL2 maps, p.432. Its standing hypotheses are split reductive, without semisimplicity or simply connectedness. For a simple root alpha, reduced-root sign expansions show that s_alpha permutes the other positive roots. Use the existing root-sign-word-deletion node to deduce that length increases precisely when v^{-1}(alpha) is positive: if it is negative, deletion shortens a shortest word for v^{-1}; if positive, apply that implication to s_alpha v. The elementary bound on word lengths gives the two cases. This argument only needs v in the subgroup generated by the simple reflections, so the new statement does not presuppose the item's generation clause.

Choose a rank-one representative n for s_alpha and use B's positive-root product coordinates to calculate D_s D_v = B(F) U_{-alpha}(F) n n_v B(F). When v^{-1}(alpha) is positive, conjugating U_{-alpha} by (n n_v)^{-1} gives U_{v^{-1}(alpha)}, contained in B. This yields D_s D_v = D_{sv}. The already split narrower product node contains a detailed calculation with these same inputs; its extra semisimple/simply-connected assumptions are not used in those calculations, but its statement does not permit direct application here. The new node has no proof and will receive its own independent split.
