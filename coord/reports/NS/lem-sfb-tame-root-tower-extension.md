### Old step 1

Apply the residual-chart lemma to $(Y,D,I)$ and retain one member $V,U,S_U,T,T^\circ$. It supplies affine charts, the pro-etale cover of the entire stratum, compatible primitive roots, and the actual power-transition tower. Cited statements: lem-sfb-snc-universal-residual-charts.

### Old step 2

Let $A=\mathcal O_E$ and choose a uniformizer $\varpi$. The finite-type Noetherian lemma applies to $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ (a field is Noetherian since its only ideals are zero and the unit ideal); its open-subscheme version applies to $W$. Regularity makes $W$ normal; separation is inherited from $Y$. Identify internal modules with section-ring module sheaves and pulled-back $C(-,E)$ with continuous-function coefficients. The integral reduction equivalence identifies the coefficient rings with $B_W=\varprojlim_n(A/\varpi^n)_W$ and $B_W[1/\varpi]$. The normal completed integral-model theorem gives a lisse integral model $N$ with $N[1/\varpi]\cong L$. Thus $F_n=N/\varpi^nN$ is a normalized tower of finite locally free discrete-coefficient sheaves. Restrict $N,F_n$ to the open part of any chart.

Cited statements: lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian, lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-open-subscheme-noetherian, rem-pc-regular-normal, def-weil-pbc-internal-abelian-sheaf-module-equivalence, lem-weil-pbc-condensed-field-continuous-realization, lem-sfb-integral-lisse-reduction-equivalence, lem-a1-normal-completed-integral-model.

### Old step 3

At a geometric boundary point pull $N$ to its punctured strict-local full root tower $Z$. This $Z$ is connected and qcqs by the chart lemma's strict-local assertion. Tameness makes its rationalization constant, say $E_Z^d$. The lattice-class sheaf lemma identifies the class of $(N|_Z,E_Z\otimes N|_Z\cong E_Z^d)$ with a section of the constant discrete sheaf $\operatorname{GL}_d(E)/\operatorname{GL}_d(A)$. The continuous-function realization for a discrete space identifies that section with a continuous map from $|Z|$ into this discrete set. Connectedness makes it constant, with value the coset of a matrix $h$. By the lemma's embedded-image normalization the integral module is $hA_Z^d$, isomorphic to $A_Z^d$. Exact pullback and the integral coefficient reduction formula identify the pulled-back $F_n$ with $(A/\varpi^n)_Z^d$. Every $F_n$ therefore satisfies the finite tame hypothesis, also where fewer boundary branches vanish. Integral tameness has been deduced from rational tameness.

Cited statements: lem-sfb-snc-universal-residual-charts, lem-weil-pbc-lattice-sheaf-locally-constant, lem-sfb-totally-disconnected-coefficient-realization, lem-sfb-integral-lisse-reduction-equivalence, sk-prop-sfb-cartesian-coefficients.

### Old step 4

Apply finite tame-fibre classification to the restrictions of all $F_n$ on this chart. The same $U$ works for every $n$: it was formed from all finite residual covers. This gives finite free $A/\varpi^n$-modules $Q_n$ with continuous $G_I$-action, compatible quotient transitions, and natural identifications $F_n|_{T^\circ}\cong\underline{Q_n}$. Its product and coefficient compatibilities give the linear operations and reduction isomorphisms. The descent action is exactly the action on $Q_n$, not an action selected independently at the boundary. The finite-local-system assertion here is on the pro-etale site, as stated in that lemma.

Cited statements: lem-sfb-snc-tame-finite-fibre-classification.

### Old step 5

Set $Q=\varprojlim_nQ_n$ and $M=E\otimes_AQ$. Adic finite-module reconstruction gives $Q/\varpi^nQ\cong Q_n$, finite generation, completeness and the inverse-limit topology. It is finite free: choose a basis of $Q_1$ and lift it successively through $Q_{n+1}\to Q_n$. Each lifted basis is a basis over $A/\varpi^{n+1}$, since its change-of-basis determinant is a unit modulo $\varpi$. These choices give compatible $Q_n\cong(A/\varpi^n)^d$, hence $Q\cong A^d$. Define $g(q_n)_n=(gq_n)_n$. This action is jointly continuous because each composite $G_I\times Q\to Q_n$ is continuous. In a basis its matrix entries are inverse limits of continuous finite-valued entries; inverse matrices come from $g^{-1}$. Extension of scalars gives a continuous homomorphism $\rho:G_I\to\operatorname{GL}_E(M)$. No finite-image claim is needed.

Cited statements: lem-pod-adic-limit-finite-modules.

### Old step 6

Identify the actual torsor. At order $n$, root rotations send $x_i$ to $\zeta_n^{a_i}x_i$. On the punctured locus every $x_i$ is a unit. For two root tuples over the same base the ratios $y_i/x_i$ have $n$th power one. The factorization $z^n-1=\prod_{a\bmod n}(z-\zeta_n^a)$ has pairwise comaximal factors, since distinct-root differences are units. Its Chinese-remainder idempotents decompose the base uniquely into pieces on which each ratio is one $\zeta_n^{a_i}$. This gives the scheme isomorphism $T_n^\circ[(\mathbf Z/n)^I]\cong T_n^\circ\times_{U^\circ}T_n^\circ$, $(x,a)\mapsto(x,\zeta_n^ax)$. Power transitions send $a$ to its residue and preserve this formula. Taking affine inverse limits gives $T^\circ[G_I]\cong T^\circ\times_{U^\circ}T^\circ$. Here $\varprojlim_{(n,p)=1}(\mathbf Z/n)^I=(\prod_{q\ne p}\mathbf Z_q)^I$ by compatible Chinese-remainder decomposition into prime powers. The covering assertion was supplied by the chart lemma.

Cited statements: lem-sfb-snc-universal-residual-charts, def-sfb-profinite-test-objects.

### Old step 7

The composite $T^\circ\to W$ is a pro-etale object. The coefficient-section localization lemma identifies its actual inverse image with slice restriction. Restriction to a slice preserves all limits, since limits of sheaves are computed objectwise. Apply it to $N\cong\varprojlim_nF_n$. The natural finite-level identifications in step 4 and compatible bases in step 5 identify this restricted limit with $\varprojlim_n(A/\varpi^n)_{T^\circ}^d\cong A_{T^\circ}^d$, by the coefficient reduction equivalence and its actual projection maps. Inverting $\varpi$ gives $L|_{T^\circ}\cong p_{T^\circ}^*C(-,M)$, since $C(P,M)=C(P,E)^d$ in the chosen basis. Every projection identifies deck descent with $\rho_n$, so their limit identifies it with $\rho$ on $M$. Equality of actions is tested by the limit projections before localization.

Cited statements: lem-sfb-coefficient-sections-localization, lem-sfb-integral-lisse-reduction-equivalence, lem-sfb-snc-tame-finite-fibre-classification, sk-def-sfb-pulled-back-coefficients.

### Old step 8

Define on all of $T$ the module $\widetilde L=p_T^*C(-,M)$. In the chosen basis it is $C_{E,T}^d$, hence finite-rank lisse. Give it the continuous equivariant structure induced by $G_I\times M\to M$ and root rotations on $T$. The cocycle identity is $\rho(gh)=\rho(g)\rho(h)$. Step 7 identifies its restriction, with this action, with the actual deck-equivariant pullback of $L$. Define $i:S_U\to T$ by $H_\infty\to H/JH$ taking every root variable to zero: all defining and transition equations map to zero. Root rotations fix $i$. Composition of pullback from the point gives $i^*\widetilde L\cong p_{S_U}^*C(-,M)$ with exactly the same representation $\rho$. This constructs the extension and its specified boundary action.

Cited statements: sk-def-sfb-pulled-back-coefficients, def-sfb-lisse-coefficient-module.

Pointed refinement maps of the charts and finite-fibre classification give maps of rings, finite fibres and actions commuting with every quotient projection. Cited statements: lem-sfb-snc-universal-residual-charts, lem-sfb-snc-tame-finite-fibre-classification, sk-def-sfb-pulled-back-coefficients.
