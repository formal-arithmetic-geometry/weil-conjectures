Extracted from former step 2, for lem-cbc-constant-ring-module-sheaves:

Apply [lem-cbc-local-dense-ordinary-sheaves] with $\mathcal A=\operatorname{Mod}_{\Lambda}$. This category has all small limits, so the required comma limits exist. Denote its restriction equivalence by $L$ and its right adjoint by $T$; the underlying presheaf of $TN$ is $\operatorname{Ran}_{p^{\mathrm{op}}}N$. Sheaves of fixed-$\Lambda$ modules are modules over the constant sheaf of rings $\underline\Lambda$ [def-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf]. To check this identification, for an abelian sheaf $N$ form the ring presheaf $\mathcal E_N(C)=\operatorname{End}(N|_{\mathcal C/C})$, with addition and composition and restriction by further localization. It is a sheaf: endomorphisms agreeing on a covering sieve act compatibly on the restrictions of each section over each object of the slice; the sheaf axiom glues their images uniquely, and uniqueness verifies naturality and additivity. A fixed-$\Lambda$ action is a ring map from the constant presheaf $\Lambda$ to $\mathcal E_N$. By the sheafification universal property in rings it extends uniquely to $\underline\Lambda\to\mathcal E_N$, giving the required module-sheaf action by evaluation. Restricting this action to the constant presheaf is inverse; for a fixed-$\Lambda$-linear map $\phi:N\to N'$, use the ring subsheaf of $\mathcal E_N\times\mathcal E_{N'}$ consisting of pairs $(s,t)$ with $\phi s=t\phi$ on the slice. The constant scalar map lands in this subsheaf; its unique extension has the already constructed actions as projections, so $\phi$ is $\underline\Lambda$-linear. Thus the objects and morphisms of the two module-sheaf categories are identified. Hence these are exactly the module-sheaf categories in [lem-cbc-bounded-below-hypersheaf-model]. The functor $L$ is additive since maps add sectionwise; $T$ is additive by its limit formula. By [lem-weil-pbc-equivalence-injective-resolutions], they are exact, preserve injectives, and preserve kernels and cokernels. Here is the mapping-complex calculation needed for that model. For an abelian category $\mathcal A$ of modules or module presheaves, a complex $A$ and a K-injective $J$, the canonical map from $\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(A,J)$ to the derived mapping complex is a quasi-isomorphism. Indeed its degree-$n$ cohomology map is exactly
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{K(\mathcal A)}(A,J[n])\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathcal A)}(QA,Q(J[n])).$$
The identification on the left sends a degree-$n$ cocycle to the chain map into $J[n]$; boundaries are precisely the null-homotopic maps, by the Hom differential $dh=d_Jh-(-1)^n h d_A$. For the identification on the right, use the stable dg enhancement of step 5: its mapping complex into $J[n]$ is its mapping complex into $J$ shifted by $n$, and $H^0$ is Hom in its homotopy category. That homotopy category is the ordinary localization $D(\mathcal A)$ [def-weil-abstract-derived-category], since both localizations invert the same quasi-isomorphisms. Thus this uses the stable presentation, not an enhanced assertion in the ordinary derived-category definition. The shift $J[n]$ is K-injective, because shifting $\operatorname{Hom}^{\bullet}(B,J)$ preserves acyclicity for acyclic $B$. Therefore every displayed map is bijective by [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-hom-derived]. This proves the quasi-isomorphism in every degree, rather than promoting only a degree-zero bijection to an enhanced assertion. The comparison is the canonical map supplied by localization, so it is natural as a dg bifunctor and respects composition. Quasi-isomorphisms between K-injectives are homotopy equivalences by [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-hom-derived], hence induce homotopy equivalences on Hom complexes; source quasi-isomorphisms are also inverted by the just-proved comparison. The opposite and multivariable clauses of [lem-coef-enhanced-localization-universal] consequently retain this comparison and its composition diagrams on the localized domains, including coherent transformations.
