## Review by NS

I read Milne, Abelian Varieties, III §§6 and 8 in the local PDF: Theorem 6.6 and its Lemmas 6.7–6.9, Summary 6.11, and Theorem 8.1 with the relative Picard discussion, printed pp.105–111. I checked the sign in the pullback identity: the existing node uses the inversion image Theta-minus and f cross inversion. In the assembly this gives r composed with phi_{L-minus} composed with inversion equal to the identity, rather than omitting the inversion. Biadditivity then identifies phi_{L-minus} with phi_L. I also read Milne I §8, especially Proposition 8.1/Remark 8.2 and Proposition 8.14. The effectivity hypothesis in the ample criterion is available from the Abel-image divisor theorem. Milne explicitly omits the converse proof of Proposition 8.1; the existing calculus already exposes its supporting nodes, and I do not claim to have completed that proof here.

For the new relative-theta node I checked Milne III §8 for the relative Picard setting and read Stacks perfect.tex, Tags 0FJX and 0FJY (the two-term determinant and pullback construction), and the proper-flat perfect-image setting at Tag 0B91. These supply the construction's ingredients, not a claim that Stacks itself states the full relative-theta theorem. The mathematical reason descent is plausible is precise: the universal degree-g-minus-one object has Euler characteristic zero, so scalar automorphisms act trivially on its determinant; twisting by a line from the parameter changes the determinant by its zeroth power. Locally the theta section is det(E^0 → E^1) and its line is det E^1 tensor (det E^0)^{-1}; this sign is the inverse of the usual determinant of Rf_* and is the effective theta sign. On every geometric fibre it is the classical theta Cartier divisor. Flatness of the Picard torsor and the fibrewise non-zero-divisor criterion give relative Cartier flatness, making arbitrary, including nonflat, pullback legitimate. The detailed global descent and multiplicity identification remain obligations of the new unsplit node, whose statement records them explicitly. The statement does not silently assume a universal module globally on the torsor: the formula is on charts which carry a representative. I checked that degree-g-minus-one line modules locally exist by choosing a local section of the smooth surjective curve. The torsor action and changes of such sections are the tensor action, with exactly the shift (g-1)(p_j-p_i).

For Step 1 I opened the complete statements of lem-sfb-nodal-picard-etale-schematic, thm-pd-jacobian-general-field, and lem-sfb-connected-proper-fibres-proper. They respectively provide the separated smooth finitely presented group, the abelian geometric fibres, and properness. Smooth stable fibres are nonempty connected reduced nodal fibres; on these smooth geometric fibres there is one component, so multidegree zero equals degree zero. The field curve is projective by the stable-family convention. There is no invocation of stable-relative-picard-kummer or the parent's canonical line theorem, which would create a cycle.
