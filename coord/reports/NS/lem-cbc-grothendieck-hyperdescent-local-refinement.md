## STATEMENT-DISPUTED

The last sentence, “This equivalence is natural under restriction to subposets”, cannot mean arbitrary subposets. Here is an explicit counterexample, independent of coefficient or enhancement conventions. Let I be the diamond with elements 0, 1, 2, 3 and 0 < 1 < 3 and 0 < 2 < 3. Give every fibre the terminal one-object category with the trivial topology, and make every transition the identity. All full-sieve and restricted-topology hypotheses hold, and the lower-refinement condition holds by choosing the label 0 and the identity cover. The total category is I itself. Its topology is generated by the singleton arrows, and every nonempty covering sieve of an object contains the arrow from 0. Thus the site is presented by the one-object subsite {0}; the constant presheaf Z[0] is a hypersheaf. More directly, projecting any hypercover to the terminal fibre gives a simplicial set with all boundary-lifting properties, hence a contractible augmented simplicial set, on which a constant derived coefficient has descent.

Now restrict to J = {1, 2, 3}. In the total category J, pull the generating singleton cover {1 -> 3} back along 2 -> 3. There is no object of J below both 1 and 2, so the pullback sieve on 2 is empty. Consequently the empty family covers 2, and a hypersheaf must have value zero there (the limit of the empty coefficient diagram is zero). The restricted constant presheaf has value Z[0] at 2. It is therefore not a hypersheaf, even though its fibre presheaves are hypersheaves and its transition maps are equivalences. This also shows why it is invalid to claim that an arbitrary subposet inclusion preserves the relevant total hypersheaves.

Correction: naturality is asserted only for subposets whose restricted diagram satisfies the same local lower-refinement condition. Lower-closed subposets satisfy that condition automatically, since the refinement labels below a retained index are retained. The last proof step proves this qualified naturality. The parent `lem-cbc-grothendieck-hypersheaf-sections` also says arbitrary subposets; this report flags that same clause for maintenance, without editing the parent's file.

There is a second statement-interface omission. The parent explicitly says: “The topology on C_i is the restriction of the topology of C_j; in particular covering sieves of objects of C_i are the same in the two sites.” The assigned child did not repeat this. An inclusion of underlying full sieves alone does not imply this assertion. For example, take both categories to be the terminal category and their inclusion to be the identity; give one the trivial topology and the other the topology in which the empty sieve covers. The inclusion is a full-sieve inclusion, but the empty sieve covers in exactly one site. The inference “a covering sieve on an object of C_i in C_j covers in C_i” fails for this example. This is an omission of the intended site-interface hypothesis, not a claim that the child theorem without it has been disproved. I restored precisely the parent's convention, rather than silently relying on it. This correction does not alter any coefficient category or target statement and is directly compatible with the parent's use of this child.
