For `lem-sfb-polarization-calculus-over-field`, the normalized Poincare datum gives phi_N by the translated-difference family and its group law. Tensoring those families proves additivity, and pulling them back proves phi_(b* N) = b^vee phi_N b. These are identities of families and hence morphisms, not just identities on rational points. An ample L induces an isogeny, giving a rational two-sided inverse; biduality gives the Rosati anti-involution. Hom is torsion-free (Milne I Lemma 10.6), so vanishing in rational Hom implies actual phi_N = 0. Proposition 8.4 and Remark 8.5 then give inversion-pullback N = N inverse, up to the harmless identity-section constant line. Symmetry therefore gives N squared trivial. This is over k: use rigidifications along the k-rational identity, or first verify geometric triviality and descend using the identity rigidification. No k-rational square root or algebraic-closure hypothesis is required. In particular, one must not claim that a symmetric kernel line itself is trivial.

For `lem-sfb-ample-polarization-positive-spectrum`, one can verify the assertion after extension to an algebraic closure, while retaining the k-rational endomorphism u. Base extension is faithful on morphisms, and on rational Hom. The polarization maps are self-dual (Milne I Proposition 14.2), giving u dagger = u. Finite-dimensionality of End^0 and the positive Rosati trace form make left multiplication by u a self-adjoint real linear operator. Consequently its minimal polynomial has distinct real roots, and Q[u] tensored with R is a product of copies of R. To check their signs, write the polynomial

    F(t) = chi(A, M tensor L^t).

By abelian Riemann–Roch this polynomial has the mixed intersection coefficients of the two ample lines; all are strictly positive. The degree/characteristic-polynomial identities give

    det(t + u on V_l A) = F(t)^2 / deg(phi_L).

Check the identity on nonnegative integral t using phi_(M tensor L^t) = phi_M + t phi_L, and then as a polynomial identity. Every root of the minimum polynomial of u occurs in its characteristic polynomial, by the faithful Tate realization (I Theorem 10.15 and Corollary 10.24). If a real eigenvalue of u were negative, this determinant would vanish at some positive real t, whereas F(t) is strictly positive there. Zero is excluded since phi_M is an isogeny. This proves strict positivity. The mixed-intersection positivity, Riemann–Roch and the spectral argument belong to this new unsplit lemma, not to an allegedly elementary step of the item. In dimension zero End^0 is the zero algebra and the asserted product has zero factors. This separates positivity of u from mere positivity of a Rosati form: the latter by itself would not distinguish a polarization from its negative.

For `lem-sfb-split-real-algebra-positive-squares`, here is a direct argument over Q that avoids assuming a real square root lies in C. Let V be the rational vector space underlying C and let mu: Sym^2(V) -> C be multiplication. This rational linear map is surjective, since every c is a product 1*c. The primitive idempotent basis of C_R = R^d gives a positive definite real Gram matrix with image x: its diagonal entries are the strictly positive coordinates of x. The affine linear system mu(G) = x is defined over Q; row reduction gives a rational particular solution and a rational basis of its kernel. Thus its rational points are dense in its real points. Positive definiteness is open, so choose a rational positive definite Gram matrix in that same fibre. Rational elimination diagonalizes it by congruence with strictly positive rational diagonal coefficients. Each such coefficient a/b, for positive integers a,b, is the sum of a*b copies of (1/b)^2. Substitution into the diagonal expression writes x as a finite sum of squares of elements of C. The zero-dimensional case is x=0 in the zero ring and uses one zero square. This is a genuine algebraic lemma with a rational Gram-matrix argument, not the definition of IsSumSq or a statement that only gives a real sum of squares.
