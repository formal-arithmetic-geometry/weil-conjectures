Step 1 uses the statement of lem-constructible-constant-exact, which really does cover exact sequences of finite left modules over a finite ring. I take that ring to be Z/m, so all three cyclic groups have the required scalar action. The scalar-forgetting node says it is exact, and the long-exact-ladder node explicitly supplies the long exact cohomology sequence for abelian sheaves. The cyclic sequence is injective on the left because multiplication by p maps Z/(m/p) into Z/m with zero kernel; its quotient is Z/p. This is strong induction, not a circular assumption of the desired composite case.

Step 2 uses the exact Noetherianity, locality/residue-field, henselianity and dimension statements of the four completion nodes. The local completion node identifies the maximal ideal with the extended maximal ideal, so the complete-pair node gives henselianity for this maximal ideal. Same separably closed residue field makes it strictly henselian. The two definition nodes say quasi-excellence includes the G-ring condition and that at each prime its local-ring completion map is regular. At the maximal ideal the localized ring is R, exactly the map needed here; arbitrary completions of arbitrary local rings are not claimed regular.

Step 3 uses the full statement of lem-per-regular-etale-direct-image-base-change: prime-power constant coefficients, Noetherian regular base map, any qcqs upper morphism, and every bounded-below object. The inverse-image node identifies the constant ring sheaf and the underlying abelian pullback. The total-cohomology-exchange node explicitly identifies homology of this same exchange at the closed point with canonical total-space cohomology pullback. Thus the step does not mistake a closed fibre for the entire affine open.
