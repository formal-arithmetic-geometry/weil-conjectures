I read the item and all six steps against Bhatt–Scholze Lemma 4.2.12 and Proposition 6.8.4(1), keeping in mind that the latter says “Easy and left to the reader.” Lemma 4.2.12 is stated for every scheme; the qcqs qualification applies only to its extra connected-component formula. Our proof does not use that formula. Lemma 6.8.2(1) applies to a finite extension with its chosen uniformizer, exactly the coefficient datum here. Definition 6.8.3 permits locally finite free integral sheaves. There is no need for a rational lattice-existence assertion or for full faithfulness of rationalization on qcqs schemes. The conclusion is ordinary extension of scalars along the specified inclusion.

For step 1 I opened lem-sfb-totally-disconnected-coefficient-realization. Its statement explicitly identifies the pulled-back condensed object with C(|U|,D), naturally in continuous maps and compatibly with finite products. Applied to the operations of the three rings, this compares the actual coefficient actions and quotient maps. A morphism of free module sheaves is then described directly by the images of finitely many standard global sections: every section is the sum of its coordinates times those sections. Applying the same calculation to its inverse shows pointwise invertibility. Thus the global discrepancy is a locally constant GL_d(A_n)-valued function without assuming X connected. This is a finite-basis calculation, not an unproved claim that arbitrary sheaf morphisms come from constant scalar matrices. It also explains why disconnected X causes no obstruction.

For step 2 I opened lem-sfb-integral-square-zero-matrix-lift. It assumes an arbitrary unital ring and a two-sided square-zero ideal and concludes any matrix whose quotient is invertible is invertible. Here R=A/varpi^(n+1), I=varpi^n/varpi^(n+1), and n>=1 ensures 2n>=n+1. Entrywise lifts exist by the quotient definition. Choice gives a section of the surjection of finite discrete GL groups; no homomorphism property is needed. Composing that section with a global locally constant matrix gives a global lift. In particular I did not infer surjectivity on global sections merely from an epimorphism of sheaves. The square-zero inversion node is the sole non-elementary input to this step.

Step 3 is the explicit inductive argument. A chosen c_(n+1) reduces to an isomorphism on the actual quotient F_n, because these are the scalar quotients and actual transitions stipulated in the item. With u_n=b_n times inverse(bar c_(n+1)), the order b_(n+1)=lift(u_n) times c_(n+1) gives exactly b_n after reduction. The base is the user's prescribed b_1. The recursively corrected isomorphisms commute with every transition by composition. This step proves the clause rather than renaming it as a new lemma.

For step 4 I opened lem-sfb-integral-lisse-module-complete and lem-sfb-integral-lisse-coefficient-limit, and also their definition interface def-per-integral-lisse-reduction-data. The first states precisely that the canonical scalar-cokernel cone of any locally finite free integral module on any scheme is limiting, including rank zero. The second states that the cone of the continuous integral coefficient ring over its positive finite quotients, with pointwise quotient legs, is limiting. The definition interface asserts no theorem and is not being used as a substitute for either existence assertion. Step 1 supplies the comparison of these continuous rings with the pulled-back rings in this item's statement.
