## Review by Q137

I reconstructed the source's argument without using the withdrawn historical graph. ILO XX 4.4 is the source for both this item and its one new input; XX 2.4.3 and Stacks 05GG explain the non-Noetherian quotient calculation. I opened each cited node and copied the statement, rather than quoting its proof, into citations.json. The essential distinctions I checked are below.

In Step 1, lem-weil-pbc-fg-ideal-radical-power applies because both ideals are finitely generated; its conclusion gives a positive power inclusion. For J and K, the product has the same radical, and the new criterion with n=1 applies to the pair (J,JK) and then (K,JK). Identity and composition supply the preorder laws, so the new node does not merely restate filteredness. The same finite-generation hypotheses hold after extension.

In Step 2, lemma-nagata-admissible-blowup-over-open supplies exactly the specified identification over U. Its hypothesis follows because J is the unit ideal at each point outside V(J), and I is finitely generated so U is quasi-compact. The separate principalization statement supplies locally principal equations that are non-zero-divisors, which proves schematic density by injectivity of localization. I checked that this argument does not assume U was dense before blowing up. This corrects the potential C4 misuse of the preserved-open lemma.

In Step 3, the completion-mod-power node states surjectivity, the exact kernel and compatibility with the original quotient map for every positive N; these are exactly the facts used to identify the quotient rings. Ideal correspondence, finite lifting of generators modulo I^N, and extension/contraction are written out. The argument first treats all finitely generated open ideals, not just full-support ones, because the factor ideal in Step 4 need not have full support. Full support is then detected by the two power bounds. The contraction of J'^s contained in IA' is tested modulo I, so it uses no flatness or injectivity of A into A'.

In Step 4, the new criterion applies only to J,K (and their extensions), whose radicals are equal. Its factor L' is finitely generated, as required for descent in Step 3. From (KA')^n = (JA')L' one gets (KA')^n contained in L', so L' contains I^{rn}A'. This is the missing hypothesis that makes its descent legitimate. The descended product JL and K^n both contain powers of I, and equality is reflected in a single sufficiently deep quotient, giving the converse domination. Thus the correspondence is an isomorphism of preorders, stronger than mere cofinality.

In Step 5, the radical-power leaf supplies I^r contained in J. The completion-mod-power statement applies to both quotient ideals because I^{m+rn} is contained in I^m J^n and I^{rn} is contained in J^n. The latter quotient is zero for n=0, handled explicitly. The kernels of the displayed quotient arrows are the desired modules. An isomorphism of the two ambient quotient rings and the two target quotient rings therefore induces the kernel isomorphism, with no exactness claim about completion itself.

In Step 6, I checked products and m-transitions on actual representatives, so compatibility is not left to an assertion of transport. def-nagata-affine-blowup defines the blowup as Proj of the Rees algebra. lemma-nagata-relative-proj-base-change-arbitrary states the canonical Proj base-change isomorphism for arbitrary morphisms, so it can be used for the non-flat quotient map. Its coefficientwise pullback is precisely the direct sum of J^n/I J^n. The graded isomorphism from Step 5 therefore gives the asserted closed-fibre isomorphism. The canonical map, not an arbitrary abstract ring isomorphism, is used throughout.

For the new statement I checked unit ideals (identity blowups; L=R works), zero/nilpotent ideals (empty Proj; a large power is zero and L=0 works), and ordinary principal ideals with the same radical (e.g. (t) and (t^2)). For the item, I=0 gives A'=A and nilpotent full-support ideals, so the blowups are empty and the assertions remain valid. Nilpotent I likewise makes completion A, and the zero ring causes no exception. For nonempty indices n=0 gives exactly A/I^m on both sides. The proof does not use regularity of the completion map, excellence, Noetherianity or finite generation of arbitrary subideals. Henselianity is retained in the item but is not needed for these algebraic comparisons.
