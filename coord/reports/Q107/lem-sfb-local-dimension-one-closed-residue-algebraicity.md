For step 1 I opened lem-weil-pbc-prime-contains-minimal-prime and checked its actual statement supplies Q contained in P by taking I=0; it does not require reducedness, Noetherianity, or finiteness of the minimal-prime set. Only this choice is used. I also opened the two definition nodes cited there. Noetherianity of B/q is checked directly: lift an ideal to B, choose its finite generators, and project them. Finite type of C/Q over B/q follows by projecting the given algebra generators; the definition node explicitly gives this generator formulation. Injectivity follows because q is the contraction of Q. The quotients are domains because q and Q are prime. The quotient identities preserve the actual field maps: D/p=C/P and R/n=B/m. Thus the residue extension used at the end is the canonical one in the item, not just an abstract pair of isomorphic fields. Primality, locality of the quotient, and ideal correspondence are checked directly by representatives and inverse images. These are short quotient constructions, not concealed geometric comparison results.

For step 2 I checked the vertical case against 0AB6. When q=m, the image of B/m is a field inside D and the assumed generic field extension is algebraic. The written polynomial calculation removes an initial power of T and solves explicitly for the inverse of each nonzero d. Consequently D is already a field and p=0. This proves the vertical case rather than citing another node that restates it. The cited transcendence-degree definition says that absence of independent singletons gives degree zero. No finite-field, algebraically closed, normal, or reduced-base assumption was introduced.

For step 3 the cited Krull-dimension definition is literally the supremum of lengths of strict prime chains. Since q is strictly below m, any prime strictly below q would give a chain of length two in B. Quotient prime chains lift strictly, and the chain 0<n gives ht(n)=1, while p contracts to n and is therefore nonzero. These are direct uses of that definition. There is no claim that all local rings of a nonreduced ring are domains: only the explicitly chosen prime quotients R and D are used as domains.

For step 4 I read the whole statement of lem-trf-affine-dimension-inequality. It assumes a Noetherian domain R, a finite type domain D, an inclusion R into D, and a pair of primes with the specified contraction; step 1 verifies all four. It imposes no universal catenarity for the inequality; that extra condition is only for equality in the source. The generic term vanishes by clearing the finitely many denominators of one annihilating polynomial, which leaves a nonzero polynomial since R is a domain. Combining the source inequality with the lower height bound gives 1 <= ht(p) <= 1-t, forcing t=0. Step 1 then identifies precisely the field extension in the conclusion. citations.json contains a verbatim statement quote for every cited node in every step. The dimension inequality is declared in split_report.tool_uses because it is generic commutative algebra from another unit, exactly the tool 0AB6 itself calls for; using its statement changes no step of the unit route.
