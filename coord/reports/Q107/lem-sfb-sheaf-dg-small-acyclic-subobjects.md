## Source error

The item's statement is unchanged. The auxiliary assertion in the proof of 0FST that adjoining sections always gives `|F'| <= max(|F|, |K|, kappa)` for any sufficiently large infinite kappa is not valid for the source's size defined by *all sections on all opens*. Sheaf gluing involves products, not only finite sums. There is also a harmless typing slip in the displayed local formula: the summand y_i must be a section on U_i, not on U.

Here is an explicit test of the cardinal problem. Let X be countably infinite discrete and O=A the sheaf of F_2-valued functions, concentrated in degree zero with zero differential. Use covers without repetitions. Write c=2^(aleph_0), theta=2^c, and kappa=sup_n theta^(+n). The site has at most c objects and arrows and at most theta covering families, with cover sizes at most c; the total-section size of A is c. Thus kappa is larger than every fixed bound the paragraph requests. At each point let M have the F_2-vector space with basis indexed by kappa; let M be the product sheaf of these fibres. For each alpha<kappa take the global section x_alpha whose value at every point is the alpha-th basis vector. The sheaf generated by these kappa sections is all of M, because this is true on each singleton. Nevertheless M(X) has cardinality kappa^(aleph_0)>kappa, since kappa has countable cofinality (König's inequality). Take F=0. The asserted maximum bound would give the contrary inequality.

The repair is internal to the proof: choose lambda bounding the topology and the total sections of A and put mu=2^lambda. Then mu^lambda=mu. Every cover is represented by a subset of the topology and every family of local expressions has at most lambda entries, so their total number is at most mu. All choices remain inside the fixed universe. This does not add a hypothesis to the item or change its conclusion.

A sectionwise countable union of subsheaves can fail to be a sheaf on a non-quasi-compact space. Step 4 consequently takes the subsheaf generated by the union. Its local description, supplied by the new lemma's *statement*, ensures that every section is locally in some finite stage. There is no assumption of a uniform stage on an entire open and no quasi-compactness assumption.

## Argument retained for the new node

For each open V and degree q define B^q(V) to be the finite-expression subset in the new statement, before allowing an open cover. These subsets are additive subgroups, contain F, and are stable under restriction and multiplication by local homogeneous elements of A. They are also stable under d: d(a x)=d(a)x+(-1)^|a| a dx and d(b dx)=d(b)dx since d^2x=0, while dF is contained in F. The displayed degrees are exactly the ones needed for these formulas. Closure under O follows from its action through the unit O -> A^0.

Define G^q(U) as those sections of M^q(U) which locally belong to B. Refining two open covers by intersections proves additivity. Restricting, applying d, or multiplying by a homogeneous local section preserves local membership by the preceding formulas. Sections in G on a covering of U glue uniquely in M; their local B descriptions together form a cover of U, so their glue is again in G. Thus G is a dg subsheaf. It contains F and every x_k. Every dg subsheaf containing those data contains B and, by its own sheaf condition, every section locally in B. This proves minimality and the local iff formula, without an unstated existence or image comparison theorem.

For the bound, there are at most lambda pairs (V,q) and at most mu elements of F over all such pairs. The coefficient sections over all degrees and opens have cardinality at most lambda. A finite expression chooses finitely many elements of K, coefficient sections and a section of F, so there are at most mu choices over all pairs. A covering without repeated opens is a subset of the topology, hence there are at most 2^lambda <= mu possible covers. On any one cover at most lambda expressions are chosen; there are at most mu^lambda=mu such choices. Each compatible family glues to at most one section because M is a sheaf. Multiplying these bounds, and then summing over at most lambda pairs (U,q), leaves at most mu sections in G. This proves exactly the new bound and explains why arbitrary maxima in the original source are insufficient.
