## Review by Q107

I read BMT sections 5.2 and 6.1--6.3, including its empty initial boundary and the discussion of the dimension-profile correction, before writing the proof. BMT's section 6.3 is motivation and common-presentation input, not an explicit proof of every component-incidence assertion, so proof_origin is agent. I also read CJS section 2 at the places above. Its initial hypotheses are an excellent scheme, not a reduced scheme or a scheme of dimension two. The new near-point statement uses Bennett's raw equality and Hironaka's characteristic-zero directrix conclusion. The equality is normalized by the residue transcendence degree: subtract the common summation exponent using finite differences; the tower formula gives the difference of exponents. I explicitly avoided identifying the BMT function with CJS's component-dependent global function. Definition 2.1 excludes components contained in the center; in our formulation they are allowed and the near-point polynomial-cone assertion excludes them over a near point. This is an extension that the new unsplit input must justify, not a claim that the CJS words alone already state the full node. One can localize near the chosen point and use the numerical normal-flatness criterion before the near-point argument; the component-exclusion calculation of steps 3--4 then rules out such a component. Neither the item nor the new statement deletes these edge cases.

For the boundary input I checked the actual formal-certificate and nonreduced-coefficient node statements, as well as BMT's transformation conventions. Its missing proof is a structural finite-prefix induction: initially there is no boundary; line variables are independent; at a retaining permissible chart the supported division basis makes the exceptional coefficient coordinate injective, and the previous boundary coordinate quotients retain the same supported division; boundary coordinate substitutions preserve these facts. Quotient successively by a subset of boundary coordinates and use the same remainder basis to obtain injectivity of the next equation. Whole-series certificates, rather than just equality of Hilbert functions at an isolated point, are needed for this quotient assertion. The drop exclusion is the strict-drop/saturation assertion of the formal-certificate input propagated from the last permissible blowup, as in the existing presentation proof; it is not deduced from the definition of a presentation. This argument concerns regular equations and Hilbert levels only; the geometric component argument stays in this item's steps.
