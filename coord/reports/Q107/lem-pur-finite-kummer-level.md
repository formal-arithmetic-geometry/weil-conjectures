### Returned step 6

We verify reduction over the constant sheaf $R=(\mathbf Z_\ell)_T$ separately. Use the coefficient resolution of [lem-weil-pbc-standard-quotients-perfect] and the constant-sheaf construction [def-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf] to form
$$C^\bullet=[R\xrightarrow{\ell^N}R],\qquad C^{-1}=C^0=R,$$
augmented in degree zero by $R\to(R_N)_T$. This is a resolution on the site: the coefficient exact sequence in the quotient lemma gives a sectionwise exact sequence of constant presheaves. Sheafification of presheaves of modules over the constant ring is exact by [lem-weil-pbc-site-r-module-presheaf-sheafification-exact], and its terms are exactly the constant sheaves just defined. Thus the augmented constant-sheaf complex is exact, with the stated quotient augmentation. Each term $R$ is flat because $R\otimes_R-$ is the identity, by the balanced tensor relations [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product] and [def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules]. Thus $C^\bullet$ is K-flat by [lem-pc-bounded-above-flat-kflat-site]. Multiplication by $\ell^N$ is also injective on $L_T$: writing its sections multiplicatively as $(x_r)$ by [def-pur-sheaf-inverse-limit], if $x_r^{\ell^N}=1$ for every $r$, then compatibility gives $x_r=x_{r+N}^{\ell^N}=1$ for every $r$. Hence
$$L_T\otimes_R C^\bullet=[L_T\xrightarrow{\ell^N}L_T]$$
has only degree-zero cohomology $L_T/\ell^NL_T$, identified by the projection $p_N$ with $\mu_{\ell^N,T}$ in step 3. Its augmentation to the ordinary tensor $L_T\otimes_R(R_N)_T$ is the quotient in degree zero, and induces the identity on this cokernel; it is a quasi-isomorphism.

To check that this computes the specified derived tensor, rather than assume that one may resolve the other factor, take a bounded-above flat K-flat resolution $q:P^\bullet\to L_T[0]$ using [lem-pc-kflat-bounded-flat-replacement] and [lem-pc-bounded-above-flat-kflat-site]. Both $P^\bullet$ and $C^\bullet$ have flat terms. The bifunctor and natural comparison of [lem-weil-pbc-site-derived-tensor-descent] identify $P^\bullet\otimes_R C^\bullet$ with $L_T\otimes_R^L(R_N)_T$. More explicitly the arrows
$$P^\bullet\otimes_R(R_N)_T\longleftarrow P^\bullet\otimes_R C^\bullet\longrightarrow L_T\otimes_R C^\bullet$$
are quasi-isomorphisms: each has cone the tensor of an acyclic cone with the K-flat other factor. Acyclicity is the definition [def-pc-kflat-complex], and the quasi-isomorphism criterion is [lem-pur-quasi-iso-cone-acyclic]. Here are the cone identifications with their signs. Use $\operatorname{Cone}(f)^n=Y^n\oplus X^{n+1}$ and $d(y,x)=(d_Yy+f(x),-d_Xx)$ for $f:X\to Y$. For a complex $K$, the map from $\operatorname{Tot}(K\otimes\operatorname{Cone}(f))$ to $\operatorname{Cone}(1_K\otimes f)$ is the identity on $K^i\otimes Y^j$ and multiplication by $(-1)^i$ on the shifted summand $K^i\otimes X^{j+1}$. In that second summand the $d_K$ coefficient after this map is $(-1)^{i+1}$ and the $d_X$ coefficient is $-1$ on both sides, while the component entering $K^i\otimes Y^{j+1}$ is $(-1)^i(1\otimes f)$ on both sides. For $\operatorname{Tot}(\operatorname{Cone}(f)\otimes K)\to\operatorname{Cone}(f\otimes1_K)$ the identity on both summands is a chain isomorphism: on $X^{j+1}\otimes K^i$ both differentials have coefficients $-1$ for $d_X$ and $(-1)^j$ for $d_K$. These calculations use exactly the total differential of [def-pc-tensor-complex-sheaves]. Finally the tensor symmetry $x\otimes y\mapsto(-1)^{ij}y\otimes x$ for degrees $i,j$ commutes with the total differential. With $\epsilon:C^\bullet\to(R_N)_T$, the first cone is therefore isomorphic to $\operatorname{Cone}(\epsilon)\otimes_R P^\bullet$ after this symmetry, and the second is isomorphic to $\operatorname{Cone}(q)\otimes_R C^\bullet$. The cones of $\epsilon$ and $q$ are acyclic by the cited cone criterion, so the two tensor cones are acyclic by K-flatness of $P^\bullet$ and $C^\bullet$, respectively. The augmentation square to $L_T\otimes_R(R_N)_T$ commutes, since both composites are $q\otimes(C^\bullet\to(R_N)_T)$. Therefore the canonical derived-to-ordinary comparison is the isomorphism just computed.

Finally choose the basis $A\cong R_N^{ef}$ established in step 2. Its basis inclusions and projections express $A_T$ as a finite direct sum of $(R_N)_T$ and give the resolution $(C^\bullet)^{\oplus ef}\to A_T$. Tensoring this resolution with $L_T$ gives the finite direct sum of the two-term complexes just calculated: balanced tensor relations identify $x\otimes(a_i)_i$ with $(x\otimes a_i)_i$, with inverse given by summing the basis inclusions, and these maps commute with the differentials and augmentations. Kernels and images of a finite direct-sum differential are the direct sums of the kernels and images, so the augmentation is again a quasi-isomorphism. Put $D^\bullet=(C^\bullet)^{\oplus ef}$ with this augmentation $\epsilon_A:D^\bullet\to A_T$. Its terms are finite free $R$-modules, hence flat: tensoring with $R^{ef}$ is a finite direct sum, whose kernels and cokernels are computed componentwise. It is bounded above, hence K-flat by the cited criterion. The two arrows $P^\bullet\otimes_R A_T\leftarrow P^\bullet\otimes_R D^\bullet\to L_T\otimes_R D^\bullet$ have cones isomorphic to $\operatorname{Cone}(\epsilon_A)\otimes_R P^\bullet$ and $\operatorname{Cone}(q)\otimes_R D^\bullet$ by the explicit sign maps above. Each is acyclic by K-flatness. Both composites to $L_T\otimes_R A_T$ are $q\otimes\epsilon_A$, so this resolution computes the canonical derived-to-ordinary comparison for $A_T$. Thus $L_T\otimes_R^L A_T\to L_T\otimes_R A_T$ is the canonical isomorphism, and step 4 identifies its target with $B_T$ by $x\otimes a\mapsto p_N(x)\otimes a$. The chosen basis proves invertibility only; the comparison itself is defined without that choice. This calculation is entirely over the constant ring sheaf $R$, and makes no use of completed-coefficient derived reduction.

### Returned step 9

By the universal property of the inverse limit [def-pur-sheaf-inverse-limit] defining the Tate object in [def-coefficient-twists-detailed], the compatible maps $h^{-1}L_T\xrightarrow{h^{-1}p_r}h^{-1}\mu_{\ell^r,T}\xrightarrow{\beta_{r,h}}\mu_{\ell^r,T'}$ give a map $c_h:h^{-1}L_T\to L_{T'}$ satisfying $p'_r c_h=\beta_{r,h}h^{-1}p_r$ for every $r$. The map $c_h$ is $\mathbf Z_\ell$-linear: for $a\in\mathbf Z_\ell$, its $r$-th coordinate on $ax$ is $(a\bmod\ell^r)\,\beta_{r,h}(h^{-1}p_r(x))$, by the scalar compatibility in step 8, and these are the coordinates of $a c_h(x)$. Equality of all coordinates is equality in the sectionwise limit. No assertion that inverse image preserves this infinite limit is needed. The inverse image of a constant sheaf is the same constant sheaf: it is the constant object functor, obtained by coproducts of the terminal sheaf and the relations presenting the set or module, and inverse image preserves the terminal object and these colimits by [lem-weil-pbc-left-exact-site-inverse-image-tensor]. For the finite rings $A,R_N$ the precise canonical coefficient maps are isomorphisms by [prop-weil-pbc-proetale-constant-ring-map]. Tensor compatibility [lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-module-pullback-tensor] therefore extends $c_h$ to the usual finite-twist comparison $b_h:h^{-1}A(1)_T\to A(1)_{T'}$. By the explicit formula in step 4 and the displayed relation for $r=N$,
$$\alpha_{T'}b_h=(\beta_{N,h}\otimes1)\,h^{-1}\alpha_T.$$
Indeed both maps take $x\otimes a$ to $\beta_{N,h}(h^{-1}p_N(x))\otimes a$; pure tensors locally generate the sheaf tensor [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product]. Thus the square commutes and $b_h$ is an isomorphism, since its other three sides are. For $g:T''\to T'$, the two maps $c_{h\circ g}$ and $c_g\,g^{-1}c_h$ have equal $r$-th projections by the formula for $\beta_{r,h\circ g}$ in step 8; hence they are equal by the limit universal property. Tensoring with $A$ gives $b_{h\circ g}=b_g\,g^{-1}b_h$, since both send $x\otimes a$ to $c_g(g^{-1}c_h(x))\otimes a$. The identity equations follow from $\beta_{r,\mathrm{id}}=\mathrm{id}$ in every coordinate. The coefficient and tensor identifications used here respect the inverse-image composition maps by [lem-weil-pbc-left-exact-site-inverse-image-tensor].
