## Review by Q107

I read the item, its unit `sk-i-curve-zero`, its chapter, and the complete Tag 0GSG proof in `literature/stacks-project/more-morphisms.tex`, especially lines 5067–5083. I also read Algebra Tag 00HA, the finite-generation/finite-presentation definitions at Tag 0518, and the module finiteness statements in the existing kernel node. The one new helper is the explicit assertion in the source that the closed-fibre ring is Noetherian and the module quotient is finitely presented. It is valid even when the valuation ring is non-Noetherian: quotienting an essentially-finite-type presentation by the maximal ideal gives a localization of a finite-type algebra over the residue field. It is therefore Noetherian, and the module quotient is finite over it. The quotient algebra need only be essentially of finite type, not finite type. I checked the Mathlib hits `Algebra.EssFiniteType.isNoetherianRing` and `Module.finitePresentation_of_finite`; they supply ingredients, while the residue-field quotient algebra, its essential finite type, and finiteness of the module quotient still require an argument. Hence the helper is left statement-only rather than labeled an exact Mathlib leaf.

Step 1 uses the opened definitions `def-weil-finitely-generated-module` and `def-sc-finitely-presented-module`: the former supplies a finite generating list and hence the surjection from a finite free module; the latter identifies the explicitly displayed cokernel of a finite matrix as finitely presented. Finite subsets of the relation module are a small directed set, and inclusions give surjective quotient maps. Step 2 checks the colimit by its universal property: a compatible family kills every relation because that relation occurs at a finite-subset stage. These are direct constructions from the definitions, not applications of an unstated approximation theorem.

Step 3 cites the new closed-fibre helper only for Noetherianity of R = B/(m_A B). Step 4 applies `lem-pc-noetherian-finite-kernels-cokernels`, whose opened statement says exactly that the kernel of a map between finite modules over a Noetherian commutative ring is finite. I checked its hypotheses here: R is Noetherian by the helper, R^r has the standard finite basis, and the images of that basis generate N/(m_A N). The cited statement therefore supplies a finite generating set for the kernel of the reduced presentation map. This is an existing Mathlib leaf, so it is a permitted general algebra tool across units.

Step 5 proves the needed identification of that kernel with the image of the original relation module by an explicit lift-and-subtract argument. Only finitely many terms occur in an element of JN, and the original presentation is surjective, so each term can be lifted. The chosen kernel generators then lift to finitely many original relations. Step 6 identifies both successive quotients directly by the condition that the difference of representatives belongs to K_S + J B^r. Once S contains those lifted relations, their images already generate the entire reduced relation module, making the canonical quotient map an isomorphism. Thus every non-elementary input is a cited node; the identifications and the colimit are proved in the steps. Every citation is recorded with a verbatim quote in `citations.json`, and `proof_deps` is precisely the union of `uses`. No step is longer than 2000 characters.

Edge cases checked: N = 0 (the finite generating list may be empty), A a field (J = 0 and B is essentially of finite type over a field), and an already finitely presented N. In the field case the conclusion is valid because B is Noetherian, so the relation module is finite. No extra flatness or finite-presentation hypothesis on B or N was added.
