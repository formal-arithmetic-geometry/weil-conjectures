### Returned step 1

Put $A=R^\Gamma$, $C=R^I$, and $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap A$. By [lem-dl-a1-finite-invariant-prime-orbits-residues], $R$ is integral over $A$, the primes above $\mathfrak p$ form one finite $\Gamma$-orbit, and their residue extensions are algebraic normal with finite automorphism groups (each such group is the image of a subgroup of finite $\Gamma$). Apply [lem-dl-a1-integral-normal-residue-etale-splitting] to $A\to R$ and the chosen $\mathfrak q$. We obtain an etale $A$-algebra $B$, $\mathfrak p'\subset B$, a lift $\mathfrak q'\subset S=B\otimes_A R$ of $\mathfrak q$ and $\mathfrak p'$, and a product $S=\prod_{j=1}^nD_j$ with one fibre prime per factor, all residue extensions over $\mathfrak p'$ purely inseparable. Label the factor containing $\mathfrak q'$ by $1$.

Uses: lem-dl-a1-finite-invariant-prime-orbits-residues, lem-dl-a1-integral-normal-residue-etale-splitting

### Returned step 2

The action on $S$ is $g(b\otimes r)=b\otimes g(r)$. For any prime $Q$ of a ring with a group action, its inertia subgroup is exactly $\{g:g(r)-r\in Q\text{ for all }r\}$: the difference condition gives identity on the quotient and, using $g^{-1}$, preservation of $Q$, hence identity on its fraction field; the reverse implication follows from the injection of the domain quotient into its fraction field. If $g\in I$, then for $s=\sum b_a\otimes r_a$ we have $g(s)-s=\sum b_a\otimes(g(r_a)-r_a)\in\mathfrak q'$. Conversely, the difference condition at $\mathfrak q'$ applied to $1\otimes r$ contracts to the difference condition at $\mathfrak q$. Thus $I_{\mathfrak q'}=I$. This argument also works for any later base localization.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product

### Returned step 3

Since $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is etale, $B$ is flat over $A$ by [def-weil-etale-morphism]. Apply [lem-dl-a1-flat-base-change-finite-invariants] first with $H=\Gamma$ and then with $H=I$. The canonical identifications are $S^\Gamma=B\otimes_A R^\Gamma=B$ and $S^I=B\otimes_A C$. Therefore the square formed by $\operatorname{Spec}S^I\to\operatorname{Spec}C$ and $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is cartesian. The top map is etale by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-scheme-base-change]. Its point $\mathfrak q'\cap S^I$ maps to $\mathfrak q_I$. The same conclusions hold after any principal localization of $B$.

Uses: def-weil-etale-morphism, lem-dl-a1-flat-base-change-finite-invariants, lem-weil-pbc-etale-scheme-base-change

### Returned step 4

Let $U_j=\operatorname{Spec}D_j\subset\operatorname{Spec}S$. Refine the finite clopen partition by all its $\Gamma$-translates: its nonempty atoms are the intersections $\bigcap_{g\in\Gamma}g(U_{a(g)})$. They are clopen, partition the spectrum, are permuted by $\Gamma$, and each contains at most one point of the fibre over $\mathfrak p'$. Let $Z$ be the union of atoms containing none of those points. It is closed. Since $S^\Gamma=B$, [lem-dl-a1-finite-invariant-prime-orbits-residues] gives integrality of $B\to S$, so [lem-weil-pbc-integral-spec-closed-map] says its image of $Z$ is closed and misses $\mathfrak p'$. Choose $b\in B\setminus\mathfrak p'$ with $D(b)$ disjoint from that image, and replace $B,S$ by $B_b,S_b$. This is permissible by step 3 and [lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation], since a principal localization is an open immersion. Now every remaining atom has exactly one fibre point. By [lem-weil-pbc-spec-clopen-idempotent], the atoms give orthogonal idempotents $e_j$ summing to $1$: disjointness gives $D(e_ie_j)=\varnothing$, so uniqueness of the idempotent of the empty clopen gives $e_ie_j=0$; the union gives $1-\sum e_j=0$. Thus $S\simeq\prod_j e_jS$ via $s\mapsto(e_js)_j$, with inverse summation. Relabel these factors and their fibre points. This product is now permuted by $\Gamma$; the residue fields have not changed, because we localized away from $\mathfrak p'$.

Uses: lem-dl-a1-finite-invariant-prime-orbits-residues, lem-weil-pbc-integral-spec-closed-map, lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation, lem-weil-pbc-spec-clopen-idempotent

### Returned step 5

Write $D=\{g:g\mathfrak q'=\mathfrak q'\}$. Every $g\in D$ acts over $\kappa(\mathfrak p')$ on the purely inseparable field $\kappa(\mathfrak q')$, hence acts trivially: in characteristic $p$, $x^{p^a}=c$ has at most one root, and in characteristic zero the extension is trivial. Thus $D=I_{\mathfrak q'}=I$. Because the factors are permuted and each has exactly one fibre point, the stabilizer of the first factor is $I$. By [lem-dl-a1-finite-invariant-prime-orbits-residues], $\Gamma$ is transitive on these fibre points and therefore on these factors. Choose representatives $g_jI$ with $g_1=1$ sending factor $1$ to factor $j$. Projection gives a ring isomorphism $S^\Gamma\simeq(e_1S)^I$: its inverse sends $a\in(e_1S)^I$ to the tuple whose $j$-th entry is $g_j(a)$. If $g g_j=g_k h$ with $h\in I$, then $g(g_j(a))=g_k(a)$, so the tuple is invariant; this also proves independence of the representatives. Projection and this tuple map are inverse since every invariant tuple is determined by its first coordinate. In particular $B\simeq(e_1S)^I$.

Uses: lem-dl-a1-finite-invariant-prime-orbits-residues

### Returned step 6

Since $I$ fixes $e_1$, taking invariants gives $S^I=(e_1S)^I\times((1-e_1)S)^I$. Under step 5 the composite $B=S^\Gamma\to S^I\to(e_1S)^I$ is the identity of $B$. Thus the first component $W=\operatorname{Spec}(e_1S)^I$ of $\operatorname{Spec}S^I$ is isomorphic over $\operatorname{Spec}B$ to $\operatorname{Spec}B$ and contains $\mathfrak q'\cap S^I$. The component inclusion is an open immersion by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-affine-coproduct-spectrum]. Its composite $W\to\operatorname{Spec}C$ is etale by step 3 and [lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation]. Its image $V$ is open by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-morphism-open-map], and contains $\mathfrak q_I$. The map $W\to V$ is surjective and etale (cancellation with the open inclusion $V\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}C$). The composite $W\to V\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is etale, since it is the isomorphism $W\simeq\operatorname{Spec}B$ followed by the etale neighbourhood map. Apply [lem-trf-etale-descent-through-source-cover] with $Y=W$, $X=V$, and $T=\operatorname{Spec}A$. It proves that $V\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is etale, as required.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-finite-affine-coproduct-spectrum, lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation, lem-weil-pbc-etale-morphism-open-map, lem-trf-etale-descent-through-source-cover
