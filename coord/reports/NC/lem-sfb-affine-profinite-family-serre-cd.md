Step 1 uses the statements of lem-exc-etale-field-gsets, def-sc-ring-strict-henselization and lem-exc-affine-strict-local-ring-comparison. The first identifies pointed étale schemes over a separably closed field with pointed discrete sets; selecting that point gives the field factor. The second is exactly the colimit definition to which those factor maps apply. The third identifies that algebraic construction, including its structural map, with the scheme's pointed-neighbourhood construction. The elementary local-ring calculation is written out: reduction survives localization and a reduced zero-dimensional local ring is a field, since its unique prime equals its zero nilradical. The residue field is separably closed by hypothesis. I did not identify it with its algebraic closure, which could be wrong in positive characteristic.

Step 2 uses def-weil-etale-morphism for étale base change, lem-weil-pbc-etale-ring-map-quasi-finite for finite generation of an affine étale algebra, def-weil-finite-type-morphism for composition over C, and lem-sc-etale-local-dimension for the dimension equality at corresponding primes. The latter really states equality of local ring dimensions, sufficient to bound every chain in the affine source by a chain length in the target. No surjectivity, algebraic-closedness of an étale test scheme, Noetherianity of C or finite presentation of g is used. Morphisms between the affine schemes in question are affine and hence quasi-compact and separated; this checks both hypotheses of the stalk formula.

Step 3 uses lem-kun-dimension-extension, lem-sf-separably-closed-torsion-cd and thm-sc-affine-torsion-cd. Their statements give respectively invariance under every field extension, torsion cohomological dimension zero for a separably closed field, and the affine dimension-plus-field bound, including its separate empty case. The fibre is affine finite type over the residue field. Its dimension bound comes from the stated algebraically closed geometric fibre and field-extension invariance, rather than an unsupported replacement of fibres. The two dimension definitions both count strict chains of irreducible closed subsets.

Step 4 uses prop-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-stalk-formula for a quasi-compact quasi-separated map and a torsion sheaf, which are exactly the hypotheses checked. Its statement gives the actual fibre-product cohomology group being used. The restriction of a locally torsion sheaf stays locally torsion. lemma-weil-pbc-geometric-stalk-conservative says exactly that isomorphisms can be tested on geometric stalks; apply it to the map from zero to the higher direct image. One geometric point over each underlying point suffices, independently of enlarging its algebraically closed target.

Step 5 uses lem-cbc-affine-sepclosed-etale-acyclic, whose statement needs only affineness, closed points and separably closed residue fields, not finite type or Noetherianity. The obstruction in H^1 of a kernel shows exactness of base global sections. def-weil-pbc-exact-global-sections-leray, despite its historical definition-shaped id, explicitly states the higher-direct-image/global-cohomology isomorphism for any bounded-below complex when those base sections are exact. That is exactly the identification taken from its statement, with F in degree zero. The result is the uniform torsion bound for every affine étale V, not just for U.

Step 6 uses only the new coefficient-transfer statement. I checked its statement word for word against the coefficient topologies, the six coefficient choices, the universe, the actual internal uncompleted derived sections, the exact finite scalar-model generators and the explicit weak-Serre closure operations of the item. The preceding geometric proof gives the whole hypothesis with d=e. The new node does not assume the item or any of its ancestors. Its analytic proof remains open, clearly labelled in the node and report; it would be incorrect to substitute the HRS colimit-topology result or a theorem about a completed derived category here.

For the new statement I checked the immediate edge cases: empty X and zero ring are vacuous; on an algebraically closed geometric point evaluation of internal modules at the terminal object is exact, so the bound holds even for all modules. For finite discrete coefficients, classical sheaves form a weak-Serre class and their coproducts stay classical, and the ordinary étale/pro-étale comparison reduces the assertion to the assumed torsion bound. These checks are consistency tests, not a proof of its topological branches. In the item, empty C forces empty U; empty test objects/fibres are handled by the empty clause of the torsion theorem; e=0 and characteristic ell need no exceptional exclusion.
