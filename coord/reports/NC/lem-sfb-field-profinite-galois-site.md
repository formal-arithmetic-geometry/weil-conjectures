### Every conclusion clause

1. `∃ E : Scheme.ProEt.Qcqs s ≌ ContAction Profinite G` is the asserted
   equivalence, with both full functors and their unit/counit isomorphisms.
2. For every object U and sieve R, membership in the topology induced from
   `Scheme.ProEt.topology s` is equivalent to the existence of `n : ℕ`,
   `V : Fin n → Qcqs s`, arrows `f i : V i ⟶ U` belonging to R, and
   `∀ x, ∃ i y, (E.functor.map (f i)).hom.hom y = x`.
   Thus a covering sieve contains finitely many jointly surjective arrows
   after E. This is exactly the covering-family criterion: a finite family
   in the sieve generated by a family factors through finitely many of its
   members, so joint surjectivity is equivalent to a finite jointly
   surjective subfamily of the original family. Conversely such a subfamily
   itself generates a covering sieve. This formulation supplies the full
   transported topology without introducing a second category or a new
   generic topology definition.
3. `qcqsInclusion s |>.IsDenseSubsite (inducedTopology ...) (topology s)`
   asserts cover density and the topology comparison, using Mathlib's
   actual dense-subsite predicate. Its local fullness/faithfulness fields
   concern the recorded full inclusion, not a different site.
4. `∃ Eb : Scheme.ProEt.Qcqs bar_s ≌ Profinite` supplies the separably closed
   analogue. Its induced topology equals
   `Eb.functor.inducedTopology (coherentTopology Profinite)`, namely the
   finite jointly surjective topology on profinite sets.
5. The natural isomorphism in `Over bar_s` compares
   `E.functor ⋙ forget₂ ... Profinite ⋙ Eb.inverse ⋙ qcqsOver bar_s`
   with `qcqsOver s ⋙ Over.pullback (Spec.map (algebraMap k Ω))`.
   Thus geometric base change corresponds naturally to forgetting the action.
   The functor on the right is the actual scheme base change. Its values
   have a qcqs pro-etale realization by the isomorphism, with no extra
   base-change closure assumption added.
6. `a` identifies `trivial G ⋙ E.inverse ⋙ qcqsOver s` with `testOver s`.
   The latter is exactly the recorded `profiniteToProEtOf s` followed by
   forgetting to schemes over s. It is the given `s[P]`, not another limit.
   `trivial` uses Mathlib `Action.trivial` with identity action and continuous
   projection as the action map. Being a natural isomorphism encodes all P

7. `b` identifies `regular G ⋙ E.inverse ⋙ qcqsOver s` with
   `testOver bar_s ⋙ Over.map (Spec.map (algebraMap k Ω))`.
   This is the actual `bar_s[P]` considered over s. The helper `regular`
   has carrier `G × P`, action `g • (h,p) = (g*h,p)`, and parameter map

8. `projection G` is the natural equivariant projection `(h,p) ↦ p`.
   `normalizedArrow` is its image under `E.inverse ⋙ qcqsOver s`, conjugated

   `bar_s[P]` to `s[P]`. The last equation asserts that its underlying

### Edge cases checked before writing

Empty P is allowed: both trivial and regular carriers are empty, and the test
functor gives the empty scheme. `n = 0` is allowed in the covering criterion;
it covers exactly an empty fibre. No nonempty-fibre assumption excludes the
zero algebra. A singleton P makes `bar_s[P] → s[P]` the closure structural map
and its profinite image `G → *`. Finite disconnected P keeps every component.
For separably closed k the group is trivial and both equivalences reduce to
the nonequivariant profinite model. Imperfect k and positive characteristic
remain included because Ω is a separable closure, not assumed algebraically
closed. Infinite profinite P and infinite G are allowed: the action is jointly
continuous, not an action through an assumed finite quotient. Non-qc site
objects remain in the ambient site and are covered by the dense qcqs subsite.
