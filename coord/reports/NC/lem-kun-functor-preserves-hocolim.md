## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported phrase “the canonical morphism” has no specified referent for an arbitrary triangulated category equipped only with telescope cone choices. This is a type/data failure: `def-kun-truncations` provides a distinguished triangle and explicitly supplies no functorial cone choice; TR3 in `def-weil-triangulated-category` provides an existential third map, not a uniquely defined map. The actual coproduct comparison is canonical, but a completion on the cone vertices is not specified by the given data.

Read Stacks `derived.tex`, Tag 090Z (`definition-derived-colimit`) and its following paragraph: homotopy colimits are unique up to **non-unique** isomorphism. Tag 014B (`lemma-cone-triangle-unique-isomorphism`) likewise says that two cone triangles admit an isomorphism `(1,1,c)`, and expressly denies uniqueness. The blueprint's inference “which determines the hocolim up to canonical isomorphism” is therefore invalid.

A small concrete test of that inference is the zero map `k[0] -> k[1]` in the derived category of vector spaces over any field k. Its split cone triangle has third vertex `k[1] ⊕ k[1]`, second arrow the inclusion into the first summand, and last arrow the projection to the second. For every scalar a, the third-vertex automorphism `(y,x) -> (y+a*x,x)` preserves both arrows. Thus `(1,1,c_a)` is an isomorphism of the same cone triangle to itself, and `c_0` and `c_1` are distinct. This tests the blueprint's general cone-uniqueness inference; it is not asserted to be a telescope counterexample. For the telescope statement itself the failure is the absence of specified comparison-map data, as above.

The corrected statement keeps every original hypothesis and the intended conclusion that F preserves sequential telescope objects. It names both chosen triangles, the canonical coproduct map alpha, and the two equations required of a completion c. It asserts existence of such c and invertibility of **every** such c. It adds no enhancement, compactness, boundedness, or coefficient hypothesis. Accordingly it does not silently replace ordinary triangulated categories by enhanced ones.

The existing citers are `lem-kun-0f1n` and `lem-amb-etale-adjoint-locality`. The former uses preservation of telescope objects in its reduction to truncations, which this correction still supplies. The latter already constructs its natural comparison independently and invokes a morphism of telescope triangles and invertibility; its use is also supported. The correction does not, by itself, prove that arbitrary natural transformations between exact functors commute with shifts or that an arbitrary map between the two cone objects is invertible.
