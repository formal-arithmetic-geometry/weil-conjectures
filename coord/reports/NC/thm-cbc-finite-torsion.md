The arbitrary-base geometry in the two new nodes is a generalization of the stated Stacks hypotheses, not an assertion that those hypotheses appear in the source. Here are the arguments establishing that scope. For two supplied compactifications, take the scheme-theoretic closure of the diagonal image of X in their product over Y. The image is a quasi-compact immersion; on the inverse image of the open X in either factor it is the closed graph into the separated other factor. Its closure therefore contains X as an open subscheme and is closed in the proper product. The two projections are proper refinements. For parallel arrows take their closed equalizer in the proper source; both agree on its open X, so the equalizer contains that open and remains proper. These constructions need relative quasi-compactness of f, not quasi-compactness of Y. Base change preserves the proper/open maps and each equality by the fibre-product universal property; it preserves an equalizing refinement, without claiming that scheme-theoretic closure commutes with arbitrary base change.

For a compatible tower, take the closure of the immersion X into W times_Z barY induced by a_0 and if. Its first projection restricts to a_0, making X open in this closure; the closure is proper over barY and maps properly to W. Restriction over the open Y gives U and c_X. For a common refining tower, first take the dense common refinement barY_0 of the two compactifications of Y. Stacks 0ATU(c)'s argument gives u_nu^{-1}(Y)=Y because Y is dense in barY_0. Take the closure of X in the product, over barY_0, of the two pulled-back ambient compactifications of X. It is proper over barY_0 and refines both ambient schemes. On restriction over Y the induced U_0 to U_nu maps are base changes of those proper refinements, hence proper. This is precisely the properness needed for all the comparison squares in step 7; mere commuting ambient maps would not suffice. Density is used before base change, and is not asserted to survive arbitrary base change.
