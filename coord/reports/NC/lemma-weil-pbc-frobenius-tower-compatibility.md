Why the helper is non-elementary: scalar restriction is exact, but need not preserve K-injectives (the quotient need not be flat). Therefore an arbitrary restricted resolution cannot silently be used to compute the smaller-ring direct image. One construction of the comparison uses the ringed-topos coefficient-change maps on the identity topoi: derived scalar extension is left adjoint to exact restriction, and pullback commutes with derived scalar extension by the pullback-composition isomorphism. Taking the corresponding right adjoints gives Res Ru_* = Ru_* Res, naturally. Pullback restriction is the termwise identity on the underlying exact inverse-image complexes. The composition comparisons respect this identification; the units and counits are the corresponding adjunction units and counits, so inserting these comparisons in the unit--composition--counit formula of 07A7 identifies the two exchange maps. After this identification, naturality is in the common Lambda category and therefore applies to cross-level ambient morphisms, which need not be linear over a common P_i. This is more than a two-line naturality lemma. No general assertion about tensor change of coefficients commuting with Rf_* is made.

Small cases: K = 0 and empty schemes give zero maps; identity Frobenius gives the identity mate; n = 1 is valid and the first adjacent pair is 2 to 1. If Lambda is a field and I = 0, all quotients and restrictions are identities; if I is the unit ideal, all reductions are zero. For a nonflat quotient such as Z/4 to Z/2 the statement concerns restriction and canonical derived comparisons, not preservation of injectives or an underived tensor computation. No statement dispute remains.
