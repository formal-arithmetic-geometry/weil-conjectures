Step 1 uses definitions only: A and finitely presented A-modules are finite discrete sets. The images of a finite generating family are locally constant, so finitely many clopen refinements make the scalar map fixed. Its finite kernel has finitely many generators and relations over the finite ring, hence is itself one of the prescribed local models. A finite partition is an allowed cover. This covers nonreduced A, a noninjective map, and the zero ring; it does not assume that A is a field.

Step 2 uses exactly `lem-sfb-profinite-integral-scalar-realization`. I checked its statement includes arbitrary finitely presented O_E-modules, not just free ones, their compact quotient topology, continuous families of scalar-linear morphisms, and the equality of the actual sheaf kernel with the inverse limit of the finite reduced kernels. The same statement realizes each finite O_E-module kernel as its scalar model. Each reduction is locally constant because its source and target are finite; its finite clopen partition may depend on k. Coefficient reduction gives actual compatible kernel maps, but these need not be onto. I checked O_E/pi^r, O_E itself, zero modules and the empty base; inverse limits recover these compact modules. Z_l is the E=Q_l case. Quotient-topology realization is more than the existing finite-free tensor identity and more than the scalar ring's inverse-limit presentation.

Step 3 uses exactly `lem-sfb-relative-finite-bundle-roos`. Its statement says both that derived products are represented termwise and that the ordinary limit of these finite continuous kernel bundles is the first term of the Roos triangle. These are conclusions of the new theorem, not consequences claimed from the definition of a derived limit. To test the stronger non-uniform formulation, for a section (y_k) the space of solutions x_k-t_k x_(k+1)=y_k is a closed subspace of the product of the pulled-back finite bundles. Each finite subsystem is solvable backwards, so compactness makes this space nonempty over every point. The solution space is itself a profinite cover, on which the universal solution is continuous. This gives local sheaf surjectivity even when no continuous solution exists on the original test space; it uses no uniform stabilization. For exact products one can take the fibre product over the test space of the profinite covers giving the individual lifts. The common indexing universe permits these products. The triangle then gives the hull membership by rotation and extension closure. Finite models at distinct k are not required to share a partition.

Step 4 uses the statement of `lem-sfb-ringed-site-free-matrix-scalar-extension`, which I opened and read. It gives precisely the free-Hom/matrix identification with terminal ring sections, compatibility with multiplication, and scalar extension of finite bases. The site has terminal object P. Over a finite field extension E of Q_l the finitely presented scalar modules are finite-dimensional. Consequently the arbitrary sheaf morphism really is the continuous matrix F used next. This citation is a generic tool, recorded in split_report.tool_uses, not a replacement of a blueprint unit argument.

Step 5 uses `lem-sfb-stonean-continuous-field-diagonalization`, `lem-sfb-profinite-field-scalar-annihilator-hull`, and again the free-matrix tool. I read all three statements when constructing the citations. The first says LFU is diagonal for every rectangular continuous matrix, with continuous inverse factors, without a constant-rank or constant-valuation hypothesis. The topology test is bounded division: on the nonzero locus of a maximal pivot, each quotient lies in the compact valuation ring; the closure of its graph surjects onto the clopen closure of that locus and admits a section by Stonean projectivity. Extending over the complementary clopen gives the needed continuous quotient. Finite clopen pivot choices followed by row and column operations reduce the rectangular dimensions. This is the varying-entry diagonalization needed here; existing local Smith statements assume constant type or rank and do not give it.

The second new statement is only the scalar multiplication case, with the actual functor-of-tests formula for K_a. Its class J has the same hull operations and the finite locally free generators that H_P contains. I checked that there is no tacit closure under arbitrary kernels, no arbitrary filtered derived colimit, and no completed product in that statement. At a=0 it gives R[0]; for an invertible a it gives zero; for the beta N example it retains the nonzero boundary sheaf rather than incorrectly identifying it with the zero scalar kernel. The new statement is strictly smaller than the matrix problem and is left unsplit; the nontrivial reduction of all field matrices to it is written in steps 4 and 5. The diagonal equations split as a finite direct sum on every test, and inverse matrices identify the original sheaf kernel with that sum. Finite sums use split triangles already allowed by the hull. I also checked zero ranks, empty P, identity maps and zero maps, including the extra free kernel summand when m>n.
