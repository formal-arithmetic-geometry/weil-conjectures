Step 2 uses `def-nagata-extended-ideal` for the actual closed-inverse-image ideal, not tensor pullback asserted to inject. On affine charts its kernel is IB because the inverse-image closed ring is B/IB. This proves its local finite generation and quasi-coherence and the transitivity formula (IB)C=IC. The coherence criterion `pqf-lem-coherent-ft-locnoeth` applies since Step 1 gives locally Noetherian S^nu, and the definition `pqf-def-coherent` supplies the finite-generation meaning. The zero set is closed and contains no generic point, by the generic-point property established in Step 1. Therefore it is nowhere dense on each irreducible component; this remains true with no components. No preservation of density under arbitrary base change is assumed.

Step 3 uses `lem-trf-normalized-blowup-finality` for all three expressly stated properties: X normal, p generic-to-generic, and invertibility of the extended J. Every component of X therefore dominates the component of S containing the image of its generic point. `lem-sfb-component-dominant-normalization-lift` states existence AND uniqueness of a factorization from such a normal source, and applies because S has finitely many components. This produces the asserted n, including uniqueness among all factorizations, not just among dominant ones.

Step 4 uses the precise generic-fibre clause of `lem-sfb-componentwise-normalization-generic-properties`: the fibre over a generic point is Spec of its residue field, hence has just its corresponding generic point. This checks that n is generic-to-generic; dominance of p alone without that check would be insufficient. The ideal transitivity calculation from Step 2 makes the source ideal invertible. The hypotheses of `lem-trf-normalized-blowup-finality` over the NEW base S^nu are all checked: it is reduced finite type over Q, and J^nu is coherent quasi-coherent with componentwise dense complement.
