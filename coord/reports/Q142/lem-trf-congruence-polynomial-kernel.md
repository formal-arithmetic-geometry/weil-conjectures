## Preserved argument and ownership

The following is the complete previous argument, retained to make the repair reviewable. The two children are deliberately statement-only; the calculations here are the task descriptions for their later proof workers, not proof fields on unsplit nodes.

Original step 1; assigned to parent assembly step 1:

Write $B=A[M]$, $Q=c.\operatorname{Quotient}$ and $\phi:B\to A[Q]$ for the given map. Let $I$ be the ideal generated by $[u]-[v]$ for $c(u,v)$. Since $q(u)=q(v)$ for such a pair, $\phi([u]-[v])=[q(u)]-[q(v)]=0$. Every element of $I$ is a finite sum $\sum_i h_i([u_i]-[v_i])$ with $h_i\in B$ and $c(u_i,v_i)$. Applying $\phi$ gives $\sum_i\phi(h_i)0=0$, so $I\subseteq\ker\phi$.

Original step 2; assigned to lem-trf-finite-fibre-basis-decomposition, with ideal closure in assembly step 2:

Take $f\in\ker\phi$ and write its finite basis expansion $f=\sum_{m\in T}a_m[m]$, where $T\subset M$ is finite. Put $C=q(T)$ and $T_z=\{m\in T:q(m)=z\}$ for $z\in C$. These finite subsets partition $T$: each $m\in T$ lies in the unique subset indexed by $z=q(m)$. Thus $\phi(f)=\sum_{z\in C}b_z[z]$, where $b_z=\sum_{m\in T_z}a_m$. This equality is obtained by assigning each summand $a_m[q(m)]$ to its unique index $q(m)$. The coefficient of $[z]$ in the zero element $\phi(f)$ is zero, so $b_z=0$ for every $z\in C$.

Original step 3; assigned to lem-trf-finite-fibre-basis-decomposition, with ideal closure in assembly step 2:

For each $z\in C$ choose $r_z\in T_z$, possible because $z\in q(T)$. For $m\in T_z$ one has $q(m)=q(r_z)$ and hence $c(m,r_z)$ by the definition of quotient classes. Distributivity gives the explicit identity $\sum_{m\in T_z}a_m[m]=\sum_{m\in T_z}a_m([m]-[r_z])+\bigl(\sum_{m\in T_z}a_m\bigr)[r_z]$. Indeed, expanding the right-hand side cancels the two sums of terms $a_m[r_z]$. The last term is $b_z[r_z]=0$ by step 2.

Original step 4; assigned to lem-trf-finite-fibre-basis-decomposition, with ideal closure in assembly step 2:

Each $[m]-[r_z]$ in step 3 is a generator of $I$. Multiplication by $a_m[0]\in B$ gives $a_m([m]-[r_z])\in I$, since $[0]$ is the multiplicative unit. Adding these terms over $m\in T_z$ and then over $z\in C$ expresses $f$ as an element of $I$. If $T$ is empty, $f=0\in I$ and neither a representative nor a nonempty class is needed. We have proved $\ker\phi\subseteq I$, and together with step 1 this proves the first kernel formula.

Original step 5; assigned to lem-trf-ideal-monomial-relation-congruence, with ideal inclusions in assembly step 3:

Now suppose $c$ is the smallest congruence containing $S$. Let $J$ be the ideal generated by $[u]-[v]$ with $(u,v)\in S$. Because $S\subseteq c$, its generators belong to $I$, and every finite $B$-linear combination of them belongs to $I$; hence $J\subseteq I$. Define a relation $d$ on $M$ by $d(u,v)\iff [u]-[v]\in J$. It is reflexive because $[u]-[u]=0\in J$, symmetric because $[v]-[u]=-([u]-[v])$, and transitive because $[u]-[w]=([u]-[v])+([v]-[w])$ and an ideal is closed under addition.

Original step 6; assigned to lem-trf-ideal-monomial-relation-congruence, with ideal inclusions in assembly step 3:

The relation $d$ is preserved by addition: if $[u]-[v]\in J$ and $w\in M$, then $[u+w]-[v+w]=[w]([u]-[v])\in J$ by the monoid-algebra multiplication rule and the ideal property. Thus $d$ is a monoid congruence. Every $(u,v)\in S$ satisfies $d(u,v)$ because its difference generates $J$. By the defining minimality of $c$ among congruences containing $S$, it follows that $c\subseteq d$.

Original step 7; assigned to lem-trf-ideal-monomial-relation-congruence, with ideal inclusions in assembly step 3:

Consequently every generator $[u]-[v]$ of $I$, with $c(u,v)$, lies in $J$. Every element of $I$ is a finite sum of multiples of such generators, so closure of $J$ under multiplication and addition gives $I\subseteq J$. Combining this with step 5 yields $I=J$. Substituting into the kernel equality of steps 1-4 gives $\ker\phi=J$, exactly the assertion for a generating set $S$.
