### Original step 1

Use the finite constant ring $A=A_{E,n}$ throughout, on the ambient ordinary pro-etale derived categories. For every open $v$, write $\eta^v:1\xrightarrow{\sim}v^*v_!$ for the unit normalized as the identity on sections over the open. The open normalization lemma supplies these units, full faithfulness of $v_!$, and the canonical isomorphisms $\beta$ (open base change) and $\gamma$ (open projection) whose restrictions are identities. It identifies their constant finite-coefficient specializations with the maps used in the question. Thus its hypotheses hold for every open and Cartesian square below; its objects may be arbitrary unbounded complexes, so no intermediate constructibility assertion is needed.

### Original step 2

For composable opens $w:V\hookrightarrow U$ and $v:U\hookrightarrow Z$, use the composite adjunction $v_!w_!\dashv w^*v^*$. Restriction along $vw$ is canonically $w^*v^*$, so uniqueness of left adjoints with their units gives the normalized isomorphism $c_{v,w}:v_!w_!\xrightarrow{\sim}(vw)_!$. Its restriction is the identity after the open-unit identifications: this is exactly the unit equation defining that comparison. For three opens, both composites of these comparisons carry the same pasted unit to the unit of the composite open, hence are equal by the same uniqueness. This specifies the composition identifications used below, without choosing an arbitrary scalar multiple of a composition map.

### Original step 3

Put $S=X\times_B\bar Y$, with $r:S\to X$, $s:S\to\bar Y$, $j_1:S\hookrightarrow\bar T$, and $k_1:T\hookrightarrow S$. Thus $u=j_1k_1$, $rk_1=p$, and $sk_1=kq$. Set $P=Lr^*K$ and $N_0=L\bar q^*k_!L$. The square with vertical opens $j,j_1$ and horizontal projections $\bar p,r$ is Cartesian. Its $\beta$ gives $L\bar p^*j_!K\cong j_{1!}P$. Tensoring with $N_0$ and then applying $\gamma$ for $j_1$ gives
$$L\bar p^*j_!K\otimes^LN_0\longrightarrow j_{1!}P\otimes^LN_0\longrightarrow j_{1!}(P\otimes^Lj_1^*N_0)=j_{1!}(P\otimes^LLs^*k_!L).$$
The equality uses the canonical composition of pullbacks and $\bar qj_1=s$. Both arrows are natural isomorphisms by the open normalization lemma.

### Original step 4

The square with opens $k,k_1$ and projections $s,q$ is also Cartesian. Apply its $\beta$ to obtain $Ls^*k_!L\cong k_{1!}Q$, where $Q=Lq^*L$. Inside $j_{1!}$ use the following completely typed sequence, with $\sigma$ the signed tensor symmetry:
$$P\otimes^Lk_{1!}Q\xrightarrow{\sigma}k_{1!}Q\otimes^LP\xrightarrow{\gamma_{k_1,Q,P}}k_{1!}(Q\otimes^Lk_1^*P)\xrightarrow{k_{1!}(\sigma)}k_{1!}(k_1^*P\otimes^LQ)=k_{1!}D.$$
