### Original step 1

Work first with ordinary module sheaves. Let $I=f_{\mathrm{proet}}^{-1}\nu_Y^{-1}$ and $J=\nu_X^{-1}f_{\mathrm{et}}^{-1}$ on sheaves of sets, and let $\theta:I\xrightarrow{\sim}J$ be the specified geometric square. The first assertion of [sk-thm-pe-direct-image-comparison] gives this square for every discrete coefficient ring, without its later qcqs hypothesis. Its module version $d_R^0$ is the lift of this same $\theta$, not a choice of unrelated isomorphisms for $A$ and $B$. Indeed [def-weil-pbc-site-comparison-morphism] describes the coefficient lift by applying the inverse image to addition and scalar endomorphisms. Naturality with these operations makes $\theta$ additive and scalar-compatible. The components on constant ring sheaves identify their canonical pullbacks: for each $r\in R$, use naturality on $r:1\to\underline R$; the terminal-object component is the identity. These sections generate the constant sheaf by its sheafification definition [def-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf]. In particular $\theta_B$ respects $a$ relative to $\theta_A$, by naturality on $\underline a$.

Uses: sk-thm-pe-direct-image-comparison, def-weil-pbc-site-comparison-morphism, def-weil-pbc-constant-sheaf.

### Original step 2

For an ordinary $A_Y$-module $N$, write $Q=B_Y\otimes_{A_Y}N$ and $q:B_Y\times N\to Q$ for the universal balanced pairing. Under the product identifications, $\theta_{B_Y\times N}=\theta_{B_Y}\times\theta_N$: composing with either projection gives this by naturality. Naturality on the pairing itself therefore gives
$$\theta_Q\circ I(q)=J(q)\circ(\theta_{B_Y}\times\theta_N).$$
Use the ordinary scalar/pullback maps normalized by scalar insertion and the generalized pure-tensor formula [def-weil-pbc-canonical-ringed-tensor-comparison]; such a normalized comparison exists for the module pullbacks along $I$ and $J$ by [lem-weil-pbc-canonical-ringed-tensor-comparison-construction], applied to the $A_Y$-modules $B_Y$ and $N$ and composed with the constant-ring identification of Step 1. Identify $I(Q)$ with $B_X\otimes_{A_X}I(N)$ and $J(Q)$ with $B_X\otimes_{A_X}J(N)$ by these maps. Then the equation just written says that the route using $\theta_Q$ sends $b\otimes m$ to $b\otimes\theta_N(m)$. The route first using scalar/pullback and then $B_X\otimes\theta_N$ has exactly that value. The coefficient is $b$ because Step 1 identifies $\theta_{B_Y}$ with the canonical constant-ring identification. The relation $ba(r)\otimes m=b\otimes rm$ goes to $ba(r)\otimes\theta_N(m)=b\otimes r\theta_N(m)$, so this is an equality of balanced maps. Pure tensors generate each presheaf tensor [def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product], and its maps extend uniquely to its module sheafification [def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification]. Thus the two ordinary cube paths are equal as sheaf morphisms, including on generalized sections, not only on global constant sections.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-canonical-ringed-tensor-comparison, lem-weil-pbc-canonical-ringed-tensor-comparison-construction, def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product, def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification.

### Original step 3

Represent $M$ by a complex $K$ and choose a quasi-isomorphism $P\to K$ with $P$ K-flat and flat in every degree [lem-pc-kflat-resolution]. Regard every geometric inverse image, comparison inverse image, and coefficient extension as a ringed-topos pullback; for coefficient extension the underlying geometric morphism is the identity and the structural ring map is $a$. By [lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-derived-tensor-pullback], ordinary pullback preserves K-flat complexes with flat terms. Consequently every intermediate complex along either path, including $B\otimes_A P$ and its subsequent geometric pullbacks, is K-flat with flat terms over its current coefficient ring. The representative counits are isomorphisms and identify every iterated derived pullback with the ordinary iterated pullback of this one $P$ [lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-derived-pullback]. For the scalar edges this is also the K-flat computation in [lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings]. Flatness here is a property of the resolving complexes; it imposes no flatness condition on $a$.

Uses: lem-pc-kflat-resolution, lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-derived-tensor-pullback, lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-derived-pullback, lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings.
