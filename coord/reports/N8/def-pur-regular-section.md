I checked the definition against Stacks Tag 067M, `divisors.tex`, section-regular-ideal-sheaves: regular sequences require successive nonzerodivisors, with the ring-level final quotient nonzero. I also read Tag 01WY (definition-regular-section) and Tag 02OQ (definition-zero-scheme-s) in the same file to check the rank-one convention and the evaluation ideal. The rank-one definition in Stacks asks injectivity globally; our stalkwise condition at zeros agrees there because outside the zero scheme the component is a unit. The higher-rank condition is the stated stalkwise convention, not a claim that the chosen components must be a regular sequence on the whole affine ring.

This item is a definition, so there are no proof steps to audit and no step/node quotations to manufacture. I nevertheless read the statement of every dependency and checked each construction paragraph against what it supplies. The setting paragraph uses def-pur-conventions for the field and finite context and def-amb-finite-locally-free-module for the open frames. The dual is ordinary Zariski sheaf Hom, exactly the Hom-of-restrictions construction in def-pur-sheaf-hom. Taking coordinate projections gives its dual frame immediately. Hence def-pur-vector-bundle-zero-section applies to F dual with exactly the required constant finite rank and base hypotheses, and its augmentation gives z.

It uses lem-weil-pbc-affine-morphism-relative-spec(3) for the anti-equivalence and variance: Sym(F dual) to O_T gives T to E(F). The inverse identification with a global section is written out in frames, with agreement on overlaps characterized by evaluation; no unidentified sheaf biduality theorem is assumed. Every point has a frame, so dual coordinates determine the section there.

For the zero-scheme paragraph, def-pc-fibre-product supplies the construction and def-weil-affine-spectrum fixes the direction of the coordinate maps. The affine comparison of the symmetric algebra is exactly the statement of lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-symmetric-algebra-affine, applied to F dual, which is locally free and thus quasi-coherent by def-amb-finite-locally-free-module. The regularity paragraph invokes def-weil-pbc-regular-sequence for both successive injectivity and the nonzero final quotient. The transverse-sum paragraph introduces G and its rank explicitly, takes a common refinement of the two frame covers, and uses def-pc-fibre-product and lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra for the scheme intersection. The identifications respect both projections and therefore glue by uniqueness of the fibre-product maps. None of these arguments requires smoothness, reducedness, flatness, or regularity of the ambient scheme.

I tested rank zero (Sym(0) = O_T, both maps identity, empty regular sequence in each nonzero local ring), empty T, a nowhere-zero rank-one section (empty zero scheme; the test is vacuous), the zero section of positive rank on a nonempty base (first multiplication by zero is not injective), s = x on affine one-space, and s = x squared on affine one-space (regular but nonreduced zero scheme). On T = Spec k[epsilon]/(epsilon squared), s = epsilon is not regular, since epsilon kills epsilon. For sums, (x,y) on affine two-space is transverse, while (x,x) on affine one-space is not: the second component is zero modulo the first. The quotient ideal in the intersection calculation gives (x,y) and (x), respectively, confirming that transversality is not being confused with disjointness or smoothness. Rank-zero summands reduce the predicate to that of the other section.
