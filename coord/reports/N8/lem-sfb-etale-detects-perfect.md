## Review by N8

I checked the item against 08HG with arbitrary scheme and arbitrary unbounded module-derived object. No quasi-coherence, qcqs, Noetherianity, or global cohomological bound has been added. In particular, I did not use the D_QCoh equivalence to establish perfectness for an object not yet known quasi-coherent.

Step 1 cites def-pc-perfect-complex for the open cover and bounded summand models. This statement explicitly gives bounded complexes of direct summands of finite free modules. Exact tensor pullback is part of the item's specified setup; applying it to the retraction maps preserves ts=1 and takes the finite free middle module to the etale structure module of the same rank. A Zariski cover is an etale cover by the definition of that site. This is an elementary computation, not an unrecorded pullback-perfectness theorem. The two directions of the same formula at a point or a rank-zero summand also check it.

Step 2 uses exactly the new ringed-site criterion's implication from perfect to pseudo-coherent plus locally finite Tor dimension. I read 08G8 and the preceding definitions; the criterion has no scheme or quasi-compactness hypothesis. The criterion is therefore needed on the small etale site, where the existing scheme-only 08CQ nodes cannot be applied. All notions used in its statement are explicitly defined there, with derived categories, module sheaves and K-flat tensor conventions cited as statement dependencies.

Step 3 takes m-pseudo-coherence for each integer m from step 2, and the new 08HE statement says precisely that this descends for arbitrary E. Quantifying over all m gives pseudo-coherence. The second cited statement explicitly includes local finite Tor dimension, not merely a globally fixed Tor interval. This distinction matters on non-quasi-compact T. Neither cited statement is a clause of the item: they also apply to complexes which are not perfect.

For the local clause of the Tor node I checked the omitted globalization detail rather than confusing local and global bounds. Work over any affine open U. A cover on which the etale complex has finite Tor dimension has a finite subcover over U (the images are open, and U is quasi-compact). Enlarge its finitely many intervals to one interval [min a_i,max b_i]. For every etale structure-module F, the cohomology of the derived tensor vanishes off that interval after restricting to each covering member, and hence vanishes as a sheaf on U_et. Apply 08HF(1) over U to descend that fixed interval. Varying U gives Zariski-local finite Tor dimension. The other direction follows from 08HF(1) on a Zariski cover, viewed as an etale cover. This supporting proof plan stays here in the report; the new node contains only its statement. The global-amplitude part is exactly 08HF(1), using 08H8's exactness/faithfulness in its source proof, with no QCoh assumption.

Step 4 cites lem-pc-pseudo-coherent-locally-finite-tor-perfect. I opened its statement: its inputs are precisely a scheme, an arbitrary module-derived object, pseudo-coherence and locally finite Tor dimension, and its output is the Zariski perfectness already defined by def-pc-perfect-complex. I also read the other direction of 08CQ but did not cite it unnecessarily. citations.json includes the actual statement text of every node cited in every step, without using a proof of a cited node as its interface.

Small cases checked: empty scheme and zero complex satisfy every conclusion vacuously; finite free complexes and their retractions satisfy both notions; on Spec k an infinite-dimensional vector space in degree zero has Tor amplitude [0,0] but is not pseudo-coherent, so Tor descent alone cannot supply perfectness; an unbounded direct sum of shifted copies of k on a point is not perfect, so no general boundedness was inferred from exactness. On a disjoint union of points with a different one-term shift on each component, perfectness and finite Tor dimension are local but no uniform global bound exists. Both the item and the new criterion allow this example, and step 3 preserves that locality.
