Hypotheses, in the order of the node:

- A Noetherian regular separated scheme is `{X : Scheme.{u}} [IsNoetherian X]
  [X.IsSeparated] (hX : IsRegularScheme X)`. `IsRegularScheme` includes local
  Noetherianity and the upstream stalkwise `IsRegular` condition. This uses the
  recorded scheme predicate rather than silently using only regular stalks.
- The strict normal crossings divisor is `(B : X.IdealSheafData)
  (hB : IsStrictNormalCrossingsBoundary B)`. This is the recorded reduced effective
  Cartier boundary with finite regular integral components and regular intersections.
- A finite group action is `{G : Type v} [Group G] [Finite G] (a : G →* Aut X)`.
  The group universe is independent of the scheme universe; no enumeration or
  faithful-action hypothesis is added.
- Preservation of B is `hpres : ∀ g, B.comap (a g).hom = B`, an equality of ideals,
  not merely of supports.
- Tameness is `htame`: for every algebraically closed Ω and geometric point t,
  `IsUnit (Nat.card {g // t ≫ (a g).hom = t} : Ω)`. This is geometric inertia,
  not the stabilizer of the underlying topological point and not the order of G.
  The residue-field embedding preserves and reflects invertibility of this integer.
- Generic freeness is `hgen`: an open U with dense support, invariance under each
  `(a g).hom`, and trivial stabilizer of every geometric point with image in U.
- G-strictness is `hstrict`: for each irreducible component C of B.subscheme,
  the image `B.subschemeι '' C` and each of its translates are equal or disjoint.
  This is exactly the formula of `GabberModification.GStrict`, written inline as
  a hypothesis. Its owning module currently cannot be imported by the prescribed
  compiler because one of its imports is reported as non-module; the inline formula
  introduces no second named definition and does not require log data on X.

Conclusions, clause by clause:

- The fixed scheme is `(schemeActionFixedIdeal a H).subscheme`, the existing sum
  of closed equalizer ideals, constructed by `schemeActionFixedIdeal`. The first conjunct says that its
  ideal pulls back to zero along any `f : T ⟶ X` exactly when every `h : H` fixes f.
  Thus it is the scheme-theoretic equalizer on all tests, including nonreduced ones.
- The second conjunct equates the sum of the H and K ideals with the ideal for
  `H ⊔ K`, Mathlib's subgroup generated by both. Sum of quasi-coherent ideals is
  scheme-theoretic intersection, retaining the full closed scheme structure.
- The third conjunct is conjugation: pullback of the H-ideal by g inverse is the
  ideal for `H.map (MulAut.conj g).toMonoidHom`. This is exactly g X^H = X^(gHg⁻¹)
  in ideal form; using g inverse is essential for the covariance of images.
- The existential finite list C names exactly B's reduced irreducible components.
  Every C i is integral and regular; every support is the image of a component
  of B.subscheme; and every such component has a unique index. Integral subschemes
  are reduced, so a closed component's support determines the required ideal.
- For every `H ≠ ⊥`, `IsRegularScheme` states regularity, and `interior support = ∅`
  states nowhere density: each support is already closed. The existing predicate
  `HasAdaptedNormalCrossingsCenter (schemeActionFixedIdeal a H) C` states normal
  crossings with B using one regular system of parameters in the actual local ring.
  Its equations allow shared boundary and center parameters, hence containment in a
  boundary component. It uses affine representatives of those very germs and exactly
  the boundary components through the point. Finitely generated ideals allow these
  stalk equations to spread to the stated affine equations, so this adds no hypothesis.
- The same three assertions hold for `⨆ H ∈ s, schemeActionFixedIdeal a H` for every
  finite nonempty s of nontrivial subgroups. These are all nonempty-index intersections;
  empty schemes are included vacuously. The empty-index intersection is X and is not
  incorrectly asserted nowhere dense. Pairwise ideal equality gives the identification
  with the subgroup generated by any such finite family.
- For every H and geometric point t of its fixed scheme, precomposition with the
  actual `localCotangentMap` of the closed immersion is injective. Its range equals
  exactly those ambient `geometricLocalTangent` vectors fixed by every h. `HEq`
  transports the cotangent domain from h(x) to x, which are equal at the fixed
  geometric point, without choosing an auxiliary automorphism of a local model.
  The tangent definition is the Ω-vector subspace of integral-linear maps satisfying
  the residue balancing equation, i.e. Hom over κ(x) from m/m² into Ω. By tensor-Hom
  adjunction this is the dual after extension of scalars to Ω. Thus the comparison
  is canonical; it is not a bare existence of an isomorphism of equal dimensions.
- The last conjunct supplies the largest free open U. The union over nontrivial H
  equals `(U : Set X)ᶜ`. U is invariant, every geometric stabilizer on U is trivial,
  and every open V with that freeness property is contained in U. In particular this
  is the largest invariant free open as well.

Empty fixed schemes need not be removed syntactically: regularity and the adapted
condition are vacuous there, their supports and tangent tests are empty, and they
contribute nothing to the union or to nonempty geometric intersections. This is
exactly the node's convention of omitting them from the geometric family.

## New nodes

`def-per-geometric-local-tangent` defines the residue-balanced Ω-vector space of
linear forms on Mathlib's local cotangent space and the actual cotangent map of any
scheme morphism. Source: ILO VIII 4.2.3, p.119. Its submodule closure proofs are
explicit and its map is `Ideal.mapCotangent` of the real stalk homomorphism.
The searches return the cotangent primitives, and the project has a different,
relative dual-number tangent functor. Neither supplies this local-dual definition
for arbitrary regular schemes, including mixed characteristic. This new node is
in the item's chapter, with the item's blueprint ancestor and via chain.
