## Argument retained for the new statement-only nodes

For integral coset saturation, let a be F-rational and integral over a valuation ring dominating D. Its image h in H extends to M(D). After extension to the dominating valuation ring it agrees with the image of the integral B-point, by separation of M. Thus its specialization lies in Q. The inverse image of Q under the D-section h contains the closed point, hence all of Spec D. The split-torus torsor above h is trivial over the local ring D, so choose a lift b in B(D). Then a/b is in the split torus over F. If it becomes integral and invertible in the dominating valuation ring, each of its coordinates is already a D-unit: a nonzero power of a uniformizer cannot become a unit in a ring dominating D. Hence a belongs to B(D).

For the coset calculation after D→A, first extend the H-image of g using the base-changed Néron model (BLR 10.1/3). Every geometric component of its special fibre is already defined over the separably closed residue field of D and has a D-section: use smoothness and the henselian lifting property. Choose such a section h_0 in its component. The fibre of G→H over its generic point is a split-torus torsor over F, and Hilbert 90 makes it trivial; choose a_0 in G(F). The quotient of the H-images of g and a_0 is in Q(A). Its B-torsor is trivial because invertible modules over a local ring A are free, so it has a lift b in B(A). The remaining factor g/(a_0 b) is in (Frac A)^{×r}; write each coordinate as a unit times a power of the original uniformizer. Those powers are F-rational torus points, so their product with a_0 supplies the required coset representative. This is where retaining the same uniformizer is essential. Smooth local DVR extensions have this property and separable residue extension, by BLR 3.6/1. The torus torsor and component-lifting facts are genuine inputs to the unsplit arithmetic node, not uncited inputs used directly in the item.

For the saturated gluing statement, the diagonal can be tested on chart pairs. A valuation-ring pair of sections that agree generically either stays over the generic point, stays over the special point, or dominates D. The first two cases are separatedness of the common generic fibre or of one special chart; distinct special charts are disjoint. In the dominating case, if the two sections lie in charts gamma and eta, equality generically says that a_eta^{-1}a_gamma becomes an integral B-section. Saturation forces gamma=eta; separatedness of B now forces equality. The diagonal is quasi-compact: intersections of affine chart opens lie in the common finite-type separated generic fibre, or in a single finite-type separated chart, and hence are quasi-compact. The valuative uniqueness criterion therefore proves separatedness. The same charts give smoothness and local finite type. This is a real lemma, not an immediate specialization of the general gluing theorem.

For general strict-local descent, BLR 10.1/3 supplies ramification-one base change with the residue separability condition of 3.6/1. Identity components commute because their geometric fibres are unchanged up to field extension. For the converse, canonical generic descent isomorphisms extend by the Néron mapping property over the étale neighborhoods; uniqueness enforces the cocycle and group compatibility. The finite-type effective group/torsor descent argument of BLR 6.5/1, 6.5/3 is used on the identity subgroup and its translated component torsors, and the resulting open pieces are glued. Finite unions of the relevant components have finite type; one must not claim that the whole lft-group is of finite type. The affine-neighborhood fact for finite sets in fibres recorded immediately after BLR 10.1 Definition 1 is the input that makes this componentwise descent schematic. BLR 10.1/7 explicitly uses this descent for the translated group. The new node packages its generic lft version, which still needs splitting and a proof below it; it is not claimed to be a verbatim finite-type theorem from 6.5 or a finished proof. The full mapping property descends by uniqueness and fpqc descent of morphisms. Thus the converse gives the particular Néron model, with its generic identification, rather than mere abstract existence.
