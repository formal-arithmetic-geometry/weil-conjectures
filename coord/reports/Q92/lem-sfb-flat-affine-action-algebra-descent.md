The statement introduces the actual linearization and coaction equations, not an undefined appeal to equivariance. In coordinates, recover theta(m)=delta(m tensor 1); conversely theta(m)l respects balancing because theta(em)=rho_E(e)theta(m). The inverse is the displayed finite sum using the antipode. Applying coassociativity and then either antipode identity gives the two inverse equations; the tensor formula multiplies the two C-coordinates. These are the argument retained for its future split, not a proof smuggled into its statement. Mathlib HopfAlgebra.mul_antipode_rTensor_comul_apply and .mul_antipode_lTensor_comul_apply (RingTheory/HopfAlgebra/Basic.lean:85,91) are relevant ingredients, but neither provides the balanced module map, its compatibility with E, or the tensor linearization dictionary. Rep.linearization instead constructs free representations of abstract group actions. The general Pseudofunctor.isEquivalence_toDescentData in Sites/Descent/IsStack.lean:70 assumes an existing IsStack instance; it does not establish the quasi-coherent stack or its tensor and affine-coordinate comparisons, so the module node is not a one-line specialization.

Step 4 uses the module node's explicit iff for surjections in both directions. Step 5 uses the definition's compatible-family global sections and the module node's full faithfulness and unit object.
