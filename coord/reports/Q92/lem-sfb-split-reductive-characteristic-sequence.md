## Statement error and correction

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: The imported definition calls a Borel a maximal connected solvable ground-field subgroup. Over an arbitrary field that is not the definition in the cited source. Milne 17.66, printed p.375, explicitly requires a subgroup becoming Borel over an algebraic closure (equivalently a connected solvable group with complete quotient).

A concrete counterexample to the imported definition is the norm-one real torus SO(2) inside SL(2,R). It is smooth, connected, closed and solvable, and maximal among smooth connected solvable real subgroups. A larger such subgroup of dimension two would become a Borel over C, and a real Borel of SL2 stabilizes a real line; the rotation torus has no invariant real line. Dimension three would give the nonsolvable SL2. But SO(2)_C is only a one-dimensional torus, not a two-dimensional Borel, and SL2_C/SO(2)_C is not proper. This shows the ground-field maximality definition cannot support the asserted universal projective-quotient clause.

I corrected just the Borel terminology to “smooth geometrically connected ... whose extension to an algebraic closure is maximal among smooth connected solvable closed subgroups”. This follows Milne 17.66, does not add algebraic closedness/perfectness to the ground field, and leaves the statement's character map, sign, exactness and scalar-extension conclusions intact. No unit or target statement is changed, and stmt_deps is unchanged. The source field and split_report explain the correction. This also avoids using any algebraically closed-field result directly over F.

The root-free and rank-zero cases are included. Step 2 applies the new parabolic construction to obtain the actual split-unipotent kernel and T retraction; the Borel assertion comes from step 1, not an unstated general-field variant of an algebraically closed node.

Step 3 starts with any Borel in the corrected sense, so it can use the converse regular-cocharacter clause over the algebraic closure. Since T is split, the cocharacter is defined over F. Equality of the two affine closed subgroups is reflected by tensor extension. I corrected an initial ambiguity here: the projection r_B must agree with the cocharacter retraction, so I explicitly cited unipotent-to-multiplicative-trivial and proved it kills U_mu and is the identity on T. Thus U=U_mu and r_B=r_mu, with equality as scheme morphisms, not only on rational points. The split-solvable node says exactly Zariski local triviality for every represented fppf torsor and Picard triviality after every field extension. Its finite Ga/Gm-series hypothesis follows by appending coordinate-torus quotients to the given additive series; it is not assumed from solvability over an imperfect field. Smooth-scheme-over-field-regular supplies precisely the regularity needed later.

Step 4 uses the effective and fully faithful fpqc descent statement, which includes preservation of finite local freeness of rank one. Its torsor cover is smooth surjective of finite presentation and quasi-compact, so it is fpqc. The tensor and zero-character identities are actual multiplier identities, which is why this step is elementary relative to the descent theorem. The frame [h,1] trivializes the pulled-back associated bundle, not the line bundle on Q itself.

Thus the derived identity is valid as regular functions over F and hence on all test algebras. Step 6 invokes exactly the no-unipotent-characters conclusion, with Gm a group of multiplicative type, and computes eta(ut)=eta(t); it is an elementary consequence of that cited theorem and the semidirect product.
