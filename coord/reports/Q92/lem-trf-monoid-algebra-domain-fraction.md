## Argument ownership and preserved handoff

The following are the complete returned steps, preserved verbatim as handoff material. They are not proofs inside the new unsplit node files. Step 1 belongs to the injective-map leaf; steps 2–3 to the existing unique-sums leaf and new coefficient/domain leaf; steps 4–5 to finite-support-monoid-denominator; steps 6–7 to unit-denominator-fraction-equivalence. In the last two steps replace e^b and e^d by arbitrary denominators i(s) and i(t), and use injectivity i to reflect cross-product equalities. The old calculation gives exactly the new abstract conclusion.

Returned step 1:

Write $k[M]=\bigoplus_{m\in M}ke^m$, with $e^me^n=e^{m+n}$ and coefficientwise addition. Extend $Q\hookrightarrow G$ linearly to $\iota:k[Q]\to k[G]$, sending $e^q$ to $e^q$. The displayed multiplication shows that this is a unital ring map. Its coefficient at any $q\in Q$ is the original coefficient at $q$, so its kernel is zero. Thus we may identify $k[Q]$ with its image. Both rings are commutative, and $e^0\ne0$ since its coefficient at $0$ is $1\ne0$ in the field $k$.

Returned step 2:

The submonoid $Q$ is commutative and cancellative because it lies in the abelian group $G$. If $nx=ny$ in $Q$, with $n>0$, then $n(x-y)=0$ in $G$, hence $x=y$ by torsion freeness of $G$. Together with finite generation, these are exactly the hypotheses of [lem-trf-affine-monoid-unique-sums]. Consequently any two nonempty finite subsets of $Q$ have a sum attained by exactly one ordered pair.

Returned step 3:

Let $f,h\in k[Q]$ be nonzero and let $A,B$ be their finite nonempty supports. By [lem-trf-affine-monoid-unique-sums] choose $a_0\in A,b_0\in B$ with the unique-pair property. Expanding the finite product, the coefficient of $e^{a_0+b_0}$ in $fh$ is $\sum_{a\in A,\,b\in B,\,a+b=a_0+b_0} f_a h_b=f_{a_0}h_{b_0}\ne0$, since both coefficients are nonzero in the field $k$. Thus $fh\ne0$, and $k[Q]$ is an integral domain.

Returned step 4:

The subset $H=\{a-b:a,b\in Q\}\subseteq G$ contains $0$, is closed under addition by $(a-b)+(c-d)=(a+c)-(b+d)$, and is closed under negation by $-(a-b)=b-a$. It is a subgroup containing $Q$ (write $a=a-0$), so the group-generation hypothesis gives $H=G$. For $g=a-b$ we therefore have $e^g e^b=e^a$ in $k[G]$; here $e^b$ is nonzero and is a unit with inverse $e^{-b}$. This is the asserted quotient-of-monomials identity.

Returned step 5:

For an arbitrary $F=\sum_{g\in T}c_ge^g\in k[G]$ with finite support $T$, choose $a_g,b_g\in Q$ such that $g=a_g-b_g$, using step 4. Put $b=\sum_{g\in T}b_g\in Q$. For each $g\in T$, $b+g=a_g+\sum_{t\in T\setminus\{g\}}b_t\in Q$, so $e^bF=\sum_{g\in T}c_ge^{a_g+\sum_{t\in T\setminus\{g\}}b_t}\in k[Q]$. For $T=\varnothing$, choose $b=0$ and the same identity is $0=0$. Thus a single explicitly chosen monomial clears every exponent of a finite sum.

Returned step 6:

If $F,H\in k[G]$ are nonzero, choose $b,d\in Q$ by step 5 so that $f=e^bF$ and $h=e^dH$ lie in $k[Q]$. They are nonzero because $e^b,e^d$ are units in $k[G]$. If $FH=0$, then $fh=e^{b+d}FH=0$ in $k[G]$ and hence in $k[Q]$ by step 1, contradicting step 3. Thus $k[G]$ is an integral domain.

Returned step 7:

Use fractions $a/s$ with $s\ne0$ to construct the fraction fields, identifying $a/s=a^\prime/s^\prime$ exactly when $as^\prime=a^\prime s$. The inclusion of step 1 induces $J:\operatorname{Frac}(k[Q])\to\operatorname{Frac}(k[G])$ by the same numerator and denominator. This is well defined and preserves addition and multiplication by their fraction formulas; it is injective since equality of cross products in $k[G]$ is equality in $k[Q]$. For any $F/H$ in the target with $H\ne0$, choose $f=e^bF\in k[Q]$ and $h=e^dH\in k[Q]$ as in step 5. Then $h\ne0$ and $F/H=(e^d f)/(e^b h)$. The numerator and denominator on the right belong to $k[Q]$, and the denominator is nonzero by step 3. Hence $J$ is surjective. It is the claimed canonical fraction-field identification, and step 4 identifies every monomial as the quotient of two monomials of $k[Q]$.
