### Original step 2, assigned to `lem-trf-coinduction-tensor-comparison-model`

Choose a complex representing $K$ and a K-flat resolution $F$ of it by [lem-pc-kflat-resolution], specialized to the small one-object ringed site. Original cited nodes: `lem-pc-kflat-resolution`, `def-pur-derived-internal-hom`, `def-pc-module-kflat-complex`, `lem-trf-derived-coinduction-adjunction`, `lem-pc-derived-tensor-second-factor-functor`.

### Original step 3, assigned to `lem-trf-coinduction-tensor-comparison-model`

Define $t:F\otimes_R V\to\operatorname{Hom}_R^\bullet(B,I)$ by $t(f\otimes h)(b)=q(f\otimes h(b))$. This is balanced and $B$-linear: $t(f\otimes bh)(b')=q(f\otimes h(b'b))=(b\,t(f\otimes h))(b')$. For homogeneous $f$ of degree $a$, the differential of its value is $q(df\otimes h(b)+(-1)^a f\otimes dh(b))$, exactly the total-tensor differential; there is no contribution from $d_B$ since $B$ is in degree zero. Evaluation at $1$ gives $q(f\otimes h(1))$. Hence under Step 2's identifications its counit composite is precisely tensoring $K$ with $D_B\to R[0]$. By the adjunction in [lem-trf-derived-coinduction-adjunction], $Qt$ is the comparison specified in the item. Forgetting the $B$-action leaves the usual Hom/tensor evaluation comparison, with the same formula and these K-flat/K-injective models.

Original cited nodes: `lem-trf-derived-coinduction-adjunction`.

### Original step 4, assigned to `lem-trf-perfect-source-evaluation-quasi-isomorphism`

By [def-pc-perfect-ring-complex], choose a bounded complex $P$ of finitely generated projective $R$-modules with $QP\simeq B[0]$. Since $P$ is bounded above and projective, [lem-pc-ring-derived-map-representative] realizes this isomorphism by a chain map $p:P\to B[0]$. Its localization is an isomorphism, so it induces isomorphisms on every cohomology module and is a quasi-isomorphism, using [lem-weil-pbc-unbounded-derived-iso-cohomology]. This construction gives an actual map for precomposition below; no $B$-action or algebra structure on $P$ is needed.

Original cited nodes: `def-pc-perfect-ring-complex`, `lem-pc-ring-derived-map-representative`, `lem-weil-pbc-unbounded-derived-iso-cohomology`.

### Original step 5, assigned to `lem-trf-perfect-source-evaluation-quasi-isomorphism`

For any complex $N$, put $D=\operatorname{Hom}_R^\bullet(P,R[0])$. The degreewise normalized isomorphisms of [lem-pc-perfect-ring-hom-tensor-degrees] assemble to a chain isomorphism $A_N:D\otimes N\simeq\operatorname{Hom}^\bullet_R(P,N)$ by [lem-pc-perfect-ring-hom-tensor-differential]. Thus the composite $F\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(P,N)\xrightarrow{1\otimes A_N^{-1}}F\otimes D\otimes N\xrightarrow{\text{symmetry}}D\otimes F\otimes N\xrightarrow{A_{F\otimes N}}\operatorname{Hom}(P,F\otimes N)$ is a chain isomorphism. It is exactly $e(f\otimes h)(x)=f\otimes h(x)$: for degrees $|f|=a,|\lambda|=r,|n|=s$, the symmetry and final normalization give $(-1)^{ar+r(a+s)}=(-1)^{rs}$, the input normalization of $A_N(\lambda\otimes n)$. Boundedness and finite projectivity are used only for $P$; no bound on $F,N$ is used. This is a direct composition of the two cited normalized statements, not an assertion that an arbitrary tensor-Hom isomorphism is evaluation.

Original cited nodes: `lem-pc-perfect-ring-hom-tensor-degrees`, `lem-pc-perfect-ring-hom-tensor-differential`.

### Original step 6, assigned to `lem-trf-perfect-source-evaluation-quasi-isomorphism`

Precomposition with $p$ gives a square with top $t$, bottom $\operatorname{Hom}(P,q)\circ e$ for $N=J$, left vertical $1_F\otimes p^*:F\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(B,J)\to F\otimes\operatorname{Hom}(P,J)$, and right vertical $p^*:\operatorname{Hom}(B,I)\to\operatorname{Hom}(P,I)$. The square commutes: both composites evaluate on $f,h,x$ to $q(f\otimes h(p(x)))$. Both $p^*$ maps are quasi-isomorphisms by [lem-pur-rhom-well-defined], with identity as its K-injective-target comparison and $p$ as its source quasi-isomorphism. Tensoring the first with $F$ preserves that property by [def-pc-module-kflat-complex]. The bottom map is a quasi-isomorphism: $e$ is an isomorphism by Step 5, and $\operatorname{Hom}(P,q)$ is one by the canonical, covariantly natural Hom model of [lem-pod-projective-hom-evaluation-model], since $q$ is a quasi-isomorphism and $P$ is bounded above projective. On cohomology the two vertical maps and bottom map are isomorphisms, so commutativity implies that $t$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Its underlying $R$-map is therefore an isomorphism in $D(R)$, and conservativity in [lem-trf-derived-coinduction-adjunction] makes $Qt$ an isomorphism in $D(B)$.

Original cited nodes: `lem-pur-rhom-well-defined`, `def-pc-module-kflat-complex`, `lem-pod-projective-hom-evaluation-model`, `lem-trf-derived-coinduction-adjunction`.
