This is genuine extra geometry, not a clause of coherent finiteness. For the restriction input I read cohomology.tex Proposition 0A3G(2) and Lemma 01F1(1). The former is for quasi-compact opens in spectral spaces; Noetherian schemes meet those hypotheses, and 01F1 identifies module and underlying abelian cohomology. Step 1 uses the finite-type algebra Noetherian theorem and the actual finite-cover definition to make X Noetherian; the affine coherent finite-type criterion then gives a finite module over the finite-dimensional ring furnished by the existing dimension-zero theorem. The spanning products a_i m_j are the elementary argument for finite dimension, including nonreduced finite schemes and the zero module.

Step 2 cites the finite-cohomology extension theorem with the structure ring map from k and the 01YF filtration, which states precisely that the quotients are closed pushforwards of nonzero coherent ideals on integral subschemes. Closed-immersion cohomology supplies exactly the displayed equality; above-dimension vanishing covers the smaller supports. All closed subschemes remain separated finite type and Noetherian. The cohomology definition explicitly includes the long exact sequence, rather than merely naming the cohomology notion.

Step 3 reads the same filtration and the stalk-exactness statement to keep quotient support inside the original support. I added the closed-pushforward stalk theorem and closedness of coherent support: their statements justify that the support of each nonzero integral ideal pushforward is exactly its integral closed subscheme. This is needed to bound each quotient's dimension. Degree d-1 is top degree only for supports of that dimension; smaller ones use the cited vanishing. Extension closure then proves the intermediate claim without citing the item recursively.

Step 4 uses coherent kernels/cokernels and closed finite-type support to make the ideal quotient coherent with closed proper support. The generic stalk of a nonzero ideal in an integral scheme is the function field. The cited nowhere-dense dimension-drop theorem therefore applies to a nonempty locally Noetherian scheme of finite dimension. Step 3 and the exact cohomology segment give the reduction to O_X; finite kernels and images are elementary vector-space facts.

Step 5 uses the existing Chow existence and the new integral/relative-ampleness refinement, not a proper-X-only line-bundle theorem. Equality of dimensions follows from the cited equality for every nonempty open of an integral scheme locally of finite type over a field. The relative twisting theorem assumes Noetherian base X, proper pi, coherent O_Y and ampleness on each inverse image of an affine open. These are exactly the hypotheses in force. n is chosen nonnegative and large enough; all positive higher images vanish.

Step 6 applies coherent higher images to pi, with X locally Noetherian, so G is coherent. A coordinate nonzero at the generic image gives the required section by its nth power; the projective twist definition records the transition functions. On integral affine charts multiplication by this generically nonzero section cannot kill a nonzero function. After shrinking the birational open to the nonvanishing locus, the map is an isomorphism. Kernel/cokernel coherence, closed support and nowhere-dense dimension drop give the asserted small-support Q. I did not claim that pi_* of a line bundle has rank one everywhere, nor that the section is a unit on the original U.

Step 7 uses the long exact sequence and step 3 for the cokernel reduction. The cited linear acyclic-pushforward comparison states exactly a Gamma(T,O)-linear equivalence for arbitrary scheme maps and vanishing positive higher images. Taking T=Spec k gives the required k-linear equivalence, rather than an unspecified additive identification.

Step 8 uses the projective-closure statement, which says Y is a dense open and its closure embeds closed in projective space. Integrality of that closure is checked on affine charts by embedding the quotient ring in the function field; its dimension is d by the nonempty-open dimension theorem. The transition functions explicitly identify the restricted twist with L^n. The new restriction theorem applies in degree d on the Noetherian projective closure, to its quasi-compact open Y. Projective coherent finiteness applies over the Noetherian field k. The elementary quotient-of-a-finite-vector-space argument finishes this step. Step 9 only assembles the prior steps and closes the finite filtration and strong induction; it has no hidden new theorem.

For small cases, the geometric node allows pi=id on A^1_k with the usual immersion into P^1_k, and on P^1_k; its integrality hypothesis excludes the empty scheme. The restriction node works for the identity open and empty open, and in dimension zero opens of a Noetherian scheme are unions of clopen points, so restriction of sections is onto. It does not claim that Gamma(P^1,O)→Gamma(A^1,O) is onto: the theorem there concerns degree 1.
