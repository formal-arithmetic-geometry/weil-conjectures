## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported definition's final sentence falsely says that arbitrary sheaves of étale origin, intrinsic constructible sheaves, and bounded constructible étale complexes are equivalent descriptions of one class. The original first sentence and the unit both quantify over every étale module sheaf. Constructibility is an additional finiteness condition, and a sheaf class is not a category of complexes in arbitrary degrees.

Explicit small counterexample: take the global datum with $p=q=2$, $k=\overline{\mathbf F}_2$, $\ell=3$, $E=\mathbf Q_3$, $n=1$, $A=\mathbf F_3$, and $\overline X=\operatorname{Spec}k$. For $M=\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbf N}A$, let $\mathcal G=M_{\overline X,\Et}$ be the constant étale module sheaf and $\mathcal F=\nu_{\overline X}^*\mathcal G$. This sheaf belongs to the class by its defining existential. The comparison pullback of a constant sheaf is constant: the composite inverse image from the point is the inverse image from the point on the pro-étale site. Its geometric stalk is the infinite module $M$. The only nonempty stratum of the one-point scheme is the point, so no finite stratification can make this stalk finite. Thus $\mathcal F$ is not constructible. Even its degree-zero complex fails to be bounded constructible. Separately the bounded constructible étale complex $A[1]$ is not a degree-zero sheaf, exposing the type mismatch in the claimed identification with all complexes.

I removed that false final identification and retained the defining class of all sheaves isomorphic to $\nu^*\mathcal G$. I introduced $E$, its uniformizer and the quotient ring explicitly and named the existing definition nodes for the global datum and finite-type $k$-scheme. The defining existential now states precisely that membership is a property, not a choice of étale-origin witness. This does not restrict the unit's quantifier or change its proof route. The constructible comparison statements already live in `lem-sf-proetale-constructible-origin`, whose clause (i) assumes constructibility on both sides and whose clause (ii) concerns bounded constructible complexes. No replacement theorem node is needed.

The old statement dependencies on finite-torsion constructibility, constructible complexes and their comparison theorem were removed because the actual definition uses none of them. Added the global finite-field datum, coefficient extension, scheme-over-base and finite-type definitions; retained the two module-sheaf categories and comparison morphism. The item remains a definition, with no proof or proof dependencies.
