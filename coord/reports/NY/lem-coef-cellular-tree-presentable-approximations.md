## Argument for the new unsplit ordinary-cover node

This argument is recorded here for review; the new node itself has only its statement. The transition map u_ij is obtained by restricting the j-th cocone to C_i and applying its colimit property. Both u_jk u_ij and u_ik have composite lambda_k,c with every lambda_i,c, so uniqueness gives the composition law; the same test gives identities. Similarly v_j u_ij=v_i.

Given a compatible family f_i:L_i→H, for c in C choose i containing c and put g_c=f_i lambda_i,c. A different choice j gives the same map after comparing at an upper bound k of i,j. For an arbitrary arrow c→d, choose a common upper stage containing c and d. Fullness places that arrow in the stage subcategory, and the stage cocone equation proves the required equation for g_c,g_d. Thus these assignments really give a global cocone. Conversely a global cocone restricts to each C_i and hence factors uniquely through L_i. Uniqueness on every stage leg proves compatibility of the resulting f_i and that the two assignments are inverse. This construction respects postcomposition in H, and the colimit property of L supplies exactly one map L→H for each such family. The stage legs are the prescribed v_i, so the resulting cocone is the asserted one. Empty C causes no exception: all its colimits L_i and L are initial, all transition maps are the unique maps, and I is nonempty connected. Empty individual C_i contribute the unique maps from their initial colimits.

This lemma is true without any tree or presentability assumptions. I checked a singleton I, repeated subcategories, empty C and all-empty C_i, empty early stages followed by nonempty ones, two incomparable indices with a later common upper bound, a category with parallel arrows, and an arbitrary target without monomorphism assumptions. Directedness and fullness are exactly what put an arbitrary arrow and any two choices into a common stage. The proof does not assume a poset structure on C. There is no cardinal bound or cofinality theorem hidden in its conclusion.
