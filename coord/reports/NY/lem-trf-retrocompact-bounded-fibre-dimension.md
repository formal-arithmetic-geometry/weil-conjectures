## Review by NY

I checked the mathematical statements of every cited node, not merely their ids or sources. The following describes what each step actually obtains.

Step 1 uses `def-sbc-locally-finite-presentation` for a finite polynomial presentation on one affine pair at each source point. The variable classes are finite algebra generators. `lem-weil-pbc-lft-pointwise-criterion` states precisely that such pointwise affine pairs suffice for locally finite type; this avoids assuming that the definition alone proves an affine-local equivalence. `lem-weil-pbc-local-fibre-dimension-open` then states openness for every n >= 0 under locally finite type alone. Its dimension is the same topological dimension at the distinguished fibre point as in the item. I did not use a theorem about dimensions of local rings in place of dimension at a point.

Step 2 uses `def-weil-constructible-subset` only for its explicit definition of retrocompactness. `lem-weil-pbc-affine-opens-basis` actually allows the affine source chart to lie inside the arbitrary open W and the inverse image of an affine target neighbourhood. Compactness of W selects finitely many charts by the definition of compactness. `def-pc-scheme-fibre` states the homeomorphism of the fibre with the corresponding inverse-image subspace. Consequently the restricted fibre has the topology of an open subspace of the original fibre. `lem-weil-pbc-dimension-at-point-open-subspace` states exactly the needed invariance of local dimension on that open subspace. No intersection of two arbitrary affine opens is asserted to be affine.

Step 3 uses `lem-weil-pbc-lfp-affine-opens-finite-presentation` under precisely f locally finitely presented and an affine pair with f(U) in V. `lem-weil-pbc-limit-finite-presentation` explicitly supplies all three properties of R_0, B_0 and B required here, including its clause (5); I have not inferred that clause from a bare colimit statement. `lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra` states both the tensor-product spectrum isomorphism and the projection formulas. Together these make the model square cartesian with the correct structural maps.

Step 4 proves Noetherianity of Z directly by the least-positive-integer and Euclidean-division argument for its ideals, using the exact ideal-finiteness meaning in `def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring`. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian` can then be applied twice. The hypotheses come from Step 3; no flatness, reducedness or domain property is used for R_0 or B_0.

Step 5 obtains local Noetherianity from the single affine chart and the affine-cover definition in `pqf-def-locnoeth`. `lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs` supplies the missing quasi-compactness hypothesis. Thus `lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-scheme-closed-subsets-minimal` applies and supplies the topological Noetherian condition required by `lem-sfb-noetherian-space-compact-subsets`, which states quasi-compactness of every subset. Openness of the model locus is a separate application of `lem-weil-pbc-local-fibre-dimension-open`; `lem-weil-pbc-lft-pointwise-criterion`, with the whole affine source and target, supplies local finite type from B_0 finite type over R_0. This separation ensures the inverse-image theorem in Step 6 is applied to an open subset as well as a compact one.

Step 6 uses `lem-sc-fibre-dimension-after-base-change`, whose statement gives equality of local fibre dimensions at each source point for an arbitrary base change of a locally finite type morphism. Step 3 gives the cartesian square, and Step 5 gives the required local finite type. The equality of loci is then just testing the inequality <= n at each point. To establish quasi-compactness of q, `lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs` supplies compact source and quasi-separated target, and clause (1) of `lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence` gives exactly that implication for an arbitrary scheme morphism. Finally `lem-weil-pbc-quasi-compact-morphism-preimage-qc-open` states precisely the preimage compactness used. I avoided inferring affineness of an arbitrary map between affines from a permanence lemma which does not state it.

Step 7 writes out the finite-subcover argument for the finite union and then applies the retrocompactness definition in `def-weil-constructible-subset`. It is elementary and contains no additional geometric assertion. All other steps are marked non-elementary because they use actual cited theorems. No step exceeds 2000 characters or conceals a separate comparison lemma. `citations.json` quotes the full statement of each cited node, so all used clauses and their hypotheses remain inspectable.

Checked small cases: empty X and empty affine charts give empty loci and the empty finite cover; zero rings are allowed in the approximation and Noetherian statements. For the identity morphism the fibres have dimension zero and U_n is all X, which is retrocompact by definition. For affine space of positive dimension d over a field, U_n is empty for n < d and the whole space for n >= d; n = 0 therefore behaves as expected. An infinite disjoint union of affine schemes over a field need not make U_n quasi-compact globally, and our proof asserts only retrocompactness: compact W selects finitely many charts. The model construction only makes the model Noetherian, never the original base or source. These checks confirm that the original hypotheses and both conclusions are preserved.
