Step 3 uses exactly lem-sc-noetherian-closed-subset-finite-components for the finite component cover of the closed set F, lem-weil-pbc-scheme-sober for existence of each generic point, and def-weil-irreducible-components for nonemptiness, irreducibility and maximality. I opened all three statements: the first applies to any closed subset of a Noetherian scheme; the second to every irreducible closed subset of any scheme; the third supplies the closed-cover meaning of irreducibility. Components closed in F are closed in U because F is closed. The density restriction is not a black-box assertion: the step removes the other finitely many components, proves the remaining open is nonempty by maximality and finite induction on irreducibility, and intersects it with an arbitrary nonempty open in Z_i. Since that intersection is open in F and C is dense in F, it meets C inside Z_i. Countability is inherited by C intersect Z_i. This repairs the compressed inference in the source. It is 1020 characters, not a long hidden-lemma step.

Finiteness is proved in the item: choose an infinite countable A in M; step 3 puts every component generic point of its closure in Q; maximality of each a in A then identifies a with one of those finitely many generic points. This contradiction carries the essential countable-density argument. No assertion that arbitrary Noetherian topological spaces have finite generalization height is used. The helper is scheme-specific, as it must be.

Step 5 is the finite union of the already chosen C_m. The pointwise covering from step 4 gives E contained in its closure, and its inclusion in E gives the other closure inclusion. This is elementary and includes M empty.
