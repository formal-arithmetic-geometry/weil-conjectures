### Returned step 1

$\operatorname{Spec}k$ is Noetherian, since a field has only the ideals $0$ and $k$. The finite-type theorem makes $X$ Noetherian; it is quasi-compact by this conclusion and quasi-separated by the locally Noetherian theorem. The valuation-ring theorem says that $R$ is a DVR with finite residue field $A$. Its characteristic is $\ell$: $\ell$ has absolute value less than $1$, so lies in the maximal ideal $(t)$, and the nonzero field $A$ consequently has characteristic $\ell$. Identify $B$ canonically with $\varprojlim_n(R/t^n)_X$. The finite-discrete coefficient equivalence identifies $A_X=p_X^*\underline A$ with the constant residue field sheaf and identifies its ordinary relative derived tensor and constructible local models. These are the coefficient identifications used below.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian, lem-weil-pbc-locally-noetherian-quasi-separated, lem-weil-l-adic-extension-integers-dvr, det-prop-sfb-qlbar-colimit-x-integral-limit, lem-sf-module-object-ordinary-coefficient-equivalence.

### Returned step 2

For each nonzero cohomology sheaf of $K$ and $L$, apply the integral lfp/constant-model comparison on the Noetherian scheme $X$. Thus the same bounded objects have constructible cohomology in the finite scalar local-model sense, including torsion scalar modules. Apply implication (1)$\Rightarrow$(2) of the integral cohomological adic criterion separately to $K$ and $L$, using the coefficient identification of step 1. It gives local derived $(t)$-completeness of both inputs and bounded constructible finite-Tor étale $A$-complexes $C_K,C_L$ with $\nu_X^*C_K\simeq K_1$ and $\nu_X^*C_L\simeq L_1$. No completeness or scalar-model hypothesis has been added to the inputs.

Uses: lem-sfb-integral-lfp-constant-model-comparison, lem-sfb-integral-cohomological-adic-criterion.

### Returned step 3

The module-object/ringed-site derived comparison, applied to $\Lambda=\underline R$ and $B=p_X^*\underline R$, identifies $H=R\mathcal Hom_B(K,L)$, after the termwise equivalence $D(F)$ (which leaves underlying abelian sheaves unchanged), with the functorial K-injective ringed-site Hom $R\mathcal Hom_{B_{\mathrm{sec}}}(D(F)K,D(F)L)$, compatibly with restriction to every slice; by step 1, $B_{\mathrm{sec}}=\varprojlim_n(R/t^n)_X$. Apply the ringed-site internal-Hom completeness theorem with $\mathcal O=B_{\mathrm{sec}}$, $\mathcal I=(t)B_{\mathrm{sec}}$, first argument $K$, and complete second argument $L$ from step 2. Its definition of completeness is precisely the local one in the statement, on every slice and for every local section of the ideal. Therefore $H=R\mathcal Hom_B(K,L)$ is locally derived $(t)$-complete. The theorem imposes no perfectness condition on $K$.

Uses: lem-sfb-module-object-ringed-site-derived-comparison, lem-sf-ringed-rhom-derived-complete.

### Returned step 4

Apply the integral Hom reduction theorem at quotient exponent $n=1$ to $K,L$ and the completed coefficient sheaf of step 1. It identifies $H\otimes_B^LA_X$ with $R\mathcal Hom_{A_X}(K_1,L_1)$. Its comparison is defined as the transpose of reduced evaluation, exactly the map specified here, and its statement asserts that this map is invertible. Thus the conclusion concerns the canonical map, not merely an unspecified isomorphism of its source and target. This use needs neither site-perfectness of $K$ nor torsion-freeness of its cohomology. The reduction theorem is stated for the ringed-site Hom and K-flat reduction; the module-object/ringed-site derived comparison, applied to $\underline R$ and to $\underline A$ (with $p_X^*\underline A\cong A_X$ constant by step 1), identifies both $H$ and $R\mathcal Hom_{A_X}(K_1,L_1)$ with their ringed-site models, the derived relative tensors with the ordinary K-flat tensors, and carries evaluation to evaluation, so the transpose of reduced evaluation in the statement is the reduction theorem's map $c_{1,K,L}$.

Uses: lem-sfb-module-object-ringed-site-derived-comparison, lem-coef-integral-hom-reduction.

### Returned step 5

The constructible site-comparison equivalence sends the bounded constructible étale complexes $C_K,C_L$ from step 2 to bounded constructible pro-étale complexes. With the finite-discrete coefficient identification of step 1, $K_1,L_1$ therefore satisfy the finite constant local-model hypotheses of the finite-field internal-Hom finiteness theorem. Apply that theorem to the finite field $A$ of characteristic $\ell$, on the original separated finite-type $k$-scheme $X$. It gives bounded constructible cohomology for $R\mathcal Hom_{A_X}(K_1,L_1)$, and hence by step 4 for $H\otimes_B^LA_X$. No separable-closedness, perfection or finiteness of $k$ is required.

Uses: sk-thm-pe-constructible-comparison, lem-sfb-finite-field-internal-hom-finiteness.

### Returned step 6

Apply essential surjectivity of the bounded constructible site-comparison equivalence to $H\otimes_B^LA_X$. Its hypotheses hold: $X$ is qcqs by step 1 and the discrete finite field $A$ is Noetherian. Obtain $D\in D^b_c(X_{\mathrm{ét}},A)$ and $\nu_X^*D\simeq H\otimes_B^LA_X$. Constructibility here means a finite constructible locally closed stratification with locally constant finite-type $A$-module values; since $A$ is a field these values are finite-dimensional. The bounded representative theorem for the abelian category of étale $A$-module sheaves lets us choose a bounded complex representing $D$.

Uses: sk-thm-pe-constructible-comparison, lem-sfb-bounded-representative.

### Returned step 7

Choose integers $a\le b$ outside which the cohomology of $H\otimes_B^LA_X$ vanishes, using step 5 (for the zero object any interval suffices). The constant-field Tor-bounds theorem gives Tor amplitude in $[a,b]$ for this pro-étale $A$-complex. Via the isomorphism of step 6, the same holds for $\nu_X^*D$. Apply the finite-Tor site-comparison theorem to $D$, again using qcqs $X$ and Noetherian discrete $A$: it reflects this exact Tor interval to the étale site. Consequently $D\otimes_A^LN$ has cohomology only in $[a,b]$ for every degree-zero étale $A$-module sheaf $N$. Together with steps 3, 4 and 6 this supplies every clause of the required complete residue witness.

Uses: lem-sfb-constant-field-tor-bounds, det-pe-finite-tor-comparison-noetherian.
