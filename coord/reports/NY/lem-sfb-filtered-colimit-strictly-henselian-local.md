## Review by NY

I read delivery-errors sections B–E in full and checked this split with the node statements and Stacks text open. The statement retains nonempty small directed indexing, commutative nonzero local rings, strict henselianity at every stage, and local transition maps. There is no added injectivity, surjectivity, noetherianity or finite-presentation hypothesis.

Step 1 uses both clauses of `lem-pc-filtered-colimit-commrings-elementwise`: representatives and equality witnessed at a common later stage. These are exactly its statement. The union ideal is closed under sums and arbitrary multiplication by taking a common index. Contraction of that ideal is proved using equality witnesses and locality of the transition, not injectivity of a transition. Choosing an index proves that 1 is not in the union. This is a short calculation from that cited statement and the ideal axioms, so it is elementary and does not become a new node.

Step 2 uses representative existence from the same node and exactly the unit equivalence in `lem-weil-pbc-local-ring-unit-criterion`, applied only to the stage rings which are already local. An inverse is mapped to the colimit. Maximality of the union and uniqueness of that maximal ideal are written out via units and the quotient; no criterion assuming locality of R is applied before proving locality. In particular R cannot be the zero ring.

Step 3 uses the universal property actually stated by `def-weil-pbc-category-colimit`. It constructs the induced quotient maps, proves their injectivity by the contraction equality from step 1, and checks the residue-colimit universal property against arbitrary commutative rings S, not just fields. A compatible cocone gives a ring map R to S which kills the union ideal, and unique factorization through R/M is the defining quotient property. This is a direct application of those definitions and the preceding contraction computation, not an unquoted theorem about residue fields. The induced field maps are injective even though the original ring transitions need not be.

Step 4 uses the full finite-data-and-relations conclusion of `lemma-nagata-filtered-colimit-finite-data`, now legitimately applied to the ring colimit of residue fields proved in step 3. The Bezout identity is supplied by the statement of `def-pc-separable-polynomial`. There are only finitely many coefficient equations, including the derivative coefficients n*c_n. The nonzero positive-degree coefficient remains nonzero in any chosen lift because its image is nonzero. The statements of `def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology` and `def-pc-separably-closed` give respectively separable closedness of each stage residue field and a root for the nonconstant separable polynomial. The mapped root is calculated, not merely asserted to transport. This is the finite-coefficient argument in 04GI, not a new lemma restating a clause of the item.

Step 5 uses finite-data descent for the lower coefficients of a monic polynomial and the representative clause for its residue root. The leading coefficient is set to 1 at the chosen stage, rather than assuming that an arbitrary polynomial lift is monic. A common upper bound combines the independent choices in R and its residue field. Smallness and nonemptiness hold for both colimits, and directedness gives the finite upper bounds.

Step 6 uses (i) implies (ii) of `lem-weil-pbc-henselian-local-root-lifting`, whose statement was opened in full; it says exactly that monic polynomials with simple residue roots lift over a henselian local ring in the specified idempotent convention. Its hypothesis holds at R_i by the definition cited in the step. The zero residue evaluation descends because rho_i is injective, while the derivative is nonzero because its image is nonzero. Neither inference requires ring-transition injectivity. Both the root equation and its prescribed residue are then explicitly evaluated after applying alpha_i and rho_i.

Step 7 uses the converse (ii) implies (i) of the same criterion only after step 2 has made R local and steps 5–6 have proved its condition for every allowed monic f and a0. The henselian terminology node then gives exactly the strict conclusion together with step 4. This confirms both directions of the definitional comparison, instead of citing a definition for a root-lifting theorem.
