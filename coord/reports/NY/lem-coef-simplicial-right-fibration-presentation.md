## Extracted arguments retained for the new nodes

For mapping detection, SM7 makes every Map(X,Z) Kan when Z is fibrant. Choose functorial trivial cofibrations X→RX and Y→RY with fibrant targets. Restriction Map(RX,Z)→Map(X,Z) and its Y analogue are trivial Kan fibrations by SM7, so the tests for f and Rf are equivalent. Every object is cofibrant; the tensor with Δ¹ is a good cylinder because the endpoint pair is a cofibration and the projection is a weak equivalence (an endpoint section is trivial by the simplicial axiom). A weak map Rf between bifibrant objects has an inverse for these cylinders by the existing model-cylinder Whitehead lemma. Precomposition by its inverse and its homotopies makes all mapping tests homotopy equivalences and therefore weak equivalences. Conversely, if Rf:A→B passes all tests, surjectivity on π₀ at Z=A gives g:B→A with gRf homotopic to 1_A. Injectivity at Z=B gives Rfg homotopic to 1_B, since their composites with Rf are homotopic. Kan mapping spaces allow single-edge representatives of these component equalities. Such a homotopy inverse is a model weak equivalence: its images in the homotopy category are inverse, and the model localization detects weak equivalences, or equivalently apply model Whitehead's converse for bifibrant objects. Two-out-of-three with X→RX and Y→RY finishes. This is independent of right fibrations, the cone construction, unstraightening, and the item; no localization node that depends on the item is cited as a dependency of the new unsplit node.

For cellular comparison, preservation of the empty colimit makes both P(∅) and R(∅) initial, so η_∅ is an isomorphism. Induct on dimension: assume η weak for every simplicial set of dimension below n. Its value on each boundary ∂Δⁿ is weak by that hypothesis and on Δⁿ by assumption. The boundary-cell decomposition constructs the n-skeleton from the preceding skeleton by attaching its set of nondegenerate n-simplices. Well-order that small set and attach one cell at a time. Both functors preserve these pushouts and the boundary inclusions; the existing simplicial-set gluing lemma therefore applies at successors to the three weak comparison maps. At every nonzero limit stage the predecessor ordinal is a small nonempty filtered poset, so the existing filtered-colimit lemma applies. This proves the comparison for the n-skeleton (including n=0, whose boundary is empty). Finally K is the filtered union of its skeleta and both functors preserve this colimit. This uses exactly the boundary-cell and gluing/filtered inputs previously cited by Step 5. The application P=V and R=id is permitted because the existing boundary-collapse test also gives preservation of all monomorphisms by V. No assumption that V preserves arbitrary limits is made.
