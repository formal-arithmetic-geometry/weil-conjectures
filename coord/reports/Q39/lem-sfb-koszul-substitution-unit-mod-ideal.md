Step 1 uses the Koszul complex definition, the exterior algebra quotient definition and the Koszul differential node. I opened all three. The differential node explicitly states the full alternating contraction formula, not just square-zero. Tensor functoriality descends because the defining square relation is preserved, and comparison of each contraction summand proves the chain equation. This is an elementary calculation from those statements, not an invocation of the nearby augmented chain map's unspecified lower components.

Step 2 uses exactly lem-k3-koszul-generator-homotopy, whose statement gives multiplication by each generator as a null-homotopic map on the finite Koszul complex. On a cycle, the homotopy identity makes that multiple a boundary; taking finite linear combinations gives annihilation by the common ideal. This generic tool belongs to another unit and is explicitly declared in split_report.tool_uses; there is no replacement node in this unit. No unquoted theorem that homotopic maps act identically on homology is needed: the cycle calculation is written out.

Step 3 uses the ordinary module localization definition, the full increasing-wedge basis statement, the differential formula, and the two-sided adjugate identity. All four statements were read. The basis theorem applies over both A and its localization without a domain or nonzero-ring hypothesis. Under the same standard wedge bases the localized differential and substituted map have the displayed localized coefficients. The adjugate identity applies to every finite square matrix over a commutative ring, including rank zero. The determinant after coefficient localization is delta/1 by its finite-sum polynomial formula. Multiplying the adjugate by its inverse supplies an actual two-sided inverse; taking wedges of the two linear maps gives inverse exterior maps. No general non-elementary base-change theorem for exterior powers is silently used here: this identification only concerns these explicitly based free modules. The inverse chain equation follows directly by multiplying the original chain equation by its degreewise inverses.

Step 4 uses precisely the short-exact-localization statement, not a flatness definition, and the fraction construction. Applying it to the cycles/terms/boundaries and boundaries/cycles/homology short exact sequences gives the displayed quotient identification; injectivity of the boundary inclusion also follows by localizing its short exact sequence. The comparison sends [z]/delta^n to [z/delta^n], so its naturality is checked on these elements. This does not confuse sheaf sections with sheaf cohomology or use a derived base-change assertion. The only substantive exactness input is the cited existing Mathlib leaf.

Step 5 uses the module-fraction equivalence relation exactly as its node states it. I checked the exponents: multiplying delta^k(delta^m x-delta^n y)=0 by c^(k+n+m) gives c^n x=c^m y because c delta acts as 1. Thus the proposed inverse is well-defined even with zero divisors. Both inverse identities and the naturality square are explicit. This elementary fraction argument is why support control suffices; it does not assert that delta was already invertible on the original complexes.

Step 6 uses the actual definition of ordinary derived localization, which explicitly says both that quasi-isomorphisms are inverted and that cohomology factors through Q. These two clauses prove both directions of the claimed equivalence for the specified T after the indicated degree reindexing; they do not merely produce some isomorphism between its two objects.
