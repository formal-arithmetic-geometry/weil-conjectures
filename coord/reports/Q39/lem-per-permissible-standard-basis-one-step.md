## Argument retained for the new node's eventual split

The proof follows (20.3)–(20.12) of Hironaka pp.177–181, using two existing reducedness-free inputs. In this paragraph J, H, q_j, g_j and d have exactly the meanings in the new statement.

First any v in a intersection P^h divides by x_0^h in the chart and belongs to a': expand v in degree-h x-monomials, then use injectivity of x_0 on the target chart inside R[1/x_0]. The triangular identities give (q_1,...,q_j)=(g_1,...,g_j), and all w_j lie in HJ. The quotient T'/H=L maps M' onto N, so powers of N lift to corresponding powers of M'.

For a homogeneous syzygy sum C_j phi_j=0 of total degree h, lift C_j to c_j in P^(h-d_j). Then u=sum c_j f_j lies in a intersection P^h intersection M^(h+1). Apply the existing lem-trf-normal-flat-initial-coefficient-lifting, which has no reducedness hypothesis: u-sum p_i f_i=v lies in a intersection P^(h+1), with p_i in M P^(h-d_i). Dividing by x_0^h shows u/x_0^h lies in H a', since p_i/x_0^(h-d_i) lies in H and the remainder is x_0 times v/x_0^(h+1). No cancellation of nilpotents in R occurs here; x_0 is a nonzerodivisor on the specified chart by its definition.

For z=sum h_j q_j in J intersection (H+M'^d), put beta_j=h_j mod H. At stage e, 1<=e<d, maintain beta_j in N^max(e-d_j,0), and preserve the represented sum modulo HJ+M'^d. Its reduction lies in N^d. The active indices d_j<=e therefore satisfy the hypotheses of lem-trf-projective-order-leading-syzygies, also without reducedness. It gives syzygies C_vj of total degree h_v<=e and a_v in N^(e-h_v) for which beta_j-sum a_v delta(C_vj) has order at least e-d_j+1. Lift these coefficients to the chart. The preceding syzygy calculation shows the corresponding sum of q_j lies in H a', up to HJ. The assumed finite-jet condition gives H a' subset HJ+M'^(d+1). Subtracting these lifted coefficients consequently preserves the invariant and raises the active residue orders. Inactive degrees are already at least e+1, so require no subtraction.

After stages 1,...,d-1, lift each residue beta_j to h_j^0 in M'^max(d-d_j,0). Its product with q_j lies in M'^d and h_j-h_j^0 lies in H, proving z in HJ+M'^d. The empty-prefix case has J=0; d=1 has no raising stages. This is a proper smaller lemma with a finite termination invariant, not the whole standard-basis correction relabelled. Its two substantial inputs are already existing nodes whose statements were read. This argument is retained here for the next worker; the new node itself has proof=[], proof_deps=[] and proof_origin=null.

## Review by Q39

I read Hironaka's definitions of the degree sequence and standard base on p.155, his normal-flat basic assumptions (2.1)–(2.2) on pp.155–156, and Lemma 20 from (19.1) through its conclusion on pp.176–181. In particular (20.1) supplies the finite jet, (20.2) is the relation in the exceptional-fibre local ring, and (20.3)–(20.12) remove coefficient residues using normal flatness. For the new node I checked precisely this latter elimination, including the lifting at (20.6)–(20.7) and the strictly increasing coefficient order at (20.12). There is no reducedness step. I did not infer a general theorem from the proof of a more restricted cited node.

Step 1 uses the parameter-chart lemma's injectivity of multiplication by the chosen parameter, together with its localization description, to embed D_T in T[1/x_0]. I read its statement: it concerns a regular local ambient ring with a nonempty parameter subset, exactly our x-prefix. The monomial division, quotient modulo H, and residue formula are written out. I also read def-nagata-blowup-chart-ring: it really defines the chart as a subalgebra of R[1/x_0]. That supplies the injectivity used to prove g_i belongs to a', even if R has nilpotents. It makes no reducedness assumption.

Step 2 uses exactly the two conclusions of lem-trf-projective-order-initial-minimality: exact orders of theta_j and minimality of their initial forms. Its hypotheses are nonzero minimal homogeneous forms in U; these are the given prefix. A prefix of a minimal family is minimal, since a redundant member there would also be redundant in the full family. Quotient maps of graded rings then send the order-d_j class of q_j to that of theta_j.

Step 3 is a finite induction on degrees below d_a, written out rather than hidden behind standard-basis terminology. Previously generated lower degrees identify the decomposable part of F. A same-degree dependence of the xi_j would reduce to a dependence of the eta_j, contradicting Step 2. The first a-1 entries of nu* and the bound on the next entry give exactly the required number of degree-e generators for e<d_a. No statement about the unknown later entries is assumed except their nondecreasing order. Cancelling finitely many degree classes gives a' subset J+M'^d, for every element of a', not just g_a. The degree-zero base uses that a' is proper and the residue-field map is the identity.

Step 4 uses the actual homogeneous-approximation statement, not merely an invariant-algebra definition: it provides C_j in B_(d-d_j) from the relation in N^d. I checked all its hypotheses against the prefix and used t=d. If the corrected homogeneous form vanished, phi_a would lie in the preceding ideal, contradicting the given minimality.

Step 5 needs only lifting finitely many residue-field coefficients and multiplying associated-graded classes. The resulting c_j have precisely P-order at least d-d_j, including P^0 when degrees tie. The explicit division from Step 1 proves each displayed fraction belongs to T'. The nonzero degree-d class proves the first exact order, not only an inequality.

Step 6 cites the new finite-jet intersection inclusion. I checked its statement line by line: use b=a-1, d=d_a, the original full family, the actual q_j witnesses, the sorted degree inequalities, and the finite-jet condition just established for all of a'. The point and chart hypotheses are identical. Applying it to e in J intersection (H+M'^d) gives v_a in HJ with e-v_a in M'^d. The triangular identities explicitly identify J with the ideal of the original g-prefix, so this is the requested ideal, not a different corrected ideal.

Step 7 uses the singleton case of the same projective-order minimality lemma. The corrected form is nonzero and in U_d. Its dehomogenization has exact order d, so reduction modulo H excludes M'^(d+1) upstairs. This supplies the second exact order. I checked that no full-family minimality of the corrected transform was silently needed.

Equal consecutive degrees permit scalar coefficients c_j in P^0 and are covered by same-degree independence. With r=0, U_d is zero for d>0, so a nonzero singleton cannot occur; the point and degree assumptions rule out an apparent exceptional correction rather than proving a false order claim.
