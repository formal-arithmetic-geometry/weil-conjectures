I also read the full statements of all the existing nodes cited below; `citations.json` quotes those statements without paraphrasing them into stronger results.

1. The curve, integral-scheme and dimension definitions give nonemptiness, finite type and local-ring dimension one. The local-dimension/generalization-chain theorem really states the equality needed, and the sober-scheme statement supplies generic points of irreducible closed subsets. Taking all starting points converts that formula into topological dimension one. The Noetherian finite-type theorem assumes a Noetherian base, satisfied by a field. The invertible-power definition and coherent/finite-type equivalence apply on the resulting locally Noetherian scheme. This checks coherence instead of presupposing it.
2. The coherent-cohomology finiteness theorem requires properness and a Noetherian affine base, both present. The above-dimension vanishing theorem is for quasi-coherent sheaves on a Noetherian scheme, also present. With dimension one it removes H^q for q>1, so the Euler-characteristic and degree definitions give chi(O)=1 and chi(M)=2 exactly; no smooth-curve Riemann--Roch is used.
3. The nonzero-section Cartier theorem is stated for an integral scheme and an invertible module, with no regularity assumption. It gives the nonzero local coefficient needed for injectivity. The coherent-cokernel theorem applies because step 1 proved coherence. The local quotients write out the tensor-twist identification, avoiding a hidden assertion that tensor preserves arbitrary exact sequences. The zero set is closed, excludes the unique generic point, and its chains extend by the integral dimension-one C, establishing the support bound needed subsequently.
4. Euler additivity says exactly chi(M)=chi(O)+chi(Q_0). The finite-support Euler node explicitly includes vanishing, h0=chi, and invariance under invertible twists; its properness, coherence and support hypotheses have just been checked. In particular Q_n has h0=1, and the support is not empty.
5. I checked `lem-pc-zero-support-finite-scheme`: it includes a finite-dimensional k-algebra, a **closed immersion**, a coherent G with Q_0=i_*G, and equality of global sections. The affine adjunction node includes the quasi-coherent counit isomorphism and preservation of colimits, hence cokernels; it applies to coherent G. A nonzero q spans the entire k-vector space and therefore generates its A-module. The closed-immersion definition supplies the surjection from the ambient structure sheaf, so the affine generation really implies generation on C. This repairs the tempting but unsupported use of global generation from the Euler node alone.
6. The cohomology node explicitly supplies the long exact sequence for O_C-modules. H1(O)=0 supplies the lift t. The assertion that its coefficient is a unit is a local-ring calculation: b mod (a) generates, so b is outside the maximal ideal containing a. Away from D, a itself is a unit. No global choice of a local trivialization is assumed.
7. The same stated long exact sequence and the already proved H1(Q_n)=0 inductively give H1(L_n)=0 and the short exact sequence on H0. The dimensions are finite by step 2. The n=0 start uses the canonical identification H0(O)=k from the hypothesis.
8. The monomial basis is proved by induction, not cited as a projective embedding theorem. At a point of the nonempty D, t has unit coefficient, so t^{n+1} has nonzero image in Q_n; the latter's H0 is one-dimensional. Exactness then gives the spanning induction and the dimension count gives independence. The tensor-power definition specifies the powers and their products; no new lemma is hidden in this elementary induction.
