## Review by Q39

I checked the item against the sources above with the seven steps and every cited node's statement open. There is no statement correction or STATEMENT-DISPUTED finding. I did not assume W terminal, constant coefficients, a nonempty diagram, a nonzero ring, or bounded complexes. Each non-elementary input is a cited existing statement, and each step's citations have verbatim statement excerpts in `citations.json`.

Step 1 uses the definition `def-weil-sheaf-of-modules-category`, which says the underlying object is a sheaf of abelian groups with semilinear restrictions. The sheaf condition on the given finite disjoint cover supplies the displayed product decomposition: self-intersections impose no condition, mixed overlaps have zero group of sections, and the empty covering forces zero at the initial object. The maps gluing a section and zero are linear over O(U), natural in the diagram, and their projection/inclusion identities are verified componentwise. This is a direct application of the definitions, not an invocation of an uncited extensivity or module-equivalence theorem.

Step 2 has no black-box citation. It explicitly gives the direct-sum-and-relations presentation of a module colimit and its universal property, showing in particular that the same underlying additive quotient is used after restricting scalars. The finite-component projections and inclusions of step 1 descend to that quotient; their identities descend as identities of additive maps and their finite sum is inverse to restriction. This covers arbitrary small diagrams, including disconnected and empty ones. The finite index set is essential: no commutation of arbitrary set colimits with products is asserted. The argument is a short elementary module calculation, not a separate node repeating a clause of the item.

Step 3 uses exactly the statement of `def-weil-presheaf-modules-sheafification` to obtain abelian sheafification with a linear unit. I then use `lem-pc-sites-sheafification-underlying-sets`, whose statement covers abelian groups, not just rings, and says the underlying unit itself is a set sheafification. Finally `lem-zl-partition-refinement-sheafification` states natural bijectivity at W under exactly the site's strict-initial-object, disjoint stable coproduct, pullback, covering and finite-refinement assumptions. The preceding steps give all its presheaf hypotheses, including the empty coproduct. Bijective linear maps have linear inverses, so no algebra-structure compatibility is left implicit.

Step 4 uses `lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-colimits-forgetful` for its explicit arbitrary-small-diagram sheafified sectionwise colimit formula and abelian category assertion. It is stated for a sheaf of rings on an arbitrary site admitting sheafification, not just constant coefficients on an etale site. Smallness of our site ensures that sheafification exists in the chosen universe. `lem-weil-pbc-module-sheaves-limits-sectionwise` states evaluation preserves all small limits, hence kernels. The colimit comparison is the canonical sheafification map from step 3, not a separately chosen bijection. Preservation of kernels and cokernels gives preservation of images and exactness; additivity is sectionwise. Thus both cited nodes say what the step takes from them.

Step 5 spells out preservation of cohomology through kernels, images and quotients; it applies to every integer degree without any infinite totalization. `def-weil-abstract-derived-category` defines exactly these cohomology objects, quasi-isomorphisms and the ordinary localization being used. `lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-derived-factorization` explicitly supplies the unbounded factorization, its natural comparison, and preservation of quasi-isomorphisms for an additive exact functor, the properties established in step 4. This is a Mathlib leaf already in the DAG, not a new missing lemma.

Step 6 uses `prop-pur-kinjective-resolutions-exist`, whose statement covers sheaves of arbitrary commutative rings on a small site, matching our size and coefficient assumptions. It supplies resolutions of unbounded complexes. The actual theorem `lem-weil-pbc-exact-functor-derived-functor`, rather than merely a definition named after exact derivation, says the termwise localization with its inverse factorization comparison is a right derived functor. `def-sfb-right-derived-functor` specifies uniqueness of the pair including its unit and the K-injective resolution model. I checked the arrows: naturality gives eta_K = RE(Qs)^(-1) eta_I Q(E(s)), and the map from the RE(K) model to E(I) is eta_I^(-1) RE(Qs); composing gives precisely E(s). Thus no claim that E sends K-injectives to K-injectives is being used.

Step 7 uses the same unit-sensitive universal property, not an arbitrary equivalence RE ≅ D(E). After the prescribed comparison, the coefficient-object unit is eta followed by its inverse, so it is the identity on A[0]. In a resolution model the two maps are Q(E(s)) and its inverse. This verifies the normalization even though O[0] need not be injective or K-injective. This final calculation is elementary once step 6 is established.

One component gives identity restriction. Two components give the explicit sum of the two zero-extension inclusions as inverse.
