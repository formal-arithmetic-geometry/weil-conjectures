## Review by Q39

I checked the node JSON and the sources against delivery-errors B–E. For B, every new statement introduces its field, groups or representation, scheme, torus and point. Torus, cocharacter, eigenspace, fixed subscheme and solvable/Borel meanings are written out; definition references were checked against their statements. A new node has no proof hidden in its statement and has no proof dependencies. JSON is valid and TeX has single decoded backslashes. No node is declared Mathlib merely because a search found similarly named results. All new nodes have the exact queries and actual outputs of the logged searches.

For the new cocharacter lemma I read Milne 17.10, together with the split-multiplicative image discussion: a cocharacter image is a torus, it belongs to a maximal torus, and conjugacy moves that maximal torus to T. This remains true for the trivial cocharacter and in positive characteristic when the cocharacter has an infinitesimal kernel, since its image, not its kernel, is the torus being conjugated. The statement demands equality on every algebra, not only k-points.

For the new extremal-chart lemma I read 12.12, 13.51–13.53 and the coordinate paragraph of 17.65. Nondegeneracy is explicitly linear spanning of E, and finite reduced torus fixed locus is required. These hypotheses are essential for the minimum eigenspace to be a line. A smooth connected projective source is required for the affine-cell argument. I checked P1 with distinct torus weights (the chart is A1), the zero-dimensional rank-zero case (E has dimension one and U is a point), and repeated nonextreme weights. With a trivial action on a positive-dimensional X the fixed-locus hypothesis fails, so the lemma does not wrongly claim a unique extreme line.

For the new proper-stabilizer lemma I read 17.16 and 17.10 and the use in 17.65. The intended future argument first obtains a Borel fixing a point by properness, conjugates it to fix the specified point by homogeneity, and conjugates its maximal torus to T within the reduced identity stabilizer. Both T and that Borel lie in this smooth connected reduced stabilizer over the perfect field k; its possible extra infinitesimal stabilizer structure is not assumed absent. Tests include Y a point (choose any Borel through T), G=SL2 on P1 (the stabilizer is the corresponding Borel), and Frobenius-twisted homogeneous actions in characteristic p (the full stabilizer can be non-smooth but still contains the required smooth Borel).

For C1/C4, I read every cited statement, rather than taking an extra assertion from its proof. Step 1 uses the general flag node for smoothness, projectivity, transitivity, stabilizer and T-fixed points; the homogeneous orbit node identifies G/B; the existing isolated-linear-action node supplies exactly its embedding and some cocharacter; the new conjugacy node moves that cocharacter into T. The fixed scheme for T is then a closed subscheme of a finite reduced scheme, hence reduced and finite, and b_B witnesses nonemptiness. This repairs the otherwise unjustified prescribed-T use of the existing linear-action lemma.

Step 2 checks the finite-dimensional invariant-span hypothesis using the existing rational-point subspace test for smooth G. Smoothness over the field implies reducedness by the cited smooth-fibres node. The cited affine Nullstellensatz vanishing clause, applied on affine charts, makes the embedding land scheme-theoretically in the span. The new extremal-chart statement now applies with precisely its smooth connected projective, nondegenerate, finite reduced fixed-locus hypotheses. No reductivity is inserted.

Step 3 uses the general flag node's T-fixed characterization and the normalizer node's explicit simultaneous conjugacies. Normalizing T is what preserves the torus-orbit-closure condition. Conjugating the weight decomposition, cocharacter, minimum line and its dual functional transports the chart to the specified Borel; I did not assume V is normal to do this.

Step 4 uses the orbit-geometry node for smooth locally closed orbits and invariant boundary. The closedness argument is written in full: the orbit of e0 spans E, every dual orbit meets D(e0), and the exponents m_chi minus m_min are strictly positive on the remaining normalized coordinates. A nonempty boundary has a rational point by the cited Nullstellensatz; contracting its translate puts f0 in that boundary, which contradicts the definition of the orbit. A closed subscheme of projective space is projective, so the cited projective-proper lemma gives the needed properness. Connectedness is the image of connected G. This is one coordinate-and-boundary argument, not a new lemma invoked without citation.

It supplies a Borel C containing T and fixing the dual line scheme-theoretically. The item's containment hypothesis gives V contained in C as subgroup schemes. The displayed unit-scaling identity then proves stability of D(f0) on all algebras and consequently gives the restricted action morphism. Inverting a rational element proves invariance of the entire underlying complement, including nonclosed points. There is no argument from rational-point invariance to scheme invariance and no use of Chevalley's intersection equality, which is above this item in the DAG.
