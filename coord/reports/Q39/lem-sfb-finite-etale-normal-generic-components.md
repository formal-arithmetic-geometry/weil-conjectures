### Old step 1

First $Y$ is Noetherian. Indeed, over any affine $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X$, finiteness gives $p^{-1}V=\operatorname{Spec}B$ with $B$ a finite $A$-module [def-weil-finite-integral-morphism]. Thus $p$ is of finite type by [def-weil-finite-type-morphism]. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian] to the given Noetherian $X$.

### Old step 2

Apply [lem-fin-etale-normal-components] to the etale map $Y\to X$, using step 1 and normal integrality of $X$. This gives a finite disjoint union of open-and-closed normal integral subschemes $Y_i$. Write $I$ for the finite indexing set. Each $Y_i$ is nonempty because an integral scheme is nonempty [def-pc-integral-scheme]. If $Y$ is empty use the empty family. Conversely a nonempty summand cannot occur in an empty scheme.

Uses: lem-fin-etale-normal-components, def-pc-integral-scheme

### Old step 3

For every $i$, the inclusion $j_i:Y_i\hookrightarrow Y$ is also a closed immersion. It is a homeomorphism onto a closed subset. For any open $W\subseteq Y$, a section on $W\cap Y_i$ extends to $W$ by the zero section on the complementary open $W\setminus Y_i$; the sheaf axiom gives this extension since the two opens are disjoint. Hence $\mathcal O_Y\to (j_i)_*\mathcal O_{Y_i}$ is surjective, which is the definition [def-weil-closed-immersion]. By [lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-finite] and [lem-weil-pbc-finite-composition], $p_i=p\circ j_i$ is finite. By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation], the open immersion $j_i$ is etale and its composite with $p$ is etale.

Uses: def-weil-closed-immersion, lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-finite, lem-weil-pbc-finite-composition, lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation

### Old step 4

The image $p_i(Y_i)$ is closed by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-closed-map-finite-fibres] and open by [lem-weil-pbc-etale-morphism-open-map]. It is nonempty by step 2. The integral scheme $X$ is irreducible [def-pc-integral-scheme], so its only nonempty open-and-closed subset is $X$: otherwise this subset and its nonempty open complement would be disjoint nonempty opens. Thus $p_i$ is surjective. In particular $p_i^{-1}(V)$ is nonempty for every nonempty affine open $V$ of $X$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-finite-closed-map-finite-fibres, lem-weil-pbc-etale-morphism-open-map, def-pc-integral-scheme

### Old step 5

Fix $i$ and a nonempty affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $X$. Put $p_i^{-1}V=\operatorname{Spec}B$. By [def-weil-finite-integral-morphism], $B$ is finite over $A$. Both rings are domains [def-pc-integral-scheme]. The map $A\to B$ is injective: surjectivity of $p_i$ supplies a prime of $B$ contracting to $(0)\subset A$, and the kernel lies in this contraction. By [lem-sc-integral-function-field-affine-fraction], $F=\operatorname{Frac}A$ and $F_i=k(Y_i)=\operatorname{Frac}B$, via the germ maps. Let $S=A\setminus\{0\}$. The generic fibre of $p_i$ is $\operatorname{Spec}(B\otimes_A F)$ by [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra]. The maps $b\otimes(a/s)\mapsto ab/s$ and $b/s\mapsto b\otimes(1/s)$ identify this tensor algebra with $S^{-1}B$, a nonzero domain since $A\to B$ is injective. By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-fibre-discrete-separable-points], this same fibre is a disjoint union of spectra of finite separable extensions of $F$. The spectrum of the nonzero domain $S^{-1}B$ is irreducible [def-pc-integral-scheme], so this disjoint union has exactly one nonempty summand. Consequently $S^{-1}B$ is a finite separable field over $F$. As it is a field of fractions of $B$ (it is a field containing $B$, and all its elements have the form $b/s$), its identification with $\operatorname{Frac}B=F_i$ is the unique one fixing $B$. This identifies the generic fibre with $\operatorname{Spec}F_i$ and proves that $F_i/F$ is finite separable.

Uses: def-weil-finite-integral-morphism, def-pc-integral-scheme, lem-sc-integral-function-field-affine-fraction, lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra, lem-weil-pbc-etale-fibre-discrete-separable-points

### Old step 6

In the chart of step 5, $B$ is integrally closed in $F_i=\operatorname{Frac}B$. To see this using an existing statement rather than a new affine-normality lemma, apply [lem-sbc-normal-sections-finite-product] to the normal locally Noetherian $Y_i$ and its quasi-compact affine open $p_i^{-1}V$. It writes $B$ as a finite product of integrally closed domains. Because $B$ is a nonzero domain, this product has exactly one factor: zero factors give the zero ring, and two or more factors give a nonzero idempotent different from $1$, contrary to $e(1-e)=0$ in a domain. Thus $B$ itself is integrally closed. By [lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-is-integral], every element of $B$ is integral over $A$. Conversely an element $z\in F_i$ integral over $A$ is integral over $B$ by the same monic polynomial, whose coefficients are mapped into $B$; integral closedness then gives $z\in B$. Therefore $B$ is precisely the integral closure of $A$ in $F_i$.

Uses: lem-sbc-normal-sections-finite-product, lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-is-integral

### Old step 7

By [def-fin-normalization-extension], the normalization of $X$ in $F_i$ is obtained on each affine $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ by taking the spectrum of the integral closure of $A$ in $F_i$, with localization-compatible gluing. Step 6 identifies this ring with $\Gamma(p_i^{-1}V,\mathcal O_{Y_i})$ as a subring of $F_i$, not merely up to an abstract ring isomorphism. On a principal open $D(a)\subseteq V$ the identification is therefore the same equality after localizing at $a$. These principal opens form a basis on each affine overlap, so the identifications agree on all overlaps and glue to a canonical $X$-isomorphism from $Y_i$ to that normalization. Its restriction to the generic fibre is the identity on $F_i$. Thus this normalization is finite etale by step 3.

Uses: def-fin-normalization-extension

### Old step 8

Finally apply [lem-sc-etale-fibre-product-base-change-coproduct] to $Y=\coprod_iY_i$ and $\operatorname{Spec}F\to X$. With the component identifications of step 5 this gives the required compatible $F$-isomorphism $Y\times_X\operatorname{Spec}F\cong\coprod_i\operatorname{Spec}F_i$. Equivalently its algebra is $\prod_iF_i$. The primitive nonzero idempotents of a finite product of fields are exactly the coordinate idempotents: an idempotent in a field is $0$ or $1$, and one with two nonzero coordinates splits into two orthogonal nonzero idempotents. Hence every field factor, understood as a factor indexed by its primitive idempotent, comes from exactly one component $Y_i$; distinct isomorphic field factors are still distinct factors. Step 7 identifies its normalization with that $Y_i$. All assertions for the empty cover hold with $I=\varnothing$, the empty fibre and the zero generic algebra.

Uses: lem-sc-etale-fibre-product-base-change-coproduct
