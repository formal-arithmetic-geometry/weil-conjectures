Edge cases checked before writing:

- P = 0: every element annihilator is the unit ideal, which has no minimal primes;
  the premise is false. No nonzero-module hypothesis is added.
- S is the zero ring: no prime q satisfies the premise; a zero ring is permitted.
- Identity map: the two scalar actions and contractions agree, giving the same weak association.
- Surjective finite maps: the conclusion uses the actual quotient-induced scalar action,
  with no injectivity assumption on φ.
- A product finite algebra, for example k → k × k: both component primes contract to
  the zero prime and the conclusion uses an element in the corresponding summand.
- k[x] → k[x] × k, with the second component evaluation at zero: the minimal prime
  k[x] × 0 contracts to (x), which need not be a minimal prime of R, but is weakly
  associated to the restricted module through (0,1). This rules out the mistaken
  stronger conclusion about minimal primes of the rings themselves.
- P may be infinitely generated and R non-Noetherian; the signature places no such
  restrictions, and does not use Mathlib's different radical-annihilator definition
  `associatedPrimes` in place of weak association.
