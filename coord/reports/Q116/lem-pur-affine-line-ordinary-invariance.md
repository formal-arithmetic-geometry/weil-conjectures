Then $y=\ell^{s-1}x$ has order $\ell$, $C=\langle y\rangle\cong\mathbf Z/\ell$, and $D=B/C$ has smaller cardinality and is still killed by the same power $\ell^a$. The exact sequence $0\to C\to B\to D\to0$, regarded as modules over the finite ring $\mathbf Z/\ell^a$, remains exact for constant sheaves by [lem-constructible-constant-exact]. We use the underlying abelian sheaves: the kernel and cokernel of a module-sheaf map carry the induced module actions, so forgetting those actions preserves this short exact sequence. Apply the long exact sequence of [lem-weil-pbc-right-derived-universal-delta-functor] to global sections. The vanishing for $C$ and the induction hypothesis for $D$ give $H^q(\mathbf A^1_k,B)=0$ for $q>0$ and a short exact sequence $0\to H^0(C)\to H^0(B)\to H^0(D)\to0$. In its comparison with $0\to C\to B\to D\to0$, the outer constants maps are isomorphisms. The middle map is injective because an element mapping to zero first maps to zero in $D$ and then comes from a zero element of $C$; it is surjective because a section can first be matched in $D$ and its remaining difference then lifted from $C$. Hence $B\to H^0(\mathbf A^1_k,B)$ is the constants isomorphism. The same induction proves that $|B|$ is a power of $\ell$: $|0|=1$, $|C|=\ell$, and $|B|=|C||D|$ by partitioning $B$ into the cosets of $C$. In particular this holds for the additive group of every $A_{E,n}$; no freeness of $A$ over a cyclic coefficient ring and no divisibility of $n$ by the ramification index has been assumed.
