## Review by Q116

I read Milne, Algebraic Groups (iAG2017), printed pp.254--257 for the item, including the full proof of Lemma 13.6, Lemma 13.3's parameter approximation and Lemma 13.4's local difference ideal. For the new local-jet node I also read Proposition 1.10 and Proposition 1.13 on printed pp.9--10, the action/coaction discussion in Section 4(d), printed pp.86--87, and the finite-local-quotient construction in the proof of Proposition 8.9. Smoothness of G makes G reduced; density then detects the constant orbit morphism of x scheme-theoretically. This uses neither smoothness nor irreducibility of X. The affine action preserves the maximal ideal of a fixed rational point, and hence its powers; quotienting gives finite algebraic representations. Localization and quotient by a maximal-ideal power agree with the corresponding finite local quotient of the affine coordinate ring. The Gamma difference ideal is the ideal of the represented common-fixed local functor. I deliberately did not assert that the entire local ring is a rational G-module. Pullback by gamma inverse gives an honest left representation; the subgroup is closed under inversion, so it does not change the fixed ideal.

For the new semisimple-restriction node I read Proposition 4.3 and Corollary 4.4 on p.85 and Proposition 4.17 on p.90, not just their names. A Gamma-stable subspace has a closed scheme stabilizer containing Gamma; reducedness and density give G-stability. Thus a rational simple summand stays simple on Gamma. A finite direct sum of such summands has invariant complements by Proposition 4.17's maximal-independent-summands argument (the same argument works for abstract representations). A complement of ker(q) maps isomorphically onto the quotient and its inverse supplies the claimed equivariant section. There is no averaging over Gamma and no hidden assumption that its order is invertible. Maschke's finite-group theorem is consequently not a substitute. I read Mathlib/RepresentationTheory/Semisimple.lean:33--47: its comparison of abstract representation and group-algebra semisimplicity does not establish the rational-group/dense-subgroup bridge, so this is not a Mathlib leaf.

I opened the statement of every cited node. Step 1 uses the affine quotient clause of def-weil-closed-immersion, clause (1) of lem-wf-affine-nullstellensatz-vanishing for rationality of closed points of the finite-type quotient, and lem-weil-pbc-smooth-scheme-over-field-regular for regularity of A. It does not use the vanishing principle in the Nullstellensatz node. Step 2 takes the local-ring isomorphism, finite-dimensional algebraic jets and equivariant quotient from precisely the new local-jet statement. Step 3 applies the new semisimple restriction/section statement to the finite-dimensional modules from step 2, whose complete reducibility follows from the item's actual definition of linearly reductive. It obtains exactly a Gamma-equivariant section, which is all that subsequent steps require.

Steps 4 and 5 are elementary calculations from those supplied representations, not uncited geometric lemmas. For step 4 I checked that the difference span of a nontrivial simple Gamma module is nonzero and invariant, including the explicit h(gw-w) identity, and hence is the whole simple summand. For step 5 I checked the induction on degree which generates each truncated algebra from a cotangent basis. The ideal generated by nontrivial summands is invariant; its quotient is generated by the trivial summands and scalars. These two inclusions prove equality with the image of the actual Gamma difference ideal, without an unsupported assertion that a chosen basis consists of individual differences.
