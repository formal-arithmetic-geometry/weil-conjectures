## Review by Q155

I read the above parts of Conrad for the item and, separately, matched each new node to its indicated source. The amalgamation node expresses Corollary 6.2.7 together with the precise centralizers used in Step 1, rather than merely saying that maps glue. Its saturated lattice L_J is important: using the possibly disconnected full kernel of the simple-root characters instead would change the centralizer under consideration. The low-rank node is Example 6.2.6 and the exponent-one portion of the rank-two construction using Lemma 6.2.8. The characteristic-two and characteristic-three exceptional exponents are not imported; the retained universal integer relations require no divisions. Its restriction clause is precisely what Step 1 of Conrad's proof uses to check compatibility. The recognition node is Proposition 3.3.10 with geometric surjectivity already in its hypotheses; the fppf quotient description is the discussion before that proposition. The root-rigidity node is the exponent-one root polynomial formula of Lemma 6.1.4 and the uniqueness argument of Proposition 6.1.13(2), with the canonical root parametrizations of Theorem 4.1.4. It assumes an existing quasi-finite surjection; it does not construct one.

For step 1 I checked that the amalgamation statement supplies base existence, reductive representability and the full original torus in every G_J, as well as extension to the affine target G_0. The finite-index assumption makes h rationally invertible, so b transports signed integral simple-root expressions and the rational spans defining the subsystems. Coroot equalities restrict without changing either lattice. The low-rank statement explicitly supplies both normalized maps and their nested restrictions, so compatibility is not an unproved assertion that equal differentials force arbitrary maps to agree. The empty J map is the specified monomial map, not an unspecified fibrewise torus map. These facts are all in the two cited new statements and the cited realization definition.

For step 2 I opened the full statement of `lem-sfb-root-monomial-kernel-coordinates`. Its algebraically closed field hypothesis is used only after A maps to such a field. The geometric root data agree with the prescribed data by the realization definition. All q are one, so every displayed truncated additive kernel factor is the trivial scheme. The kernel lies in the torus and each root kills it because each root is h of a target root. The cited adjoint-torus statement identifies exactly this common root-character kernel with the centre, on arbitrary algebras. This proves finite central kernels on the geometric fibres before the new recognition criterion is used. I specifically checked that no geometric-fibre equality is silently used as an equality over a nonreduced ring: the recognition theorem provides the relative centrality and finite flat conclusion.

For step 3 I read `lem-sfb-adjoint-torus-root-coordinates` and `lem-sfb-relative-diagonalizable-character-duality` in full. The former really identifies the entire scheme-theoretic centre as a subgroup of T_1; the latter states the kernel of a fixed D_A(h), not just diagonalizable-group anti-equivalence. Thus the equality of kernels follows on every algebra, not just closed points. The recognition node states the torsor isomorphism G_1 times K = G_1 times_{G_0} G_1. With K trivial the base change of g is the identity. I read Stacks 02L4 (`descent.tex`, lemma-descending-property-isomorphism) and the exact DAG statement of fpqc isomorphism descent: its cover must be flat, surjective and quasi-compact. Our map is finite and faithfully flat, so it satisfies these hypotheses. This justifies the isomorphism conclusion without adding an unquoted monomorphism theorem.

For step 4 I checked the new root-map statement's quasi-finite-surjective hypothesis is now supplied by step 2, and that its exponent-one condition is the original root equality, not an assertion that an arbitrary additive polynomial has invertible differential. Its polynomial formula gives multiplication by a unit on each free rank-one root line. The existing adjoint-torus statement supplies the action of every Hom(Q_0,A-units) point on the whole group, fixes the torus, and scales each root line by its root value. The integral-basis argument for Delta_0 is written out, so there is no additional root-lattice lemma hidden in the formula for t. The ratio is A_beta/a_beta in the direction F=c_t composed with f. The root-rigidity statement applies a second time because conjugation by an automorphism preserves quasi-finiteness and surjectivity. Hence equality of the torus and simple-root tangent maps really proves equality of the group homomorphisms.

For step 5 the only quoted result is the adjoint-torus action on the root modules. Cancelling each unit a_beta gives equality on a basis of Q_0, so this final uniqueness step is elementary. In particular I did not assert that the quotient-torus point lifts to T_0(A), which need not hold. The proof's five step lengths are 1203, 1039, 824, 1168 and 622 characters: none hides an argument in a step exceeding 2000 characters.

I checked small and degenerate cases separately. A torus has empty base and G=T, so the amalgamation problem has just its torus map and the normalized low-rank map is D_A(h). With zero-rank lattice every group and every map in question is trivial. With nonzero central torus and a rank-one or rank-two root system the full torus is retained in each centralizer; there is no erroneous semisimplicity assumption. A disconnected ring such as a product of fields allows different root-unit ratios on its components but still gives one A-unit in each coordinate and the fixed constant h. Dual-number rings are allowed because relative recognition and the root polynomial theorem, rather than fibrewise comparison of arbitrary morphisms, control nilpotents. The characteristic-p power map of a torus has kernel mu_p, and the rank-one central isogeny SL_2 to PGL_2 in characteristic 2 has a non-smooth central kernel: none of the new statements excludes either. The new recognition theorem also permits non-split groups; it requires centrality in every geometric fibre, which is essential.
