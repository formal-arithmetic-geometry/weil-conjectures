### Returned step 1

Put $F=g^{-1}I$. The continuous-cocontinuous adjunction theorem [lem-weil-pbc-continuous-cocontinuous-topos-adjunction] identifies the underlying set sheaf of $F$ with $V\mapsto I(u(V))$, including restriction maps. This identification also respects the abelian group operations: apply the same inverse-image functor to the addition, zero and negation maps of $I$; its finite-product comparison identifies addition with the componentwise addition on $I(u(V))$. Thus $F(V)=I(u(V))$ as abelian groups, naturally in $V$.

### Returned step 2

Fix any covering $\mathcal U=\{U_t\to U\}_{t\in T}$ of $\mathcal C$. By continuity [def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology](3), $u(\mathcal U)=\{u(U_t)\to u(U)\}_{t\in T}$ is a covering of $\mathcal D$. For a tuple $\mathbf t=(t_0,\ldots,t_n)$ write $W_{\mathbf t}=U_{t_0}\times_U\cdots\times_U U_{t_n}$ and $P_{\mathbf t}=u(U_{t_0})\times_{u(U)}\cdots\times_{u(U)}u(U_{t_n})$, with the choices of [def-weil-pbc-site-cech-complex]. The canonical comparison $c_{\mathbf t}:u(W_{\mathbf t})\to P_{\mathbf t}$ is invertible by induction on $n$: for $n=0$ it is the identity (up to the chosen product isomorphism); to append $t_n$, apply continuity (3)(b) to the original covering and the map $W_{(t_0,\ldots,t_{n-1})}\to U$, then use the preceding comparison. For each projection $\delta_j$ omitting one factor, $c_{\mathbf t\setminus t_j}\,u(\delta_j)=\delta_j\,c_{\mathbf t}$, because both sides have the same projections to every remaining factor and the product universal property makes such a map unique.

### Returned step 3

In degree $n\ge0$, step 1 identifies $\check{\mathcal C}^n(\mathcal U,F)$ with $\prod_{\mathbf t\in T^{n+1}}I(u(W_{\mathbf t}))$. Applying $I(c_{\mathbf t}^{-1})$ componentwise gives an isomorphism to $\prod_{\mathbf t\in T^{n+1}}I(P_{\mathbf t})=\check{\mathcal C}^n(u(\mathcal U),I)$. These are the groups and restriction differentials of [def-weil-pbc-site-cech-complex]. The projection identity of step 2, contravariance of $I$, and the restriction formula of step 1 show that each omitting-factor restriction commutes with these isomorphisms. Hence their alternating sums commute too, so we obtain an isomorphism of Cech complexes (both are zero in negative degrees). Its inverse induces inverse maps on kernels modulo images. Consequently $\check H^p(\mathcal U,F)\cong\check H^p(u(\mathcal U),I)$. Since $I$ is an injective abelian sheaf on $\mathcal D$ and $u(\mathcal U)$ is a covering, [lem-weil-pbc-site-injective-cech-acyclic] makes the right side zero for every $p>0$. The common universe contains both small sites, their covering indices and the abelian-group values, as required by that lemma.

### Returned step 4

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-site-cech-vanishing-criterion] on $\mathcal C$ with $\mathcal B=\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal C)$, $\mathrm{Cov}$ the site's entire set of coverings, and the abelian sheaf $F$. All covering objects and all their iterated fibre products belong to $\mathcal B$. The coverings in $\mathrm{Cov}$ are cofinal, since any covering refines itself using the identity map on its index set and on each member. The required positive Cech vanishing for every covering is step 3. The criterion therefore gives $H^p(U,F)=0$ for every object $U$ and every $p>0$, with $H^p$ the ordinary right derived functor of evaluation specified in its statement. Substituting $F=g^{-1}I$ proves the assertion.
