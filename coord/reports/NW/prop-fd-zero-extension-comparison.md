2. **Boundary.** The all-stage open–closed theorem states exactly `j_!j^*P → P → i_*i^*P` and its Weil equivariance. I also read `det-sf-open-closed-sequence-ring-sheaf` for exact open extension and closed direct image over an arbitrary ring sheaf, `det-sf-open-closed-pullback-limits` for exact closed restriction, and `prop-weil-localized-tstructure` for the rational heart as the essential image of the integral heart under t-exact localization. These make degree-zero preservation explicit; all schemes in this use are qcqs and the open immersion is quasi-compact. Scalar-compatible standard truncations pass these sheaf calculations to the common-stage heart. The heart-triangle lemma's clause (5) then supplies the required long exact sequence. The decomposition over the finitely many disjoint closed points is the product formula for sheaf sections; it is written inline. I denote the actual restriction sheaf by `Ecal_s` and its chosen stalk by `E_s`, so the nonrational point is not replaced by one geometric skyscraper.
3. **Point geometry and integrand.** The finite-field Galois lemma states separability, the finite-separable-extension leaf gives an étale spectrum map, and the étale and finite base-change leaves apply to its base change along the dual line. Freeness of `κ(s)[ξ]` of rank d(s) gives the degree. `def-fd-kernel` gives the restricted function `sξ`; `prop-kernel-lisse` supplies rank one and finite Tor amplitude. The lisse-adic definition gives pullback and the tensor construction on the finite-level local constant modules. All schemes are finite type and separated, and all coefficients are the same common-stage coefficients.
4. **Transform of a point.** The closed-immersion properness leaf covers `i_s` and `i'_s`, and the finite-proper leaf covers `π_s`. The package clauses explicitly state proper base change, compact-support composition, proper coincidence and the projection formula with a finite-Tor second factor. That factor is precisely the Fourier kernel, whose finite Tor amplitude is in `prop-kernel-lisse`. The transform definition contributes exactly `[1]`, with no Tate twist. This is a calculation of a smaller input, not a new node restating a clause of the item.
5. **Correction/lissity.** `lem-a1-finite-map-lisse` states both equality of derived and ordinary pushforward and lissity for a finite étale map and a lisse rational Weil sheaf. These are exactly the hypotheses proved in Step 3. Finite direct sums preserve lissity by refining the finitely many local trivializations, and Fourier additivity follows from the stated triangulated functor. No one-clause duplicate lemma was introduced.
6. **Boundary weights.** Semicontinuity explicitly states the same punctual inequality for every boundary stalk. Its smooth, separated, geometrically connected curve, finite complement and positive rank hypotheses all hold on A1 and its dense open. Purity supplies the inequality with β=w; it is not treated as an unspecified global mixedness theorem.
7. **Powers on the coefficient module.** `prop-weil-operations` identifies the pullback Weil map, and the local-operator definition gives the degree-n iterate. `lem-trf-rational-local-frobenius-powers` states the power/conjugacy formula at E and explicitly does not require an integral invertible Weil lattice. The underlying geometric point components have free finite-stage lattices; that is the only lattice condition used. The spectral statement for powers is proved by factoring `T^r-ν`, so diagonalizability is unnecessary.
8. **Kernel weights.** The same local-power interface applies to the pullback along `f_s`. I use the trace theorem at its allowed slope `a=1` on the original line and then pull back; it is never applied with an unsupported slope in `κ(s)`. Rank one makes the trace the sole eigenvalue. The root-of-unity lemma gives complex modulus one, and the tensor definition states the tensor Weil map. Thus the twist preserves the bound already proved for the coefficient stalk.
9. **Finite-fibre comparison.** I use `Z0 = A'_s ×_{A'_0} Spec κ(y)`, which is finite over k0, and apply proper base change at the chosen lift of y. `lem-a1-finite-boundary-cohomology` states vanishing for every degree other than zero. `lem-a1-finite-weil-sections-decomposition` gives the natural evaluation isomorphism and the cyclic return identity for the full finite k0-scheme. Taking the summand for the chosen embedding of κ(y) gives the fibre stalk sum. The comparison maps are Weil-equivariant, and the local-operator definition makes F_y the degree-d(y) iterate. Its cycles have length d(z)/d(y), and the cited return identity gives F_z after that many steps. All indexing schemes are finite; no large-index colimit is used.
10. **Eigenvalue estimate.** This elementary step uses the established equations in Steps 8–9 only. Projection of an eigenvector onto a nonzero component, followed by the positive e-th root of the real inequality, gives the bound with q_y, not q_s. No eigenvalue decomposition or semisimplicity is needed.

The partner comparison is an explicit hypothesis (its existence is the existing middle-extension proposition cited in the statement).
