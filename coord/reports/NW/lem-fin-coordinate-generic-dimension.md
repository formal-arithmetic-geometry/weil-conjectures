## Review by NW

I checked B–E against the opened source and node statements. There are no new nodes to audit for hollow statements, duplicated clauses, missing searches, or false new hypotheses. All six proof steps have explicit source locations, Boolean elementary flags and exact statement quotes in citations.json; proof_deps is the union of uses. The imported fields and chapter/unit records are preserved. No compiler or Lean write was used.

Step 1 uses the integral-scheme definition to obtain domain charts; def-sc-function-field explicitly covers general integral schemes, not only curves. Relative affine space gives R[T] and the ordinary fiber definition gives the residue-field fiber. The fiber-product universal property identifies the iterated products. This is a direct calculation, with K(T) defined as fractions in the coordinate variable. The generic point of affine space lies over the generic point of S, so this is precisely the claimed fiber, even for a non-Noetherian S.

Step 2 takes composition of immersions and locally finite type morphisms from proper-fp-permanence (e), and base change plus local finite type of immersions from immersion-base-change-lft (1),(2). Its hypotheses apply to X open in the closed Y. The polynomial chart gives local finite type of affine space. lft-affine-pairs explicitly says every affine open over Spec K has finitely generated coordinate ring. This avoids the unjustified global finite-type assertion over S.

Step 3 specializes finite-affine-tensor-base-change to a singleton, so no coproduct finiteness issue remains. fibre-ring-localization-quotient applies to the zero prime of K[T], whose residue field is K(T), and its ideal term is zero. Its statement provides the localization isomorphism, with structural maps. Finite generation over K(T) is written out by fractions, using the finite-type-algebra definition. The immersion-base-change statement and the ordinary fiber's subspace homeomorphism show that these affine fibers are open and cover F; no theorem about global sections alone is mistaken for an affine-scheme identification.

Step 4 uses all three precise clauses of localization-prime-residue-field-iso: primes avoiding W and the compatible residue-field isomorphism. Closed-irreducible-dimension-trdeg applies to U=Spec C, a finite type K-scheme from step 2, and to V(p) with generic point p. It does not require U reduced. The bound by dim X_K is justified by closing chains from the open U, preserving strictness on reintersection. This step never assumes that p is minimal or that an irreducible component was already constructed.

Step 5 invokes the Mathlib-leaf tower statement with the actual compatible maps K → K(T) → E. The single transcendental variable gives degree one by the transcendence-degree definition. The preceding bound makes the degrees finite, so cardinal addition becomes ordinary addition. The second dimension comparison applies over K(T) to the finite type scheme Spec D and V(Q). Compatibility over K(T) follows from compatibility with t and its polynomial inverses, not from an unsubstantiated assertion that field isomorphisms respect all scalars.

Step 6 uses scheme-sober for generic points and the dimension definition for the supremum of finite chains. In an open chart containing the smallest generic point, every larger generic point is a generalization and belongs to the chart. Each intersection has the same closure as the original closed set; therefore strictness survives. This proves the global bound without a hidden finite-component or dimension-open-cover theorem. The step is elementary after the cited generic-point existence and definitions.

Edge cases checked: X empty; m=0 (there are no coordinates); n=0 (no surviving prime can exist); a coordinate algebraic on a component (a nonzero polynomial is inverted and eliminates it); affine space with a genuine coordinate (equality n−1); reducible sources with both constant and dominant components; nilpotent thickenings (the prime/residue and chain argument never assumes reducedness). The base can be non-Noetherian, since all finite generation used is over its function field. Generic tool uses of lft-affine-pairs, singleton affine tensor base change and localization residue fields are declared in split_report, without replacing the unit's route.
