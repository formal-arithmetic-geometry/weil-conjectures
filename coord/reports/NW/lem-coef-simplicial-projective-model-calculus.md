### Original Step 6

Define the tensor and cotensor of diagrams pointwise, with action maps induced by tensor associativity and by its adjunction, respectively. Their mapping simplicial set is the equalizer
$$\prod_i\underline{\operatorname{Map}}(P_i,Q_i)\rightrightarrows\prod_{i,j}\underline{\operatorname{Map}}_{\mathsf{sSet}}\bigl(I(i,j),\underline{\operatorname{Map}}(P_i,Q_j)\bigr).$$
The two arrows compose the family of maps with the actions of $P$ and $Q$. A map from a parameter $K$ to this equalizer is exactly a family $K\otimes P_i\to Q_i$ satisfying enriched naturality, which verifies the asserted tensor and cotensor adjunctions and the enriched-end description. For a generating arrow $F_i(a)$ in either $S_I$ or $T_I$ and a simplicial cofibration $b$, the pushout product is $F_i(a\mathbin\square b)$, since $F_i$ commutes with tensors and colimits. The simplicial model axiom in $\mathcal A$ makes $a\mathbin\square b$ a cofibration, trivial if $a$ or $b$ is trivial; $F_i$ preserves these two classes by Step 2's lifting adjunction. Here is the extension from generators to arbitrary projective (trivial) cofibrations, without an assertion that pushout product literally preserves composites. For $b:K\to L$ and any diagram arrow $p:X\to Y$, form the cotensor corner map
$$p^b:X^L\longrightarrow X^K\times_{Y^K}Y^L.$$
The tensor/cotensor adjunction gives a bijection between lifting squares, and their fillers, from $c\mathbin\square b$ to $p$ and from $c$ to $p^b$. If $b$ is a cofibration and $p$ is an objectwise trivial fibration, the calculation for $F_i(a)$ with $a\in S$ makes every $S_I$ generator lift against $p^b$. Thus $p^b\in S_I^{\square}$, and every $c\in C_I$ lifts against it by definition. It follows that $c\mathbin\square b\in C_I$. If $b$ is trivial and $p$ is any objectwise fibration, the generating pushout products are trivial, so again $p^b\in S_I^{\square}$; hence $c\mathbin\square b$ lifts against every objectwise fibration and belongs to $D_I$. Finally, for a simplicial cofibration $b$ and an objectwise fibration $p$, the calculation with $a\in T_0$ gives $p^b\in T_I^{\square}$. Every $c\in D_I$ therefore lifts against $p^b$, so $c\mathbin\square b\in D_I$. By Step 5, $D_I$ is precisely the projective trivial cofibrations. This proves the simplicial model axiom for the actual displayed mapping sets of $\mathcal A^I$ by tensor adjunction [def-coef-simplicial-tensor-cotensor] and the model lifting systems [lem-coef-simplicial-boundary-collapse-test]. Evaluation preserves cofibrations and trivial cofibrations by Step 4, and fibrations by their definition. Hence a projectively cofibrant fibrant diagram has cofibrant fibrant values, since initial and terminal diagrams are pointwise initial and terminal.

### Original Step 14

For completeness construct the ordinary localization used for the final reduction, without the enhanced-localization nodes. For any simplicial model category $\mathcal M$, let $H(\mathcal M)$ have bifibrant objects and morphisms $\pi_0\operatorname{Map}_{\mathcal M}(X,Y)$. These mapping sets are Kan by the simplicial model axiom; their compositions are well-defined on components. Steps 10 and 11 say precisely that a map between these objects becomes invertible in $H(\mathcal M)$ if and only if it is a model weak equivalence: an inverse component is represented by a vertex and the inverse equations are finite homotopy zigzags.
Choose functorial cofibrant and fibrant replacements $QX\to X$, $X\to RX$ from the weak factorization systems [lem-coef-simplicial-boundary-collapse-test]. Naturality and two-out-of-three show that $Q,R$ preserve all weak equivalences. The composite $RQ$ takes values in bifibrant objects, since $QX\to RQX$ is a cofibration. Thus $q:X\mapsto RQX$, followed by the component quotient, inverts weak equivalences. Enlarge universes and form the ordinary localization $\mathcal L$ explicitly by finite strings of arrows and formal inverses of weak equivalences, modulo the composition, identity and inverse relations. Its universal property follows by sending a string to the corresponding composite. The functor $q$ factors through $\mathcal L$.
Conversely, the functor from bifibrant objects to $\mathcal L$ identifies the two ends of every simplicial homotopy: if the target $Y$ is fibrant, both evaluations $Y^{\Delta^1}\to Y$ are trivial fibrations by the simplicial model axiom and [lem-coef-kan-quillen-right-proper]. Their common constant-path section is a weak equivalence, so their images in $\mathcal L$ are the same inverse of that section. Tensor/cotensor adjunction [def-coef-simplicial-tensor-cotensor] now identifies the endpoint maps. Hence this functor descends to $H(\mathcal M)$. The natural zigzag $X\leftarrow QX\to RQX$ consists of weak equivalences and identifies the composite $\mathcal L\to H(\mathcal M)\to\mathcal L$ with the identity. For bifibrant $X$, $QX$ is itself bifibrant, since $QX\to X$ is a fibration; the same zigzag lies entirely among bifibrant objects. It gives the inverse natural identification on $H(\mathcal M)$, with naturality checked on vertex representatives, which represent every morphism there. Thus $\mathcal L\simeq H(\mathcal M)$.
In particular a map $a$ of $\mathcal M$ is weak exactly when its image in $\mathcal L$ is invertible: invertibility is equivalent to that of $[RQa]$, hence to $RQa$ being weak by Steps 10-11, hence to $a$ being weak by the two natural replacement squares. Moreover every morphism in $\mathcal L$ between bifibrant representatives is represented by an actual map. This establishes the ordinary saturation and representation facts here, rather than citing a node whose enhanced dependencies lead to straightening.

### Original Step 15

For cofibrant $P$ and fibrant $Q$ in a simplicial model category, the natural map from $\pi_0\operatorname{Map}(P,Q)$ to the ordinary localization Hom set is bijective. Indeed choose a trivial cofibration $P\to RP$ and a trivial fibration $CQ\to Q$ with $CQ$ cofibrant. Both $RP,CQ$ are bifibrant. The maps $\operatorname{Map}(RP,CQ)\to\operatorname{Map}(RP,Q)\to\operatorname{Map}(P,Q)$ are trivial Kan fibrations, by the simplicial model axiom and [def-coef-simplicial-tensor-cotensor], and therefore induce bijections of components [lem-coef-kan-quillen-right-proper]. Step 14 identifies the first component set with the Hom between these bifibrant objects in the localization, and the replacement arrows identify their endpoints with $P,Q$. Endpoint evaluations of cotensor paths, as in Step 14, show that these identifications send each actual map to its localization class. This also proves naturality wherever source cofibrancy and target fibrancy hold.
Now consider any simplicial Quillen adjunction $L:\mathcal M\rightleftarrows\mathcal N:U$ between such models. The Brown split factorizations [lem-coef-brown-split-factorizations] show that $L$ preserves weak equivalences between cofibrant objects: write a weak equivalence $f$ between them as $pi$ with $p$ trivial fibration and a cofibration section $s$; two-out-of-three makes both $i$ and $s$ trivial cofibrations. Thus $L(i),L(s)$ are weak, $L(p)L(s)=1$ makes $L(p)$ weak, and $L(f)$ is weak. Dually $U$ preserves weak equivalences between fibrant objects. Consequently $LQ$ and $UR$ preserve all weak equivalences and descend to functors on the ordinary localizations. For cofibrant $P$ and fibrant $Q$ the simplicial adjunction and the preceding component calculation give
$$\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf LL(P),Q)=\pi_0\operatorname{Map}(LP,Q)=\pi_0\operatorname{Map}(P,UQ)=\operatorname{Hom}(P,\mathbf RU(Q)).$$
For arbitrary objects insert the chosen replacements. Naturality of the adjunction bijections and the replacement arrows proves naturality for every actual map; it then holds for formal inverses and composites, so these are natural bijections on the ordinary localizations. They define an ordinary derived adjunction. Its unit and counit are the ordinary adjunction maps with replacement arrows inverted, because the bijections send identities to those maps; their triangle identities follow from the same bijections. A functor already preserving all weak equivalences has its direct localized functor, by its replacement natural weak equivalence. This is an ordinary adjunction argument, with no assertion about infinity localizations.

### Original Step 17

Reduce an arbitrary DK equivalence $v:I\to J$ to the case just proved, using [lem-coef-simplicial-interval-lifting]. If $I$ is empty, essential surjectivity forces $J$ to be empty and both diagram categories are terminal, so all assertions hold. Otherwise let $O=\operatorname{Ob}I\amalg\operatorname{Ob}J$. In the fixed-object model category $\mathrm{sCat}_O$, factor the map from the disjoint union $I\amalg J$ to the category having mapping set $\Delta^0$ between every pair of elements of $O$ as a cofibration $I\amalg J\to D$ followed by a trivial fibration. The latter is a trivial Kan fibration on every mapping set, so all $D(x,y)$ are nonempty contractible Kan complexes. The cofibration is mappingwise monic: by the cited node it is an arrow retract of a degreewise free extension; a degreewise free category extension includes the old arrows: its arrows are finite words in old arrows and new generators, reduced only by the old composition and identity relations, so two old arrows cannot become equal, and a retract of an injection is injective (if two source elements have the same image, apply the arrow-retract inclusion, use injectivity there, then the retraction). Write $d_I:I\to D$ and $d_J:J\to D$ for its restrictions. Set $K=J\times D$, $p:K\to J$, $g=(v,d_I):I\to K$, and $s=(1_J,d_J):J\to K$. Every mapping map of $p$ is a trivial Kan fibration, being the pullback of $D(x,y)\to\Delta^0$; on $\pi_0$, $p$ is fully faithful and essentially surjective since $O$ is nonempty and $\pi_0D$ has one arrow between any two objects. Hence $p$ is a DK equivalence. The identities $pg=v$ and $ps=1_J$ show that $g,s$ are DK equivalences as well (on mapping sets by two-out-of-three; on $\pi_0$ because a composite and one factor are equivalences). Their mapping maps are monomorphisms: for $g$ its second coordinate is injective, and for $s$ its first coordinate is the identity. This supplies exactly the reduction of HTT A.3.3.8 without imposing local Kan conditions on the original $I$ or $J$. The Kan properties used here are those of [lem-coef-kan-quillen-right-proper], and DK terminology is the inline definition in the item. The fixed-object model of [lem-coef-simplicial-interval-lifting] and its retract-of-free-extension clause have no straightening dependency.
