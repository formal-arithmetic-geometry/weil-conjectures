### Returned step 1

Apply the directed local tower theorem to R→S→S and M. Both targets are essentially of finite presentation over R by hypothesis, and the second map is the local identity. Write the resulting tower R_i→A_i→S_i. For fixed-arrow realization the exact inverse transition formula is x⊗b↦(x⊗1)⊗b, and the two composites are identities by balancing. The same formula works at the limit. On a pure tensor the arrow square has value v(x)⊗a_{kj}(a) on both sides. Tensoring a finite cokernel presentation works because maps out of the tensored cokernel are exactly balanced maps annihilating the image of the tensored relation map. This is a universal-property calculation, with no flatness hypothesis. To prove the additive colimit, a compatible family of additive maps induces a Z-bilinear map on (x,a) by representing a at a stage; eventual equality gives independence. Additivity and A_c-balancing follow at a common stage, giving the map out of P⊗A. Uniqueness follows on pure tensors, hence on their finite sums. The directed quotient construction then gives the stated eventual equality, also for sums of pure tensors; no assertion that every tensor is itself pure is used.
