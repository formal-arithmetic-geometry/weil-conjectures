Step 1 uses the actual statement of `lem-weil-pbc-ring-localization-universal-property`, which gives both extension and uniqueness for an arbitrary multiplicative set. I checked the required denominator condition directly: the isomorphism and its inverse preserve annihilator-zero elements, and these elements form a multiplicative set. Their images are units in the target localization. The two extensions compose to the identity because they agree there on the original rings, precisely the uniqueness clause this cited node states. The fraction maps are defined for total rings of fractions even when H has zero divisors; no fraction-field or domain hypothesis is slipped in. Injectivity of each base map follows directly from its zero-fraction equivalence and cancellation by a denominator.

Step 2 uses `def-weil-pbc-integral-closure`, whose statement says exactly that integral elements satisfy a monic equation over the base ring. Applying F to the equation maps its coefficients by the given ring isomorphism; applying the inverse supplies surjectivity on closures. This uses a varying-base ring isomorphism, not the fixed-base algebra-equivalence hypothesis of Mathlib's mapIntegralClosure. Step 3 uses no black box: the formula on additive quotient classes is checked for representative independence, inverse, additivity, and the precise semilinear identity. H is a submodule of its normalization, so this quotient and its scalar action are defined.

Step 4 cites `def-weil-pbc-module-length`, whose statement defines length by strict submodule chains. The written image/preimage formulas verify closure under every target scalar by pulling that scalar back through the surjective ring isomorphism. This proves a full order isomorphism, hence a lattice isomorphism, and a length-preserving bijection on strict chains, without any k-linearity. It also checks the same correspondence for the integral closures themselves; finite length is claimed only for their defects.

Step 5 cites that length definition and `lem-weil-pbc-finite-length-module-simple-filtration`. I checked that this theorem requires just a commutative ring and finite module length; the dimension bound on all strict chains establishes exactly that hypothesis here. Its statement provides simple factors, and I do not take the equality of the number of factors with length from its proof. Step 6 cites the actual `def-weil-pbc-simple-module` and length definition. For each nonzero simple factor, a cyclic generator gives a surjection H to the factor. Its kernel is maximal by the explicit ideal/image argument, and therefore is the unique maximal ideal of H. The structural residue map k to that quotient is an isomorphism by the item's hypothesis; this is exactly where that hypothesis is used to get dimension one. The step proves the small cyclic-generator fact in full, without quoting an uncited classification lemma or needing existence of another maximal ideal.

Step 7 uses `lem-weil-pbc-length-additive-short-exact`, whose statement gives length additivity with injective first map, surjective second map, and image equal to kernel. The inclusion and quotient maps in each filtration have precisely those three properties. Induction from the zero module therefore computes the length; lifting a one-element basis of each vector-space quotient computes the k-dimension. This is done independently for the two structural k-actions. Step 8 uses only the coordinate equation e_i(e_i-1)=0 in each nonzero domain: singleton supports are exactly the primitive nonzero idempotents. Preservation and reflection of orthogonal decompositions are written out using E and its inverse. Step 9 checks the scalar identities explicitly under the additional k-algebra hypothesis.
