## Source error

STATEMENT-DISPUTED (previous child only, now repaired): the claim that the stable locus is generization-stable is false for nonproper curves. An explicit test is S = Spec k[s,t], with empty marking, X = (A^1_x x S) minus the closed point (x,s,t)=(0,0,0), and Y the restriction to X of the blow-up of A^1_x x S along (x,s). Its curve fibres are flat and nodal; the pair map is proper and generically an isomorphism. Over the generic point of the divisor s=0 it has an exceptional P^1 meeting the strict transform once. Over (s,t)=(0,0) the entire exceptional fibre has been removed from the target and its preimage, so the map is an isomorphism on the punctured smooth fibre. Thus the origin is stable while its generization (s) is not. The stable set is still constructible. Temkin 5.1(ii) itself asserts constructibility, not the false generization assertion. The item's own statement was and remains correct.

For the field-invariance node I read the geometric definitions in §1.2 on printed p. 2 and the explicit equivalence-under-field-extension assumption of Remark 3.1.1(i)(a),(ii), printed pp. 14--15. Here is the mathematical justification I checked, kept in this report rather than an unsplit node's proof field: embed an algebraic closure of the smaller field into an algebraically closed extension of the larger field. Smoothness and etaleness of finite-type schemes ascend and descend under field extension, and the nodal geometric condition is unchanged under algebraically closed extension. Over an algebraically closed field the geometric irreducible components remain components after such extension. A contracted P^1 component remains a contracted P^1 and its finitely many marked/singular special points remain distinct with the same incidences. Conversely the required component, an isomorphism with P^1, contraction equality, and finite special-point data descend to a finitely generated subalgebra of the larger algebraically closed field. After localizing to preserve the finitely many inverse identities and distinctness conditions, specialize to a rational closed point of that subalgebra over the smaller algebraically closed field. It gives the exceptional component downstairs. This establishes reflection as well as ascent; the existing base-change node supplied ascent alone and was not cited for reflection. This is independent of valuation approximation, so the new node does not restate the item.
