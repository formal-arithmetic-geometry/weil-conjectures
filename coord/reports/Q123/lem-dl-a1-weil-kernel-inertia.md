For step 1 I opened lem-sc-smooth-normal, lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian and rem-pc-regular-normal. The first asserts normality over any field, not just an algebraically closed field; the second applies over the Noetherian spectrum of the finite field; clause (2) of the third gives disjoint open integral components, so connected nonempty C_0 is integral. I deliberately did not use lem-sc-smooth-connected-curve-local-rings, whose field must be algebraically closed. The open U_0 has the same function field. I corrected the generic base point to Spec of an algebraic closure containing the separable closure, matching def-weil-geometric-point; purely inseparable extension does not change field automorphisms.

For step 2 the new 0BTD node gives both surjections and both kernels. I checked the statement of lem-galois-pi1-functoriality: its equality of actions on pulled-back fibres gives b=fa, not just an abstract existence of group homomorphisms. At points in U_0 the actual local rings in U_0 and C_0 coincide, so their strict henselizations and their chosen embeddings may be chosen identically. No normalization/localization compatibility theorem is silently required.

For step 3 the new 0BSW node identifies Aut(Omega/K_x^sh) with the kernel of the residue map for the absolute Galois group of K_x=Frac(O_{C_0,x}^h). This is exactly the missing comparison, not the definition of inertia alone. I opened d-real-boundary: it specifies that same henselization, the residue kernel, the path-transport construction, and that another path conjugates the homomorphism. I read the functoriality statement again and checked that both constructions act on each finite generic fibre by the same field automorphism. Thus matching the subgroups does not rely on their names, on a stalk comparison or on the rejected Lean attachment. Choosing different paths only changes generators by P-conjugation before forming their P-normal closure.

Step 4 is the elementary quotient argument, written out: lift an element of ker(f) along a; its lift lies in N_C. The preimage of any candidate closed normal subgroup contains all J_x, including the ones on U_0 killed by a. This verifies both inclusions rather than simply asserting that kernels commute with quotients. Closedness of a(N_C) follows from compactness in the profinite source and Hausdorffness in P. The step uses the statement of the new 0BTD node, not anything hidden in its future proof.

For step 5 I opened lem-wf-dense-open-pi1-surjective and used only its degree-compatible Weil restriction clause (2), not its later extension-of-sheaves clauses that could obscure a cycle. I opened def-wf-fundamental-representation: it gives the pullback in G times discrete Z, and says the degree-zero geometric subgroup is open and has its profinite topology. This is why a Weil kernel equals N and why a closed subgroup H contained in degree zero is compact and then closed in P, despite the different topologies on the whole Weil and arithmetic groups. I also checked lem-real-boundary-decomposition-landing, whose actual conclusion puts the prescribed inertia in the geometric subgroup.

For step 6 I opened lem-real-boundary-local-constants: it says both that kappa(x)/k0 is finite and that the image of the chosen local decomposition group under the arithmetic projection is Gal(k/kappa(x)). I initially used procyclic notation, then replaced it by the finite statement lem-sfb-finite-field-galois-frobenius, whose hypotheses and cyclic-generator conclusion I read. Applying that lemma to the finite extension kappa(x)/k0 supplies an integer n matching r(g) on kappa(x); its inverse generator is geometric Frobenius. The explicit choice of d and calculation w=gd^{-1} prove that every P-conjugate of the prescribed inertia is a Weil-conjugate. Normality inside local decomposition follows directly from the residue-kernel definition already identified in step 3. Thus H contains every conjugate and its closed generated subgroup N. No density assertion with the wrong topology is needed. Each elementary step is a direct subgroup or topology calculation from the earlier steps and the cited statements; there is no additional comparison lemma hidden in these calculations.
