## Source and edge-case details for the new prerequisites

For `lem-sfb-abelian-degree-multiplicative`, I read Milne I §7, printed p.32, where isogeny degree is identified with function-field degree, and Remark 10.11 and the use of multiplicativity in Lemma 10.22. Finite isogeny degrees multiply by the tower formula. A composite of two endomorphisms of the same-dimensional abelian variety can be an isogeny only if both are: surjectivity of the composite makes its first applied factor dominant onto a same-dimensional image and its second factor surjective, while a positive-dimensional kernel of the first factor survives in the composite. Thus the zero extension is multiplicative. This is a genuine geometric lemma, not part of the definition of degree. The identity has degree one; the zero map has degree zero when (g>0). The positive-dimension hypothesis avoids assigning degree zero to the unique map on the trivial abelian variety, whose degree is one.

For `lem-sfb-multiplicative-polynomial-norm`, I read Milne I Proposition 10.13 and the proof of Theorem 10.9, printed pp.47–48, and Lemma 10.22, printed p.51. In coordinates, polynomiality gives a scalar extension of δ; its normalization, multiplication and homogeneity identities extend because they are polynomial identities over the infinite field ℚ. Homogeneity makes the coefficient of (t^d) in δ(t1−u) equal to δ(1)=1. Factoring (F) over a splitting field and using multiplicativity proves the root-product formula, including its leading coefficient; homogeneity supplies the factor (c^d) for a nonmonic (F). The signs cancel when (P_u(t)=δ(t1−u)) is used. For integrality, let the monic integral (H) annihilate (u). If (a) is a root of (P_u) but (H(a)≠0), divide (H(T)−H(a)) by (T−a): evaluation at (u) shows (u-a) is invertible in the scalar-extended algebra. Multiplicativity forces its δ-value to be nonzero, contradicting (P_u(a)=0). Hence every root of (P_u) is an algebraic integer. Its rational coefficients are elementary symmetric functions of integral roots and therefore integral over ℤ; rational algebraic integers lie in ℤ. This fills the integral-coefficient issue without the false assertion that integer-valued rational polynomials have integer coefficients. Tests: δ(x)=x^d on (E=ℚ) gives (P_u=(X-u)^d); determinant on matrix algebras gives the usual polynomial; (u=0) gives (X^d). The condition (d>0) is explicit.

For `lem-sfb-padic-resultant-rigidity`, I read Milne I Lemma 10.21 and its proof, printed p.51. Continuity extends the test identity from integer coefficients to ℤ_ℓ coefficients; multiplying by a power of ℓ then extends it to ℚ_ℓ coefficients, since both polynomials have the same degree. Testing the minimal polynomial of a point near one root, and using conjugate invariance of the extended absolute value, identifies its multiplicity. No squarefreeness hypothesis is used: repeated roots are the content of the argument. The degree-zero case is (P=Q=1), and the zero test polynomial is harmless. Norms are zero at zero; the statement does not use the integer-valued valuation at zero.

For `lem-sfb-abelian-degree-tate-valuation`, I read Milne I Proposition 10.20, printed p.50, and its first proof display on printed p.52. For an isogeny the ℓ-primary part of its finite kernel is étale because ℓ differs from the characteristic; the prime-to-ℓ part and the connected characteristic-primary part have ℓ-adic absolute value one. The Tate cokernel has the order of the ℓ-primary kernel, and its order is the index determinant of the integral Tate map. This gives precisely the displayed absolute-value equality, including inseparable isogenies. For a non-isogeny, the identity component of its reduced kernel over the algebraically closed field is a positive-dimensional abelian subvariety; its nonzero rational Tate module injects into the kernel of the map on (V_ℓA). Thus that map has determinant zero, matching the degree-zero convention. Tests: for ([n]), degree (n^{2g}) and determinant (n^{2g}) agree; for zero on (g>0), both vanish; for characteristic-(p) Frobenius and ℓ≠p, the equality concerns norms (both are one), and does not identify the full degree with the number of geometric kernel points.
