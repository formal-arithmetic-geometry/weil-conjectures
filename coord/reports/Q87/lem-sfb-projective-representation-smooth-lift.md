I checked this split against delivery-errors B–E with all eighteen cited node statements open. Each proof step is under 1300 characters, so none exceeds the long-step threshold. The component-image paragraph is a single elementary finite-closed-cover argument using the cited lemmas, not a new black-box assertion.

In step 1 the fibre-product definition provides the rank-one identification E=G, and the smooth-geometrically-reduced node applies to G -> Spec F, giving reducedness. Determinant on a rank-one vector space is the scalar itself, hence SL_1 and PGL_1 are trivial. This separate case is necessary because the matrix-cover node assumes n>=2.

In step 2 the matrix-cover statement explicitly gives finite surjective SL_n -> PGL_n and its full scalar mu_n kernel over any field. The projective-frames statement gives local matrix lifts modulo scalars, so the quotient convention matches the item's fppf quotient. The fibre-product definition supplies existence, compatible projections, and the uniqueness used to check componentwise group laws. The affine-spectra statement gives affineness. The finite-base-change tool states stability for all finite morphisms, not just radicial ones; the surjectivity-base-change node states arbitrary base change. Finite type over F follows by combining finite module generators with the finite set of algebra generators for F[G], and requires no unstated smoothness of E.

In step 3 the locally algebraic identity-component statement explicitly includes (E^0)_red as a smooth connected finite-type group and gives the unchanged underlying space. Its hypotheses hold because F is algebraically closed and E is a finite-type group. Restricting the nilradical quotient to the open identity component identifies this closed subscheme with H=(E_red)^0. The reduction definition and affine quotient description justify the identification and affineness. Closed immersions are finite by the cited node, and the finite-morphism tool supplies composition, making q finite.

In step 4 the component-translation node explicitly gives finitely many components and translation representatives in E(F), without requiring E reduced or smooth. The finite closed-map node states that the image of every closed subset is closed, so it applies to E^0 inside E, not only to the whole source. The identity-component node applied separately to connected G gives irreducibility of G. Equivariance of p identifies all component images as translates of Z=p(E^0); their finite closed cover forces a translate to be G. Translation back gives Z=G. This proves actual topological surjectivity before applying any flatness criterion. The unchanged spaces from step 3 then give q surjective.

In step 5 the finite-geometric-point-lift statement explicitly permits the same algebraically closed field F and any finite surjective map q; it gives surjectivity on F-points. The affine-image statement explicitly equates that surjectivity with faithful flatness of the coordinate map when the target is smooth. Both groups are affine finite type and G is smooth as required. This generic geometric-point lemma is the only off-unit non-closed, non-Mathlib tool and is declared in split_report.tool_uses; it is not a proper-base-change proof route.

In step 6 the group-scheme definition states kernels as fibres over the identity, and the fibre-product definition proves ker p=ker s and ker q=H x_E ker p. The matrix-cover statement supplies the scheme-theoretic scalar mu_n, and closed-immersion base change makes ker q a closed subgroup of it. The explicit relations z^n=1 and z^{-1}=z^{n-1} give a finite spanning set for its coordinate algebra. The torus-subgroup node applies over algebraically closed F to this closed subgroup of G_m and explicitly states that finite-dimensional coordinate algebra makes its character group finite. It supplies exactly D(M) and the Hopf operations required, without replacing the kernel by its reduced scheme or its set of F-points.

In step 7 the second projection and its restriction are the homomorphisms already constructed, and the defining square gives the projectivization identity after precomposition with the inclusion. No compatibility theorem beyond that universal property is hidden.

I tested n=1, G=1, and the trivial representation (where H=G even though E=G x mu_n can be nonreduced and disconnected). For a=PGL_n's identity, E=SL_n and the full mu_n kernel persists, including in characteristic dividing n. For G=G_m with a(t)=class diag(t,1) in PGL_2, H=G_m and q(u)=u^2; in characteristic 2 its kernel is nonreduced mu_2. These cases verify why the proof must not assume that q is etale, that its kernel is reduced, or that the raw E is smooth. I found no statement dispute.
