## Review by Q87

I read Milne iAG2017 in the local PDF, using extracted text: the standing split hypothesis and Lemma 18.21 proof on printed p.392; the arbitrary-field split reductive setup of Section 21h, Proposition 21.70, Theorems 21.73 and 21.80 and the big-cell discussion on pp.446–451. This is the source of the sole new node. Milne's source theorem applies to split reductive groups, not just algebraically closed fields; the arguments sometimes pass to an algebraic closure to check scheme identities, which does not restrict the standing field. I also read Brion Lemma 2.3 online, where the desired field invariance is reduced to the root datum of a split reductive pair. I used Milne's divisor argument to expose this item's content instead of using that comparison as a black box. proof_origin is agent, because the independence and scalar-extension details are assembled here.

Step 1 uses the new Bruhat statement for the distinguished open cell and smooth projective geometrically integral quotient. I checked the actual statements of smooth-over-a-field regularity, projective-implies-proper, projectivity and properness definitions, and the divisor dictionary. Smoothness gives locally Noetherian regularity; the projectivity convention explicitly gives quasi-compactness; properness gives the separatedness required by the dictionary. No algebraic-closure or perfectness assumption is added. The projective-implies-proper theorem is declared as a generic tool of another unit.

Step 2 uses only the finite stratification from the new node, reduced-scheme-image (Stacks 056B, morphisms.tex lemma-scheme-theoretic-image-reduced) and flat scheme-image base change (081I, lemma-flat-base-change-scheme-theoretic-image). I opened both statements and the Stacks proofs. The complement is a finite union of irreducible closures because the open cell is a stratum and its complement is closed. Taking maximal closures therefore gives exactly the boundary components. Each affine cell is reduced, and its base change is affine space over L, so its scheme-theoretic image after scalar extension is the reduced irreducible closure. The stratum immersion is quasi-compact because Q is Noetherian. The extension is flat: on charts R tensor_F L is free over R. The surjective projection reflects and preserves the inclusions of the pulled-back closed sets, so maximal closures remain precisely the distinct maximal boundary components. This also supplies geometric integrality after arbitrary inseparable and transcendental extensions. No unproved general theorem about reduced base change is used. Reduced-image is declared as a generic tool.

Step 3 uses affine-open-boundary-codimension-one and regular-prime-divisor-local-equations. I opened both full statements, and read the Stacks lemma 0AGA in divisors.tex. The first has precisely the separated, locally Noetherian, dense affine hypotheses established in step 1; the second has precisely the integral regular locally Noetherian ambient and the integral codimension-one closed subscheme established in step 2. It concludes that the actual defining ideal is an effective Cartier ideal. This avoids assuming that a divisor arising from the Weil dictionary automatically has the required reduced closed subscheme structure. The same verification over each L proves the assertion for the actual scalar extensions. The prime-divisor theorem is declared as a generic tool of another unit.

Step 4 uses affine-space-invertible-trivial for any field, the regular divisor dictionary, and the divisor-line definition. I checked that the dictionary states both representation by a rational section and reconstruction of its line module. Trivializing M on the dense U produces that rational section, so its support is contained in the boundary. Distinct codimension-one boundary primes give the indicated finite integer combination. Tensor additivity, including negative powers, is written by multiplying the local Cartier equations, rather than being left as an uncited identification. The dual-ideal convention matches def-pc-divisor-line-bundle exactly.

Step 5 uses the same dictionary to turn a trivial line relation into a rational function and to deduce it is a unit on U. The polynomial-unit argument is elementary and written out with highest homogeneous terms; it works in every characteristic and d=0. Equality with a nonzero constant on dense U is equality in the function field. Thus its divisor is zero and the distinct prime coefficients vanish. This proves independence and does not assume it from the affine-cell generation lemma.

Step 6 uses the dictionary's flat-pullback clause, which explicitly gives an isomorphism of canonical divisor-line pairs, and the new Bruhat statement for the torus case. A field extension gives a flat projection, and local equations show that this pullback is the actual D_{i,L}. Repeating the preceding Picard argument over L proves the basis clause. The boundary indexing is already established in step 2 and is not assumed here.
