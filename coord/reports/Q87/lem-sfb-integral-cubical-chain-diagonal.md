## Preserved original argument and distribution

The following is the complete original proof text, retained verbatim as handoff material for the new unsplit nodes. These are report paragraphs, not proof steps of the new node files. Original steps 1 and 4 belong to lem-sfb-cubical-partition-graded-equivariance, 2 and 3 to lem-sfb-cubical-partition-boundary-expansion, and 5 and 6 to lem-sfb-graded-partition-counit-evaluation. The last node's general statement simply abstracts the support-of-augmentation argument; its cubical specialization is exactly the text below.

### Original step 1

Use the differential and augmentation of [lem-sfb-integral-lattice-cubical-contraction], including $\partial^2=0$ and $\varepsilon\partial_1=0$. Write $(C\otimes C)_n=\bigoplus_{a+b=n}C_a\otimes_{\mathbf Z}C_b$. The tensor differential is well-defined by bilinearity [def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product]. For homogeneous $x\in C_a,y\in C_b$ its square is $\partial^2x\otimes y+((-1)^{a-1}+(-1)^a)\partial x\otimes\partial y+x\otimes\partial^2y=0$; when $a=0$ the middle term is zero directly. Thus this is a chain complex [def-weil-pbc-module-chain-complex-homology]. The displayed formula for $\Delta[v,I]$ has $2^{|I|}$ terms, all of total degree $|I|$, and therefore extends uniquely to a degree-preserving homomorphism by the basis property [def-weil-pbc-free-module]. Set it to zero on zero source degrees. No contraction, exactness or comparison of resolutions is needed.

### Original step 2

For a fixed $i\in I$ and a disjoint partition $B\sqcup C=I\setminus\{i\}$, put $p=\#\{b\in B:b<i\}$, $q=\#\{c\in C:c<i\}$, $t=\#\{b\in B:i<b\}$ and $N=\#\{(b,c)\in B\times C:c<b\}$. Then $|B|=p+t$ and the position of $i$ in $I$ is $p+q$. In $\partial\Delta[v,I]$, removing $i$ from the first factor of the partition $(B\cup\{i\},C)$ gives sign $(-1)^{N+q+p}$. Removing $i$ from the second factor of the partition $(B,C\cup\{i\})$ gives sign $(-1)^{N+t+|B|+q}=(-1)^{N+p+q}$, including the tensor differential sign $(-1)^{|B|}$. These formulas follow by counting the extra inversion pairs involving $i$: there are $q$ in the first partition and $t$ in the second. Denote their common sign by $s=(-1)^{p+q}\epsilon(B,C)$.

### Original step 3

For the fixed $(i,B,C)$ of step 2, the two first-factor faces contribute $$s\bigl([v+u_i,B]\otimes[v+u_B+u_i,C]-[v,B]\otimes[v+u_B+u_i,C]\bigr),$$ and the two second-factor faces contribute $$s\bigl([v,B]\otimes[v+u_B+u_i,C]-[v,B]\otimes[v+u_B,C]\bigr).$$ The middle terms cancel. Every term of $\partial\Delta[v,I]$ belongs to exactly one such triple: its removed direction specifies $i$ and its surviving direction sets specify $B,C$. On the other hand the $(B,C)$ term of $\Delta\bigl((-1)^{p+q}([v+u_i,I\setminus\{i\}]-[v,I\setminus\{i\}])\bigr)$ is precisely the two surviving terms. Summing over $i,B,C$ proves $\partial\Delta=\Delta\partial$ on each basis vector. For $I=\varnothing$ both sides are zero. Equality on a basis gives equality everywhere; zero source degrees also give zero on both sides. This is the chain-map condition of [def-weil-pbc-module-chain-complex-homology].

### Original step 4

Let $\tau_w[v,I]=[v+w,I]$. In each summand of $\Delta\tau_w[v,I]$ the two bases are $v+w$ and $v+w+u_A$; in $(\tau_w\otimes\tau_w)\Delta[v,I]$ they are $v+w$ and $(v+u_A)+w$. They agree by addition in $\mathbf Z^r$, with the same direction sets and sign. Thus the two homomorphisms agree on every generator and $\Delta$ is equivariant for diagonal translation.

### Original step 5

Extend $\varepsilon$ by zero in positive degrees to a map $C\to\mathbf Z[0]$; it is a chain map because $\varepsilon\partial_1=0$ by [lem-sfb-integral-lattice-cubical-contraction], and every other target differential or component is zero. In $(\varepsilon\otimes1)\Delta[v,I]$ only $A=\varnothing$ survives, with sign $1$, and its value is $1\otimes[v,I]$. In $(1\otimes\varepsilon)\Delta[v,I]$ only $C=\varnothing$ survives, with sign $1$, and its value is $[v,I]\otimes1$. The unit identifications send these to $[v,I]$: explicitly $\mathbf Z\otimes M\to M$, $n\otimes x\mapsto nx$, has inverse $x\mapsto1\otimes x$, and likewise on the right; the composites are identities by bilinearity [def-weil-pbc-module-tensor-product]. These tensor maps commute with differentials by the displayed tensor differential and $\varepsilon\partial_1=0$. This proves both counit identities as equalities of chain maps.

### Original step 6

For $|I|>0$ every partition has a nonempty part, so $(\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon)\Delta[v,I]=0=\varepsilon[v,I]$. For $I=\varnothing$ there is one summand, and its image is $1\otimes1$, identified with $1$. Hence the double augmentation equals $\varepsilon$ in every degree and, by step 5 and the chain-map identity, as a chain map to $\mathbf Z[0]$. When $r=0$ there is just the vertex $[0,\varnothing]$, identified with $1\in\mathbf Z$, and the sole partition is empty in both parts. The formula is therefore $\Delta(1)=1\otimes1$; there are no positive source degrees.
