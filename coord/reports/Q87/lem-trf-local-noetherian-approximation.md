### Original step 1

Let $I$ be the set of finitely generated $\mathbf Z$-subalgebras $R_i\subseteq R$, ordered by inclusion. By [lemma-nagata-ring-finite-type-subalgebras] this is a small nonempty directed set and $R=\varinjlim_iR_i$. Apply [lem-kun-fp-algebra-descent] to $S/R$: choose $i$ and a finitely presented $R_i$-algebra $B_i$ with $S\cong B_i\otimes_{R_i}R$ as $R$-algebras. For every $j\geq i$ put $B_j=B_i\otimes_{R_i}R_j$, with transitions induced by the inclusions. The subalgebras containing $R_i$ are still directed and have union $R$: a finite set of elements together with generators of $R_i$ lies in one finitely generated subalgebra. Thus their colimit is still $R$.

Uses: lemma-nagata-ring-finite-type-subalgebras, lem-kun-fp-algebra-descent.

Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex — Tags 00R0 (lemma-limit-finite-presentation) and 05N9 (lemma-colimit-category-fp-algebras), finite-presentation descent

### Original step 2

The tensor base changes $R_j\to B_j$ are finitely presented by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-presentation-base-change-composition]. Use [lem-weil-pbc-colimit-flat-ring-map] for the directed base rings $R_j$ ($j\geq i$), initial algebras $R_i$ and $B_i$, and initial algebra homomorphism $R_i\to B_i$. Its limit map is $R\to R\otimes_{R_i}B_i\cong S$, which is flat by hypothesis. It gives $j_f\geq i$ such that every $R_j\to B_j$ with $j\geq j_f$ is flat. This application requires finite presentation of the algebra map, not finite presentation of $S$ as an $R$-module.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-finite-presentation-base-change-composition, lem-weil-pbc-colimit-flat-ring-map.

Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex — Tags 05G5 (lemma-base-change-finiteness), part (4), and 02JO (lemma-flat-finite-presentation-limit-flat), part (3); the flat input used in More on Morphisms 05FE

### Original step 3

Set $A=R_{j_f}$ and $B=B_{j_f}$. By [lem-trf-geometrically-reduced-fibre-locus] and [lem-trf-geometrically-irreducible-fibre-locus], the two loci $E_{\mathrm{red}},E_{\mathrm{irr}}\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A$ are constructible. Finite presentation, needed for these results, was proved in step 2; neither locus result needs a Noetherian base. By [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra], the morphism $\operatorname{Spec}S\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ is the base change of $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$; the same identification gives its fibres as the spectra of the stated fibre rings. The reduced-locus equality in the first cited locus node shows that the image of $\operatorname{Spec}R\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ lies in $E_{\mathrm{red}}$, because all given fibres are geometrically integral and hence geometrically reduced. For irreducibility apply [lem-sbc-radicial-preparation-fibre-comparison] with the identity universal homeomorphism of the base-changed scheme. It says exactly that a fibre over a point of $\operatorname{Spec}R$ is geometrically irreducible if and only if the original fibre at its image in $\operatorname{Spec}A$ is. Thus this image also lies in $E_{\mathrm{irr}}$. The implications from integral to reduced and irreducible are [def-pc-integral-scheme] applied after each field extension, using [def-pc-geometrically-irreducible].

Uses: lem-trf-geometrically-reduced-fibre-locus, lem-trf-geometrically-irreducible-fibre-locus, lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra, lem-sbc-radicial-preparation-fibre-comparison, def-pc-integral-scheme, def-pc-geometrically-irreducible.

Source: literature/stacks-project/more-morphisms.tex — Tags 05FG and 05FH, proofs: Tags 0579/055B for the loci and Tags 0576/0555 for their inverse-image equalities

### Original step 4

The affine inverse system $(\operatorname{Spec}R_j)_{j\geq i}$ has limit $\operatorname{Spec}R$ by [lem-weil-pbc-limit-affine-transition] and the ring-colimit computation of step 1. Its stages are affine, hence quasi-compact and quasi-separated, and all transition maps are affine. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-limit-contained-in-constructible] at $j_f$ first to $E_{\mathrm{red}}$ and then to $E_{\mathrm{irr}}$, using both image containments of step 3. It gives indices $j_r,j_t\geq j_f$ such that every sufficiently late stage maps into the respective locus. Choose one upper bound $j_0$ for $j_r,j_t$. Then the whole image of $\operatorname{Spec}R_{j_0}\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ lies in both loci. No surjectivity of a projection from the limit is asserted or needed.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-limit-affine-transition, lem-weil-pbc-limit-contained-in-constructible.

Source: literature/stacks-project/limits.tex — Tag 01YW (lemma-directed-inverse-system-affine-schemes-has-limit) and Tag 05F4 (lemma-limit-contained-in-constructible); the refinement in More on Morphisms 05FG and 05FH

### Original step 5

For every prime $\mathfrak p_0$ of $R_{j_0}$, its image in $\operatorname{Spec}A$ belongs to $E_{\mathrm{red}}\cap E_{\mathrm{irr}}$. Apply the reduced-locus base-change equality [lem-trf-geometrically-reduced-fibre-locus] to $A\to R_{j_0}$. For irreducibility use [lem-sbc-radicial-preparation-fibre-comparison] again with the identity universal homeomorphism; [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra] identifies this base change with $\operatorname{Spec}B_{j_0}\to\operatorname{Spec}R_{j_0}$. Thus every fibre of this map is geometrically reduced and geometrically irreducible. For each field extension of $\kappa(\mathfrak p_0)$ it is nonempty, reduced and irreducible, hence integral by [def-pc-integral-scheme]. ### Original step 6

Take $R_0=R_{j_0}$ and $S_0=B_{j_0}$. The ring $R_0$ is of finite type over $\mathbf Z$ by construction. Step 2 gives flatness because $j_0\geq j_f$. It also gives a finite presentation of $S_0$ over $R_0$, whose finitely many polynomial variables generate it as an algebra, so the required finite-type assertion follows directly from [def-weil-pbc-finitely-presented-algebra]. Step 5 gives geometrically integral fibres. Finally, the algebra maps induced by the original model have the canonical identification
$$S_0\otimes_{R_0}R=(B_i\otimes_{R_i}R_0)\otimes_{R_0}R\cong B_i\otimes_{R_i}R\cong S.$$
The first isomorphism sends $(b\otimes a)\otimes r$ to $b\otimes ar$, with inverse $b\otimes r\mapsto(b\otimes1)\otimes r$; both inverse and algebra-compatibility identities hold on pure tensors by the tensor relations. Tensor products are coproducts of commutative algebras, so this is precisely the required cocartesian diagram. The universal map into any compatible $R$-algebra sends $b_0\otimes r$ to the product of the two images, and is uniquely determined by this formula.
