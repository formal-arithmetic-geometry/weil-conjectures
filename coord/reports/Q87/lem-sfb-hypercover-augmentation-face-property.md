I checked sections B–E of delivery-errors against the written statements, the cited node files and the Stacks passages, not just the JSON fields. The new node introduces S, Z, W, h, its canonical ringed-etale morphism and the structural ring map. It cites algebraic-space and module definitions, the existing construction of that canonical ringed morphism, flat module sheaves, and scheme flatness. Its chartwise definition uses every etale chart T over Z ×_W V; it does not incorrectly assume this fibre product itself is a scheme. It is a statement only, with no proof or hidden construction in the statement. It has no Noetherian, separatedness or finite-presentation hypothesis, exactly as 073C. The underlying statement concerns the structure sheaves, not constant torsion coefficients.

For step 1 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-simplicial-index-finite-initial`: its last clause quantifies over EVERY category and diagram and identifies a full simplex-category limit with a finite truncation. Thus it applies in C/X, even though other Hom-object nodes in that chapter apply only in SR(X). `def-galois-finite-limits-colimits` gives existence for the resulting finite diagram. The compatible-family definition makes no use of coproducts in C; the empty indexing set is dealt with separately by terminal X. Limits in the slice are constructed from finite limits in C. This repairs a potential wrong-category C4 error before it reached the proof.

For step 2 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-matching-object-proper-faces`: clauses (2) and (3) identify the limit cone over injective proper faces and the canonical matching map in ANY finite-limit category. The step applies it in C/X and writes the extension of families to nonsurjective simplices explicitly by the surjection/injection image factorization. Evaluation at the identity proves the standard-simplex identification. In degree zero the boundary is empty and the matching map is U_0 → X, not U_0 → an unrelated terminal object of C. These are direct universal-property calculations, so this step is elementary and cites precisely the matching-object statement it uses.

For step 3 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-simplicial-set-glue-simplex` and read its pushout clause. The proof applies that clause to the simplicial SET of morphisms from a test object T to U. It obtains compatible families on the pushout, so the pullback assertion follows from their representing property, proved in step 1. This avoids citing the SR(X)-only Hom pushout theorem outside its hypotheses. The bottom arrow of the resulting square is exactly U_r → M_r(U); membership in P comes from the hypothesis and base-change closure, not from an assumed closure under arbitrary finite limits.

For step 4 I opened `lem-weil-pbc-simplicial-set-add-simplices`: it supplies a finite chain with exactly the three hypotheses of the gluing node used in step 3. Both endpoints and all intermediate subcomplexes are finite in every degree, have finitely many nondegenerate simplices and are nonempty in every degree, since they contain the starting face. The induction starts at the identity restriction and composes the pullbacks of matching maps. This is where the faces are proved, not merely renamed in a new lemma. For step 5 the augmentation induction starts at the stipulated U_0 → X, and removing missing vertices factors an arbitrary injective order map; identity injections are treated explicitly. No uncited non-elementary fact is needed for this factorization.

For step 6 I opened `lem-trf-space-fibre-products-etale-covers`, which states existence of fibre products of arbitrary algebraic spaces, and `lem-sfb-stack-chartwise-smooth-etale-calculus`, which states composition and base-change stability for flatness, local finite presentation and surjectivity. They apply to algebraic spaces, viewed as representable stacks; this is a generic chartwise-calculus use, not a replacement of a unit step. Isomorphisms satisfy the property by their inverse and their etale charts. I explicitly retained the degree-zero matching/covering hypothesis; assuming only the positive matching maps would not suffice (a constant simplicial object on a noncover of X would be a counterexample). Finite limits are needed in the slice, and fibre products supply them, so no terminal algebraic space over a missing base is assumed.

For step 7 I compared the new node word for word with 073C: chartwise flatness is equivalent to the flatness of O_Z over h^{-1}O_W. It applies independently to all faces and augmentations already proved flat, with no additional hypotheses. The conclusion used in the step is literally in its statement and is quoted in citations.json. For step 8 I opened `lem-trf-geometric-inverse-image-exact`, which includes exactness with transported module scalars, `def-pc-flat-sheaf-modules`, which defines flatness as exactness of tensor, and `def-trf-space-etale-module-functors`, which gives the actual tensor formula for h^*.
