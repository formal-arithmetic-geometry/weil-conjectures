### Old step 1

Orientation and conventions. As in the source's matrix equation, the Jacobian $J_x(f)=(\partial f_j/\partial x_i)$ of a list $f_1,\ldots,f_s$ has row index $j$ (the generators) and column index $i$ (the variables): it is an $s\times n$ matrix over $T/I$, and $D_r^x(f)$ denotes its ideal of $r\times r$ minors with the stated conventions. If $r=0$, every ideal compared in steps 2 and 3 is $T/I$ by convention. If $r>n$, every Jacobian compared there has $n$ columns, so every ideal compared is $0$ by convention. If only one of two generating lists is shorter than $r$ (say $s<r\le\min(l,n)$ in step 2), then $D_r^x(f)=0$ by convention, while the vanishing of the $r\times r$ minors of the other Jacobian is not a convention: it is the empty-sum case of the Cauchy--Binet formula in step 2. These cases include empty lists and empty matrices.

### Old step 2

Let $g_1,\ldots,g_l$ be another finite generating list of $I$. Since each $g_a$ lies in $I=(f_1,\ldots,f_s)$, write $g_a=\sum_{b=1}^sc_{ab}f_b$ with $c_{ab}\in T$. Termwise differentiation is additive and satisfies the product rule $\partial(cf)/\partial x_i=(\partial c/\partial x_i)f+c\,\partial f/\partial x_i$ (comparison of coefficients of the Cauchy product), so $\partial g_a/\partial x_i=\sum_bc_{ab}\,\partial f_b/\partial x_i+\sum_b(\partial c_{ab}/\partial x_i)f_b$, and the last sum lies in $I$. Hence over $T/I$ the $l\times n$ matrix $J_x(g)$ is the product $\bar C\,J_x(f)$ of the $l\times s$ matrix $\bar C=(\bar c_{ab})$ and the $s\times n$ matrix $J_x(f)$. Fix $1\le r\le\min(l,n)$, rows $a_1<\cdots<a_r$ and columns $i_1<\cdots<i_r$. The corresponding $r\times r$ submatrix of $J_x(g)$ is the product of the $r\times s$ matrix formed by the rows $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ of $\bar C$ and the $s\times r$ matrix formed by the columns $i_1,\ldots,i_r$ of $J_x(f)$. The Cauchy--Binet formula over $T/I$ [lem-weil-pbc-cauchy-binet], with its $m=r$ and $n=s$, writes the determinant of this product as $\sum_{S}\det(\bar C_{\{a\},S})\det(J_x(f)_{S,\{i\}})$ over the $r$-element subsets $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}$: a $T/I$-linear combination of $r\times r$ minors of $J_x(f)$, and $0$ when $s<r$ (empty sum). Hence $D_r^x(g)\subseteq D_r^x(f)$ for every $r$ (step 1 for $r=0$ and $r>\min(l,n)$). Exchanging the roles of the two lists gives the reverse inclusion, so the ideals are equal.

### Old step 3

Let $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ be another regular system of parameters of $T$, with the $F$-algebra coordinates $T=F[[y_1,\ldots,y_n]]$ of the statement, and let $Q=(\partial y_k/\partial x_i)_{k,i}$ be the $n\times n$ matrix over $T$ with row index $k$ and column index $i$. By [lem-trf-formal-jacobian-coordinate-chain-rule], for every $h\in T$ one has $\partial h/\partial x_i=\sum_k(\partial h/\partial y_k)(\partial y_k/\partial x_i)$, i.e. the row $(\partial h/\partial x_i)_i$ is the row $(\partial h/\partial y_k)_k$ times $Q$, and $Q$ is invertible over $T$. Applying this to $h=f_1,\ldots,f_s$ and stacking the rows gives $J_x(f)=J_y(f)\,Q$ as $s\times n$ matrices over $T$, hence over $T/I$ after reducing entries. Reduction modulo $I$ is a ring map, so it preserves matrix products and $\bar Q$ is invertible over $T/I$ with inverse $\overline{Q^{-1}}$; thus $J_y(f)=J_x(f)\,\overline{Q^{-1}}$. Clause (3) of [lem-trf-fitting-ideal-minor-inclusions] over $R=T/I$ with $p=s$, applied to $A=J_y(f)$ and $C=\bar Q$, gives $D_r^x(f)\subseteq D_r^y(f)$; applied to $A=J_x(f)$ and $C=\overline{Q^{-1}}$, it gives $D_r^y(f)\subseteq D_r^x(f)$. That clause includes $r=0$ and the oversized cases, so the two ideals agree for every $r\ge0$. Together with step 2, the ideal depends neither on the generating list nor on the regular system of parameters.
