## Supporting lemma argument for the next worker

For each nonzero linear equation ell_i, extend it to a linear coordinate system. In those coordinates set the first coordinate to zero. The restriction of P vanishes at every point of k^(r-1). An induction on the number of variables, using that a nonzero univariate polynomial has only finitely many roots, shows this restricted polynomial is zero over the infinite field k. Thus its first-coordinate constant term vanishes and ell_i divides P. The coordinate quotient by ell_i is a polynomial ring in r-1 variables, hence a domain; ell_i is a prime element. Nonproportional linear forms are nonassociate prime elements. Successively cancelling these distinct primes shows their product divides P. The product is nonzero in the polynomial domain. For homogeneous P, multiplication by this nonzero degree-m homogeneous product shifts every nonzero homogeneous component of the quotient by m. Thus a quotient can exist in degree d<m only if P=0, and in degree d=m it is a scalar. This is a genuine coordinate-restriction and distinct-prime argument, not merely the UFD assertion or one clause of the item. This argument stays in the report; the unsplit node's statement contains no proof sketch.
