## Review by Q87

I checked the actual statement, rather than the proof, of every cited node with the sources above open. The citations file copies a verbatim statement for every use in the five proof steps. I checked the following applications individually.

Step 1 uses def-sc-ring-strict-henselization for exactly its pointed etale algebra colimit definition, and lem-weil-pbc-strict-henselization-triples for filteredness and the local colimit with residue map. The conversion of a pair to a triple is written explicitly: its kernel is the prime, and its injection into K extends to the fraction field. Conversely the residue embedding gives the same map to K; taking kernels and fractions checks morphisms as well. The source permits all commutative base rings, so applying it to the local ring at its maximal ideal is valid; R localized at that ideal is R because every denominator is a unit. Thus the cited triples construction really identifies the object defined by the item. No existence theorem for a large-index colimit is invoked: C is the supplied universal colimit, and the definition already records essential smallness.

Step 2 uses lem-sc-strict-henselization-faithfully-flat for the structural map of that same strict henselization, def-weil-pbc-faithfully-flat-ring-map for the implication to flatness, and lem-pc-module-flat-transitive for a flat algebra followed by a flat module. These statements supply all three facts under precisely the hypotheses here: a local commutative R, its chosen separable residue closure K, and the arbitrary C-module M. Restriction uses the structural map throughout.

Step 3 uses def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime for fractions at q, lem-trf-etale-local-ring-flatness for the equivalence at a point of an etale scheme morphism, and lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization(c) for the actual germ-map localizations. The local residue map needed here has been established in step 1, not inferred from a definition of flatness. If s is outside q then its image has nonzero residue in K, hence is a unit in the local ring C; the displayed formula therefore defines the extension S_q to C and the given module action restricts along it. The etale morphism is Spec S to Spec R, with q lying over m by the triples statement. Its local base ring is R, and its local source ring is S_q. The stalk-map action equals the original action on R by evaluation on r/1. Thus the local-ring lemma applies to this M without a finite-generation or finite-presentation assumption on M.

Step 4 uses lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimit-algebras-elementwise only for the representative property its statement actually gives. Step 1 supplies a colimit in R-algebras, so that hypothesis is satisfied. Taking a common stage for a finite family uses the already established filteredness. I checked that only coefficients are represented in the stage; the elements x_i stay in the fixed module M, where the relation already holds. There is no unwarranted claim that a relation between module elements descends as a relation in a colimit of modules. The scalar formula j_t(b_i/1)=c_i proves the needed relation directly.

Step 5 uses both directions of lem-weil-pbc-flat-equational-criterion. Its statement gives exactly the elements y_j and coefficient matrix d_ij used here, with arbitrary finite n,m, and has no finiteness hypothesis on M. Flatness over S_q is supplied by step 3. The two matrix equations are mapped through the ring homomorphism j_t; the module equations use its restricted action, and the coefficient equations use preservation of finite sums and products. Consequently every C-relation is trivial, which is the reverse implication of that same criterion. This is the complete directed-colimit argument for this module and hides no comparison or standalone lemma.
