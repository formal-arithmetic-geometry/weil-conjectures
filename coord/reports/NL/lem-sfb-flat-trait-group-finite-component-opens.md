### An explicit counterexample to the unrestricted item

Let R=F_2[t]_(t), K=F_2(t), and R'=R[u]/(u^2+u+t). This is a connected finite etale quadratic R-algebra: the derivative is 1, its fraction field K'=F_2(u) has t=u^2+u and degree 2 over K, and its two closed points are u=0 and u=1. The involution tau(u)=u+1 exchanges them. Let S_0,S_1 be the open traits in Spec R' obtained by deleting respectively the other closed point; their overlap is Spec K'.

Let Gamma=C_2*C_2=<a,b | a^2=b^2=1>, an infinite group, and let tau exchange a and b. On S_0 put A=<a>. The finite flat additive group
  G_A=Spec O(S_0)[x]/(x^2-tx)
has generic fibre the constant A (roots 0,t) and closed fibre alpha_2 (x^2=0). Make a group scheme P_0 whose scheme is G_A disjoint union one copy of Spec K' for each gamma outside A. Its generic fibre is the constant Gamma. On G_A x G_A use the additive law; every product involving a generic-only summand is over K' and uses multiplication in Gamma. These definitions satisfy the group laws on each product summand. Inversion is defined in the same way. Thus P_0 is separated, flat and locally finitely presented over S_0. It is not quasi-compact.

Construct P_1 on S_1 in exactly this explicit manner with B=<b>. Glue P_0 and P_1 along the common constant generic group Gamma to get a separated flat locally finitely presented R'-group scheme P. Separatedness and the other stated properties are local on the base and hold on S_0,S_1. The involution exchanging u and u+1 and the labels a and b gives P a group-compatible quadratic descent datum. Descend its fpqc sheaf to a group sheaf F on Spec R. Sheaf descent is effective, and pulling back along the finite etale fpqc cover Spec R' gives P. This supplies exactly the item's hypotheses without invoking representability of F on R.

Suppose H were an open subgroup with H_R' finitely presented. Its special fibre on either branch contains the identity and is open in alpha_2, whose underlying space is one point; hence it is all of alpha_2. On branch 0 both generic points of G_A specialize to this point, so an open containing it contains both, and H_K' contains A. On branch 1 the same reasoning forces B. The single generic subgroup therefore contains <A,B>=Gamma. But a finite-presentation group scheme over K' is quasi-compact and an open of the discrete constant Gamma can contain only finitely many of its point-components if it is quasi-compact. Contradiction. Even taking the generated open subgroup cannot cure this example: its generic fibre is the infinite Gamma.

This counterexample explains why replacing the old product clause by a bare generated-subgroup assertion over arbitrary DVRs would merely hide the same error. After strict henselization the finite etale cover splits into two separate branches; the respective neutral envelopes are G_A and G_B on their own copies and never have to contain both generators in one generic subgroup.

## Repair of each returned point

E2: Removed the assertion for arbitrary Z,Z' over an arbitrary DVR. The surviving product assertion is only for special identity components, and only if R is strictly henselian. The general envelope existence/minimality/finite-presentation clauses are retained; neither their proofs nor their conclusions imply the old product equality. The earlier minimality repair is also retained: minimality is among opens whose generic fibre is component-saturated, not all opens. Flat-map compatibility and isomorphism compatibility are separate assertions of the intermediate node.

The strict-local geometric reason for the corrected product assertion is worth spelling out for its future splitter. Fix an algebraic closure of K. A henselian valuation has a unique extension to it, so all conjugate geometric generic components contributing to the neutral envelope specialize to the geometric special identity component for this same valuation. The identity component is geometrically connected, so its extension to the residue field remains the one special component. To combine two such specializations, choose valuation lifts of points on the two geometric generic components, and take their fibre product over the extended valuation ring. Its special fibre is nonempty, and flatness over the valuation ring gives a generic generization. The generic image lies in the product of the chosen geometric components. Connected components of a group scheme over an algebraically closed field are geometrically irreducible topologically (pass to the reduced group); thus this product is one geometric component. It follows that every geometric generic component of the product of the two neutral envelopes specializes to their special product component. Conversely, projections send a component meeting the special product into components meeting each factor. Component-saturation and the envelope's minimal characterization now give equality. The nonhenselian failure was precisely that conjugates could meet different closed branches of the normalization; uniqueness of the extended valuation removes that inference. This is substantive geometry recorded as part of the new unsplit node, not an elementary assertion in the item proof.

E3: The item's hypothesis now explicitly includes strict henselianity, and Step 1 checks it before applying this corrected neutral-product clause. Identity and inverse come from the envelope lemma. Multiplication is flat and locally finitely presented because (x,y)->(xy,y) identifies it with a projection after the representing cover. Its special restriction maps the identity-component square onto the identity component. The separately stated flat-map compatibility then carries the product envelope into the neutral envelope. This constructs an actual subgroup H; it does not infer closure from minimality alone. The existing coset lemma and Steps 2-7 are retained, so there is no change to the accepted normalization/chart argument.
