## Problems found and corrections

No statement dispute. The original source correctly gives the result for arbitrary coefficient fields and arbitrary finite extensions. The actual argument of BGR 5.3.3/1 is that the total-degree valuation is discrete, is weakly stable by 3.5.3/3, and is then stable by 3.6.2/1. Merely saying that this is a discrete valuation would omit the essential weak-stability hypothesis: BGR p.155 explicitly records that some discretely valued fields are not weakly stable.

The proof now spells out why the fraction formula is well-defined, multiplicative, and ultrametric, and why its value group is exactly exp(Z). The weak-stability assertion is kept distinct from the exact orthogonality conclusion. Definitions of polynomial rings and norms are not cited as though they prove weak stability or existence of an orthogonal basis.

The current blueprint under literature/blueprint/latest was read for lem-trf-local-term-locality and sk-i-curve-zero. Its current route is contracting local terms, whereas this inherited analytic item serves the historical normalization chain already recorded in blueprint.from. This delivery splits that analytic input below the node level; it does not claim that the current blueprint states it or change the unit's proof route. Blueprint searches for orthogonal, weakly stable, total degree, weak cartesian, cartesian basis, degree of a product, and discrete valuation found no blueprint version of either analytic supporting lemma. ## Not duplicates

lem-trf-degree-valuation-weak-stability vs lem-trf-degree-valuation-orthogonal-basis: the new lemma gives only two-sided coordinate bounds, not an orthogonal basis. It is the independent weaker input BGR 3.5.3/3. The item's argument must additionally establish discreteness and apply the general vector-space criterion.
lem-trf-degree-valuation-weak-stability vs lem-trf-tate-fraction-weak-stability: the latter concerns fractions of restricted power series over a complete nontrivially valued field with the Gauss norm; the new lemma concerns a rational function field over an arbitrary field with the total-degree absolute value, trivial on constants.
lem-trf-degree-valuation-weak-stability vs lem-trf-char-zero-spectral-coordinate-topology: the latter assumes characteristic zero for an arbitrary valued base; the new lemma treats all characteristics for the specific total-degree valuation. Neither statement specializes to the other in the positive-characteristic cases needed here.
lem-trf-degree-valuation-weak-stability vs lem-trf-fraction-root-module-weak-stability-criterion: the existing criterion assumes bounded separating linear functionals for all finitely generated root modules in positive characteristic. The new lemma has no such hypothesis and concerns a specific rational function field; checking that criterion is part of its future proof, not part of the criterion's statement.
lem-trf-degree-valuation-weak-stability vs lem-trf-frobenius-root-coordinate-test: the existing node is a criterion with a root-space coordinate-topology hypothesis, not weak stability for the total-degree valuation without that hypothesis.
lem-trf-discrete-weak-cartesian-basis vs lem-trf-degree-valuation-orthogonal-basis: the new lemma is linear algebra for any norm satisfying the displayed equivalence over a discretely valued field; it neither establishes that the total-degree spectral norm has that property nor constructs that absolute value.
lem-trf-discrete-weak-cartesian-basis vs lem-trf-cartesian-subspace-scalar-tower: the latter presupposes cartesianness and transports it to subspaces or through a scalar tower. It does not turn weak cartesianness into cartesianness using discreteness.
lem-trf-discrete-weak-cartesian-basis vs lem-trf-spectral-reduction-dimension-criterion: the latter concerns reduction dimensions and unit-norm bases under an equality of residue dimensions. Those hypotheses are neither present nor needed in the new criterion.

## Review by NL

I read BGR Proposition 5.3.3/1 on printed p.214, and the orthogonal-basis sentence of the proof of Lemma 5.3.3/4 on p.217. I then followed the two references actually used in Proposition 1: Proposition 3.5.3/3 on p.155 gives weak stability for total degree over an arbitrary field, and Proposition 3.6.2/1 on p.157 cites Corollary 2.4.3/11 on p.102. I read that corollary itself; it assumes a discrete base valuation and weak cartesianness, not completeness. The two new statements express those inputs with norms and coordinate inequalities written inline so that future workers need not guess the meaning of Cartesian. The weak-stability node has statement only. Its eventual proof must retain BGR's distinction between the characteristic-zero case and positive characteristic, where the decomposition into exponent classes modulo p and bounded coefficient projections separates the root module. The second node likewise has statement only; the source's completion/descent argument behind weak cartesianness is not hidden in an elementary step of this item.

For step 1 I opened lem-sfb-mvpolynomial-total-degree-product and checked that its statement gives the exact product identity, over every integral domain and every variable set. A field supplies the integral-domain hypothesis. I opened def-weil-pbc-mv-polynomial-ring and used its explicit monomial and coefficientwise-addition description to show that summation cannot introduce a monomial of larger total degree; no separate theorem is concealed in that observation. Cross multiplication gives independence of representatives. Degree addition gives multiplicativity. The common-denominator expression gives the ultrametric inequality, with zero sums treated separately. Constants, negatives, and the positive and negative powers of X_1 check normalization, symmetry, and the entire nonzero value group. This step is elementary relative to its two citations. The product-degree leaf, not an uncited general commutative algebra assertion, supplies the substantive polynomial identity.

For step 2 I reread the new weak-stability statement and verified that its conclusion is exactly the pair of inequalities used, for the same Q, L, basis, and spectral norm. Step 1 has already supplied the absolute value. F need not be perfect and the extension need not be separable; these are not silently added in applying BGR. The weaker inequalities alone are deliberately not asserted to imply orthogonality until the discrete criterion is invoked.

For step 3 I opened lem-trf-incomplete-spectral-algebra-norm and checked its displayed definiteness, ultrametric inequality, and scalar homogeneity. These give precisely the norm axioms required by the new general criterion. The value group is exp(Z), so rho=exp(1)>1 supplies the criterion's discrete hypothesis. The bounds and chosen basis come from step 2, and finiteness comes from [L:Q]. The criterion then gives exactly the desired equality with weights s(u_i), without claiming that s is multiplicative on L (it can fail to be a field absolute value when Q is incomplete). Each cited statement was copied verbatim into citations.json.

I tested the small cases: L=Q has the basis 1 and its spectral norm restricts to the base norm; n=1 gives the usual infinity absolute value; degree-zero nonzero constants have value 1, while zero is assigned 0 separately. In characteristic p the extension adjoining a p-th root of X_1 is permitted and has the expected distinct value cosets for its power basis; the statement therefore does not require a separability repair. Imperfect constant fields are explicitly allowed by BGR 3.5.3/3. For the general vector-space criterion the zero space has the empty basis and zero maximum; the one-dimensional case is immediate from homogeneity. The criterion retains the coordinate lower bound: removing it would incorrectly cover incomplete-field norms whose completion loses dimension. No extra assertion of completeness, unit weights, or spherical completeness was introduced.
