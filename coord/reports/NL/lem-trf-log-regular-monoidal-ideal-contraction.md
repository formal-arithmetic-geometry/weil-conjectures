### Argument assigned to lem-trf-sharp-monoid-ideal-minimal-generators

Use R = Z[P]. For any ideal K of P, the integer span of its monomials is closed under multiplication by every basis monomial, so it equals KR. Since P has finitely many generators, R is a finite-type Z-algebra and is Noetherian (the existing lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian and Noetherianity of Z supply this). Choose finitely many ring generators of KR; each is a finite sum of monomials with exponents in K. The union S of these finite supports lies in K and its monomials generate KR. For each k in K, comparing the coefficient of e_k in an expression in these ring generators yields k = s + q for some s in S, q in P. Conversely S+P is contained in K, hence K is the union of these translates. This includes K empty by taking S empty.

Define x <= y by y in x+P. Sharpness and cancellation make it antisymmetric: y=x+q and x=y+r imply q+r=0, hence q=r=0. If J is properly contained in K, some generator s lies outside J. In the finite nonempty set of generators below s select a minimal p. Then p is outside J, since otherwise s belongs to J. If p=k+q with k in K and q nonzero, write k=t+r with t in S. Then t <= p <= s, and t is strictly below p: equality would imply r+q=0 by cancellation, contradicting sharpness. This contradicts minimality. Thus p is outside K+P^+. This is the finite-generation and minimal-exponent portion of old step 3, with no argument omitted.

### Argument assigned to lem-trf-monomial-quotient-residue-surjection

The basis argument above works for every monoid ideal I without finite generation: IR is the integer span of e_i for i in I. Consequently KR/JR is free over Z on the classes e_k for k in K minus J. Sharpness says a sum can equal 0 only if both summands are 0, so constant coefficient epsilon is a ring map. Its kernel is the integer span of all nonzero monomials, exactly m, and R/m is Z. Since p is outside J, coefficient at p vanishes on JR and descends to the quotient. For k in K and q nonzero, k+q cannot equal p by the hypothesis on p; thus phi_p(e_q e_k)=0=epsilon(e_q)phi_p(e_k). For q=0 the same identity is immediate. Integer bilinearity extends this check to every ring element and quotient element, establishing R-linearity. The class e_p maps to 1, so the map is surjective. Notice that this does not assert e_p is annihilated by nonzero monomials.

Right exactness of tensoring (the already existing lem-weil-pbc-tensor-right-exact) makes phi_p tensor A surjective. The tensor of R/m with A is A/mA: b mod mA maps to [1] tensor b, and [r] tensor b maps to f(r)b mod mA; balancing makes these well defined and inverse. Using R/m = Z through epsilon gives precisely Phi_p([r] tensor b)=r_p b mod mA. This proves the basis, epsilon, coefficient-functional, surjectivity and tensor-identification portions of old step 3; the empty J case has JR=0 throughout.

## Returned proof retained verbatim

### Original step 1

Put $R=\mathbf Z[P]$ and let $K=a^{-1}(JA)$. Because $a$ is multiplicative and $JA$ is an ideal of $A$, $K$ is a monoid ideal: if $p\in K$ and $q\in P$, then $a(p+q)=a(p)a(q)\in JA$. Every $j\in J$ lies in $K$, so $J\subseteq K$. The current chart definition [def-trf-fs-log-blowup] identifies the chart monoid modulo the units of $A$ with the characteristic monoid; together with the sharp-chart lemma, this says that $P$ is finitely generated, integral and sharp and that every nonzero element of $P$ maps to a nonunit of the local ring $A$. Hence the ideal $\mathfrak m_P A$ generated by $P\setminus\{0\}$ is proper.

### Original step 2

Assume for contradiction that $J\subsetneq K$. The exact sequence of $R$-modules $JR\to KR\to KR/JR\to0$, after tensoring with $A$, is right exact. For each of the monoid ideals $J$ and $K$, the Tor-vanishing statement [lem-trf-kato-tor-independence-chart] applied to $R/IR$ identifies $IR\otimes_R A$ with its multiplication image $IA\subseteq A$. Thus the first map has image $JA=KA$, so the right-exact sequence forces $(KR/JR)\otimes_R A=0$.

### Original step 3

Since $J\subsetneq K$, some element of $K$ lies outside $J$. The monomials $e_q$ ($q\in P$) form a $\mathbf Z$-basis of $R$, and for a monoid ideal $I$ the ideal $IR$ is the $\mathbf Z$-span of the $e_q$ with $q\in I$ (this span contains the generators and is stable under multiplication by monomials because $I+P\subseteq I$); so $KR/JR$ is free over $\mathbf Z$ on the classes of $e_k$, $k\in K\setminus J$. Because $P$ is finitely generated [lem-trf-log-regular-sharp-chart], $R$ is a finite-type $\mathbf Z$-algebra and hence Noetherian [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian]. A finite generating set of $KR$ consists of $\mathbf Z$-combinations of monomials from $K$, so finitely many $k_1,\dots,k_m\in K$ generate $KR$, and comparing coefficients of $e_k$ for $k\in K$ gives $K=\bigcup_i(k_i+P)$. Write $P^+=P\setminus\{0\}$; by sharpness and integrality $P^+$ is a monoid ideal, and $k_j\in k_i+P$, $k_i\in k_j+P$ force $k_i=k_j$ (cancel to get $q+q'=0$, so $q=q'=0$). Pick $k_i\notin J$ (one exists, else $K\subseteq J$) and, among the finitely many $k_j$ with $k_i\in k_j+P$, a $p=k_j$ that is minimal for this order. Then $p\notin J$ (else $k_i\in J$), and $p\notin K+P^+$: if $p=k+q$ with $k\in K$, $q\ne0$, then $k\in k_l+P$ for some $l$, so $p\in k_l+P^+$ with $k_l\ne p$ by cancellation and sharpness, contradicting minimality. Let $\varepsilon:R\to\mathbf Z$ send $e_0\mapsto1$ and $e_q\mapsto0$ for $q\ne0$; it is a ring map with kernel $\mathfrak m_PR$ because $P^+$ is an ideal, so $R/\mathfrak m_PR\cong\mathbf Z$. Define $\varphi:KR/JR\to\mathbf Z$ by $\varphi(e_p)=1$ and $\varphi(e_k)=0$ for the other $k\in K\setminus J$. It is $R$-linear for $\varepsilon$: for $q\ne0$ and $k\in K$, $q+k\in K+P^+$ is never $p$, so $\varphi(e_qe_k)=0=\varepsilon(e_q)\varphi(e_k)$. It is surjective. Since $\otimes_RA$ is right exact, it gives a surjection $(KR/JR)\otimes_R A\twoheadrightarrow (R/\mathfrak m_P R)\otimes_R A\cong A/\mathfrak m_P A$. This covers $J=\varnothing$, where $KR/JR=KR$.

### Original step 4

The ideal $\mathfrak m_P A$ is proper by step 1, since every generator $a(q)$ for $q\ne0$ is a nonunit in the local ring $A$. Therefore $A/\mathfrak m_P A\ne0$, so the surjection in step 3 contradicts the vanishing obtained in step 2. Thus $K=J$, which is exactly $a^{-1}(JA)=J$. The argument includes $J=\varnothing$: then $JA=(0)$, and any nonempty $K$ would give the same nonzero quotient contradiction.
