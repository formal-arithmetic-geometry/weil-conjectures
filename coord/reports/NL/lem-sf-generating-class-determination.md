## Source error — STATEMENT-DISPUTED

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported item and unit omit compatibility of the natural transformation with the specified exact-functor shift isomorphisms. The unit's inference “Shifts: alpha at X[1] is alpha at X shifted, by exactness” does not follow from exactness of the two functors. Naturality alone also does not supply the last square of a morphism of their image triangles.

A small counterexample is `C = D = D^b(F₂-vector spaces)`, `Φ = Ψ = Id`, with the natural endomorphism α which is the identity on even-degree cohomology and zero on odd-degree cohomology. Every bounded complex of vector spaces is the direct sum of its cohomology objects in the derived category; maps between distinct degrees vanish, so this prescription is natural. It is invertible on the thick generator F₂ in degree zero and is zero on its shift F₂[1]. Thus the unqualified imported first clause is false. Even the split distinguished triangle F₂[-1] → 0 → F₂ → F₂ gives two invertible components (at zero and F₂) but a noninvertible third. This also diagnoses the same omission in the existing sibling `lem-sf-triangle-two-out-of-three`; I leave that other worker's node untouched.

The repaired item explicitly requires `c_{Ψ,n,X} α_{X[n]} = α_X[n] c_{Φ,n,X}`. Its proof uses the existing, correctly stated `lem-sf-triangle-morphism-iso` after writing all three squares, rather than relying on the sibling's overly broad statement. The general triangulated-category setting is the unit's own setting; the imported six-functor datum supplied no relevant hypothesis and is replaced by the actual category and perfect-complex definitions. No target statement or chapter/unit source text has been changed. This correction is necessary for the source's intended invertibility argument, and the counterexample/non-determination conclusion is retained in full.

The unit also says “a morphism whose cone has vanishing cohomology is zero”; the intended conclusion is that the morphism is an isomorphism (and the cone is zero). The identity of a nonzero complex disproves the literal sentence.
