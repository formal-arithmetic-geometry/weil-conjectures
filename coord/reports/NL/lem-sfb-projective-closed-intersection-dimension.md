Step 1 cites def-kun-topological-krull-dimension. I opened its statement: dimension is the supremum of lengths of nonempty irreducible closed chains, with the empty space assigned bottom. Since r is a finite integer, some chain has length r. Its top member has dimension exactly r: chains in that member remain closed irreducible chains in X. The same argument applies to s. This avoids introducing a redundant maximal-component theorem. All needed closed inclusions are written out, and the final bound transfers by exactly the same chain argument. The step is elementary; no Noetherian or finite-component theorem is being silently used.

Step 2 cites the new cone node. Its statement really supplies closedness, irreducibility, dimensions r+1 and s+1, and the actual origin-containing set formula. The chosen projective subsets are nonempty irreducible closed subsets with finite dimensions, so every hypothesis is met. An irreducible space has just itself as its maximal irreducible subset, hence the cones are equidimensional. I checked this against the definition in def-weil-irreducible-components. No claim about fibres is imported from an unstated comparison.

Step 3 cites the new affine node. I reread the explicit final implication in that node: if the intersection is nonempty its dimension is at least a+b-n. Here a=r+1, b=s+1 and n=N+1, and the common origin establishes its extra hypothesis. The integer calculation gives the strictly positive cone-intersection bound r+s-N+1. Positivity is essential; a merely zero-dimensional affine intersection could be only the origin.

Step 4 cites the cone node and the Krull dimension definition, and is elementary assembly from those cited statements. Set-theoretic inverse images distribute over intersection, so the affine intersection is precisely C(X_0 intersect Y_0), not a chosen closure identified without justification. If the projective intersection were empty this is a singleton, whose sole nonempty irreducible closed subset is itself and whose dimension is zero. After ruling that out, the projective intersection is closed in X_0, so its chains have lengths bounded by r; nonemptiness gives dimension at least zero. Thus its dimension is a finite integer and the cone lemma applies without assuming the theorem we are proving. Subtracting one gives the bound. The passage to the original X,Y is the closed-chain inclusion already established in step 1. citations.json contains a verbatim statement quote for every cited node at every step.

I checked small cases explicitly. N=0 gives a point in projective space, cones A^1, and the result 0>=0. When r+s=N the affine bound is at least one and still excludes a singleton intersection. Two different points in P^1 have affine cones meeting only at the origin; their projective dimensions sum to less than N, so they test why the numerical hypothesis is necessary. A non-rational closed point over a non-algebraically-closed field has a one-dimensional affine cone and does not require a rational-point lift. For reducible X or Y the chain argument chooses a maximal-dimensional closed irreducible piece and makes no equidimensionality assumption on the original set. For affine n=0 the nonempty closed subsets are the point; for disjoint affine parallel lines the affine theorem asserts no nonemptiness. For the empty cone convention, C(empty) is exactly a singleton and no false formula dim C(empty)=dim empty+1 is asserted.
