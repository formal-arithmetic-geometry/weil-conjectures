Step 1 uses the exact output of `lem-zl-smooth-affine-geometric-filtration`: its initial scheme is literally the input, and its first nonempty smooth connected affine piece is open in that input and lies in a member of the chosen cover. I applied it to the original reduced finite-type scheme, not to an open whose finite type had yet to be established. Integrality supplies reducedness and irreducibility. The cover is the two locally closed sets T minus S and S, so finite closed S causes no constructibility issue. `lem-sc-dimension-locally-algebraic` says every nonempty open has dimension d. A finite closed-point subspace is discrete, so it cannot contain this open when d is at least two. `lem-weil-pbc-open-subspace-irreducible-closed-correspondence` and the definitions of integrality, dimension and pure dimension justify the properties of the chosen open. This repairs the tempting but invalid application of a smooth lemma directly to singular T.

Step 2 cites `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian`, `lem-sfb-noetherian-open-immersion-qc`, `lem-weil-pbc-qc-open-immersion-finite-type` and `lem-sfb-finite-type-composition-base-change` for exactly their stated conclusions, in that order. The base is the spectrum of a field, hence Noetherian. This proves finite type for every open used later. The generating-sections definition gives local expressions, which restrict; expanding with respect to a basis preserves their span. The definition of the projective coordinate morphism identifies its restriction, and clause (e) of `lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence` explicitly includes composition of immersions. I did not infer composition from a mere definition node.

Step 3 applies `lem-zl-bertini-finite-cover-transverse-component` to the identity finite etale cover of the smooth affine open, with exceptional set empty. I checked algebraically closed base, smoothness, connectedness, affineness, finite type, pure dimension d at least two, a finite basis, generation everywhere, and immersion everywhere. Identity finiteness and etaleness are the identity-ring cases of their definitions. The node's statement really supplies maximality in the two zero loci, codimension exactly two, and noncontainment in the third divisor. Its smooth affine hypotheses are available here, whereas they were not available on T. This is a specialization of an existing node, not a new node rephrasing clause (a).

Step 4 uses `lem-pc-zero-scheme-properties` for its closedness and local equation clause, so no unproved base-change theorem for arbitrary morphisms is assumed. Restriction to an open simply restricts the trivializations and their equations. The full order isomorphism in `lem-weil-pbc-open-subspace-irreducible-closed-correspondence` supplies both closure and intersection and strict inclusion preservation. I wrote out maximality and both directions of the chain correspondence. Every superset of the closure meets the open, which is the necessary hypothesis for this order isomorphism. Thus no extra component appearing on the boundary can enlarge this particular component, and no smoothness of a boundary point is used. This is a few-line deduction from the cited tools, so it is marked elementary.

Step 5 uses `lem-weil-pbc-invertible-generating-sections-fibre`, whose statement really gives a nonzero fibre value for at least one basis section at every point, and `lem-weil-pbc-section-nonvanishing-zero-locus`, whose statement identifies that condition with the complement of the zero support. Hence the common zero set is a subset of S; it need not equal S and no vanishing on all of S is assumed. Finite discreteness proves that its components are singletons.

Step 6 uses `lem-pc-affine-finite-type-algebra` after step 2, and the integral-scheme definition for the domain hypothesis. A closed point in the affine neighbourhood corresponds to a maximal ideal. Clause (c) of `lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization` gives the local-ring identification. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-domain-dimension-local-ring-localization` assumes exactly a field, a finite-type domain and a maximal ideal, all now verified. `lem-weil-pbc-spec-topological-krull-dimension-ring` converts ring to topological dimension. `lem-sc-dimension-locally-algebraic` identifies the dimension of the nonempty affine open with d. Finally `lem-sc-scheme-stalk-dimension-generalization-chains` explicitly equates local-ring dimension with the chain-supremum codimension used in this item. This checks the convention, rather than assuming an unspecified meaning of codimension. No regularity at points of S is required.

I tested d=2, when the component in (a) may be a point and the base locus points have codimension exactly two; S empty; a singular point in S; and a nonproper/nonaffine integral T. In all these cases the proof uses only the checked smooth open and the general closed-point dimension computation.
