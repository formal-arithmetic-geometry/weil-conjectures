I read Anantharaman's actual Lemma 4.2.1 and its two proof paragraphs in the Numdam primary scan, rather than relying on the parent delivery's source claim. It allows a general finite-presentation sheaf model without a group hypothesis. Its first paragraph descends the model and its descent datum to a finite type subalgebra; its second chooses a quasi-section and a finite normal (possibly inseparable) field extension and uses conjugate subtraits. This is exactly why the two new nodes are independent tools: the first produces a finite type cover carrying a model without changing the fraction field; the second supplies base-local maps into an arbitrary flat cover and mentions no sheaf. Neither restates representability over a normalized semi-trait.

For the first new statement I also read limits.tex, Tag 01ZM, lemma-descend-finite-presentation (1)-(3), and more-algebra.tex, Tag 0AUW. The object, the two projections' comparison and its inverse descend over the tensor-square system, and the cocycle equality descends over the tensor-cube system after enlarging the same subalgebra. Flatness of a finite type subalgebra follows from torsion-freeness over R; surjectivity follows since Spec B surjects onto Spec R and factors through that subalgebra. One must not assume B is faithfully flat over C: to identify Q with the original sheaf over C, compare their canonical descent data on the genuinely fpqc cover Spec(B tensor_R C) -> Spec C and its overlaps, then use the sheaf equalizers. This compatibility, not merely an abstract scheme model of P, is included in the new statement. The zero-dimensional field case is allowed by this ingredient and reduces to ordinary finite-presentation descent.
