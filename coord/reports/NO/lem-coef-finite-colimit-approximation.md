## Returned step 2

We first establish a reusable intermediate assertion for any $G:A\to B$ with the finite-limit and right approximation hypotheses of step 1: its map on cores is a weak homotopy equivalence. Fix $b\in B$ and let $P_b$ be the homotopy fiber of $G^\simeq:A^\simeq\to B^\simeq$ over $b$. These cores are Kan, and the mapping-path construction makes $P_b$ Kan by [lem-cbc-kan-fibration-fiber-criterion]. A map from a finite simplicial set $K$ to $P_b$ gives a diagram $X:K\to A^\simeq$ and a natural equivalence $\tau:GX\to\Delta_K b$. Choose its inverse $\lambda:\Delta_K b\to GX$. Choose a limit cone $\iota:\Delta_K z\to X$ in $A$; its image is a limit cone in $B$. The cone mapping-space criterion of [lem-cbc-enhanced-functor-cone-calculus] says that
$$\operatorname{Map}_B(b,Gz)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Map}_{\operatorname{Fun}(K,B)}(\Delta_K b,GX)$$
is an equivalence. Hence there is $\psi:b\to Gz$ together with a path $G\iota\circ\Delta_K\psi\simeq\lambda$, on the whole coherent diagram, not merely on its vertices. Right approximation gives $v:z'\to z$ and an equivalence $e:b\to Gz'$ with $Gv\circ e\simeq\psi$. Put $\alpha=\iota\circ\Delta_K v:\Delta_K z'\to X$. Then $G\alpha\circ\Delta_K e\simeq\lambda$. Every component of $G\alpha$ is therefore an equivalence, and conservativity makes every component of $\alpha$ an equivalence. The pointwise criterion makes $\alpha$ a natural equivalence. Its simplicial map $K\times\Delta^1\to A$ factors through $A^\simeq$: all edges of $X$, of the constant diagram and of $\alpha$ are equivalences, and the remaining edges are their composites as witnessed by the transformation simplices. Together with $\tau G\alpha\simeq\Delta_K(e^{-1})$, this gives a homotopy in $P_b$ between the given $K$-diagram and the constant diagram at $(z',e^{-1})$. More explicitly the mapping-path fiber is the space of pairs $(X,\tau)$, and a path between such pairs is a path $\alpha$ in the first coordinate with a homotopy from $\tau G\alpha$ to the second path; the displayed coherent homotopy supplies precisely that compatibility. For $K=\varnothing$ the same construction uses a terminal $z$ and produces a vertex of $P_b$.

## Returned step 5

The functor $G_t$ also reflects equivalences and has right approximation. Check this in the arrow presentations $T_t,T_{Gt}$, using their natural equivalences with the join slices. The join-slice projections are right fibrations by [lem-coef-infinity-slice-right-fibration]. Apply the conservativity assertion of [lem-cbc-left-fibration-equivalence-lift] to their opposites, which are left fibrations because reversing vertices interchanges the horn ranges $0<i\leq n$ and $0\leq i<n$. Hence those projections reflect equivalences. Their arrow presentations are equivalent over the base by [lem-coef-infinity-slice-arrow-equivalence], so the arrow projections also reflect equivalences. In particular an edge of $T_t$ is an equivalence whenever its source component is, and the same holds over $B$. It follows that $G_t$ is conservative, since $G$ is. For right approximation let $a:c\to t$, $b:d\to Gt$ be objects and let $\psi:b\to Ga$ be an arrow of $T_{Gt}$. Its data are $\psi_0:d\to Gc$ and a homotopy $Ga\,\psi_0\simeq b$. Apply right approximation in $A,B$ to $\psi_0$, obtaining $v:c'\to c$ and an equivalence $e:d\to Gc'$ with $Gv\,e\simeq\psi_0$. Define $a'=av:c'\to t$. The edge $v:a'\to a$ is a morphism over $t$. Equip $e$ with the homotopy $G(a')e=Ga\,Gv\,e\simeq Ga\,\psi_0\simeq b$, obtaining an equivalence $b\to G(a')$ over $Gt$. The homotopy $Gv\,e\simeq\psi_0$, with exactly this chosen composite compatibility, identifies its composite with $G_t(v)$ with the original $\psi$ in the slice homotopy category. This is a statement about the whole slice mapping space: the cone-calculus formula expresses that mapping space as the homotopy fiber of $\operatorname{Map}_B(d,Gc)\to\operatorname{Map}_B(d,Gt)$ over $b$; concatenating the indicated paths gives the path in that fiber. It is not an inference from equality of underlying morphisms alone. Hence right approximation holds in the arrow slices and, via their coherent quasi-inverses over the base, in the join slices. Equivalences transport finite limits by the cone mapping-space criterion and the mapping-space equivalences in [lem-cbc-enhanced-functor-cone-calculus].
