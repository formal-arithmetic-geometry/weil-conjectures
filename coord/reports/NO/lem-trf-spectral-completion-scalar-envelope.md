## Construction argument for the new unsplit prerequisite

This is report material, not a proof smuggled into a statement-only node. For the norm n, |n(a)-n(b)| <= n(a-b), so the norms of a Cauchy sequence are Cauchy real numbers and give a representative-independent limit. Its zero locus is precisely the sequences identified with zero. Every Cauchy sequence is bounded. Thus if a_j and b_j are Cauchy, the identity a_j b_j-a_k b_k = (a_j-a_k)b_j+a_k(b_j-b_k), together with submultiplication and the strong triangle inequality, proves that termwise products are Cauchy. The same bound proves independence of representatives. Termwise ring axioms descend, and n(1)=1 keeps the unit nonzero.

For scalar Cauchy sequences c_j in F the bound n(c_j a_j-c_k a_k) <= max(|c_j-c_k| n(a_j), |c_k| n(a_j-a_k)) proves that scalar multiplication is defined on both quotient classes and independent of representatives. The scalar and algebra axioms descend from F and A. Taking limits in n(c_j a_j)=|c_j|n(a_j) gives the exact H-homogeneity, not just an upper bound. Similarly the strong triangle, submultiplication and n(a_j^m)=n(a_j)^m pass to limits for each fixed positive m; powers of a Cauchy sequence are Cauchy by the product argument. The constant-sequence map preserves the norm, so it is injective, and preserves all F-algebra operations. Its image is dense because the constant sequence at a_j approximates [(a_i)] as j tends to infinity. Completeness is the usual diagonal construction: for a Cauchy sequence of classes choose a subsequence with successive distances <2^(-j), approximate its j-th member by a constant sequence within 2^(-j), and use the triangle inequality to see these chosen A-elements are Cauchy and converge to the original sequence's limit class. No inverse in A or completeness of F was used.
