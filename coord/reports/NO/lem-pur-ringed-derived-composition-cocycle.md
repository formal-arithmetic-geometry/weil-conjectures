## Original argument, retained verbatim as a proof handoff

1. Work first at $Q_0I$ with $I$ K-injective on $X_0$, writing $Q_i$ for localization on $X_i$. The ringed-topos definition supplies additive termwise direct images; use their canonical underived composition identifications throughout. Choose K-injective resolutions $j:f_*I\to J$, $k:g_*J\to K$, and $t:g_*f_*I\to T$, available on the small ringed sites by [prop-pur-kinjective-resolutions-exist]. The normalization of [lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-derived-pushforward-composition] says $g_*j$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Thus $k\circ g_*j$ is also a quasi-isomorphism. Neither $f_*I$ nor $g_*J$ is assumed K-injective.

2. Since $T$ and $K$ are K-injective, [lem-weil-pbc-kinjective-hom-derived] represents the derived morphism $Q_2(k\circ g_*j)\circ(Q_2t)^{-1}:Q_2T\to Q_2K$ by a unique homotopy class $u:T\to K$. Moreover $u\circ t$ and $k\circ g_*j$ are homotopic: their images under $Q_2$ agree, and the same cited Hom bijection applies with source $g_*f_*I$ and K-injective target $K$. This is a comparison between actual resolutions, with no claim that termwise direct image preserves K-injectives.

3. Compute $(\kappa_{h,g}R_f)\circ\kappa_{hg,f}$ at $Q_0I$. The comparison for $(hg,f)$, using $j$, is represented by $(hg)_*j=h_*g_*j$. The comparison for $(h,g)$ at $R_f(Q_0I)=Q_1J$, using $k$, is represented by $h_*k$. Both assertions are precisely the K-injective normalization of [lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-derived-pushforward-composition]. Hence this side, in the common source $Q_3(h_*g_*f_*I)$ and target $Q_3(h_*K)$, is $Q_3(h_*(k\circ g_*j))$. The identifications here are the canonical resolution identifications, not additional choices of the composition isomorphisms.

4. For the other side, $\kappa_{h,gf}$ is represented by $h_*t$, by [lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-derived-pushforward-composition]. The map $\kappa_{g,f}$ is $Q_2(g_*j)$. Apply [lem-sf-kinjective-derived-map-computation] to $h_*$, to this morphism, and to the resolutions $t:g_*f_*I\to T$ and $k:g_*J\to K$. Its representing map between K-injective targets is exactly $u$ from Step 2. Therefore $(R_h\kappa_{g,f})\circ\kappa_{h,gf}$ is $Q_3(h_*u)\circ Q_3(h_*t)=Q_3(h_*(u\circ t))$. Termwise application of the additive functor $h_*$ preserves the homotopy of Step 2: apply $h_*$ to the equation $ut-kg_*j=ds+sd$. Thus this equals $Q_3(h_*(k\circ g_*j))$, the expression in Step 3.

5. For any complex $M$ on $X_0$, choose a K-injective resolution $a:M\to I$. Naturality of the two composites in the cocycle square identifies their components at $Q_0M$ with their components at $Q_0I$ conjugated by the images of the isomorphism $Q_0a$. The equality just proved at $Q_0I$ therefore gives equality at $Q_0M$. Every derived object is represented by a complex (up to the canonical localization equivalence), so the natural transformations agree. Canonical changes of resolution are compatible by the identity and composition formulas of [lem-sf-kinjective-derived-map-computation].

6. Construct the identity comparison explicitly. For $a:N\to I$ a K-injective resolution on $X$, let $c_a:R_{\mathrm{id}_X}(QN)\xrightarrow{\sim}QI$ be the canonical resolution identification. Set $\epsilon_{X,QN}=(Qa)^{-1}\circ c_a$. For a derived morphism $v:QN\to QN'$ and resolutions $a:N\to I$, $b:N'\to J$, [lem-sf-kinjective-derived-map-computation] gives a representing class $w:I\to J$ with $Qw=Qb\circ v\circ(Qa)^{-1}$. Applying its formula for the identity functor gives $\epsilon_{X,QN'}R_{\mathrm{id}_X}(v)=v\epsilon_{X,QN}$. Its change-of-resolution formula proves independence of $a$. Thus these are a natural isomorphism to the identity; they are exactly those induced by termwise identity direct image. In particular at K-injective $I$, choosing $a=1_I$ makes $\epsilon_{X,QI}$ the identity under the canonical computation.

7. For the left unit law evaluate at K-injective $I$ on $X_0$, choose $j:f_*I\to J$ K-injective, and apply the normalization of [lem-weil-pbc-ringed-topoi-derived-pushforward-composition] with $g=\mathrm{id}_{X_1}$. It gives $\kappa_{\mathrm{id},f}=Q_1j$ in the resolution coordinates. Step 6 gives $\epsilon_{X_1,Q_1(f_*I)}=(Q_1j)^{-1}$ in the same coordinates, so their product is the identity. For the right unit law the first morphism is $\mathrm{id}_{X_0}$; use the identity resolution $I\to I$ of its direct image. The comparison $\kappa_{f,\mathrm{id}}$ is then the class of $f_*(1_I)$, while $R_f(\epsilon_{X_0,Q_0I})$ is the identity by Step 6. The product is again the identity. Transport along resolutions exactly as in Step 5 gives both laws at every derived object.

8. An identity-underlying-topos coefficient change is a ringed-topos morphism: its module direct image is restriction of scalars along the structural ring map, as in [def-weil-pbc-ringed-topos-morphism]. All resolutions above are taken in the module category for the ring on the relevant $X_i$; the composition normalization applies to these morphisms as well. No step used an isomorphism or flatness of the structural ring map. Consequently the same calculations give every asserted law when any of $f,g,h$ is such a coefficient change.
