### Original Step 1

For the one-generator polynomial case let $P=R[T]$. The polynomial ring is a domain, is free (hence flat) as an $R$-module, and is finite type over $R$; therefore it is Noetherian. For $\mathfrak q\subset P$ over $\mathfrak p$, the flat height formula gives $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)+\operatorname{ht}(\bar{\mathfrak q})$, where $\bar{\mathfrak q}$ is the corresponding prime of $(R/\mathfrak p)[T]$. The algebraic transcendence degree $\operatorname{trdeg}_R P$ is one: after passing to fraction fields, $\operatorname{Frac}(P)=\operatorname{Frac}(R)(T)$, while the fraction-field extensions over $R$ and over $P$ are algebraic, so the domain tower identity reduces $\operatorname{trdeg}_R P$ to $\operatorname{trdeg}_{\operatorname{Frac}(R)}\operatorname{Frac}(P)=1$.

### Original Step 2

Analyze the polynomial fibre. Put $D=R/\mathfrak p$, $F=\operatorname{Frac}(D)=\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, and localize $D[T]$ at $W=D\setminus\{0\}$, which gives $F[T]$ ($D[T]$ is a domain and every element of $F[T]$ is a polynomial over $D$ divided by a common denominator in $W$). Since $\mathfrak q\cap R=\mathfrak p$, the prime $\bar{\mathfrak q}$ and every prime of $D[T]$ contained in it meet $D$ only in $0$, hence are disjoint from $W$, and the inclusion-preserving bijection of the localization statement matches the chains of primes below $\bar{\mathfrak q}$ with those below its image; so the localization-prime and residue-field isomorphism identifies $\bar{\mathfrak q}$ with a prime $Q$ of $F[T]$, preserves the height of the prime interval below $\bar{\mathfrak q}$, and identifies $\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ with $\kappa(Q)$. If $Q=0$, then $\operatorname{ht}(Q)=0$ and $\kappa(Q)=F(T)$, so the residue transcendence degree is $1$. If $Q\ne0$, the PID statement for $F[T]$ makes $Q$ maximal, hence $\operatorname{ht}(Q)=1$; writing $\theta$ for the image of $T$, the quotient field $F(\theta)$ is a finite algebraic extension by the simple-algebraic power-basis theorem, so its transcendence degree over $F$ is $0$. Thus in both cases $\operatorname{ht}(\bar{\mathfrak q})+\operatorname{trdeg}_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\kappa(\mathfrak q)=1$. Substitution into the flat height formula proves the desired inequality, in fact equality, for $P=R[T]$.

### Original Step 3

For the one-generator quotient case write $S=P/(\mathfrak n)$, where $\mathfrak n$ is a nonzero prime of $P$ and $T$ maps to $\theta$. Since $P$ and $S$ are domains and $\mathfrak n\cap R=0$, a nonzero polynomial in $\mathfrak n$ gives a nontrivial algebraic relation for $\theta$ over $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. The power-basis theorem then makes $\operatorname{Frac}(R)(\theta)=\operatorname{Frac}(S)$ (the smallest subfield of $\operatorname{Frac}(S)$ containing $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ and $\theta$, since $S=R[\theta]$) a finite extension of $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$, of degree $d$ say. For $s\in S$ the $d+1$ elements $1,s,\ldots,s^d$ are linearly dependent over $\operatorname{Frac}(R)$, so $g(s)=0$ for a nonzero $g\in\operatorname{Frac}(R)[Y]$; multiplying $g$ by the product $c\in R\setminus\{0\}$ of the denominators of its coefficients gives a nonzero $cg\in R[Y]$ with $(cg)(s)=0$. Thus no element of $S$ is algebraically independent over $R$, and $\operatorname{trdeg}_R S=0$ by the definition of transcendence degree. For $\mathfrak q\subset S$ let $\mathfrak q\prime$ be its inverse image in $P$. Quotienting identifies $\kappa(\mathfrak q\prime)$ with $\kappa(\mathfrak q)$. Prime chains below $\mathfrak q$ correspond to chains from $\mathfrak n$ to $\mathfrak q\prime$; because $P$ is a domain and $\mathfrak n$ is nonzero, $0\subsetneq\mathfrak n$, so adjoining $0$ at the bottom shows $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)+1\le\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q\prime)$. Apply the polynomial result of Steps 1–2 to $\mathfrak q\prime$: $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q\prime)+\operatorname{trdeg}_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\kappa(\mathfrak q)=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)+1$. Comparing these inequalities (with the finite and infinite-height cases interpreted in the extended natural order) gives $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)\le\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)-\operatorname{trdeg}_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\kappa(\mathfrak q)$, which is the target because $\operatorname{trdeg}_R S=0$.

### Original Step 4

Finally induct on $n\ge0$ for the claim: for every inclusion of domains $R\subseteq S$ with $R$ Noetherian and $S$ generated as an $R$-algebra by $n$ elements $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ (such a list exists by the finite-type definition), and all primes $\mathfrak q\subset S$, $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap R$, the displayed inequality holds. The one-generator case is Steps 1–3: if $S=R[x_1]$, the kernel $\mathfrak n$ of $R[T]\to S$, $T\mapsto x_1$, is prime because $S$ is a domain; if $\mathfrak n=0$ then $S\cong R[T]$ and Steps 1–2 apply, otherwise Step 3 applies. For zero generators $S=R$, the primes and residue fields agree and all relevant transcendence degrees are zero. For $n>0$, set $A=R[x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1}]\subseteq S$ and $\mathfrak r=\mathfrak q\cap A$. The intermediate ring $A$ is a domain as a subring of $S$ and is Noetherian by finite-type Noetherianity. Apply the induction hypothesis to $R\subseteq A$ and the one-generator estimate of Steps 1–3 to $A\subseteq S$. Add the two inequalities. The residue fields form the tower $\kappa(\mathfrak p)\subseteq\kappa(\mathfrak r)\subseteq\kappa(\mathfrak q)$, so the field tower identity adds the residue transcendence degrees; the domain tower identity adds $\operatorname{trdeg}_R A$ and $\operatorname{trdeg}_A S$ to $\operatorname{trdeg}_R S$. Cancelling the intermediate height and collecting terms yields the displayed inequality.
