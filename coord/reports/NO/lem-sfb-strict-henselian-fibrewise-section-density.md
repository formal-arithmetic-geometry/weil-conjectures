Step 3 writes out the finite-variable polynomial-grid argument: nonzero coefficient induction followed by finite root avoidance. This works for arbitrary infinite subsets of Omega, without assuming a coefficient field inside R. Strict henselianity supplies a separably closed residue field. The finite-field contradiction T^N-T-1, derivative -1, proves infinitude rather than assuming algebraic closedness of the residue field. The d=0 induction base is explicit.

Step 4 uses the fact that every prime lies below the unique maximal ideal to choose t in m minus q. The surjection R/q to k makes R/q infinite, and multiplication by the nonzero image of t is injective in that domain. Thus the image of m, and each coordinate coset alpha_i+m, is infinite in Omega. The previous polynomial argument now gives the desired coordinate vector. The fibre product is affine and etale over R by the actual affine base-change statement. Its closed fibre has a point from e(s), since the new vector has the same residue. The strict-henselian neighbourhood-section theorem requires exactly etaleness and a point over the closed point, both verified. The fibre-product equations give pi a = r; the chosen polynomial then forces a_Omega into V. No approximation, completion, properness or algebraization theorem is being used.
