For the normal-field node the source argument is as follows (recorded here, not in the unsplit node). Put N = |Aut_k(E)|. Every finite separable subfield L of E has [L:k] distinct embeddings into E: normality of E ensures the conjugates lie in E. By 0BME each embedding extends to an automorphism of E, so [L:k] <= N. All finite subsets of E_s lie in a finite separable subfield. Among their integer degrees, bounded by N, choose a largest degree. Adjoining any further separable element cannot increase it, so that maximal finite subfield is all of E_s. This argument avoids using Nat-valued finSepDegree=0 as evidence for finite degree. Mathlib's Normal.algHomEquivAut (`FieldTheory/Normal/Defs.lean:207`) and Field.Emb.cardinal_eq (`FieldTheory/CardinalEmb.lean:343`) are candidate ingredients, not a one-line proof of this conclusion. The existing normal-degree node assumes finite E/k and positive characteristic and therefore cannot justify this inference.

For the finite-model node, 0BSR's first proof constructs S-prime; the following fraction variant avoids localizing A. Choose a finite set of field generators of each finite F_i/k and express each as a_i/b_i in Frac(D/r_i), with lifts a_i,b_i in D and b_i not in r_i. Include all these numerator and denominator lifts in C, together with, for every ordered pair of distinct fibre primes, an element in one and not the other (integral incomparability). The generated A-subalgebra is module-finite because these finitely many elements are integral. The separating elements make the contractions distinct; integral lying-over for the injective integral map C -> D makes them exhaust the C-fibre. Each Frac(C/s_i) contains all chosen fractions and hence F_i, and is finite over k since C is finite over A. For alpha in E_i, a p-power lies in F_i and hence in Frac(C/s_i); in characteristic zero E_i=F_i and the intermediate field is equal to it. D remains integral over C because the same monic equations over the image of A also have coefficients in C. This construction is a genuine auxiliary finite-model result, not an etale splitting clause moved to a new node. The case of an empty fibre uses no generators and C=image(A); lying-over still proves its fibre empty.
