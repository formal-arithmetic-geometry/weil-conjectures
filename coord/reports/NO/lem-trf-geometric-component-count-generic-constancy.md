### Retained Step 1: parent Step 1

Fix $y\in Y$ and let $S$ be the reduced induced closed subscheme on $\overline{\{y\}}$. Its underlying space is this closure and has generic point $y$: the closure of a point is irreducible, since if it is covered by two closed subsets, the one containing the point contains its closure. Form $h:X_S=X\times_Y S\to S$. This is finite type by base-change stability. The base-change count theorem gives $n_{X_S/S}=n_{X/Y}|_S$ and independence from all separably closed extensions. It therefore suffices to find a nonempty open of $S$ on which this function has one finite value. In the remaining steps rename $X_S,S,h,y$ as $X,S,h,\eta$; thus $S$ is irreducible and $\eta$ is its generic point.

Cites: `lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme`, `lem-sfb-finite-type-composition-base-change`, `lem-trf-geometric-fibre-component-count-base-change`. Source: literature/stacks-project/more-morphisms.tex, Tag 055A, proof paragraph 1; Tag 0556.

### Retained Step 2: lem-trf-generic-split-component-upper-bound

The generic fibre $X_\eta$ is finite type over $k=\kappa(\eta)$. Apply generic component splitting to choose a field $L$ and a finite etale morphism $a:\operatorname{Spec}L\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ such that every irreducible component of $G=X_\eta\times_k\operatorname{Spec}L$ remains irreducible and nonempty after every extension of $L$. Base-change stability makes $G$ finite type over $L$. The spectrum of a field is Noetherian (its only ideals are zero and the whole field), so the finite-type-over-Noetherian theorem and the finite-components theorem give a finite list of distinct components $A_1,\ldots,A_n$ covering $G$. Allow $n=0$, in which case $G$ is empty.

Cites: `lem-sfb-finite-type-composition-base-change`, `lem-sbc-generic-components-split`, `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-over-noetherian`, `lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-finitely-many-components`. ### Retained Step 3: lem-trf-generic-split-component-upper-bound

Apply the geometric generic-cover spreading theorem to $h:X\to S$ with the specified $L,a$ and the closed cover $(A_i)_{1\leq i\leq n}$. Its geometric-irreducibility hypothesis is precisely the component-splitting conclusion of Step 2. Obtain an open $W\subseteq S$ containing $\eta$ such that, for every $s\in W$ and every field extension $K/\kappa(s)$, there are a continuous surjective scheme morphism $p:Z\to (X_s)_K$ and a cover of $Z$ by $n$ nonempty irreducible subsets $B_i$. Each $\overline{p(B_i)}$ is irreducible and closed, and these $n$ subsets cover $(X_s)_K$. Every irreducible component $D$ of this fibre lies in one of them: apply irreducibility to the finite closed cover $D=\bigcup_i(D\cap\overline{p(B_i)})$. Maximality then gives $D=\overline{p(B_i)}$. Thus the fibre has at most $n$ components. For $n=0$, the cover forces $Z$ empty and surjectivity forces the fibre empty. In particular $n_{X/S}(s)\leq n$ on $W$.

Cites: `lem-sbc-geometric-generic-cover-spread`, `def-weil-irreducible-components`. Source: literature/stacks-project/more-morphisms.tex, Tag 055A, proof final paragraph, upper bound; geometric-cover spreading is the existing Tag 055G closure-and-shrinking input.

### Retained Step 6: lem-trf-etale-generic-component-lower-bound

For $t\in U$ and a separable closure $K$ of $\kappa(t)$, the projection $(P_t)_K\to P_t$ is surjective: $\operatorname{Spec}K\to\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(t)$ is surjective and surjectivity is stable under arbitrary base change. The closures of their images are $m$ closed irreducible subsets covering $P_t$. The finite closed-cover and maximality argument of Step 3 shows that each ordinary component is one of these closures; hence the ordinary count is at most $m=n_{P/T}(t)$.
