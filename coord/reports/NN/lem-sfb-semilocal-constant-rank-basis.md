Its actual statement assumes a maximal ideal q and an element f outside q with P_f free of rank r; it concludes both maximal-localization freeness and residue-quotient dimension r. The unit-ideal relation supplies such an f for each q by a direct contradiction. Substituting R, M, n meets every hypothesis. Its conclusion about the quotient, not an unasserted tensor comparison or material from its proof, is what the step uses. A dimension-n vector space has an n-element basis, and its quotient classes have representatives. The quoted statement in citations.json includes both clauses being used.

Step 3 proves the required finite CRT weights from maximality: distinct maximal ideals have sum R, so choose u_ab in q_b congruent to 1 modulo q_a and take their finite products. The two congruences follow factor by factor. This is an elementary calculation written in full, not a new lemma or an unexplained CRT citation. No idempotence of these lifts is assumed; generally it is false. For a single maximal ideal the product is empty and equals 1.

Step 4 is the elementary reduction of the finite weighted sum x_k = sum e_a x_a,k modulo q_b. Every summand except b is zero and the b coefficient is one.
