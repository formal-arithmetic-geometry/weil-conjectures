Independently checked the density assertion: for an inseparable element x with minimal polynomial P of degree d, P' = 0. Choose nonzero epsilon in K with arbitrarily small absolute value. The polynomial P(T) + epsilon T has derivative epsilon and hence distinct roots, all separable over K. At x its value is epsilon x. In the algebraic closure its factorization implies that some root y has |x-y| at most (|epsilon| |x|)^(1/d), tending to zero. For separable x use y=x, including x=0. This gives the new node's precise approximation conclusion, with no stability assumption. Multiplicativity of the spectral norm is provided by the existing complete-base spectral-norm node.

For dense cartesian permanence, specialize BGR 2.4.3/8 to K already complete. The content is genuinely more than an elementary assembly step: given a basis u_i of a finite subspace of V, norm equivalence over the complete base gives a positive c with N(sum a_i u_i) at least c max |a_i|. Choose w_i in W within c of u_i. The ultrametric inequality makes the linear map u_i -> w_i an isometry, so the finite subspace spanned by the w_i supplies the desired orthogonal basis. The approximation uses a bound on the whole coefficient space, not just closeness of individual basis vectors. This is a generic normed-space lemma; completeness of V or W is unnecessary.
