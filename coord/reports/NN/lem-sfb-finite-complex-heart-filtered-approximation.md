## Statement correction and consequences

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the original arbitrary-affine assertion is false. The local cohomology bound does not supply the missing t-exactness. HRS Section 8 is about a stable derived Ind-category, not the kernel closure of the particular degree-zero presentation class used in this item.

Here is a counterexample including the original local-bound hypothesis. Let k be algebraically closed of characteristic ell, S=beta(N), and X=Spec(C_lc(S,k)), where k has the discrete topology and C_lc denotes locally constant functions. This is the profinite scheme with components Spec(k) indexed by S. Its local rings are k; S is extremally disconnected, so X is w-contractible affine. Take Lambda=Q_ell with its usual topology. Write R=C(S,Q_ell). The function f extending n -> ell^n vanishes on the boundary S\N. Multiplication f on R is injective, because N is dense and Q_ell is Hausdorff. Multiplication f on Lambda_X has a nonzero sheaf kernel K, but Gamma(X,K)=0. This is HRS Example 4.2 and Remark 6.4 transported to X by Proposition 4.3; the pullback there is fully faithful and t-exact on sheaves. It is important that this invisible kernel alone does not yet prove failure for the larger finite-complex class.

For that extra step, use HRS Lemma 4.1. Write L: D(R)->D(X,Lambda) for its fully faithful colimit-preserving left adjoint to Gamma, which is exact and colimit-preserving. Put L_0(M)=H^0(L(M[0])). Every constructible scalar-model module on this X is lisse: constructible locally closed subsets of S are clopen, and a finite partition can be refined to a clopen one. Such a module corresponds to a perfect R-complex under Lemma 4.1(2). Thus a bounded termwise constructible complex is L(P) for a perfect complex P over R, and its N_K is H^0(L(P)). Here the symbol K in N_K denotes the complex, not the invisible kernel above.

The ring R is semihereditary (HRS Theorem 6.12). It is coherent and every finitely presented R-module has projective dimension at most one. Every R-module is a filtered colimit of finitely presented ones. Consequently L on modules has cohomological amplitude [-1,0]: first resolve finitely presented modules by length-one finite projectives, then use filtered colimits and exactness of filtered colimits. Coherence ensures H^q(P) is finitely presented for every perfect P. The two-row hyper-Tor calculation for P therefore gives

0 -> L_0(H^0(P)) -> H^0(L(P)) -> H^(-1)(L(H^1(P)[0])) -> 0.

The first arrow is the degree-zero counit L_0(Gamma(N_K))->N_K, since Gamma is exact and Gamma(L(P))=P. In particular that counit is injective for every N_K. The counit is still injective for every filtered colimit of such N_K: both Gamma and L_0 commute with these colimits, and the ambient module category has exact filtered colimits.

Let B=im(f:Lambda_X->Lambda_X) and Q=coker(f). Exactness of Gamma gives Gamma(B)=fR, which is isomorphic to R by multiplication f. The counit L_0(Gamma(B))->B, under this identification, is the quotient Lambda_X->B induced by f. Its kernel is the nonzero invisible sheaf K. Thus B is not in the stated class I_X. But Lambda_X is in I_X, and Q is H^0 of [Lambda_X --f--> Lambda_X] in degrees -1,0, hence in I_X. Since B=ker(Lambda_X->Q), the claimed kernel closure fails.

Finally HRS Lemma 8.6(2) applies to this qcqs characteristic-ell X and topological Q_ell and gives the uniform bound (8.1) (one may use c=2). The finite termwise constructible complexes above are derived constructible, since their terms are lisse. Pullback to each affine pro-etale U remains derived constructible, and inverse image is exact, so the hypothesis applies to precisely the N_K in this counterexample. Thus omitting the local-bound hypothesis did not manufacture the counterexample.

Repair: add that X is Noetherian, retaining the entire coefficient list, arbitrary module arrows, ambient extensions, same-universe presentations, and the original local-bound hypothesis. That hypothesis is now unnecessary for this smaller assertion. Noetherianity guarantees constructible subsets have finitely many irreducible components locally, allowing HRS Theorem 6.2(3) to supply the missing constructible-heart step. No finite-subextension replacement of the continuous Omega coefficient ring is made.
