Definite norms make the attained finite maximum positive, which guarantees a nonempty target basis and a coordinate c_j(u) that can be divided by. Linearity of coordinates follows directly by comparing the unique expansions of f(v+v') and f(av).

Step 2's sole cited node is def-weil-field. I opened its statement: it explicitly says that every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse, exactly what dividing by c_j(u) needs. citations.json quotes the full statement verbatim. The scalar functional's bound is the displayed identity and two inequalities: multiplicativity cancels the nonzero denominator, orthogonality bounds each target coordinate, and contractivity bounds the target norm by the input norm. No Hahn–Banach or existence theorem for continuous functionals is used.

Step 3 has no external citation. It explicitly proves existence and uniqueness of the decomposition by lambda(u)=1. It obtains the first lower bound on N(au+h) by Step 2 and the second by writing h=(au+h)-au. Since |-1|=1, symmetry is a consequence of the norm's stated homogeneity. The given ultrametric inequality supplies the reverse bound. Thus the asserted max equality is proved, including possible equality of the two summand norms; an ordinary bounded projection alone would not be enough without this calculation.

Step 4 also has no external citation. It constructs n-1 actual kernel vectors from the original algebraic basis and checks spanning and independence by their coefficients. Consequently there is no hidden rank-nullity or dimension-of-kernel lemma. Both n=1 and the empty kernel basis are checked. Its norm is the restriction, and the same maps on U still satisfy the hypothesis for each nonzero vector of H. Unique decomposition and the orthogonal norm formula then prove both the basis property and the required formula on V.
