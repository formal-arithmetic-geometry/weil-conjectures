I read the source scan, not just the imported statement: Theorem 3.3(ii) and proof 1°, 2°, 4° on printed pp.12–13; Proposition 1.2 and Corollary 1.3 on p.2 for the ringed image and uniqueness; Proposition 1.5(i) on p.3 for the affine neighbourhood; Proposition 2.3(ii),(iii) on pp.4–7, Proposition 2.4(A),(B) and Corollary 2.5 on pp.7–8 for the envelope and localization. I also read the statement of each node cited by the proof. The new envelope statement combines that single construction's outputs rather than moving the integral descent theorem into a new name: it mentions no integral cover and cannot alone conclude the item. The affine-neighbourhood and ringed-image inputs likewise make no integral-descent assertion.

For step 1 the new ringed-image node says exactly that the image is stable under generalization. pqf-lem-integral-univclosed says integral implies universally closed, which supplies the closed image of the closure of the chosen point. The definition of integral supplies affine inverse images; quasi-compactness of j' and continuous images prove retrocompactness directly. The source's statement that this is clear was expanded into this argument.

For step 2 Proposition 1.5(i) supplies an affine arrow over a neighbourhood of the entire image; closedness of q then removes the image of its complement. The affine-morphism-permanence node explicitly supplies both base-change stability and affine source over an affine target. The integral definition supplies Y_D affine. This checks the affine-source hypothesis needed later without incorrectly assuming Y or F' globally affine.

For step 3 the new envelope statement applies because step 1 has proved generalization stability and retrocompactness. It gives h^{-1}(H)=k(H) and hence disjointness of the boundary image. The existing subset-maximal-generalization node applies to the affine open D of the locally Noetherian Z, so D is Noetherian. Its statement allows arbitrary subsets, not just constructible or closed ones, exactly as needed for B. I checked its statement and its proof-dependency list; it does not depend on this item.

For step 4 the canonical-local-spectrum node explicitly identifies the image as the generalizations of b. The envelope node explicitly gives the point-localization comparison, including the boundary image, so maximality makes that boundary a singleton. A closed point of the local spectrum is a closed singleton. Since it is outside H, the closure-disjointness criterion in Proposition 2.4 applies. This is where E_T becomes a scheme; it is not treated as one before the criterion.

For step 5 the integral-base-change and surjective-base-change nodes say exactly what the names suggest. Affine permanence gives P affine after the reduction of step 2. Flatness and quasi-compactness are separately supplied by the two base-change nodes, and monicity follows by cancellation in the fibre-product definition. The ringed-image node's factorization assertion gives the cartesian square on every test scheme, so this proof does not confuse a containment of point sets with an arbitrary scheme-theoretic factorization. The factorization assertion is itself a consequence of its ringed-space identification: a continuous map into the subset factors through its subspace topology; the sheaf map factors uniquely by inverse-image adjunction; on stalks the new map remains local. This also explains why equality of images gives a unique isomorphism, rather than just an unspecified equivalence of sets.

For step 6 I read the general affineness node (Stacks 05YU, limits.tex, proposition-affine, and its integral parenthesis). It permits an arbitrary integral surjective cover with affine source, so applies to P to E_T without finiteness or a Noetherian assumption on Y_T. This is a generic off-unit tool, declared in split_report.tool_uses, and the strengthened Stacks formulation supplies precisely Raynaud Proposition 3.1(ii). The resulting affine E_T makes k_T an isomorphism by the new envelope equivalence, contradicting the nonempty boundary. This proves B empty rather than merely proving a stalkwise schematicity assertion, which Raynaud Remark 2.6 warns would be insufficient.

For step 7 the same induced ringed space is used on every open H intersect D; thus the local affine charts really prove it is a scheme by the definition, with no separate gluing isomorphisms to assume. The stalks of an inverse-image sheaf at a point are the original stalks, so its canonical map is flat. Monicity is checked using the inverse-image adjunction, not inferred from point injectivity alone. Step 1 gives the precise quasi-compactness condition on all affine opens of Z. This assembly is elementary from the cited definitions, not another lemma node.

For step 8 the two factorization equivalences are stated in the ringed-image node for all test schemes. Their conjunction is exactly the fibre-product universal property from def-pc-fibre-product. Applying it to F' gives p and the isomorphism over Y, including the cartesian clause. No additional descent theorem is hidden in the identification.

I checked edge cases: H empty gives C=0 and the empty envelope, F empty and the required empty pullback; H all of Z gives F=Z and the identity image, even for an integral cover with nilpotents and no flatness. For a generic-point localization the source need not be an open subscheme, which is why the Mathlib finite-presentation open-immersion theorem is not accepted as a match. For an open H, the envelope boundary can have nonempty image whose closure must be checked; the proof never drops that closure. No general assertion about a flat monomorphism without quasi-compactness is made: Raynaud's glued local spectra over Spec Z show why that hypothesis matters for the topological identification. A Noetherian ring can have unbounded global dimension, so the maximal-generalization step uses the existing local finite-height result through the existing selection node instead of assuming a global dimension bound.
