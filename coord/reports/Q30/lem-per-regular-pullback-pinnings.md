## Statement correction: STATEMENT-DISPUTED

The imported statement explains geometric regularity by saying that **every field extension** of the residue field gives a regular fibre. With the project's Noetherian definition of regular scheme this is stronger than standard regularity and is not equivalent to the ILO hypothesis. I changed this to **every finite extension**. This corrects a definition of the hypothesis; the intended ILO regular-morphism theorem, including its absence of finite-type or perfect-field assumptions, is preserved.

Explicit counterexample to the imported equivalence: let k=Q and F=Q(t_1,t_2,...) with infinitely many algebraically independent variables. The field extension Spec F -> Spec Q is a regular morphism in the standard sense, between Noetherian excellent schemes. After the field extension Q -> F, its coordinate ring is F tensor_Q F. If I is the kernel of multiplication to F, then I/I^2 is Omega_{F/Q}, an infinite-dimensional F-vector space with basis dt_i. Thus I is not finitely generated, so this base-changed ring is not Noetherian and not a regular ring in the project's definition. Finite extensions do not have this defect. The correct standard definition is also the one used by the already existing regular-ring-map nodes: Stacks 07C1 and 07QI test geometric regularity on finite (in particular finite purely inseparable) extensions. No new formal lemma is asserted about this diagnostic example.

The other statement changes are explicit references to the inverse-image definition and to the two helpers specifying the comparison and inverse classes. The dimensions, costalk shifts, coefficient ring and all conclusions are unchanged. In the proof the implicit localization restriction is written j^* before applying i^!, avoiding a misleading unlocalized expression.

The inherited blueprint ancestry referred to Step 3 of lem-per-artin-gabber-affine-curve. The current DAG ancestor places dimension-normalized Verdier duality in Step 1. The provenance was updated accordingly, without changing the ancestor, its statement or its unit.
