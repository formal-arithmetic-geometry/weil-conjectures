Step 1 cites exactly the three existing statements giving one-variable Noetherian normality, the local/unit criterion, and completeness for the combined ideal. I opened and read each statement. Step 2 cites the existing coefficient-reduction kernel node, whose arbitrary-variable statement requires the coefficient ideal to be finitely generated. C is Noetherian, so n satisfies that hypothesis. It is applied to n, not to an unrelated ideal. The new coordinate node has exactly the nonzero reduction hypothesis just obtained and gives the automorphism and last-axis condition used. I checked that an automorphism preserves prime-ideal chains, and that last-axis reduction equals reduction of coefficients in A by m. No infinite residue field or linear change of variables is assumed.

Step 3 uses only the preparation leaf. Step 1 supplied a complete local A, and step 2 supplied the nonzero residue series. The leaf's definition gives a distinguished polynomial and unit, so multiplying by the inverse unit puts F in q. F=1 is excluded by properness, not by an unstated positive-order hypothesis.

Step 4 uses all three relevant clauses of the normal-domain factorization node: equality as a finite product, distinguishedness, and nonzero domain polynomial quotients. Primality selects a factor by induction on that finite product. Repetitions cause no problem. I did not infer that an arbitrary irreducible element of a normal domain is prime, which would be false without further hypotheses; the domain quotient is explicitly supplied by the new node (and can be proved by monic division and injection into K[T]/(h)).

Step 5 uses exactly the quotient leaf, applied with I=m to the distinguished selected factor. Its domain source is the explicit output of step 4. A quotient being a nonzero domain implies its kernel ideal is proper prime directly by the definitions. Nonzeroness of h follows from its leading coefficient 1. Height one is used only after this nonzero prime ideal has been established; the final generator is the inverse image under the actual automorphism. All these uses have verbatim quotes in citations.json; none uses a cited node's hidden proof.

F=1 gives the empty product in the polynomial node; positive-degree factors are nonunits as polynomials. The quotient leaf allows F=1, giving two zero rings, with no claim of a domain in that case. Coefficient rings may be nonfactorial normal singularities and may have finite or imperfect residue field; none of the steps assumes otherwise. Over a field this specializes to the familiar formal-series case. The coefficient normality assumption is essential to descent of monic field factors and was not dropped.
