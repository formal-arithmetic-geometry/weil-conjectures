## Review by Q30

I checked the item against the sources just listed and read the complete statement of every cited node, not just its title or proof. There are no new-node statements to audit. The existing statement has all required data: arbitrary ring maps, the ordinary semilinear system categories, specified composition and identity comparisons, an actual scheme isomorphism, and a cartesian square in the second clause. It asks for the geometric lift to be invertible, not for an arbitrary clopen inclusion to induce an equivalence. No conclusion about the proper/open exchange mate is taken from this lemma.

In step 1 the only cited node is `def-sp-varying-ring-operations`. Its statement supplies ordinary inverse-image composition as the unit-preserving adjoint comparison and the normalized identity comparisons. These are the given ordinary comparisons of the item. I wrote out both composites: `HF` reduces along `s o t = id_X`, and `FH` along `t o s = id_U`. Thus their sources and targets are correct. Since they are transformations of functors on system categories already, their components commute with semilinear transitions; no appeal to faithfulness of derived evaluation or an omitted transition-compatibility theorem is made. Composing and inverting these given natural isomorphisms is elementary. The proof constructs no new pullback functor and uses no additional result on its exactness.

For step 2, the statement of `lem-sf-quasi-inverse-hom-bijection` explicitly assumes two natural inverse comparisons without triangle identities and concludes precisely bijectivity of the indicated Hom map. Those are the maps of step 1. Its Mathlib match uses `Equivalence.mk` (Equivalence.lean:353), `fullyFaithfulFunctor` (:551), and `map_bijective` (Functor/FullyFaithful.lean:173); I read these declarations. The statement of `lem-trf-fully-faithful-ess-surj-equivalence` requires that Hom bijectivity and an object whose image is isomorphic to each target object. Here the latter witness is explicitly `HN`, with isomorphism `(u_U)_N`; I also read `Functor.asEquivalence` at Equivalence.lean:642. The categories can be placed in one larger universe, as that leaf requires. The conclusion that the *particular* `H=t^*` is a quasi-inverse uses the two already constructed natural isomorphisms, rather than claiming that an arbitrary inverse chosen by `asEquivalence` equals H. The two requested inverse comparisons are retained; I do not identify them with an adjointified unit. This step is marked non-elementary and cites both leaves.

This is the three-line universal-property argument, not a hidden isomorphism-detection lemma. It is elementary. Properness and quasi-compact openness are stated but unused here, which is legitimate because the universal property is sufficient; the statement was not altered to suppress them.
