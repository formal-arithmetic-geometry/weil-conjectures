## Proof details retained for the new nodes

These arguments are retained here for the next worker; their node statements contain no proof or proof dependencies.

For compatible charts: choose an etale chart on the target and refine each source etale locally to a chart compatible with its pullback. This is the usual chart lemma for a morphism of fine/fs log schemes, independent of smoothness. Use the same target chart for both sources. Products of the etale source neighbourhoods over the target neighbourhood are etale neighbourhoods of the ordinary product. Refine them by affine opens. Over each affine open of W, choose finitely many such neighbourhoods whose open images cover it; their rings are Noetherian because the base is locally Noetherian and the neighbourhoods are etale. The disjoint union is an fpqc cover. This chart theorem is a genuine input: I have not hidden it behind the definition of an fs log scheme. There is no claim that a Kummer characteristic map can be represented by a Kummer map of arbitrary whole charts; unit twists would obstruct that claim without suitable root extensions.

For the saturated-chart universal property: an integral monoid is a submonoid of its group completion. Any map from S to an integral monoid factors uniquely through the image S^int. If the target is saturated, extend its group map to S^fs: for u in S^fs, some positive multiple nu lies in S^int, so the group image of u belongs to the target by saturation. This is unique because S^int and S^fs have the same group completion. Apply this at log stalks of an fs test T. The resulting unique extensions glue to local log sections, hence global sections, because all stalk extensions agree under restriction. Their structural values extend beta to the monoid algebra of S^fs. Together with the given A-map this gives exactly a ring map B -> Gamma(T,O_T), hence a scheme map to Spec B; the associated-log universal property supplies its log map. Conversely a log map to C gives those structural values, and both constructions undo one another. This proves the asserted bijection, including naturality and arbitrary non-affine test schemes. The assumptions that S^fs is finitely generated and B is Noetherian are explicitly present in the new node; the item proves them before applying it. No unique choice of roots in a ring is assumed: uniqueness takes place in the integral log monoid, with its specified group-completion map.

For the algebraic extension lemma, here is a more explicit route than a bare reference to roots. Write F, E, J for the nonzero-evaluation faces of P,Q,R. Localize by these faces. Put C=(P-F)/F^gp, A=(Q-E)/E^gp and D=(R-J)/J^gp. These are sharp fs monoids; the induced C -> A is injective and Kummer, and C -> D is local (only zero maps to zero), since a(h(p))=b(k(p)). Their rational group completions satisfy A^gp tensor Q = C^gp tensor Q and their rational cones agree. The rational group of their pushout consequently identifies with D^gp tensor Q and the cone generated by the two source monoids is exactly the cone of D. This cone is pointed. A nonzero element of A maps to a nonzero point of that cone: take a positive multiple from C and use locality of C -> D. A nonzero element of D also stays nonzero.

Let G=Q^gp amalg_{P^gp} R^gp and N be the saturation of the image of Q amalg_P R in G. Let L be the face of N mapping to zero in this rational cone. Its inverse images in Q and R are exactly E and J by the previous paragraph. The original nonzero values a|E and b|J give a character of the subgroup generated by E and J in G. To check well-definedness, a relation between E^gp and J^gp in G is induced by some p in P^gp. Its image in A^gp is zero; injectivity of C^gp -> A^gp forces p into F^gp. The equality ah=bk therefore gives equality of the two unit characters on that relation. The group Omega^times is divisible (every nonzero element has an nth root for every n>0 in the algebraically closed field), so extend this subgroup character to G. Define d(u) by this character for u in L and by zero otherwise. Since L is a face, this is a multiplicative monoid map. Its restrictions to Q and R equal the given a,b, proving the new node. This argument permits torsion in G and roots whose order equals char Omega; it uses existence and never separability or unique roots.

In this last argument one can equivalently work after localizing S by the images of E and J. The cone quotient discards those units; all original nonzero chart values lie in that unit face. In particular this is an extension of the actual field-valued chart data, not merely a statement that a sharp monoid pushout has a point.
