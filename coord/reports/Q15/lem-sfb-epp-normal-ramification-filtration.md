Here is an explicit counterexample to the old statement, with p=2. Let k=F_3(a), A=B'=k[[t]], K=E^0=k((t)), and E^1=K(y), y^3-y=t^{-1}. The polynomial has no root in K: a negative t-adic valuation of a root would satisfy 3v(y)=-1, impossible for an integer valuation; a root of nonnegative valuation cannot have right side t^{-1}. It is an irreducible cubic, and its three roots y,y+1,y+2 lie in E^1, so it is cyclic Galois of degree 3. The equation gives v(y)=-1/3; thus its ramification index is 3 and its residue field is k. The single old step satisfies (c) for p=2: degree and index 3 are prime to 2, with trivial residue extension.

Now take K'=K(x), x^3-x=a/t. The same valuation argument makes K'/K a totally ramified cyclic cubic with residue field k. In a field compositum W containing x,y, z=x/y is a unit and
z^3=(t x+a)/(t y+1).
Both t x and t y have positive valuation, hence the residue of z has cube a. Since a is not a cube in k, the residue extension of W/K' contains k(a^{1/3})/k, a nontrivial purely inseparable extension. In particular W/K' has degree at least 3 and at most 3, so it has degree 3 and is the sole field factor of E^1 tensor_K K'. Its residue extension is not separable. This step is not (a) because the characteristic is 3 rather than 2; not (b) because the degree is 3 rather than 2; not (c) or (d) because the residue extension is inseparable.
