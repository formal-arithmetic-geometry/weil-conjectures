## Review by Q15

I read Gieseker's technical claim on printed pp.32-33 and its proof on pp.36-37 in the online transcription. The argument selects the minimal primary factor for the distinguished component, takes the intersection of the other factors, and proves injection by intersecting this ideal with a sufficiently high power of the distinguished component's prime. That is exactly the argument here. The Hilbert slope and allowance for the empty complement are additional bookkeeping from Stacks varieties.tex: 0AYW, lemma-degree-in-terms-of-components, and 0AYU, lemma-degree-birational-pullback. I read both statements and their proofs locally, together with 0BEM, lemma-numerical-polynomial-from-euler, and the coordinate/Euler interface already present in the DAG. I did not infer a theorem from a definition or borrow a construction hidden in a cited proof.

For the sole new node I read Altman-Kleiman Theorem 20.27 and Exercises 20.25-20.26: homogeneous cores of primary factors yield homogeneous primary decomposition. The theorem supplies the genuinely missing graded assertion. The remaining interfaces are the ordinary primary-decomposition calculus: a prime containing a finite intersection contains a factor, so minimal primes occur; finitely many generators b_i of a radical with b_i^{h_i} in Q give radical^h contained in Q for h=1+sum(h_i-1); at a prime not containing that radical, one such power is invertible, and that localized primary factor is the unit ideal. Finite intersections commute with localization by multiplying finitely many denominators. These explain why the statement includes no false minimal-component uniqueness or global sheaf decomposition claim. Hilbert basis for the polynomial ring gives the Noetherian clause. This proof handoff is recorded here, not inserted as proof into the unsplit node.

Step 1 cites the coordinate-ideal node for exactly X=Proj(B/I), the new algebra node for homogeneous finite primary factors with distinct prime radicals and finite generation, and the quotient-Proj presentation for a projective proper closed subscheme, including the unit ideal. The Proj definition and spectrum/minimal-prime statement give the component identification on standard charts. The prime of Y is minimal, so the factor removed exists and is unique in this decomposition. The base is a field, hence locally Noetherian, and every homogeneous ideal used in the presentation is finitely generated.

Step 2 uses the radical-power clause of the new node and writes the kernel computation: p_1^Q intersect J is contained in Q_1 intersect J=I. I checked this after localization and taking degree zero; the degree-one variable is invertible on each chart, so products can be represented with degree-zero factors. This proves the sheaf injection directly using the cited Proj, closed-immersion and ideal-power definitions. It is elementary once the cited algebra decomposition is available. Step 3 uses the actual inclusion of higher ideal powers and local free rank-one trivializations of the twist. A section in the kernel is zero locally and thus globally; no unjustified exactness of higher cohomology is claimed.

Step 4 uses the prime and localization clauses of the new algebra node, with Proj's point and chart descriptions. I verified support as a finite union, absence of the removed generic point, and equality of the remaining generic local rings. Embedded primes only contribute proper, hence zero-dimensional, subsets of curve components; they remain in J and are not discarded. Local ring equality implies equality of ambient generic lengths, exactly as required.

Step 5 applies the existing multiplicity-weighted degree node with X and C proper, of dimension at most one, and an invertible hyperplane module. Its statement explicitly gives finite positive generic lengths, the entire degree sum, and the empty-sum case. For C the proper/invertible-coherent node supplies coherence and Noetherianity, and Snapper supplies a polynomial of degree at most dim(C), which is at most one.
