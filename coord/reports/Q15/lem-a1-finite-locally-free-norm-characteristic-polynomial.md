### Retained old step 1

First suppose $A=0$. Since $\varphi$ is unital, $1_B=\varphi(1_A)=\varphi(0_A)=0_B$, so $B=0$ too. Both polynomial rings are zero rings. There is a unique function $B\to A$ and a unique function $B[T]\to A[T]$; these satisfy every local determinant condition, since their spectra have no points. The polynomial extension has empty source and target, so the finite, flat, locally finitely presented and fibre-degree conditions are vacuous. Its unique polynomial equals $T^r+\sum_{j<r}0T^j$, and its value at the unique element of $B$ is zero. Henceforth assume $A\ne0$.

### Retained old step 2

Let $f:\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $E=f_*\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}B}$. The hypotheses of [def-pc-finite-locally-free] give finiteness, flatness and local finite presentation. The rank in [lem-weil-pbc-finite-flat-rank-iso] is explicitly the residue-field dimension in our degree hypothesis, hence is $r$ everywhere. Apply [lem-sc-finite-pushforward-rank-on-open] to the whole target. Thus $E$ is locally free of constant rank $r$. In the meaning of [def-amb-finite-locally-free-module], choose a trivializing open cover $V_a$ with $E|_{V_a}\cong\mathcal O_{V_a}^{\oplus r}$; $E$ is quasi-coherent. The structure sheaf is itself locally free of rank one, so it too is quasi-coherent.

Cited nodes: def-pc-finite-locally-free, lem-weil-pbc-finite-flat-rank-iso, lem-sc-finite-pushforward-rank-on-open, def-amb-finite-locally-free-module.

Refine the $V_a$ by the principal-open basis of [lem-weil-pbc-spec-localization-away-basic-open]. By [lem-weil-pbc-spec-basic-opens-unit-ideal-quasi-compact], choose a finite subcover $D(f_1),\ldots,D(f_m)$; the $f_i$ generate the unit ideal. Discard empty members. ### Retained old step 4

The ring isomorphism $B\otimes_A A[T]\cong B[T]$ sends $b\otimes\sum_j a_jT^j$ to $\sum_j b\varphi(a_j)T^j$; its inverse sends $\sum_j b_jT^j$ to $\sum_j b_j\otimes T^j$. The tensor relations and coefficientwise polynomial operations show these are inverse ring homomorphisms. By [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra], the polynomial extension on spectra is therefore the base change of $f$ along $\operatorname{Spec}A[T]\to\operatorname{Spec}A$. It is finite by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence](a), flat by [lem-weil-pbc-flat-morphism-base-change], and locally of finite presentation by [lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence](3). Its rank at every point is $r$ by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-flat-rank-iso](2). Hence it is finite locally free of degree $r$ in the required sense. Applying [lem-sc-finite-pushforward-rank-on-open] also to this morphism makes its structure-sheaf pushforward locally free of rank $r$, hence quasi-coherent by [def-amb-finite-locally-free-module].

### Retained old step 5

Put $g_i=C(f_i)\in A[T]$. They generate the unit ideal because an equality $\sum_i u_if_i=1$ maps to $\sum_i C(u_i)g_i=1$. There are canonical isomorphisms $A[T]_{g_i}\cong A_{f_i}[T]$ and $B[T]_{g_i}\cong B_{f_i}[T]$: send a polynomial divided by $f_i^n$ to its coefficientwise fractions. Conversely a polynomial with finitely many fractional coefficients has a common denominator $f_i^N$, where $N$ is the largest of their exponents. The localization relations show the maps are well-defined and inverse. If $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ is the basis in step 3, its constant polynomials form a basis of $B_{f_i}[T]$: expand each coefficient uniquely in the $e_k$ and gather the coefficient polynomials for each $k$. Thus both pairs $(A,B)$ and $(A[T],B[T])$ have finite principal covers with free module rank $r$.

### Retained old step 7

Define the determinant norm on either pair by the real construction $N_{D/R}(u)=(-1)^r\chi_u(0)$. ### Retained old step 8
