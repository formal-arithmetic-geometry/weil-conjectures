### Old step 1

Work componentwise. By the componentwise-normalization definition, each irreducible component of the reduced finite-type scheme S is replaced by its function-field normalization. The normalization existence theorem gives a finite, surjective and birational map on each integral component. The source has integrally closed local domains by the definition of normalization, hence is normal and reduced; finiteness over a finite-type Q-scheme gives finite type over Q. Taking the finite disjoint union of the component normalizations gives the asserted normal, reduced, finite-type scheme B_J(S) and a map to S that is surjective and birational on each component. Since V(J) is nowhere dense, no generic point is in the center, so the ordinary blowup and its normalization retain all generic points.

### Old step 2

The ordinary blowup has invertible extended ideal by the principalization theorem, and its universal property gives a unique lift of every morphism to S on which the extended ideal is invertible. Let Y be a normal S-scheme whose generic points map to generic points of S and on which J is invertible. On each integral component, the lift Y to the ordinary blowup is dominant; the existing normalization-lifting theorem factors this lift uniquely through the normalization of the blowup. These componentwise factorizations glue because they agree on the generic points and on overlaps by uniqueness. Thus B_J(S) is final among the stated normal S-schemes. Applying finality to an S-automorphism of B_J(S) gives the identity.

### Old step 3

For the comparison over S^nu, construct the maps by the two universal properties rather than by commuting integral closure with adjoining fractions. Put X=B_J(S) and Y=B_{J O_{S^nu}}(S^nu). The morphism Y	o S has invertible J and Y is normal, so the finality proved in Step 2 gives a unique S-map Y	o X. Conversely, X is normal and its componentwise generic maps to S lift uniquely to S^nu by the normalization-lifting theorem. The pullback of J to X is invertible by principalization, so the blowup universal property over S^nu gives a map X	o Bl_{J O_{S^nu}}(S^nu); its generic map is dominant on each component, and the same normalization-lifting theorem factors it uniquely through Y. The two composites are identities because both are maps over S (respectively over S^nu) inducing the identity on the generic points, and the uniqueness clauses in Steps 2 and in the normalization-lifting theorem apply componentwise. This yields the canonical isomorphism X\simeq Y over S^nu. The calculation is valid for J=(x^2,y^3) in Q[x,y]: it uses the universal properties and makes no claim that normalization commutes with adjoining the fractions x^2/y^3.

### Old step 4

Let h:T→S be smooth. The smoothness criterion [sbc-lem-smooth-criterion] gives that h is flat and locally of finite presentation with geometrically regular, hence geometrically reduced, fibres. On affine charts over the reduced finite-type Q-scheme S, the standard smooth-algebra argument in ILO Lemma 2.2.9 shows that T is reduced. The same lemma identifies the integral closures defining the componentwise normalizations after this smooth base change; the identification is local for localization and therefore glues to T^nu≅S^nu×_S T. Applying the same argument to the Rees charts of the ordinary blowup gives B_{J O_T}(T)≅B_J(S)×_S T. The projection is smooth by stability of smoothness under base change. The open S\V(J) is dense in every component of S.
