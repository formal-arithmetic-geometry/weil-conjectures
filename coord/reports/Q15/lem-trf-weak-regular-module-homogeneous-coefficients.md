Edge cases checked mathematically and against the definitions before writing:

- `s=0, n=0`: J=0, one empty multi-index, monomial 1. The premise is exactly
  membership of the sole coefficient in zero, which gives the conclusion.
- `s=0, n>0`: Mathlib's `antidiagonalTuple_zero_succ` is empty. The coefficient
  family has empty domain and the conclusion is vacuous.
- `n=0` for arbitrary s: `antidiagonalTuple_zero_right` gives only the zero
  tuple, its monomial is 1, and premise and conclusion both assert `c ∈ JN`.
- `N=0` (also the zero ring): multiplication on successive zero quotients is
  injective; every coefficient belongs to every relevant submodule.
- A unit parameter, for example s=1 and y=1: weak regularity holds on every
  module, and JN=N makes the conclusion automatic. Requiring `IsRegular` would
  incorrectly exclude this case.
- Negative test: R=N=k[epsilon]/(epsilon^2), y=epsilon, n=1 and coefficient
  epsilon. The sum is zero, but multiplication by epsilon is not injective.
  Thus the injectivity assumption cannot be discarded. For an actual failure
  without injectivity, take y=0, n=1 and coefficient 1 over a nonzero field:
  the zero monomial sum lies in J^2N=0, whereas 1 is not in JN=0.
- Over k[x], N=R and y=x, the criterion is the expected cancellation of x^n
  modulo x^(n+1); with two polynomial parameters it is independence of the
  degree-n monomials modulo the next ideal power.
