## Review by Q15

I read delivery-errors B through E against the final node and the sources, rather than treating the dry run as a mathematical review. The statement introduces $Y$, its geometric point, both sites, their size convention and sheaf categories, $L$, $S$ and the proposed right adjoint. It needs no extra finiteness or coefficient hypothesis. The proof is for sheaves of sets; it does not silently identify this category with modules, complexes, or topological-space sheaves.

Step 1 uses the actual cofilteredness assertion of `lem-weil-pbc-pointed-etale-neighbourhoods-cofiltered` with base $Y$ and field $\Omega$. The base is an arbitrary scheme, as allowed; the theorem does not require noetherianity. The pro-etale definition `def-weil-proetale-geometric-stalk` explicitly says that the opposite index category is filtered; I use that sentence, not an unstated construction from its proof. Its definition and `def-weil-pbc-geometric-stalks` give exactly the two stalk diagrams in the item. `def-weil-size-convention` provides a small site skeleton in the larger universe and forms these colimits there. The category of pointed objects is small there as a union of sets of lifts and pointed arrows, so the later commutation theorem's smallness hypothesis is met. The cocone universal property in `def-weil-pbc-category-colimit` gives both the displayed germ relation and the functor on sheaf maps. This avoids assuming a colimit over an uncontrolled large category.

Step 2 uses `def-weil-pbc-etale-site` for etale, jointly surjective covering arrows and `def-weil-geometric-point` for the algebraically closed field. I opened `lem-weil-pbc-etale-cover-geometric-point-lift`: its statement provides a lift with the same field and the exact composite, which is what this step uses. It is not merely a theorem on surjectivity of underlying points. This is the etale lifting fact used in the proof of 04FM.

Step 3 follows the lifting argument of 0F6C with all the non-elementary ingredients explicitly cited. `def-weil-pbc-proetale-site` makes the original family fpqc with weakly etale arrows. `lem-galois-fpqc-covering-base-change` applies to an arbitrary morphism, so it applies to $u:\operatorname{Spec}\Omega\to U$. `def-weil-pbc-fpqc-covering` explicitly gives joint surjectivity, hence a nonempty base-changed member. `lem-weil-pbc-weakly-etale-composition-base-change` says the base-changed projection is weakly etale. Schemes are locally affine as in `def-pc-scheme`; the forward direction of `lem-weil-pbc-weakly-etale-source-locality`, applied to the affine-open covering, makes the chosen affine restriction weakly etale. The ring/scheme equivalence needed to apply the field theorem is actually in `def-weil-pbc-ind-etale-algebra`. `lem-weil-pbc-weak-etale-field` says all residue fields of this algebra are separable algebraic over the base field, with no finite-presentation assumption. Then `lem-weil-pbc-separable-extension-embeds-separably-closed` is applied over $k=\Omega$, not over $\kappa(y)$: the extension is $\kappa(z)/\Omega$ and the target is the same $\Omega$. Algebraic closedness from `def-weil-geometric-point` implies the required separable closedness. `def-weil-affine-spectrum` converts the composite ring map to an $\Omega$-scheme map; it is a section because the ring map is an $\Omega$-algebra map. `def-pc-fibre-product` then supplies equality with the original morphism $u$. Every cited statement gives the indicated fact. No assertion that an arbitrary weakly etale map is surjective is used.

Step 4 proves the sheaf clause directly, using the lifting already established in steps 2 and 3. The sheaf condition is that in `def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology`. `def-weil-pbc-etale-site` explicitly identifies overlaps with scheme fibre products. In the pro-etale case I checked membership of the scheme fibre product in the site: its projection to $U_j$ is a weakly etale base change, and its composite to $Y$ is weakly etale by `lem-weil-pbc-weakly-etale-composition-base-change`. The full category of $Y$-morphisms in `def-weil-pbc-proetale-site` then gives the same universal property. This supplies the overlap identification from cited statements, rather than treating that identification as part of a definition which does not say it. `def-pc-fibre-product` supplies exactly the unique lift of a compatible pair to an overlap. Matching then makes the choice of a lift irrelevant, and surjectivity gives uniqueness of the glued function. This is the elementary equalizer check required by the sheaf definition, with the argument written in the step, not hidden in a new lemma or a source tag. Composition gives the stated restrictions and functor on sets.

Steps 5 and 6 write both adjunction maps. Their only construction beyond the displayed formulas is the cocone universal property of `def-weil-pbc-category-colimit`. The relation has the correct restriction direction from step 1. `def-weil-pbc-sites-and-cohomology` explicitly identifies sheaf maps with natural transformations, so the pointwise naturality check is sufficient to obtain a sheaf map. The inverse follows by colimit uniqueness and function extensionality; the two displayed identities with $\alpha$ and $t$ verify naturality in both variables. Thus there is no reliance on a generic Yoneda theorem whose statement has not been supplied, or on a skyscraper theorem assuming the desired point.

Step 7 uses `lem-weil-pbc-site-sheaf-sets-limits-sectionwise` for arbitrary small diagrams of set sheaves and only specializes it to finite $J$. It says that the presheaf limit is already a sheaf, not merely that a particular representable is a sheaf. `lem-weil-pbc-filtered-colimits-commute-finite-limits` is stated for a small filtered $I$, finite $J$, and a functor on $I\times J$; these are precisely the data constructed here. The same pointed-neighbourhood index is used for all entries of the finite diagram. The naturality of diagram arrows ensures the two diagram directions commute. Its comparison is the canonical one induced by coprojections and projections, so it identifies the actual limit comparison for $S$. The empty finite diagram is included. The final topoi assertion then uses the explicit definition of a morphism of topoi as an adjunction with finite-limit-preserving left adjoint, with no added claim of conservativity (geometric points need not form a conservative family on the pro-etale site).

For the small-case audit I checked the empty target scheme (there is no geometric point, so no instance to prove); empty $L(U)$ (there is one function to any $T$, even $T=\varnothing$); an empty covering (its target has no lifts, since a lift would contradict steps 2 or 3); arbitrary $T$, including $T=\varnothing$; the empty finite limit; repeated lifts and identity refinements; and a nonempty weakly etale affine over an algebraically closed field. The latter has a residue-field map back to the same field and gives an actual section, as the proof requires. No quasi-compactness, quasi-separatedness, characteristic restriction, or finiteness of $Y$ is introduced. I did not assume intersections of affine opens are affine: step 3 chooses one affine neighbourhood of one point, and step 4 uses scheme fibre products.
