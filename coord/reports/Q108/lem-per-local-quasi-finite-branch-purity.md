## Review by Q108

I compared every proof step with the **statement** of every node it cites. The 57 citations cover 41 existing nodes, and the quotes in `citations.json` are their actual statements. There are no new nodes whose hypotheses, sources or searches need separate review. I checked the following mathematical applications.

Step 1 uses `lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-local-finite-krull-dimension` to get an integer induction parameter, the finite-type affine neighbourhood in `pqf-def-quasifinite`, and `lem-weil-pbc-quasi-finite-at-point-isolated` for the isolated-fibre hypothesis. The latter assumes local finite type; that is why the restriction from quasi-finiteness is made first. An open neighbourhood of the specialization retains all the generalizations involved in the hypothesis.

Step 2 applies exactly the singleton-point version `lem-weil-pbc-etale-local-finite-at-point-elementary`, not merely a general quasi-finite factorization. Its statement gives the etale target chart, the open finite piece, the unique point over the chosen target point, and its image in X. `lem-weil-pbc-etale-base-change-finite-limits` explicitly gives preservation under an arbitrary scheme base change; I did not use the weaker statement about base change along an etale map. `lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation` then handles the open immersion. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-local-on-target` allows shrinking the target, and `def-weil-finite-integral-morphism` identifies the inverse image of an affine as a finite algebra. The locally Noetherian assertion used here is stated in `pqf-def-locnoeth` itself; choose a Noetherian affine chart in U, and `lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-noetherian` gives S Noetherian. I checked this against More on Morphisms 02LK and its affine proof.

Step 3 uses `lem-trf-finite-singleton-fibre-stalk-ring` only after the singleton-fibre condition has been obtained. It really states module finiteness of the stalk map. `lem-sfb-etale-stalk-invariants` gives each of normality, regularity and the two dimension equalities at these precise points; no identification of fraction fields is taken from it. `lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization` identifies the stalks with R_p and S_q. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-local-radical` and `lem-weil-pbc-localization-spectrum-embedding` justify the local-base-change ring: maximal ideals of the localized finite algebra lie over the base maximal ideal, and exactly one such prime is present. The resulting local ring equals S_q since all elements outside q are units. `lem-weil-pbc-localize-localization-at-prime` identifies the stalks at all other points. This is the argument in 05B8, which I read in full; I did not infer that finiteness of arbitrary stalk maps follows from finiteness alone without the singleton hypothesis.

Step 4 uses that same iterated localization and `lem-weil-pbc-localization-dimension-chains-below-prime` to identify height one. Apply `lem-sfb-etale-stalk-invariants` at the image of each such point, not only at v. The image is a generalization of x, so the original unramifiedness assumption applies. `lem-trf-unramified-locus-base-change` explicitly identifies the locus under arbitrary base change and requires only local finite type; its hypotheses hold before each base change. I checked Tag 0475 and the preceding point criterion 02GF in `morphisms.tex`.

Step 5 uses `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-noetherian-finite-presentation` for both finite algebras. `lem-sc-etale-at-prime-flat-unramified-criterion` supplies the genuine open etale neighbourhood and has exactly the flatness-over-R_p hypothesis needed here. `lem-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials-localization` localizes the **source** of the differential module. It says nothing about replacing R by R_p, so I supplied that replacement: every R-derivation kills r/t by the displayed inverse rule. `def-weil-pbc-kahler-differentials` gives the universal property identifying the resulting modules. This correction prevents a hidden target-localization comparison. I read Algebra 00RT and 08WD and Morphisms 02GU for these criteria.

Step 6 uses `lem-weil-pbc-etale-morphism-open-map` to make the image O an open neighbourhood of x. `lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation` both composes the etale maps to Y and shows that N to O is etale. Its surjectivity is precisely the definition of O. Thus `lem-trf-etale-descent-through-source-cover` applies with a genuinely surjective etale map and etale composite. I do not apply this global descent theorem to a nonsurjective neighbourhood of X. The explicit image restriction is essential. I checked source-locality 036W and the etale openness statement in `morphisms.tex`.

Step 7 uses `lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-is-integral`, `lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-localization` and `lem-weil-pbc-regular-ring-normal`. The last two together give a normal **domain** because A is local. The quotient by the kernel embeds integrally in the domain B; only then is `lem-weil-pbc-integral-injective-dimension-equal` used. `def-weil-pbc-krull-dimension-ring` makes the contradiction explicit: a chain of length d in A/P, preceded by (0) if P is nonzero, exceeds dim A. I read the corresponding 00GK, 00OK, 0AFS and 0567 source statements. No assertion about the dimension of an arbitrary nonzero ideal quotient is silently assumed.

Step 8 applies `lem-sc-regular-dim1-dvr` and uses the PID content actually stated in `def-sc-discrete-valuation-ring`. The former node's external tag says 00DV, which is not the DVR tag (it is Nakayama); I checked the correct source **00PD**, `algebra-lemma-characterize-dvr`, which proves the assertion. This is an existing metadata error, not a change to our statement. `lem-weil-pbc-pid-torsion-free-module-flat` gives the required flatness, now that Step 7 proves torsion freeness. Finite presentation is supplied by the same Noetherian finite-type lemma. `def-weil-unramified-morphism` explicitly states the affine differential criterion; localization of differentials and the local-ring maximal-ideal observation give Omega=0. The etale-at-a-prime criterion therefore gives the conclusion at the closed point. Every open containing a local spectrum's closed point is the whole spectrum. I read 00PD, the torsion-free-flat statement 0AUW in `more-algebra.tex`, and 08WD. This fills the details omitted in 0BMB's d=1 paragraph.

Step 9 is elementary assembly from three cited statements, not an unproved dimension-formula application. `lem-weil-pbc-integral-incomparability` gives strict contractions; `lem-weil-pbc-going-down-normal-integral` lifts backwards from a **prescribed** prime, using the injection and normality proved in Step 7; `lem-weil-pbc-localization-dimension-chains-below-prime` identifies the suprema. Both chain inequalities are written out. I checked the exact 00GT and 00H8 hypotheses in `algebra.tex`.

Step 10 checks every induction hypothesis at a nonzero nonmaximal prime. `lem-weil-pbc-localization-normal-domain` gives normal source stalks, `lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-localization` gives regular target stalks, and `pqf-lem-finite-qf` gives quasi-finiteness. The cited localization dimension statement and the extra last prime m_B give the strict dimension drop. The rings are Noetherian by Step 3. Height-one generalizations are exactly those covered by Step 4. No zero-dimensional point is passed to the positive-dimensional induction hypothesis.

Step 11 handles the remaining generic point with a finite maximal chain, using just the definition of dimension and the localization chain formula. Its first nonzero member must have height one, since a longer prefix could be substituted to contradict maximal length. For d at least two it is nonmaximal, so Step 10 applies. An etale open containing that member contains the generic point. This is a routine chain argument, not an additional branch-purity lemma.

Step 12 uses incomparability to show that the inverse image of the punctured base is exactly the punctured source. `lem-weil-pbc-finite-local-on-target` preserves finiteness under this open restriction. `lem-weil-pbc-etale-local-on-source` assembles the etale neighbourhoods in the puncture. `lem-sbc-regular-local-punctured-etale-purity` states extension of any finite etale cover of a regular local ring's puncture, exactly with dimension at least two. It gives the extension and its specified comparison isomorphism, not yet etaleness of B itself. I checked 0BMA's statement, and read the existing Lean source only as evidence of what auxiliary declaration it implements, without compiling anything.

Step 13 applies `lem-sbc-finite-normal-dense-open-comparison` to the actual supplied extension. Normal stalks come from `lem-weil-pbc-localization-normal-domain`; `def-pc-integral-scheme` supplies integrality of the spectra of the domains. The punctured base is nonempty, and its inverse image contains the generic point of Spec B, so the required density holds. Both maps are finite, the supplied extension is etale, and their isomorphism is over the actual same puncture. The comparison node states exactly an isomorphism over T under these conditions. Etale composition with that isomorphism gives h etale. I checked this comparison against the normal-algebra reformulation 0BM9. In particular no existence of a cover is taken from the comparison theorem.

I tested small and excluded cases. At dimension one an identity DVR map works, while t=s^n with n greater than one prime to the residue characteristic fails the unramified hypothesis at the closed point, as it should. In characteristic p the Frobenius map of k[[t]] is likewise excluded. A closed-point immersion into a regular curve has unequal local dimensions and is excluded, agreeing with the injectivity argument. For dimension at least two the identity on a regular local ring exercises the height-one/generic-point argument. A nonsplit finite etale residue extension is allowed: no step assumes a rational residue field or strict henselianity. Zero rings are excluded by the stalk/domain and positive-dimension assumptions. The original schemes may be nonseparated; 02LK has no separation hypothesis. No excellence, completeness, henselianity or separability hypothesis has been added.
