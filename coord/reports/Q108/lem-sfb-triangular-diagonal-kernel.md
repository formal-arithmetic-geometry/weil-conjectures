## Review by Q108

I checked delivery-errors B–E against the node files and Milne, with every cited statement open. Both new nodes introduce their fields, all schemes and homomorphisms, the meaning of each matrix group or diagonalizable group, and their relevant coverings. Their proof and proof-dependency arrays are empty. No proof sketch is hidden in their statements. There is no elementary kernel-only node or duplicate of an existing theorem. All search records come from actual retained outputs. The new statements use Milne places rather than fictitious Stacks tags.

Step 1 uses exactly the functor-of-points and closed-kernel assertion of `lem-sc-kernel-subgroup`. The diagonal multiplication/inverse equalities, normality, equations for the intersection, and the coordinate-algebra closed immersion are written out. They hold for arbitrary commutative algebras, not just reduced ones, so the scheme-theoretic conclusion follows.

Step 2 reads all clauses of `lem-sfb-algebraic-group-homomorphism-factorization`: the faithfully flat finitely presented map with the given kernel is the represented fppf quotient, and its image map is a closed immersion. J and the split torus are separated finite-type groups. Only after imposing algebraic closedness is `lem-sfb-split-torus-subgroups-diagonalizable` applied, to precisely its stipulated closed subgroup of a split torus. It provides the group algebra D(M), without claiming smoothness.

Step 3 uses the complete coordinate filtration and conjugation formula of the new filtration node. Applying the existing homomorphism factorization to the restricted coordinate map gives the closed additive-subgroup quotient with the correct kernel. The ratio of two diagonal coordinates restricts to a character of D. The filtration is normal in J and its factors have trivial V-action, exactly the data needed in step 4. The restrictions are not assumed surjective onto all of Ga.

Step 4 states an induction claim broader than the original matrix realization so that it can legitimately be applied to E/N. Its base is a zero-length filtration; its decrease removes the last nontrivial term, not merely a zero-dimensional group. I read the quotient/correspondence clauses of `lem-sfb-affine-normal-quotient-geometry` and checked that all groups and quotients remain affine finite type and that E/N has kernel W/N and the shorter filtration with the same characters. Pullback along the inductively obtained group section gives an extension by N, with local surjectivity inherited from E→E/N. The new additive-kernel splitting lemma applies because N is the last embedded additive factor and the action extends by the specified scalar character. Composing the resulting pullback section is visibly a group section of E→D. This is Milne's diagram (118), fully assembled in the item; the argument is not delegated to a node restating the section clause.

Step 5 gives mutually inverse morphism formulas for the scheme product, with the first inverse component checked to lie in the kernel. No mistaken direct-product group isomorphism is asserted.

Step 6 uses the precise lifting definition in `def-sbc-smooth-morphisms`, not a theorem about smooth group quotients with extra hypotheses. Each factor is a scheme retract of J, so a lifting problem is solved by section, lift in J, and retraction. I checked that `lem-pc-affine-finite-type-algebra` supplies the finite-type coordinate algebra, and that `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-noetherian-finite-presentation` applies to the field k, whose ideals are explicitly 0 and k. Thus the missing finite-presentation condition is supplied for both factors. Connectedness follows by continuous surjection; there is no assumption that a kernel of a smooth map must be smooth or connected. The two algebraic facts from other units are declared as generic tool uses in split_report.

Step 7 uses `lem-sfb-geometric-unipotence-unitriangular-embedding`, whose statement explicitly provides a closed immersion over k from the stated smooth geometric-point hypothesis, not merely an immersion over an algebraic closure. The converse is the finite-chain proof of nilpotence of a strictly upper triangular matrix. The quantifier is existence of a faithful representation, exactly as in the item's wording.

Small cases checked: n=1 has U_1=1, a zero-length filtration and a trivial kernel. J=1, J contained in the diagonal torus, and J contained in U_n give respectively the trivial group, q an isomorphism, and D=1. In characteristic p, alpha_p inside U_2 is allowed, and mu_p inside a diagonal G_m is allowed: both show why smoothness and connectedness cannot be used before step 6 and why geometric points alone do not define the kernel or the quotient. The rank-one splitting input allows both trivial N and infinitesimal additive N; it never assumes N=Ga. Its linear-action hypothesis is essential and is supplied by the explicit conjugation characters. The smooth geometric-point equivalence is not extended to nonsmooth groups (mu_p is the counterexample). No statement correction was needed. Every proof step is under 2000 characters, and all non-elementary uses have quoted node statements in citations.json.
