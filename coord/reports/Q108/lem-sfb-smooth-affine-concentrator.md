## Review by Q108

I read Milne Definition 13.21 and Proposition 13.24 with the node open: the ideal is generated by the degree-zero part of the invariant ideal and all negative-degree functions, using Milne's right action on functions convention. This convention is exactly the orbit formula used here; reversing the grading would reverse the limit. The item still requires affine finite type X, smooth X and Z, an invariant closed immersion, and all commutative test algebras. No perfectness, algebraic closure, connectedness, reduced test ring or fixedness of all of Z has been added.

For the new lemma I read the hypotheses before Lemma 13.25 and Lemma 13.27 itself, printed pp.262–263. I made explicit that the displayed additive subgroup m is an ideal; this is essential for a Z-grading and does not follow merely from A0 being local. The ideal a is homogeneous and contained in m, and both regularity hypotheses refer to the localizations at m and m/a, not arbitrary localizations. The conclusion is at m/b. Its notions are defined inline or cite the actual definitions of Noetherian ring, regular local ring, and localization at a prime. It has no proof/proof-dependency payload. Its proof route, for the future worker, is Milne's homogeneous parameter selection, the graded Nakayama criterion of Lemmas 13.25–13.26, and identification of b with the ideal of the selected negative parameters and the nonpositive parameters belonging to a. This is a proper local algebra ingredient, rather than any clause of the geometric item renamed.

Step 1 uses only the affine coordinate dictionary and explicitly compares the two-variable coaction coefficients. It obtains the infinite-dimensional grading directly; I deliberately did not cite the finite-dimensional representation weight-basis node for this. Homogeneity of a follows from the coaction on the quotient. Construction of the ideal quotient and finite type are direct generator calculations.

Step 2 is an explicit elementary calculation, not an appeal to Proposition 13.24 as an uncited black box. The Laurent basis proves uniqueness over every R, including nilpotents and the zero ring. Killing negative coefficients gives existence; killing a0 gives membership in Z. The degree-zero evaluation is multiplicative modulo b, including mixed positive/negative terms, which proves the actual morphism Y to Z. Thus no set-theoretic factorization through a closed immersion is used.

Step 3 cites the statement of `lem-sfb-scheme-large-algclosed-jacobson` for BOTH of its specified conclusions: closed-point density in every closed subset and rational residue fields after choosing K. It applies to the finite-type Y here. `lem-pc-proper-base-change` is used only for its smoothness clause (2), not its properness clause. `lem-weil-pbc-smooth-scheme-over-field-regular` gives precisely regular local rings of X_K and Z_K. The comparison of ideals with field extension is written out using tensor products of the homogeneous direct sums. No coefficient category is involved.

Step 4 cites `lem-wf-affine-noetherian-components` only for Noetherianity of the finite-type field algebra A. Localization remains Noetherian by extension/contraction of ideals, as written. The one new lemma supplies exactly the needed regularity of the quotient by a0 and the negative pieces. I verified A0 localized at the contraction of the rational fixed point is local, all nonzero graded components vanish at that point, m is genuinely its maximal ideal, a is contained in m because z is in Z, and both input local rings are exactly the ambient and Z local rings already proved regular in step 3. The localizations do not change the point or confuse the Z ideal with the Y ideal. The statement of the new lemma gives the Y local ring at z, not at y; the next step handles this distinction.

Step 5 cites `lem-weil-pbc-regular-closed-point-smooth-source` at a maximal ideal of the finite-type K-algebra B, with K algebraically closed and the local ring regular. The affine comparison `lem-weil-pbc-smooth-ring-map-iff-smooth-affine` converts the resulting localized smooth algebra into a smooth open neighbourhood. The remaining argument is explicit: the orbit homomorphism kills the homogeneous ideal b, the inverse image of that neighbourhood contains D(h) with h(0) nonzero, an infinite field supplies c nonzero there, and the automorphism multiplying by c moves y into the neighbourhood. I checked that the action preserves b, so this is an automorphism of Y itself. The source's passage from the limit to y is therefore not hidden in the word “transport”.

Step 6 uses the density clause of the same Jacobson node on the closed complement of the union of smooth opens. This union is open by definition; no separate openness theorem is smuggled in. The local-on-source node states exactly that a cover by smooth opens is sufficient, and explicitly includes an empty cover when Y is empty.

Step 7 uses `lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-noetherian-finite-presentation` over the Noetherian field k to meet the local finite presentation hypothesis in `lem-sfb-scheme-lfp-smooth-flat-base-change`. The field extension is flat by the field-flatness node. The field-surjectivity and surjective-base-change nodes provide a lift of every point. The cited pointwise base-change node states both directions of smoothness equivalence and requires no extra flatness of Y; its reverse direction is exactly what is used here. Finally local-on-source assembles the descended smooth neighbourhoods. Each citation was checked against its statement, not its proof, and `citations.json` quotes those statements verbatim.

Small cases checked: a=0; trivial action (b=a and Y=Z); Z empty (a=A, b=A, Y empty, with the local lemma never invoked); Z=X; a point with all weights zero; positive and negative affine-space weights; the zero-dimensional local ring case; and R=K[epsilon]/(epsilon^2). For the graded lemma specifically, polynomial rings with parameters of degrees -1,0,1 and a generated by subsets of those parameters give the expected regular parameter quotient. The explicit ideal hypothesis excludes that invalid application. In the geometric application uv evaluates to zero at the fixed point, so the hypothesis holds.
