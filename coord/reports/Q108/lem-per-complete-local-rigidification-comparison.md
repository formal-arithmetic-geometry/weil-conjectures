## Review by Q108

I read ILO VIII 5.1, 5.3.4, 5.3.6--5.3.11, 5.4.2--5.4.5 and the complete proof of 5.5.2 (printed pp.122--128), together with 2.3.10--2.3.12, 3.2.19, 3.4.3--3.4.9, 3.4.13--3.4.16 (pp.93--94,106,110--114). The item is the equality in 5.5.2, including its disjoint-union paragraph, with the source's inducing rule made explicit. The two new nodes isolate the actual coordinate lifting and the cited general theorem, not the item's conclusion. They have complete statements introducing all schemes, actions, boundaries and log structures; the source labels are provenance, not substitutes for meanings. Their proof and proof-dependency fields are empty. The paragraphs describing the ambient pieces state properties of the objects, not proof sketches.

For the ambient-lift node I checked the source's reduction to H=G_x before averaging: it is inertia, not the full decomposition group acting nontrivially on the residue field. Tameness makes |H| a unit, abelianity and the separably closed residue field allow the character projections, and the two systems agree on the original-boundary parameters. Cohen coefficients land in A^H, not in A with an unproved trivial action. The lifts t'_i are character-weighted averages; very tameness forces the transverse characters to be trivial. Hence the ambient strata have Cohen characteristic-zero generic points even though their special fibres do not. For a general G-action I use the induced G-space from H, as described in 5.3.7. The resulting map U to X is a finite union of component isomorphisms and is inert, even when a decomposition group acts on the residue field; Y is obtained by inducing the ambient H-pieces. Taking all components at once retains the synchronization required by the last paragraph of 5.5.2. The ambient schemes and quotients have integral affine components: each ambient ring is a domain and its invariant subring is a domain.

For the general independence node I checked that “generic points of log strata have characteristic zero” is the actual hypothesis of 3.4.15, not the stronger assertion that the whole scheme has characteristic zero. Its proof first restricts to the rational open using 3.4.13, then uses completed monoid charts and the same inducing algorithm, and finally takes the union of the fans for synchronization. Remark 3.4.16(i) explicitly explains why quasi-excellence of the original log schemes is not assumed. Existence of a regular-functorial algorithm is stated separately in the first paragraph, from 2.3.10 and 2.3.12, rather than misusing the existing finite-type smooth-functorial lemma on a non-finite-type complete quotient.

Step 1 uses the entire ambient-lift statement: it really supplies both maps, exact boundary pullbacks, inertness, the common quotient, fs log regularity and the characteristic-zero generic points. All its hypotheses are the item's hypotheses, and no additional excellence or finite-type condition is inserted.

Step 2 uses the general independence statement with V=W=Q and phi the identity, and the ambient-lift statement for its hypotheses. In particular both quotient log structures meet the generic-characteristic condition, and the rule is now the one required by the theorem. The equality is centered and ordered on all of Q, not merely equality of the final modifications or componentwise contracted towers.

On re-reading its statement, I replaced that shortcut with the existing `lem-trf-log-blowup-normalization` and the universal property in `def-trf-fs-log-blowup`. The former states existence, log regularity and the normalized ordinary blowup in precisely the generated ring ideal. The latter makes fs pullback the blowup of the pulled-back log ideal by its test-object property. These apply one stage at a time on the integral components supplied by the ambient lemma. The equality alpha(f*I)O = f*(alpha(I)O) is just the compatibility in a log morphism. The induction has its base (the same Y to Q) and its step (the same next extended center and function field), so it gives the same intermediate schemes and next maps. The normalization theorem is a declared off-unit generic tool; no perverse or Fourier argument is taken from another unit.

Equality of each ideal descends by restricting to a copy mapping isomorphically onto its component, not by an unproved generic-point detection claim or by an uncited fpqc existence theorem. The induction uses the common global indexing; it does not infer synchronization from separate contracted comparisons.
