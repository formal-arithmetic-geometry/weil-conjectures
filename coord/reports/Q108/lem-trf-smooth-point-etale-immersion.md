### Returned step 1

For every input satisfying the hypotheses, $d=\dim_x(X_s)$ is a finite nonnegative integer. Indeed, smoothness includes local finite presentation by [def-sbc-smooth-morphisms]. Choose affine neighbourhoods $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $x$ and $V=\operatorname{Spec}R$ of $s$ with $f(U)\subseteq V$ and $A$ finitely presented over $R$, using [def-sbc-locally-finite-presentation]. The affine fibre-product formula [lem-weil-pbc-fibre-product-affine-spectra] identifies $U_s$ with $\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_R\kappa(s))$: the fibre computed over $V$ is also the fibre over $S$, since both have the same universal property. The images of the finitely many algebra generators of $A$ generate this tensor product over $\kappa(s)$, so $U_s$ is of finite type over that field. Its dimension is finite by [lem-kun-dimension-finite-type]. By [def-kun-topological-dimension-at-point], dimension at $x$ is unchanged on restricting to the open neighbourhood $U_s$, and is at most its dimension: intersect any other neighbourhood with $U_s$ in the minimum defining dimension at a point. We prove the desired conclusion simultaneously for all such inputs by strong induction on $d$. The induction hypothesis is the full assertion, including immersion, etaleness over the same base, and singleton designated fibre, for every smaller value of $d$.

### Returned step 2

If $d=0$, [lem-weil-pbc-etale-near-point-smooth-zero-fibre-dimension], with the smooth open neighbourhood taken to be $X$ itself, gives an open neighbourhood $W\subseteq X$ of $x$ such that $W\to S$ is etale. By [lem-weil-pbc-etale-fibre-discrete-separable-points], the point corresponding to $x$ is open in $W_s$. By [def-pc-scheme-fibre], the topology of $W_s$ is precisely the subspace topology on the set-theoretic fibre in $W$. Consequently there is an open $Z\subseteq W$ whose intersection with that fibre is exactly $\{x\}$. Give $Z$ its open subscheme structure and let $z$ be its point $x$. The fibre $Z_s$ has underlying set $\{z\}$ by the same fibre-topology statement. Its structural morphism is etale, since the open immersion $Z\to W$ is etale and etale morphisms compose [lem-weil-pbc-etale-composition-cancellation]. Both $Z\to W$ and $W\to X$ are immersions (factor each through its identity closed immersion as in [def-weil-closed-immersion]); their composite $i:Z\to X$ is an immersion by [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence](e). It takes $z$ to $x$ and proves the induction base.

### Returned step 3

Let $d>0$. Apply [lem-trf-smooth-separable-hypersurface-slice] to obtain an affine open $U\subseteq X$ and an effective Cartier divisor $D\hookrightarrow U$ containing $x$, smooth over $S$, with $\dim_x(D_s)=d-1$. All its hypotheses are exactly our smoothness, closedness in the fibre, finite separability, and positive dimension. Write $x_D$ for the point of $D$ over $x$. The fibre morphism $D_s\to U_s$ is a closed immersion by [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence](b); here the two fibres are the pullbacks of the closed immersion along $U_s\to U$. Thus $x_D$ is closed in $D_s$, since its image is a closed point of $U_s$ and a closed immersion is a homeomorphism onto its image [def-weil-closed-immersion]. Moreover its residue field is unchanged: on an affine neighbourhood a closed immersion is $\operatorname{Spec}(B/I)\to\operatorname{Spec}B$, and at the corresponding prime $\mathfrak p\supseteq I$ its residue field is $(B/I)_{\mathfrak p/I}/(\mathfrak p/I)=B_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$. The residue field at $x$ is also unchanged by the open restriction $U\subseteq X$ [def-weil-open-immersion]. Therefore $\kappa(x_D)/\kappa(s)$ is the same finite separable extension, so the induction hypothesis applies to $D\to S$ at $x_D$.

### Returned step 4

The induction hypothesis produces an immersion $j:Z\to D$ and $z\in Z$ with $j(z)=x_D$, such that $Z\to S$ is etale and $Z_s$ has underlying set $\{z\}$. The composite $D\hookrightarrow U\hookrightarrow X$ is an immersion by its displayed closed-then-open factorization [def-weil-closed-immersion]. Compose it with $j$; [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence](e) shows that $i:Z\to X$ is an immersion. Its structural morphism to $S$ is exactly the one obtained by induction, since all compositions used are over $S$. Thus $i(z)=x$ and both the etale and singleton-fibre conclusions hold. Since $d-1<d$, this closes the strong induction.
