## Review by NK

I checked every cited node's statement, not just its title or proof. The item keeps exactly the original hypotheses: an affine target and a separated locally quasi-finite morphism. The source need not be quasi-compact, Noetherian, reduced, or finite type over any field. The finite set can contain nonclosed or comparable points. These distinctions matter: quasi-compactness is established only for the auxiliary open U, and prime avoidance applies to arbitrary prime ideals.

Step 1 uses `lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-section-vanishing-prime` clause (b), which says that a basic open in an affine scheme is affine. Taking the section zero gives an empty affine scheme. The empty open in X has the same unique sheaf on an empty space. This treats E empty, including X empty, before any lemma requiring a positive number of primes is invoked.

Step 2 uses `lem-weil-pbc-affine-opens-basis` to choose one affine neighbourhood per point, and `lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs` for their quasi-compactness. The finite-union compactness argument is written out using finite subcovers. It does not assume that an intersection of affine opens is affine or that X itself is quasi-compact.

Step 3 uses `lem-weil-pbc-lqf-restriction-to-opens` with source open U and target open Y, whose statement explicitly allows this restriction. For separatedness, `def-pc-fibre-product` gives the test-object identification of the pullback of the old diagonal, and `def-weil-open-immersion` gives injectivity on test maps through the open inclusion. The pullback is exactly U with its diagonal to U ×_Y U. `lem-weil-pbc-closed-immersion-base-change` then applies to the closed diagonal of f. I wrote the identification rather than treating restriction of separatedness as an uncited theorem. The step is non-elementary because it applies the base-change theorem and the local-quasi-finite restriction theorem.

Step 4 uses `lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-qcqs` for Y quasi-separated and `lem-weil-pbc-qcqs-morphism-permanence` clause (1) for quasi-compactness of U → Y; it does not infer quasi-compactness merely from U compact without checking the target. `lem-weil-pbc-lqf-locally-finite-type` gives exactly the extra local finite-type hypothesis. Thus all four assumptions of `lemma-nagata-quasi-affine-morphism-of-quasi-finite` have been verified. `def-nagata-quasi-affine-morphism` evaluates its conclusion on the whole affine target Y, giving the quasi-affine scheme U. Quasi-affine here means a quasi-compact open in an affine scheme, as in Stacks Properties Definition 01P6, not a claim that U itself is affine. The three non-Mathlib supporting lemmas from other units are declared as generic tools in `split_report.tool_uses`; they implement the source's existing 02LR route.

Step 5 uses `lem-weil-pbc-spec-localization-away-basic-open` clause (i), which provides the required basic open around each image prime inside O. It uses `lem-weil-pbc-prime-avoidance`, whose statement allows any finite nonempty list of prime ideals without maximality, incomparability, Noetherianity or finiteness of residue fields. The ideal J generated by the chosen a_i is not contained in any image prime because its matching generator avoids that prime. The obtained a avoids all the image primes. For every prime outside O, all generators lie in it; hence a lies in it. This proves the needed containment D(a) ⊆ O explicitly. There is no omitted denominator clearing, compatibility assertion, or separate comparison theorem.

Step 6 uses `lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-section-vanishing-prime` clause (b) on the ambient affine spectrum. Since D(a) lies wholly in O, the given isomorphism U ≅ O restricts to W ≅ D(a). By the actual sheaf-restriction meaning in `def-weil-open-immersion`, W is the corresponding open subscheme of X. Affineness and containment of E then follow. It does not treat an arbitrary immersion with image in an open as an unproved scheme factorization: it restricts a given isomorphism of open subschemes.
