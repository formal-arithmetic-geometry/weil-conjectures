## Preserved argument allocation

### Returned step 1 → parent step 1

Fix a polynomial presentation $P=R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\twoheadrightarrow A$ for which $A[0]$ is perfect, as in the hypothesis. The finite-type Noetherian-algebra theorem applied to $R\to P$ makes $P$ Noetherian. Unpack the definition of a perfect ring complex to choose a bounded complex $E$ of finite projective $P$-modules representing $A[0]$, with $E^i=0$ outside some interval $[u,v]$. The polynomial model of algebraic upper shriek says that, after forgetting the $A$-action, $\varphi^!K$ is $R\operatorname{Hom}_P(A,K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}P)[n]$. We will compute this model; no flatness of $R\to A$ is assumed.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian, def-pc-perfect-ring-complex, lem-trf-algebraic-shriek-calculus

### Returned step 2 → lem-trf-polynomial-bounded-finite-model

Let $K\in D(R)$ have bounded finitely generated cohomology. The Noetherian pseudo-coherence criterion applies because $R$ is Noetherian, all $H^i(K)$ are finite, and they vanish for $i\gg0$. Together with the definition of ring pseudo-coherence it gives a bounded-above finite-free $R$-complex $F$ representing $K$. Choose $b$ with $F^j=0$ for $j>b$. Finite free modules are flat directly: tensoring with $R^r$ is taking $r$ copies of a sequence, which preserves exactness coordinatewise. Thus the bounded-above flat-complex lemma makes $F$ K-flat. Derived change of rings, on the one-point ringed site, identifies $K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}P\in D(P)$ with the termwise scalar extension $L=F\otimes_RP$. Each $L^j$ is finite free over $P$, and $L^j=0$ for $j>b$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-pseudocoherent-cohomology-criterion, def-pc-ring-pseudo-coherent-complex, lem-pc-module-bounded-flat-kflat, lem-pur-derived-change-of-rings

### Returned step 3 → lem-trf-polynomial-bounded-finite-model

The monomial description of $P$ identifies its underlying $R$-module with a direct sum of copies of $R$. For every module $M$, $M\otimes_RP$ is accordingly the direct sum of copies of $M$: the inverse maps send $m\otimes\sum r_\alpha x^\alpha$ to $(r_\alpha m)_\alpha$ and the $\alpha$-th summand to $m\otimes x^\alpha$. Kernels and images in a direct-sum sequence are computed coordinatewise, since every element has finite support; hence $P$ is $R$-flat. Flat-tensor exactness identifies the kernels and images of the differential of $L$ with those of $F$ tensored with $P$, so $H^j(L)=H^j(F)\otimes_RP$. Choose $a\le b$ below the cohomological support of $K$. Then $H^j(L)=0$ for $j<a$ as well as for $j>b$.

Uses: def-weil-pbc-mv-polynomial-ring, lem-weil-pbc-flat-tensor-exact

### Returned step 4 → lem-trf-polynomial-bounded-finite-model

Replace $L$ by the explicit lower truncation $G$: set $G^j=0$ for $j<a$, $G^a=\operatorname{coker}(L^{a-1}\to L^a)$, and $G^j=L^j$ for $j>a$, with induced differential. The projection $L\to G$ induces the identity on cohomology above $a$; in degree $a$ its kernel-of-differential modulo boundaries is exactly $H^a(L)$; below $a$ both cohomologies vanish. It is therefore a quasi-isomorphism. The images of any finite generating set of $L^a$ generate its quotient $G^a$, and the other nonzero terms are finite free. Thus $G$ is a bounded complex of finite $P$-modules in degrees $[a,b]$ representing $K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}P$.

### Returned step 5 → lem-trf-finite-projective-hom-coherent-model

The projective-complex derived-Hom model applies over $P$ to $E$ (bounded above and projective) and $G$ (bounded below). It represents $R\operatorname{Hom}_P(A,K\otimes_R^{\mathbf L}P)$ by $C=\operatorname{Hom}_P^\bullet(E,G)$, with $C^t=\bigoplus_{i=u}^v\operatorname{Hom}_P(E^i,G^{i+t})$; the product formula is a finite direct sum because $E$ is bounded. Each $E^i$ is a direct summand of a finite free module: a finite generating set gives a surjection $P^{r_i}\twoheadrightarrow E^i$, and projectivity supplies a section. Applying $\operatorname{Hom}_P(-,G^j)$ makes $\operatorname{Hom}_P(E^i,G^j)$ a retract of $(G^j)^{r_i}$. The projections of finite generators of this latter module generate the retract, so every $C^t$ is finite. A nonzero summand requires $u\le i\le v$ and $a\le i+t\le b$, hence $a-v\le t\le b-u$. Consequently $C$ is bounded in $[a-v,b-u]$.

Uses: lem-sc-affine-projective-complex-derived-hom, def-weil-pbc-projective-module

### Returned step 6 → lem-trf-finite-projective-hom-coherent-model (first two sentences), then parent step 4 (remaining scalar restriction)

Because $P$ is Noetherian and each $C^t$ is finite, the Noetherian submodule theorem makes $\ker(C^t\to C^{t+1})$ finite. Its quotient $H^t(C)$ is finite, generated by the images of generators of that kernel. Step 1 identifies restriction of $\varphi^!K$ to $D(P)$ with $C[n]$. Restriction of scalars is exact (kernels and quotients have the same underlying groups), so its cohomology is the underlying $P$-module of each $A$-cohomology module. Thus $H^t(\varphi^!K)$ vanishes outside $[a-v-n,b-u-n]$ and is finite as a $P$-module. The same generators also generate it over $A$: a $P$-linear expression is an $A$-linear expression after applying $P\to A$ to its coefficients. This proves bounded finitely generated cohomology over $A$ for every allowed $K$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-noetherian-submodule-finite, lem-trf-algebraic-shriek-calculus
