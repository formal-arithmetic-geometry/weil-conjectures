## Review by NK

I read each proof step against its source and against the statement of each cited node, not the proof or a definition standing in for a theorem. Step 1 uses the new field comparison precisely for extension of automorphisms, the equivariant bijection and profiniteness. Passing from lifts to P to lifts to P_K is the fibre-product universal property, with the second coordinate forced to be s. The comparison in the item's direction is the inverse of precomposition with r; this avoids reversing the direction of the scheme morphism. Equivariance of a bijection immediately proves the subgroup-triviality equivalence.

Step 2 uses `lem-galois-pi1-functoriality`, whose statement actually provides the continuous a_*, the natural first-projection bijection and its action identity. Its hypotheses hold because X is connected and Spec K has one point, and the chosen geometric point maps to bar a. The field comparison gives j with exactly the precomposition action; composing provides the action on P. I wrote the contravariance calculation for Spec explicitly, and checked that multiplication is not reversed. Naturalness follows by associativity of postcomposition, and inverses are given by sigma inverse. `thm-galois-fundamental` describes the fundamental group with its profinite topology; this supplies the target's profiniteness.

Step 3 uses both applications of `thm-galois-fundamental`, the field identification, and the new abstract restriction criterion. The former supplies equivalences on finite sets with continuous actions, not a bare faithful embedding. Essential surjectivity is needed here: every pair of finite Pi-sets can be represented by covers. The action identity from step 2 then makes the assumed Hom bijections exactly those of restriction. The abstract lemma needs a continuous map and two profinite groups; these were obtained before its application. No normality, Noetherianity, perfectness or finite-type hypothesis has been introduced.

Step 4 is a short elementary use of the equivalence already cited: full faithfulness applied to (X,P) yields the first bijection; the identity cover has singleton fibre, and a map from a singleton is equivariant exactly when its value is fixed. The field identification specifies the action, so the second bijection uses G acting through q_a, not an unrelated action. I gave the evaluation formula u composed with s, thereby checking the actual bijection, not just its cardinality.

For the new field statement I checked 0BNE/0BQ9 with an arbitrary imperfect K, not only a perfect field. In characteristic p, the algebraic closure of the separably closed Omega is its purely inseparable perfection. A putative extension is forced on every alpha by the equation image(alpha)^(p^n)=sigma(alpha^(p^n)); existence follows by taking the unique root, consistently over all n. Finite separable embeddings into the algebraic closure land in Omega, giving the b_Q comparison componentwise. Reconstruction and its Krull-topology compatibility are the content of 0BNE; they are left in the new statement-only node rather than silently inferred from a Galois-set definition. In characteristic zero the two closures coincide. Thus the node is a non-elementary field theorem, not a new spelling of the field-point quotient result.

For the new abstract criterion I checked the compactness hypothesis and the converse: the image C=h(H) is compact and closed. If it is proper, choose an open normal N for which CN is proper, and put U=CN. The finite transitive G-set G/U has an H-fixed point U but no G-fixed point. Fullness for maps from the singleton then fails. If h is onto, H-equivariance is G-equivariance, proving the other direction. This is a genuine independent topological lemma. The source's closing implication labels in the proof of 0BN6 are inconsistent with its preceding sentence, but the singleton/G-U argument above proves exactly the stated (1)-(2) equivalence; there is no change to the mathematical statement.
