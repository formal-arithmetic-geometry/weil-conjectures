### Original argument retained, with each new owner

Original Step 1, now parent Step 1:

Put $A=\mathcal O_{C,x}$, $B=\mathcal O_{D,y}$ and let $\varphi:A\to B$ be the actual stalk map of $e$. By [def-weil-etale-morphism], $e$ is flat and unramified; by [def-weil-unramified-morphism] it is locally of finite type. By [def-weil-flat-morphism], $\varphi$ is a flat local homomorphism. Thus neither flatness nor locality is being inferred from a merely abstract isomorphism of rings.

Original Step 2, now parent Step 2:

Apply implication (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii) of [lem-trf-unramified-maximal-ideal-residue-criterion] to $e$ at $y$, using the whole source $D$ as the unramified neighbourhood. Its locally-finite-type hypothesis holds by Step 1. It gives $\mathfrak m_A B=\mathfrak m_B$ for $\varphi$, and says that the actual induced map $\alpha:\kappa(x)\to\kappa(y)$ is a finite separable algebraic field extension.

Original Step 3, now lem-trf-algebraic-field-tower-rationality:

Write $i:k\to\kappa(x)$ and $j:k\to\kappa(y)$ for the structural field maps. Functoriality of the maps induced on stalk quotients in [def-weil-residue-field] gives $j=\alpha i$. If $i$ is an isomorphism, every element of $\kappa(y)$ is algebraic over the image of $j$, by Step 2. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-algebraic-over-algebraically-closed-subfield] to each element: every element lies in $j(k)$. Thus $j$ is surjective, and $\alpha$ is surjective as well. Field maps are injective, so both are isomorphisms. If instead $j$ is an isomorphism, then for $a\in\kappa(x)$ take $c\in k$ with $j(c)=\alpha(a)$; injectivity of $\alpha$ gives $i(c)=a$. Thus $i$ is an isomorphism, and $\alpha=j i^{-1}$ is one too. In both cases the structural maps $i,j$, as well as $\alpha$, are isomorphisms, not just abstract field identifications.

Original Step 4, now lem-trf-finite-residue-tower-local-geometry:

Apply [lem-trf-finite-residue-point-closed-in-fibre] to the structural morphism $C\to\operatorname{Spec}k$. It is locally of finite type by hypothesis, and Step 3 says that its residue-field extension at $x$ has degree one. Hence $x$ is closed in the scheme-theoretic fibre over the unique point of $\operatorname{Spec}k$. By [def-weil-affine-spectrum] this base has one point; by [def-pc-scheme-fibre] that fibre is homeomorphic to all of $C$. Consequently $x$ is closed in $C$.

Original Step 5, now lem-trf-finite-residue-tower-local-geometry:

Apply [lem-trf-finite-residue-point-closed-in-fibre] to $e$, which is locally of finite type by Step 1. The residue extension at $y$ is finite by Step 2 (indeed degree one by Step 3). Thus $y$ is closed in $D_x$. By [def-pc-scheme-fibre], $D_x$ is homeomorphic to the subspace $e^{-1}(\{x\})$. This subspace is closed in $D$, because $x$ is closed by Step 4 and $e$ is continuous. A subset closed in a closed subspace is closed in the ambient space, so $y$ is closed in $D$.

Original Step 6, now lem-trf-finite-residue-tower-local-geometry:

The field $k$ is Noetherian: its only ideals are $(0)$ and $(1)$, both finitely generated. Hence $\operatorname{Spec}k$ is locally Noetherian by [pqf-def-locnoeth]. Apply [pqf-lem-locnoeth-lft] first to $C\to\operatorname{Spec}k$, then to $e:D\to C$, using its local finite type from Step 1. Both $C$ and $D$ are locally Noetherian. Choose, from the affine covers in [pqf-def-locnoeth], an affine neighbourhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}R$ of $x$ and one $V=\operatorname{Spec}S$ of $y$ with $R,S$ Noetherian. By clause (c) of [lem-weil-pbc-affine-open-stalk-localization], the germ maps $R\to A$ and $S\to B$ are localizations at the corresponding primes. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian] to these two maps. Thus $A$ and $B$ are Noetherian local rings, as required for the completion theorem. No global quasi-compactness is needed.

Original Step 7, now parent Step 5:

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-flat-local-completion-iso] to the actual map $\varphi:A\to B$. Its hypotheses are verified separately: Noetherian local rings by Step 6, flatness and locality by Step 1, $\mathfrak m_AB=\mathfrak m_B$ by Step 2, and an isomorphism on residue fields by Step 3. Its conclusion is that $\varphi^\wedge:A^\wedge\to B^\wedge$ is an isomorphism.
