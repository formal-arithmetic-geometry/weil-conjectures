## Statement and source corrections

**STATEMENT-DISPUTED:** read literally without the chapter's standing setting, the imported constructibility assertion is false. Take a field k of characteristic p different from ell, X=Y=Spec k[x_1,x_2,...], p=id, d=0, and the open immersion a:U=union_i D(x_i) into X. Give U the rank-one constant integral lattice. The finite quotient extended by zero has support precisely U. This open is not quasi-compact: a proposed finite subcover using variables indexed by a finite set I misses the prime ideal (x_i : i in I), which belongs to D(x_j) for j outside I. An open constructible subset of an affine scheme is quasi-compact. The support of a finite constructible sheaf is constructible, so this extension by zero cannot be constructible. The remaining V hypothesis is satisfiable: affine schemes admit affine weakly contractible pro-étale covers (Stacks, proetale.tex, proposition-find-w-contractible and lemma-get-many-weakly-contractible). The failure already occurs in the sheaf clause on X.

I restored explicitly the standing separated finite-type schemes over the fixed field from `sk-def-sfb-dc-setting`. This is the intended setting of the six-functors chapter, not a change to a target or an attempt to weaken a target. The correction is confined to this imported auxiliary statement. It makes X Noetherian and all relevant open complements quasi-compact. It does **not** impose a Noetherian or finite-type hypothesis on the weakly contractible V; the arbitrary-base proper torsion bound remains essential.

I also specified the integral coefficient as the pullback of the condensed topological valuation ring, the reduction coefficient as the finite discrete constant ring, the relative ring of the tensor product, and the inverse-image restriction r* from X to X_V.
