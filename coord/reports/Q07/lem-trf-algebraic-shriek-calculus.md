### Original step 1

Use ordinary module complexes throughout, identifying modules with sheaves on a one-object ringed site. For any map $T\to B$, choose a K-injective $T$-resolution $K\to I$; these exist because module categories are Grothendieck. Give $\operatorname{Hom}_T(B,I)$ the action $(b h)(b')=h(b'b)$, and call its derived object $C_B^T(K)$. The degreewise adjunction is explicitly $v\mapsto(m\mapsto[b\mapsto v(bm)])$, with inverse evaluation at $1$. It is an adjunction of Hom complexes, so if $N$ is an acyclic $B$-complex then $\operatorname{Hom}_B^\bullet(N,\operatorname{Hom}_T(B,I))=\operatorname{Hom}_T^\bullet(N,I)$ is acyclic. Thus the coinduced complex is K-injective over $B$. The K-injective Hom-bijection on both sides descends this adjunction to $D(B)\to D(T)$ and $C_B^T$. Changing K-injective resolutions gives homotopy-equivalent outputs: a quasi-isomorphism of K-injectives is a homotopy equivalence by that same bijection. This makes $C_B^T$ a functor, right adjoint to restriction, with counit $h\mapsto h(1)$. On forgetting the action its model is exactly $R\operatorname{Hom}_T(B,K)$ by the definition of derived Hom.

Source: literature/stacks-project/dualizing.tex, Tags 0A70 (lemma-right-adjoint), 0A71 (lemma-RHom-ext); the K-injective model and explicit ordinary adjunction.

### Original step 2

For $T\to B\to C$, the composite coinduction on a K-injective $I$ is computed by $\operatorname{Hom}_B(C,\operatorname{Hom}_T(B,I))$, since step 1 makes the inner complex K-injective. The chain isomorphism from $\operatorname{Hom}_T(C,I)$ sends $h$ to $(c\mapsto[b\mapsto h(bc)])$; its inverse is $v\mapsto(c\mapsto v(c)(1))$. Both maps are $C$-linear and commute with differentials. The composite of the two counits is $v\mapsto v(1)(1)$, exactly evaluation of the inverse image at $1\in C$. This proves the canonical composition comparison, with its counit compatibility, for arbitrary ring maps and unbounded complexes.

### Original step 3

Let $T\to B=T/(s)$ have $s$ a nonzerodivisor. If $B=0$, both coinduction and tensor with $B$ are zero, proving the claim immediately; assume $B\ne0$ for the regular-sequence calculation. The two-term resolution $[T\xrightarrow{s}T]$ in degrees $-1,0$ makes $B$ perfect over $T$. The sequence $(s)$ is Koszul-regular, and its conormal has basis $\bar s$ by the cited regular-sequence results. The dual Koszul calculation gives $H^i(C_B^T(T))=0$ for $i\ne1$ and $H^1(C_B^T(T))\cong B$, with its $B$-action, by evaluating the dual of $\bar s$. Since an object with only one cohomology module is isomorphic to that module in its cohomological degree, $C_B^T(T)\cong B[-1]$ in $D(B)$. The perfect-algebra coinduction tensor comparison therefore gives $C_B^T(L)\cong L\otimes_T^{\mathbf L}B[-1]$ for every $L\in D(T)$.

### Original step 11

The open immersion is a monomorphism: two maps to its open subscheme with the same composite have the same underlying map and sheaf map, since the inclusion restricts the structure sheaf. Hence its self fibre product is itself. The affine-fibre-product theorem identifies the resulting multiplication map $A\otimes_RA\to A$ as an isomorphism, with both coprojections identified with the identity. Set $P_A=P\otimes_RA=A[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, and write $a_i$ for the images of $x_i$ in $A$. The scalar extension of $P\twoheadrightarrow A$ is the evaluation $P_A\to A$, $x_i\mapsto a_i$, since $A\otimes_RA=A$. Its kernel is generated by $x_i-a_i$: subtracting the evaluation of a polynomial gives a sum of multiples of these differences. Successively quotienting by the first $i$ differences leaves a polynomial ring over $A$ in the remaining variables, and the next difference is monic. Thus this sequence is Koszul-regular; its conormal basis is the classes of these differences. The dual Koszul theorem gives $R\operatorname{Hom}_{P_A}(A,P_A)\cong A[-n]$, as an underlying $P_A$-complex; its sole nonzero cohomology is identified $A$-linearly using the ordered conormal generator.
