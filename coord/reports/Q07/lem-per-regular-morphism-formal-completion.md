## Review by Q07

I read Temkin's actual Corollary 2.4.5, its proof, and both paragraphs of Lemma 2.4.6 on printed pp.16--17. I also read Lemma 2.4.4 and its proof on p.16, and the definitions and completion theorems on pp.9,14. In this PDF Theorem 2.1.6 is the characteristic-zero Nishimura--Nishimura theorem, while the general version is Theorem 2.1.8 (Gabber); I kept those distinct. The seed's broad reference to 2.1.6 suffices over Q but is not a general-characteristic citation. For the new quasi-excellence statement I additionally read the local ILO Expose I 9.2 on printed p.17, which explicitly says a Noetherian I-adically complete ring is quasi-excellent if its quotient is. Sections 2.4.1/2.4.3 identify its application to arbitrary formal affine charts. No SGA source is used.

For lem-per-completed-base-regularity the source is Temkin Lemma 2.4.4. I wrote out the target ideal of definition J and the I-image containment, rather than silently requiring that the source and target topologies be generated by the same ideal. The proof there tests open primes, identifies the base local completions and residue fields, obtains flatness by completion and faithful-flat descent, and applies formal-smoothness localization over a quasi-excellent base. This is a genuine supporting ring theorem, not a restatement of either clause of the item. I did not put its proof into the statement-only node.

For lem-per-formal-affine-principal-refinement I read Stacks formal-spaces.tex Tag 0AHY (section-formal-schemes-EGA), including the displayed inverse-limit formula for sections on D(f), its canonical restriction maps, and equation 0AHZ. I read schemes.tex Tag 01IW (lemma-standard-open-two-affines) and its actual restriction-of-functions argument: shrink to one principal inside the other affine, then restrict a principal function from the larger affine. The same argument works for these formal affine structure sheaves. The faithful-flat clause is specifically at an open maximal ideal, not at an arbitrary non-open prime: modulo the ideal of definition the completed localization has the same localized quotient, giving the open maximal ideal over m; ordinary localization followed by Noetherian completion is flat, and its local map is faithfully flat. This structural lemma has neither the target's regularity assumption nor its conclusion.

For Step 1 I opened the statements of flat-morphism-affine-criterion, fibre-product-affine-spectra, scheme-fibre, regular-scheme and coherent. The first, rather than the mere definition of flatness at local rings, supplies flatness of the affine ring map. The second identifies the affine fibre as the tensor product with the residue field, and the finite extensions as the corresponding base changes. Regularity of those fibres is precisely the hypothesis. The quasi-excellent-formal-affines statement explicitly covers arbitrary formal affine opens, so Step 5 does not assume its conclusion just for the originally chosen charts. Noetherianity of original affine rings makes their ideals finitely generated.

For Step 2 I read def-exc-excellent-ring, g-ring-completion-regular and regular-composition. Quasi-excellence includes the G-ring hypothesis; the latter theorem has a Noetherian-fibre side condition. I checked it by identifying the fibre with a localization of D/pD and citing the actual surjective-image-noetherian and localization-noetherian statements. This applies repeatedly in Steps 4 and 5 as the final completed rings are Noetherian.

For Step 3 the completion definition gives the maps of compatible sequences. The containment IB subset J makes all A/I^n to B/J^n maps well-defined and compatible. The completion-mod-ideal statement supplies actual completeness for JD; it is not merely a definition of completion. Modulo J the extended I-ideal is zero, so the completed-base criterion has exactly its stated containment. I checked its base is the original quasi-excellent A and its target is the already constructed complete D.

For Step 4 I read localization-regular, regular-through-localization, finite-type-over-g-ring and def-exc-excellent-ring. The first states regularity of arbitrary localization maps; the second really cancels a localization of the source. For C_s and D_t, finite-type-over-g-ring applies to the explicit one-generator quotient presentations, and J-2 follows because finite lists of algebra generators compose. I replaced the initial use of a scheme finite-type quasi-excellence theorem by this ring-level G-ring plus J-2 argument, avoiding an unstated scheme/ring locality premise. I corrected the formal-open containment issue by t -> t*v(s) before forming C_s to D_t. The extended original ideals give the same containment after localization and completion, including that replacement.

For Step 5 the formal principal-refinement statement supplies simultaneous principal neighbourhoods in the old and new affine opens, inside any specified neighbourhood. I used this twice, first at f(y) in the target and then at y in the inverse image. Thus the map between the smaller rings is an actual restriction of a Step-3 chart map and Step 4 applies. Composition then gives E to the smaller source ring, without assuming the desired E to F regularity.

For Step 6 I read regular-local-maximal and regular-permanence in full. The former includes the criterion at all primes, as well as the maximal-target criterion, so it gives E_p to the selected smaller localized ring. The structural formal-chart node supplies a faithfully flat LOCAL map from F_m to that ring, hence flatness and surjectivity on spectra, exactly the hypotheses of regular-permanence. That gives E_p to F_m. Testing every maximal m of the Noetherian F proves E to F. Every maximal m is open by the structural node, so no point needed in this test is lost by passing to Spf.

Small cases: I=0 gives the discrete source completion; J may still be nonzero and the argument uses a genuine target completion. I=J=0 reduces to the original regular map and ordinary affine refinements. For A=Q[x], B=A[y], I=(x), J=(x,y), the topologies differ and neither equality nor adicity of the morphism is assumed. Identity maps with unequal compatible ideals are included. The zero ring and empty completion (unit ideal) have no maximal ideals, so the final criterion is vacuous; a compatible map into an empty target forces the source completion empty. Nonreduced quasi-excellent rings are allowed: no step needs reducedness or universal catenarity. Principal opens with t topologically nilpotent are empty and are never chosen around a point; t=1 gives the whole affine. No finite-type hypothesis is inserted on f.
