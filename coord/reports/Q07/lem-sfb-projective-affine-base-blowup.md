## Review by Q07

I checked the node and every cited statement against delivery-errors B--E with the Stacks sources open. No new node is being used to repackage either conclusion. The proof constructs the required projective-bundle factorization before applying the H-projectivity tools. Every step is shorter than 1500 characters, and none hides a new long lemma. The only step marked elementary is the explicit graded-surjectivity calculation at Step 3.

At Step 1, `def-kun-projective` supplies a closed immersion into a finite projective space. `lem-weil-pbc-projective-ample-invertible` states, for exactly an immersion into projective space over Spec A, that the pullback of O(1) is invertible and absolutely ample. The given A, N, i and its factorization meet that theorem's hypotheses; I do not infer absolute ampleness from relative ampleness without this statement.

At Step 2, `def-nagata-sheaf-rees-algebra` really constructs a quasi-coherent graded algebra with the given multiplication, rather than only naming one. `def-sbc-ideal-sheaf-powers` explicitly gives I^n(V)=I(V)^n on affine opens. `lem-weil-pbc-affine-tilde-gamma-adjunction` clauses (2)--(4) provide the unit isomorphism, counit on quasi-coherent modules, and preservation of small colimits by tilde. Apply the coproduct clause to the small discrete index N: it identifies the sheaf sum on V with the tilde of the direct sum, and its unit computes the sections. Comparing the homogeneous products gives the graded algebra in `def-nagata-rees-algebra`. Thus there is no assertion that arbitrary sections commute with infinite sums on non-affine opens.

At Step 3, I read both parts of `lem-weil-pbc-quasi-coherent-symmetric-algebra`: part (a) constructs the algebra map from the degree-one inclusion, and part (b) is the exact affine identification used to compute it. `lem-weil-pbc-symmetric-algebra-graded` defines its homogeneous pieces as spans of symmetric monomials and proves their direct-sum grading. A finite sum of products in an ideal power lifts to the same sum of monomials, including coefficients; degree zero is the identity. Hence both grading and surjectivity are explicit and remain valid for zero divisors or zero ideals. Surjectivity on an affine cover gives surjectivity of the sheaf map.

At Step 4, I opened `def-nagata-relative-proj`, `lemma-nagata-relative-proj-exists`, `def-nagata-affine-blowup`, `def-nagata-global-blowup-local-model`, `def-nagata-global-blowup-glue-data` and `def-nagata-global-blowup`. I also opened `def-nagata-rees-localization-map`, `def-nagata-affine-blowup-restriction-map`, `prop-nagata-affine-blowup-restrict` and `def-nagata-global-blowup-overlap-basic-map`, and cited them explicitly after the first successful dry run. Their statements give the coefficient map x to x/1, its induced Proj restriction isomorphism, and the overlap formula r_j composed with inverse r_i. These identify the actual local schemes, structure maps and transitions on each side. The comparison is the identity on the coefficientwise Rees data identified at Step 2. Non-affine intersections use affine refinements in both constructions. Crucially, `lemma-nagata-relative-proj-exists` explicitly states uniqueness up to an isomorphism compatible with affine charts, which yields the global comparison over X. I did not cite the existence-only statement of `lem-weil-pbc-relative-glueing-affine-opens` for a uniqueness assertion it does not make.

At Step 5, clause (3) of `prop-nagata-relative-proj-functoriality` states precisely that a surjective graded map induces a closed immersion in the reverse direction. Its source is the symmetric algebra, generated in degree one, and the map is the identity in degree zero, so the application also satisfies the source conditions of 07ZK. Both algebras are quasi-coherent by the previously cited constructions. `def-trf-projective-bundle-morphism` defines the bundle as Proj(Sym E) with E quasi-coherent of finite type, so the displayed immersion with E=I verifies that definition, without assuming I locally free.

At Step 6, `lem-trf-projective-bundle-to-h-projective` requires exactly bundle projectivity of b and an ample invertible sheaf on X. Steps 5 and 1 provide those hypotheses respectively. At Step 7, `lem-trf-h-projective-composition` states the result for arbitrary two H-projective scheme morphisms; take b and f. Neither tool asks for integrality, excellence, regularity or a nonempty centre. These generic off-unit tools are explained individually in `split_report.tool_uses` for the maintainer's review, not added as maintainer-owned top-level tool approvals.

I tested the small cases before finalizing. If I=0 then the Rees algebra has no positive degree, so its Proj is empty, and the surjection and closed immersion still apply. If I=O_X then locally the Rees algebra is R[t]; the chart D_+(t) has degree-zero ring R and is all of Proj, so the blowup is the identity, with the same coefficientwise gluing. If X is empty, the whole construction and both conclusions are vacuous. For a nonzero nilpotent ideal, all positive-degree homogeneous elements are nilpotent and the blowup can also be empty; the argument still works. For a concrete non-domain test, take X=Spec(k[e]/(e^2)), which is H-projective via P^0, and I=(e); the symmetric-to-Rees map is still surjective and the empty Proj causes no exception. No finite global generating set of I is assumed: the ample-base conversion theorem supplies the finite-space embedding after bundle projectivity has been established. No assertion that A must be a field, excellent or reduced is introduced; in fact the argument does not need its Noetherian hypothesis, which is retained.
