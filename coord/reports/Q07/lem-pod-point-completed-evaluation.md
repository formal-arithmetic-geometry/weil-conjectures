### Original step 1: lem-pod-integral-point-coefficient-adjunction

Put $L(M)=B\otimes_{\underline R}\underline M$. The valuation-ring lemma makes $R$ a PID, hence Noetherian by the PID lemma. Constant sheafification is exact by the constant-module section lemma, and $B$ is flat over $\underline R$ by the completed-constant reduction lemma. Consequently $L$ is exact. Sections $\Gamma(s,-)$ on $B$-modules are exact by the point-sections theorem. The sheaf-tensor universal property gives the adjunction $L\dashv\Gamma(s,-)$: $h$ corresponds to $x\mapsto h_s(1\otimes x)$; the inverse sends $t$ to $b\otimes x\mapsto b\,t(x)|_U$. These formulas respect balancing and restrictions and are inverse on the generators $1\otimes x$.

### Original step 2: lem-pod-integral-point-coefficient-adjunction

The scheme $s=\operatorname{Spec}k$ is nonempty, connected, separated and of finite type over $k$. Clause (1) of connected-constant $H^0$ therefore identifies $\Gamma(s,(R/\varpi^m)_s)=R/\varpi^m$ through the constant-value map, for every $m\geq1$. Limits of sheaves are computed sectionwise, so $\Gamma(s,B)=\varprojlim_mR/\varpi^m=R$; the last equality is the ring isomorphism given by the quotient maps in the integers-profinite-tower lemma. Thus all three extra hypotheses (exact $L$, exact $\Gamma$, and this constant-value identification) of point-sections reconstruction and point-derived-Hom evaluation hold. ### Original step 3: lem-pod-point-projective-dual-tensor-models

Each $P^i$ is a summand of a finite free module. Applying duals and tensor products to its splitting shows that $(P^i)^\vee$ and $P^i\otimes_R(P^j)^\vee$ are finite projective. Hence $P$, $P^\vee$, and $T=\operatorname{Tot}(P\otimes_RP^\vee)$ are bounded finite-projective complexes; their cohomology is finitely generated because $R$ is Noetherian. Point-sections reconstruction therefore gives $L(P),L(P^\vee),L(T)\in D^b_c(s,R)$, including derived completeness and a uniform bound for all reductions. The termwise balancing isomorphism $(b\otimes x)\otimes(b'\otimes f)\mapsto bb'\otimes(x\otimes f)$ identifies $C\otimes_B J$ with $L(T)$. Each term of $C$ and $J$ is a summand of a finite sum of $B$, hence flat, so the bounded-above-flat K-flat lemma says their total tensor computes $C\otimes_B^LJ$. This proves all three membership assertions. The same reconstruction applied to $R[0]$ gives $B\in D^b_c(s,R)$. Its unit and that for $P$ are the actual maps $r\mapsto r$ and $x\mapsto1\otimes x$.

Original citations: lem-pod-point-sections-reconstruction, lem-pc-bounded-above-flat-kflat-site, def-pur-sheaf-tensor-product.

### Original step 4: lem-pod-point-projective-dual-tensor-models

Apply the bounded-summand-of-finite-free internal-Hom model with first complex $C$ and second complex $B[0]$. Its boundedness hypotheses hold because $P$ is bounded. It identifies $R\mathcal Hom_B(C,B)$ with $\mathcal Hom_B^\bullet(C,B)$. The natural map $B\otimes_{\underline R}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}_R(P^i,R)}\to\mathcal Hom_B(L(P^i),B)$ sends $b\otimes f$ to $(b'\otimes x\mapsto bb'f(x))$. For $P^i=R^r$ this is the identity on $B^r$; applying the inclusion and projection of a finite-free splitting makes it an isomorphism for $P^i$. These identifications commute with precomposition by $d_P$ and with multiplication, so, using $d(f)=d_Bf-(-1)^{\deg f}fd_C$, they form an isomorphism of Hom complexes $J\cong\mathcal Hom_B^\bullet(C,B)$. It is the canonical arrow specified by evaluation. Thus $J\to R\mathcal Hom_B(C,B)$ is the claimed isomorphism.
