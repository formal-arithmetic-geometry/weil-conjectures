Step 1 uses def-cbc-enhanced-diagrams-cones solely for the inverse-triangle meaning of an equivalence and the degreewise nerve convention. The words 010 and 101 really have degenerate long edges 00 and 11, respectively. Applying a simplicial map preserves those triangles, and applying the relations defining hD gives the two inverse equations. This is elementary and needs no theorem that maps preserve equivalences.

Step 2 uses both precise clauses of the new node: edge representatives for alpha and its inverse, then the triangle criterion for their two equations with identity edges. Its second citation gives the definition that these witnesses make f an equivalence. The quasicategory hypothesis is present. This short deduction is elementary relative to that non-elementary cited theorem. I did not use Mathlib's quotient-category statement as if it already concerned hD.

Step 3 writes the combinatorial argument in full. Nondegenerate nerve simplices are alternating words. Attaching the word starting at epsilon in dimension n adds exactly that word and its face d0, which starts at the other vertex. Every other face is either the preceding word or a degeneration of a shorter word. The missing face's boundary is already attached. This proves the successive horn attachments, rather than merely claiming that the endpoint is anodyne. For n=1 the horn is the initial vertex. For epsilon=0, stopping after n=1 is exactly the forward Delta1, so its inclusion has the same filtration starting at n=2. A lifting problem through the countable union is solved by the successive compatible horn fillers; no model category theorem is hidden in that sentence.

Step 4 uses the core-Kan-fibration statement for D to a point. I checked its isofibration hypothesis directly: inner horns fill in D, and the unique point equivalence lifts to a degenerate identity edge. It therefore makes the core Kan. The f from step 2 lies in the core by definition, and step 3 extends it while keeping its endpoints. This is the needed construction of a J map. The new node alone would not give it.

Step 5 uses the literal restriction clause of isofibration-exponentiation for the boundary inclusion K into L. This is a degreewise injection of small simplicial sets, including the empty boundary in dimension zero. It supplies both functor quasicategories and their isofibration r. The core theorem then supplies the Kan fibration q. Transposing the actual boundary datum gives a map J into the boundary functor category; its inverse triangles imply that all its simplices lie in the core. The prescribed L-to-C map is a vertex, and all vertices belong to the source core. Thus q has exactly the required endpoint lifting square. No conclusion that every J-by-L map lands in C's core is asserted; horizontal L edges may be noninvertible.

Step 6 uses the endpoint horn filtration against that Kan fibration and transposes its lift back. Its restriction equality is precisely the boundary equality for a, and its endpoint equality is precisely b. The boundary dimension m=0 is treated separately in the text: Fun(empty,C) is a point, so the same proof supplies vertex surjectivity. The quoted trivial-Kan definition asks for exactly those positive-dimensional boundary lifts and vertex surjectivity, with no extra nonemptiness condition. This final deduction is elementary from step 5 and the explicit filtration.

Step 7 checks the universe and empty-input clauses, rather than removing them. The nerve degree sets are finite; the filtration is countable and all sets and choices are in the same input universe. Empty D has no vertex pair, and empty C gives the identity empty-to-empty evaluation, whose lifting problems have no base simplex. The restriction theorem requires small K and L, which are finite simplicial shapes in the required universe.

I tested the new statement and the proof on the empty quasicategory, a point, nerves of discrete sets, the nerve of [1], the walking-isomorphism nerve and a one-object groupoid. In N([1]) the forward edge is not invertible, so the proof cannot incorrectly turn it into a J map; the only isomorphic vertex pairs are equal. In an ordinary nerve, the new composition criterion is exactly the ordinary equation g composed with f equals h. In a Kan complex its triangles are homotopies of path composition, which is consistent with the source; no strict inverse of a topological path is assumed.
