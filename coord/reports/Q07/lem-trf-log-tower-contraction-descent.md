Step 2 uses that same node's assertion that a whole center has an identity blowup, together with the definition of contraction in def-trf-strict-etale-log-blowup-procedure. The induction explicitly specifies S, r(i), its initial identity, the retained case and the deleted case. Step 3 uses the procedure definition for recursive pullback of actual ideals and the fs blowup definition for uniqueness of universal factorizations. At each retained stage the two constructions have the same center after the preceding identification, so universality supplies inverse maps and proves their composites are identities. It is not enough merely to say that the schemes are isomorphic: the stage induction preserves centers, transition arrows and log maps. Step 1 already established the scheme-theoretic stage pullbacks; I did not apply a Zariski-log chart lemma to arbitrary non-Zariski log structures.

Step 4 uses synchronization to ensure that neither C(D) nor the stipulated contracted A loses stages under the surjective cover. The local isomorphism composed with step 3 is therefore between the full uncontracted pullbacks. This is exactly the hypothesis of the second paragraph of lem-trf-log-tower-cover-rigidity, which states descent of any such ordered center-preserving isomorphism without requiring an additional cocycle hypothesis. Thus the citation really supplies existence, uniqueness, and recovery of the specified local isomorphism. It is not a mere citation of effectivity for objects.

Step 5 checks index maps independently of actual log morphisms. The ideal generated by a composite image equals the iterated generated ideal by expanding its generators. The synchronization node explicitly says a whole ideal stays whole under every fs pullback, while reflection of being whole is used only for the surjective cover at the intermediate levels. This proof is elementary bookkeeping; it does not impose surjectivity on consecutive strict etale morphisms.
