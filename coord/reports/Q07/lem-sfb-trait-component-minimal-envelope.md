I read Anantharaman 4.1 (printed pp.54-55), Lemma 4.2.1 (pp.55-56), and Lemma 4.2.4 with its proof and following subgroup/action paragraph (pp.59-61) in the Numdam scan. I read Stacks more-morphisms.tex, label lemma-quasi-finite-quasi-section-meeting-nearby-open-X, Tag 05GU, including its reduction to the previous local-spectrum lemma. The new valuation-section lemma is explicitly an agent combination and not falsely attributed verbatim to 05GU or the paper. Its detailed intended argument is: pass to a finite-extension schematic model by 4.2.1, represent the generic component closure; if the entire Galois orbit of a geometric component avoids the special fibre, remove that orbit (finite and closed on the model) and descend the open, contradicting unavoidability. A component therefore meets the closed fibre. Apply 05GU/trait selection on an affine neighbourhood of its reduced closure to obtain a finite-field quasi-section. Over a henselian DVR the valuation has a unique extension to the fixed algebraic closure; the finite normalization is local. The selected quasi-section extends to its DVR normalization by the local-ring map, and then to B. The Galois action preserves B and permutes the geometric components over an ordinary connected component transitively, so the construction reaches each D. This is a genuine geometric input with its own hypotheses, strictly smaller than product compatibility or the envelope construction.

For step 1 I opened the field-effectivity, fpqc-cover-base-change, qcqs-permanence and separated-base-change statements. They give exactly the field representations and retained local presentation/separation used. Both field fibres are covered, including arbitrary imperfect fields and nonreduced groups. No claim that fpqc-local schematicity automatically implies global schematicity is made.

For step 2 I opened each cited component, local Noetherianity, base-change, algebraic-closure and Zariski-lemma statement. Local Noetherianity is used only on schemes over fields, never on T or P. The group-local-minimal-prime lemma gives disjoint irreducible components; locally finite closed components are open by removing the finitely many others near a point. The component-cover lemma supplies coverage. The field-extension theorem explicitly gives actual surjectivity of a component onto C, not merely density. A maximal ideal in an affine neighbourhood gives an algebraic residue extension by Zariski's lemma, equal to the algebraically closed base. Translation then identifies its component with the neutral component, whose finite type is exactly the lft-field-group-neutral-component statement. Quasi-compactness descends here by the elementary open-cover argument, not a hidden finite-type descent theorem. The hypothesis of finite type for the whole field group is never introduced.
