### Old step 1 — parent step 1

Choose a surjection $P=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\to S$ from [def-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra], with kernel $I$, and let $\mathfrak q$ be the inverse image of $\mathfrak p$. By [lem-weil-pbc-polynomial-ring-field-regular-ring], $R=P_{\mathfrak q}$ is regular local and hence Noetherian. The map $R/IR\to A$, $(u/v)\bmod IR\mapsto\bar u/\bar v$, is an isomorphism: both sides invert exactly the images of $P\setminus\mathfrak q$ in $P/I$, and their kernels are the fractions with numerator in $I$. Thus [lem-weil-pbc-surjective-image-noetherian] makes $A$ Noetherian. Put $C=\widehat A$. By [lem-weil-pbc-adic-completion-noetherian] and [lem-weil-pbc-local-completion-local-ring], $C$ is Noetherian local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak n=\mathfrak pAC$ and residue field $F$. By [lem-weil-pbc-adic-completion-mod-ideal], it is $\mathfrak n$-adically complete.

### Old step 2 — lem-trf-equicharacteristic-complete-local-presentation

The structural maps make $C$ contain the field $k$: a unital map from a field into a nonzero local ring is injective. In particular $C$ and its residue field $F$ have the same characteristic. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-cohen-coefficient-ring-exists] to $C$. Its coefficient subring $\Lambda$ has maximal ideal $p\Lambda$, where $p$ is the characteristic of $F$. This ideal is zero: for $p=0$ by definition, and for $p>0$ because $pC=0$ and $\Lambda\subseteq C$. Hence $\Lambda$ is a field, since each nonzero element lies outside its zero maximal ideal and is a unit. The coefficient-ring residue isomorphism therefore gives a ring map $\alpha:F\to C$ whose composite with $C\to F$ is the identity. This choice need not respect the originally given embedding of $k$; the required conclusion is an isomorphism of rings.

Source: literature/stacks-project/algebra.tex; 032A (theorem-cohen-structure-theorem), part (1), and 0326 (definition-coefficient-ring), remark case (1): a coefficient ring in a complete local ring containing a field is a coefficient field.
