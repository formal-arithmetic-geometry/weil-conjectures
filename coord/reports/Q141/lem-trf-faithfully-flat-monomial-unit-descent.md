I checked the five steps with the item, the cited statement, and the sources open. Step 1 uses the fraction equality built into the item's statement: the inverse has numerator g and denominator s^m, its product with f equals one precisely after multiplication by a power s^h, and s=a(sum p_i) is nonzero. Cancellation therefore gives fg=s^m. This is an explicit fraction calculation, not an uncited localization theorem. Step 2 applies exactly the item's factorization assumption to b=phi(f), c=phi(g), q=m(sum p_i). It uses no assertion about the coefficient v descending; v and its inverse explicitly give the equality of extended principal ideals.

Step 3 is the only non-elementary step. Its only cited node states, for every ideal I of a commutative ring and every faithfully flat map phi, that phi^{-1}(IB)=I. The item supplies precisely those hypotheses. The step applies it once to (f) and once to (a(p)); it also explains directly why extension of a principal ideal is generated by the image of its generator. `citations.json` quotes the cited node's complete statement verbatim. No universal-injectivity theorem is silently substituted for the required ideal-contraction consequence: that consequence appears in the node itself and in the second sentence of Tag 05CK.

Step 4 unpacks membership in a principal ideal into f=d a(p) and a(p)=ef. The nonzero-monomial hypothesis is used to cancel a(p), giving ed=1. Since A is commutative this makes d a unit with inverse e. This also explains why association is obtained in A even if B has zero divisors; no domain assumption on B is used. Step 5 checks the other inclusion rather than leaving it implicit: if p=sum n_i p_i, take N=sum n_i and t=product a(p_i)^(N-n_i). The identity a(p)t=s^N gives the displayed inverse for u a(p). This is just the monoid homomorphism law and finite exponent arithmetic.

I tested the statement's degeneracies. With P={0} and no generators, q_0=0 and s=1, so steps 1 and 5 say exactly that the preimage is A's units. For a nonempty list of zero monoid elements or of unit-valued generators, the same product calculation remains valid, and the factorization hypothesis forces the appropriate monomials to be associated to units. The proof rejects f=0, since fg=s^m is impossible in the nonzero domain. It does not assume P is sharp, cancellative, saturated, or embedded in A; only finite generation and the explicit homomorphism/nonvanishing hypotheses enter. For a faithfully flat completion, the argument needs exactly the stated upstairs factorization property, which is not proved or assumed automatic here.
