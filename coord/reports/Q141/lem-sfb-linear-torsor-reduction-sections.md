Lem-weil-pbc-affine-morphism-permanence supplies finite unions and lem-weil-pbc-fpqc-descend-affine supplies the final affine structure morphism. This checks both the cover and affine hypotheses and avoids taking an infinite affine coproduct to be affine as a scheme.

Step 3 uses the principal-open localization statement to identify GL with the determinant open, affine-space smoothness over every scheme, open-immersion smooth composition, and the smoothness criterion to obtain flatness. The explicit finitely presented coordinate algebra gives finite presentation, and the identity gives surjectivity. For H these properties follow from the actual hypotheses, without reductivity, a field base, or connectedness. The space flat/fp locality statement applies to schemes via their identity charts and gives precisely the separate flatness and finite-presentation assertions. The smooth/surjective locality node supplies surjectivity. The local inverse of the torsor square is written explicitly and glues using step 1. Thus a local sheaf torsor really satisfies the stronger represented-torsor convention of the item.

Step 4 uses the full categorical equivalence and rank-reflection clause of lem-pc-fpqc-descent. It has a genuine fpqc cover by step 1. With e_j=e_i g_ij, chart-j coordinates become g_ij v in chart i, and g_ik=g_ij g_jk, so the orientation is consistent with a right torsor and the standard left action on columns. This produces rank n over arbitrary, even nonreduced, T. The associated sheaf relation is checked on local representatives, not on a presumed global orbit quotient.

Step 5 uses the same node's full faithfulness to descend module isomorphisms and their inverses. Rank-n finite local freeness supplies the Zariski trivializations required to represent the frame sheaf by steps 2--3. Evaluation [f,v] maps to f(v), and the torsor-to-frame comparison sends e to (v maps to [e,v]); both are locally invertible. The formulas on arrows establish a groupoid equivalence. The statement's finite locally free notion and each morphism's type have been checked.

Step 6 uses affine-morphism permanence only for the base change of Q, which is assumed affine. The rest explicitly applies steps 1--3: locally lift cosets via the assumed H-torsor L to Q, identify the quotient sheaf with Q, represent and descend it, and check E to E/H is an H-torsor. It does not assume Q is affine merely because H is affine; nor does it assume every rational coset lifts globally.

Step 7 uses the previously established affine descent and local torsor criterion, not an uncited extension-of-structure-group theorem. The formula [p_i h,l] maps to i(h)l checks both the quotient relation and the right L-action. Changing p_i to p_i h changes [p_i,1] by right multiplication by i(h), which is exactly what is needed to glue the quotient section. An arbitrary torsor has no chosen global point in this construction.

Step 8 cites exactly the base-change clauses of the flat/fp and smooth/surjective locality nodes for sigma-pullback of E to E/H. The other assertions follow from prior steps and the two explicit inverse comparisons: [p,l] maps to pl, and p maps to ([p,1],base(p)). They become identities on trivializing covers. Their naturality on arrows, and preservation of sigma, are verified in the text. This establishes equivalence of groupoids, not just a classification of objects.

Step 9 invokes lem-pc-fpqc-descent only for the uniqueness and full faithfulness needed to identify the pullback module obtained from the pulled-back matrices. It gives the same frame on each test scheme, and gives the quotient and contracted-product comparisons on local representatives. The pulled-back covers remain fppf by the topology axioms. Formula equality proves coherence under two successive base changes, which is needed in the next two steps.

Step 10 is elementary: the forward map is the action on p, and the inverse applies g^{-1} at the point gt. The cocycle has the order g_1 acting on g_2 p, yielding g_1 g_2 p. This proves the claim for an arbitrary acting S-group G, without accidentally assuming G flat. Restricting the action to sigma^*E is possible exactly when sigma is invariant. Substitution proves unit, cocycle and arrow compatibility rather than appealing vaguely to transport.
