For the new orbit node I checked Milne 1.65(b),(c) for locally closed images and flat orbit factorization, 7.4(b) for smooth reduced orbits, 7.5 for invariant closures and boundaries, and 7.6/1.66 for strict dimension decrease. Stabilizer fibres are translates by the group identity; their common dimension and the faithfully flat orbit map give the dimension formula through 0B2L. Translation through a rational point makes the dimensions constant on the orbit. For a connected smooth group the identity-component irreducibility theorem makes its image irreducible. Faithful flatness of the orbit map persists after field extension and supplies the asserted transitivity over algebraically closed extensions. This is genuinely general orbit geometry, with no finite equivariant morphism V to W among its hypotheses.

I opened the statement of every cited DAG node. The quoted statements in citations.json are copied verbatim and the proof uses the following specific clauses.

Step 1 uses the separate finite-type, finite-morphism, surjectivity and smooth-base-change assertions, not an assumption that a finite map is flat. Finite type over a Noetherian field gives Noetherian schemes; Noetherian open subschemes are quasi-compact and the quasi-compact open immersion lemma makes their inclusions finite type. These statements apply without separatedness. Geometric reducedness is precisely the inline hypothesis of the item and is invoked only after field extension.

Step 2 uses finite surjectivity to produce a nonempty finite scheme fibre of u; the closed-point residue-field lemma over the algebraically closed field makes a closed point of this fibre rational. The new node gives the P-orbit reduced scheme, local closedness and the stabilizer dimension formula. I added closed-point density explicitly: the orbit support has all rational points of the prescribed W piece, so its closure is the whole piece; the orbit's closed complement in that closure must be empty. No reducedness of W is inferred.

Step 3 uses the full neutral-component statement, including clopen finite type, geometric irreducibility and field-extension equality. Open restriction of a smooth morphism is exactly the open-immersion smooth-composition lemma. The coset dimension observation is written out as an elementary finite-cover argument. Its complement is closed, so closed-point density applies; I corrected an initial draft which called this complement open. For an arbitrary finite-type group L, every nonempty open subgroup contains the identity and is clopen, since its other cosets form its open complement. Quasi-compactness then yields finitely many cosets. This proves the dimension equality also for a nonreduced stabilizer subgroup.

Step 4 checks J is a subgroup of H by equivariance as morphisms, not just on rational points. Finite fibres of v are exactly the clause of the finite closed-map/fibre node being used. Stabilizers and fibres of orbit maps are scheme-theoretic by the new node. The finite coset union is closed, contains H(F), and is therefore all of H by density. Each coset is isomorphic to J even when the stabilizer is nonreduced; the finite-union dimension argument proves equality of dimensions. No orbit-stabilizer dimension equality for abstract groups is substituted for a scheme dimension argument.

Step 5 applies the new dimension formula to P^0, which is smooth and finite type by step 3, and to the finite-type source. The stabilizer is exactly J intersect P^0, so every term in the displayed equality has been justified. The unique open-factorization node says precisely how the restrictions of the action and v are scheme morphisms on the prescribed open pieces. It is not used to factor through a non-open subset.

Step 6 applies the existing image/fibre dimension bound to the finite map of finite-type schemes, with d=d_j and n=0. The zero-dimensionality of the finite scheme fibres is proved explicitly using finite-dimensional algebras over fields and primes; it does not assume the fibres are reduced or etale. This is a few-line consequence of the definition of a finite morphism, not a new node.

Step 7 uses the invariant boundary and strict orbit dimension inequality in the new node. A nonempty boundary is locally closed and contains a rational closed point, using the stated closed-point density and residue-field results. Step 5 then contradicts its smaller orbit dimension. Once each orbit is a closed irreducible subset of maximal possible dimension, the definition of Krull dimension shows it is an irreducible component. The existing finite-components node applies because step 1 proved Noetherianity. Finitely many disjoint closed orbits cover rational points, and density promotes this to a cover of the scheme. I corrected the draft to distinguish this use of density from the partition of rational points: arbitrary nonclosed points do not automatically belong to a rational-point orbit before this finite-cover argument. Connectedness of each irreducible piece and the clopen partition identify the actual connected components.

Step 8 uses uniqueness of reduced induced closed subschemes on each clopen support, where V_F is reduced by hypothesis. Thus the smooth orbit scheme is the given open subscheme of V_F; no mere bijection of supports is treated as an isomorphism. Smoothness is local by its cited definition. Faithful flatness of field extension and the flat-affine criterion check the fpqc hypotheses of the base-descent lemma: the field spectrum map is affine/quasi-compact and surjective, with a flat field-extension algebra map. The descent node states the full general scheme assertion and adds no finite-type or separatedness restriction.

Step 9 uses transitivity over every algebraically closed extension from the new orbit statement and the explicit neutral-component base-change equality from the neutral-component node. This is why the conclusion includes the user's quantifier over further extensions, not only F-points.
