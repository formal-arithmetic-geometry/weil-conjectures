## Review by Q67

I checked the statement against the algebraic meaning of all its objects and read each statement cited in its proof. The unavailable Temkin and BGR sources are explicitly requested above: the item's proof is my argument, and neither source was represented as read. The two new analytic inputs are left unsplit, with complete self-contained hypotheses and no proof sketch in their statements. The supporting argument for the bounded-powers input is the familiar finite-extension one: compare the supplied finite-dimensional norm with the unique extending multiplicative norm over complete F; bounded powers force the extended norm of the element to be at most one; all conjugates have that norm, so the coefficients of its monic minimal polynomial have norm at most one, including repeated inseparable roots. This explains why a spectral-value coefficient bound alone is not an exact Mathlib attachment. The analytic Weierstrass input includes preparation as well as division because the proof needs to replace a normalized distinguished series by a monic polynomial in its ideal.

Step 1 constructs the complete normed algebra directly from coefficient decay. In particular, the product estimate uses a finite sum of the two finite exceptional exponent sets; it does not infer restriction from boundedness of coefficients alone. Completeness is a uniform, not just coefficientwise, argument. Step 2 uses exactly the existence of the monic factor and the norm-controlled division stated in the new Weierstrass node. I checked the hypotheses by choosing a unique maximum weighted exponent among the norm-one coefficients. The remaining coefficients are uniformly smaller than one, so the leading coefficient is a unit; normalizing it produces coefficient 1 in degree d and strictly smaller coefficients above d. The changes of variables and their inverse both preserve the Gauss norm by density.

Steps 3–5 give the full induction, first on the number of variables for all finite module ranks, and then on rank. The initial case is finite row reduction. The ideal step invokes the new division node on an actual monic polynomial with the stated coefficient bounds; its remainders really lie in I because w lies in I. The lower-dimensional induction applies to the submodule in the explicit finite remainder space. The rank step uses fixed lifts of finitely many ideal generators, so the constants are independent of the vector being expressed. No assertion that arbitrary finitely generated ideals are already closed is used. Step 6 then uses only the definition node def-weil-pbc-noetherian-ring, which says exactly that all ideals are finitely generated, and the explicit convergent coefficient sums prove closedness. Step 7 proves quotient completeness with approximate lifts of successive differences and treats the zero quotient separately. This corrects the potential mistake of choosing norm-minimizing lifts in a non-discretely valued field.

Step 8 uses the division node's uniqueness to prove freeness of the quotient by w. The existing leaf lem-weil-pbc-finite-algebra-is-integral explicitly applies to a commutative finite algebra, precisely L over the image D of the smaller Tate algebra. The inverse-integrality calculation proves that D is a field before the induction on its maximal ideal is invoked. I checked that the nonzero element of N exists because a positive-dimensional Tate algebra is not a field, and that d cannot be zero inside a proper maximal ideal. Thus no analytic Nullstellensatz or maximal-contraction theorem is silently quoted.

Step 9 uses the bounded-powers node with the finite-dimensional field just proved and its quotient vector-space norm, exactly the kind of nonmultiplicative norm the node permits. Each coordinate has all powers of quotient norm at most one. Step 10 invokes the division node only for powers of monic one-variable polynomials whose coefficients stay bounded by one. Their remainders commute first on polynomials, and then on the restricted algebra by density and continuity. The kernel calculation is written out, rather than identifying the polynomial and analytic infinitesimal neighborhoods by assertion. Step 11 is ordinary ideal correspondence for that specific finite-box quotient isomorphism; it proves maximal contraction and N = p_0 D_n before using their powers, and explicitly includes J in all subsequent quotients.

Step 12 cites def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime, whose statement defines the fraction ring and its maximal ideal, and def-weil-pbc-adic-completion, whose statement defines compatible sequences in the quotients. The invertibility formula modulo a power of a maximal ideal is written out. The finite-level maps all arise from the given B -> C map, so compatibility and the resulting completed map are the required canonical ones, not merely abstract isomorphisms.
