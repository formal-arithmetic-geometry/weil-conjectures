## Review by Q67

I checked the statement against the sources above with the node files open. For the new node the ambient ring is commutative and quasi-excellent, the multiplicative subset is introduced explicitly, and the ring operations on its localization are specified. The conclusion of Tag 07QU really includes arbitrary localizations; it does not restrict to a principal or finite multiplicative set. The source's omitted localization proof is left to this new unsplit node, rather than being labelled elementary inside the assigned item.

For Step 1, I read lem-weil-pbc-affine-scheme-reduced-iff, lem-per-reduced-noetherian-blowup-geometry, and lem-sfb-finite-type-composition-base-change. The first supplies reducedness of the initial spectrum; the second explicitly gives reducedness, Noetherianness and finite type for a coherent ordinary blowup without excellence or integrality; the third explicitly permits finite-type composition. The induction has its base and successor identified and does not assume the conclusion for the next stage.

For Step 2, I read the whole statement of lem-per-noetherian-normalized-blowup-finality. Although its title mentions normalization, its ordinary-blowup clause already supplies the required bijection on generic points and isomorphic residue fields. I use no normalization and no finiteness of normalization. Reducedness/local Noetherianness come from Step 1; the center is coherent and nowhere dense by hypothesis, so its finite-type ideal meets that lemma's hypotheses. I read lem-sfb-generic-residue-transcendence-dimension-bound: its statement accepts reducible schemes, needs only locally finite type and a locally Noetherian target, and gives dim(source) <= d+n from the generic residue transcendence bound. Here the two numeric arguments are d=dim A and n=0; the isomorphisms of residue fields along the tower give precisely that zero bound. The dimension-of-spectrum equality is actually stated in def-weil-pbc-krull-dimension-ring. I avoided the otherwise tempting equality theorem with a universally catenary base.

For Step 3, the cited definition gives closed irreducible chains, and lem-sc-irreducible-components-cover says every such top member lies in a component. Since a nowhere dense closed subset cannot contain a component, appending that component is strict. This is a short argument from these statements, so it stays an elementary step rather than a new dimension-drop node. The last citation, lem-pc-scheme-dimension-local-rings, explicitly identifies scheme dimension with the supremum of local-ring dimensions. I use that statement, not a claim about topological neighborhood dimension at a point, which is a different invariant.

For Step 4, I checked the affine Noetherian lemma and the Noetherian-localization lemma at their actual statements. The local ring is a prime localization of an affine coordinate ring; reducedness is part of the definition of a reduced scheme. The spectrum-of-stalk morphism has exactly the flatness statement needed, and lem-weil-pbc-flat-morphism-base-change explicitly makes each stage projection flat, with no finite-type assumption on the localization morphism.

For Step 5, I checked the hypotheses of the two schematic-image nodes. The complement immersion is quasi-compact because its target is locally Noetherian, and is quasi-separated because the diagonal of an open immersion is an isomorphism. The ambient stage is reduced. These give its full schematic image, and flat pullback gives full schematic image of the pulled-back open. Full schematic image forces density: a nonempty open disjoint from the image would have zero restriction of the schematic-image sheaf map, contrary to its injectivity on the nonzero local rings there. Thus this is density of the actual pulled-back center complement. The construction node defines the comparison for a flat map and extended ideal, while thm-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change says exactly that this comparison is an isomorphism. I cite both, rather than using a definition as if it proved the theorem. Finite lists of generators pull back to generators, and pqf-lem-coherent-ft-locnoeth actually states quasi-coherent finite type iff coherent. Its locally Noetherian hypothesis holds inductively from the initial Noetherian local ring and the blowup geometry theorem. This proves that the base-changed tower is a tower of the kind already handled, including unit ideals when a center disappears.

For Step 6, the new node supplies quasi-excellence of the local ring. I read both affine-cover locality statements: the G-ring lemma and the J-2 lemma give their predicates on every affine open from a cover. The J-2 lemma needs local Noetherianness, provided by Step 4. Their conjunction, using def-exc-excellent-ring, satisfies the explicit every-affine-open hypothesis of the existing scheme finite-type stability lemma. This avoids silently assuming a ring property is automatically an all-affines scheme property.

For Step 7, I read lem-per-quasi-excellent-finite-type-stability again: every stage has its locally finite-type hypothesis by Step 1. For a closed subscheme the affine quotient description is really in def-weil-closed-immersion; a quotient is generated as an algebra by the empty list, so it is locally finite type. Thus the same stability theorem applies. The Noetherian assertions follow from the finite affine quotient covers: ascending ideal chains in a quotient lift to chains in its Noetherian ambient ring. Reducedness of the chosen closed subschemes is a hypothesis, not a consequence of arbitrary closed immersion. Composing the given structure maps to Spec Q gives the final over-Q assertion.

I tested the tower of length zero, empty centers (unit ideals), empty W, dimension zero, and dimension one (a DVR, where nonzero ideals are principal and the closed point has dimension zero). I also checked a reducible local crossing, k[x,y]_(x,y)/(xy): the component argument uses neither equidimensionality nor a global function field. A zero ideal on a nonempty reduced scheme is not a nowhere dense center and is never admitted by that hypothesis. For the new localization lemma, T={1} returns the original ring, T containing zero gives the zero ring (Noetherian, vacuously G and J-2), and localization of a field is a field or zero. Infinite multiplicative sets are intentionally included. The dimension proof uses only an inequality, so the noncatenary case needs no repair.
