### lem-sfb-locally-prescribed-modules-small

### Original step 1

Work in the fixed universe and small-site skeleton convention. Put $\mathcal M=\Mod_{\Lambda_X}$ and $\mathcal T=D(\mathcal M)$. The sheaf-tensor structure theorem supplies abelian sheafification and its strong symmetric monoidal structure on this small site, so the hypotheses of the internal/sectionwise equivalence hold. That equivalence identifies $\mathcal M$ with sheaves of modules over the corresponding ring sheaf, preserving underlying abelian sheaves and restrictions. The small ringed-site theorem makes this category Grothendieck abelian. For two module sheaves, their morphisms are a subset of the product, over the small site, of the sets of sectionwise module homomorphisms, hence form a small set. The Grothendieck category therefore has K-injective resolutions, and the locally-small-derived-category theorem gives small ambient Hom sets, in the same universe as the site and coefficient modules. In particular all Hom sets used in the counting argument below are small; this does not assume that the collection of all objects of $\mathcal M$ is small.

Cited nodes: def-weil-size-convention, det-def-sfb-sheaf-tensor-structure, def-weil-pbc-internal-abelian-sheaf-module-equivalence, lem-weil-pbc-ring-sheaves-grothendieck, lem-sfb-grothendieck-k-injective, lem-pc-derived-category-locally-small.

### Original step 2

First treat a finite discrete admissible ring $A$. The finite-discrete condensed/constant-ring isomorphism and its scheme pullback identify the pulled-back coefficient ring with the sheafified constant ring. The internal-module equivalence in abelian groups supplies the scalar encoding $G$; use the common encoding $E_T$ on each scheme $T$. The restriction comparison identifies $j^*(E_X^{-1}M)$ with the encoded restriction of $M$ for every stratum immersion $j$. Thus an internal constructible module gives an intrinsically constructible ordinary $A$-module sheaf. Conversely, a local constant finite $A$-module $P$ is finitely presented (a surjection from a finite free module has finite, hence finitely generated, kernel), and its internal image is the scalar local model $A_T\otimes_A P$: write $P$ as a cokernel of a matrix $A^m\to A^n$; the encoding sends the constant regular module to the regular internal module and preserves finite sums and cokernels, so the encoded presentation is the same scalar matrix on $A_T^m\to A_T^n$. Applying this on the same finite stratification proves the converse. Consequently $E_X$ identifies the two constructible hearts. As an equivalence of abelian categories it preserves kernels and cokernels by their universal properties; applying it termwise therefore gives a derived equivalence preserving cohomology and cone triangles. This coefficient identification is used only for finite discrete $A$.

### Original step 3

### Original step 5

Fix integral coefficients and one stratum $T$. Its pro-étale site has a small skeleton in the fixed universe. For the weakly étale objects of a covering, the localization theorem identifies their pro-étale sites with the corresponding slice sites; cartesian coefficients identify the restrictions of the coefficient sheaves. Finitely presented scalar $\mathcal O_E$-modules have a set of representatives: take cokernels of all finite matrices over the set $\mathcal O_E$. Cover families can be chosen from a set by removing repeated arrows in the small site, and assignments of these scalar presentations form a set. Between two fixed model sheaves on an overlap, morphisms form a set: they are natural linear families in the product of sectionwise function sets over the small slice site. Thus overlap isomorphisms satisfying the cocycle condition form a set. These data determine a realization up to isomorphism: local identifications of two realizations agree on overlaps, so the equalizer sheaf axiom glues a unique morphism on sections; glue the inverses too. The glued maps are natural and linear because these properties hold locally and the sheaf axiom detects equality. Retain only data having a realization and choose one realization for each. This gives a set of local-model sheaves on every fixed stratum, without asserting effectivity for arbitrary descent data.

### Original step 6

For a finite stratification $(a_s:T_s\to X)_s$ and a tuple of local-model sheaves $F_s$ from step 5, form the finite product $P=\prod_s a_{s*}F_s$. The module pushforward/pullback adjunction and cartesian coefficient statement give $a_s^*\dashv a_{s*}$, with $a_s^*$ exact and its underlying abelian-sheaf functor equal to $a_s^{-1}$ up to the stated canonical natural isomorphism. For a constructible module $F$ with chosen $\varphi_s:a_s^*F\simeq F_s$, define $u:F\to P$ by $u_s=a_{s*}(\varphi_s)\circ\eta_{s,F}$. Let $N=\ker u$. Exactness identifies $a_s^*N$ with $\ker(a_s^*u)$. ### Original step 7

The finite stratifications of the fixed bounded scheme $X$ form a set: choose finite lists of locally closed subsets and bounded scheme structures and immersion maps with those images. ### Original step 8

For finite $A$, apply the inverse derived equivalence of step 2 to a triangle of internal modules. It remains distinguished and its cohomology is the ordinary encoding of the internal cohomology. The bounded-constructible pro-étale theorem puts the third vertex in the ordinary bounded-constructible subcategory whenever the other two are there. For the other coefficients, apply the weak-Serre cohomology theorem to the zero-containing heart in step 4: closure under kernels, cokernels and extensions is precisely its hypothesis. Concretely, shifts reindex cohomology, and for a triangle $K\to L\to M\to K[1]$ the long exact cohomology sequence gives a short exact sequence with middle term $H^n(L)$, left term $\operatorname{coker}(H^{n-1}(M)\to H^n(K))$ and right term $\ker(H^n(M)\to H^{n+1}(K))$. Thus constructibility and a finite cohomological range are preserved. In the finite case we will also need kernel closure in the heart: for $F,G$ in that heart, the cone of $F[0]\to G[0]$ is bounded constructible, and its cohomology in degrees $-1,0$ is respectively the kernel and cokernel of $F\to G$. ### Original step 9

Let $L$ be an ambient retract of $K\in\Dbc$: choose $i:L\to K$ and $r:K\to L$ with $ri=1_L$. Functoriality gives $H^n(r)H^n(i)=1$, so $H^n(L)$ is zero whenever $H^n(K)$ is zero. ### Original step 10

The well-definedness assertions of the Tor-amplitude node give closure of finite Tor amplitude under shifts, triangles and retracts for these same internal module categories. The interval bookkeeping is as follows. A shift $K[n]$ changes $[a,b]$ to $[a-n,b-n]$. If the first and third terms of a triangle have finite amplitude, enlarge their intervals to a common interval $[a,b]$; the middle term then has amplitude in $[a,b]$. In particular, if $K\to L\to C\to K[1]$ is a cone triangle with $K$ of amplitude $[a_K,b_K]$ and $L$ of amplitude $[a_L,b_L]$, rotate and use the bounds for $L$ and $K[1]$: $C$ has amplitude in $[\min(a_L,a_K-1),\max(b_L,b_K-1)]$. A retract has the same amplitude bound as the object of which it is a retract. Therefore $\Dctf=\Dbc\cap\{\text{finite Tor amplitude}\}$ is an intersection of thick subcategories, and is thick: each closure operation preserves both defining predicates. In particular this argument does not assert that standard truncation preserves finite Tor amplitude.

### Original step 11

Choose a set $S$ of representatives for the constructible heart, using steps 3, 4 and 7. For a complex $K$ with constructible cohomology supported in $[a,b]$, put $T_q=\tau_{\leq q}K$. ### Original step 12

Fix integers $a\leq b$ and construct a set of representatives for bounded constructible objects with cohomology in $[a,q]$, by induction on $q=a-1,\ldots,b$. Start at $q=a-1$ with the single zero object. Given a set $R_{q-1}$ covering all isomorphism classes at the previous stage, consider every pair $B\in R_{q-1}$, $C\in S$ and every morphism $f:C[-q-1]\to B$. These morphisms range over a set by step 1. Choose one cone for each $f$ and retain those cones with constructible cohomology in $[a,q]$; this is still a set. Every $K$ in this interval occurs up to isomorphism: step 11 and inverse rotation express it as a cone of $-\delta_q[-1]:H^q(K)[-q-1]\to T_{q-1}$. Choose isomorphisms $\beta:T_{q-1}\xrightarrow{\sim}B$ and $\gamma:H^q(K)\xrightarrow{\sim}C$. The counted arrow is explicitly $f=\beta\circ(-\delta_q[-1])\circ\gamma[-q-1]^{-1}$. Replacing the first two vertices by these isomorphic representatives gives a distinguished triangle starting with $f$ and having third vertex isomorphic to $K$. Cones of a fixed arrow are isomorphic: TR3 extends the identities on the first two vertices to a triangle morphism, and the two-isomorphisms theorem makes its third component invertible. Thus the chosen cone covers $K$. This proves the induction, including $q=a$, when the previous term is zero; it does not require a functorial cone.

### Original step 13
