For the normal-base extension node I read both assertions of Deligne 3.5.2, including the normality needed to extend the constant target; for the faithfulness node I read 3.5.1 and its proof for reducible and singular stable curves, not just its smooth special case.

Step 1 uses the existing unpointed stable coarse-moduli theorem exactly for the smooth Deligne--Mumford stack, and the stable Isom theorem exactly for finite diagonal. The new completed-groupoid statement supplies an effective pullback family and a completed presentation. The new deformation statement applies since the curve is connected, projective, nodal and stable with g>=2; its no-infinitesimal-automorphism and dimension conclusions supply 3g-3 parameters and the d node parameters. Etale unique lifting identifies the chart's completed local ring with the marked deformation ring. The discriminant assertion is part of the new deformation statement, so the equality defining U is not inferred merely from formal smoothness.

Step 2 uses the source and target isomorphisms and the identity/composition assertions of the completed-groupoid node. These give the H-action and quotient directly. The stable Isom node really asserts finiteness for two stable families over this base; a finite scheme is affine, so compatible maps from its coordinate algebra into A/m^q give a unique map into complete A. This also checks the action identities and its prescribed special fibre. It uses no tame linearization or division by |H|.

Step 3 uses the full statement of the existing smooth-curve abelian-level-rigidification node: its frame functor is representable by a finite etale surjection over an arbitrary base with ell invertible, not merely over a field. The definition of the stable moduli groupoid records the family isomorphisms and cohomology pullbacks giving functoriality. The representing property gives the tautological frame with identity on its source labels. No separate elementary tautological-frame or equivariance node is created.

Step 4 uses the power-series normal-domain statement repeatedly, beginning with the field Omega. The finite-etale generic-components node explicitly identifies a finite etale algebra on a normal integral Noetherian base with the integral closures in all its finite separable generic fields. The two integral-closure nodes state normality and finiteness respectively for the present normal Noetherian base; their hypotheses are met by A. I checked these separately rather than assuming that the definition of normalization gave finiteness. The product and localization calculations in the step are elementary monic-polynomial arguments and are written out. This proves normality, Noetherianity and density for every component of Spec B. Step 5 is the elementary semilinear transport of monic equations, with h inverse ensuring equality; it needs no additional node.

Step 6 uses exactly the two new relative Picard and frame-extension statements. The pullback is still a stable family; S is normal locally Noetherian by Step 4, V is dense and smooth, and ell is invertible. Picard base change gives the correct closed-fibre group and Kummer comparison identifies its smooth torsion with cohomology after choosing a primitive ell-th root in Omega. The tautological frame is equivariant with constant labels fixed. The extension theorem asserts both injection and uniqueness with the required naturality. Thus equivariance survives extension; it is not asserted without explanation.

Step 7 uses the relative Picard statement for the geometric-stalk identification and the separate faithfulness statement for the implication from trivial Picard ell-torsion action to an identity curve automorphism. Since the point is fixed as a geometric point, the constant target action on its stalk is the identity. Injectivity makes the special-curve torsion action trivial, and faithful scalar extension recovers h=1 over Omega. The faithfulness node explicitly includes singular and reducible stable curves and has no invertibility condition on the automorphism order. A smooth-curve-only cohomological faithfulness theorem would not suffice here.

For edge cases I checked d=0 (the empty product is 1 and U is the whole base), d=3g-3 (no complementary deformation parameters), disconnected E (all generic fields retained), reducible nodal curves with toric Picard part, and positive characteristic dividing |H|. In the nodal case torsion rank can drop, explaining the need for an injection rather than an asserted full boundary frame. Normality is essential to the extension lemma and is proved before it is used. Genus 1 is excluded by stability and finite automorphism issues; level 2 is excluded because the involution on a smooth hyperelliptic curve acts trivially on 2-torsion. These exclusions match the statement rather than being silently added later. The extension lemma allows n=1 and r=0, where its assertion reduces correctly to the zero-sheaf assertion; an actual open frame already enforces any required rank.
