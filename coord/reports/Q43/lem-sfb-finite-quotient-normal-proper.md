### Retained step 4

For each $i\in I$, let $e_i$ be the product idempotent equal to $1$ at $i$ and $0$ elsewhere. In a domain an idempotent $e$ satisfies $e(e-1)=0$, hence is $0$ or $1$. Restriction of $h$ therefore gives a ring isomorphism $h_i:B_i\to B_{hi}$. These satisfy $(gh)_i=g_{hi}h_i$ because they are restrictions of the original action.

### Retained step 5

Choose one representative $i$ of each orbit in the finite set $I$, and put $H_i=\{h:hi=i\}$ and $C_i=B_i^{H_i}$. Projection to the representative coordinates gives a ring isomorphism $B^H\cong\prod_{i\in I/H}C_i$. Indeed a fixed tuple must satisfy $x_{hi}=h_i(x_i)$, so the representative coordinates determine it and lie in $C_i$. Conversely, for $c_i\in C_i$, prescribe $x_{hi}=h_i(c_i)$ on that orbit. If $hi=h'i$, then $h^{-1}h'\in H_i$, so the prescription is independent of the choice of $h$. The identity $(gh)_i=g_{hi}h_i$ proves that this tuple is fixed by every $g$. These two constructions are inverse and preserve addition, multiplication and units coordinatewise. For $I=\varnothing$ they are the unique isomorphism of zero rings.

### Retained step 6

Each $C_i\subset B_i$ is an integrally closed domain. It is a domain as a unital subring of a domain. To prove integral closedness, take $y=c/d\in\operatorname{Frac}(C_i)$ with $d\ne0$, satisfying a monic equation over $C_i$. Embed this fraction field into $\operatorname{Frac}(B_i)$. The same equation makes $y$ integral over $B_i$, so $y\in B_i$ by its integral closedness. For $h\in H_i$, apply $h_i$ to $dy=c$ in $B_i$; since $c,d$ are fixed, $d h_i(y)=c=dy$. Cancellation of $d\ne0$ gives $h_i(y)=y$. Thus $y\in C_i$, as required. This uses neither faithfulness nor division by $|H_i|$.

### Retained step 7

Each $C_i$ is also a normal ring in the prime-localization sense. For a prime $\mathfrak p\subset C_i$, the localization $(C_i)_{\mathfrak p}$ is a domain inside $\operatorname{Frac}(C_i)$. If $y$ in that field satisfies $y^d+\sum_{r<d}a_r y^r=0$ with $a_r\in(C_i)_{\mathfrak p}$ and $d\ge1$, choose a common denominator $s\in C_i\setminus\mathfrak p$ so that every $sa_r\in C_i$. Then $sy$ satisfies the monic equation $T^d+\sum_{r<d}s^{d-r}a_rT^r=0$ over $C_i$. Step 6 gives $sy\in C_i$, hence $y\in(C_i)_{\mathfrak p}$. This proves integral closedness at every prime, exactly the definition of a normal ring.

Cited statements: def-weil-pbc-normal-ring.
