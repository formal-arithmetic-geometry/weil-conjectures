## Argument retained for the new node

This is report material, not a proof hidden in the statement-only node. In a basis, every tensor is finitely supported on B^p. An invariant tensor has constant coefficients on each permutation orbit; distinct finite orbit sums therefore form a basis, even when B is infinite. A nonconstant orbit has size p! divided by multiplicity factorials; none of its multiplicities equals p, so this size is divisible by p. For a linear map h, expand h on basis elements. The coefficient of a constant output tensor d^tensor-p in h^tensor-p(s_O) is |O| times the product of the corresponding matrix entries, and hence vanishes for a nonconstant O. For a singleton b^tensor-p it is h_db^p, exactly the matrix coefficient of h^(p)(b tensor 1), since (r d) tensor 1 = d tensor r^p. These coefficient computations prove naturality for the maps defined in any chosen input/output bases. Apply it to the identity map with different bases to prove basis independence. For tensor compatibility choose bases B,D. In the interleaved x tensor y, the coefficient of the constant pair (b tensor d)^tensor-p is the product of the constant coefficients of x and y. Projection therefore agrees with kappa(lambda(x) tensor lambda(y)) in the basis (b tensor d) tensor 1. The scalar-unit assertion is immediate on 1^tensor-p. This explains why the new node is a genuine reusable linear lemma, rather than one of the item's group-scheme clauses renamed.
