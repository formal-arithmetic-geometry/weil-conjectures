## Statement corrected

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the old sentence “Its distributional action is defined by transposition against tests” immediately after the unlocalized Op(a) can be read as a definition on all local distributions. That reading is false without proper support. For example, on a real coordinate line take the order-negative-infinity symbol a(x,xi)=exp(-xi²). The transpose of its Gaussian convolution sends a nonnegative nonzero compactly supported test function to a noncompact Gaussian tail. A general distribution acts only on compactly supported tests: the smooth distribution u(y)=exp(y⁴) cannot be paired with that tail by an integral. Therefore bare Op(a) has no such unrestricted transpose on D′.

I replaced that sentence by the explicit transpose rule for L_i=chi_i Re Op(a_i) psi_i, including both spatial cutoffs. The transpose has the input factor psi_i and is therefore compactly supported in U_i. This is the intended localized construction already present in the item's finite representation, and it agrees with the localization in the author's p.13 extract. I also made “usual distribution topology” precise as the strong dual topology, supplied by the new definition. No hypothesis on N, B, C, r or A was added, and the global operator class and its smoothing convention are retained.
