## Source error

The final paragraph of Stacks 0CDE says that for n>2 the blowup has a unique singular point. The calculation in its own Example 0CDC instead makes the middle chart for n=3 equal to R[r,s]/(rs-pi), which is regular; the two outer charts are also regular. For example take R=k[[pi]] and xy=pi^3. Its point blowup resolves it in one step. The proof here stops at both n=2 and n=3 and asserts a remaining singular point only for n>=4. This corrects a threshold in the source's proof, not its theorem or this item's statement. The example also has an evident missing quotient slash in the displayed prose description of its middle chart; the existing chart node states the quotient correctly.

## Review by NU

I read models.tex, Example 0CDC (example-blowup), Lemma 0CDD (lemma-etale-local-at-worst-nodal), and the statement and entire proof of 0CDE (lemma-blowup-at-worst-nodal). I compared every item clause, including the refinements beyond the printed 0CDE statement, with the explicit charts. The extra clauses do not require excellence, completeness, perfection, connected generic fibre or an extension of the original trait. Etale R' is used only to check a local construction; every actual blowup is performed on X over the original R.

For the new node I read the entire 0CDD proof, including its reduction from curves 0CBY, the quasi-local Dedekind argument producing the DVR open, and absorption of the unit in h. Its hypotheses are exactly flatness, local finite presentation and nodal pure one-dimensional geometric fibres; properness and connectedness are not inserted. The image of the chosen pi of R is a uniformizer in the local etale DVR R': its quotient by pi is a local unramified algebra over k, hence a field by 02G7. Thus the pi can be fixed before choosing the chart; absorbing the remaining unit in one coordinate gives the equation as stated. The smooth-generic clause is the zero-parameter exclusion at the start of 0CDE, independent of any existence of blowups. I tested the node on smooth points (n=0 is allowed), persistent nodes xy=0, xy=pi, nonsplit nodes requiring a separable etale extension, and imperfect residue fields. A persistent node is allowed without the smooth-generic hypothesis and is excluded only with it.

For step 1 I opened the statements of finite-type-over-Noetherian, the first-Fitting singular-locus lemma, unramified base change, and the field-fibre decomposition, and read curves 0C59 and morphisms 02G7. The first-Fitting lemma explicitly permits disconnected fibres, states closedness and unramifiedness, and identifies the base-changed nonsmooth locus. It therefore applies to this item; the connected-fibre projective-nodal-family-geometry node would not. Compactness plus a disjoint union of one-point open spectra is the written finite-subcover argument, not an unquoted finiteness theorem.

Step 2 uses the new local-equation statement and explicitly rules out the persistent model, whose nodal section would meet the generic fibre in a nonsmooth point. I opened flat-lfp-open and etale-source-smoothness: they give openness and pointwise equivalence, respectively, with no quasi-compactness assumption. Step 3 uses exactly the chart node's regularity criterion and its common-pointed-etale regularity/local-dimension clauses, including the smooth-relative-curve clause. It does not infer regularity merely from a definition of smoothness. In particular an n=1 fibre node is regular on the total space and is never a centre.

For step 4 I read the statements of qcqs permanence, unramified base change and field decomposition: taking an affine etale neighbourhood mapping into an affine neighbourhood of x makes both targets affine and hence quasi-separated, and the relevant fibres quasi-compact, and the decomposition makes them finite reduced sets. Both points being deleted are closed, so the required shrinking exists. I read divisors 0805 and opened both the comparison definition and thm-nagata-blowup-flat-base-change: the latter states that the defined comparison is an isomorphism, and the former specifies its target as the actual blowup fibre product. I cite both rather than treating a comparison definition as the base-change theorem. Etale-base-change supplies the horizontal maps after blowup.

Step 5 cites the actual chart formulas and lem-trf-nodal-family-etale-source-locality, whose statement includes flatness and local finite presentation as well as the fibre condition. It says both directions, with empty fibres permitted. Because pi is fixed from R, each chart over R' is the etale pullback of the same chart over R; this avoids silently invoking a postcomposition lemma absent from the citation list. The inverse-image open away from x and the pulled-back neighbourhood cover X+. Thus the descent assertion applies to a genuinely jointly surjective family. I also opened the complement-isomorphism statement used here. Step 6 uses only the chart node's exact assertion about the unique possibly nonregular middle origin, common etale neighbourhoods and the complement isomorphism. The rationality of the remaining origin makes its pullback above the isolated u one point, so no unexplained branching of singular descendants is assumed.

For step 7 I opened lem-sfb-projective-affine-base-blowup and checked its statement includes both b and f b in the finite-projective-space convention; crucially, it does not assume X integral. I read divisors 02NS and used the existing projectivity node, not the integral-only blowup node. I opened projective-implies-proper, proper and finite-type definitions, separated-quasi-separated, and the finite-presentation definition. Their exact statements supply properness, quasi-compactness and quasi-separatedness before the finite-presentation conclusion. The ideal is finite type because X is Noetherian, a hypothesis maintained at each step. Step 8 takes the exceptional-fibre assertion from the chart node, whose description is geometric and survives etale pullback. The residue extension k(u)/k(x) is finite separable by the field-fibre lemma already used in step 4, so choosing an embedding into an algebraic closure is legitimate. A split conic/projective line or a two-line tree is visibly nonempty and connected. No geometric connectedness of the original X_k is used.

Step 9 is elementary induction on two finite natural numbers after steps 3--8: inner exponent n drops by two, then the number of original nonregular points drops by one. I wrote the base cases and the inductive hypotheses; the entire theorem is not moved into the new node. Step 10 is an elementary induction using the complement isomorphism: the original smooth open stays isomorphic to its inverse image, so later nonregular centres cannot meet it.

For step 11 I read properties 033M (lemma-normal-Noetherian) and 04MU (lemma-dimension) and opened rem-pc-regular-normal and the scheme/local-ring dimension statement. The former explicitly gives a finite disjoint union of integral open-and-closed components for a Noetherian normal scheme. Regularity restricts to these opens. I checked the proper image argument separately for each component: flatness prevents it from being vertical, and universal closedness makes its generic-point image specialize to the closed base point. The explicit injectivity definition of a flat ring map is cited for multiplication by pi; I did not use the flat-morphism definition alone as a tensor-exactness theorem. A nonempty Noetherian special fibre contains a closed point, whose local dimension two is supplied by the arithmetic chart node. The supremum-of-local-dimensions formula gives dimension exactly two, not just an upper bound.

For step 12 I opened the principal-ideal-DVR statement and checked the noetherian-local-domain and nonzero-principal-maximal-ideal hypotheses. At a special-fibre generic point the reduced fibre has field local ring, so A/pi A is a field and the maximal ideal is exactly (pi). Regularity gives a Noetherian local ring and regular-normal gives a domain; flatness gives pi nonzero. This proves the DVR clause without using excellence or an unjustified completion argument.
