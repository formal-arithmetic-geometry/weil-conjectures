## Returned proof retained verbatim as source material

### Old Step 1

Let $z$ be a closed point of $X_{0,m}$ over $x\in X_0$, and put $d=[\kappa(x):k_0]$ and $f=[\kappa(z):\kappa(x)]=m/\gcd(d,m)$. Part (a) of [lem-zl-constant-extension-weil-heart-equivalence] identifies its local operator with $F_x^f$, up to conjugacy. Apply [p-linear-triangular] over the algebraically closed coefficient field $\Qlb$ to the finite-dimensional stalk of $\mathcal F$ at $x$. In an adapted basis $F_x$ is upper triangular with diagonal $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r$, so multiplication of triangular matrices gives diagonal $\alpha_1^f,\ldots,\alpha_r^f$ for $F_x^f$. The determinant of a triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries, so these are exactly its eigenvalues with algebraic multiplicity. By [def-wf-iota-real], reality of $\mathcal F$ says that the multiset $(\iota(\alpha_j))_j$ is conjugation stable. Its image under $t\mapsto t^f$ remains conjugation stable, since $\overline{t^f}=\bar t^f$, including multiplicities when different values have equal powers. Thus $\mathcal F^{(m)}$ is real. This calculation does not diagonalize $F_x$ and applies to Jordan blocks and zero stalks.

Citations: lem-zl-constant-extension-weil-heart-equivalence, p-linear-triangular, def-wf-iota-real

### Old Step 2

Suppose $\mathcal F^{(m)}$ has a finite filtration with punctually pure quotients of weights $u_i\le w$. Apply the inverse exact equivalence of part (b) of [lem-zl-constant-extension-weil-heart-equivalence] to this filtration. It identifies the final object with $N=g^*\mathcal F$ viewed over $k_0$ on $Y=X_{0,m}\times_{k_0}k$, carries every inclusion and quotient to the corresponding inclusion and quotient, and preserves the local operators and the absolute residue cardinalities. Consequently those quotients are pure of the same weights $u_i$, so $N$ is mixed of weights at most $w$ by [def-weil-punctual-weights]. In particular one must transport the filtration through the cyclic-sheet equivalence before taking direct image; one does not treat the one-sheet $k_m$-Weil object as a $k_0$-Weil object on the same geometric scheme.

Citations: lem-zl-constant-extension-weil-heart-equivalence, def-weil-punctual-weights

### Old Step 3

The projection $g$ is finite: over $\operatorname{Spec}A\subset X_0$ its inverse image is $\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_{k_0}k_m)$, whose algebra is generated as an $A$-module by the tensors $1\otimes b$ for a finite $k_0$-basis $b$ of $k_m$. This is the affine finite-module criterion of [def-weil-finite-integral-morphism]. Its geometric map $\bar g:Y\to X$ is finite by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence](a); both geometric schemes are separated and finite type. By [lem-zl-finite-adic-direct-image] the direct image $\bar g_*$ restricts to an exact functor of the $\Qlb$ constructible hearts, and the canonical lift of [lem-fix-weil-operations] makes $g_*$ a functor of constructible Weil sheaves; its value on $N=g^*\mathcal F$ is $P=g_*g^*\mathcal F$. Let $0=N_0\subset N_1\subset\cdots\subset N_s=N$ be the filtration of Step 2, with $N_r/N_{r-1}$ punctually pure of weight $u_r\le w$. By exactness and functoriality, $0=g_*N_0\subset g_*N_1\subset\cdots\subset g_*N_s=P$ is a finite filtration by Weil subsheaves with $g_*N_r/g_*N_{r-1}\simeq g_*(N_r/N_{r-1})$ as Weil sheaves. The finite morphism $g$ of separated finite-type $k_0$-schemes satisfies the hypotheses of [lem-zl-finite-pushforward-punctual], so each $g_*(N_r/N_{r-1})$ is punctually $\iota$-pure of weight $u_r$. By [def-weil-punctual-weights], $P$ is $\iota$-mixed of weights at most $w$. This is the finite case of the unit's quasi-finite lemma, applied to the actual direct image of the statement.

Citations: def-weil-finite-integral-morphism, lem-weil-pbc-finite-morphism-permanence, lem-zl-finite-adic-direct-image, lem-fix-weil-operations, lem-zl-finite-pushforward-punctual, def-weil-punctual-weights

### Old Step 4

We now construct the trace on the underlying heart. Part (b) of [lem-zl-constant-extension-weil-heart-equivalence] decomposes $Y$ into $m$ open-and-closed sheets. Use their identifications with $X$ given by the projection $\bar g$: on the sheet indexed by an embedding $\sigma:k_m\hookrightarrow k$, the coordinate map is $a\otimes b\mapsto a\sigma(b)$, so the restriction of $\bar g$ is the identity of $X$. The finite-clopen equivalences of [lem-pur-finite-clopen-derived-decomposition], for all the coefficient stages including $\Qlb$, therefore identify $L\bar g^*M$ with the tuple $(M)_\sigma$. Direct image is the finite product of the components: this is the section formula $(\bar g_*H)(V)=H(V\times_XY)$ of [def-weil-proetale-module-direct-image] at finite and integral coefficients, transported by the same clopen equivalences at field coefficients. Thus the underlying object of $P$ is canonically $\bigoplus_\sigma M$, the finite product being the biproduct in the abelian constructible heart. The prescribed inverse/direct image adjunction of [fix-prop-adjunctions](1) becomes the diagonal/product adjunction: a tuple $(u_\sigma:M\to H_\sigma)_\sigma$ corresponds to the map with components $u_\sigma$. Applying this to the identity on $(M)_\sigma$ identifies its unit $\eta_M$ with $v\mapsto(v)_\sigma$. Define $T:\bigoplus_\sigma M\to M$ by giving its restriction to every summand as $\operatorname{id}_M$, that is, by summation. This constructs a global heart morphism and its geometric-stalk map is summation. No descent from unspecified local morphisms is required.

Citations: lem-zl-constant-extension-weil-heart-equivalence, lem-pur-finite-clopen-derived-decomposition, def-weil-proetale-module-direct-image, def-weil-constructible-heart, fix-prop-adjunctions

### Old Step 5

Let $G_X$ be geometric $q$-Frobenius on $X$. Under step 4 the induced Weil map of $P$ is the componentwise Weil map $\theta:G_X^*M\to M$ followed by the permutation $\pi$ of the embeddings $\sigma:k_m\hookrightarrow k$ induced by geometric Frobenius. Indeed [lem-fix-weil-operations] constructs this map by Frobenius exchange followed by direct image of the pullback Weil map, and the naturality clause of [lem-zl-finite-adic-direct-image] says that a semilinear isomorphism of this finite diagram reindexes the sheets and applies their stalk transports. Here the sheet maps to the base are identities, so the latter are precisely the same maps induced by $\theta$ in every component. On the clopen product of step 4 this is an identity of component maps, not merely an inference from equality of stalk values. Summation satisfies
$$T\big((\theta(v_{\pi^{-1}\sigma}))_\sigma\big)=\sum_\sigma\theta(v_{\pi^{-1}\sigma})=\theta\big(\sum_\sigma v_\sigma\big).$$
The middle equality uses additivity of $\theta$ and bijectivity of $\pi$. Hence $T\circ\theta_P=\theta\circ G_X^*T$, the morphism condition of [def-weil-weil-sheaf-complex]. Thus $T$ defines $\operatorname{Tr}:P\to\mathcal F$ in the constructible Weil heart. The unit is also Weil equivariant by [lem-fix-weil-operations].

Citations: lem-fix-weil-operations, lem-zl-finite-adic-direct-image, def-weil-weil-sheaf-complex

### Old Step 6

There are exactly $m$ sheets, so the diagonal and sum of step 4 satisfy $T\eta_M=\sum_{\sigma=1}^m\operatorname{id}_M=m\operatorname{id}_M$. By the definition of morphisms of Weil sheaves in [def-weil-weil-sheaf-complex], equality of their underlying morphisms is equality of Weil morphisms. The coefficient field $\Qlb$ has characteristic zero and $m\ge1$, so $m$ is nonzero and invertible. Multiplication by $m^{-1}$ commutes with the $\Qlb$-linear Weil map. Thus $r=m^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}$ is a Weil morphism with $r\eta=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal F}$.

Citations: def-weil-weil-sheaf-complex

### Old Step 7

The maps $\eta$ and $r$ exhibit $\mathcal F$ as a direct summand of $P$ in the constructible Weil heart. Step 3 gives a finite mixed filtration on $P$ of weights at most $w$. Clause (3) of [lem-a1-mixed-direct-summand] therefore gives such a filtration on $\mathcal F$, with the same upper bound $w$. This concludes descent independently of the reality assumption, as required. All constructions took place in the constructible heart; none required a lisse sheaf, a connected geometric base or a semisimple local operator.

Citations: lem-a1-mixed-direct-summand
