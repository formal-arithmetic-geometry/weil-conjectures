## Review by NU

I checked the item against `more-algebra.tex`, 07P9 (lemma-intersection-regular-with-closed), read together with J-0/J-1 in 07P7 and the immediately preceding J-1 criterion 07P8. The proof first fixes a regular prime, chooses a minimal generating regular sequence there, shrinks so that it is regular and generates the given prime, then uses the hypothesis on that prime quotient and lifts regularity. The statement needs every prime quotient to have a nonempty regular open; it does not need A itself to have a regular point. I preserved that distinction.

For the first new algebra node I read `algebra.tex`, 061L (lemma-regular-sequence-in-neighbourhood), including the proof by finite kernels and shrinking. Its finitely generated module is R itself; Noetherianity is present and the regularity assumption is at precisely the fixed prime. Its conclusion gives the same ordered sequence on one principal neighbourhood, as used in step 3. This is not merely localization preserving regular sequences, which was the nearest Mathlib search result. For the second I read `algebra.tex`, 00NU (lemma-regular-mod-x): a regular sequence in the maximal ideal of a Noetherian local ring with regular quotient implies regularity of that ring. No Cohen-Macaulay or preexisting regularity hypothesis is being silently added. I also read 00NQ (regular-ring-CM), the source of the existing minimal-generators regular-sequence lemma. For the third I read the project theorem in full and `topology.tex`, 0541 (lemma-characterize-open-Noetherian), which is the topological source of the criterion. The exact project attachment removes any need to reprove that criterion in this split.

Step 1 uses the statements of `lem-weil-pbc-primes-of-localization`, `lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-localization`, and `lem-weil-pbc-localize-localization-at-prime`. I opened each node: the first supplies precisely the prime associated to a smaller prime, the second regularity of a prime localization of a regular local ring, and the third identifies the iterated localization with A_q. Inclusion has the correct direction q contained in p.

Step 2 uses the regular-local definition and the localization-at-prime definition to choose dim(A_p) maximal-ideal generators represented by fractions with numerators in p. Multiplying each generator by its own invertible denominator preserves the generated ideal. I opened `lem-weil-pbc-regular-local-generators-regular-sequence`: its hypothesis is exactly a generating list of length the dimension of a regular local ring, and its conclusion includes the ordered regular-sequence assertion used here. No arbitrary generating list is substituted for a minimal one.

Step 3 uses only the new 061L statement. A is Noetherian, the chosen ideal is prime, the f_i lie in A, and step 2 establishes the required regular sequence in A_p. Its element g is outside p, which is needed in every subsequent shrinking.

Step 4 uses the Noetherian definition for finite generators of p and the fraction-equality definition to turn generator equality after localization into finitely many equations s_j a_j in (f_i), with every s_j outside p. This explicitly proves generation after inverting h. I opened `lem-weil-pbc-localization-module-exact`: it preserves the injective multiplication maps on each successive quotient, exactly the maps needed in the regular-sequence definition. The proof writes the identification of localized quotients and separately checks the final quotient is nonzero because its ideal lies in the proper extension of p. Thus exactness alone is not incorrectly used to preserve the nonzero condition of a regular sequence.

Step 5 uses only the closed-set description in `lem-weil-pbc-spec-irreducible-closed-subsets-primes`, whose full statement I read: every closed set in an affine spectrum is V(J). The rest is a short calculation with prime ideals and the item hypothesis. If the complementary ideal were zero the purported nonempty open would be empty, so it contains a nonzero bbar; because the quotient is by a prime, its lift b is outside p. D(bbar) lies in the regular open. The product hb remains outside p; a prime q containing p and avoiding that product avoids both factors, so its quotient point is in the given regular open.

Step 6 uses the exact statement of `lem-weil-pbc-weakly-regular-sequence-localization-prime`: injectivity for a regular sequence in B together with all f_i in Q gives a regular sequence in B_Q. The explicit fractions prove B_Q is A_q; I did not take an iterated-localization node about a different ring as a black box here. The statement of `lem-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime-of-quotient` identifies the quotient by pA_q with (A/p) at q/p, and the statement of `lem-weil-pbc-localization-noetherian` makes A_q Noetherian. Finally the new 00NU node applies: A_q is local, the sequence lies in its maximal ideal, is regular, and its quotient is regular by step 5. These verifications establish the relative-neighbourhood hypothesis, not an unsupported claim about all primes of B.

Step 7 uses the new Nagata criterion with the predicate false on nonprime ideals. The generalization hypothesis is step 1, and the relative-neighbourhood hypothesis is steps 2-6 for each regular prime. Its conclusion gives a full principal neighbourhood through each regular prime. The existing closed-set statement makes these principal opens open; their union equals E. This is an elementary union argument, not an uncited global regular-locus theorem.

I rechecked the new statements on small cases. For the zero ring there are no primes, and the item's locus is the empty open. A field has its entire one-point spectrum regular. For dual numbers k[e]/(e^2), all prime quotients have regular opens but the regular locus of the original ring is empty, also open: the split never incorrectly concludes nonemptiness. For 061L the empty sequence allows g=1; with a prime localization the final ring is nonzero. For 00NU the empty sequence is tautological, while R=k[[t]], x=t has regular quotient k and satisfies the conclusion; the nilpotent e in dual numbers fails the nonzerodivisor assumption and is excluded. For the criterion, the constantly false predicate is vacuous, the constantly true predicate has h=1, and the values on nonprime ideals have no role. The r=0 case of the main proof still works: finite generation and clearing denominators make p zero on a neighbourhood, without assuming p was globally zero.
