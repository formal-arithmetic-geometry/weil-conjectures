### Original step 1

### Original step 2

### Original step 3

The map $R\to B$ is finitely presented and flat. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-syntomic-at-prime-relative-global-complete-intersection] at $\mathfrak q$, using the principal fibre neighbourhood from step 2. It gives a principal neighbourhood with $B_g=R[x_1,\ldots,x_N]/(f_1,\ldots,f_c)$ a relative global complete intersection. By [def-weil-pbc-relative-global-complete-intersection], its nonempty fibres have dimension $N-c$. These are open subsets of the given pure one-dimensional fibres, so $N-c=1$. By [lem-weil-pbc-relative-global-complete-intersection-local-regular-sequence], the relations $f_1,\ldots,f_c$ form a regular sequence in $P_{\mathfrak q'}$, where $P=R[x_1,\ldots,x_N]$ and $\mathfrak q'$ is the inverse image of $\mathfrak q$. ### Original step 6

Fix a geometric fibre $Y=X_{\bar s}$ over an algebraically closed field $\Omega$, and a reduced irreducible component $Z\subset Y$ with normalization $\nu:C\to Z$. Properness of $Y$ and of its closed component follows from [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence]. Thus [lem-trf-nodal-normalization-adjunction] applies: $C$ is a proper smooth connected curve, and $\nu^*\omega_{Y/\Omega}\simeq\Omega^1_{C/\Omega}\otimes\mathcal O_C(B)$, where $B$ consists of the ambient nodal branches on this component. Let $M$ be the points of $C$ above the marked points on $Z$. Normalization is an isomorphism near these smooth points by that same node. Consequently pulling their Cartier equations back gives each reduced point once. The points of $B$ and $M$ are distinct and $\Omega$-rational; a self-node contributes two points to $B$. Both divisors are Cartier by [lem-sc-point-cartier]. The divisor-ideal and tensor definitions then give $\nu^*L\simeq\Omega^1_{C/\Omega}\otimes\mathcal O_C(B+M)$.

Uses: lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence, lem-trf-nodal-normalization-adjunction, lem-sc-point-cartier, def-pc-divisor-line-bundle

### Original step 9

Write $g=\dim_\Omega H^1(C,\mathcal O_C)$. The Riemann--Roch formula of [lem-sc-riemann-roch], applied to a line bundle $F$ and to $\mathcal O_C$, shows $\chi(F)-\chi(\mathcal O_C)=\deg_{\mathrm{div}}F$. This identifies the Euler-characteristic degree convention of the item with the divisor degree in [lem-sc-canonical-degree]. That node gives $\deg\Omega^1_{C/\Omega}=2g-2$. The effective-divisor tensor formula and rational-point degree formula of [lem-pc-degree-base-change] now apply to the line in step 8 and the reduced effective divisor $B+M$. They give $\deg(\nu^*L)=2g-2+\#B+\#M=2g-2+r_C$. This also proves the formula for an empty marking, for a self-node, and for a smooth component with no branches.

### Original step 10

Assume all the numbers in step 9 are positive. Every geometric fibre is proper of dimension one and $L$ is invertible; thus [lem-trf-fibre-degree-ample-criterion] says that $L$ is ample on that geometric fibre, since its test is exactly the degree on each normalized reduced component. An ordinary residue-field fibre is of finite type by properness and [lem-weil-pbc-proper-fp-permanence]. The final ampleness assertion of [lem-pc-very-ample-descends], applied to an algebraic closure of its residue field, descends this ampleness to the ordinary fibre. Now [lem-trf-ample-neighbourhood] gives a neighbourhood of every point of $S$ on which $L$ is relatively ample. Refine these neighbourhoods by affine opens; the corresponding inverse images have ample restrictions. Since $f$ is quasi-compact, [lem-trf-scheme-relative-ample-affine-cover] implies ampleness above every affine open of $S$, as asserted.

### Original step 11

For reconstruction suppose simply that $L$ is relatively ample, regardless of how this was established. By [lem-trf-scheme-ample-canonical-proj-criterion], the canonical section algebra $\mathcal A=\bigoplus_{m\ge0}f_*L^{\otimes m}$ is quasi-coherent and evaluation defines an everywhere-defined open immersion $r:X\to P=\operatorname{Proj}_S\mathcal A$. Over an affine $V\subset S$ its target is $\operatorname{Proj}\Gamma_*(X_V,L|_{X_V})$ by [def-nagata-relative-proj] and the equivalent description $\mathcal A=f_*(\bigoplus_mL^{\otimes m})$ in the cited criterion. The operations are the tensor-product operations of [def-sc-invertible-section-ring]. The evaluation map on homogeneous localizations sends $t/s^a$ to the ratio of the corresponding sections. In particular its inverse image of $D_+(s)$ is $(X_V)_s$, by [lem-sc-ample-proj-local-chart].

### Original step 12

Here is the density argument on this affine base, with $T=\Gamma_*(X_V,L|_{X_V})$. If a nonempty standard open $D_+(s)$ missed the image, step 11 would give $(X_V)_s=\varnothing$. Apply [lem-sc-section-ring-localization-bijective] to the evaluation-and-division map $T_{(s)}\to\Gamma((X_V)_s,\mathcal O)$. Its required equation $\varphi(t/s^a)s^a=t$ is the evaluation formula from step 11, so the map is bijective. Its target is the zero ring; hence $T_{(s)}$ is the zero ring as well, and its spectrum $D_+(s)$ is empty, a contradiction. Since the positive homogeneous standard opens form a basis by [def-nagata-affine-proj], the image is dense. Quasi-compactness and quasi-separatedness of $X_V$ needed for the localization lemma follow from its properness. No finite generation of the entire section algebra is assumed.

### Original step 13

The affine-base target $\operatorname{Proj}(T)$ is separated by [lem-trf-proj-separated]. It is therefore separated over $V$ too: its relative diagonal is the restriction of its absolute closed diagonal to $P\times_VP$. Since $X_V/V$ is proper, [lem-weil-pbc-proper-over-separated] makes $r_V$ proper, hence its image closed by [def-weil-proper-morphism]. Step 12 made it dense, so it is surjective. As step 11 made it an open immersion, it identifies $X_V$ with the entire target open subscheme and is an isomorphism. These are restrictions of the single canonical map $r$, so their inverses agree on overlaps and give its inverse globally. This proves the relative Proj assertion and, by step 11 over $S=\operatorname{Spec}R$, the stated $R$-scheme section-ring assertion.

Uses: lem-trf-proj-separated, lem-weil-pbc-proper-over-separated, def-weil-proper-morphism

### Original step 14

For an isomorphism $h:X'\simeq X$ of marked $S$-families, the adjunction line comparisons and the fundamental class in steps 4--5 identify $h^*\omega_{X/S}$ with $\omega_{X'/S}$. The marked ideal is pulled back to the marked ideal, so $h^*\mathcal O_X(D)\simeq\mathcal O_{X'}(D')$. The functorial tensor and section comparisons of [lem-weil-pbc-invertible-pullback-tensor] give $h^*L\simeq L'$ and send a product of sections to the product of their images, giving a degree-preserving isomorphism of section algebras. By [prop-nagata-relative-proj-functoriality](2) it induces the corresponding Proj isomorphism. On each standard chart the evaluation formula is $t/s^a\mapsto t/s^a$ after these identifications, so the square with $r$ and $r'$ commutes on a cover and therefore globally. Identity and composition follow from pullback composition and the same formula. This proves compatibility with isomorphisms, without asserting arbitrary-base-change commutation of each $f_*L^{\otimes m}$.
