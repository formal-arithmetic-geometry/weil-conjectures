## Review by N6

I read Milne 16.39 on printed p.338 and the full proof of 16.40 on printed p.339 for the item and the commutator-decomposition node, and Corollary 13.8 with Theorem 13.7 on pp.256–257 for the fixed-subscheme node. I also read 1.68–1.71 (p.28), 5.59 (p.114), the quotient statements, and the existing DAG statements used below. The new nodes are actual auxiliary assertions from these sources, with all objects introduced and no proof hidden in their statements.

Step 1 states the simultaneous strong-induction claim and handles the commutative and trivial cases directly on every algebra. Step 2 uses smooth-connected-derived-subgroup for each iterated derived group, not for characteristicness, which is the explicitly written minimality argument. It then uses smooth-ambient-normality-geometric with the smooth ambient G and scheme equality under each rational conjugation, not merely equality of point sets. Lie–Kolchin applies to a solvable series with normal successors and commutative fppf quotients; the quotient-geometry statement supplies those quotients, and the commutator calculation proves their commutativity. The diagonal calculation puts N inside U_n on all algebras, which verifies the precise unipotence in the new node.

Step 3 uses affine-normal-quotient-geometry for the affine finite-type faithfully flat quotient, smoothness/connectedness, the closed quotient subgroups and their successive quotient identifications. Its statement really includes the correspondence theorem, not merely existence of G/N. Local lifts make each successive quotient commutative, so the defining minimality of the derived subgroup gives Q^(d−1)=1. This is the strict induction decrease. Semisimplicity of q(s) is immediate by composing each representation with q; no unsupported Jordan functoriality is invoked.

Step 4 uses precisely the new smooth-fixed-subscheme assertion for the three actions of G: on G and N by conjugation and on Q via q. All schemes are affine finite type and smooth by earlier steps; normality of N provides its action. The equalizer for the Q-action is C_Q(q(s)). The other new node supplies both connectedness of K and the rational-point decomposition, and N satisfies every hypothesis (in particular commutativity and unipotence).

Step 5 uses affine-group-homomorphism-image for rational lifting through q: it is faithfully flat and its target Q is smooth. It takes only the explicitly stated rational-point decomposition from the new unipotent node. The commutator convention is sus⁻¹u⁻¹ throughout; expanding it gives exactly z s⁻¹ z⁻¹ = u s⁻¹ u⁻¹ v. Commutativity of N and the defining equation of K give the required commutation with v.

Step 6 uses multiplicative-jordan-linear-compatibility for uniqueness in the faithful triangular representation already chosen. It is a theorem about invertible linear maps, exactly what is needed: both conjugates of s⁻¹ are diagonalizable, v is unitriangular, and the two right-hand factors commute. Their unipotent factor must therefore be the identity. Faithfulness then gives v=1 in G(k); cancellation proves u⁻¹z centralizes s and lifts the original point. This does not presume an unrecorded algebraic-group Jordan decomposition theorem.

Step 7 checks the scheme kernel with the defining formulas for all A, not just for k, so K is the actual normal kernel of f. The same affine-group-homomorphism-image node applies to C→D because D is smooth by step 4. Step 8 uses affine-normal-quotient-geometry for C/K and its represented quotient universal property. The faithfully-flat-group-quotient-universal statement applies to f and π: all groups are affine finite type and their kernels are the same closed K. Its uniqueness proves the two descended maps inverse. Finally the quotient-geometry statement explicitly says the inverse image of a smooth connected subgroup is smooth and connected when the kernel is smooth and connected; take the whole quotient R as that subgroup. This justifies the exact-sequence connectedness inference without an uncited theorem or an assumption that C was already connected.

Small cases checked: G=1 and any commutative smooth connected G are the base case; s=1 gives C=G, the decomposition x=[1,1]x in the new lemma, and the whole fixed scheme in the smoothness lemma. For N=1 the fixed subgroup and decomposition are trivial. For G=G_a⋊G_m, N=G_a and s acting with scalar λ, the map u↦[s,u] is multiplication by λ−1: if λ=1 the fixed group is all G_a, and otherwise it is trivial and the commutator map is surjective. A nontrivial prime-to-characteristic root of unity has the same calculation; no characteristic-zero or infinite-order assumption slipped in. The smoothness node permits X empty or disconnected (e.g. a trivial action on two points) and asserts no connectedness for it. Nonreduced fixed subschemes of unipotent elements do not challenge it because those elements are not semisimple.
