### Original step 1

Put $B(S)=C(S,R)$, the ring sheaf of [sk-def-sfb-condensed-ring], and $P_M(S)=C(S,M)$ with precomposition restrictions. Addition and negation of sections are continuous by the hypotheses on $M$, and the abelian group identities hold at each $s\in S$. For a finite jointly surjective family $q_i:S_i\to S$ and compatible sections $h_i$, let $D=\coprod_i S_i$ and $q:D\to S$. The map $H:D\to M$ given by the $h_i$ is continuous. Compatibility on $S_i\times_S S_j$ says exactly that $H$ is constant on every fibre of $q$, so there is a unique function $h:S\to M$ with $hq=H$. The finite disjoint union $D$ is compact: restrict any open cover to each of its finitely many compact components and combine their finite subcovers. Since $S$ is Hausdorff, [lem-sfb-compact-hausdorff-surjection-quotient] makes $q$ a quotient map. For an open $V\subset M$, $q^{-1}(h^{-1}V)=H^{-1}V$ is open, hence $h^{-1}V$ is open; thus $h$ is continuous. This proves existence and uniqueness of gluing on the finite covering families defining the coherent topology. On the empty profinite set there is one section, so the empty covering also satisfies the sheaf axiom. Therefore $P_M$ is an abelian sheaf. No separation hypothesis on $M$ was used.

### Original step 2

Use the objectwise tensor of [sk-def-sfb-sheaf-tensor]. For $a\in B(S)$ and $h\in P_M(S)$ set $(ah)(s)=a(s)h(s)$. This is continuous, being the composite of $s\mapsto(a(s),h(s))$ with the continuous scalar action. Pointwise distributivity gives $(a+a')h=ah+a'h$ and $a(h+h')=ah+ah'$. Consequently the assignment $a\otimes h\mapsto ah$ defines an abelian-group homomorphism $\beta_S:B(S)\otimes_{\mathbf Z}P_M(S)\to P_M(S)$: extend the formula additively on the generators and note that the two displayed identities respect the defining bilinearity relations of the tensor product. It commutes with restriction along $u:S'\to S$, because $(ah)\circ u=(a\circ u)(h\circ u)$. Hence $\beta:B\otimes_pP_M\to P_M$ is a presheaf morphism. With $\mu^p(a\otimes b)=ab$ and $e^p(n)=n\cdot1$, its presheaf module identities are $\beta(e^p(n)\otimes h)=nh$ and $\beta(ab\otimes h)=\beta(a\otimes\beta(b\otimes h))$. These follow respectively from $1h=h$ and $(a(s)b(s))h(s)=a(s)(b(s)h(s))$; additivity extends them from pure tensors to all tensors.

### Original step 3

Let $a$ denote abelian sheafification and $\epsilon_P:aP\xrightarrow{\sim}P$ its canonical isomorphism for a sheaf $P$. By [def-weil-pbc-sheafification], $\beta$ extends uniquely to $\gamma_M=\epsilon_{P_M}\circ a(\beta):a(B\otimes_pP_M)\to P_M$. This is the required action on $B\otimes P_M$ of [sk-def-sfb-sheaf-tensor], and its restriction is exactly $a\otimes h\mapsto(s\mapsto a(s)h(s))$. Apply the strong monoidal functor $a$ of that node to the two presheaf identities in step 2. Its structural isomorphisms identify $a(B\otimes_pP_M)$ with $aB\otimes aP_M$ and $a(\underline{\mathbf Z})$ with the sheaf unit, and its associativity and unit coherence identify the images of the presheaf associator and unitor with the sheaf ones. The isomorphism $\epsilon_B:aB\to B$ identifies the sheafified multiplication and unit with those of the given ring sheaf by [det-def-sfb-ring-object-sheaf]. Conjugating the resulting identities by $\epsilon_B$ and $\epsilon_{P_M}$ gives precisely $\gamma_M\circ(\eta\otimes P_M)=\lambda_{P_M}$ and $\gamma_M\circ(\mu\otimes P_M)=\gamma_M\circ(B\otimes\gamma_M)\circ\alpha_{B,B,P_M}$. Thus $(P_M,\gamma_M)$ is an internal $B$-module by [sk-def-sfb-module-objects]. This argument uses sheafification and its monoidal comparison, rather than any formula identifying sections of a sheaf tensor with tensors of sections.

### Original step 4

For a continuous $R$-linear $f:M\to N$, define $P_f(S)(h)=f\circ h$. It is continuous as a composite, additive, and natural in $S$ since $f\circ(h\circ u)=(f\circ h)\circ u$. On pure tensors $P_f(ah)(s)=f(a(s)h(s))=a(s)f(h(s))$, so $P_f\beta_M=\beta_N(\mathrm{id}_B\otimes_pP_f)$ on all tensors by their defining additive generators. Sheafifying this equality, or equivalently using the uniqueness in [def-weil-pbc-sheafification], yields $P_f\gamma_M=\gamma_N(\mathrm{id}_B\otimes P_f)$. This is exactly the morphism condition of [sk-def-sfb-module-objects]. Evaluation gives $P_{\mathrm{id}}(h)=h$, $P_{gf}(h)=g\circ(f\circ h)$, and $P_{f+g}(h)(s)=f(h(s))+g(h(s))$. The sum $f+g$ is continuous by continuity of addition in $N$. Sums of the resulting sheaf maps are internal-linear: add the two action-commutation equations, using additivity of tensor on morphisms (objectwise and then sheafified) and of composition of abelian-group maps. These identities establish the functor and its additivity.

### Original step 5

For a finite family put $M=\prod_iM_i$ with its product topology and coordinatewise operations. Those operations are continuous because each coordinate is the corresponding continuous operation on $M_i$. A map $h:S\to M$ is continuous exactly when all $\operatorname{pr}_ih$ are: preimages of the basic opens, which impose open conditions on finitely many coordinates, are finite intersections of open preimages. Thus $h\mapsto(\operatorname{pr}_ih)_i$ and $(h_i)_i\mapsto(s\mapsto(h_i(s))_i)$ are inverse additive bijections, commute with restriction, and respect the pointwise action of every $a:S\to R$. They therefore identify $P_M$ with the sectionwise product of the $P_{M_i}$ as abelian sheaves, with the action of step 3. This is also the internal-module product: for any internal module $K$ and module maps $v_i:K\to P_{M_i}$, the sectionwise tuple defines a unique natural additive map $v:K\to P_M$. Each projection of $v\gamma_K$ equals the same projection of $\gamma_M(\mathrm{id}_B\otimes v)$ by linearity of $v_i$ and of the projections; projections separate sections, so these two sheaf maps agree. Thus $v$ is a module map by [sk-def-sfb-module-objects]. This proves the product universal property and the stated isomorphism with its specified inverse. For $I=\varnothing$, the topological product is the zero module, $C(S,0)$ is a singleton for every $S$, and the same construction is the terminal internal module.

### Original step 6

Finally take $M=R$ with its regular action. Then $P_R=B$ as abelian sheaves, including the restrictions. The presheaf action $\beta_R$ is $a\otimes b\mapsto(s\mapsto a(s)b(s))$, the objectwise multiplication of the ring sheaf. By [det-def-sfb-ring-object-sheaf], the internal ring multiplication $\mu:B\otimes B\to B$ is its unique extension to the sheaf tensor. By the same uniqueness for $\gamma_R$ in step 3, $\gamma_R=\mu$. Hence $\mathscr C_R(R)$ is the regular internal module in the sense of [sk-def-sfb-module-objects].
