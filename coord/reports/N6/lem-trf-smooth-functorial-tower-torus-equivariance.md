An isomorphism is formally smooth by composing a lifting problem with its inverse, and locally of finite presentation because it is an affine ring isomorphism. Thus no additional smoothness theorem is hidden.

Step 2 uses exactly the calculus statement that normalized blowups are reduced of finite type and commute with smooth base change, with smooth induced level maps. Starting at X and p_0,a_0, this verifies each subsequent application inductively. The nowhere dense centers are provided by the item's hypotheses and preserved by the calculus. Surjectivity is only needed for the rule's explicit no-contraction hypothesis; I do not infer uncontracted towers for arbitrary smooth maps. The comparison E is over the same Y, and retaining ordered ideals supplies ideal equality in Step 6.

Step 3 is elementary: if a level automorphism is identity, the next is a relative automorphism of one normalized blowup. The calculus explicitly states that its only relative automorphism is identity. The same argument applies to a smooth pullback because the calculus explicitly identifies the pulled arrow with that normalized blowup. It does not assert that an arbitrary birational morphism is a monomorphism, and does not use generic points of a special fibre. Composing two isomorphisms over the same base map gives precisely the automorphism to which this argument applies.

Step 4 writes both directions of K_i and checks the required fibre-product equation using the original action on X. Hence F_i is an actual isomorphism before the lifted action laws have been proved. Its base change at the identity of T gives an automorphism of the original tower, so Step 3 proves the unit identity. This is the crucial correction to the tempting unsupported assertion that smooth functoriality itself applies to the unit section. It generally does not: for positive torus rank the section is not smooth. Normality of X_0 is not required, since only automorphisms of each normalized blowup are used.

Step 5 constructs U and V as base changes and compositions of the already invertible graph maps F_i, so their invertibility does not assume associativity. Their base maps agree by the assumed action law at level zero. The split-lattice projection statement applies with lattice Z^(2r), and the calculus identifies its pullback arrows, permitting the same rigidity argument. This proves equality on the product scheme itself, not merely equality on rational or geometric points. The multiplication map of T is only used for a base change of an isomorphism; no unsupported smoothness of multiplication is needed.
