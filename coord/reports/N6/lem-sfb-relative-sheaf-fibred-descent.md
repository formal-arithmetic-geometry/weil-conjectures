Images of locally equal arrows are equal by that clause; injectivity of q_T on each hom-set then gives equality upstairs. This proves uniqueness over every base scheme and requires neither fullness nor representability of q.

Step 2 uses the same clause to glue the q(a_i). I checked the orientation carefully: with b:q(x) to q(x'), the relative pair is (x,b^{-1}) over y=q(x'), and its local isomorphism to (x',id) is a_i because q(a_i)b^{-1}=id. Separatedness of the assumed P_y makes the two global classes equal. In a groupoid the isomorphism-class relation is exactly existence of an isomorphism, since arrows are invertible and compose; thus there is an actual a with q(a)=b. Faithfulness on each fibre gives its prescribed restrictions, and step 1 gives uniqueness. No prestack assumption on F was used.

Step 3 uses the definition's effective-descent clause with the assumed stack G. The overlap convention is d_ij:x_j to x_i. The chosen trivializations e_i:y to q(x_i) therefore satisfy q(d_ij)e_j=e_i. This equation says exactly that the pairs (x_i,e_i) give compatible classes in P_y. The morphism q preserves the cocycle equation because it is a functor of fibred groupoids.

Step 4 uses the assumed existence of gluing in P_y and the definition of its classes to choose (x,e) and c_i:x|_Ti to x_i with q(c_i)e=e_i. On the overlap, q(d_ij c_j)e=q(d_ij)e_j=e_i=q(c_i)e.
