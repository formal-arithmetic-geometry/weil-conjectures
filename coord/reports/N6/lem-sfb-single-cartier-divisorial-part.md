The support-power-annihilation statement applies because X is Noetherian and that quotient is coherent; the closed-immersion ideal statement identifies V(L) with D. It gives L^N contained in K, including N=0. This avoids silently assigning a finite valuation to the zero ideal. I opened these statements and their hypotheses match exactly.

Step 2 uses the affine-open Noetherian theorem on the Cartier chart, the Cartier ideal definition for injective multiplication by f, the closed-immersion affine ideal description, and the integral-scheme definition to make A/(f) a domain. The affine module-germ theorem applies both to the structure module and to the quasi-coherent ideal, so it gives the actual generic localizations and their compatible ideal maps. The regular principal-prime localization statement accepts these hypotheses without requiring A to be a domain. The localization definition identifies the maximal ideal with fR, and the DVR dimension statement gives dimension one. A different regular-Cartier node requiring ambient regularity was deliberately not cited.

Step 3 uses the DVR definition to choose a generator of the nonzero ideal: nonzeroness follows from the power bound in step 1 and the nonzero uniformizer in the domain R. Principal ideals also imply that R is Noetherian by its definition. Thus the hypotheses of the existing unit-times-uniformizer-power statement hold. The ideal power definition gives K_eta=L_eta^a. Replacing f by a unit multiple leaves precisely the same ideal power, so the one chosen integer works on every chart.

Step 4 uses only the already established generic equality and the cited affine localization and ideal descriptions. I wrote the denominator multiplier into the equality in A. The induction has base exponent zero; primeness forces f to divide h and regularity permits cancellation for the successor step. It never cancels in A merely on the assumption that A is a domain. This is the singleton elementary calculation from the middle of 0AGB, not a concealed additional theorem. Its quasi-coherent affine descriptions give the desired inclusion on the chart.

Step 5 uses the same closed-immersion ideal description outside D and the support assumption on O_X/K. The quotient stalk computation and the final subsheaf inclusion are the direct germ/sheaf arguments, now written out. Step 6 cites the power definition, cokernel-closure statement and finite-type-support-closed statement. Finite type of L^a/K is verified by the single local generator f^a, including a=0. Quotient stalks are justified by the germ description of a sheaf quotient. Thus T is the actual closed support, lies in D, and misses eta; no claim about an arbitrary quasi-coherent module having closed support is made.

Step 7 uses scheme sobriety for a generic point y of each irreducible component of T, and exactly the statement of the stalk-dimension generalization-chain node. Because eta has dimension one, it has a strict generalization z. Since y belongs to D and is not eta, y, eta, z is a strict chain of length two. Thus the generic local dimension of each component is at least two. This supplies the source's brief last inference without imposing regularity, equidimensionality or catenarity on X.
