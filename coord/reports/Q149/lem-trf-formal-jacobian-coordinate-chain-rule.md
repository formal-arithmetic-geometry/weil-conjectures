## Retained original three-step proof

The repair preserves the original argument here for comparison; new-node statements name the intermediate assertions, and their future proofs carry the longer arguments.

Original Step 1: Write the given coordinate isomorphism as $\phi:F[[Y_1,\ldots,Y_n]]\xrightarrow{\sim}T$, with $\phi(Y_k)=y_k$, and let $\mathfrak m=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$. Since the $y_k$ generate $\mathfrak m$ [def-weil-pbc-regular-system-of-parameters], $\phi$ maps $(Y_1,\ldots,Y_n)^N$ onto $\mathfrak m^N$ for every $N$. By definition $\partial h/\partial y_k=\phi(\partial\phi^{-1}(h)/\partial Y_k)$. Termwise differentiation is additive and obeys the Leibniz rule, as follows coefficientwise from the finite Cauchy sums defining multiplication; hence $\partial/\partial x_i$ and (by transport through $\phi$) $\partial/\partial y_k$ are $F$-linear derivations of $T$, and each maps $\mathfrak m^N$ into $\mathfrak m^{N-1}$ for $N\ge1$. Fix $h\in T$ and $N\ge1$, and let $H$ be the truncation of $\phi^{-1}(h)$ to total degree $<N$, a polynomial in $Y_1,\ldots,Y_n$; then $h-H(y_1,\ldots,y_n)\in\mathfrak m^N$. For the polynomial $H$, the Leibniz rule for the derivation $\partial/\partial x_i$, applied monomial by monomial, gives $\partial H(y)/\partial x_i=\sum_k(\partial H/\partial Y_k)(y)\,\partial y_k/\partial x_i$, and $(\partial H/\partial Y_k)(y)=\partial H(y)/\partial y_k$ by the definition of $\partial/\partial y_k$. Since both derivatives of $h-H(y)$ lie in $\mathfrak m^{N-1}$, $\partial h/\partial x_i\equiv\sum_k(\partial h/\partial y_k)(\partial y_k/\partial x_i)\pmod{\mathfrak m^{N-1}}$. This holds for every $N$, and $\bigcap_N\mathfrak m^N=0$ (a series in $\mathfrak m^N$ has no coefficient of total degree $<N$), so the equality holds in $T$. Exchanging the roles of the two coordinate systems (truncate $h$ itself as a polynomial $G(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ and apply the derivation $\partial/\partial y_k$) gives the symmetric formula $\partial h/\partial y_k=\sum_i(\partial h/\partial x_i)(\partial x_i/\partial y_k)$. For $n=0$ both sums are empty, $T=F$, all derivatives vanish, and the assertion is trivial.

Original Step 2: The classes of $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ form the coefficient basis of $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$: a series modulo $\mathfrak m^2$ is its constant term plus its linear coefficients. The $F$-algebra isomorphism $\phi$ and its inverse preserve $\mathfrak m$ and induce inverse $F$-linear maps on this quotient. Since $y_k\in\mathfrak m$ (the $y_k$ generate $\mathfrak m$) has no constant term, $y_k\equiv\sum_i q_{ki}x_i\pmod{\mathfrak m^2}$, where $q_{ki}$ is the constant term of $\partial y_k/\partial x_i$, the matrix of the induced map is exactly $Q(0)$. Its inverse map has matrix $P(0)$, so $Q(0)$ is invertible over $F$. When $n=0$, the quotient and both matrices are the unique zero-dimensional space and empty matrix, whose identity is the empty matrix.

Original Step 3: Use the two chain-rule formulas on the coordinate elements themselves. With $h=x_j$ in the first formula, $\delta_{ji}=\sum_k(\partial x_j/\partial y_k)(\partial y_k/\partial x_i)$, which is the $(j,i)$ entry of $PQ$; hence $PQ=1$. With $h=y_j$ in the symmetric formula, $\delta_{jk}=\sum_i(\partial y_j/\partial x_i)(\partial x_i/\partial y_k)$, which is the $(j,k)$ entry of $QP$; hence $QP=1$. Therefore $P$ is a two-sided inverse of $Q$ in the matrix ring over $T$. The same computation is the empty-matrix identity when $n=0$.
