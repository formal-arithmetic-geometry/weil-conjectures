## Proof handoff: lem-trf-closed-point-twisted-ideal-quotient

Its construction of the ideals invokes Schemes Tag 01J3, already available as lem-weil-pbc-reduced-induced-closed-subscheme. Here are the old steps 2-4 verbatim; the step selecting n is made by the parent after this lemma has constructed data for every n. These are future proof obligations, not a proof placed in the unsplit node.

Original step 2:

Fix a point x whose singleton is closed in X. Apply the common-free-generator lemma with M=N=L and W=X to choose an affine open U containing x and a generator e of L on U. Put Z=X minus U and Z' = Z union {x}. The reduced-induced-closed-subscheme lemma gives quasi-coherent ideal sheaves I=I_Z and J=I_{Z'}. Its explicit vanishing-ideal formula gives J contained in I, I restricted to U equal to O_U, and I restricted to X minus {x} equal to J restricted there. At x the inclusion J_x into I_x=O_{X,x} has image m_x, because vanishing at x means having zero image in its residue field. Both Z and Z' are closed; in particular J is among the ideal sheaves quantified in the hypothesis.

Original step 3:

In the abelian category of module sheaves put Q=I/J, the sheafification of the sectionwise quotient. The inclusion and its cokernel give 0 -> J -> I -> Q -> 0. For each n>=1 put M_n=L^{tensor n}. Tensoring this sequence by M_n gives 0 -> J tensor M_n -> I tensor M_n -> Q tensor M_n -> 0, and I tensor M_n -> M_n is injective. Here is the local verification, so no flatness assertion is implicit: on any open V where M_n has generator a, each map F tensor M_n -> F is f tensor (r a) |-> r f, with inverse f |-> f tensor a. Under these identifications both the sequence and the inclusion into M_n are the original exact sequence and I -> O_X. These identifications commute with every map in the sequence. Exactness follows locally on this covering: kernels are sectionwise, and cokernels are sheafified sectionwise cokernels. Such a cover exists because powers of an invertible module are invertible.

Original step 4:

Choose n>=1 with H^1(X,J tensor M_n)=0, using the hypothesis for the quasi-coherent ideal J, and write M=M_n. Construct q_n in Gamma(X,Q tensor M) explicitly on the open cover {U, X minus {x}}. On U use the image of e^{tensor n} under M|_U=(I tensor M)|_U -> (Q tensor M)|_U, using I|_U=O_U. On X minus {x} use zero: there I=J, hence Q=0 and Q tensor M=0. The two sections agree on U minus {x}, since this quotient sheaf is zero there, and therefore glue by the sheaf axiom. At x their germ is the class of e^{tensor n} modulo m_x M_x. In its generator coordinate this class is 1 in kappa(x), which is nonzero.

The future worker must preserve these specific outputs: both quasi-coherent reduced ideals and their restriction/stalk descriptions; the canonical short exact sequences for every n; injectivity into the power; the quotient section with the two prescribed restrictions; and its precise residue-one germ. The tensor argument is local in a free generator and checks every map, rather than asserting flatness without proof. The gluing cover really consists of opens because x is closed. The statement does not assume a lift exists.

## Proof handoff: lem-trf-supported-section-h1-affine-lift

Source read: the same 0B5P proof, lines 469-483, from the chosen H1 vanishing to the section with x in X_s contained in U and the equality X_s=D(f). The statement has been made independent of the preceding construction: it takes M, I, J, their exact tensor sequence and a quotient section with nonzero residue as hypotheses. Their proofs apply to these explicit hypotheses with M a general invertible module, and a nonzero quotient residue suffices in place of residue exactly one.

Original step 5:

The category of O_X-module sheaves is abelian with enough injectives. Global sections is additive and left exact: an element lies in the sectionwise kernel exactly when its image is zero. Apply the right-derived universal delta-functor lemma to Gamma(X,-) and the short exact sequence of step 3 at the selected n. Its R^0 Gamma = Gamma identification and exactness give Gamma(X,I tensor M) -> Gamma(X,Q tensor M) -> H^1(X,J tensor M). The last group is zero by the chosen n, so the boundary of q_n is zero; exactness gives t in Gamma(X,I tensor M) mapping to q_n. Denote by s its image in Gamma(X,M) under the inclusion I tensor M -> M. This uses only H^1 vanishing for J at this one n, not eventual vanishing or vanishing for I.

Original step 6:

The value of s at x is the nonzero class of e^{tensor n}, so x belongs to X_s. At any z in Z, trivialize M on a neighbourhood with generator a. Since s comes from I tensor M, its coordinate in this generator is a section of I, and the vanishing-ideal formula makes its residue at z zero. Thus s has zero value at every z in Z and X_s is contained in U. On U write s=f e^{tensor n}, with f in O_X(U). The definition of X_s says that for y in U, y belongs to X_s exactly when f has nonzero residue at y, equivalently its germ is a unit in the local ring O_{X,y}. Consequently X_s=U_f. The affine-principal-open lemma (0) says that U_f is affine. This completes the construction for every closed point x.
