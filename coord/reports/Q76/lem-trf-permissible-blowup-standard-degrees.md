## Review by Q76

I checked the statement of the item against Hironaka's Theorem III on p.156, the conventions on pp.154--155, and its proof on p.187. The data are a closed embedding in a regular ambient local scheme, the regular parameter centre, and normal flatness of the quotient along that centre. The stated reducedness and avoidance of minimal primes are retained, as is the assumption that the specified ambient chart local ring is regular. The theorem gives both directions, but this item asks only for numerical nearness implying equality of the sequences. I have not replaced nearness with an assumption on the sequence itself.

For the linear-section node I read Propositions 5 and 6 and Lemma 7. The first is the length-filtration inequality for one element; equality gives an injective degree-one initial multiplication and the associated-graded quotient description. Lemma 7 proves that a minimal initial basis remains minimal in the regular ambient quotient. The iterated version is precisely the first paragraph of Remark (12.2). Independent parameters ensure that every intermediate ambient quotient is regular. Equality of the two endpoints forces equality in the intermediate inequalities, so the node does not assume regularity of the parameters on the singular quotient before equality is known. Its module isomorphism is the initial-ideal quotient by the parameter multiples; it is not an assertion that the initial ideal contains those parameters. For q=0 it is the identity. For b=0 the binomial Hilbert functions give equality and both sequences contain only infinities. For V=k[[u]], b=(u), q=1, the inequality is strict in positive degrees, so the equality clause cannot wrongly preserve the disappearing linear generator.

For the projective-cone node I read Lemma 8 and the residue-extension argument (9.1)--(9.6), then Propositions 10--11, Lemma 12 and the second part of Remark (12.2). The rational-point calculation alone would be insufficient: the item allows nonclosed points and inseparable residue extensions. The source explicitly treats transcendental, separable and purely inseparable simple extensions, with Lemma 12 handling the equality case of the last kind. I kept arbitrary k and the prime p and used its residue-field transcendence degree delta, rather than assuming p maximal or k perfect. For C=0 the bound is an equality: the local projective space has dimension r-delta and summing delta+1 times gives the cone function. For r=0 the only eligible prime is that of k and existence of the point forces C=0; the comparison is 1 summed once against the function n+1. For C=(X_1^d), at the rational point t_1=0 the local tangent ideal has one generator of degree d, as required. An irrelevant-primary homogeneous ideal with no projective points satisfies no p hypothesis, so it gives no spurious assertion.
