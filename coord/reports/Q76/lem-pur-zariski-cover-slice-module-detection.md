### Hypotheses and clauses

The following correspondence applies to both theorems; `Etale`/`smallEtaleTopology`
and `ProEt`/`ProEt.topology` are the respective recorded sites.

- Scheme T: `(T : Scheme.{u})`. The separate declarations cover exactly the two site
  alternatives, without assuming the sites coincide or using the parent node's Boolean wrapper.
- Common size convention: site objects have universe `u+1` and arrows universe `u`;
  coefficients are `CommRingCat.{u+1}` and module carriers have universe `u+1`.
  These are the existing essentially small site presentations; no site morphism-universe
  equality is added. Arbitrarily large permitted schemes and coefficient rings are covered
  by universe polymorphism, raising the common universe if necessary.
- Set-indexed Zariski cover: `{I : Type v}`, `(U : I → T.Opens)`, and
  `(hU : ∀ x : T, ∃ i : I, x ∈ U i)`. No finite, nonempty or quasi-compactness binder.
- Original family covers T_tau: first conjunct, the family of `Etale.mk (U i).ι`
  or `ProEt.mk (U i).ι` mapping to the object representing `𝟙 T`.
- For each site object V the three next conjuncts give: covering in T_tau; covering
  in the small site of V.left; covering over `Over.mk (𝟙 V)` in `(topology T).over V`.
  The objects use the inverse-image opens `V.hom ⁻¹ᵁ U i`, their inclusions, and their
  composite with V.hom. These are canonical representatives of V ×_T U_i, not extra
  hypotheses or an arbitrary choice of maps. The exact existing Mathlib isomorphism
  `Scheme.pullbackRestrictIsoRestrict V.hom (U i)` identifies the chosen categorical
  pullback with this inverse-image open, and its `_hom_ι`/`_inv_fst` formulas identify
  projections. `Scheme.isPullback_morphismRestrict` states the universal square directly.
- Pro-etale covering here means `Presieve.ofArrows ... ∈ ProEt.precoverage ...` in
  the original, ambient-pullback and small-site-of-V clauses. This retains the actual
  fpqc / finite affine refinement condition, stronger data than merely a jointly
  surjective weakly-etale family. The localized site uses the recorded induced
  Grothendieck topology `ProEt.topology`, as required by def-pur-localized-ringed-site.
- Arbitrary commutative ring sheaf: `O : Sheaf topology CommRingCat.{u+1}`.
  The existing `sheafCompose` forgetting commutativity supplies its underlying RingCat
  sheaf to the dependency's `ringedSiteModules.{u+1}`. O is not required to be constant.
- Arbitrary module sheaf F: the binder `F : ringedSiteModules ...`; this is transparently
  Mathlib's `SheafOfModules`, including its semilinear restriction maps and linear morphisms.
- Sectionwise local restriction s_iF: `F.over (Etale.mk (U i).ι)` or its ProEt analog.
  The first coefficient-dependent conjunct explicitly asserts its section module at
  every `W : Over ...` equals `F.val.obj (op W.left)`, retaining the same module structure.
- Injective restriction F(V) → product_i F(V ×_T U_i): `Function.Injective` of the lambda
  sending x to `fun i ↦ F.val.map (...) .op x`. Its explicit codomain is the dependent
  product `∀ i : I, F.val.obj (op (...))`; maps are exactly the projection restrictions.
- Zero detection: `(IsZero F ↔ ∀ i : I, IsZero (F.over ...))` with no change of module
  category. `IsZero` is categorical zero object, never `IsZero` on a bare carrier type.
- Sectionwise interpretation: `(IsZero F ↔ ∀ V, Subsingleton (F.val.obj (op V)))`.
  An additive module is the zero module exactly when its underlying type is subsingleton.
- Empty T: final implication `IsEmpty T → ...` gives the empty covering sieve on every
  ambient object, every object of the small site of V.left, and every localized object,
  followed by `IsZero F` for every O and F. Bottom sieve denotes the sieve generated by
  the empty family. The original detection equivalence already includes empty I.
- No quasi-compactness, finiteness, constant coefficients, or topos points occur.

### Edge cases

Checked before and during writing: empty scheme with empty I (hU is vacuous, the
restriction target is an empty product and the sheaf axiom makes the source singleton);
empty scheme with nonempty I; singleton identity cover; repeated or empty opens;
infinite covering of a non-quasi-compact scheme; zero coefficient ring; zero module
sheaf; varying nonconstant ring sheaf; arbitrary site object V; independent indexing
universe v. None needs a hypothesis omitted by the node. Inverse-image opens are defined
also for empty V and empty U_i. The empty-site assertions are not merely a claim about
an arbitrarily chosen family: they assert bottom sieve membership on every object.
