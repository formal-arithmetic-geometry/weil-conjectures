### Former step 1

Put $R=A_{\mathfrak p}^h$ and $H=B_{\mathfrak q}^h$. The local map $A_{\mathfrak p}\to B_{\mathfrak q}$ and the henselian-local structure on $H$ give, by the universal property in [def-weil-pbc-henselization-at-prime], the canonical local map $h:R\to H$ over $A\to B$; its residue-field map is the canonical map $\kappa(\mathfrak p)\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$. Set $C=R\otimes_A B$ and let $\epsilon:C\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ be induced by the residue map of $R$ and $B\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$; write $\mathfrak n=\ker\epsilon$ and $D=C_{\mathfrak n}$. The contraction of $\mathfrak n$ to $B$ is $\mathfrak q$, and its contraction to $R$ is the maximal ideal of $R$.

Cited nodes: def-weil-pbc-henselization-at-prime, def-weil-pbc-localization-at-prime.

### Former step 2

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-quasi-finite-four-rings] to the square with original map $A\to B$, base change $A\to R$, and $C=R\otimes_A B$. The tensor map $B\otimes_A R\to C$ is the symmetry isomorphism, so the surjectivity hypothesis holds; the contraction of $\mathfrak n$ to $B$ is $\mathfrak q$. Since $A\to B$ is quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$ (hence of finite type by [def-weil-pbc-ring-map-quasi-finite-at-prime]), the conclusion is that $R\to C$ is quasi-finite at $\mathfrak n$.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-quasi-finite-four-rings, def-weil-pbc-ring-map-quasi-finite-at-prime.

### Former step 3

Apply [lem-weil-pbc-henselian-finite-type-product-decomposition] to the finite-type map $R\to C$. It decomposes $C\cong A_1\times\cdots\times A_s\times C_0$, with each $A_i$ local and finite over $R$, while $C_0$ is not quasi-finite over $R$ at primes over the maximal ideal. The prime $\mathfrak n$ cannot lie on $C_0$ by Step 2, so it lies on some $A_i$. The corresponding prime of $A_i$ is maximal: its quotient is a finite-dimensional domain over the residue field of $R$, hence a field. Therefore $D=C_{\mathfrak n}\cong A_i$ is finite and local over $R$. By [lem-weil-pbc-finite-local-henselian], $D$ is henselian.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-henselian-finite-type-product-decomposition, lem-weil-pbc-quasi-finite-four-rings, lem-weil-pbc-finite-local-henselian.

### Former step 4

Both $D$ and $H$ are ind-etale over $B$. For $H$ this is [lem-trf-henselization-prime-ind-etale] applied to $(B,\mathfrak q)$. For $D$, [lem-trf-henselization-prime-ind-etale] gives $A\to R$ ind-etale; [lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability] gives ind-etaleness after base change to $C$ and after composition with the localization $C\to D$. Explicitly, $C_{\mathfrak n}=\varinjlim_{f\notin\mathfrak n}C_f$; the principal-localization clause of [lem-weil-pbc-etale-algebra-finite-presentation-localization] makes each $C\to C_f$ etale of finite presentation, so this is an ind-etale presentation as in [def-weil-pbc-ind-etale-algebra].

Cited nodes: lem-trf-henselization-prime-ind-etale, lem-weil-pbc-ind-etale-stability, lem-weil-pbc-etale-algebra-finite-presentation-localization, def-weil-pbc-ind-etale-algebra, def-weil-pbc-category-colimit.

### Former step 5

The map $C\to H$ given by $r\otimes b\mapsto h(r) b$ sends $\mathfrak n$ into the maximal ideal of $H$, hence factors uniquely through a local $B$-algebra map $D\to H$. Conversely, since $H$ is ind-etale over $B$, the henselian-local target $D$ and the identity residue map $\kappa(\mathfrak q)\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ give by [lem-weil-pbc-map-into-henselian-colimit] a unique local $B$-algebra map $H\to D$. The same lemma applied in the other direction gives uniqueness of $D\to H$; both composites induce the identity on the common residue field and are therefore identities by uniqueness. Thus $D\cong H$ compatibly with $R\to H$.

Cited nodes: lem-weil-pbc-map-into-henselian-colimit, def-weil-pbc-henselian-terminology.

### Former step 6
