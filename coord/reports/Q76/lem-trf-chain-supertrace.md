## Source error

STATEMENT-DISPUTED: the imported lemma, also in the main-20261015 lemma statement, asks for a basis of every finite projective module. For a field k, take A = k × k and M = k × 0. This is the summand (1,0)A of A, hence finitely generated projective, but it is not free: the two component dimensions are 1 and 0, whereas a free A-module has equal component dimensions. Its identity trace is the idempotent (1,0), so a single integer rank cannot give the asserted scalar formula in this generality. No freeness or connectedness assumption was added. The corrected statement uses finite dual frames and the ordinary projective trace, whose construction and independence the proof writes out. The identity formula uses the projective rank scalar; for free terms it remains the ordinary integer rank mapped into A.

The module tensor associator takes `(p ⊗ q) ⊗ r` to `p ⊗ (q ⊗ r)` between differently parenthesized tensor modules, and its unit map takes `a ⊗ p` to `ap`; these are canonical isomorphisms, not literally identity functions on the stated ordinary tensor modules. The corrected statement suppresses these canonical maps in the triangle formulas. It also replaces the misleading phrase “model category” by the category of bounded projective complexes, and specifies evaluation's zero value outside total degree zero.

The imported proof's last displayed homotopy cancellation has an extra minus sign before the reindexed second sum. The replacement writes both finite sums and the substitution j = i − 1. More substantively, homotopy invariance alone does not identify all derived morphisms: step 11 applies the existing Hom bijection for bounded-above projectives. The old use of coefficient conventions for a derived tensor computation was replaced by the actual K-flat and derived-functor statements.

The current unit `sk-p-trace` already prescribes dual frames obtained from a finite free splitting, triangle identities, the Koszul sign, and transport under isomorphism. The corrected lemma follows this route exactly. Its general commutative-ring hypothesis is retained; the current `def-trf-categorical-trace` is restricted to finite coefficient rings, so the general trace is first stated through `sk-d-trace` and only its finite-coefficient specialization is identified with that definition.
