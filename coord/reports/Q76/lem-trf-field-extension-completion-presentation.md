### Returned Step 1: lem-trf-separable-point-flat-completion-map

Let X=Spec(S) and Y=Spec(S\otimes_kK), with x=\mathfrak p and y=\mathfrak q. Since a field is Noetherian and S is finite type over k, S is Noetherian by [lem-weil-pbc-finite-type-algebra-noetherian]; the same lemma makes S\otimes_kK Noetherian over K. The field-extension base-change lemma [lem-weil-pbc-field-extension-base-change-faithfully-flat] makes S\to S\otimes_kK faithfully flat, and [lem-weil-pbc-flat-local-ring-maps] gives a flat local map A\to B of Noetherian local rings. The fibre B/(\mathfrak pA)B is the localization of F\otimes_kK at the kernel of its evaluation to K. The finite-separable tensor lemma [lem-weil-pbc-finite-separable-tensor-field-reduced] makes F\otimes_kK reduced; it is finite over the field K, so this localization is a field. Hence (\mathfrak pA)B=\mathfrak qB, the maximal ideal of B. Finally [lem-weil-pbc-dimension-at-point-field-extension], applied to S and the prime q, gives dim(A)=dim(B). Thus the hypotheses on the local rings, residue extension, and equal dimensions in Stacks Tag 0C54 are satisfied.

### Returned Step 2: lem-trf-separable-point-flat-completion-map

The completion facts needed below follow from the existing completion lemmas. By [lem-weil-pbc-adic-completion-noetherian] and [lem-weil-pbc-local-completion-local-ring], both \widehat A and \widehat B are Noetherian local rings with residue fields F and K respectively; [lem-weil-pbc-adic-completion-mod-ideal] gives their adic completeness and identifies the completed maximal ideals with the kernels of the residue maps. The map A\to B therefore induces a completion map \widehat A\to\widehat B, and [lem-weil-pbc-flat-completion] says that this map is flat. The induced coefficient map on residues is precisely the given embedding \iota:F\hookrightarrow K.

### Returned Step 3: lem-trf-power-series-coefficient-extension-surjection

Write R_0=F[[t_1,\ldots,t_n]] and let y_i be the image in \widehat B of the chosen image of t_i in \widehat A. The presentation of \widehat A is surjective, so the images of the t_i generate the maximal ideal of \widehat A; since (\mathfrak pA)B=\mathfrak qB, their images y_i generate the maximal ideal of \widehat B. Apply the power-series universal property [lem-weil-pbc-power-series-universal-property-complete] with coefficient map K\to\widehat B and target ideal the completed maximal ideal. It gives a K-algebra map \Psi:K[[t_1,\ldots,t_n]]\to\widehat B sending each t_i to y_i.

### Returned Step 4: lem-trf-power-series-coefficient-extension-surjection

Each $f_j$ maps to zero under $\Psi$ because it already maps to zero in $\widehat A$ and the completion map is compatible with $A\to B$. Therefore $\Psi$ factors through a $K$-algebra map $\theta:K[[t_1,\ldots,t_n]]/(f_1,\ldots,f_s)\to\widehat B$. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-complete-source-surjective-mod-ideal] directly to $\Psi$ with source ideal $(t_1,\ldots,t_n)$: the full power-series source is complete by [lem-weil-pbc-power-series-field-cohen-regular-complete], its image ideal is the completed maximal ideal of $\widehat B$ by Step 3 and [lem-weil-pbc-adic-completion-mod-ideal], and the reduction $K\to K$ is the identity. Thus $\Psi$ is surjective, and its factor $\theta$ is surjective as well.

### Returned Step 5: lem-trf-power-series-coefficient-extension-surjection (Noetherian/local source and coefficient map) plus parent Step 3 (flat-module injectivity)

For injectivity, regard $R=\widehat A$ as the quotient $F[[t_1,\ldots,t_n]]/(f_1,\ldots,f_s)$, let $T=K[[t_1,\ldots,t_n]]/(f_1,\ldots,f_s)$, and regard $\widehat B$ as an $R$-module through the flat completion map of Step 2. The power-series lemma makes the ambient power-series ring Noetherian and local, so its quotient $T$ is Noetherian and local; the coefficient-extension map $R\to T$ is therefore a local homomorphism. The ring $T$ is a finite $T$-module over itself. The map $\theta:T\to\widehat B$ is $R$-linear, and its reduction modulo the maximal ideal of $R$ is the identity map $K\to K$, hence injective. Applying [lem-weil-pbc-flat-mod-injective] with $M=\widehat B$, $N=T$, and $u=\theta$ proves that $\theta$ is injective (the accompanying flat-cokernel conclusion is unnecessary here).

### Returned Step 6: parent Step 4

Thus \theta is an isomorphism. It is a K-algebra map, it sends every variable t_i to the image of the selected element of \widehat A, and its coefficient map is the prescribed embedding \iota. Transporting the complete local structure across this isomorphism gives the asserted isomorphism of complete local K-algebras.
