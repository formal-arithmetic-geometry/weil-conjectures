## Audit and changes

The imported item had no proof. Its statement is the geometric finite-type form of Prasad--Yu Theorem 1.2(c). Proposition 4.4(i) supplies its route through a DVR extension and finite smooth normalization; Proposition 4.3 supplies the reductive neutral model and maximal-boundedness argument. The target's dimension equality is retained. In the finite-type case, after the finite smooth normalization exists it is redundant for the component argument: the finite-cover lemma controls the neutral component by a finite kernel directly, without a separate dimension calculation. No hypothesis was weakened or silently changed. In particular no residue-characteristic restriction, smoothness of the original model, excellence, or finite-type hypothesis on a DVR extension was added.

The proof does not assume that an arbitrary affine smooth model has connected special fibre. It first constructs its split reductive neutral subgroup, and only then applies maximal boundedness to all its integral points. It also does not assume that surjectivity on integral points is initially available for the original model. Henselian lifting is used only for the affine smooth normalization. I replaced the source's uniqueness-of-smooth-models shortcut by a direct section-and-density argument: equality of generic sections implies equality of affine sections, every residue-field point lifts, and the closed identity component contains a dense subset.

No new proof step exceeds 2000 characters. The new nodes have statements only, with empty proofs, no proof dependencies, and no sketches hidden inside their statements. They express respectively potential good normalization for arbitrary group models, a general finite-cover criterion, a relative reductive neutral-component construction, and the bounded-subgroup maximality theorem. None asserts the item's connectedness conclusion. The finite-cover criterion is not the existing root-datum isogeny-realization theorem, which assumes both groups reductive and constructs a map.

## Review by Q23

I reread Prasad--Yu's definition of quasi-reductive on printed p.1 and Theorem 1.2(c), then the finite-type argument in Proposition 4.4(i), printed pp.8--9. The item's affine flat finite-type model, smooth geometrically connected generic fibre, reductive reduced geometric neutral fibre and dimension equality all match that argument; the characteristic-2 exceptions concern smoothness of the original model and do not limit connectedness. The finite-type hypothesis is already in the item, so the much longer proof of Theorem 1.2(a) is unnecessary.

For the potential-normalization node I read Sections 3.3 and 3.4, Corollary A.4 and Theorem A.6, and the reductions around A.9 on printed pp.29--30. The resulting input allows a local extension to a henselian DVR with the fixed algebraic-closure residue field, followed by finite extension making normalization smooth. A finite extension of a complete DVR with algebraically closed residue field still has that residue field; its localization is a DVR. Smoothness of the generic group ensures geometrically reduced and geometrically normal generic fibre, so normalization does not change it and the flat base-changed model is reduced. The coordinate subring C is a specified integral closure, not data selected from a bare existence claim. The finite normalization map is surjective and remains so on the special fibre. This is a potential-smoothness theorem for all affine smooth-generic group models, not a reformulation of quasi-reductive connectedness.

For the finite-cover node I checked the finite-kernel argument in the first paragraph of Proposition 4.3. A finite surjection q from smooth U factors through the reduction of V; its restriction from U^0 onto the reduced neutral component is finite surjective (the finitely many translated images of U^0 cover the target). A positive-dimensional smooth connected normal unipotent subgroup of U^0 would have a positive-dimensional smooth connected normal unipotent image in the reductive target, since the kernel is finite. This is impossible, so U^0 is reductive. This explains why no separability condition on the isogeny or centrality of its kernel belongs in the statement. The image/normal-unipotent argument is the content postponed to this unsplit node; it is not an uncited argument in the item.

For the neutral-model node I read Lemma 3.5, Section 3.6 and Proposition 4.3's first paragraph. Its input group N is already smooth. Its relative neutral subgroup D is the union of the entire connected generic fibre and the identity component in the special fibre. The lemma gives affineness, and the reductive-special-fibre argument gives generic reductivity and a split reductive model over a strictly henselian base. Henselian with algebraically closed residue field implies strictly henselian. This node does not assert that N has connected special fibre. For the boundedness node I read Lemma 3.7 and its use in Proposition 4.3. I spelled out the boundedness notion by valuation lower bounds on each regular function; it does not mean compactness of the set of points, and does not require a locally compact fraction field. Points are morphisms of R-schemes, not homomorphisms from a trivial group scheme; I corrected that wording during self-review.
