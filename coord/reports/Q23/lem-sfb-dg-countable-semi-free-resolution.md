For the first new node, 0CRM says to repeat the preceding subalgebra argument for modules. Explicitly, choose one cycle for each cohomology class in every degree, close under finite sums and the A-action, and then repeatedly adjoin a primitive in N of every cycle of the current countable submodule which is a boundary in N. At each stage closure under differential can be enforced by adjoining differentials too, or follows from the chosen cycles and the Leibniz rule. A countable set of generators over countable A has countably many finite expressions. The union is countable, surjective on cohomology from the initial choices, and injective since every kernel cycle acquires a primitive at the next stage. Thus the node states a genuine inclusion into the given N, not merely an unspecified isomorphic derived object. For acyclic uncountable N the zero submodule is already a valid representative; no countability of N itself is used.

For the second new node I checked 09KN's actual cover: take disk generators x_m,y_m for every homogeneous m of M with dx_m=y_m and dy_m=0, sending them to m,dm; add generators z_c for every homogeneous cycle c with dz_c=0, sending them to c. Put y_m,z_c in U and x_m in V. Extend the differential by Leibniz; its square vanishes because it vanishes on these generators and on A. This gives every clause of the new cover statement, including cycle surjectivity, the specified graded basis, and graded splitting by deleting V coordinates. Countability uses countability of actual M here, not just H(M). For M=0 the empty basis works; for A=0 unital modules are zero, so there is no exception. Over A=Z in degree zero this reduces to the familiar disk-and-sphere cover; it is not an acyclic cover in general, so the statement does not relabel the item's conclusion.
