### Objects and canonical scalar structures

Write `T` for the node's `R'`. Mathlib's tensor algebra is `T ⊗[R] A`, with its
standard `T`-algebra structure. Tensor symmetry identifies this with the node's
`A ⊗[R] R'`.

`tensorModule R T A M` is exactly
`(ModuleCat.extendScalars (Algebra.TensorProduct.includeRight :
 A →ₐ[R] T ⊗[R] A).toRingHom).obj M`.
Its data are Mathlib's extension of scalars, not a chosen module or arbitrary map.
The canonical identification
`(T ⊗[R] A) ⊗[A] M ≃ M ⊗[R] T` sends
`(t ⊗ a) ⊗ m` to `(a • m) ⊗ t`, with inverse
`m ⊗ t ↦ (t ⊗ 1) ⊗ m`. Balancing over both R and A proves these formulas
well-defined and inverse. Under this identification the action of `t ⊗ a` is
`m ⊗ t' ↦ (a • m) ⊗ (t*t')`, precisely the required tensor-algebra action.
`tensorUnit` is the actual pure tensor `(1 : T ⊗[R] A) ⊗[A] m`, corresponding to
`m ⊗ 1`. Neither helper uses a choice or a postponed theorem. These are owning-node
canonical scalar-extension and unit helpers, permitted by the missing-helper rule;
they introduce no independent notion and require no new definition node.

- `D.n = 0`: the empty index equivalence and all indexed compatibility clauses are
  valid; both modules are zero through the existing `zero_case` fields and tensor
  relations. Module finiteness and finite type remain separate conclusions.
- Identity base change: choose the same sequence under the unit tensor
  identifications; every compatibility equation reduces to the original data.
- A field extension: the generic-fibre condition tensors an isomorphism and its
  inverse; no additional assumption on the extension appears.
- A nonflat map, for example `k[t] → k` at `(t)`: the formulation admits it. Only
  right exactness is needed for cokernels, and only the smooth intermediate B_i
  are assumed flat by consequences of the existing conditions.
- Zero new base ring: there is no prime `q`, so this pointed case is correctly
  vacuous, not excluded by an extra nonzero assumption. A zero ambient algebra
  or zero N is allowed when the given dévissage exists.
- Rank zero: `Fin (D.rIndex i)` can be empty; the alpha equation then identifies
  zero maps. There is no nonzero-rank or positive-length hypothesis.
- The alpha equation is genuinely canonical: original finite free vectors tensored
  with 1 generate after extension, and ae/be/me preserve the relevant scalar
  actions. Thus specifying it on those vectors loses no component of the map.
- Successor and first-index proofs are transported only by `he`; proof
  irrelevance means these do not add constraints on the given data.
