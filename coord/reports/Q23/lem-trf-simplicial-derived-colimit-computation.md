### Original step 1 — Assembly step 1: bar/projective normalization retained.

Apply [lem-trf-derived-colimit-bar] to $\mathcal E=\mathcal D^{op}$, viewing $F$ as a presheaf on $\mathcal E$. Call the bar complex in the first-vertex sign convention stated above $B(\mathcal D,F)$. The cited node numbers faces in the reverse order: in degree $n$ its differential is $(-1)^n$ times ours. Multiplication on degree $-n$ by $c_n=(-1)^{n(n+1)/2}$ is a chain isomorphism between these conventions, since $c_n=(-1)^nc_{n-1}$. The abelian category $\operatorname{Fun}(\mathcal D,\operatorname{Mod}_R)$ has enough projectives: $R[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D}(T,-)]$ is projective since maps out of it are elements of the value at $T$ and epimorphisms are objectwise surjective, and $\bigoplus_{T,\,x\in F(T)}R[\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D}(T,-)]\to F$ is surjective. The colimit is right exact, and the cited node gives that its total left derived functor exists. Hence [lem-trf-bounded-above-acyclic-derived-computation] identifies, for a projective resolution $a:P\to F[0]$ zero in positive degrees, the object $\operatorname{colim}_{\mathcal D}P$ of the statement with the total left derived functor value by $\varepsilon_P\circ L\operatorname{colim}(Qa)^{-1}$, independently of $P$ by its naturality clause. Consequently that node gives a canonical comparison $b_{\mathcal D,F}:B(\mathcal D,F)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf L\operatorname{colim}_{\mathcal D}F$. Its reindexing maps are exactly the displayed generator formula: the factors $c_n$ are the same on source and target. The node also gives the identity and composition laws, including semilinear maps and degreewise restriction of scalars. All sums are small because objects, arrows and finite strings in $\mathcal D$ are in the working universe.

Original cited inputs: lem-trf-derived-colimit-bar, lem-trf-bounded-above-acyclic-derived-computation.

### Original step 2 — Horizontal node: construction, commuting directions and both edge chain maps.

For $p,q\geq0$ form the module
$$A_{p,q}=\bigoplus_{T_0\to\cdots\to T_p\xrightarrow{f}Q_q}F(T_0).$$
The horizontal faces delete $T_i$; deleting $T_0$ applies $F(T_0\to T_1)$, and deleting $T_p$ composes the last arrow with $f$. Use their alternating sum $\partial_h$ for $p>0$. In the other direction a face or degeneracy of $Q$ postcomposes $f$ and leaves $x\in F(T_0)$ unchanged; use the alternating faces $\partial_v$ for $q>0$. Face identities give $\partial_h^2=\partial_v^2=0$; the two directions commute because postcomposition with a map of $Q$ commutes with composition of $f$ and never changes $x$. Put $A_{p,q}$ in cochain bidegree $(-p,-q)$ and totalize by $\partial_h+(-1)^p\partial_v$. There are chain maps
$$e_Q:\operatorname{Tot}A\to s(F(Q)),\qquad e_B:\operatorname{Tot}A\to B(\mathcal D,F).$$
The first is zero on $p>0$ and sends $(T\xrightarrow f Q_q,x)$ to $F(f)x$ on $p=0$; the second is zero on $q>0$ and forgets $f:T_p\to Q_0$ on $q=0$. For $e_Q$, the two horizontal faces from $p=1$ have the same image by functoriality of $F$. For $e_B$, the two vertical faces from $q=1$ have the same image after forgetting $f$. The remaining chain-map equations are precisely the respective face formulas.

Original cited inputs: none; explicit elementary calculation.

### Original step 3 — Horizontal node: appended-identity contraction including augmented degree.

For fixed $q$, the augmented horizontal chain complex $A_{\bullet,q}\to F(Q_q)$ is contractible. Its section in augmented degree $-1$ sends $y\in F(Q_q)$ to $(Q_q\xrightarrow{1}Q_q,y)$. For $p\geq0$ set
$$h_p(T_0\to\cdots\to T_p\xrightarrow f Q_q,x)=(-1)^{p+1}(T_0\to\cdots\to T_p\xrightarrow f Q_q\xrightarrow1Q_q,x).$$
Every face except the last of this appended chain is the appended chain of the corresponding old face. Their coefficients in $\partial h+h\partial$ cancel; the last face has coefficient $(-1)^{p+1}(-1)^{p+1}=1$ and is the original chain. In degree zero the other term is the displayed section composed with $F(f)$, and in augmented degree $-1$ augmentation after section is the identity. Thus $\partial h+h\partial=1$ on the augmented complex, proving that the horizontal augmentation is a quasi-isomorphism. This calculation requires no representable contractibility assumption.

Original cited inputs: none; explicit elementary calculation.

### Original step 4 — Vertical node: free chains, finite zigzags, singleton contraction, arbitrary coefficients and direct sums.

Fix $p$ and a string $\sigma=(T_0\to\cdots\to T_p)$, and write $X=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal D}(T_p,Q_\bullet)$ and $M=F(T_0)$. Its contribution to the vertical augmented complex is $\bigoplus_{X_\bullet}M\to M$. By hypothesis $X\to *$ has a simplicial homotopy inverse. Apply [lem-weil-pbc-simplicial-homotopy-free-chain-homotopy] over $R$ to the homotopies of the two composites, adding or negating chain homotopies if a finite chain of homotopies is used. Hence $C_\bullet(X;R)\to C_\bullet(*;R)$ is a chain homotopy equivalence. The latter complex has one $R$ in each degree, differential $1$ in positive even degrees and $0$ in odd degrees. Its augmentation to $R[0]$ is a chain homotopy equivalence: use the identity section in degree zero and homotopy $h_n=1$ for odd $n$, $h_n=0$ for even $n$. Tensor these maps and homotopies with $M$. The equation $dh+hd=f-g$ is preserved by tensoring because it uses only sums and compositions, so no flatness of $M$ is needed. Under $R[X_n]\otimes_R M=\bigoplus_{X_n}M$, the resulting augmentation is exactly summation to $M$. Taking the direct sum of these homotopy equivalences over $\sigma$ gives a vertical augmentation quasi-isomorphism $A_{p,\bullet}\to B(\mathcal D,F)^{-p}[0]$.

Original cited inputs: lem-weil-pbc-simplicial-homotopy-free-chain-homotopy.

### Original step 5 — Horizontal node (e_Q and transpose sign) and vertical node (e_B): uniformly bounded, finite-diagonal total comparison.

Apply [lem-pc-bounded-above-double-complex-total-comparison] to $e_B$ regarded as a morphism of double cochain complexes from $A$ to the double complex $B$ concentrated in second degree zero. The fixed-first-index column maps are the quasi-isomorphisms of step 4. Both first and second indices are at most zero, so use the uniform bounds $x_0=y_0=0$. Every total degree has a finite diagonal, hence the required totals exist as finite direct sums of modules. Thus $e_B$ is a quasi-isomorphism. Apply the same node with indices interchanged to $e_Q$, using step 3 for the column maps. To return to our chosen total convention, the canonical identification of the two orders of totalization multiplies $A_{p,q}$ by $(-1)^{pq}$: the horizontal coefficient changes from $(-1)^q$ to $1$, and the vertical coefficient changes from $1$ to $(-1)^p$, since adjacent $pq$ exponents differ by $q$ and $p$, respectively. On the augmentation edge $p=0$ this sign is $1$, so the resulting map is exactly $e_Q$. It too is a quasi-isomorphism.

### Original step 7 — Semilinear node: full generator formula and both strict squares; assembly step 4 inverts.

For $(u,\rho,\theta,k)$ as in the statement, define a map from $A(\mathcal D,F,Q)$ to the degreewise restricted $A(\mathcal D',F',Q')$ by
$$(T_0\to\cdots\to T_p\xrightarrow fQ_q,x)\longmapsto(uT_0\to\cdots\to uT_p\xrightarrow{k_q\circ u(f)}Q'_q,\theta_{T_0}x).$$
It commutes with the first horizontal face by naturality of $\theta$, with the last by functoriality of $u$, with the intermediate faces by preservation of composition, and with vertical faces by $d_i k_q=k_{q-1}u(d_i)$. Hence it is a map of the signed total complexes. Its $e_Q$ square commutes because
$$F'(k_q u(f))\theta_{T_0}=F'(k_q)\theta_{Q_q}F(f),$$
and its $e_B$ square commutes because both routes forget $f$ and apply $u$ to the string and $\theta$ to $x$. Inverting the two $e_Q$ maps and using the natural bar comparison of [lem-trf-derived-colimit-bar] proves exactly
$$a_{Q'}\,s(F'(k)\theta_Q)=\mathbf H(u,\theta)\,a_Q.$$
Taking $u=1$ and $\rho=1$ gives naturality in $F$ and $Q$. Only the original $Q$ and the target $Q'$ need step 4: the intermediate simplicial object $uQ$ is never used as a resolution.

Original cited inputs: lem-trf-derived-colimit-bar.

### Original step 8 — Semilinear node: exact scalar restriction and localization identifications.

All constructions in step 7 are valid with scalars restricted along $\rho$. Restriction retains the underlying additive groups, so kernels and images of differentials, and therefore quasi-isomorphisms, are unchanged. It also retains direct sums: an element is the same finitely supported family with the same componentwise addition. Thus the displayed bar complexes and two-sided comparison roofs after restriction are precisely their degreewise restrictions, with the same generator maps and integer signs. The restricted $e_Q,e_B$ remain invertible and their inverses restrict as inverses. Consequently the equality of step 7 is an equality in $D(R)$ with all the prescribed restriction-of-scalars identifications.

Original cited inputs: def-weil-abstract-derived-category.
