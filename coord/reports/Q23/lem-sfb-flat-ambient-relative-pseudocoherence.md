For an explicit counterexample take an algebraically closed field k of characteristic zero, S=Spec k, A=k[t²−1,t³−t], Y=Spec A (the nodal cubic), and X=Spec B with B=k[t,1/(t−1)]. The normalization restricted to X gives i:X→Y. Away from the node it is bijective, and only t=−1 remains over the node because t=1 was deleted. It also maps the generic point to the generic point. Both are irreducible curves whose proper closed subsets are finite sets of closed points, so this bijection is a homeomorphism onto all of Y. It is affine, in agreement with 04DE, but is not finite: A lies in the valuation ring k[t]_(t−1), whereas 1/(t−1) has negative valuation, hence is not integral over A. Therefore B is not a finite A-module. Set E=O_X[0], m=0. Since X/k is smooth and finitely presented, E is pseudo-coherent relative to k. But Ri_*E=i_*O_X corresponds to B, which is not even 0-pseudo-coherent relative to k: using a polynomial presentation of A, 09UJ and 09VC would make B a finite module over that polynomial algebra and hence over A, a contradiction. This tests the nonfinite normalization case which the earlier edge checks missed.

In its affine proof one can take no extra generators b_j and T=affine n-space over S: a polynomial presentation of A composes with A→B. The two relative criteria are then the same derived pushforward on that polynomial ambient scheme. Thus the valid restricted statement applies to arbitrary E and needs no D_QCoh assumption.
