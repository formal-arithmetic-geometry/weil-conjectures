## Review by Q23

I read the item against Pikaart--de Jong Sections 1.1 and 2.3.3--2.3.7 (printed pp.2,10--11). Section 2.3.3 defines an unrestricted frame of R^1, so there is no extra symplectic condition or chosen component. Section 2.3.5 defines an actual normalization. In Proposition 2.3.6 the complete local normalization is Spf(B)/Aut(C), and freeness on its closed branches is the specific assertion used before Remark 2.3.7 concludes that the compactification stack is a scheme. I did not assume smoothness of this compactification: the later Theorem 3.1.1 explicitly distinguishes smoothness in abelian level, which would be wrong to add here.

For the finite-normalization node I read Stacks spaces-morphisms.tex, Tag 0ABP, lemma-properties-normalization (etale compatibility), and Tag 0BB0, lemma-nagata-normalization-finite (Nagata target, finite-type quasi-separated source, reduced source). A smooth finite-type field atlas is regular and excellent, and its finite etale dense-open cover is reduced; finite type over a Noetherian atlas makes the open inclusion quasi-compact. All generic factors must be retained for the cited construction and its base change. I also read varieties.tex, Tag 038O, lemma-geometrically-normal: normality over the perfect closure is equivalent to normality after all field extensions. Over a perfect field the perfect closure is the field itself; this justifies the added scalar-extension clause. The formal quotient comparison is the one on printed p.11 of Pikaart--de Jong. I require an effective Spec(R) chart in the generic statement so that its punctured algebraic cover is well-defined; the stable deformation theorem supplies exactly such an algebraized family. I isolated this substantial descent/completion theorem rather than claiming that etale compatibility alone establishes completion compatibility.

For the stable-isomorphism node I read moduli-curves.tex, Tags 0E7A, 0E9A and 0E9C with their labels. Properness of the diagonal comes from separatedness, unramifiedness from the Deligne--Mumford property, and hence its Isom functors are finite unramified; properness of the stack gives finite type. These facts require actual theorems, not merely the definition of stable curves. The pre-existing coarse statement does not contain them. The empty Isom scheme for non-isomorphic families and nonempty finite automorphism schemes are both allowed; no flatness or constant automorphism order is asserted.

For the branch-freeness node I read the complete local construction and the attribution to Deligne in the proof of Proposition 2.3.6, printed p.11, and the framing convention of Section 2.3.3. Its ring A is a versal deformation of one stable geometric curve, U inverts exactly its node parameters, and B uses the full frame torsor. The assertion is about H acting on the normalized closed fibre, not about deck transformations or an unnormalized boundary level sheaf. I checked the smooth edge case d=0, when the product is 1; genus 2 is included, and its hyperelliptic involution acts nontrivially modulo an odd prime. Characteristic 2 remains permitted when ell is odd; the statement never divides by |Aut(C)| and no tame-stabilizer assumption is inserted. Reducible curves and multiple generic components are expressly included. Level 2 is excluded as required by the source; its hyperelliptic obstruction explains why smooth triviality cannot be asserted there.

For the coarse-cover criterion I read algebraic.tex, Tag 045H, lemma-algebraic-stack-no-automorphisms, and stacks-more-morphisms.tex, Tag 0DUT, theorem-keel-mori, including the separated universal-homeomorphism coarse map and its local construction. Finite diagonal ensures finite inertia. The source of a representable finite map to a Deligne--Mumford stack is itself Deligne--Mumford, so trivial geometric automorphisms suffice to eliminate inertia and give an algebraic space. A finite representable cover then has finite geometric fibres over the coarse space; the coarse morphism is proper in this finite-type setting, so the composite is proper and quasi-finite, hence finite. A finite algebraic space over a scheme is a scheme. This is a generic theorem to split later, not a claim that a stack is automatically a scheme because it has a coarse scheme. As edge checks, with target a scheme it reduces to the usual finite-map case, with target BG for a finite etale group it includes the torsor atlas, and the identity of BG for nontrivial G fails the trivial-source-inertia hypothesis. None requires linear reductivity.
