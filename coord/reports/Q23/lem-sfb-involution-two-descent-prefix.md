## Supporting argument: deletion-parabolic-coset-length

Take a shortest J-word U for u. If U were not reduced in the ambient generators, deletion would produce a shorter J-word for u. Thus the two minimum lengths agree. Fix a reduced I-word C for h. If UC is not reduced, deletion gives two positions. If both are in U, cancel h on the right to contradict minimality of U. If both are in C, cancel u on the left to contradict minimality of C. In the remaining case write U' and C' for the words after one deletion in each. Their products satisfy u h = t_(U') t_(C'), so t_(C') = t_(U')^{-1} u h belongs to H_J h. But C' has length ell(h)-1, contradicting minimality of h in that coset. Therefore UC is reduced and has the stated additive length. This argument does not use exchange, uniqueness of the shortest coset representative, or a presentation. It is the one-sided deletion argument compared with the Bridge Lemma, not an invocation of its Coxeter-system hypotheses.

## Supporting argument: two-involution-alternating-metric

Put r=st. Then t=sr and srs=r^{-1}. The case m=1 would force s=t, so m>=2. Every word in s,t has form r^a or r^a s: substitute t=sr and move each s past r by sr=r^{-1}s. Exponents are read modulo m. The powers r^a are distinct for 0<=a<m by the definition of order. Also s is not a power of r. Otherwise s would commute with r; the conjugation identity would force r=r^{-1}, hence m<=2. For m=2 the only possible nonidentity power s is r, forcing t=sr=1, a contradiction. Consequently the two families r^a and r^a s are disjoint and give exactly 2m elements.

The alternating products are, for a>=0:

- b_s(2a)=r^a, b_t(2a)=r^{-a};
- b_s(2a+1)=r^a s, b_t(2a+1)=r^{-a-1}s.

If m=2q, the listed even lengths below m give the rotation exponents 0, ±1, ..., ±(q-1); length m adds q=-q. The odd lengths below m give exponents 0,...,q-1 and -1,...,-q in the other family. These cover all residues exactly once in each family. The two words at length m both give r^q. If m=2q+1, the even lengths give all rotation exponents 0, ±1,...,±q; odd lengths below m give exponents 0,...,q-1 and -1,...,-q, and the two words of length m both add exponent q=-q-1 in the other family. This verifies exactly the claimed exhaustive list, distinctness and equality at m, in both parities.

A shortest binary word cannot have adjacent equal letters, since cancelling their square shortens it. It is therefore alternating. Every listed element already has a word of length at most m, so no word longer than m is shortest. The distinctness just proved rules out a shorter alternating word for any listed element, and also determines all shortest words, including the two at m. This is an agent-written elementary group calculation, independent of the geometric presentation in Cashen's examples.
