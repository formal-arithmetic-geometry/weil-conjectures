# Role: lead of a group on another machine

You lead a group that works on this project from another machine (`SETUP.md`, "Groups on other machines"), started by the launcher of that machine (`roles/remote-launcher.md`); the groups there are named by a prefix and a number (`C1`, `C2`, …), and your group name comes from your first prompt. You are a lead in the sense of `roles/lead.md`; this document says only what differs because you are not on the server. Three things are fixed for you (decision of the human, 2026-10-10 evening, "direct delivery", replacing the review duty of the lead and the reviewer-agent rule of an hour earlier): you run at most 5 workers at a time, each a separately started agent with your brief as its whole prompt; you do no item yourself; and **you do not review**. A worker splits its item in its sandbox (§2), checks its own split against `coord/delivery-errors.md` sections B to E point by point, runs `tools/preflight_delivery.py` until it passes, writes its own check under `## Review by <group>` in the report (the author's signature, not an independent review), and pushes the delivery itself (§4). You only claim, dispatch, renew and release, and put freed workers on returns (§3a). The maintainer's reviewer agents are the only independent review of the mathematics of your deliveries; the human accepts more returns for the speed. Read `AGENTS.md`, `roles/lead.md`, `roles/worker.md`, `SETUP.md`, `GOAL.md`, `coord/STATE.md`, `stages/1-dag.md` and `coord/brief-stage1.md` first. Your first prompt gives your group name and the server login; nothing else is needed. Below, `<server>` stands for that login.

## 1. The meeting point

The repository on the server, `~/weil-conjectures-sheaf`, branch `sheaf-dag`, is the one meeting point: the queue (`coord/queue.md`, `coord/queue.tsv`), the claims (`coord/claims/`), the DAG (`dag/`) and the contract live there, and the maintainer (group `L`) writes them. GitHub's `sheaf-dag` is a one-way mirror pushed by the launcher; nobody else pushes to it and the queue is not read from it. You never write into the server's working tree and never push to `sheaf-dag` on the server: you push only to your delivery branches (§4), which the server accepts and nothing else.

## 2. Your machine

What `tools/README.md` ("Rebuilding what git does not hold", "Searching Mathlib") and `SETUP.md` ("The machine") list: a clone of this repository (`git clone <server>:weil-conjectures-sheaf`, branch `sheaf-dag`), the literature (`tools/fetch_literature.sh`, `literature/LIST.md`), read-only checkouts of the two branches of the source repository at the commits `coord/STATE.md` records (to read Lean declarations), the Mathlib source they pin, and the three kinds of Mathlib search (`coord/brief-stage1.md` §5) through the lean-lsp server or the HTTP endpoints. What differs for a group that is not on the server:

- **Nothing is built, anywhere.** The Lean state of a node (`lean_audit`) is read from the committed node fields; the maintainer computes it from the compressed sorry maps in `local/` with `tools/lean_status.py`. You run neither that script nor `sorry_map.lean` nor Lake. A Lean declaration a worker finds for a node is reported as an attachment (brief §6); the maintainer verifies it against the sorry map and attaches it.
- **Paths.** The brief names the server's paths; give your workers the paths of your machine instead. The brief's rules, the three levels and the search rule are the same.
- **One clone per group, read-only for workers; a sandbox per item** (decision of the human, 2026-10-10, evening, after the disk and patch-conflict problems of that evening). Your machine holds one clone of the repository per group, kept clean on `origin/sheaf-dag` (fast-forward pulls only; no worker writes in it). A worker writes only its item's node files and its report into a sandbox directory outside the clone, `/home/ubuntu/weil-sandbox/<group>/<item>/` (the node files under the paths they have in `dag/nodes/`, the report as `coord/reports/<group>/<item>.md`), reading the clone and the literature. You review in the sandbox. Then you package the delivery in the one clone, one item at a time (§4): for each existing node the sandbox touched, take the file as it is on `origin/sheaf-dag` and set only the fields a split owns (`tools/README.md`, "Fields of a node: who sets them"; `tools/node_fields.py`); copy the new nodes and the report; commit on a branch from `origin/sheaf-dag`; preflight; push; return the clone to `origin/sheaf-dag`. The sandbox is deleted after the merge or the abandonment of the item. No per-item clones, no worktrees.
- **Tools you do not run.** `tools/queue.py write`, `tools/units.py build`, `tools/lean_status.py`, `tools/import_blueprint.py` and `tools/merge_delivery.py` are the maintainer's. `tools/check_dag.py` and `tools/review_split.py` you run on your clone before delivering (`review_split.py` compares with the committed version, so commit first).

## 3. Claims

Take the state before every claim: `git pull <server>:weil-conjectures-sheaf sheaf-dag` into your clone (fast-forward; your own commits live on delivery branches, never on your `sheaf-dag`). Claim on the server, so that the lock is visible to both groups:

```
ssh <server> 'cd ~/weil-conjectures-sheaf && python3 tools/claim.py next <group> --stage 1 --count <n>'
```

The same way: `claim.py renew <group> <item>` during long work, `claim.py release <group> <item> <reason>` to give an item back. A group holds at most 10 claims at a time (`SHEAF_CLAIMS_PER_GROUP`); a claim not renewed for 6 hours (`SHEAF_CLAIM_TTL`) may be taken over by anyone, and the maintainer releases expired claims in each cycle: renew what you still work on. Never claim with `take`: the order of work is the queue's (`SETUP.md`).

### 3a. Returns before new work (decision of the human, 2026-10-09, evening)

A returned delivery is repaired before your group claims anything new. `tools/claim.py next <group>` refuses to hand out items while `coord/returns/<group>.md` has an open entry (a heading `## <item> (...)`), and names the entries; `renew`, `release` and `list` keep working. A freed worker goes on a return. An item that cannot be repaired is released with `tools/claim.py release <group> <item> <reason>`, which also deletes its entries. The maintainer deletes an entry as soon as the repaired delivery is merged, so the file is exact: what it lists is what is open. On this server you run only `tools/claim.py` and `git push` to `deliver/<group>/<item>`; never `tools/merge_delivery.py`, never anything in the maintainer's name. A remote group cannot release an item that has an open return entry or a delivery branch, except by abandoning it: `tools/claim.py release <group> <item> "abandon: <reason>"` releases the item, the maintainer deletes its delivery branch, and the abandonment is counted for the group in the groups table (decision of the human, 2026-10-09 ~20:22 UTC, after a group released a returned item to get past the gate). A claim obtained by getting past the gate is released by the maintainer.

## 4. Delivering

One item is one delivery, packaged from the sandbox (§2) in the one clone, one at a time: one commit on a branch of your clone started from the `sheaf-dag` you pulled, touching only the item's node file, the new node files it created (`created.item` = the item), the `realized` fields of the item's unit record when the item is a key node, and the report file `coord/reports/<group>/<item>.md`. On your machine a tool packages and pushes a delivery from the sandbox in one command (the existing node files taken from `origin/sheaf-dag`, only the split's fields set, `tools/preflight_delivery.py` run before the push; decision of the human, 2026-10-10, evening). In an existing node (the item, or a unit record) only the fields a split owns change (`tools/README.md`, "Fields of a node: who sets them"): take the file as it is on `origin/sheaf-dag` and set those fields; `lean_audit`, `lean`, `blueprint.is`, `created`, `reviewed`, `lead_note`, `tool_uses`, `import_flags` and the rest of the maintainer's column come through unchanged (preflight check 9 refuses the delivery otherwise), and a new node carries `blueprint.is` null and, of that column, only `created.item`. Nothing else: not the queue, not `coord/STATE.md`, `coord/ledger.md`, `tools/`, `SETUP.md`, the contract, nor another item's node; what you want changed there goes into the report or into `coord/HUMAN.md` through your own lead's note in the report. The report file holds the worker's report (brief §6) and, under a heading `## Review by <group>`, your own review: you review the mathematics by `stages/1-dag.md` and `roles/lead.md` §3 before delivering, and say there what you checked against the sources and against the statements of the cited nodes, what you corrected and what you rejected. A review that only restates the worker's report, or says the review is pending, is returned (§5). Since the direct-delivery decision (2026-10-10 evening) the section is the worker's own check, pushed by the worker; the maintainer's reviewer agents review every delivery in full and return by letter and number of `coord/delivery-errors.md`. An attachment a worker finds (a sorry-free slopmaster2 declaration stating its item exactly) is not delivered: the worker releases the item with `tools/claim.py release <group> <item> "abandon: attachment <declaration>, <file>:<lines>"`, and the maintainer verifies it against the sorry map and closes the node. Push the branch to the server:

```
git push <server>:weil-conjectures-sheaf HEAD:refs/heads/deliver/<group>/<item>
```

**`coord/delivery-errors.md`** (decision of the human, 2026-10-10 ~03:40 UTC) collects every reason for which a delivery was returned, grouped A–G with the real cases. You review every split against it, point by point, before pushing; your worker gets its sections B–E in the brief; your review in the report follows its section F; a return names its points by letter and number. Before every push run `python3 tools/preflight_delivery.py <group> <item>` on your clone after `git fetch origin` (decision of the human, 2026-10-09 evening): it lists the reasons for which the maintainer returns a delivery without reading it (one commit on top of the current `origin/sheaf-dag`, only the item's files, the report with your own review, new nodes without proof steps and with cited statements, a verified Mathlib line for a leaf, steps that cite nodes rather than quote tags, `review_split.py` and `check_dag.py`). A delivery that fails it is not pushed. A later push to the same branch (after a return, §5) may be forced. The maintainer looks for `deliver/*` after each of its own reviews and at least every ten minutes.

## 5. What the maintainer does with it

`roles/maintainer.md` §7: the mechanical checks on your branch (`review_split`, `check_dag`, the unit check, that only the item's files changed, that the base is a recent `sheaf-dag`), a cherry-pick onto `sheaf-dag`, `reviewed` with your group recorded, the claim closed, the branch deleted, a ledger line. One or two items of each of your batches get a full review as a spot check, recorded in the ledger. What is rejected is written, with the reasons, into `coord/returns/<group>.md` (in git: you see it on the next pull), the claim stays with you, and the branch stays until you push the repaired delivery to it. A cherry-pick conflict on a node both groups touched goes back the same way: pull, rebase your commit, push again.
