\chapter{Etale Sites, Constructible Sheaves, and Finiteness}
\label{ch:etale-foundations}

This chapter fixes the finite-coefficient theory from which adic cohomology is
built.  Schemes are locally noetherian, \(\ell\) is invertible on every scheme
under discussion, and
\[
 \Lambda_n=\mathbf Z/\ell^n\mathbf Z.
\]
The relevant foundations are proved in
\cite{SGA4,SGA4half,MilneEtale,FreitagKiehl,ConradEtaleNotes}.  No weight or
purity assertion occurs here.

\section{The small etale site}

\begin{definition}
A morphism \(u:U\to X\) is \emph{etale} if it is flat, unramified, and locally
of finite presentation.  Equivalently, locally on source and target it has a
Jacobian presentation
\[
 \Spec\bigl(A[t_1,\ldots,t_r]/(f_1,\ldots,f_r)\bigr)_g\to\Spec A
\]
with \(\det(\partial f_i/\partial t_j)\) invertible.  Equivalently, it is
locally of finite presentation and formally etale: maps through a square-zero
closed immersion lift uniquely.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Small etale site]
\label{def:small-etale-site}
The site \(X_{\et}\) has etale \(X\)-schemes as objects and jointly surjective
families as coverings.  A presheaf \(\cF\) is a sheaf if
\[
 \cF(U)\longrightarrow\prod_i\cF(U_i)
 \rightrightarrows\prod_{i,j}\cF(U_i\times_UU_j)
\]
is an equalizer for every cover.  Write \(\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\et})\) for
abelian sheaves.
\end{definition}

Etale maps are stable under base change and are open.  Consequently fiber
products and covering pullbacks exist.  Sheafification of abelian presheaves
is exact.  A morphism \(f:X\to Y\) gives a morphism of topoi with an exact
pullback \(f^*\) left adjoint to \(f_*\), where
\[
 (f_*\cF)(V)=\cF(V\times_YX).
\]

\subsection{Geometric points}

\begin{definition}
A geometric point is \(\bar x:\Spec\Omega\to X\) with \(\Omega\) separably
closed.  If \(x\) is its image, then
\[
 \mathcal O^{\mathrm{sh}}_{X,\bar x}
   =\varinjlim_{(U,u)}\mathcal O_{U,u},\qquad
 \cF_{\bar x}=\varinjlim_{(U,u)}\cF(U),
\]
where \((U,u)\) runs over pointed etale neighborhoods.
\end{definition}

A strictly henselian local ring has separably closed residue field and every
finite etale cover of its spectrum is a disjoint union of trivial covers.
This is the local calculation behind the following proposition.

\begin{proposition}[Enough points]
\label{prop:etale-enough-points}
Geometric stalks are exact and conservative.  A morphism of etale sheaves is
injective, surjective, or an isomorphism exactly when it is so on all
geometric stalks.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Stalks are filtered colimits, hence preserve finite limits and cokernels.  A
germ lifts or vanishes on a pointed etale neighborhood by definition.  Such
neighborhoods cover the domain when the corresponding assertion holds at
every geometric point.  The sheaf equalizer then glues local lifts and detects
zero sections.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Universal-homeomorphism invariance]
\label{prop:universal-homeomorphism}
If \(v:X'\to X\) is a universal homeomorphism, then
\[
 v^*:\operatorname{Sh}(X_{\et})\xrightarrow{\sim}
      \operatorname{Sh}(X'_{\et})
\]
is an equivalence compatible with stalks and derived global sections.
Reduction and purely inseparable base change therefore do not alter etale
cohomology.
\end{proposition}

\begin{foundation}
Work affinely.  An etale algebra over a radicial integral extension descends
to a finite stage because it is finitely presented; formal etaleness gives
unique descent of morphisms.  Base change and descent are inverse on etale
algebras and preserve covers because every base change of \(v\) is a
homeomorphism.  The resulting equivalence of sites induces the equivalence of
topoi.  Exact equivalences preserve injectives, so they preserve derived
global sections.
\end{foundation}

\section{Constructible sheaves and the fundamental group}

\begin{definition}
A finite sheaf is \emph{locally constant constructible} (lcc) if an etale
cover makes it constant.  A \(\Lambda_n\)-sheaf is \emph{constructible} if
\(X\) has a finite locally closed stratification
\[
 X=\coprod_\alpha X_\alpha
\]
on which the restrictions are lcc.
\end{definition}

On a noetherian scheme, constructible sheaves are closed under kernels,
cokernels, extensions, tensor products, and internal Homs.  Take a common
stratification; on a connected stratum the assertion is finite module algebra
with continuous action of one profinite group.

\begin{theorem}[Galois category]
\label{thm:lcc-pi1}
If \(X\) is connected and locally noetherian with geometric point \(\bar x\),
there are equivalences
\[
\begin{split}
\{\text{finite etale covers of }X\}
  &\simeq\{\text{finite continuous }\pi_1(X,\bar x)\text{-sets}\},\\
\{\text{lcc }\Lambda_n\text{-sheaves}\}
  &\simeq\{\text{finite continuous }\Lambda_n[\![\pi_1]\!]\text{-modules}\}.
\end{split}
\]
A different base point changes the group by an inner isomorphism.
\end{theorem}

\begin{foundation}
Choose a cofinal system of pointed connected finite Galois covers \(X_i\) and
define
\[
 \pi_1(X,\bar x)=\varprojlim_i\operatorname{Aut}_X(X_i).
\]
The geometric fiber of any finite etale cover has a compatible finite action.
Conversely a finite continuous action factors through one finite quotient and
descends the corresponding trivial cover of \(X_i\).  Effective descent is
affine faithfully flat descent applied to finite projective algebras with
vanishing differentials.  Replacing sets by finite modules proves the second
equivalence.
\end{foundation}

For \(X_0=\Spec\Fq\),
\[
 \pi_1(X_0,\bar x)=\Gal(\Fbar/\Fq)\simeq\widehat{\mathbf Z}.
\]
Arithmetic Frobenius \(a\mapsto a^q\) is a generator and geometric Frobenius
is its inverse.

\section{Derived cohomology and localization}

The category \(\operatorname{Ab}(X_{\et})\) is Grothendieck abelian: it has a
generator, all colimits, and exact filtered colimits.  Hence it has enough
injectives.

\begin{definition}
Set
\[
 H^r(X,\cF)=R^r\Gamma(X,\cF),\qquad
 R\Gamma(X,-):\D^+(X,\Lambda_n)\to\D^+(\Lambda_n).
\]
The category \(\D^b_c(X,\Lambda_n)\) consists of bounded complexes with
constructible cohomology sheaves.
\end{definition}

Over a field, the equivalence with discrete Galois modules identifies this
derived functor with continuous Galois cohomology: both derive the same
invariants functor under an exact equivalence.

\begin{proposition}[Closed-open localization]
\label{prop:torsion-localization}
For \(i:Z\hookrightarrow X\) closed and \(j:U\hookrightarrow X\) its open
complement, \(j_!\) is exact and
\[
 0\to j_!j^*\cF\to\cF\to i_*i^*\cF\to0.
\]
Thus for \(K\in\D^+(X,\Lambda_n)\),
\[
 j_!j^*K\to K\to i_*i^*K\to
\tag{3.1}\label{eq:torsion-localization}
\]
is a distinguished triangle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The stalk of \(j_!\cG\) is \(\cG_{\bar x}\) on \(U\) and zero on \(Z\).
Exactness and the displayed sequence follow stalkwise from
\cref{prop:etale-enough-points}.
\end{proof}

\section{Cohomological dimension and finiteness}

\begin{theorem}[Artin vanishing and the global bound]
\label{thm:artin-vanishing}
Let \(K\) be separably closed and let \(\cF\) be a constructible
\(\Lambda_n\)-sheaf on a finite-type \(K\)-scheme.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(X\) is affine of dimension \(d\), then
\[
 H^r(X,\cF)=0\qquad(r>d).
\tag{3.2}\label{eq:artin-affine-vanishing}
\]
\item If \(X\) is separated of dimension \(d\), then
\[
 H^r(X,\cF)=H^r_c(X,\cF)=0\qquad(r>2d).
\tag{3.3}\label{eq:artin-global-bound}
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{foundation}[title={Foundational proof capsule: the dimension bound}]
The affine theorem is Artin's prime-to-characteristic vanishing theorem
\cite{SGA4,SGA4half}.  Its induction uses an elementary fibration: after a
finite map and shrinking, a \(d\)-fold maps to an affine
\((d-1)\)-fold with open-curve fibers.  At a strict geometric trait,
\[
 H^r(\eta^{\mathrm{sh}},M)=0\quad(r>1)
\]
for a finite \(\ell\)-primary inertia module \(M\).  Wild inertia is pro-\(p\)
and has no positive \(\ell\)-cohomology; the pro-\(\ell\) tame quotient has
cohomological dimension \(1\).  Leray and induction on the closed complement
give \eqref{eq:artin-affine-vanishing}.  Localization by a dense affine open,
induction on the dimension of its complement, and compactification give
\eqref{eq:artin-global-bound}.

No finite-monodromy group is used to prove the bound.  If
\(\ell\mid|G|\), finite-group cohomology can occur in arbitrarily high
degrees, so a Cartan--Leray reduction alone would be invalid.
\end{foundation}

\begin{theorem}[Torsion finiteness and constructibility]
\label{thm:torsion-finiteness}
Let \(X\) be separated of finite type over a separably closed field and
\(C\in\D^b_c(X,\Lambda_n)\).  Then \(H^r(X,C)\) and \(H^r_c(X,C)\) are finite
and vanish outside a finite interval.  For a separated finite-type morphism
\(f:X\to S\), every \(R^rf_!C\) is constructible and zero for \(r\) large.
For proper \(f\), the same holds for \(Rf_*=Rf_!\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{foundation}[title={Foundational proof capsule: the simultaneous SGA induction}]
Finiteness, proper base change, and constructibility are established together,
but the dimension bound above is proved first and independently.  For each
\(d\), let:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
A_d:&\text{finite cohomology for affine schemes of dimension at most }d,\\
P_d:&\text{proper base change for fibers of dimension at most }d,\\
C_d:&\text{constructibility of }R^rf_!\text{ for such fibers}.
\end{array}
\]
The induction order is
\[
 (A_{d-1},P_{d-1},C_{d-1})
 \Longrightarrow A_d\Longrightarrow P_d\Longrightarrow C_d.
\tag{3.4}\label{eq:simultaneous-induction}
\]

For \(A_d\), noetherian devissage using
\eqref{eq:torsion-localization} leaves an lcc sheaf on a normal dense open
and a term of smaller dimension.  An elementary fibration reduces the open
part to curves.  On a smooth curve, normalization, the Kummer sequence
\[
 1\to\mu_{\ell^n}\to\mathbf G_m
   \xrightarrow{\ell^n}\mathbf G_m\to1
\]
and the strict-trait calculation show that fiber cohomology is finite and
concentrated in degrees \(0,1\).  Leray and \(A_{d-1}\) prove \(A_d\).  The
lcc monodromy is retained; it is not replaced by a finite-group resolution.

For \(P_d\), strict-localize on the base.  A Chow envelope and its proper
hypercover reduce to projective schemes; hyperplane sections, normalization,
and localization reduce the new pieces to smaller relative dimension and the
curve calculation.  The descent totalization is bounded by
\eqref{eq:artin-global-bound}, not by the as-yet unproved \(C_d\).  This is the
proper-base-change proof detailed in
\cref{thm:proper-base-change-torsion}.

For \(C_d\), generic smooth local acyclicity makes
\[
 (R^rf_!\cF)_{\bar s}=H^r_c(X_{\bar s},\cF)
\]
lcc after shrinking \(S\).  Noetherian induction handles the complement,
using \(P_d\) on proper pieces and \(C_{d-1}\) on lower-dimensional fibers.
The Artin bound supplies a uniform finite range of \(r\).

Finally the hypercohomology spectral sequence
\[
 R^af_!(\cH^bC)\Longrightarrow R^{a+b}f_!C
\]
transfers the result from sheaves to bounded complexes.  This coordinated
package uses neither trace, duality, nor weights.
\end{foundation}

\begin{theorem}[Separably closed base extension]
\label{thm:base-field-invariance}
For an extension \(K\subset K'\) of separably closed fields and
\(C\in\D^b_c(X,\Lambda_n)\),
\[
 H^r(X,C)\simeq H^r(X_{K'},C_{K'}),\qquad
 H^r_c(X,C)\simeq H^r_c(X_{K'},C_{K'}).
\]
These maps respect cup products, localization, and proper pushforward.
\end{theorem}

\begin{foundation}
Spread \(X,C\) over a finitely generated \(K\)-subalgebra of \(K'\).
Generic local acyclicity identifies the stalks over a dense open of the base.
Localization and noetherian induction treat the complement.  For compact
supports, compactify and use proper base change.  Every comparison is a
canonical base-change map, which gives the listed compatibilities.
\end{foundation}

\begin{warning}
Invertibility of \(\ell\) is essential.  Characteristic-\(p\) coefficients on
\(\A^1\) already behave differently by the Artin--Schreier sequence.
\end{warning}

\section{Formalization interface}

\begin{formalization}
The chapter exports:
\begin{enumerate}
\item the small etale site, exact sheafification, pullback, pushforward, and
      conservative exact geometric stalks;
\item lcc and constructible predicates stable under the finite abelian and
      monoidal operations;
\item the equivalence with continuous finite \(\pi_1\)-modules;
\item \(\D^b_c(X,\Lambda_n)\) and closed-open localization;
\item separate theorem objects for Artin affine vanishing, the \(2d\) global
      bound, finite cohomology, constructibility of \(Rf_!\), and base-field
      invariance;
\item explicit dependency data recording the induction
      \(A_d\Rightarrow P_d\Rightarrow C_d\).
\end{enumerate}
These are theorem-local capabilities, not global axioms, and none mentions
Frobenius weights.
\end{formalization}
